# Fahua Xuanyi Translation Notes

Zhiyi's 《妙法蓮華經玄義》(Taishō T1716) is his own account of the *Lotus Sūtra*'s doctrinal architecture, delivered as oral lectures and recorded by his disciple Guanding. Together with the *Fahua Wenju* (line-by-line commentary on the sūtra's words) and the *Mohe Zhiguan* (a system of practice built from the sūtra's principles), it forms the "Tiantai Three Great Works on the Lotus." Terminology in this project is kept consistent with this site's other Zhiyi/Tiantai translations — the *Mohe Zhiguan*, the *Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen*, the *Xiao Zhiguan Jiangyi*, and especially the *Fahua Wenju*, its direct sibling — and with the *Jiaoguan Gangzong*, which cites the *Fahua Xuanyi* itself repeatedly as an authority. Sūtra-quote terminology follows this site's own *Lotus Sūtra* translation wherever this text cites it.

## The text and its author

Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597 CE) is the founder of the Tiantai school. Unlike the *Fahua Wenju*, which works through the sūtra's own words phrase by phrase, the *Fahua Xuanyi* never proceeds sequentially through the sūtra's chapters at all. It is organized entirely around Zhiyi's own fivefold analytical scheme — Name, Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, and Teaching — applied first in outline (through his own "sevenfold common exposition") and then unfolded at enormous length, above all in the exposition of the Name through the doctrine of the "ten wondrous aspects" (十妙) of the trace gate and the origin gate, which alone occupies roughly three-quarters of the entire treatise. Scriptural citations throughout are short — usually a single phrase — and function as proof-texts embedded in a continuous, tightly argued philosophical exposition, not as the basis for line-by-line exegesis. See the "Page format" note below for how this shapes the translation.

## Source and copyright

Translated directly from the CBETA edition of the Chinese source text, which is in the public domain (Zhiyi died in 597 CE). Made independently, without reference to any published English translation. Zhanran's later sub-commentary, the *Fahua Xuanyi Shiqian* (法華玄義釋籤, T1717), is consulted only where a passage is too compressed to render confidently on its own.

## Structure and page format

Ten fascicles (卷), each split by the Taishō edition into "upper" and "lower" halves (之上/之下), treated as one continuous page per fascicle — matching the convention already used for the *Mohe Zhiguan* and the *Fahua Wenju*. Fascicle breaks follow the source text's own 卷一–卷十 divisions rather than the doctrinal outline (the doctrinal units are wildly uneven in length — the Name section alone runs to roughly 8,000 of the treatise's 11,000 lines — while the ten fascicles are close to even, at roughly 1,000–1,250 lines each), so each fascicle page uses `h2`/`h3` headings to mark the doctrinal outline (the five sections, the seven steps of the common exposition, the ten wondrous aspects, etc.) independently of where the fascicle itself happens to break.

Page layout follows the *Jiaoguan Gangzong* discourse-style template (`.fascicle`, paragraph-level `.translation-table` rows), not the *Fahua Wenju*'s sentence-by-sentence pairing or the *Xiao Zhiguan Jiangyi*'s dual root-text/commentary track. This text is continuous argumentative prose with short citations embedded in the argument, not verse-by-verse commentary on another text, so paragraph-level pairing (matching the source's own paragraph breaks) is the closer fit.

## The Five Sections — coined for this project

名/體/宗/用/教, the fivefold scheme that gives this treatise its entire structure, had no established English rendering anywhere on this site before this project. Coined here; intended as the permanent site-wide rendering for any future Tiantai text that invokes the scheme (the *Jiaoguan Gangzong*'s own translation notes flag this need explicitly).

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 釋名 | Explaining the Name | First section; occupies roughly three-quarters of the entire treatise |
| 辨體 / 顯體 | Clarifying the Essence | Second section |
| 明宗 | Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine | Third section; briefest of the five |
| 論用 | Discussing the Function | Fourth section |
| 判教 | Classifying the Teachings | Fifth section |
| 七番共解 | the sevenfold common exposition | Zhiyi's own method, applied to all five sections at once before each is unfolded separately: 標章 marking the section, 引證 citing proof-texts, 生起 showing its arising in sequence, 開合 opening and combining it with other schemes, 料簡 resolving doubts, 觀心 the contemplation of mind, 會異 reconciling it with other terms |
| 五重各說 | the five sections, each expounded separately | The particular (別) counterpart to the sevenfold common exposition |

## Fascicle 3 — complete

Fascicle 3 opens with the tail of the wondrous of the object (境妙) — cross-referencing all six objects from Fascicle 2 against one another, then against the sūtra's own text — then covers the wondrous of wisdom (智妙), the second of the ten wondrous aspects, in its entirety: the general discussion of wisdom (enumerating twenty kinds; classifying them; characterizing each in detail, with heavy direct citation of Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma sources — above all the *Vibhāṣā* — for the four good roots and four fruits; illuminating the six objects; judging coarse vs. wondrous; opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous), and then wisdom discussed in relation to each object in turn (the twelve links, the Four Noble Truths, the two truths' twenty-one-fold provisional/real scheme, the three truths, the one truth, and successive mutual reflection among all of them). The fascicle closes by opening the wondrous of practice (行妙), the third of the ten wondrous aspects: practice by increasing enumeration, both in general and according to each of the Four Teachings, and — under the "five practices" heading — the distinct teaching's precept holy practice worked out branch by branch, ending with a Q&A on the *Brahma Net Sūtra*'s bodhisattva precepts. Verified at 99.7–99.8% coverage across each committed chunk.

## Fascicle 4 — complete

Fascicle 4 closes the wondrous of practice (行妙) entirely: the concentration holy practice (mundane dhyāna's root and root-pure approaches; world-transcending dhyāna's observing/training/permeating/cultivating; world-transcending superior dhyāna) and the wisdom holy practice (the four kinds of Four Noble Truths; the ground of fearlessness; the twenty-five samādhis) complete the distinct teaching's five practices begun in Fascicle 3 with the brahma, heavenly, infant, and sick practices closing it out; the perfect teaching's own five practices follow, correlated exhaustively against the ten dharma-realms, the three truths, the four kinds of twelve links, the four kinds of Four Noble Truths, the seven kinds of two truths, the five kinds of three truths, and the one real truth, before being relocated within a single contemplation of mind. The fascicle then opens the wondrous of stage (位妙), the fourth of the ten wondrous aspects, working through five of its six "Medicinal Herbs"-parable stages: the small medicinal herb (human/heavenly vehicle, via extensive *Saddharmasmṛtyupasthāna Sūtra* cosmology of the desire-realm heavens); the middle medicinal herb (the two vehicles, via the classic Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma path scheme — the seven worthies and seven sages, using this site's existing "stream-enterer/once-returner/non-returner/arhat" and "path of seeing/path of cultivation" terminology confirmed via *Mohe Zhiguan* — plus the pratyekabuddha); the superior medicinal herb (the Tripiṭaka bodhisattva's three incalculable eons, correlated with the same seven-worthy ladder); the small tree (the shared teaching's ten common stages, with separate bodhisattva-only names shown to carry the identical underlying sense, and correlated again with the distinct teaching's own ten-faiths/thirty-minds/ten-grounds vocabulary); and the great tree (the distinct teaching's own stage, its position-count settled by triangulating the *Yingluo Sūtra*, the *Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra*'s three contemplations, and the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s five practices). This closes 卷四下 and Fascicle 4 in its entirety. Verified at 99.0–99.8% coverage across each committed chunk. Fascicle 5 opens with the perfect teaching's own "most real matter" — the sixth and final stage of the six-stage scheme.

Fascicle 4 opens directly with the concentration holy practice (定聖行): mundane dhyāna (the root dhyāna of flavor's four root dhyānas, then the root pure dhyāna's three approaches — the six wondrous gates, the sixteen special excellences, the penetrating-and-clear dhyāna), world-transcending dhyāna (observing via the nine contemplations/eight liberations/eight bases of mastery/ten totalities; training via the nine sequential concentrations; permeating via the lion's swift-leaping concentration; cultivating via the transcendent concentration — culminating in the ground of forbearance and the four withered/flourishing applications of mindfulness), and world-transcending superior dhyāna (the nine great dhyānas, named only), closed by the Five Flavors coarse/wondrous ladder and opening the coarse. Then the wisdom holy practice: the four kinds of Four Noble Truths in full (generation-and-extinction, no generation, the immeasurable, the unconditioned), entering the ground of fearlessness (the initial ground of joy), the five fears, the twenty-five samādhis named and explained one by one against the twenty-five states of existence, and why they are called "samādhi" and "king." Fascicle 4 then closes the distinct teaching's five practices (別五行) begun in Fascicle 3, with the brahma, heavenly, infant, and sick practices, and a closing Q&A on why only three of the five practices (holy/brahma/wisdom→precept,concentration,wisdom) realize a named ground — this closes 卷四上 of the source text. 卷四下 opens the perfect teaching's five practices (圓五行, promised back in Fascicle 3), matched exhaustively against the *Lotus Sūtra*'s room/robe/seat imagery and then against every prior doctrinal scheme built up so far (the ten dharma-realms, the twenty-five states of existence via the three truths, the four kinds of twelve links, the four kinds of Four Noble Truths, the seven kinds of two truths, the five kinds of three truths, the one real truth), before being relocated within a single contemplation of mind — **this closes the wondrous of practice (行妙) in its entirety, the third of the ten wondrous aspects**. The fascicle then opens the wondrous of stage (位妙), the fourth of the ten, surveying prior authorities on stage-terminology before adopting the *Lotus Sūtra*'s own implicit six-stage scheme from the "Parable of the Medicinal Herbs" (small/middle/superior herb = three Tripiṭaka-teaching stages; small tree = shared teaching; great tree = distinct teaching; "the most real matter" = perfect teaching). Verified at 99.0–99.8% coverage across each committed chunk.
