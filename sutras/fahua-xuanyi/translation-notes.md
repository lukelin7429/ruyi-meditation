# Fahua Xuanyi Translation Notes

Zhiyi's 《妙法蓮華經玄義》(Taishō T1716) is his own account of the *Lotus Sūtra*'s doctrinal architecture, delivered as oral lectures and recorded by his disciple Guanding. Together with the *Fahua Wenju* (line-by-line commentary on the sūtra's words) and the *Mohe Zhiguan* (a system of practice built from the sūtra's principles), it forms the "Tiantai Three Great Works on the Lotus." Terminology in this project is kept consistent with this site's other Zhiyi/Tiantai translations — the *Mohe Zhiguan*, the *Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen*, the *Xiao Zhiguan Jiangyi*, and especially the *Fahua Wenju*, its direct sibling — and with the *Jiaoguan Gangzong*, which cites the *Fahua Xuanyi* itself repeatedly as an authority. Sūtra-quote terminology follows this site's own *Lotus Sūtra* translation wherever this text cites it.

## The text and its author

Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597 CE) is the founder of the Tiantai school. Unlike the *Fahua Wenju*, which works through the sūtra's own words phrase by phrase, the *Fahua Xuanyi* never proceeds sequentially through the sūtra's chapters at all. It is organized entirely around Zhiyi's own fivefold analytical scheme — Name, Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, and Teaching — applied first in outline (through his own "sevenfold common exposition") and then unfolded at enormous length, above all in the exposition of the Name through the doctrine of the "ten wondrous aspects" (十妙) of the trace gate and the origin gate, which alone occupies roughly three-quarters of the entire treatise. Scriptural citations throughout are short — usually a single phrase — and function as proof-texts embedded in a continuous, tightly argued philosophical exposition, not as the basis for line-by-line exegesis. See the "Page format" note below for how this shapes the translation.

## Source and copyright

Translated directly from the CBETA edition of the Chinese source text, which is in the public domain (Zhiyi died in 597 CE). Made independently, without reference to any published English translation. Zhanran's later sub-commentary, the *Fahua Xuanyi Shiqian* (法華玄義釋籤, T1717), is consulted only where a passage is too compressed to render confidently on its own.

## Structure and page format

Ten fascicles (卷), each split by the Taishō edition into "upper" and "lower" halves (之上/之下), treated as one continuous page per fascicle — matching the convention already used for the *Mohe Zhiguan* and the *Fahua Wenju*. Fascicle breaks follow the source text's own 卷一–卷十 divisions rather than the doctrinal outline (the doctrinal units are wildly uneven in length — the Name section alone runs to roughly 8,000 of the treatise's 11,000 lines — while the ten fascicles are close to even, at roughly 1,000–1,250 lines each), so each fascicle page uses `h2`/`h3` headings to mark the doctrinal outline (the five sections, the seven steps of the common exposition, the ten wondrous aspects, etc.) independently of where the fascicle itself happens to break.

Page layout follows the *Jiaoguan Gangzong* discourse-style template (`.fascicle`, paragraph-level `.translation-table` rows), not the *Fahua Wenju*'s sentence-by-sentence pairing or the *Xiao Zhiguan Jiangyi*'s dual root-text/commentary track. This text is continuous argumentative prose with short citations embedded in the argument, not verse-by-verse commentary on another text, so paragraph-level pairing (matching the source's own paragraph breaks) is the closer fit.

## Terminology decisions made for this project

Three points of inconsistency across this site's existing Tiantai translations had to be resolved before starting. Recorded here so later projects don't have to re-litigate them:

| Point | Existing site usage | Decision for this project | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 止觀 | "calming-and-contemplation" (*Mohe Zhiguan*, *Shi Chan Boluomi*) vs. "calming and insight" (*Xiao Zhiguan Jiangyi*) | **calming-and-contemplation** | This is a doctrinal treatise, not a meditation manual — closer in genre to the *Mohe Zhiguan*, its companion among the Three Great Works |
| 了因 (one of the three causes of buddha-nature) | "the complete cause" (*Jiaoguan Gangzong*) vs. "the revealing cause" (*Fahua Wenju*) | **the revealing cause** | The *Fahua Wenju* is this text's direct sibling — same base sūtra, same author, same three-cause discussion |
| 一心三觀 triad naming | Three different phrasings across *Mohe Zhiguan* fascicles 3, 5–6 and *Xiao Zhiguan Jiangyi* ch. 10 (see that project's own notes) | Follow whatever specific wording Zhiyi uses in context here; record any newly settled short form below as it is confirmed | No single site-wide form existed to inherit; this project's own usage, once stable, should become the reference going forward |

## The Five Sections — coined for this project

名/體/宗/用/教, the fivefold scheme that gives this treatise its entire structure, had no established English rendering anywhere on this site before this project. Coined here; intended as the permanent site-wide rendering for any future Tiantai text that invokes the scheme (the *Jiaoguan Gangzong*'s own translation notes flag this need explicitly).

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 釋名 | Explaining the Name | First section; occupies roughly three-quarters of the entire treatise |
| 辨體 / 顯體 | Clarifying the Essence | Second section |
| 明宗 | Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine | Third section; briefest of the five |
| 論用 | Discussing the Function | Fourth section |
| 判教 | Classifying the Teachings | Fifth section |
| 七番共解 | the sevenfold common exposition | Zhiyi's own method, applied to all five sections at once before each is unfolded separately: 標章 marking the section, 引證 citing proof-texts, 生起 showing its arising in sequence, 開合 opening and combining it with other schemes, 料簡 resolving doubts, 觀心 the contemplation of mind, 會異 reconciling it with other terms |
| 五重各說 | the five sections, each expounded separately | The particular (別) counterpart to the sevenfold common exposition |

## Terminology newly settled in this project

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 兼 / 但 / 對 / 帶 | combined / exclusive / contrasted / accompanied | Describes how each of the Five Periods relates the perfect teaching to the other three; see Fascicle 1 reading note |
| 妙 (as a technical term, not just "wondrous" in the ordinary sense) | wondrous | Kept as the single running English word throughout, per Zhiyi's own repeated insistence that 妙 names something with no separate essence apart from praise of the dharma itself |
| 麁 | coarse | Paired opposite of 妙 throughout |
| 悉檀 | siddhānta | Transliterated, following site-wide convention already set by the *Fahua Wenju* |

## Fascicle 1 — in progress

Confirmed from the source text so far: Guanding's account of the text's transmission (法華私記緣起); Zhiyi's own Preface (序王) with its six similes of the lotus flower, three for the provisional-and-real and three for origin-and-trace; Guanding's private preface (私序王) and a further passage on the meaning of "wondrous"; the opening of Fascicle 1 proper, including the distinction between the common and particular senses of the Five Sections, the sevenfold common exposition defined, and the first step (標章, marking the section) worked through in brief for the Name, the Essence, the Cardinal Doctrine, and the Function. Still to come in Fascicle 1: marking the Teaching; citing proof-texts for all five sections; their arising in sequence; opening and combining them with other schemes; resolving doubts; the contemplation of mind; an extended reconciliation with the four siddhāntas; and the opening rounds of Zhiyi's critique of earlier masters' readings of "wondrous," including an extended rebuttal of Guangzhai (Fayun).
