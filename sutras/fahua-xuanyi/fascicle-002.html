The Nine Realms in One

Having spent all of Fascicle 1 refuting Guangzhai's theory of "wondrous," Zhiyi turns at last to his own account: the six senses in which a single dharma-realm already contains the other nine, and in which the Buddha's own fruit pervades every place and every time. From here the treatise opens its two central frameworks — the ten suchnesses applied across the ten dharma-realms (the seed of the later doctrine of "three thousand realms in a single thought"), and the threefold division into the dharmas of sentient beings, of the Buddha, and of mind — before turning to the relative and absolute readings of "wondrous" and opening, at last, the ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate.

Zhiyi's Own Account: Six Senses of Cause and Fruit

Chinese Source

天台智者大師說

四、正論今意，為二：先、略用彼名顯於妙義。因具三義者，一法界具九法界，名體廣。九法界即佛法界，名位高。十法界即空、即假、即中，名用長。即一而論三，即三而論一，非各異，亦非橫，亦非一，故稱妙也。果體具三義者，體遍一切處，名體廣。久已成佛久遠久遠，名位高。從本垂迹，過、現、未來三世益物，名用長。是為因果六義，異於餘經，是故稱妙。

Ru-Yi Translation

Expounded by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai.

Fourth, directly discussing the present meaning, in two parts: first, briefly using Guangzhai's own terms to reveal the sense of "wondrous." As for the cause possessing three senses: that a single dharma-realm possesses the other nine dharma-realms within it is named "the essence is broad." That the nine dharma-realms are themselves the buddha-dharma-realm is named "the stage is high." That the ten dharma-realms are precisely empty, precisely provisional, precisely the middle is named "the function is long." Speaking of the one in terms of the three, and speaking of the three in terms of the one — neither each separately different, nor merely arranged side by side, nor merely one — hence it is called wondrous. As for the fruit's essence possessing three senses: that its essence pervades every place is named "the essence is broad." That it has long, long since attained buddhahood, incalculably long ago, is named "the stage is high." That, from the origin, the trace is let down, benefiting beings across the past, present, and future — the three times — is named "the function is long." These are the six senses of cause and fruit, differing from every other sūtra — hence it is called wondrous.

又乳經一種因果廣、高、長，一種因果狹、下、短，則一麁一妙(云云)。酪經唯一種因果狹、下、短，但麁無妙。生蘇經三種因果狹、下、短，一種因果廣、高、長，則三麁一妙。熟蘇經二種因果狹、下、短，一種因果廣、高、長，則二麁一妙。醍醐經一種因果廣、高、長，但妙無麁。又醍醐經妙因、妙果，與諸經妙因、妙果不異，故稱為妙也。

Again, the milk sūtra has one kind of cause and fruit that is broad, high, and long, and one kind that is narrow, low, and short — thus it has one coarse and one wondrous, and so on. The cream sūtra has only the one kind of cause and fruit that is narrow, low, and short — it is coarse only, without any wondrous. The fresh-curdled-butter sūtra has three kinds of cause and fruit that are narrow, low, and short, and one kind that is broad, high, and long — thus it has three coarse and one wondrous. The ripened-butter sūtra has two kinds of cause and fruit that are narrow, low, and short, and one kind that is broad, high, and long — thus it has two coarse and one wondrous. The ghee sūtra has only the one kind of cause and fruit that is broad, high, and long — it is wondrous only, without any coarse. Moreover, the ghee sūtra's wondrous cause and wondrous fruit are no different from the wondrous cause and wondrous fruit of any other sūtra — hence it, too, is called wondrous.

復次觀心釋。若觀己心不具眾生心、佛心者，是體狹；具者是體廣。若己心不等佛心是位下；若等佛心是位高。若己心、眾生心、佛心，不即空、即假、即中者，是用短；即空、即假、即中者，是用長。復次，於一法界通達十法界六即位者，亦是體廣，亦是位高，亦是用長。初約十法界，是顯理一；次約五味，是約教一；次約觀心，是約行一；次約六即，是約人一。略示妙義竟。

Moreover, an interpretation through the contemplation of mind: if, contemplating one's own mind, it is found not to contain the minds of sentient beings and of the Buddha, this is a narrow essence; if it is found to contain them, this is a broad essence. If one's own mind is not equal to the Buddha's mind, this is a low stage; if it is equal to the Buddha's mind, this is a high stage. If one's own mind, the minds of sentient beings, and the Buddha's mind are not precisely empty, precisely provisional, precisely the middle, this is a short function; if they are precisely empty, precisely provisional, precisely the middle, this is a long function. Moreover, to penetrate through the six identities' stages across the ten dharma-realms within a single dharma-realm is likewise a broad essence, a high stage, and a long function. The first account, in terms of the ten dharma-realms, reveals the oneness of principle; the next, in terms of the Five Flavors, is the oneness of teaching; the next, in terms of the contemplation of mind, is the oneness of practice; the next, in terms of the six identities, is the oneness of the person. This briefly indicates the sense of "wondrous," now complete.

Reading note

Zhiyi opens his own positive account by deliberately reusing Guangzhai's own six terms (essence/stage/function, broad/high/long, applied to cause and fruit) — the very framework his twenty-four-fold rebuttal just dismantled — but relocates them: instead of separating "coarse" past teachings from a "wondrous" Lotus, he shows that a single dharma-realm already, inherently, contains the other nine within it. This single move is the germ of the doctrine later developed at length in the Mohe Zhiguan as "three thousand realms in a single thought" (一念三千), already established site-wide.

Three Dharmas: Sentient Beings, the Buddha, and Mind 眾生法・佛法・心法

Chinese Source

廣說者：先法，次妙。南岳師舉三種，謂眾生法、佛法、心法。如經：「為令眾生，開示悟入佛之知見。」若眾生無佛知見，何所論開？當知佛之知見蘊在眾生也。又經：「但以父母所生眼」，即肉眼；「徹見內外彌樓山」，即天眼。洞見諸色而無染著，即慧眼。見色無錯謬即法眼。雖未得無漏，而其眼根清淨。若此一眼具諸眼用，即佛眼。此是今經明眾生法妙之文也。《大經》云：「學大乘者，雖有肉眼，名為佛眼。耳鼻五根，例亦如是。」《殃掘》云：「所謂彼眼根，於諸如來常，具足無減修，了了分明見。」乃至意根亦如是。《大品》云：「六自在王，性清淨故。」又云：「一切法趣眼，是趣不過。」眼尚不可得，何況有趣、有非趣？乃至一切法趣意，亦如是。此即諸經明眾生法妙也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Given at length: first "dharma," then "wondrous." Master Nanyue raises three kinds: the dharma of sentient beings, the dharma of the Buddha, and the dharma of mind. As the sūtra says: "For the sake of causing sentient beings to open, show, awaken to, and enter the Buddha's own knowledge and vision." If sentient beings did not possess the Buddha's knowledge and vision at all, what could there be to speak of "opening"? One should know that the Buddha's own knowledge and vision is already stored within sentient beings. Again, the sūtra says: "Merely with the eyes given by his father and mother" — this is the physical eye; "seeing through, inside and out, to Mount Sumeru" — this is the heavenly eye. Seeing through all forms without being stained or attached to them is the wisdom-eye. Seeing forms without error or confusion is the dharma-eye. Though not yet having attained the taintless, his eye-faculty is nonetheless pure. If this single eye already possesses the function of all the eyes, this is the buddha-eye. This is the present sūtra's own text clarifying the wondrous dharma of sentient beings. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "For one who studies the Mahāyāna, though he has only the physical eye, it is named the buddha-eye. The ear, nose, and the remaining five faculties are, by the same pattern, likewise so." The Aṅgulimālīya Sūtra says: "As for that very eye-faculty, in every one of the tathāgatas it is eternal, complete without diminishment in its cultivation, seeing with perfect clarity and distinctness" — and this extends even to the mind-faculty, likewise so. The Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra says: "The six sovereign kings, since their nature is pure" — and again: "All dharmas converge upon the eye; this convergence is unsurpassed." If the eye itself is unattainable, how much more so is there any "converging" or "not converging"! And this extends even to all dharmas converging upon the mind, likewise so. This is how the various sūtras clarify the wondrous dharma of sentient beings.

佛法妙者，如經：「止止不須說，我法妙難思。」佛法不出權、實，「是法甚深妙，難見難可了。一切眾生類，無能知佛者」，即實智妙也。「及佛諸餘法，亦無能測者」，即佛權智妙也。如是二法，唯佛與佛乃能究盡諸法實相，是名佛法妙。

As for the wondrous dharma of the Buddha: as the sūtra says, "Stop, stop — there is no need to speak; my dharma is wondrous and hard to conceive." The Buddha's own dharma does not go beyond the provisional and the real: "This dharma is exceedingly deep and wondrous, hard to see and hard to fathom; among every kind of sentient being, none is able to know the Buddha" — this is the wondrousness of real wisdom. "And as for the Buddha's every other dharma, none is able to measure it either" — this is the wondrousness of the Buddha's provisional wisdom. Only buddha together with buddha is able to fully fathom, to their very limit, the true mark of all dharmas through these two dharmas together — this is named the wondrous dharma of the Buddha.

心法妙者，如〈安樂行〉中，修攝其心，觀一切法，不動不退。又一念隨喜等。《普賢觀》云：「我心自空，罪福無主」「觀心無心，法不住法。」又「心純是法」。《淨名》云：「觀身實相，觀佛亦然。諸佛解脫當於眾生心行中求。」《華嚴》云：「心、佛及眾生，是三無差別」「破心微塵，出大千經卷」，是名心法妙也。

As for the wondrous dharma of mind: as in "Peaceful Practices," cultivating and gathering the mind, contemplating all dharmas, unmoving and unretreating; and again, a single thought-moment of rejoicing, and so on. The Contemplation of Samantabhadra Sūtra says: "My mind is itself empty; offense and merit have no master," and: "Contemplating mind, there is no mind; dharmas do not abide in dharmas," and again: "Mind is purely dharma." The Vimalakīrti says: "Contemplate the true mark of the body; contemplate the Buddha in just the same way. The liberation of all buddhas ought to be sought within the mental activity of sentient beings." The Avataṃsaka says: "Mind, Buddha, and sentient beings — these three are without distinction," and: "Breaking open a single mote of dust of the mind, one brings forth scrolls of sūtras as vast as a great-thousand world-system." This is named the wondrous dharma of mind.

△今依三法，更廣分別。若廣眾生法，一往通論諸因果及一切法。若廣佛法，此則據果。若廣心法，此則據因。

Now, in dependence on these three dharmas, let me differentiate them still further at length. In expounding the dharma of sentient beings at length, this generally discusses, all together, every cause and fruit and all dharmas. In expounding the dharma of the Buddha at length, this is based on the fruit. In expounding the dharma of mind at length, this is based on the cause.

Reading note

Master Nanyue is Huisi (慧思, 515–577), Zhiyi's own teacher. The five eyes (physical, heavenly, wisdom, dharma, buddha) are the same set already established site-wide via the Jiaoguan Gangzong; here Zhiyi's point is that all five are, in some sense, already latent in the single physical eye. "Peaceful Practices" and the destitute-son parable's language echo material already translated in full in the Fahua Wenju.

The Ten Suchnesses 十如是

Chinese Source

眾生法為二：先列法數，次解法相。數者，經論或明一法攝一切法，謂心是：三界無別法，唯是一心作。或明二法攝一切法，所謂名、色：一切世間中，但有名與色。或明三法攝一切法：謂命、識、煖。如是等增數，乃至百千。今經用十法攝一切法：「所謂諸法：如是相、如是性、如是體、如是力、如是作、如是因、如是緣、如是果、如是報、如是本末究竟等。」南岳師讀此文皆云如，故呼為十如也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The dharma of sentient beings has two parts: first, listing the dharma-numbers; second, explaining the dharma-marks. As for the numbers: sūtras and treatises sometimes clarify that a single dharma gathers in all dharmas, namely mind — "the three realms have no separate dharma of their own; they are made by the one mind alone." Sometimes they clarify that two dharmas gather in all dharmas, namely name and form — "within all the world, there is only name and form." Sometimes they clarify that three dharmas gather in all dharmas, namely life, consciousness, and warmth. Numbers of this kind increase in this way, up to a hundred, a thousand. The present sūtra uses ten dharmas to gather in all dharmas: "That is to say, all dharmas: such a mark, such a nature, such an essence, such a power, such an activity, such a cause, such a condition, such a fruit, such a retribution, and such an ultimate equality from beginning to end." Master Nanyue, reading this passage, read every instance as "thus," or "such" — hence it is called "the ten suchnesses."

天台師云：「依義讀文，凡有三轉。一云：是相如、是性如，乃至是報如。二云：如是相、如是性，乃至如是報。三云：相如是、性如是，乃至報如是。若皆稱如者，如名不異，即空義也。若作如是相、如是性者，點空相性，名字施設，邐迤不同，即假義也。若作相如是者，如於中道實相之是，即中義也。」分別令易解故，明空、假、中，得意為言，空即假、中。約如明空，一空一切空。點如明相，一假一切假。就是論中，一中一切中，非一二三而一二三，不縱不橫，名為實相。唯佛與佛，究竟此法。是十法，攝一切法。若依義便，作三意分別。若依讀便，當依偈文云：「如是大果報，種種性相義」(云云)。

The Tiantai master says: "Reading the text in accordance with its sense, there are, in general, three ways of parsing it. The first way reads: 'the mark, thus,' 'the nature, thus,' down to 'the retribution, thus.' The second way reads: 'thus, the mark,' 'thus, the nature,' down to 'thus, the retribution.' The third way reads: 'the mark, thus it is,' 'the nature, thus it is,' down to 'the retribution, thus it is.' If each is uniformly called 'thus,' then 'thus' as a name admits no distinction — this is the sense of emptiness. If it is read as 'thus, the mark' and 'thus, the nature,' pointing to emptiness while marking out mark and nature, names and designations proliferating differently from one another — this is the sense of the provisional. If it is read as 'the mark, thus it is,' where 'thus it is' refers to the true mark of the middle way — this is the sense of the middle." This differentiation is given to make it easier to understand; to clarify emptiness, the provisional, and the middle, and to grasp their meaning as one speaks of them: emptiness is itself the provisional and the middle. In terms of "thus," emptiness is clarified — one instance of emptiness, and every instance is empty. Pointing to "thus," the mark is clarified — one instance of the provisional, and every instance is provisional. In terms of "it is," the middle is discussed — one instance of the middle, and every instance is the middle: neither one, two, nor three, and yet one, two, and three; neither arranged in sequence nor set side by side — this is named the true mark. Only buddha together with buddha fully fathoms this dharma. These ten dharmas gather in all dharmas. If one follows what is convenient for the sense, this threefold differentiation is made; if one follows what is convenient for the reading, one should follow the verse text, which says: "Such is the great retribution, and the various meanings of nature and mark," and so on.

次判權實者，光宅以前五如是為權，屬凡夫；次四如是為實，屬聖人；後一如是，總結權實。引偈證云：「如是大果報」，大故知是實；「種種性相」，故知是權。今恐不爾，大義有三：大、多、勝。若取大為實者，亦應取多取勝。種種之名，豈非多義？若言權屬凡夫，凡夫何意無實？若實屬聖人，聖人何意無權？如此抑沒，義不可依。又北地師以前五為權，後五為實。此皆人情耳。

Next, judging the provisional and real status of the ten suchnesses: Guangzhai takes the first five suchnesses as provisional, belonging to ordinary beings, the next four suchnesses as real, belonging to sages, and the last one suchness as a general summation of the provisional and the real together. He cites the verse as proof, saying: "Such is the great retribution" — since it is "great," one knows it is real; "the various meanings of nature and mark" — hence one knows this is provisional. I fear this is not so. The sense of "great" has three aspects: great, many, and superior. If "great" is taken to mean "real," then "many" and "superior" ought likewise to be taken as such. Does the term "various" not itself carry the sense of "many"? And if the provisional is said to belong to ordinary beings, why should ordinary beings have no share in the real at all? And if the real is said to belong to sages, why should sages have no share in the provisional at all? Suppressing and obscuring the sense in this way cannot be relied upon. Again, a northern master takes the first five as provisional and the last five as real. All of this is nothing but personal opinion.

Reading note

This is the treatise's own account of the "three readings" (三轉讀文) of the ten-suchness passage — one of the most-cited passages in the entire Tiantai corpus, since it grounds the three truths (empty/provisional/middle) directly in the sūtra's own grammar rather than importing them from the Middle Treatise. The ten suchnesses themselves (mark/nature/essence/power/activity/cause/condition/fruit/retribution/ultimate-equality-from-beginning-to-end) are already established site-wide via the Mohe Zhiguan and Fahua Wenju; this passage is their doctrinal origin point within this sūtra's own text.

The Hundred Realms, Thousand Suchnesses 百界千如

Chinese Source

今明權實者，以十如是約十法界，謂六道、四聖也。皆稱法界者，其意有三：十數皆依法界，法界外更無復法。能、所合稱，故言十法界也。二、此十種法，分齊不同，因果隔別，凡聖有異，故加之以界也。三、此十皆即法界，攝一切法。一切法趣地獄，是趣不過。當體即理，更無所依，故名法界。乃至佛法界，亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, clarifying the provisional and the real: the ten suchnesses are applied in terms of the ten dharma-realms, namely the six destinies and the four kinds of sages. That all ten are called "dharma-realms" has, in general, three senses. First: the ten numbers all depend upon the dharma-realm, and there is no further dharma outside the dharma-realm; what depends and what is depended upon are named together, and so it is called "the ten dharma-realms." Second: these ten kinds of dharma each have their own distinct boundaries, their causes and fruits held apart from one another, ordinary beings and sages differing from each other — hence "realm" is added to their name. Third: these ten are, every one of them, identical with the dharma-realm, gathering in all dharmas. That all dharmas converge upon hell is itself an unsurpassed convergence; identical, in its very essence, with principle, with nothing further to depend upon — hence it is named "dharma-realm." And this extends even to the buddha-dharma-realm, likewise so.

若十數依法界者，能依從所依，即入空界也。十界界隔者，即假界也。十數皆法界者，即中界也。欲令易解，如此分別。得意為言，空即假、中，無一二三，如前(云云)。

If the ten numbers depend on the dharma-realm, then what depends follows from what is depended upon — this is entering the realm of emptiness. That the ten realms are held distinctly apart, realm from realm, is the realm of the provisional. That the ten numbers are, every one of them, the dharma-realm, is the realm of the middle. This differentiation is given only to make it easier to understand; grasping the meaning as one speaks of it, emptiness is itself the provisional and the middle, with no separate one, two, or three, as before, and so on.

此一法界具十如是，十法界具百如是。又一法界具九法界，則有百法界，千如是。束為五差：一、惡。二、善。三、二乘。四、菩薩。五、佛。判為二法：前四是權法，後一是實法，細論各具權實，且依兩義。

This single dharma-realm possesses the ten suchnesses within it; the ten dharma-realms possess a hundred suchnesses within them. Again, since a single dharma-realm possesses the other nine dharma-realms within it, there are, in all, a hundred dharma-realms, and a thousand suchnesses. Gathered together, these fall into five groupings: first, evil; second, good; third, the two vehicles; fourth, bodhisattvas; fifth, buddhas. Judged as two dharmas: the first four are provisional dharmas, and the last one is the real dharma — though, examined in fine detail, each of the five in fact possesses both the provisional and the real within it; for now, I rely on this twofold sense.

然此權實不可思議，乃是三世諸佛二智之境。以此為境，何法不收？此境發智，何智不發？故文云「諸法」。諸法者，是所照境廣也。唯佛與佛乃能究盡者，明能照智深，窮邊盡底也。其智慧門難解難入者，歎境妙也。我所得智慧，微妙最第一者，歎智與境相稱也。〈方便品〉長行，略說此法。後開示悟入，廣說此法。火宅，譬喻此法。信解，領解此法。長者，付子此法。藥草，述成此法。化城，引入此法。如是等種種，秖名十如權實法耳。

Yet this provisional and real is inconceivable — it is precisely the object of the buddhas' twin wisdom across the three times. Taking this as the object, what dharma is not gathered in? This object gives rise to wisdom — what wisdom is not thereby given rise to? Hence the text says "all dharmas." "All dharmas" means that the object illuminated is vast. "Only buddha together with buddha is able to fathom it fully" clarifies that the wisdom which illuminates it is profound, reaching to the very limit, exhausting its every depth. "Its gate of wisdom is hard to understand and hard to enter" praises the wondrousness of the object. "The wisdom I have attained is subtle and wondrous, most supreme of all" praises how wisdom and its object correspond to one another. The prose section of "Skillful Means" briefly teaches this dharma; the later "opening, showing, awakening to, and entering" teaches this dharma at length. The burning house gives a parable of this dharma. "Faith and Understanding" receives and understands this dharma. The elder bestows this dharma on his son. Medicinal Herbs restates and confirms this dharma. The phantom city draws beings in to this dharma. All such things as these are simply named the dharma of the ten suchnesses' provisional and real.

The Tathāgata sees through completely, fathoming the very depth of the ten dharmas, exhausting their every limit, clearly recognizing sentient beings: whether their seed has been planted or not, whether it has sprouted or not, whether it is ripe or not, whether they can be liberated or cannot be liberated — knowing this truly, as it really is, without any error. Aṅgulimāla, though an evil man, had his true-mark nature already ripe, and so was liberated at once. The bhikṣu of the fourth dhyāna, though a good man, had his evil nature and mark already ripe, and so could not be liberated. One should know that the dharma of sentient beings is inconceivable: though real, it is yet provisional; though provisional, it is yet real; the real and the provisional are mutually identical, and do not obstruct one another. One cannot observe sentient beings with the eyes of an ox or a sheep; one cannot appraise sentient beings with the mind of an ordinary person. Only wisdom equal to the Tathāgata's own is able to appraise them. Why is this? Because the dharma of sentient beings is wondrous.

Reading note

This "hundred dharma-realms, thousand suchnesses" (百界千如) passage is the direct textual seed of "three thousand realms in a single thought," developed at length in the Mohe Zhiguan: multiply the hundred realms and thousand suchnesses by the three kinds of worlds (of the five aggregates, of sentient beings, and of lands) and the figure of three thousand results. Aṅgulimāla, the murderer converted by the Buddha and the subject of his own sūtra, and the "bhikṣu of the fourth dhyāna" who mistook a meditative attainment for arhatship and, on losing it, wrongly believed the Buddha had lied to him and so fell into wrong view at death, are both stock Tiantai examples of how surface appearance (evil deeds, outward virtue) can mislead about a being's actual underlying readiness for liberation.

The Ten Suchnesses of the Four Evil Destinies 四趣十法

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying the ten dharmas of the four evil destinies: "such a mark" is precisely the mark of evil, signifying a fall into a place contrary to one's wishes. It is like a person not yet struck by misfortune, whose unlucky complexion is already visible; a physiognomist, examining and distinguishing it, is able to foretell the coming calamity and decline. When an evil mark arises, it points, from far off, toward the hells; ordinary people do not know this, the two vehicles know it only vaguely, bodhisattvas know it without full depth, and the Buddha knows it to its very limit. Like a skilled physiognomist who sees through to the beginning and end alike — hence it is called "such a mark."

It is like wood containing fire: when it meets the right condition, the fire ignites at once. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Because a conditioned dharma possesses the nature of generation, it is able to generate what generates it." This evil possesses the generative nature of the four evil destinies, and so a condition is able to ignite it. It is unlike a clay or wooden image, which, though it has an outward mark, has no generative nature within it, and so, though it might "generate," cannot actually generate anything. The evil nature is not like this — hence it is called "such a nature."

"Such an essence" is gathering together that which is broken and crushed — coarse and evil form and mind — as its own substantial essence. Moreover: in this present life, the mind is already broken first; in the life to come, the body is broken. Again, this present life's flowering retribution likewise breaks both body and mind; the life to come's fruit-retribution likewise breaks both body and mind. Hence body and mind, as what is broken, are taken as the essence.

如是力者，惡功用也。譬如片物，雖未被用，指擬所任，言其有用。《大經》云：「作舍取木，不取縷線，作布取縷，不取泥木。」地獄有登刀上劍之用，餓鬼吞銅、噉鐵之用，畜生強者伏弱，魚鱗相咀，牽車挽重，皆是惡力用也。

"Such a power" is evil's own capability. It is like an object not yet actually put to use: pointing to its intended purpose, one still speaks of it as having a "use." The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "In building a house, one takes wood, not thread; in weaving cloth, one takes thread, not clay or wood." In hell, there is the capability of climbing blades and treading swords; among hungry ghosts, the capability of swallowing molten copper and devouring iron; among animals, the strong subduing the weak, fish devouring one another scale by scale, being harnessed to pull carts and haul heavy loads — all of these are the capability of evil's power.

如是作者，構造經營，運動三業，建創諸惡，名之為作。《大經》第八云：「譬如世間，為惡行者，名為半人。」既行惡行，名地獄作也。

"Such an activity" is the constructing and carrying out, the setting in motion of the three karmic activities, the establishing and initiating of every evil deed — this is named "activity." The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, fascicle eight, says: "It is like, in the world, one who does evil deeds being called a 'half-person.'" Since he carries out evil deeds, this is named the activity of hell.

如是因者，惡習因也。自種相生，習續不斷，以習發故，為惡易成，故名如是因。如是緣者，緣助也。所謂諸惡我、我所，所有具度，皆能助成習業，如水能潤種，故用報因為緣也。

"Such a cause" is the habituated cause of evil. Its own seed generates its own kind, its habituation continuing without cease; because it is set off through habituation, doing evil is easily accomplished — hence it is named "such a cause." "Such a condition" is what assists. That is to say, all the various evils belonging to self and to what is self's own, everything one possesses in full measure, are all able to assist in accomplishing the habituated karma — like water able to moisten a seed. Hence the retributive cause is used as the condition.

如是果者，習果也。如多欲人受地獄身，見苦具，謂為欲境，便起染愛，謂此為習果也。如是報者，報果也。如多欲人，在地獄中，趣欲境時，即受銅柱鐵床之苦。故名如是報也。

"Such a fruit" is the habituated fruit. It is like a person of much desire who, receiving a body in hell, sees the instruments of torment there and, mistaking them for objects of desire, at once gives rise to defiled craving — this is called the habituated fruit. "Such a retribution" is the retributive fruit. It is like the person of much desire who, while in hell, in the very act of approaching what he takes for an object of desire, at once suffers the torment of the copper pillar and the iron bed. Hence it is named "such a retribution."

本末究竟等者，即有三義：本空、末亦空，故言等。又惡果報，在本相性中，此末與本等。本相性，在惡果報中，此本與末等。若先無後事，相師不應預記。若後無先事，相師不應追記。當知，初後相在，此假事論等。中實理心，與佛果不異。一色一香，無非中道，此約理論等。以是義故，故言本末究竟等，三義具足，故言等也。

"Ultimate equality from beginning to end" has, in general, three senses: the beginning is empty, and the end is likewise empty — hence it is called "equal." Again, the evil fruit-retribution is already present within the initial mark and nature — this is the end being equal to the beginning. The initial mark and nature is already present within the evil fruit-retribution — this is the beginning being equal to the end. If there were no later matter present from the start, the physiognomist ought not to be able to foretell it in advance; if there were no earlier matter still present at the end, the physiognomist ought not to be able to trace it back afterward. One should know that the mark's presence remains consistent from beginning to end — this discusses "equality" in terms of provisional phenomena. The mind of the middle, real principle is no different from the Buddha's own fruit; a single color, a single fragrance, is nothing other than the middle way — this discusses "equality" in terms of principle. For this reason, it is said "ultimate equality from beginning to end," with all three senses fully present — hence it is called "equality."

This is comparable to the first fruit of stream-entry, where, though the truth seen has already been severed, seven further births still remain — it is likewise so here. If affliction has not yet been severed or suppressed, then the practice of skillful means, with the true taintless as cause and ignorance as condition, is used. If it has been suppressed or severed, then attachment to the dharma of according with the path serves as the cause, and ignorance as the condition, giving birth within the land of change, and so on.

"A nature, based on what is internal" — that wisdom and vow remain present, never lost — this wisdom is precisely the revealing cause serving as buddha-nature. The cause is the adornment of wisdom. The condition is the adornment of merit and virtue. The fruit is the single thought-moment's correspondence, great awakening shining forth clearly, unsurpassed bodhi — this is the habituated fruit. The retribution is the fruit of great parinirvāṇa, the fruit's virtue of severance, meditative concentration and samādhi entirely complete — this is the retributive fruit. As for the equality of beginning and end: this is that the three truths of mark and nature are no different from the three truths of the ultimate — hence it is called equality. As for the equality of the truth of emptiness: from the very first suchness of sentient beings down to the buddha's own suchness, all are equal. Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

This present sūtra says: "Attaining the immeasurable, taintless, pure fruit-retribution. Long cultivating a pure life within the Dharma of the Dharma-king, only today at last attaining its fruit-retribution." And again: "What is attained through long-cultivated karma." The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "May the food I now offer bring me the unsurpassed retribution." The Sūtra for Humane Kings says: "The three worthy stages and the ten sagely stages abide in fruit-retribution." The Compendium of the Mahāyāna says: "Birth-and-death through causes and conditions, and birth-and-death that has a further birth to follow." All of these discuss fruit-retribution in its various portions, and fruit-retribution is itself arising-and-ceasing. Why is this? When a portion of ignorance is exhausted, this is spoken of as "ceasing"; when true clarity grows increasingly full, this is spoken of as "arising." Again, so long as residual ignorance remains, this is spoken of as "arising"; when a portion of affliction is removed, this is spoken of as "ceasing." The Śāstra says: "One person is able to weed, and one person is able to plant." The ten thousand practices, nourishing and completing, are like the planting; wisdom, breaking through delusion, is like the weeding. The meaning of "increasing the path, diminishing rebirth" lies precisely here. All forty-one stages of the bodhisattva path each possess these ten dharmas.

若就妙覺，亦九亦十。何者？中道智慧，乃是損生，生既未盡，故有諸地生滅不同。妙覺損生義足，最後那得論報？故云：「唯佛一人居淨土，三十生盡等大覺。」無後有生死，煩惱盡故。智德已圓，無復習果，不受後身，故無報果。又約現生後，論九論十(云云)。若按《涅槃經》文，願得無上報者，即明佛界報無上也。佛報既言無上，佛相性等九法悉皆無上。何者？六道相性，全表五住。二乘相性，表破四住，全表無明。菩薩相性，表次第破五住。佛相性，表一切種智淨若虛空，不為五住所染故。佛十法，最為無上(云云)。

As for the subtle awakening of full buddhahood itself, it is likewise both nine and ten. Why is this? The wisdom of the middle way itself diminishes rebirth, and since rebirth is not yet exhausted along the path leading to it, there are, at each of the various stages, different degrees of arising-and-ceasing. But at subtle awakening, the diminishing of rebirth is already complete — how, then, at this final stage, could retribution still be discussed? Hence it is said: "Only the Buddha, this one person, dwells in the pure land, his thirty births exhausted, equal to great awakening." There is no further birth-and-death to come, since affliction is exhausted. The virtue of wisdom is already complete, with no further habituated fruit, and he undergoes no further body — hence there is no retributive fruit. Again, in terms of present birth and later birth, it may be discussed as either nine or ten, and so on. According to the text of the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, "may I attain the unsurpassed retribution" clarifies precisely that the buddha-realm's own retribution is unsurpassed. Since the Buddha's retribution is thus called unsurpassed, the Buddha's mark, nature, and the remaining nine dharmas are, every one of them, likewise unsurpassed. Why is this? The mark and nature of the six destinies entirely signify the five abodes of affliction. The mark and nature of the two vehicles signify the breaking through of four of the five abodes, while still entirely signifying ignorance. The mark and nature of bodhisattvas signify the sequential breaking through of all five abodes. The mark and nature of the Buddha signify the wisdom knowing all modes, pure as empty space, unstained by any of the five abodes. Hence the Buddha's ten dharmas are, of all, the most unsurpassed, and so on.

復次，六趣相表生死苦。二乘相表涅槃樂。佛界相表非生死、非涅槃，中道常樂我淨，故言佛界最是無上。復次，四道表惡，人天表善，二乘表無漏善，菩薩佛表非漏非無漏善，故佛界最為無上。復次，六道表諸有因緣生法，二乘表即空，菩薩表即假，佛表即空即假即中，故佛界最為無上。復次，四趣但表惡，不能表善。人天相但表善，亦不能表惡。二乘但表無漏，不兼善惡。佛相兼表一切相。若解佛相，即遍解一切相，是故佛界最為無上。故《賢聖集》云：「地獄中陰，但見地獄，不能知上趣。若天中陰，能知天及下。」其相表之，不名正遍知。佛相表正遍知也。佛智既遍知諸相，而經教應遍說之。

Moreover: the mark of the six destinies signifies the suffering of birth-and-death. The mark of the two vehicles signifies the bliss of nirvāṇa. The mark of the buddha-realm signifies what is neither birth-and-death nor nirvāṇa, the middle way's own eternity, bliss, self, and purity — hence the buddha-realm is said to be, of all, the most unsurpassed. Moreover: the four evil destinies signify evil, humans and gods signify good, the two vehicles signify taintless good, and bodhisattvas and buddhas signify good that is neither tainted nor taintless — hence the buddha-realm is, of all, the most unsurpassed. Moreover: the six destinies signify dharmas that arise from the causes and conditions of existence, the two vehicles signify what is precisely empty, bodhisattvas signify what is precisely provisional, and the Buddha signifies what is precisely empty, precisely provisional, and precisely the middle — hence the buddha-realm is, of all, the most unsurpassed. Moreover: the four evil destinies signify only evil, and cannot signify good; the marks of humans and gods signify only good, and likewise cannot signify evil; the two vehicles signify only the taintless, without encompassing good and evil together; but the Buddha's mark encompasses and signifies every mark at once. If one understands the Buddha's mark, one thereby understands every mark without exception — hence the buddha-realm is, of all, the most unsurpassed. Hence the Sagely Assembly Collection says: "A being in the intermediate state bound for hell sees only hell, and cannot know the destinies above it; but a being in the intermediate state bound for the heavens is able to know both the heavens and what lies below." To signify a mark in this way is not yet called correct and universal knowing. The Buddha's mark signifies correct and universal knowing. Since the Buddha's wisdom already universally knows every mark, the sūtra's own teaching, too, ought to universally teach them all.

Reading note

The "five abodes of affliction" (五住) are views-and-habituated-thought (counted as one), plus four grades of increasingly subtle attachment to existence in the desire, form, and formless realms, plus ignorance itself as the fifth and subtlest — a standard Tiantai scale for measuring how completely a given stage has purified affliction, already presupposed (though not spelled out) in earlier fascicle discussions of the "three delusions."

This present Lotus Sūtra clarifies all nine kinds of nature and mark alike entering precisely emptiness, precisely the provisional, and precisely the middle. "You are truly my son, and I am truly your father" — a single color, a single flavor, purely and solely the Buddha's own dharma, with no further dharma beyond it — hence one knows the buddha-realm is, of all, the most unsurpassed. Moreover: for those nine natures and marks clarified in other sūtras that are not yet able to enter the Buddha's own nature and mark of precisely emptiness, precisely the provisional, and precisely the middle, this present sūtra opens up skillful means for every one of them, universally causing them all to be able to enter. And, examined in terms of their own nature and mark, they are already precisely emptiness, precisely the provisional, and precisely the middle, without needing to be drawn in from elsewhere at all — this is precisely why the Tathāgata so earnestly and repeatedly praises this Lotus Sūtra as, of all, the most unsurpassed; the meaning lies precisely here.

復次，百界千法，縱橫甚多，以經論偈結之，令其易解。《中論》偈云：「因緣所生法，我說即是空，亦名為假名，亦名中道義。」六道相性，即是因緣所生法也。二乘及通教菩薩等相性，是我說即是空。六度別教菩薩相性，是亦名為假名。佛界相性，是亦名中道義。結要雖少，攝得前多，義則可見(云云)。

The verse of the Middle Treatise says: "A dharma arisen through causes and conditions — I say that this is itself empty; it is also called a provisional name; it is also called the meaning of the middle way." The mark and nature of the six destinies is precisely "a dharma arisen through causes and conditions." The mark and nature of the two vehicles and the bodhisattvas of the shared teaching is "I say that this is itself empty." The mark and nature of the six-pāramitā and distinct-teaching bodhisattvas is "it is also called a provisional name." The mark and nature of the buddha-realm is "it is also called the meaning of the middle way." Though the summarizing verse is short, it gathers in all the many senses discussed above — its meaning may thereby be seen, and so on.

又涅槃偈云：「諸行無常，是生滅法；生滅滅已，寂滅為樂。」六道相性，即是諸行。二乘通教相性，即是無常。別教菩薩相性，即是生滅滅已。佛界相性，即是寂滅為樂。又生滅滅已，寂滅為樂，即是別教相性。即於生滅，仍是寂滅，不待滅已，方稱為樂，是為圓教佛界相性(云云)。

Again, the Nirvāṇa verse says: "All conditioned things are impermanent — this is the dharma of arising and ceasing. When arising and ceasing have themselves ceased, this quiescent extinction is bliss." The mark and nature of the six destinies is precisely "all conditioned things." The mark and nature of the two vehicles and the shared teaching is precisely "impermanent." The mark and nature of the distinct-teaching bodhisattva is precisely "when arising and ceasing have themselves ceased." The mark and nature of the buddha-realm is precisely "this quiescent extinction is bliss." And again: "when arising and ceasing have themselves ceased, this quiescent extinction is bliss" is itself only the distinct teaching's own mark and nature — for it still waits for cessation to occur before bliss can be spoken of. But to find, within arising-and-ceasing itself, that this is already quiescent extinction, without waiting for a separate cessation before calling it bliss — this is the perfect teaching's own mark and nature of the buddha-realm, and so on.

又七佛通戒偈云：「諸惡莫作，眾善奉行，自淨其意，是諸佛教。」四趣相性，即是諸惡。人天相性，即是眾善。自淨其意，即有析體淨意，是二乘相性。入假淨意，是菩薩相性。入中淨意，是佛界相性(云云)。若能解十相性，與眾經論律合者，即通達三藏、通、別，識一切法，無有障礙。廣明眾生法相竟。

Again, the verse of the precept common to the seven buddhas says: "Commit no evil whatsoever; uphold and practice every good; purify one's own mind entirely — this is the teaching of all buddhas." The mark and nature of the four evil destinies is precisely "evil." The mark and nature of humans and gods is precisely "every good." "Purify one's own mind" itself contains both the analytical and the embodying purification of mind, which is the mark and nature of the two vehicles. Purification that enters the provisional is the mark and nature of bodhisattvas. Purification that enters the middle is the mark and nature of the buddha-realm, and so on. If one is able to understand these ten marks and natures, and how they accord with the various sūtras, treatises, and vinaya texts, one thereby penetrates through the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct teachings, knowing all dharmas without any obstruction. This closes the broad clarification of the dharma-marks of sentient beings.

The Dharma of the Buddha, and the Dharma of Mind 佛法・心法

Second, expounding the dharma of the Buddha at length: does the Buddha, then, have some separate dharma of his own? It is simply that the hundred realms and thousand suchnesses are themselves the Buddha's own state — only buddha together with buddha fully fathoms this principle. As the box is large, so its lid, too, follows in largeness. Illuminating the vast, boundless buddha-state with boundless buddha-wisdom, reaching to its very source and depth, this is named the dharma that follows one's own intention. If it illuminates the nature, mark, beginning, and end of the nine other dharma-realms, leaving not so much as a mustard-seed's worth unaccounted for, this is named the dharma that follows others' intentions. From these two dharmas as their root, the traces of the ten dharma-realms are let down. At times he shows his own body, at times he shows another's body; at times he speaks his own intended words, at times he speaks words intended for others — his own intention and others' intention are inconceivable, his own body and another's body subtle and utterly quiescent, neither provisional nor real, and yet able to respond with the provisional of the nine realms and the real of the one realm, all without any diminishment whatsoever to the Buddha's own dharma. How could the dharma of all buddhas fail to be wondrous? This matter can be understood readily enough, and needs no lengthy exposition here — it shall be clarified again, further, in the "Skillful Means" chapter.

三、廣釋心法者，前所明法，豈得異心？但眾生法太廣，佛法太高，於初學為難。然心、佛及眾生是三無差別者，但自觀己心則為易。《涅槃》云：「一切眾生具足三定。上定者，謂佛性也。」能觀心性，名為上定。上能兼下，即攝得眾生法也。《華嚴》云：「遊心法界如虛空，則知諸佛之境界。」法界即中也，虛空即空也，心佛即假也。三種具即佛境界也。是為觀心，仍具佛法。

Third, explaining the dharma of mind at length: how could the dharma already clarified above be any different from mind itself? It is only that the dharma of sentient beings is exceedingly vast, and the dharma of the Buddha exceedingly lofty, and so both are difficult for a beginning student. Yet since mind, Buddha, and sentient beings — these three are without distinction, it is far easier simply to contemplate one's own mind directly. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "All sentient beings possess the three concentrations in full. The supreme concentration is buddha-nature itself." To be able to contemplate the nature of mind is named the supreme concentration. What is supreme is able to encompass what is lesser, and so it already gathers in the dharma of sentient beings as well. The Avataṃsaka says: "Let the mind roam through the dharma-realm as through empty space, and one thereby knows the state of all buddhas." The dharma-realm is precisely the middle; empty space is precisely emptiness; mind and Buddha are precisely the provisional. All three complete together is precisely the buddha-state. This is the contemplation of mind, which already contains the dharma of the Buddha within it.

又遊心法界者，觀根、塵相對，一念心起於十界中必屬一界；若屬一界，即具百界千法。於一念中，悉皆備足。此心幻師，於一日夜，常造種種眾生、種種五陰、種種國土，所謂地獄假實國土，乃至佛界假實國土。行人當自選擇何道可從。又如虛空者，觀心自生，心不須藉緣，藉緣有心，心無生力。心無生力，緣亦無生。心、緣各無，合云何有？合尚叵得，離則不生。尚無一生，況有百界千法耶？以心空故，從心所生一切皆空，此空亦空；若空非空，點空設假，假亦非假；無假無空，畢竟清淨。又復佛境界者，上等佛法，下等眾生法。又心法者，心、佛及眾生是三無差別，是名心法也。

Again, as for letting the mind roam through the dharma-realm: contemplating the sense-faculty and its object confronting one another, a single thought-moment arising must belong to one of the ten realms; and if it belongs to that one realm, it already possesses the hundred realms and thousand dharmas within it, fully provided for, complete, within that single thought-moment. This illusion-conjuring mind, within a single day and night, constantly creates various sentient beings, various sets of the five aggregates, various lands — that is, the provisional and real lands of hell, all the way up to the provisional and real lands of the buddha-realm. It is for the practitioner himself to choose which path he will follow. Again, as for "like empty space": contemplating that mind arises of itself, mind does not actually need to depend on conditions; if it depended on conditions to have mind at all, mind would have no power of its own to generate anything. If mind has no power to generate, then conditions, too, have no power to generate. Since mind and condition are each without generative power, how could their combination possess it? Their combination, then, is itself unattainable; and apart from one another, neither can generate anything either. If not even a single thing can be generated, how much less the hundred realms and thousand dharmas! Because mind is empty, everything generated from mind is likewise entirely empty — and this very emptiness, too, is empty. If emptiness is not merely emptiness, then, pointing to emptiness, the provisional is set forth — yet this provisional, too, is not truly provisional; with neither the provisional nor emptiness fixed as such, this is ultimate purity. And, moreover, as for the "state of the Buddha": above, it is equal to the dharma of the Buddha; below, it is equal to the dharma of sentient beings. And as for the dharma of mind: that mind, Buddha, and sentient beings — these three are without distinction — this is what is named the dharma of mind.

Second, clarifying "wondrous": first, the general explanation; second, the specific explanation. The general explanation, too, has two parts: first, the relative; second, the absolute. This sūtra discusses only these two wondrouses, and has no further passage speaking of anything neither absolute nor relative. Were one to construct such a thing, what confusion would it dispel? What principle would it reveal? Hence it is not discussed any further. Guangzhai uses the wondrousness of the Lotus, relative to which every earlier teaching is coarse — this has already been shown to be greatly problematic, as objected to above, and so on. Here, the coarse and wondrous discussed relatively are: relative to the half-word, the full-word is clarified as wondrous — likewise the eternal and the impermanent, the great and the small, discussed relatively as coarse and wondrous. The Vimalakīrti says: "Teaching the Dharma neither as existent nor as nonexistent, since it is through causes and conditions that all dharmas arise" — this itself clarifies the full-word. From his first sitting beneath the bodhi tree, subduing Māra by his own power, attaining the extinction of sweet dew and accomplishing the path of awakening — this itself raises up the past's half-word relative to which the full-word emerges. The Prajñāpāramitā says: "In Jambudvīpa, a second turning of the Dharma-wheel is seen" — this, too, treats the Deer Park as the first, relative to which the Prajñā is the second. Question: If, from the Vaipulya period onward, the principle of fullness is no different, should not all of these alike be called wondrous? Answer: Here, too, I do not fix rigidly on any single teaching or determinate time — how, then, could one suddenly say "from the Vaipulya period onward"? Even granting that this were so, there is a further reason to distinguish them. Why is this? For a sharp-faculty bodhisattva, entering the wondrous at that point is no different from entering it at the Lotus. But dull-faculty bodhisattvas and the two vehicles are still accompanied by skillful means, tempered by the various flavors along the way. The Vaipulya period discusses the wondrous, relative to the coarse, while still accompanied by the fresh-curdled-butter teaching; the Prajñā period discusses the wondrous, relative to the coarse, while still accompanied by the ripened-butter teaching; the present sūtra alone discusses the wondrous, relative to the coarse, with no accompaniment of either of these two flavors' skillful means at all, being purely and genuinely the ghee itself. This wondrous and that wondrous do not differ in the sense of "wondrous" itself — the only difference lies in whether it is accompanied by skillful means or not.

復次，三藏但半字生滅門，不能通滿理，故名為麁。滿字是不生不滅門，能通滿理，故名妙。能通滿理，復有二種：一、帶方便通滿理，二、直顯滿理。方等、般若帶方便通滿理，今經直顯滿理。故《中論》云：「為鈍根弟子說因緣生滅相；為利根弟子說因緣不生不滅相(云云)。」《中論》偈(云云)。若不即空為通真方便，是故言麁；若能即空是通中方便。通中方便，若帶即空即假通中者麁；不帶空假直通中者妙(云云)。

Moreover, the Tripiṭaka teaching is only the half-word gate of arising-and-ceasing, unable to penetrate through to the principle of fullness — hence it is named coarse. The full-word is the gate of neither arising nor ceasing, able to penetrate through to the principle of fullness — hence it is named wondrous. This ability to penetrate through to the principle of fullness, moreover, has two kinds: first, penetrating through to the principle of fullness while accompanied by skillful means; second, directly revealing the principle of fullness. The Vaipulya and Prajñā periods penetrate through to the principle of fullness while accompanied by skillful means; the present sūtra directly reveals the principle of fullness. Hence the Middle Treatise says: "For disciples of dull faculties, the mark of arising-and-ceasing through causes and conditions is taught; for disciples of sharp faculties, the mark of neither arising nor ceasing through causes and conditions is taught," and so on. If what does not directly reveal emptiness serves as a skillful means for penetrating through to the real, it is therefore called coarse; if it is able to directly reveal emptiness, this is a skillful means for penetrating through to the middle. As for the skillful means for penetrating through to the middle: if it is accompanied by directly-revealed emptiness and the provisional in penetrating through to the middle, this is coarse; if, without any accompaniment of emptiness or the provisional, it directly penetrates through to the middle, this is wondrous, and so on.

問：乳至醍醐，同稱為滿，是譬云何？答：今以譬解。譬如官有三航及以私船，從於此岸，度人彼岸。乳教如大中兩航，共度人彼岸。酪教如私船，度人中洲。生蘇如四種：小航與私船，度人於中洲：兩航度人於彼岸。熟蘇如三航：一航中洲，二航彼岸。醍醐如大航，度人彼岸。三航同是官物，故俱稱為滿。私船非官物，是故言半。官物之中，二航小，所容蓋寡；大航壯麗，容載倍多，獨稱為妙。智者以譬喻得解，其譬義如是(云云)。

Question: From the milk teaching to the ghee, all alike are called "full" — what is the simile for this? Answer: Let me now explain this through a simile. It is like the government possessing three ferries, along with private boats as well, carrying people across from this shore to the far shore. The milk teaching is like two of the great and middle ferries together carrying people across to the far shore. The cream teaching is like a private boat, carrying people only to the mid-river islet. The fresh-curdled-butter teaching is like four kinds combined: the small ferry and the private boat carry people to the mid-river islet, while the two larger ferries carry people across to the far shore. The ripened-butter teaching is like three ferries: one ferry to the islet, two ferries to the far shore. The ghee is like the great ferry alone, carrying people across to the far shore. Since all three ferries are alike government property, they are all together called "full"; the private boat is not government property, and so it is called "half." Among the government's own property, two of the ferries are small, and hold relatively little; the great ferry is grand and magnificent, holding double the capacity, and is alone called "wondrous." The wise grasp understanding through such similes — the sense of this simile is as given here, and so on.

Reading note

This is the first of the treatise's two general readings of "wondrous": relative wondrousness (相待妙), where each period's teaching is called wondrous only in relation to what it surpasses — a status the Lotus shares in kind, if not in degree of directness, with the Vaipulya and Prajñā periods' own partial revelations of the same "principle of fullness." The absolute reading (絕待妙) follows next, where wondrousness no longer depends on any such comparison at all.

Clarifying "Wondrous": The Absolute Reading 絕待明妙

Chinese Source

二、絕待明妙者，為四：一、隨情三假法起，若入真諦，待對即絕。故身子云：「吾聞解脫之中，無有言說。」此三藏經中絕待意也。二、若隨理三假，一切世間皆如幻化，即事而真，無有一事而非真者，更待何物為不真耶？望彼三藏，絕還不絕。即事而真，乃是絕待，此通教絕待也。三、別教若起，望即真之絕，還是世諦。何者？非大涅槃，猶是生死世諦，絕還有待。若入別教中道，待則絕矣！四、圓教若起，說無分別法，即邊而中，無非佛法，亡泯清淨。豈更佛法待於佛法？如來法界故，出法界外，無復有法可相形比。待誰為麁？形誰得妙？無所可待，亦無所絕，不知何名，強言為絕。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying "wondrous" through the absolute reading: this has four parts. First, when the threefold provisional following worldly convention arises, once one enters the truth of the real, all relative contrasts are themselves cut off. Hence Śāriputra says: "I have heard that, within liberation, there is nothing that can be spoken." This is the sense of the absolute within the Tripiṭaka teaching's own sūtras. Second, if the threefold provisional following principle arises, then all the world is like illusion and transformation, and, precisely in phenomena, it is the real — there is not a single phenomenon that is not the real; what, then, is there left to be relatively contrasted against as "not real"? Measured against the Tripiṭaka teaching, this absolute is, after all, not truly absolute. Yet that, precisely in phenomena, it is the real — this is itself the absolute; this is the shared teaching's own absolute. Third, when the distinct teaching arises, measured against this very "absolute of phenomena being the real," it is, after all, still only conventional truth. Why is this? Because it is not yet the great nirvāṇa itself, but still the conventional truth of birth-and-death — this "absolute," too, still has something relative about it. Only upon entering the distinct teaching's own middle way is the relative truly cut off! Fourth, when the perfect teaching arises, teaching a dharma beyond all differentiation, precisely in the extreme is the middle, with nothing that is not the Buddha's own dharma, utterly dissolved and pure. How, then, could the Buddha's own dharma still be relatively contrasted against the Buddha's own dharma? Since it is the Tathāgata's own dharma-realm, beyond the dharma-realm there is no further dharma that could be compared or contrasted against it at all. Relative to what could it be called coarse? Contrasted against what could it attain "wondrous"? There is nothing it can be relative to, and likewise nothing it can be cut off from — not knowing what to call this, one is forced to speak of it as "absolute."

《大經》云：「大名不可稱量，不可思議，故名為大。譬如虛空，不因小空，名為大也。涅槃亦爾，不因小相，名大涅槃。」妙亦如是，妙名不可思議，不因於麁而名為妙。若謂定有法界廣大獨絕者，此則大有所有，何謂為絕？今法界清淨，非見聞覺知，不可說示。文云：「止止不須說，我法妙難思。」止止不須說，即是絕言；我法妙難思，即是絕思。又云：「是法不可示，言辭相寂滅。」亦是絕歎之文。不可以待示，不可以絕示，滅待滅絕，故言寂滅。又云：「一切諸法，常寂滅相，終歸於空。」此空亦空，則無復待絕。《中論》云：「若法為待成，是法還成待。」今則無因待，亦無所成。《法華》首云：「既得無生忍，亦不生無生。」無生即無生，是名絕待。

The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The name 'great' is beyond measure and reckoning, inconceivable — hence it is named 'great.' It is like empty space, which is not named 'great' by contrast with some lesser space; nirvāṇa, too, is likewise so, not named 'great nirvāṇa' by contrast with some lesser mark." The wondrous, too, is likewise so: the name "wondrous" is itself inconceivable, not named "wondrous" by contrast with the coarse. If one supposes there is definitely some vast dharma-realm, uniquely absolute, existing as such, then this is "having something as a possession" — how could this be called absolute? The dharma-realm as it truly is, is pure, beyond seeing, hearing, sensing, and knowing, and cannot be shown or spoken of. The text says: "Stop, stop — there is no need to speak; my dharma is wondrous and hard to conceive." "Stop, stop — there is no need to speak" is itself the cutting off of speech; "my dharma is wondrous and hard to conceive" is itself the cutting off of thought. And again: "This dharma cannot be shown; the marks of speech are themselves quiescent and extinct" — this, too, is a passage praising it by cutting it off. It cannot be shown through relative contrast, and cannot be shown through absolute negation either — extinguishing both the relative and the absolute alike, hence it is called "quiescent extinction." And again: "All dharmas, ever bearing the mark of quiescent extinction, in the end return to emptiness" — and this emptiness, too, is itself empty, and so there is no longer any relative or absolute left at all. The Middle Treatise says: "If a dharma is established through relative contrast, that dharma remains, after all, something merely relatively established." Here, then, there is no cause to be relative to, and likewise nothing established at all. The opening of the Lotus says: "Having already attained the forbearance of non-arising, he no longer even gives rise to 'non-arising' itself." Non-arising is itself without arising — this is named the absolute.

降此已外，若更作者，絕何物？顯何理？流浪無窮，則墮戲論。乃是迷情分別，絕待不絕，非絕非待待於亦待亦絕，言語相逐，永無絕矣！何者？言語從覺觀生，心慮不息，語何由絕？如癡犬逐塊，徒自疲勞，塊終不絕。若能妙悟寰中，息覺觀風，心水澄清，言思皆絕。如黠師子放塊逐人，塊本既除，塊則絕矣！妙悟之時，洞知法界外無法而論絕者，約有門明絕也。是絕亦絕，約空門明絕也。如快馬見鞭影，無不得入，是名絕待妙也。用是兩妙，妙上三法。眾生之法，亦具二妙，稱之為妙。佛法、心法，亦具二妙，稱之為妙。

Beyond this point, were one to construct anything further, what would it cut off? What principle would it reveal? To wander on without end in this way is to fall into idle speculation. This is nothing but the differentiation of a deluded mind, where the absolute fails to be truly absolute, and "neither absolute nor relative" becomes relative, in turn, to "both relative and absolute" — words chasing after words, with never an end to it! Why is this? Because words arise from discursive and reflective thought, and so long as the mind's deliberation does not cease, how could words ever be cut off? It is like a foolish dog chasing after a clod of earth thrown at it — merely wearing itself out in vain, while the clod itself never comes to an end. But if one is able to awaken to the wondrous at the very center of the matter, stilling the wind of discursive and reflective thought, the water of the mind becomes clear and still, and words and thought are both cut off at once. It is like a clever lion, who, when a clod is thrown, chases after the person who threw it rather than the clod — the very root of the clod is thereby removed, and the clod itself comes to an end! At the moment of wondrous awakening, seeing through completely that there is no dharma outside the dharma-realm, and discussing the absolute in these terms — this clarifies the absolute in terms of the gate of existence. That this very absolute, too, is itself cut off — this clarifies the absolute in terms of the gate of emptiness. Like a swift horse who enters the right path at the very sight of the shadow of the whip, entering without fail — this is named the wondrous of the absolute reading. Using these two wondrouses, the three dharmas above are made wondrous: the dharma of sentient beings likewise possesses both wondrouses, and is thereby called wondrous; the dharma of the Buddha and the dharma of mind likewise possess both wondrouses, and are thereby called wondrous.

若將上四種絕待約五味經者，乳教兩絕，酪教一絕，生蘇四絕，熟蘇三絕，此經但有一絕。若開權絕者，無不入一妙絕也。

If these four kinds of the absolute discussed above are applied across the Five-Flavor sūtras: the milk teaching has two absolutes, the cream teaching has one absolute, the fresh-curdled-butter teaching has four absolutes, the ripened-butter teaching has three absolutes, and the present sūtra has only the one absolute. If the provisional is opened and cut off, then there is nothing that fails to enter this single, wondrous absolute.

問：何意以絕釋妙？答：秖喚妙為絕，絕是妙之異名，如世人稱絕能耳。又妙是能絕，麁是所絕，此妙有絕麁之功，故舉絕以名妙。如迹中先施方便之教，大教不得起。今大教若起，方便教絕，將所絕以名於妙耳。又迹中大教既起，本地大教不得興。今本地教興，迹中大教即絕。絕於迹大，功由本大，將絕迹之大，名於本大，故言絕也。又本大教若興，觀心之妙不得起。今入觀緣寂，言語道斷，本教即絕。絕由於觀，將此絕名，名於觀妙，為顯此義，故以絕為妙。

Question: Why is "wondrous" explained through "absolute"? Answer: This is simply calling the wondrous "absolute" — "absolute" is another name for "wondrous," just as ordinary people speak of "unsurpassed skill." Again, the wondrous is what does the cutting off, and the coarse is what is cut off; since this wondrous has the function of cutting off the coarse, "cutting off" is used to name the wondrous. It is like how, within the trace gate, the teaching of skillful means is first bestowed, before the great teaching can arise at all; now, once the great teaching arises, the skillful-means teaching is cut off — and it is this very cutting-off that is used to name the wondrous. Again, once the trace gate's great teaching has arisen, the origin-ground's own great teaching cannot yet flourish; now, once the origin-ground's teaching flourishes, the trace gate's great teaching is at once cut off. That the trace's greatness is cut off is due to the merit of the origin's greatness — and it is this cutting-off of the trace's greatness that is used to name the origin's greatness, hence it is spoken of as "absolute." Again, once the origin's own great teaching has flourished, the wondrousness of the contemplation of mind cannot yet arise; now, entering contemplation, its conditioned objects fall quiescent, and the path of words is cut off — the origin's own teaching is at once cut off. This cutting-off comes about through contemplation, and it is this very name of "cutting-off" that is used to name the wondrousness of contemplation — and it is to make this very sense clear that "absolute" is used to name "wondrous."

今將迹之絕妙，妙上眾生法；將本地之絕妙，妙上佛法；將觀心之絕妙，妙上心法。前四絕橫約四教；今三絕竪約圓教(云云)。

Now, using the trace's own absolute wondrous, the dharma of sentient beings is made wondrous; using the origin-ground's own absolute wondrous, the dharma of the Buddha is made wondrous; using the contemplation of mind's own absolute wondrous, the dharma of mind is made wondrous. The four absolutes discussed earlier are arranged horizontally, in terms of the Four Teachings; these three absolutes are arranged vertically, in terms of the perfect teaching alone, and so on.

Reading note

This closes "clarifying wondrous in general" (通釋): the relative reading (each period's own wondrous defined against a coarser predecessor) and the absolute reading (wondrous as beyond all relative contrast whatsoever, worked through the Tripiṭaka/shared/distinct/perfect teachings in turn, then re-mapped vertically onto trace/origin/mind-contemplation within the perfect teaching alone). The lion-and-clod simile — a dog chases the thrown object, a lion turns on the thrower — is a standard Buddhist image for the difference between treating symptoms and addressing a root cause; here it distinguishes idle regress from genuine awakening. The specific reading of "wondrous," applied separately to each of the ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate and the origin gate, follows next.

The Framework of Sixty (and One Hundred Twenty) Wondrous Aspects 別釋妙

Chinese Source

△別釋妙者，為三：若鹿苑三麁，鷲頭一妙，皆迹中之說，約迹開十重論妙。此妙有迹、有本，本據元初，元初本妙十重論妙。迹本俱是教，依教作觀，觀復有十重論妙。迹中有眾生法妙、佛法妙、心法妙，各十重，合三十重。此與眾經論妙有同有異。本中三十妙，與眾經一向異。此六十重一一復有待妙、絕妙，則有一百二十重。若破麁顯妙，即用上相待妙。若開麁顯妙，即用上絕待妙(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the specific interpretation of "wondrous": this has three parts. As for the Deer Park's three coarse teachings and the vulture-peak assembly's one wondrous teaching — these are both spoken of within the trace gate; in terms of the trace, ten aspects are opened up to discuss "wondrous." This wondrous has both a trace and an origin: the origin is based on the original beginning — the original, primordial wondrous, likewise opened into ten aspects to discuss "wondrous." The trace and the origin are both teachings; in dependence on the teaching, contemplation is practiced, and contemplation, too, has its own ten aspects to discuss "wondrous." Within the trace gate there is the wondrous of the dharma of sentient beings, the wondrous of the dharma of the Buddha, and the wondrous of the dharma of mind, each with its own ten aspects, thirty aspects in all. These have both agreements and differences with how "wondrous" is discussed in other sūtras and treatises. The thirty wondrous aspects within the origin gate, however, differ from every other sūtra without exception. And each of these sixty aspects, in turn, has both its relative wondrous and its absolute wondrous, giving one hundred twenty aspects in all. If breaking through the coarse to reveal the wondrous, the relative wondrous discussed above is used; if opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous, the absolute wondrous discussed above is used, and so on.

Reading note

This is the treatise's own map of its remaining scope: ten wondrous aspects for the trace gate, applied to each of the three dharmas (sentient beings, Buddha, mind) for thirty; ten more wondrous aspects for the origin gate, likewise applied three ways for another thirty; sixty in total, each capable of being read both relatively and absolutely, for a notional one hundred twenty. In practice, the treatise develops the ten trace-gate aspects at the greatest length (occupying most of Fascicles 2 through 6), then the ten origin-gate aspects more briefly (Fascicle 7), with the threefold sentient-beings/Buddha/mind lens applied more as an interpretive reminder than as thirty fully separate expositions.

Marking the Ten Wondrous Aspects of the Trace Gate 迹中十妙・標章

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate are: first, the wondrous of the object of contemplation; second, the wondrous of wisdom; third, the wondrous of practice; fourth, the wondrous of stage; fifth, the wondrous of the three tracks; sixth, the wondrous of sympathetic response; seventh, the wondrous of spiritual powers; eighth, the wondrous of teaching the Dharma; ninth, the wondrous of the retinue; tenth, the wondrous of merit and benefit. Explaining the ten wondrous aspects is done in five rounds: first, marking each aspect; second, citing proof-texts; third, showing their arising in sequence; fourth, the broad interpretation; fifth, concluding with the provisional and the real. As for marking each aspect: what is the wondrous of the object of contemplation? It refers to the ten suchnesses, dependent origination, the Four Noble Truths, the three truths, the two truths, the one truth, and the like. These are what all buddhas take as their own teacher — hence it is called the wondrous of the object of contemplation.

智妙者，所謂二十智，四菩提智，下、中、上、上上，七權實，五三智，一如實智，以境妙故，智亦隨妙。以法常故，諸佛亦常。函蓋相稱，境智不可思議，故稱智妙。行妙者，謂增數行、次第五行、不次第五行，智導行故，故言行妙。位妙者，謂三草位、二木位、一實位，妙行所契，故言位妙。

The wondrous of wisdom: that is to say, the twenty kinds of wisdom, the four kinds of bodhi-wisdom, the lower, middling, upper, and superior-upper, the sevenfold provisional-and-real, the fivefold threefold wisdom, and the single true-and-real wisdom. Since the object is wondrous, wisdom, too, follows it in being wondrous. Since the dharma is eternal, all buddhas, too, are eternal. Box and lid fitting each other exactly, object and wisdom are inconceivable — hence it is called the wondrous of wisdom. The wondrous of practice: that is to say, the practices classified by increasing numbers, the five sequential practices, and the five non-sequential practices. Since wisdom guides practice, it is called the wondrous of practice. The wondrous of stage: that is to say, the stages of the three kinds of herbs, the two kinds of trees, and the one real stage. Since the wondrous practice arrives at and accords with these, it is called the wondrous of stage.

三法妙者，謂總三法，縱三法、橫三法、不縱不橫三法、類通三法，皆祕密藏，故稱為妙。感應妙者，謂四句感應、三十六句感應、二十五感應、別圓感應。水不上升、月不下降，一月一時普現眾水；諸佛不來、眾生不往，慈善根力見如此事，故名感應妙。

The wondrous of the three tracks: that is to say, the three tracks taken as a whole, the three tracks arranged vertically, the three tracks arranged horizontally, the three tracks neither vertical nor horizontal, and the three tracks extended by analogy to other triads — all of these being the secret treasury, hence it is called wondrous. The wondrous of sympathetic response: that is to say, the fourfold sympathetic response, the thirty-six-fold sympathetic response, the twenty-five-fold sympathetic response, and the distinct and perfect teachings' own sympathetic response. The water does not rise upward, and the moon does not descend downward, and yet a single moon, in a single moment, appears fully within every water; the buddhas do not come, and sentient beings do not go, and yet, through the power of their compassionate good roots, this very event is seen to occur — hence it is named the wondrous of sympathetic response.

神通妙者，謂報通、修通、作意通、體法通、無記化化通，無謀之權，稱緣轉變，若遠、若近、若種、若熟、若脫，皆為一乘，故言神通妙。說法妙者，謂說十二部法、小部法、大部法、逗緣法、所詮法、圓妙法，如理圓說，咸令眾生開、示、悟、入佛之知見，故言說法妙。

The wondrous of spiritual powers: that is to say, the spiritual power belonging to karmic retribution, the spiritual power gained through cultivation, the spiritual power of deliberate intention, the spiritual power of embodying dharmas, and the neutral, self-transforming spiritual power — a skillful means without deliberation, transforming and changing in accordance with conditions, whether for those distant or near, whether planting the seed, ripening it, or bringing about liberation, all of it for the sake of the one vehicle — hence it is called the wondrous of spiritual powers. The wondrous of teaching the Dharma: that is to say, teaching the twelve divisions of scripture, the teachings belonging to the lesser divisions, the teachings belonging to the greater divisions, the teachings matched to conditions, the dharma expounded, and the perfect, wondrous dharma — taught perfectly, in accordance with principle, causing sentient beings, every one, to open, show, awaken to, and enter the Buddha's own knowledge and vision — hence it is called the wondrous of teaching the Dharma.

The two "conceivable" kinds of dependent origination are discussed for the sake of both sharp and dull conditions, as a discussion of dharmas within the three realms. The Middle Treatise says: "For disciples of dull faculties, the arising-and-ceasing mark of the twelve links of dependent origination is taught." This distinguishes it from the non-Buddhist paths, which wrongly hold that all dharmas are born from Maheśvara, or speak of a primal nature, or of atoms, or of parents, or of no cause at all — various false speculations, none according with the true principle. This correct dependent origination is unlike such false calculations: it is solely that, within the deluded, inverted mind of past ignorance, various volitional formations are constructed, able to bring forth the suffering-fruit of the six destinies in the present life, differing in their goodness or badness. The Sūtra of the Right Mindfulness of Dharma says: "A painter, laying out the five colors, depicts every kind of form — upright and handsome, or ugly and deformed, beyond all reckoning." Tracing this back to its very root, it issues from the painter's own hand; the distinctions among the six destinies are not made by Maheśvara or any such being, but issue entirely from the single thought-moment of the ignorant mind. When ignorance combines with upper-grade evil karmic action, the causes and conditions of hell at once arise — like the painter producing the color black. When ignorance combines with middle-grade evil action, the causes and conditions of the animal destiny arise — like producing red. When ignorance combines with lower-grade evil action, the causes and conditions of the hungry-ghost destiny arise — like producing blue-green. When ignorance combines with lower-grade good action, the causes and conditions of the asura destiny arise — like producing yellow. When ignorance combines with middle-grade good action, the causes and conditions of the human destiny arise — like producing white. When ignorance combines with upper-grade good action, the causes and conditions of the heavenly destiny arise — like producing the purest, most brilliant white. One should know that it is because ignorance combines with the various volitional formations that the six destinies come to be — name-and-form, the six sense-fields, contact, sensation, craving, grasping, becoming, birth, old age, sickness, and death, and the rest, each differing according to upper, middle, or lower grade. Among the various destinies of humans and gods, suffering and pleasure take ten thousand forms; from birth one returns to death, and from death one is born once again — cycling through the three times, like a cart-wheel spinning through fire. Hence the sūtra says: "There is a river whose eddies drown sentient beings, blinded by ignorance, unable to escape." The sūtra also calls this the twelve links binding and constraining one another in turn, also named the twelve-layered city, also named the twelve-thorned garden. These twelve links, ever newly arising and ceasing, never abiding from thought-moment to thought-moment — hence they are named the arising-and-ceasing twelve links of dependent origination.

Reading note

Zhiyi's fourfold reading of the twelve links (conceivable-arising-ceasing / conceivable-neither / inconceivable-arising-ceasing / inconceivable-neither) maps directly onto the Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, and perfect teachings respectively — the same fourfold pattern already seen for the Four Noble Truths and will recur below in this same section. The painter simile for how a single ignorant mind produces all six destinies through differing grades of karma is original to the Sūtra of the Right Mindfulness of Dharma (正法念處經) and recurs elsewhere in Tiantai literature as a standard image for karmic causation.

料簡者，《纓珞》第四云：「無明緣行，生十二，乃至生緣老死，亦生十二，是則一百二十因緣。初是癡，乃至老死亦是癡。不覺故癡，初亦不覺，至老死亦不覺；癡故生，癡故死。若能覺因緣，因緣即不行，癡不行故，則將來生死盡，名為黠，黠即隨道。」又十二緣起、十二緣生，為同為異？此同是一切有為法，故無異。亦有差別，因是緣起，果是緣生，則二緣起，五緣生，三緣起，二緣生。又無明是緣起，行是緣生，乃至生是緣起，老死是緣生。又四句：緣起非緣生，未來二支法是也。緣生非緣起，過去二支，現在阿羅漢最後死陰是也。緣起緣生者，除過去、現在羅漢死五陰，諸餘過去、現在法是也。非緣起非緣生者，無為法是也。

Among the twelve links, two belong to the past — this checks the extreme of eternalism; two belong to the future — this checks the extreme of annihilationism; and the present reveals the middle way. Reasoning from the present's three causes, the future's two fruits are spoken of; reasoning from the present's five fruits, the past's two causes are spoken of. All three time-periods, each, possess the full twelve links — it is only for the sake of reasoning between cause and fruit that they are spoken of in this differentiated way.

"Consciousness" is the time of the continuing mind and its associated factors. Times such as these are named "name-and-form." "The six sense-fields" is when the four kinds of physical faculties have already arisen, and the six sense-fields are complete, yet these faculties are not yet able to serve as the basis for contact — this time is named "the six sense-fields." When these faculties are already able to serve as the basis for contact, yet suffering and pleasure are not yet differentiated, unable to avoid danger and harm, grasping fire, touching poison, or handling an unclean blade — this time is named "contact." When one is able to differentiate suffering and pleasure, to avoid danger and harm, and the like, able to give rise to craving desire, yet without yet arousing sexual desire, and not yet giving rise to defiled attachment toward any object — this time is named "sensation." Possessing the three sensations above — this time is named "craving." Because of craving one's objects, seeking after them in every direction — this time is named "grasping." At the very time of this seeking, giving rise to actions of body, speech, and mind — this time is named "becoming." Just as present consciousness exists in the future — this time is named "birth." Just as present name-and-form, the six sense-fields, contact, and sensation exist in the future — this time is named "old age and death."

一剎那十二緣者，若以貪心殺生，彼相應愚是「無明」。相應思是「行」。相應心是「識」。起有作業，必有「名色」。起有作業，必有「六入」。彼相應觸是「觸」。彼相應受是「受」。貪即是「愛」。彼相應纏是「取」。彼身口作業是「有」。如此諸法生是「生」。此諸法變是「老」。此諸法壞是「死」。

As for the twelve conditions within a single thought-moment: if one kills a living being with a mind of craving, the delusion associated with this act is "ignorance." The intention associated with it is "volitional formation." The mind associated with it is "consciousness." That an action arises at all necessarily involves "name-and-form." That an action arises at all necessarily involves "the six sense-fields." The contact associated with it is "contact." The sensation associated with it is "sensation." The craving itself is "craving." The entanglement associated with it is "grasping." The action of body and speech is "becoming." The arising of these various dharmas is "birth." The changing of these various dharmas is "aging." The perishing of these various dharmas is "death."

Reading note

The five embryonic stages (kalala, arbuda, peśī, ghana, praśākhā) are a standard Abhidharma developmental sequence describing conception through early gestation, reused here to give the twelve links a literal, lifetime-long timescale; the single-thought-moment reading immediately below compresses the same twelve links into one instant of a single evil act, illustrating how the same causal structure operates at radically different scales — a point that will matter directly to the "inconceivable" readings still to come.

Further Doubts, and Why Only Twelve Links

Chinese Source

問：何不說病為支？答：一切時、一切處盡有者，立支。自有人從生無病，如薄拘羅，生來不識頭痛，況餘病！是故不立。問：憂悲是支不？答：非也，以終顯始耳，如老死必憂悲。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Why is illness not spoken of as a link? Answer: A link is established only for what is present at every time and in every place. There are, however, people who have never had illness from birth — such as Bakkula, who from birth never even knew a headache, how much less any other illness! Hence it is not established as a link. Question: Are grief and sorrow a link? Answer: No — they only reveal the beginning through the end, as old age and death necessarily bring grief and sorrow.

問：無明有因不？老死有果不？若有，應是支。若無，則墮無因無果法。答：有而非支。無明有因，謂不正思惟；老死有果，謂憂悲。又無明有因，謂老死；老死有果，謂無明。現在愛、取，是過去無明；現在名色、六入、觸、受，此四若在未來，名老死。如說受緣愛，當知說老死緣無明也。猶如車輪，更互相因也。

Question: Does ignorance have a cause? Does old age and death have a fruit? If they do, they ought to be established as further links. If they do not, then this falls into the view of a dharma without cause and without fruit. Answer: They do have these, and yet are not established as separate links. Ignorance has a cause, namely incorrect reflection; old age and death has a fruit, namely grief and sorrow. Again: ignorance has a cause, namely old age and death; old age and death has a fruit, namely ignorance. Present craving and grasping are precisely past ignorance; present name-and-form, the six sense-fields, contact, and sensation, if projected into the future, are named old age and death. Just as sensation is said to condition craving, one should know that old age and death, too, is said to condition ignorance — it is just like a cart-wheel, each part conditioning every other part in turn.

欲界胎生者，具十二支。色界者十一，無名色也。無色界有十，除名色、六入。又言具有。色界初生，諸根未猛利時，是名「名色」。無色界雖無色而有名，當知悉具十二支也。問：無明、行與取、有何異？答：過、現、新故，已與果、未與果等異。

For womb-born beings of the desire realm, all twelve links are present in full. For the form realm, there are eleven — lacking name-and-form. For the formless realm, there are ten — omitting both name-and-form and the six sense-fields. Yet it may also be said that these, too, possess them in full: at the form realm's initial birth, before the sense-faculties have yet become vigorous and sharp, this very time is itself named "name-and-form." The formless realm, though lacking form, still possesses name — one should therefore know that it, too, possesses all twelve links in full. Question: How do ignorance and volitional formation differ from grasping and becoming? Answer: They differ as past versus present, and as old versus new — that is, as having already borne fruit versus not yet having borne fruit, and the like.

Reading note

Bakkula (薄拘羅) is an arhat renowned in Buddhist literature for perfect health across many lifetimes, cited here precisely because his case is the counterexample that keeps illness from qualifying as a universal twelfth link. This closes the "conceivable, arising-and-ceasing" reading of the twelve links.

The Object of Dependent Origination: The Conceivable, Non-Arising Reading

Chinese Source

二、思議不生不滅十二者，此以巧破拙。《中論》云：「為利根弟子說十二不生不滅。」癡如虛空，乃至老死如虛空：無明如幻化，不可得故，乃至老死如幻化，不可得。《金光明》云：「無明體相，本自不有，妄想因緣，和合而有。」不善思惟心行所造，如幻師在四衢道，幻作種種象、馬、纓珞、人物等，癡謂真實、智知非真。無明幻出六道依正，當知本自不有，無明所為。如知藤本非蛇，則怖心不生，不生故不滅。是名思議不生不滅十二因緣相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the conceivable, neither-arising-nor-ceasing twelve links: this uses the skillful to break through the clumsy. The Middle Treatise says: "For disciples of sharp faculties, the twelve links of neither arising nor ceasing are taught." Delusion is like empty space, and so on down to old age and death being like empty space; ignorance is like illusion and transformation, unattainable — and so on down to old age and death being like illusion and transformation, unattainable. The Suvarṇaprabhāsa Sūtra says: "The essence and mark of ignorance is, at root, inherently nonexistent; it exists only through the combining of deluded thought and causal conditions." It is constructed by unwholesome, reflective mental activity — like a magician standing at a crossroads, conjuring various illusory elephants, horses, ornaments, and human figures: the deluded take these as real, while the wise know they are not real. Ignorance conjures up the dependent and principal aspects of the six destinies — one should know that these, too, are, at root, inherently nonexistent, brought about only by ignorance. As when one recognizes that what one took for a snake is really only a rope, the fearful mind no longer arises — and since it no longer arises, it likewise no longer ceases. This is named the mark of the conceivable, neither-arising-nor-ceasing twelve links of dependent origination.

Reading note

The "rope mistaken for a snake" is one of Buddhist philosophy's most standard similes for how ignorance projects a false reality onto something that was never actually threatening to begin with — recognizing the rope does not require destroying anything, only correcting a misperception, which is precisely Zhiyi's point about why "neither arising nor ceasing" belongs to sharper faculties than the first, more literal reading of the twelve links.

The Inconceivable Readings of the Twelve Links

Chinese Source

三、不思議生滅因緣者，破小明大，為利鈍兩緣說界外法也。《華嚴》云：「心如工畫師，作種種五陰。一切世間中，莫不從心造。」畫師，即無明心也。一切世間，即是十法界假實國土等也。諸論明心出一切法不同，或言：阿黎耶是真識，出一切法。或言：阿黎耶是無沒識，無記無明出一切法。若定執性實，墮冥初生覺、從覺生我心過。尚不成界內思議因緣，豈得成界外不思議因緣？惑既非不思議境，翻惑之解，豈得成不思議智？破此，如《止觀》中說。今明無明之心，不自、不他、不共、不無因，四句皆不可思議。若有四悉檀因緣，亦可得說：如四句求夢不可得，而說夢中見一切事；四句求無明不可得，而從無明出界內外一切法。出界內十二因緣，如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the inconceivable, arising-and-ceasing causal conditions: this breaks through the small to clarify the great, teaching the dharma beyond the three realms for the sake of both sharp and dull conditions. The Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "Mind is like a skilled painter, creating every kind of the five aggregates. Within all the world, there is nothing that is not made by mind." The painter is precisely the ignorant mind; all the world is precisely the provisional and real lands of the ten dharma-realms, and the rest. The various treatises differ in clarifying how mind brings forth all dharmas: some say the ālaya is the true consciousness, bringing forth all dharmas; some say the ālaya is the never-perishing consciousness, its neutral ignorance bringing forth all dharmas. If one holds fixedly to some real, inherent nature, this falls into the error of a primordial darkness first giving rise to awareness, and awareness then giving rise to a self-mind. This does not even succeed as an account of the conceivable causal conditions within the three realms — how much less could it succeed as an account of the inconceivable causal conditions beyond the three realms? Since delusion itself is not an inconceivable object, how could the understanding that reverses delusion possibly become inconceivable wisdom? The refutation of this is set forth in the Mo-ho Chih-kuan. Here, let me clarify that the mind of ignorance is neither self-caused, nor other-caused, nor jointly caused, nor causeless — all four propositions are inconceivable. Yet, if there is a causal condition matched to the four siddhāntas, it may nonetheless still be spoken of: just as, though the four propositions cannot locate a dream as ultimately real, one may still speak of seeing all manner of things within a dream; though the four propositions cannot locate ignorance as ultimately real, all dharmas within and beyond the three realms still issue from ignorance. Ignorance's issuing of the twelve links within the three realms is as explained above.

出界外十二因緣者，如《寶性論》云：「羅漢、支佛空智，於如來身本所不見。」二乘雖有無常等四對治，依如來法身，復是顛倒，顛倒故即是無明。住無漏界中有四種障，謂緣、相、生、壞。緣者，謂無明住地，與行作緣也。相者，無明共行為因也。生者，謂無明住地，共無漏業因，生三種意生身也。壞者，三種意生身，緣不可思議變易死也。還如界內十二因緣，從無明至老死也。緣者，即無明支也。相者，行支也。生者，即名色等五支也。愛、取、有三支，例前可知也。壞即生死支也。此十二支，數同界內，義意大異。彼論云：「三種意生身，未得離無明垢，未得究竟無為淨；無明、細戲論未永滅，未得究竟無為我；無明、細戲論集，因無漏業生意陰未永滅，未得無為樂；煩惱染、業染、生染未究竟滅，未證甘露究竟常。」以「緣」煩惱道故，不得大淨；以「相」業道故，不得八自在我；以「生」苦道故，不得大樂；以「壞」老死故，不得不變易常者，由不思議生滅十二因緣也。是為界外不思議生滅十二因緣相(云云)。

As for the twelve links' issuing beyond the three realms: as the Ratnagotravibhāga says, "The empty wisdom of arhats and pratyekabuddhas has, from the start, never seen the Tathāgata's own body." Though the two vehicles possess the fourfold antidote of impermanence and the rest, measured against the Tathāgata's own Dharma-body, this too is an inverted view — and being inverted, it is itself ignorance. Abiding within the taintless realm, there are four kinds of obstruction: condition, mark, birth, and destruction. "Condition" means that the abode of ignorance serves as a condition for volitional formation. "Mark" means that ignorance, together with volitional formation, serves as a cause. "Birth" means that the abode of ignorance, together with the cause of taintless karma, gives birth to the three kinds of mind-made body. "Destruction" means that the three kinds of mind-made body are conditioned by the inconceivable death of change. This, too, follows the same pattern as the twelve links within the three realms, from ignorance to old age and death. "Condition" is precisely the link of ignorance. "Mark" is the link of volitional formation. "Birth" is precisely the five links, name-and-form and the rest. The three links of craving, grasping, and becoming may be understood by the same pattern as before. "Destruction" is precisely the link of birth-and-death. These twelve links, though equal in number to those within the three realms, differ greatly in their meaning and sense. That treatise says: "The three kinds of mind-made body, not yet having attained separation from the taint of ignorance, have not yet attained the ultimate, unconditioned purity; ignorance and its subtle idle speculation not yet permanently extinguished, they have not yet attained the ultimate, unconditioned self; the accumulation of ignorance and subtle idle speculation, and the mind-aggregates born through taintless karma, not yet permanently extinguished, they have not yet attained the unconditioned bliss; the taint of affliction, the taint of karma, and the taint of birth not yet ultimately extinguished, they have not yet realized the ultimate, eternal sweet dew." Because of "condition," the path of affliction, they do not attain great purity; because of "mark," the path of karma, they do not attain the eight sovereign selves; because of "birth," the path of suffering, they do not attain great bliss; because of "destruction," old age and death, they do not attain the unchanging eternal — all of this is due to the inconceivable, arising-and-ceasing twelve links. This is the mark of the inconceivable, arising-and-ceasing twelve links of dependent origination beyond the three realms, and so on.

不思議不生不滅十二因緣者，為利根人即事顯理也。《大經》云：「十二因緣名為佛性」者，無明、愛、取既是煩惱，煩惱道即是菩提。菩提通達無復煩惱，煩惱既無，即究竟淨，了因佛性也。行、有是業道，即是解脫，解脫自在，緣因佛性也。名色、老死是苦道，苦即法身。法身無苦、無樂，是名大樂。不生不死是常，正因佛性。故言無明與愛，是二中間，即是中道。無明是過去，愛是現在，若邊若中無非佛性、並是常樂我淨，無明不生亦復不滅。是名不思議不生不滅十二因緣也。

As for the inconceivable, neither-arising-nor-ceasing twelve links: this is for sharp-faculty persons, revealing principle precisely within phenomena. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The twelve links of dependent origination are named buddha-nature" — since ignorance, craving, and grasping are themselves affliction, the path of affliction is itself bodhi. Bodhi penetrates through completely, with no further affliction remaining; and since affliction is thus nonexistent, this is ultimate purity, the revealing cause of buddha-nature. Volitional formation and becoming are the path of karma, which is itself liberation; liberation's own freedom is the conditioning cause of buddha-nature. Name-and-form and old age-and-death are the path of suffering; suffering is itself the Dharma-body. The Dharma-body is neither suffering nor pleasure — this is named great bliss. Neither arising nor dying is eternity, the correct cause of buddha-nature. Hence it is said that ignorance and craving, these two, mark the interval between — this is precisely the middle way: ignorance is the past, craving is the present, and whether at the extreme or at the middle, none of it is other than buddha-nature, all of it being eternity, bliss, self, and purity; ignorance neither arises nor, likewise, ceases. This is named the inconceivable, neither-arising-nor-ceasing twelve links.

Reading note

This closes the fourfold reading of the twelve links (the correct explanation, 正釋). The "three kinds of mind-made body" (三種意生身) belong to advanced bodhisattvas who have transcended ordinary birth-and-death but not yet the subtler "birth-and-death of change" — already introduced in Fascicle 1's discussion of the shared and distinct teachings. The final, inconceivable-neither-arising-nor-ceasing reading directly maps the twelve links onto the three causes of buddha-nature (correct/revealing/conditioning) already established via the Fahua Wenju, closing the circle between causal analysis and buddha-nature doctrine.

Dependent Origination: Coarse and Wondrous, Relative and Absolute

Chinese Source

二判麁妙者，因緣之境不當麁妙，取之淺深，致有差降耳。若從無明生諸行乃至老死，從三生二，從二生七，從七生三，更互因緣，煩惱業因緣、業苦因緣，無常生滅。《中論》判此教鈍根法。《涅槃》稱慇懃半字。此經：「但離虛妄，名為解脫。」故知此境則麁。若無明體相本自不有，妄想因緣和合而有，境既如幻，智亦叵得。經言：「若有一法過於涅槃，我亦說如幻、如化。」《中論》明教利根。《涅槃》稱長者教。《毘伽羅論》、《大品》名為「如實巧度」，此經名小樹。斯境則巧。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, judging the coarse and the wondrous: the object of dependent origination is not, in itself, either coarse or wondrous — it is only in how shallowly or deeply it is grasped that the difference between them arises. If ignorance gives rise to the volitional formations down to old age and death, three links generating two, two generating seven, seven generating three, one condition generating another in turn — affliction-conditions-karma, karma-conditions-suffering, impermanent, arising and ceasing — the Middle Treatise judges this to be the teaching for dull faculties, the Nirvāṇa Sūtra calls it the earnestly-repeated half-word, and this present sūtra says: "Only by leaving falsehood behind is it named liberation." One thus knows that this object, read in this way, is coarse. If the essence and mark of ignorance is, at root, inherently nonexistent, existing only through the combining of deluded thought and causal conditions, then, since the object is itself like an illusion, wisdom too becomes unattainable as any fixed thing. The sūtra says: "If there were any single dharma surpassing even nirvāṇa, I would say that this, too, is like an illusion, like a conjuration." The Middle Treatise clarifies this as the teaching for sharp faculties; the Nirvāṇa Sūtra calls it the elder's own teaching; the Vyākaraṇa treatise and the Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra name it "the skillful crossing that accords with the real"; this present sūtra names it the small tree. This object, read in this way, is skillful.

若無明是緣，從緣生相，從相有生，從生故壞。滅緣故淨，除相故我，盡生則樂，無壞故常。《中論》云：「因緣生法，亦名為假名。」《大品》稱「十二緣，獨菩薩法。」《涅槃》稱因滅無明，則得熾燃。此經則是大樹，而得增長。比前為妙，方後為麁。若言：無明三道即是三德，不須斷三德更求三德。《中論》云：「因緣所生法，亦名中道義。」《大品》說十二因緣，是為坐道場。《涅槃》云：「無明與愛，是二中間，即是中道。」此經：「佛種從緣起，是故說一乘。」亦名最實事。豈非妙耶！前三是權，故為麁；後一是實，故為妙。用此麁妙歷五味教者，乳教具二種因緣，一麁一妙。酪教一麁。生蘇三麁一妙。熟蘇二麁一妙。《法華》但說一妙。是名待麁因緣，明妙因緣也。

If ignorance is itself a condition, from which the mark arises, from the mark existence arises, and from arising, destruction follows — then extinguishing the condition brings purity, removing the mark brings the self, exhausting arising brings bliss, and having no destruction brings eternity. The Middle Treatise says: "A dharma arisen through causes and conditions is also called a provisional name." The Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra calls it "the twelve links, the dharma unique to bodhisattvas." The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says that, through extinguishing ignorance, one attains a blazing illumination. This present sūtra treats it as the great tree that comes to full growth. Compared to what came before, this is wondrous; measured against what follows, it is coarse. If it is said that the three paths of ignorance are themselves the three virtues, with no need to sever the three virtues in order to seek yet other three virtues — the Middle Treatise says: "A dharma arisen through causes and conditions is also called the meaning of the middle way." The Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra says that the twelve links of dependent origination are what constitutes sitting at the seat of enlightenment. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Ignorance and craving, these two, mark the interval between — this is precisely the middle way." This present sūtra says: "The buddha-seed arises through conditions — hence I teach the one vehicle," also named the most real matter. How could this fail to be wondrous! The first three readings are provisional, and hence coarse; the last is real, and hence wondrous. Applying this coarse-and-wondrous distinction across the Five-Flavor teachings: the milk teaching contains two kinds of dependent origination, one coarse and one wondrous. The cream teaching has one, coarse only. The fresh-curdled-butter teaching has three coarse and one wondrous. The ripened-butter teaching has two coarse and one wondrous. The Lotus alone teaches only the one wondrous. This is named the relative reading of dependent origination, clarifying dependent origination as wondrous.

三、開麁顯妙者，如經：「我法妙難思」，前三皆是佛法，豈有思議之麁異不思議之妙？無離文字說解脫義，秖體思議即不思議。譬如長者，引盆器米麵給與窮子，成窮子物。若定天性，窮子非復客作人，盆器還家，安是他物？如來於不思議方便說麁，何得保麁異妙？今決了聲聞法，是諸經之王。即是開兩因緣，即論於妙。又《大經》云：「為諸聲聞開發慧眼」者，昔慧眼但見於空，不見不空。今開慧眼，即見不空，不空即見佛性。故云：「慧眼見故，而不了了；佛以佛眼，見則了了。」此即決菩薩慧眼，開第三因緣，即絕待論於妙。

Third, opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous: as the sūtra says, "My dharma is wondrous and hard to conceive" — the first three readings are, every one of them, already the Buddha's own dharma; how could there really be a coarse conceivable set apart from a different, wondrous inconceivable? There is no teaching of the meaning of liberation apart from words themselves — it is simply that the very substance of the conceivable is itself the inconceivable. It is like the elder who hands over baskets, vessels, rice, and flour to the destitute son, which become the destitute son's own belongings; and if his true parentage is settled, the destitute son is no longer a hired laborer at all, and the baskets and vessels, returning to the household, how could they still be regarded as another's property? Since the Tathāgata teaches the coarse only as an inconceivable skillful means, how could one insist on holding the coarse to be truly different from the wondrous? Now, resolving and making plain the śrāvaka's own dharma is the king of all sūtras — this is precisely opening up the first two readings of dependent origination and thereby discussing the wondrous. Again, the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "For the sake of the śrāvakas, he opens and develops their wisdom-eye" — formerly, their wisdom-eye saw only emptiness, and did not see the not-empty; now, their wisdom-eye is opened, and they see the not-empty as well, and seeing the not-empty is precisely seeing buddha-nature. Hence it is said: "The wisdom-eye sees it, yet not with full clarity; the Buddha, with the buddha-eye, sees it with complete clarity." This is precisely resolving the bodhisattva's own wisdom-eye, opening the third reading — and this is discussing the wondrous through the absolute reading.

四、觀心者，觀一念無明即是明。《大經》云：「無明明者，即畢竟空。」空慧照無明，無明即淨。譬如有人，覺知有賊，賊無能為。既不為無明所染，即是煩惱道淨。煩惱淨故則無業，無業故無縛，無縛故是自在我。我既自在，不為業縛，誰受是名色、觸、受？無受則無苦。既無苦陰，誰復遷滅？即是常德。一念之心既具十二因緣，觀此因緣，恒作常、樂、我、淨之觀。其心念念住祕密藏中恒作此觀，名託聖胎。觀行純熟，胎分成就。若破無明，名出聖胎(云云)。

Fourth, the contemplation of mind: contemplating that the single thought-moment of ignorance is itself clarity. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Ignorance's own clarity is precisely ultimate emptiness." The wisdom of emptiness illuminates ignorance, and ignorance itself becomes pure. It is like a person who, becoming aware there is a thief present, thereby renders the thief unable to act. Since one is thus no longer stained by ignorance, this is itself the purity of the path of affliction. Since affliction is pure, there is then no karma; since there is no karma, there is no bondage; since there is no bondage, this is the sovereign self. The self being sovereign, no longer bound by karma — who, then, receives this name-and-form, contact, and sensation? Without sensation, there is no suffering. Since there is no longer any aggregate of suffering, what, then, could still be transformed and extinguished? This is precisely the virtue of eternity. Since a single thought-moment of mind already possesses the twelve links of dependent origination within it, contemplating these very links, one constantly cultivates the contemplation of eternity, bliss, self, and purity. The mind, moment after moment, abiding within the secret treasury, constantly cultivating this contemplation, is named "entrusting oneself to the sagely womb." As the contemplative practice matures fully, the embryo comes to completion. Once ignorance is broken through, this is named "emerging from the sagely womb," and so on.

Reading note

This closes the entire object of dependent origination (因緣境): the fourfold correct explanation, judged for coarse and wondrous both relatively (across the Five Flavors) and absolutely (the first three readings opened to reveal they were never really separate from the fourth), and the contemplation of mind — the thief-recognized-is-rendered-harmless simile for how simply illuminating ignorance with wisdom, rather than fighting it, is itself sufficient. "Entrusting oneself to the sagely womb" and "emerging from the sagely womb" are stock Tiantai images for gradual contemplative maturation, borrowed from ordinary gestation.

The Object of the Four Noble Truths 四諦境

Chinese Source

三、明四諦境，為四：一、明四諦。二、判麁妙。三、開麁顯妙。四、觀心。初又二：一、出他解，二、四番四諦。有師解《勝鬘》無邊聖諦，對二乘有餘，彰佛究竟。二乘是有作四聖諦，作者，有量四聖諦也。無作四聖諦者，無量四聖諦也。作、無作就行；量、無量就法。二乘觀諦，得法不盡，更有所作，故名有作。得法不盡，則有限量。經言：「因他知，知是有作行也。」因他知，非一切知，不知無量法也，故言有作、有量。無作、無量者，佛知無窮盡，更無所作，故名無作。自力知一切，知者無作行也。一切者，是無量法也。如此釋者，雖唱四名，但成二義，非今所用。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the object of the Four Noble Truths, in four parts: first, clarifying the Four Noble Truths themselves; second, judging the coarse and the wondrous; third, opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous; fourth, the contemplation of mind. The first, in turn, has two parts: first, presenting other masters' interpretations; second, the fourfold Four Noble Truths. One master interprets the Śrīmālādevī Sūtra's "boundless noble truths" as contrasting with the two vehicles' "truths with remainder," making manifest the Buddha's own ultimate truths. The two vehicles' truths are the "constructed" Four Noble Truths — "constructed" meaning the measured Four Noble Truths. The "unconstructed" Four Noble Truths are the immeasurable Four Noble Truths. Constructed and unconstructed pertain to practice; measured and immeasurable pertain to the dharma itself. When the two vehicles contemplate the truths, their grasp of the dharma is not exhaustive, and there remains further construction yet to be done — hence it is named "constructed." Since their grasp of the dharma is not exhaustive, it is therefore measured, finite. The sūtra says: "Knowing through another, this knowing is a constructed activity." Knowing through another is not knowing everything — one does not know the immeasurable dharma — hence it is called "constructed" and "measured." As for the unconstructed and the immeasurable: the Buddha's own knowledge is without limit or exhaustion, with nothing further to be constructed — hence it is named "unconstructed." Knowing everything by his own power, this knowing is an unconstructed activity. "Everything" is precisely the immeasurable dharma. This interpretation, though it proclaims four names, in fact amounts to only two senses, and is not the interpretation used here.

The Arising-and-Ceasing Four Noble Truths 生滅四諦

Chinese Source

四種四諦者：一、生滅。二、無生滅。三、無量。四、無作。其義出《涅槃．聖行品》，約偏、圓、事、理分四種之殊。所言生滅者，迷真重故，從事受名。然苦、集是一法，分因、果成兩；道、滅亦然。《雜心》偈云：「諸行果性，是說苦諦。因性說集諦；一切有漏法究竟滅，說滅諦；一切無漏行，說道諦。」《大經》云：「陰入重檐，逼迫繫縛，是苦諦。見愛煩惱，能招來果，是集諦。戒、定、慧、無常、苦、空，能除苦本，是道諦。二十五有子、果縛斷，是滅諦。」《遺教》云：「集真是因，更無別因。滅苦之道，即是真道。」此皆明生滅四聖諦相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The four kinds of the Four Noble Truths are: first, arising-and-ceasing; second, non-arising-and-ceasing; third, immeasurable; fourth, uncreated. Their sense derives from the "Chapter on Sagely Practice" in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, differentiated fourfold in terms of the partial and the perfect, phenomena and principle. As for "arising-and-ceasing": since confusion about the real is heavy here, it takes its name from phenomena. Now, suffering and its arising are, in fact, a single dharma, divided into cause and fruit to make two; the path and cessation are likewise so. A verse of the Saṃyuktābhidharma-hṛdaya says: "The nature of the fruit of volitional formations — this speaks of the truth of suffering; the nature of the cause speaks of the truth of its arising. That all conditioned, taint-bearing dharmas ultimately cease speaks of the truth of cessation; that all taintless practices exist speaks of the truth of the path." The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The aggregates and sense-fields, a heavy burden, pressing and binding one — this is the truth of suffering. The afflictions of views and craving, able to summon a future fruit — this is the truth of its arising. Precept, concentration, and wisdom, and impermanence, suffering, and emptiness, able to remove the very root of suffering — this is the truth of the path. The severing of the bonds of the twenty-five states of existence's own children and fruits — this is the truth of cessation." The Sūtra of the Buddha's Final Bequest says: "The gathering of the real is itself the cause — there is no separate cause apart from it. The path that extinguishes suffering is itself the true path." All of this clarifies the mark of the arising-and-ceasing Four Noble Truths.

次第者，從麁至細。苦相麁故先說，滅雖非真，因滅會真，滅相麁亦先說。又舉世苦果，令厭世集。滅能會出世果，令其欣道。作如此次第也。聖者對破邪法，故言正聖也。諦者有三解(云云)：謂自性不虛，故稱為諦。又見此四得不顛倒覺，故稱為諦。又能以此法顯示於他，故名為諦。《大經》：「凡夫有苦，無諦。聲聞、緣覺有苦，有苦諦。」當知凡夫不見聖理，不得智，不能說，但苦無諦。聲聞具三義，故稱為諦。此釋與經合也。

As for the sequence: proceeding from the coarse to the fine. The mark of suffering is coarse, and so it is spoken of first; though cessation is not yet the true real, one meets the real through cessation, and the mark of cessation is likewise coarse, and so it, too, is spoken of first. Again: raising up the worldly fruit of suffering causes one to grow weary of the world's arising; cessation, able to meet the world-transcending fruit, causes one to delight in the path. It is in this sequence that they are given. "Noble" contrasts with and refutes what is false — hence it is spoken of as "correctly noble." "Truth" admits of three interpretations, and so on: that is to say, that its own nature is not empty of substance is why it is called "truth." Again, that seeing these four brings an unerring, undistorted awakening is why it is called "truth." Again, that one is able to use this dharma to reveal it to others is why it is named "truth." The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Ordinary beings have suffering, but no truth; śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas have suffering, and have the truth of suffering." One should know that ordinary beings, not seeing the sagely principle, do not attain wisdom, and cannot teach it — they have only suffering, without the truth. Śrāvakas possess all three senses, and so it is called truth for them. This interpretation accords with the sūtra.

Reading note

These four kinds of the Four Noble Truths (arising-and-ceasing / non-arising / immeasurable / uncreated) are the same fourfold scheme already flagged in the earlier discussion of matching the siddhāntas against the truths — here given their full doctrinal grounding in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's "Chapter on Sagely Practice," and, like the twelve links, mapped one-to-one onto the Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, and perfect teachings respectively.

The Non-Arising, Immeasurable, and Uncreated Four Noble Truths 無生・無量・無作四諦

Chinese Source

無生者，迷真輕故，從理得名。苦，無逼迫相；集，無和合相；道，不二相；滅，無生相。又，習應苦空，三亦如是。又，無生者，生名集、道。集、道即空。空故不生集、道。集、道不生則無苦、滅，即事而真，非滅後真。《大經》云：「諸菩薩等解苦無苦，是故無苦而有真諦。」三亦如是。是故名為無生四聖諦。聖諦義，如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the non-arising reading: since confusion about the real is light here, it takes its name from principle. Suffering has no mark of oppression; its arising has no mark of combination; the path has no mark of duality; cessation has no mark of arising. Again: cultivation accords with the emptiness of suffering, and the other three are likewise so. Again, as for "non-arising": "arising" names its arising and the path. The arising and the path are themselves empty. Being empty, they do not truly give rise to the arising or the path. Since the arising and the path do not truly arise, there is likewise no suffering and no cessation — it is precisely within phenomena that one finds the real, not a real that comes only after cessation. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The various bodhisattvas understand suffering as without suffering — hence there is no ordinary suffering, yet there is the true truth." The other three are likewise so. Hence this is named the non-arising Four Noble Truths. The sense of "noble truth" is as explained above.

無量者，迷中重故，從事得名。苦有無量相，十法界果不同故。集有無量相，五住煩惱不同故。道有無量相，恒沙佛法不同故。滅有無量相，諸波羅蜜不同故。《大經》云：「知諸陰苦，名為中智。分別諸陰，有無量相，非諸聲聞、緣覺所知。我於彼經，竟不說之。」三亦如是。是名無量四聖諦。

As for the immeasurable reading: since confusion about the middle is heavy here, it takes its name from phenomena. Suffering has immeasurable marks, since the fruits of the ten dharma-realms differ from one another. Its arising has immeasurable marks, since the five abodes of affliction differ from one another. The path has immeasurable marks, since the buddha-dharmas, numerous as the sands of the Ganges, differ from one another. Cessation has immeasurable marks, since the various pāramitās differ from one another. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "To know that the aggregates are suffering is named middling wisdom. To differentiate that the aggregates possess immeasurable marks is not something known by śrāvakas or pratyekabuddhas — in that other sūtra, I did not, in the end, teach it." The other three are likewise so. This is named the immeasurable Four Noble Truths.

無作者，迷中輕故，從理得名。以迷理故，菩提是煩惱，名集諦。涅槃是生死，名苦諦。以能解故，煩惱即菩提，名道諦。生死即涅槃，名滅諦。即事而中，無思無念，無誰造作，故名無作。《大經》云：「世諦即是第一義諦。有善方便，隨順眾生，說有二諦。出世人知即第一義諦。」一實諦者，無虛妄、無顛倒、常樂我淨等，是故名為無作四聖諦。

As for the uncreated reading: since confusion about the middle is light here, it takes its name from principle. Because of confusion about principle, bodhi itself is called affliction, named the truth of its arising; nirvāṇa itself is called birth-and-death, named the truth of suffering. Because of the ability to understand, affliction is itself bodhi, named the truth of the path; birth-and-death is itself nirvāṇa, named the truth of cessation. Precisely within phenomena is the middle, without deliberation, without thought, with no one who constructs anything at all — hence it is named "uncreated." The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The conventional truth is itself the truth of ultimate meaning. Through skillful means, according with sentient beings, two truths are spoken of; but those who have transcended the world know that they are, in fact, one and the same, the truth of ultimate meaning." The single, real truth — free of falsehood, free of inversion, eternal, blissful, self, and pure, and the rest — this, therefore, is named the uncreated Four Noble Truths.

然《勝鬘》說無作四諦中，別取一滅諦，是佛所究竟，是常、是諦、是依，三是無常，非諦，非依。何者？三入有為相中，故無常。無常則虛妄，故非諦。無常則不安，故非依。滅諦離有為，故是常。非虛妄，故是諦。第一安隱，故是依。故名第一義諦，亦名不思議也。

Yet the Śrīmālādevī Sūtra, in speaking of the uncreated Four Noble Truths, specially singles out the one truth of cessation as what the Buddha ultimately realizes — it alone is eternal, is truth, is a refuge; the other three are impermanent, not truth, not a refuge. Why is this? The other three fall within the mark of the conditioned, and are therefore impermanent. Being impermanent, they are false, and therefore not truth. Being impermanent, they give no security, and therefore are no refuge. The truth of cessation is free of the conditioned, and is therefore eternal. It is not false, and therefore is truth. It is the foremost security, and therefore is a refuge. Hence it is named the truth of ultimate meaning, also named the inconceivable.

Reading note

These four readings deepen in a consistent pattern: arising-and-ceasing is heaviest confusion about the real, read through phenomena; non-arising is lighter confusion about the real, read through principle; immeasurable is heavy confusion about the middle, read through phenomena; uncreated is light confusion about the middle, read through principle — the same "heavy/light" and "phenomena/principle" cross-classification Zhiyi uses throughout the treatise to generate fourfold schemes from a twofold distinction applied twice.
