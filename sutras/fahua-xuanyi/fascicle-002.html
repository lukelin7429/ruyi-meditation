The Nine Realms in One

Having spent all of Fascicle 1 refuting Guangzhai's theory of "wondrous," Zhiyi turns at last to his own account: the six senses in which a single dharma-realm already contains the other nine, and in which the Buddha's own fruit pervades every place and every time. From here the treatise opens its two central frameworks — the ten suchnesses applied across the ten dharma-realms (the seed of the later doctrine of "three thousand realms in a single thought"), and the threefold division into the dharmas of sentient beings, of the Buddha, and of mind — before turning to the relative and absolute readings of "wondrous" and opening, at last, the ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate.

Status: in progress Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

Zhiyi's Own Account: Six Senses of Cause and Fruit

Chinese Source

天台智者大師說

四、正論今意，為二：先、略用彼名顯於妙義。因具三義者，一法界具九法界，名體廣。九法界即佛法界，名位高。十法界即空、即假、即中，名用長。即一而論三，即三而論一，非各異，亦非橫，亦非一，故稱妙也。果體具三義者，體遍一切處，名體廣。久已成佛久遠久遠，名位高。從本垂迹，過、現、未來三世益物，名用長。是為因果六義，異於餘經，是故稱妙。

Ru-Yi Translation

Expounded by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai.

Fourth, directly discussing the present meaning, in two parts: first, briefly using Guangzhai's own terms to reveal the sense of "wondrous." As for the cause possessing three senses: that a single dharma-realm possesses the other nine dharma-realms within it is named "the essence is broad." That the nine dharma-realms are themselves the buddha-dharma-realm is named "the stage is high." That the ten dharma-realms are precisely empty, precisely provisional, precisely the middle is named "the function is long." Speaking of the one in terms of the three, and speaking of the three in terms of the one — neither each separately different, nor merely arranged side by side, nor merely one — hence it is called wondrous. As for the fruit's essence possessing three senses: that its essence pervades every place is named "the essence is broad." That it has long, long since attained buddhahood, incalculably long ago, is named "the stage is high." That, from the origin, the trace is let down, benefiting beings across the past, present, and future — the three times — is named "the function is long." These are the six senses of cause and fruit, differing from every other sūtra — hence it is called wondrous.

又乳經一種因果廣、高、長，一種因果狹、下、短，則一麁一妙(云云)。酪經唯一種因果狹、下、短，但麁無妙。生蘇經三種因果狹、下、短，一種因果廣、高、長，則三麁一妙。熟蘇經二種因果狹、下、短，一種因果廣、高、長，則二麁一妙。醍醐經一種因果廣、高、長，但妙無麁。又醍醐經妙因、妙果，與諸經妙因、妙果不異，故稱為妙也。

Again, the milk sūtra has one kind of cause and fruit that is broad, high, and long, and one kind that is narrow, low, and short — thus it has one coarse and one wondrous, and so on. The cream sūtra has only the one kind of cause and fruit that is narrow, low, and short — it is coarse only, without any wondrous. The fresh-curdled-butter sūtra has three kinds of cause and fruit that are narrow, low, and short, and one kind that is broad, high, and long — thus it has three coarse and one wondrous. The ripened-butter sūtra has two kinds of cause and fruit that are narrow, low, and short, and one kind that is broad, high, and long — thus it has two coarse and one wondrous. The ghee sūtra has only the one kind of cause and fruit that is broad, high, and long — it is wondrous only, without any coarse. Moreover, the ghee sūtra's wondrous cause and wondrous fruit are no different from the wondrous cause and wondrous fruit of any other sūtra — hence it, too, is called wondrous.

復次觀心釋。若觀己心不具眾生心、佛心者，是體狹；具者是體廣。若己心不等佛心是位下；若等佛心是位高。若己心、眾生心、佛心，不即空、即假、即中者，是用短；即空、即假、即中者，是用長。復次，於一法界通達十法界六即位者，亦是體廣，亦是位高，亦是用長。初約十法界，是顯理一；次約五味，是約教一；次約觀心，是約行一；次約六即，是約人一。略示妙義竟。

Moreover, an interpretation through the contemplation of mind: if, contemplating one's own mind, it is found not to contain the minds of sentient beings and of the Buddha, this is a narrow essence; if it is found to contain them, this is a broad essence. If one's own mind is not equal to the Buddha's mind, this is a low stage; if it is equal to the Buddha's mind, this is a high stage. If one's own mind, the minds of sentient beings, and the Buddha's mind are not precisely empty, precisely provisional, precisely the middle, this is a short function; if they are precisely empty, precisely provisional, precisely the middle, this is a long function. Moreover, to penetrate through the six identities' stages across the ten dharma-realms within a single dharma-realm is likewise a broad essence, a high stage, and a long function. The first account, in terms of the ten dharma-realms, reveals the oneness of principle; the next, in terms of the Five Flavors, is the oneness of teaching; the next, in terms of the contemplation of mind, is the oneness of practice; the next, in terms of the six identities, is the oneness of the person. This briefly indicates the sense of "wondrous," now complete.

Reading note

Zhiyi opens his own positive account by deliberately reusing Guangzhai's own six terms (essence/stage/function, broad/high/long, applied to cause and fruit) — the very framework his twenty-four-fold rebuttal just dismantled — but relocates them: instead of separating "coarse" past teachings from a "wondrous" Lotus, he shows that a single dharma-realm already, inherently, contains the other nine within it. This single move is the germ of the doctrine later developed at length in the Mohe Zhiguan as "three thousand realms in a single thought" (一念三千), already established site-wide.

Three Dharmas: Sentient Beings, the Buddha, and Mind 眾生法・佛法・心法

Chinese Source

廣說者：先法，次妙。南岳師舉三種，謂眾生法、佛法、心法。如經：「為令眾生，開示悟入佛之知見。」若眾生無佛知見，何所論開？當知佛之知見蘊在眾生也。又經：「但以父母所生眼」，即肉眼；「徹見內外彌樓山」，即天眼。洞見諸色而無染著，即慧眼。見色無錯謬即法眼。雖未得無漏，而其眼根清淨。若此一眼具諸眼用，即佛眼。此是今經明眾生法妙之文也。《大經》云：「學大乘者，雖有肉眼，名為佛眼。耳鼻五根，例亦如是。」《殃掘》云：「所謂彼眼根，於諸如來常，具足無減修，了了分明見。」乃至意根亦如是。《大品》云：「六自在王，性清淨故。」又云：「一切法趣眼，是趣不過。」眼尚不可得，何況有趣、有非趣？乃至一切法趣意，亦如是。此即諸經明眾生法妙也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Given at length: first "dharma," then "wondrous." Master Nanyue raises three kinds: the dharma of sentient beings, the dharma of the Buddha, and the dharma of mind. As the sūtra says: "For the sake of causing sentient beings to open, show, awaken to, and enter the Buddha's own knowledge and vision." If sentient beings did not possess the Buddha's knowledge and vision at all, what could there be to speak of "opening"? One should know that the Buddha's own knowledge and vision is already stored within sentient beings. Again, the sūtra says: "Merely with the eyes given by his father and mother" — this is the physical eye; "seeing through, inside and out, to Mount Sumeru" — this is the heavenly eye. Seeing through all forms without being stained or attached to them is the wisdom-eye. Seeing forms without error or confusion is the dharma-eye. Though not yet having attained the taintless, his eye-faculty is nonetheless pure. If this single eye already possesses the function of all the eyes, this is the buddha-eye. This is the present sūtra's own text clarifying the wondrous dharma of sentient beings. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "For one who studies the Mahāyāna, though he has only the physical eye, it is named the buddha-eye. The ear, nose, and the remaining five faculties are, by the same pattern, likewise so." The Aṅgulimālīya Sūtra says: "As for that very eye-faculty, in every one of the tathāgatas it is eternal, complete without diminishment in its cultivation, seeing with perfect clarity and distinctness" — and this extends even to the mind-faculty, likewise so. The Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra says: "The six sovereign kings, since their nature is pure" — and again: "All dharmas converge upon the eye; this convergence is unsurpassed." If the eye itself is unattainable, how much more so is there any "converging" or "not converging"! And this extends even to all dharmas converging upon the mind, likewise so. This is how the various sūtras clarify the wondrous dharma of sentient beings.

佛法妙者，如經：「止止不須說，我法妙難思。」佛法不出權、實，「是法甚深妙，難見難可了。一切眾生類，無能知佛者」，即實智妙也。「及佛諸餘法，亦無能測者」，即佛權智妙也。如是二法，唯佛與佛乃能究盡諸法實相，是名佛法妙。

As for the wondrous dharma of the Buddha: as the sūtra says, "Stop, stop — there is no need to speak; my dharma is wondrous and hard to conceive." The Buddha's own dharma does not go beyond the provisional and the real: "This dharma is exceedingly deep and wondrous, hard to see and hard to fathom; among every kind of sentient being, none is able to know the Buddha" — this is the wondrousness of real wisdom. "And as for the Buddha's every other dharma, none is able to measure it either" — this is the wondrousness of the Buddha's provisional wisdom. Only buddha together with buddha is able to fully fathom, to their very limit, the true mark of all dharmas through these two dharmas together — this is named the wondrous dharma of the Buddha.

心法妙者，如〈安樂行〉中，修攝其心，觀一切法，不動不退。又一念隨喜等。《普賢觀》云：「我心自空，罪福無主」「觀心無心，法不住法。」又「心純是法」。《淨名》云：「觀身實相，觀佛亦然。諸佛解脫當於眾生心行中求。」《華嚴》云：「心、佛及眾生，是三無差別」「破心微塵，出大千經卷」，是名心法妙也。

As for the wondrous dharma of mind: as in "Peaceful Practices," cultivating and gathering the mind, contemplating all dharmas, unmoving and unretreating; and again, a single thought-moment of rejoicing, and so on. The Contemplation of Samantabhadra Sūtra says: "My mind is itself empty; offense and merit have no master," and: "Contemplating mind, there is no mind; dharmas do not abide in dharmas," and again: "Mind is purely dharma." The Vimalakīrti says: "Contemplate the true mark of the body; contemplate the Buddha in just the same way. The liberation of all buddhas ought to be sought within the mental activity of sentient beings." The Avataṃsaka says: "Mind, Buddha, and sentient beings — these three are without distinction," and: "Breaking open a single mote of dust of the mind, one brings forth scrolls of sūtras as vast as a great-thousand world-system." This is named the wondrous dharma of mind.

△今依三法，更廣分別。若廣眾生法，一往通論諸因果及一切法。若廣佛法，此則據果。若廣心法，此則據因。

Now, in dependence on these three dharmas, let me differentiate them still further at length. In expounding the dharma of sentient beings at length, this generally discusses, all together, every cause and fruit and all dharmas. In expounding the dharma of the Buddha at length, this is based on the fruit. In expounding the dharma of mind at length, this is based on the cause.

Reading note

Master Nanyue is Huisi (慧思, 515–577), Zhiyi's own teacher. The five eyes (physical, heavenly, wisdom, dharma, buddha) are the same set already established site-wide via the Jiaoguan Gangzong; here Zhiyi's point is that all five are, in some sense, already latent in the single physical eye. "Peaceful Practices" and the destitute-son parable's language echo material already translated in full in the Fahua Wenju.
