The Nine Realms in One

Having spent all of Fascicle 1 refuting Guangzhai's theory of "wondrous," Zhiyi turns at last to his own account: the six senses in which a single dharma-realm already contains the other nine, and in which the Buddha's own fruit pervades every place and every time. From here the treatise opens its two central frameworks — the ten suchnesses applied across the ten dharma-realms (the seed of the later doctrine of "three thousand realms in a single thought"), and the threefold division into the dharmas of sentient beings, of the Buddha, and of mind — before turning to the relative and absolute readings of "wondrous" and opening, at last, the ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate.

Status: in progress Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

Zhiyi's Own Account: Six Senses of Cause and Fruit

Chinese Source

天台智者大師說

四、正論今意，為二：先、略用彼名顯於妙義。因具三義者，一法界具九法界，名體廣。九法界即佛法界，名位高。十法界即空、即假、即中，名用長。即一而論三，即三而論一，非各異，亦非橫，亦非一，故稱妙也。果體具三義者，體遍一切處，名體廣。久已成佛久遠久遠，名位高。從本垂迹，過、現、未來三世益物，名用長。是為因果六義，異於餘經，是故稱妙。

Ru-Yi Translation

Expounded by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai.

Fourth, directly discussing the present meaning, in two parts: first, briefly using Guangzhai's own terms to reveal the sense of "wondrous." As for the cause possessing three senses: that a single dharma-realm possesses the other nine dharma-realms within it is named "the essence is broad." That the nine dharma-realms are themselves the buddha-dharma-realm is named "the stage is high." That the ten dharma-realms are precisely empty, precisely provisional, precisely the middle is named "the function is long." Speaking of the one in terms of the three, and speaking of the three in terms of the one — neither each separately different, nor merely arranged side by side, nor merely one — hence it is called wondrous. As for the fruit's essence possessing three senses: that its essence pervades every place is named "the essence is broad." That it has long, long since attained buddhahood, incalculably long ago, is named "the stage is high." That, from the origin, the trace is let down, benefiting beings across the past, present, and future — the three times — is named "the function is long." These are the six senses of cause and fruit, differing from every other sūtra — hence it is called wondrous.

又乳經一種因果廣、高、長，一種因果狹、下、短，則一麁一妙(云云)。酪經唯一種因果狹、下、短，但麁無妙。生蘇經三種因果狹、下、短，一種因果廣、高、長，則三麁一妙。熟蘇經二種因果狹、下、短，一種因果廣、高、長，則二麁一妙。醍醐經一種因果廣、高、長，但妙無麁。又醍醐經妙因、妙果，與諸經妙因、妙果不異，故稱為妙也。

Again, the milk sūtra has one kind of cause and fruit that is broad, high, and long, and one kind that is narrow, low, and short — thus it has one coarse and one wondrous, and so on. The cream sūtra has only the one kind of cause and fruit that is narrow, low, and short — it is coarse only, without any wondrous. The fresh-curdled-butter sūtra has three kinds of cause and fruit that are narrow, low, and short, and one kind that is broad, high, and long — thus it has three coarse and one wondrous. The ripened-butter sūtra has two kinds of cause and fruit that are narrow, low, and short, and one kind that is broad, high, and long — thus it has two coarse and one wondrous. The ghee sūtra has only the one kind of cause and fruit that is broad, high, and long — it is wondrous only, without any coarse. Moreover, the ghee sūtra's wondrous cause and wondrous fruit are no different from the wondrous cause and wondrous fruit of any other sūtra — hence it, too, is called wondrous.

復次觀心釋。若觀己心不具眾生心、佛心者，是體狹；具者是體廣。若己心不等佛心是位下；若等佛心是位高。若己心、眾生心、佛心，不即空、即假、即中者，是用短；即空、即假、即中者，是用長。復次，於一法界通達十法界六即位者，亦是體廣，亦是位高，亦是用長。初約十法界，是顯理一；次約五味，是約教一；次約觀心，是約行一；次約六即，是約人一。略示妙義竟。

Moreover, an interpretation through the contemplation of mind: if, contemplating one's own mind, it is found not to contain the minds of sentient beings and of the Buddha, this is a narrow essence; if it is found to contain them, this is a broad essence. If one's own mind is not equal to the Buddha's mind, this is a low stage; if it is equal to the Buddha's mind, this is a high stage. If one's own mind, the minds of sentient beings, and the Buddha's mind are not precisely empty, precisely provisional, precisely the middle, this is a short function; if they are precisely empty, precisely provisional, precisely the middle, this is a long function. Moreover, to penetrate through the six identities' stages across the ten dharma-realms within a single dharma-realm is likewise a broad essence, a high stage, and a long function. The first account, in terms of the ten dharma-realms, reveals the oneness of principle; the next, in terms of the Five Flavors, is the oneness of teaching; the next, in terms of the contemplation of mind, is the oneness of practice; the next, in terms of the six identities, is the oneness of the person. This briefly indicates the sense of "wondrous," now complete.

Reading note

Zhiyi opens his own positive account by deliberately reusing Guangzhai's own six terms (essence/stage/function, broad/high/long, applied to cause and fruit) — the very framework his twenty-four-fold rebuttal just dismantled — but relocates them: instead of separating "coarse" past teachings from a "wondrous" Lotus, he shows that a single dharma-realm already, inherently, contains the other nine within it. This single move is the germ of the doctrine later developed at length in the Mohe Zhiguan as "three thousand realms in a single thought" (一念三千), already established site-wide.

Three Dharmas: Sentient Beings, the Buddha, and Mind 眾生法・佛法・心法

Chinese Source

廣說者：先法，次妙。南岳師舉三種，謂眾生法、佛法、心法。如經：「為令眾生，開示悟入佛之知見。」若眾生無佛知見，何所論開？當知佛之知見蘊在眾生也。又經：「但以父母所生眼」，即肉眼；「徹見內外彌樓山」，即天眼。洞見諸色而無染著，即慧眼。見色無錯謬即法眼。雖未得無漏，而其眼根清淨。若此一眼具諸眼用，即佛眼。此是今經明眾生法妙之文也。《大經》云：「學大乘者，雖有肉眼，名為佛眼。耳鼻五根，例亦如是。」《殃掘》云：「所謂彼眼根，於諸如來常，具足無減修，了了分明見。」乃至意根亦如是。《大品》云：「六自在王，性清淨故。」又云：「一切法趣眼，是趣不過。」眼尚不可得，何況有趣、有非趣？乃至一切法趣意，亦如是。此即諸經明眾生法妙也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Given at length: first "dharma," then "wondrous." Master Nanyue raises three kinds: the dharma of sentient beings, the dharma of the Buddha, and the dharma of mind. As the sūtra says: "For the sake of causing sentient beings to open, show, awaken to, and enter the Buddha's own knowledge and vision." If sentient beings did not possess the Buddha's knowledge and vision at all, what could there be to speak of "opening"? One should know that the Buddha's own knowledge and vision is already stored within sentient beings. Again, the sūtra says: "Merely with the eyes given by his father and mother" — this is the physical eye; "seeing through, inside and out, to Mount Sumeru" — this is the heavenly eye. Seeing through all forms without being stained or attached to them is the wisdom-eye. Seeing forms without error or confusion is the dharma-eye. Though not yet having attained the taintless, his eye-faculty is nonetheless pure. If this single eye already possesses the function of all the eyes, this is the buddha-eye. This is the present sūtra's own text clarifying the wondrous dharma of sentient beings. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "For one who studies the Mahāyāna, though he has only the physical eye, it is named the buddha-eye. The ear, nose, and the remaining five faculties are, by the same pattern, likewise so." The Aṅgulimālīya Sūtra says: "As for that very eye-faculty, in every one of the tathāgatas it is eternal, complete without diminishment in its cultivation, seeing with perfect clarity and distinctness" — and this extends even to the mind-faculty, likewise so. The Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra says: "The six sovereign kings, since their nature is pure" — and again: "All dharmas converge upon the eye; this convergence is unsurpassed." If the eye itself is unattainable, how much more so is there any "converging" or "not converging"! And this extends even to all dharmas converging upon the mind, likewise so. This is how the various sūtras clarify the wondrous dharma of sentient beings.

佛法妙者，如經：「止止不須說，我法妙難思。」佛法不出權、實，「是法甚深妙，難見難可了。一切眾生類，無能知佛者」，即實智妙也。「及佛諸餘法，亦無能測者」，即佛權智妙也。如是二法，唯佛與佛乃能究盡諸法實相，是名佛法妙。

As for the wondrous dharma of the Buddha: as the sūtra says, "Stop, stop — there is no need to speak; my dharma is wondrous and hard to conceive." The Buddha's own dharma does not go beyond the provisional and the real: "This dharma is exceedingly deep and wondrous, hard to see and hard to fathom; among every kind of sentient being, none is able to know the Buddha" — this is the wondrousness of real wisdom. "And as for the Buddha's every other dharma, none is able to measure it either" — this is the wondrousness of the Buddha's provisional wisdom. Only buddha together with buddha is able to fully fathom, to their very limit, the true mark of all dharmas through these two dharmas together — this is named the wondrous dharma of the Buddha.

心法妙者，如〈安樂行〉中，修攝其心，觀一切法，不動不退。又一念隨喜等。《普賢觀》云：「我心自空，罪福無主」「觀心無心，法不住法。」又「心純是法」。《淨名》云：「觀身實相，觀佛亦然。諸佛解脫當於眾生心行中求。」《華嚴》云：「心、佛及眾生，是三無差別」「破心微塵，出大千經卷」，是名心法妙也。

As for the wondrous dharma of mind: as in "Peaceful Practices," cultivating and gathering the mind, contemplating all dharmas, unmoving and unretreating; and again, a single thought-moment of rejoicing, and so on. The Contemplation of Samantabhadra Sūtra says: "My mind is itself empty; offense and merit have no master," and: "Contemplating mind, there is no mind; dharmas do not abide in dharmas," and again: "Mind is purely dharma." The Vimalakīrti says: "Contemplate the true mark of the body; contemplate the Buddha in just the same way. The liberation of all buddhas ought to be sought within the mental activity of sentient beings." The Avataṃsaka says: "Mind, Buddha, and sentient beings — these three are without distinction," and: "Breaking open a single mote of dust of the mind, one brings forth scrolls of sūtras as vast as a great-thousand world-system." This is named the wondrous dharma of mind.

△今依三法，更廣分別。若廣眾生法，一往通論諸因果及一切法。若廣佛法，此則據果。若廣心法，此則據因。

Now, in dependence on these three dharmas, let me differentiate them still further at length. In expounding the dharma of sentient beings at length, this generally discusses, all together, every cause and fruit and all dharmas. In expounding the dharma of the Buddha at length, this is based on the fruit. In expounding the dharma of mind at length, this is based on the cause.

Reading note

Master Nanyue is Huisi (慧思, 515–577), Zhiyi's own teacher. The five eyes (physical, heavenly, wisdom, dharma, buddha) are the same set already established site-wide via the Jiaoguan Gangzong; here Zhiyi's point is that all five are, in some sense, already latent in the single physical eye. "Peaceful Practices" and the destitute-son parable's language echo material already translated in full in the Fahua Wenju.

The Ten Suchnesses 十如是

Chinese Source

眾生法為二：先列法數，次解法相。數者，經論或明一法攝一切法，謂心是：三界無別法，唯是一心作。或明二法攝一切法，所謂名、色：一切世間中，但有名與色。或明三法攝一切法：謂命、識、煖。如是等增數，乃至百千。今經用十法攝一切法：「所謂諸法：如是相、如是性、如是體、如是力、如是作、如是因、如是緣、如是果、如是報、如是本末究竟等。」南岳師讀此文皆云如，故呼為十如也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The dharma of sentient beings has two parts: first, listing the dharma-numbers; second, explaining the dharma-marks. As for the numbers: sūtras and treatises sometimes clarify that a single dharma gathers in all dharmas, namely mind — "the three realms have no separate dharma of their own; they are made by the one mind alone." Sometimes they clarify that two dharmas gather in all dharmas, namely name and form — "within all the world, there is only name and form." Sometimes they clarify that three dharmas gather in all dharmas, namely life, consciousness, and warmth. Numbers of this kind increase in this way, up to a hundred, a thousand. The present sūtra uses ten dharmas to gather in all dharmas: "That is to say, all dharmas: such a mark, such a nature, such an essence, such a power, such an activity, such a cause, such a condition, such a fruit, such a retribution, and such an ultimate equality from beginning to end." Master Nanyue, reading this passage, read every instance as "thus," or "such" — hence it is called "the ten suchnesses."

天台師云：「依義讀文，凡有三轉。一云：是相如、是性如，乃至是報如。二云：如是相、如是性，乃至如是報。三云：相如是、性如是，乃至報如是。若皆稱如者，如名不異，即空義也。若作如是相、如是性者，點空相性，名字施設，邐迤不同，即假義也。若作相如是者，如於中道實相之是，即中義也。」分別令易解故，明空、假、中，得意為言，空即假、中。約如明空，一空一切空。點如明相，一假一切假。就是論中，一中一切中，非一二三而一二三，不縱不橫，名為實相。唯佛與佛，究竟此法。是十法，攝一切法。若依義便，作三意分別。若依讀便，當依偈文云：「如是大果報，種種性相義」(云云)。

The Tiantai master says: "Reading the text in accordance with its sense, there are, in general, three ways of parsing it. The first way reads: 'the mark, thus,' 'the nature, thus,' down to 'the retribution, thus.' The second way reads: 'thus, the mark,' 'thus, the nature,' down to 'thus, the retribution.' The third way reads: 'the mark, thus it is,' 'the nature, thus it is,' down to 'the retribution, thus it is.' If each is uniformly called 'thus,' then 'thus' as a name admits no distinction — this is the sense of emptiness. If it is read as 'thus, the mark' and 'thus, the nature,' pointing to emptiness while marking out mark and nature, names and designations proliferating differently from one another — this is the sense of the provisional. If it is read as 'the mark, thus it is,' where 'thus it is' refers to the true mark of the middle way — this is the sense of the middle." This differentiation is given to make it easier to understand; to clarify emptiness, the provisional, and the middle, and to grasp their meaning as one speaks of them: emptiness is itself the provisional and the middle. In terms of "thus," emptiness is clarified — one instance of emptiness, and every instance is empty. Pointing to "thus," the mark is clarified — one instance of the provisional, and every instance is provisional. In terms of "it is," the middle is discussed — one instance of the middle, and every instance is the middle: neither one, two, nor three, and yet one, two, and three; neither arranged in sequence nor set side by side — this is named the true mark. Only buddha together with buddha fully fathoms this dharma. These ten dharmas gather in all dharmas. If one follows what is convenient for the sense, this threefold differentiation is made; if one follows what is convenient for the reading, one should follow the verse text, which says: "Such is the great retribution, and the various meanings of nature and mark," and so on.

次判權實者，光宅以前五如是為權，屬凡夫；次四如是為實，屬聖人；後一如是，總結權實。引偈證云：「如是大果報」，大故知是實；「種種性相」，故知是權。今恐不爾，大義有三：大、多、勝。若取大為實者，亦應取多取勝。種種之名，豈非多義？若言權屬凡夫，凡夫何意無實？若實屬聖人，聖人何意無權？如此抑沒，義不可依。又北地師以前五為權，後五為實。此皆人情耳。

Next, judging the provisional and real status of the ten suchnesses: Guangzhai takes the first five suchnesses as provisional, belonging to ordinary beings, the next four suchnesses as real, belonging to sages, and the last one suchness as a general summation of the provisional and the real together. He cites the verse as proof, saying: "Such is the great retribution" — since it is "great," one knows it is real; "the various meanings of nature and mark" — hence one knows this is provisional. I fear this is not so. The sense of "great" has three aspects: great, many, and superior. If "great" is taken to mean "real," then "many" and "superior" ought likewise to be taken as such. Does the term "various" not itself carry the sense of "many"? And if the provisional is said to belong to ordinary beings, why should ordinary beings have no share in the real at all? And if the real is said to belong to sages, why should sages have no share in the provisional at all? Suppressing and obscuring the sense in this way cannot be relied upon. Again, a northern master takes the first five as provisional and the last five as real. All of this is nothing but personal opinion.

Reading note

This is the treatise's own account of the "three readings" (三轉讀文) of the ten-suchness passage — one of the most-cited passages in the entire Tiantai corpus, since it grounds the three truths (empty/provisional/middle) directly in the sūtra's own grammar rather than importing them from the Middle Treatise. The ten suchnesses themselves (mark/nature/essence/power/activity/cause/condition/fruit/retribution/ultimate-equality-from-beginning-to-end) are already established site-wide via the Mohe Zhiguan and Fahua Wenju; this passage is their doctrinal origin point within this sūtra's own text.

The Hundred Realms, Thousand Suchnesses 百界千如

Chinese Source

今明權實者，以十如是約十法界，謂六道、四聖也。皆稱法界者，其意有三：十數皆依法界，法界外更無復法。能、所合稱，故言十法界也。二、此十種法，分齊不同，因果隔別，凡聖有異，故加之以界也。三、此十皆即法界，攝一切法。一切法趣地獄，是趣不過。當體即理，更無所依，故名法界。乃至佛法界，亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, clarifying the provisional and the real: the ten suchnesses are applied in terms of the ten dharma-realms, namely the six destinies and the four kinds of sages. That all ten are called "dharma-realms" has, in general, three senses. First: the ten numbers all depend upon the dharma-realm, and there is no further dharma outside the dharma-realm; what depends and what is depended upon are named together, and so it is called "the ten dharma-realms." Second: these ten kinds of dharma each have their own distinct boundaries, their causes and fruits held apart from one another, ordinary beings and sages differing from each other — hence "realm" is added to their name. Third: these ten are, every one of them, identical with the dharma-realm, gathering in all dharmas. That all dharmas converge upon hell is itself an unsurpassed convergence; identical, in its very essence, with principle, with nothing further to depend upon — hence it is named "dharma-realm." And this extends even to the buddha-dharma-realm, likewise so.

若十數依法界者，能依從所依，即入空界也。十界界隔者，即假界也。十數皆法界者，即中界也。欲令易解，如此分別。得意為言，空即假、中，無一二三，如前(云云)。

If the ten numbers depend on the dharma-realm, then what depends follows from what is depended upon — this is entering the realm of emptiness. That the ten realms are held distinctly apart, realm from realm, is the realm of the provisional. That the ten numbers are, every one of them, the dharma-realm, is the realm of the middle. This differentiation is given only to make it easier to understand; grasping the meaning as one speaks of it, emptiness is itself the provisional and the middle, with no separate one, two, or three, as before, and so on.

此一法界具十如是，十法界具百如是。又一法界具九法界，則有百法界，千如是。束為五差：一、惡。二、善。三、二乘。四、菩薩。五、佛。判為二法：前四是權法，後一是實法，細論各具權實，且依兩義。

This single dharma-realm possesses the ten suchnesses within it; the ten dharma-realms possess a hundred suchnesses within them. Again, since a single dharma-realm possesses the other nine dharma-realms within it, there are, in all, a hundred dharma-realms, and a thousand suchnesses. Gathered together, these fall into five groupings: first, evil; second, good; third, the two vehicles; fourth, bodhisattvas; fifth, buddhas. Judged as two dharmas: the first four are provisional dharmas, and the last one is the real dharma — though, examined in fine detail, each of the five in fact possesses both the provisional and the real within it; for now, I rely on this twofold sense.

然此權實不可思議，乃是三世諸佛二智之境。以此為境，何法不收？此境發智，何智不發？故文云「諸法」。諸法者，是所照境廣也。唯佛與佛乃能究盡者，明能照智深，窮邊盡底也。其智慧門難解難入者，歎境妙也。我所得智慧，微妙最第一者，歎智與境相稱也。〈方便品〉長行，略說此法。後開示悟入，廣說此法。火宅，譬喻此法。信解，領解此法。長者，付子此法。藥草，述成此法。化城，引入此法。如是等種種，秖名十如權實法耳。

Yet this provisional and real is inconceivable — it is precisely the object of the buddhas' twin wisdom across the three times. Taking this as the object, what dharma is not gathered in? This object gives rise to wisdom — what wisdom is not thereby given rise to? Hence the text says "all dharmas." "All dharmas" means that the object illuminated is vast. "Only buddha together with buddha is able to fathom it fully" clarifies that the wisdom which illuminates it is profound, reaching to the very limit, exhausting its every depth. "Its gate of wisdom is hard to understand and hard to enter" praises the wondrousness of the object. "The wisdom I have attained is subtle and wondrous, most supreme of all" praises how wisdom and its object correspond to one another. The prose section of "Skillful Means" briefly teaches this dharma; the later "opening, showing, awakening to, and entering" teaches this dharma at length. The burning house gives a parable of this dharma. "Faith and Understanding" receives and understands this dharma. The elder bestows this dharma on his son. Medicinal Herbs restates and confirms this dharma. The phantom city draws beings in to this dharma. All such things as these are simply named the dharma of the ten suchnesses' provisional and real.

如來洞達，究十法底、盡十法邊，明識眾生：種、非種，芽、未芽，熟、不熟，可度脫、不可度脫，如實知之，無有錯謬。殃掘摩羅雖是惡人，實相性熟，即時得度。四禪比丘雖是善人，惡性相熟，即不堪度。當知，眾生之法不可思議，雖實而權，雖權而實，實、權相即，不相妨礙。不可以牛、羊眼觀視眾生，不可以凡夫心評量眾生。智如如來，乃能評量。何以故？眾生法妙故。

The Tathāgata sees through completely, fathoming the very depth of the ten dharmas, exhausting their every limit, clearly recognizing sentient beings: whether their seed has been planted or not, whether it has sprouted or not, whether it is ripe or not, whether they can be liberated or cannot be liberated — knowing this truly, as it really is, without any error. Aṅgulimāla, though an evil man, had his true-mark nature already ripe, and so was liberated at once. The bhikṣu of the fourth dhyāna, though a good man, had his evil nature and mark already ripe, and so could not be liberated. One should know that the dharma of sentient beings is inconceivable: though real, it is yet provisional; though provisional, it is yet real; the real and the provisional are mutually identical, and do not obstruct one another. One cannot observe sentient beings with the eyes of an ox or a sheep; one cannot appraise sentient beings with the mind of an ordinary person. Only wisdom equal to the Tathāgata's own is able to appraise them. Why is this? Because the dharma of sentient beings is wondrous.

Reading note

This "hundred dharma-realms, thousand suchnesses" (百界千如) passage is the direct textual seed of "three thousand realms in a single thought," developed at length in the Mohe Zhiguan: multiply the hundred realms and thousand suchnesses by the three kinds of worlds (of the five aggregates, of sentient beings, and of lands) and the figure of three thousand results. Aṅgulimāla, the murderer converted by the Buddha and the subject of his own sūtra, and the "bhikṣu of the fourth dhyāna" who mistook a meditative attainment for arhatship and, on losing it, wrongly believed the Buddha had lied to him and so fell into wrong view at death, are both stock Tiantai examples of how surface appearance (evil deeds, outward virtue) can mislead about a being's actual underlying readiness for liberation.
