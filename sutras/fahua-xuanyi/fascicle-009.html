The Perfect Teaching's Gate

Fascicle 9 opens with the perfect teaching's own gate into the real mark, first distinguishing it sharply from the distinct teaching's own gate — sharing identical vocabulary yet differing in ten separate ways — before giving its own contemplation, continuing Clarifying the Essence (顯體).

Distinguishing the Perfect Gate 簡圓門

Chinese Source

次、明圓門入實觀者，先、簡圓門。次、明圓觀。

上三藏門，滅實色通真，不得意多諍；體門即幻色通真，示人無諍法；別門體滅生死色，次第滅法性色通中，不得意多諍；圓門即生死色是法性色，即法性色而通中，示人無諍法，故文云：「無上道」，又云：「而行深妙道」，即此義也。

上兩門不通中，不俟分別。別、圓兩種俱通中。論其同異略為十：一、融不融。二、即法不即法。三、明佛智非佛智。四、明次行不次行。五、明斷斷惑不斷斷惑。六、明實位不實位。七、果縱果不縱。八、圓詮不圓詮。九、約難問。十、約譬喻。尋此十意，明識八門同異也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, clarifying the perfect teaching's own gate for entering the real: first, distinguishing the perfect gate; next, clarifying the perfect contemplation.

The Tripiṭaka teaching's own gate, discussed above, extinguishes real form to lead through to the truth — failing to grasp this gives rise to much dispute; the embodying gate takes illusory form itself as leading through to the truth, showing people a dharma free of contention; the distinct teaching's own gate embodies the extinguishing of birth-and-death's own form, sequentially extinguishing the dharma-nature's own form to lead through to the middle — failing to grasp this, too, gives rise to much dispute; the perfect teaching's own gate takes birth-and-death's own form as itself the dharma-nature's own form, this same dharma-nature's own form itself leading through to the middle, showing people a dharma free of contention — hence the text says, "the unsurpassed path," and again, "yet practices the profound and wondrous path" — precisely this same sense.

The two gates discussed above do not lead through to the middle, and need no further distinguishing here. The distinct and perfect teachings' own two kinds both lead through to the middle. Discussing their sameness and difference, this is briefly treated under ten senses: first, merged or unmerged; second, identical with the dharma or not identical with the dharma; third, clarifying the buddha's own wisdom versus wisdom that is not the buddha's own; fourth, clarifying sequential practice versus non-sequential practice; fifth, clarifying severing-that-still-severs versus severing-that-does-not-sever the afflictions; sixth, clarifying the real stage versus the non-real stage; seventh, the fruit permitted freely versus the fruit not permitted freely; eighth, perfect exposition versus non-perfect exposition; ninth, in terms of difficult questions; tenth, in terms of illustration. Examining these same ten senses, the sameness and difference of the eight gates may be clearly recognized.

一、明融不融者，別教四門，所據決定妙有善色，不關於空；據畢竟空，不關於有，乃至非空非有門亦如是。四門歷別，當分各通。

不得意者，作定相取，似同性實，殆濫冥初生覺(云云)。前、三藏有門，已破外道邪計先盡；次、空等三門，破邪則少；又、通巧四門，破三藏之拙；又、別教之門，破通門之近，已不與二乘共，何況外道冥覺而濫妙有！妙有依如來藏分判四門，何得同彼尼犍性實？如周璞、鄭璞，名同質異，貴賤天懸。今時學地論人，反道還俗，竊以此義，偷安莊老，金石相糅，遂令邪正混淆，盲暝之徒不別涇渭。若得諸四門意，精簡真偽，偷盜不生。

然別門雖作定說，如是諍論，諸佛境界，二乘不知，豈同外道耶？

圓門虛融微妙，不可定執，說有不隔無，約有而論無；說無不隔有，約無而論有。有無不二，無決定相，假寄於有，以為言端。而此有門亦即三門，一門無量門，無量門一門，非一、非四，四一一四，此即圓門相也。

First, clarifying merged or unmerged: the distinct teaching's own four gates, in what they rely upon, fix wondrous being and good form as decisively determined, having nothing to do with emptiness; and fix ultimate emptiness as decisively determined, having nothing to do with existence — and so on, likewise, for the gate of neither emptiness nor existence. The four gates, taken sequentially and separately, each lead through only within its own particular division.

For one who fails to grasp this, taking up a fixed mark and clinging to it, this resembles the claim that "the same nature is the real," and comes dangerously close to "the primordial darkness first gives rise to awareness," and so on. The Tripiṭaka's own gate of existence, discussed earlier, has already broken through the non-Buddhist outsiders' own deviant conceptions entirely beforehand; the three gates of emptiness and the rest that follow break through relatively little further deviance; and the shared teaching's own skillful four gates break through the Tripiṭaka's own clumsiness; and the distinct teaching's own gate breaks through the shared teaching's own remaining proximity to it, no longer shared even with the two lesser vehicles — how much more so, then, could it possibly be confused with the non-Buddhist outsiders' own "primordial darkness" and their claim to "wondrous being"! Wondrous being, distinguished into its four gates by way of the tathāgatagarbha, how could it possibly be the same as the Nirgrantha's own "real nature"? It is like the uncut jade of Zhou and the uncut jade of Zheng — sharing an identical name, yet differing utterly in substance, as far apart as the noble and the base, heaven from earth. Students of the Dilun school in recent times, turning against the path and reverting to worldly ways, secretly borrow this very sense, stealthily seeking refuge in Zhuangzi and Laozi, mixing gold together with mere stone — causing, as a result, the deviant and the correct to become thoroughly confused, so that the blind and benighted can no longer tell one river's own waters from another's. If one grasps the true sense of these same four gates, distinguishing the true from the false with precision, no such theft can ever occur.

Yet, though the distinct teaching's own gate makes fixed, definite statements, and disputes of this kind arise, this is still the very domain of every buddha's own state, which the two lesser vehicles do not know — how, then, could it possibly be the same as the non-Buddhist outsiders?

The perfect teaching's own gate, empty, merged, and subtle, cannot be fixed or clung to at all: speaking of existence, it is never cut off from non-existence, discussing non-existence in terms of existence itself; speaking of non-existence, it is never cut off from existence, discussing existence in terms of non-existence itself. Existence and non-existence are not two, with no fixed, determinate mark at all, provisionally lodging itself within "existence" merely as a starting point for speech. And this very gate of existence is, moreover, itself the other three gates as well — one gate being immeasurable gates, immeasurable gates being one gate, neither one nor four, four being one and one being four — this, precisely, is the mark of the perfect teaching's own gate.

復次更約破會明融不融相。若破外道邪見，不破二乘邪曲，亦不破大乘方便。又會不圓者，如《淨名》中會凡夫反復，聲聞無也。會塵勞之儔為如來種，無為入正位，不能反復。生死惡人，煩惱惡法而皆被會；二乘善法，四果聖人而不被會。又般若中，明二乘所行，念處、道品，皆摩訶衍。貪欲、無明、見愛等，皆摩訶衍。善惡之法，悉皆被會。亦不會惡人及二乘人等，不辨其作佛，此即別門攝也。

若圓破者，從別教已去，皆是方便。故迦葉自破云：自此之前，我等皆名邪見人也。既言邪見之人，即無圓正道法，則人法俱被破也。別教人法尚爾，何況草庵人法！二乘尚爾，何況凡夫人法！是則圓破，無所固留。

圓會者，會諸凡夫著法之眾，「汝等皆當作佛」，「我不敢輕於汝等」。五逆調達亦與受記，龍畜等亦與受記，況二乘菩薩等！「世間治生產業，皆與實相不相違背」，即會一切惡法也。「汝等所行是菩薩道」，析法二乘尚被會，況通況別！「汝是我子，我則是父」，無有人法而不被會，俱皆融妙，此即圓門攝也。

Again, further clarifying merged or unmerged in terms of breaking-through and gathering-together. If it breaks through the non-Buddhist outsiders' own deviant views, but does not break through the two lesser vehicles' own deviant crookedness, nor break through the Mahāyāna's own skillful means, and if the gathering-together is not perfect — as when, within the Vimalakīrti Sūtra, the ordinary being caught in reversal and repetition is gathered in, while the śrāvaka is not; the companions of worldly toil and affliction are gathered in as the Tathāgata's own seed, yet the unconditioned who have already entered the correct stage cannot themselves fall into reversal and repetition; the evil person of birth-and-death, afflicted with every evil dharma, is, alike, gathered in, while the two lesser vehicles' own wholesome dharmas, the sages of the four fruits, are not gathered in. Again, within the Perfection of Wisdom teachings, clarifying that the two lesser vehicles' own practice — the applications of mindfulness, the factors of the path — are all, alike, the Mahāyāna; craving and desire, ignorance, views and craving, and the rest, are all, alike, the Mahāyāna; wholesome and evil dharmas are, every one, gathered in. Yet the evil person and the two lesser vehicles' own persons and the rest are not gathered in, their own future buddhahood left undistinguished — this is precisely what belongs to the distinct teaching's own gate.

As for the perfect breaking-through: from the distinct teaching downward, all of it is merely skillful means. Hence Kāśyapa himself breaks through his own former position, saying: "Before this very moment, all of us were, alike, named persons of deviant view." Having already called them persons of deviant view, there is, then, no perfect and correct dharma of the path present in them at all — and hence both person and dharma alike are broken through. If the distinct teaching's own person and dharma are treated this way, how much more so the person and dharma of the thatched hut! If the two lesser vehicles are treated this way, how much more so the person and dharma of the ordinary being! This, then, is the perfect breaking-through, holding nothing back at all.

As for the perfect gathering-together: it gathers in every ordinary being's own multitude, still attached to dharmas — "every one of you shall, without exception, attain buddhahood," "I dare not look down upon any one of you." Even Devadatta, guilty of the five heinous deeds, is granted his own prediction; nāgas and beasts, too, are granted their own predictions — how much more so, then, the two lesser vehicles and bodhisattvas! "Every worldly occupation and productive undertaking is never at odds with the real mark itself" — this is gathering in every evil dharma. "What you yourselves have practiced is itself the bodhisattva path" — even the two lesser vehicles of the analytical dharma are, thereby, gathered in, how much more so the shared or the distinct teachings! "You are truly my own children, and I am your father" — there is no person and no dharma that is not, thereby, gathered in, all of it, alike, merged and made wondrous — this is precisely what belongs to the perfect teaching's own gate.

復次，更約經文前後明圓不圓相。若先明不融門，此說地前；後明不融門而言證融，此說向後。或先明證融門，此說向後；後明不融門，此說地前者，此皆別門攝。若先明融門證亦融，此說信後；後明證不融，此說住前。或先明證不融，此說住前；後明證融，此說信後者，此皆屬圓門攝(云云)。

Again, further clarifying perfect versus non-perfect in terms of the sequence of the sūtra's own text: if the unmerged gate is clarified first, this speaks of the stage before the bodhisattva grounds; and if the unmerged gate is clarified afterward, yet spoken of as having realized merging, this speaks of what comes later still. Or, if the gate of realized merging is clarified first, this speaks of what comes later still; and if the unmerged gate is clarified afterward, this speaks of the stage before the grounds — all such cases belong to the distinct teaching's own gate. If the merged gate is clarified first, its realization likewise merged, this speaks of what comes after the ten faiths; and if unmerged realization is clarified afterward, this speaks of the stage before the ten abidings. Or, if unmerged realization is clarified first, this speaks of the stage before the abidings; and if merged realization is clarified afterward, this speaks of what comes after the faiths — all such cases belong, instead, to the perfect teaching's own gate, and so on.

Reading note

The jade-of-Zhou-and-jade-of-Zheng image (identical name, wholly different substance) is this treatise's sharpest warning yet against a specific contemporary error: Dilun scholars mistaking the distinct teaching's own "wondrous being" for the non-Buddhist Sāṃkhya's "primordial darkness giving rise to awareness," simply because both use similarly grand-sounding language about a foundational reality. The break/gather distinction that follows gives real teeth to what "merged" and "unmerged" mean in practice: the distinct teaching's own gathering, however generous, still stops short of certain persons (the two lesser vehicles' own sages, the unconditioned already settled in the correct stage); the perfect teaching's own gathering, demonstrated through Devadatta's prediction and "every worldly occupation... is never at odds with the real mark," holds absolutely nothing back — the difference between the two teachings is not a difference in generosity of vocabulary but in whether any exception clause survives at all.

二、即法、不即法者，若說有為門，此有非生死有，出生死外，別論真善妙有。空門者，出二乘真外，別論畢竟空，乃至非有非無門亦如是。是為別四門相。若有為門，即生死之有是實相之有，一切法趣有，有即法界，出法界外，更無法可論；生死即涅槃，涅槃即生死，無二無別，舉有為門端耳；實具一切法，圓通無礙，是名有門。三門亦如是，此即生死之法，是圓四門相也。復次，即法有遍不遍，判圓別相，例前分別(云云)，約五住遍不遍。復次，即法不即法，或前或後，判別圓相，如前分別(云云)。

三、約佛智、非佛智者，若有為門，分別一切智，了達空法；分別道種智，照恒沙佛法。差別不同者，是菩薩智即別四門相也。若有為門，分別一切種智，五眼具足，圓照法界正遍知者，即諸佛之智，是圓四門相也。復次，別門說圓智，圓門說別智，或前或後，分別別、圓相，例如前(云云)。復次，別門證圓智，圓門證別智，或前或後，分別別、圓，如前分別(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, identical with the dharma or not identical with the dharma: if the gate of existence is taught such that this "existence" is not birth-and-death's own existence, but, departing beyond birth-and-death, discusses separately a true, good, wondrous being — and the gate of emptiness, departing beyond the two lesser vehicles' own truth, discusses separately an ultimate emptiness — and so on, likewise, for the gate of neither existence nor non-existence — this is the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates. If, as the gate of existence, birth-and-death's own existence is itself the real mark's own existence, every dharma tending toward existence, existence itself being the dharma-realm, with no further dharma to discuss beyond the dharma-realm at all; birth-and-death is itself nirvāṇa, and nirvāṇa is itself birth-and-death, with no duality and no distinction — the gate of existence is here raised merely as a starting point, yet in reality complete with every dharma, perfectly interpenetrating and unobstructed — this is named the gate of existence. The remaining three gates are exactly the same — this same dharma of birth-and-death is the mark of the perfect teaching's own four gates. Again, whether being identical with the dharma pervades fully or not pervades fully determines the perfect versus the distinct mark, following the same pattern of distinction as before, and so on, in terms of whether the five dwelling-grounds of affliction are pervaded fully or not. Again, whether identical with the dharma or not identical with the dharma comes first or comes later determines the distinct versus the perfect mark, exactly as distinguished before, and so on.

Third, in terms of the buddha's own wisdom versus wisdom that is not the buddha's own: if, as the gate of existence, one distinguishes the wisdom that knows all things, thoroughly penetrating the dharma of emptiness, and distinguishes the wisdom of the paths, illuminating every buddha-dharma numerous as the sands of the Ganges — differentiated and distinct from one another — this is the bodhisattva's own wisdom, and is the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates. If, as the gate of existence, one distinguishes the wisdom knowing all modes, complete with the five eyes, perfectly illuminating the dharma-realm's own correct and universal knowing — this is precisely every buddha's own wisdom, and is the mark of the perfect teaching's own four gates. Again, whether the distinct teaching's own gate teaches the perfect wisdom, or the perfect teaching's own gate teaches the distinct wisdom, coming first or coming later, distinguishing the distinct from the perfect mark, follows the same pattern as before, and so on. Again, whether the distinct teaching's own gate realizes the perfect wisdom, or the perfect teaching's own gate realizes the distinct wisdom, coming first or coming later, distinguishing the distinct from the perfect, follows the same distinction as before, and so on.

四、約次第、不次第者，若以有為門，依門修行，漸次階差，從微至著，不能一行中即無量行，乃至非空非有門亦如是，是別四門相。若以有為門，一切法趣有門，依門修行，亦一切行趣有行，一行無量行名為遍行，乃至非空非有門亦如是，是圓四門相。復次，別門圓行，圓門別行，或前或後，分別別圓，例如前(云云)。

五、約斷斷、不斷斷者，夫至理虛無，無明體性本自不有，何須智慧？解惑既無，安用圓別？《涅槃》云：「誰有智慧？誰有煩惱？」《淨名》曰：「婬、怒、癡性即是解脫」，又「不斷癡愛起於明脫」，此則不論斷不斷。《大經》云：「闇時無明，明時無闇。有智慧時，則無煩惱。」此用智慧斷煩惱也。

若別有門，多就定分割截，漸次斷除五住，即是思議智斷也，乃至三門亦如是，是為別四門相。若圓有門，解惑不二，多明不斷斷，五住皆不思議，即是不思議斷，乃至三門亦如是，是為圓四門相。

復次，圓門說斷，別門說不斷斷，或前或後，判別圓相者，例如前說(云云)。

Fourth, in terms of sequential or non-sequential: if, taking the gate of existence, one relies upon this gate to cultivate practice, gradually advancing through distinct stages, proceeding from the subtle to the manifest, unable, within a single practice, to already possess immeasurable practices at once — and so on, likewise, for the gate of neither emptiness nor existence — this is the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates. If, taking the gate of existence, every dharma tends toward the gate of existence, and, relying upon this gate to cultivate practice, every practice likewise tends toward the practice of existence, a single practice being immeasurable practices, named the pervading practice — and so on, likewise, for the gate of neither emptiness nor existence — this is the mark of the perfect teaching's own four gates. Again, whether the distinct teaching's own gate teaches the perfect practice, or the perfect teaching's own gate teaches the distinct practice, coming first or coming later, distinguishing the distinct from the perfect, follows the same pattern as before, and so on.

Fifth, in terms of severing-that-severs or severing-that-does-not-sever: the ultimate principle itself is empty and without substance, ignorance's own essential nature fundamentally never having existed at all — what need, then, is there for wisdom? Understanding and delusion both already being non-existent, what use could there possibly be for "perfect" or "distinct" at all? The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Who, then, possesses wisdom? Who, then, possesses affliction?" The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "The very nature of lust, anger, and delusion is itself liberation," and again, "without severing delusion and craving, illumination and liberation arise" — this, then, does not discuss severing or not-severing at all. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "When it is dark, there is ignorance; when it is light, there is no darkness. When there is wisdom, then there is no affliction" — this uses wisdom to sever affliction.

If, as the distinct teaching's own gate of existence, one for the most part fixes definite divisions and cuts them apart, gradually and sequentially severing away the five dwelling-grounds of affliction — this is precisely a discursive, conceivable severing through wisdom, and so on, likewise, for the remaining three gates — this is the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates. If, as the perfect teaching's own gate of existence, understanding and delusion are not two, for the most part clarifying severing-that-does-not-sever, the five dwelling-grounds of affliction all alike inconceivable — this is precisely an inconceivable severing, and so on, likewise, for the remaining three gates — this is the mark of the perfect teaching's own four gates.

Again, whether the perfect teaching's own gate teaches severing, or the distinct teaching's own gate teaches severing-that-does-not-sever, coming first or coming later, distinguishing the distinct from the perfect mark, follows the same pattern already explained, and so on.

六、約實位、非實位者，若有門明斷界內見思，判三十心位；斷界外見、思、無明，判十地位；等覺後心，斷無明盡；妙覺常果，累外無事，此乃他家之因，將為己家之果，皆方便非實位也。後三門大同小異，皆是別四門相。

Sixth, in terms of the real stage versus the non-real stage: if, as the gate of existence, one clarifies severing the views and thought within the three realms, distinguishing the stages of the thirty minds; severing the views, thought, and ignorance beyond the three realms, distinguishing the stages of the ten grounds; the final mind of equal enlightenment severing ignorance to its very exhaustion; wondrous enlightenment's own eternal fruit, entirely free of entanglement, with nothing further to do — this, however, takes another household's own cause as one's own household's own fruit, all of it merely skillful means, not the real stage at all. The remaining three gates are largely the same, with only minor differences — all of these, too, are the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates.

Reading note

Items two through six each isolate one further axis along which the distinct and perfect teachings diverge despite sharing the same starting vocabulary — separateness versus identity with birth-and-death itself, graded bodhisattva wisdom versus the buddha's own complete wisdom, sequential practice versus a single practice already containing every practice, severing that still discursively counts what it severs versus an inconceivable severing where understanding and delusion were never two, and a stage-ladder borrowed from elsewhere versus one genuinely one's own. The "another household's own cause taken as one's own household's own fruit" line closing item six is a pointed indictment: the distinct teaching's own final stages, however impressive-sounding, are, in Zhiyi's account, still borrowed furniture — genuine attainments, but not attainments that were ever really the distinct teaching's own to claim.

七、約果縱、果不縱者，若有為門，從門證果，三德縱橫：言法身本有，般若修成，解脫始滿。不但果德縱成，因亦局限。如地人云：初地具足檀波羅蜜，於餘非為不修，隨力隨分，檀滿初地，不通上地。餘法分有而不具足者，是義有餘，三門亦如是，是為別四門相。

若有為門，從門證果，三德具足，不縱不橫，亦因如是一法門具足一切法門，通至佛地。《華嚴》云：「從初一地具足諸地功德。」《大品》云：「初阿字具足四十一字功德。」三門亦如是，是為圓四門相。復次，別門說果不縱，圓門說果縱，或前或後，判圓別相，例前(云云)。

If, as the gate of existence, from the very first arousing of the intent, the three contemplations are practiced within a single mind, severing the afflictions within the three realms; perfectly subduing the ignorance beyond the three realms, distinguishing the stage of the ten faiths, advancing to arouse true wisdom; perfectly severing the views, thought, and ignorance beyond the three realms, distinguishing the stage of the forty minds; the final mind of equal enlightenment, ignorance forever exhausted; wondrous enlightenment, entirely free of entanglement — this is the ultimate, truly real stage, and so on, likewise, for the remaining three gates — this is named the mark of the perfect teaching's own four gates. Again, whether the distinct teaching's own gate teaches the real stage, or the perfect teaching's own gate teaches the non-real stage; whether the distinct teaching's own gate realizes the real stage, or the perfect teaching's own gate realizes the non-real stage; coming first or coming later — all of this follows the same distinction as before, and so on.

Seventh, in terms of the fruit permitted freely or the fruit not permitted freely: if, as the gate of existence, one realizes the fruit through this gate, the three virtues stand vertically and horizontally with respect to one another — saying that the Dharma-body is inherently already present, prajñā is accomplished only through cultivation, and liberation is completed only at the very end. Not only is the fruit's own virtue thus accomplished in a fixed sequence, the cause itself is likewise confined within its own limits. As the Dilun masters say: at the initial ground, the pāramitā of giving is already complete — this is not to say the remaining pāramitās are not cultivated at all, but that, according to one's own capacity and portion, giving alone reaches completion at the initial ground, not extending through to the higher grounds. That the remaining dharmas are only partially possessed, not yet complete, is a doctrine that still leaves something remaining — the remaining three gates are exactly the same — this is the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates.

If, as the gate of existence, one realizes the fruit through this gate, the three virtues are complete all at once, neither vertical nor horizontal, and the cause, too, is exactly the same — a single Dharma-gate already complete with every Dharma-gate, leading through directly to the ground of buddhahood itself. The Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "From the very first ground onward, the merit of every ground is already complete." The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra says: "The very first letter 'a' is already complete with the merit of all forty-one letters." The remaining three gates are exactly the same — this is the mark of the perfect teaching's own four gates. Again, whether the distinct teaching's own gate teaches the fruit as not permitted freely, or the perfect teaching's own gate teaches the fruit as permitted freely, coming first or coming later, distinguishing the perfect from the distinct mark, follows the same pattern as before, and so on.

八、約圓詮、不圓詮者，若有為門，門不圓融，或融一或融二：門前章，偏弄引；門中章，詮述不融不即菩薩智，乃至偏譬喻等；門後，還結不融不即等，三門亦如是，是為別四門相。若有為門，一門即三門：門前，圓弄引；門中，詮述融即佛智，乃至圓譬喻等；門後，結成融即等。三門亦如是，是為圓四門相。復次，別門詮圓，圓門詮別，或前或後，分別別圓之相，例前(云云)。

九、約問答者，若有門明義未辨圓、別，須尋問答覈徵，自見圓、別指趣，三門亦如是(云云)。

十、約譬喻者，諸門前後，或舉金銀寶物為譬，或舉如意日月為譬，或用別合，或用圓合，圓、別之相自顯(云云)。

今以十意玄覽眾經，圓、別兩門，朗然明矣。

Eighth, in terms of perfect exposition or non-perfect exposition: if, as the gate of existence, the gate itself is not perfectly merged, merging only one or two of the other gates — its opening section merely gestures one-sidedly toward what follows; its middle section expounds the unmerged, non-identical bodhisattva's own wisdom, even down to one-sided illustrations and the rest; its closing section, likewise, concludes with the unmerged, non-identical, and the rest — the remaining three gates are exactly the same — this is the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates. If, as the gate of existence, a single gate is itself the other three gates at once — its opening section gestures perfectly toward what follows; its middle section expounds the merged, identical buddha's own wisdom, even down to perfect illustrations and the rest; its closing section concludes by establishing the merged, identical, and the rest — the remaining three gates are exactly the same — this is the mark of the perfect teaching's own four gates. Again, whether the distinct teaching's own gate expounds the perfect, or the perfect teaching's own gate expounds the distinct, coming first or coming later, distinguishing the distinct from the perfect mark, follows the same pattern as before, and so on.

Ninth, in terms of questions and answers: if the sense clarified through the gate of existence has not yet distinguished perfect from distinct, one must seek out the questions and answers, examining and verifying, and thereby, of oneself, see the perfect and the distinct's own respective aims — the remaining three gates are exactly the same, and so on.

Tenth, in terms of illustration: throughout the various gates, from beginning to end, sometimes gold, silver, or precious treasure is raised as an illustration; sometimes the wish-fulfilling jewel, the sun, or the moon is raised as an illustration; sometimes matched to the distinct teaching's own sense, sometimes matched to the perfect teaching's own sense — the mark of the perfect and the distinct thereby reveals itself, and so on.

Here, by means of these same ten senses, surveying broadly through the various sūtras, the perfect and the distinct gates are thereby made brilliantly clear.

Again, in terms of the Five Flavors, distinguishing their relative number: the milk teaching has two kinds of fourfold gates; the cream teaching has one kind of fourfold gate; the curdled-butter teaching has four kinds of fourfold gates; the clarified-butter teaching has three kinds of fourfold gates; this present sūtra has one kind of fourfold gate, and so on.

As for this present sūtra's own ten senses: "contemplating every dharma as empty, as the real mark itself," decisively resolving the śrāvaka's own dharma, being the king of every sūtra, opening the gate of skillful means — this is merging the ordinary being, the lesser, and the great persons and dharmas together. "Every worldly occupation and productive undertaking is never at odds with the real mark itself" — this is precisely the hired servant who is, in fact, the elder's own son — this is the sense of being identical with the dharma. "Opening, showing, awakening, and entering the Buddha's own knowledge and vision" — what ought now to be accomplished is solely the Buddha's own wisdom — this is precisely the buddha's own wisdom. "Wearing the Tathāgata's own robe, sitting upon his own seat, entering his own room, and the rest" — this is precisely non-sequential practice, not severing the five desires and yet purifying every faculty. "Again, passing beyond five hundred yojanas" — this is precisely the sense of severing-that-does-not-sever. "The five ranks" and "the six purified faculties," "riding the jeweled vehicle and roaming the four directions" — this is precisely the real stage. "The Buddha himself abides in the Great Vehicle, adorned with the power of concentration and wisdom, and by means of this delivers sentient beings" — this is precisely the fruit made freely so. "With palms joined and a reverent mind, wishing to hear the complete and perfect path" — this is precisely the perfect exposition preceding the present buddha's own teaching-text. "The sense of the real mark of all dharmas has already been taught for your sake" — this is precisely the perfect exposition following the former buddha's own teaching-text. "Prajñākūṭa" and "the dragon girl" — question and answer revealing the perfect. "The wheel-turning king's own crown-jewel" and "this cart, high and broad" — both, alike, are perfect illustrations. The ten senses being thus complete, the perfect gate is made manifest!

融門四相，今當說。若言「佛之智慧微妙第一」，又云：「我以如來智慧觀彼久遠猶若今也」。智知妙法，有門也。一切法空，常寂滅相，終歸於空，空門也。諸法常無性，佛種從緣起，即亦空亦有門也。非如非異，非虛非實，雙非兩捨，即非空非有門也。

四相標門，十意簡別，故知此經，明圓四門也(云云)。

The merged gate's own fourfold mark shall now be stated. Where it says, "the Buddha's own wisdom is subtle and supreme," and again, "by means of the Tathāgata's own wisdom, I behold that vast, distant time as though it were the present" — wisdom knowing the wondrous dharma is the gate of existence. "Every dharma is empty, constantly bearing the mark of quiescent extinction, ultimately returning to emptiness" — this is the gate of emptiness. "Every dharma is constantly without any fixed nature of its own, the buddha's own seed arising from conditions" — this is the gate of both emptiness and existence. "Neither the same nor different, neither empty nor real, doubly negating and setting both aside at once" — this is the gate of neither emptiness nor existence.

The fourfold mark marking out the gates, the ten senses distinguishing them — one thereby knows that this present sūtra clarifies the perfect teaching's own four gates, and so on.

Reading note

This closes distinguishing the perfect gate (簡圓門) in its entirety. The Five Flavors correlation quietly makes a striking claim in passing — that the *Lotus Sūtra* alone, among the Five Flavors, needs only a single kind of fourfold gate, where the curdled-butter teaching (the vaipulya sūtras) requires all four kinds at once — and the ten proof-texts that follow are a small masterclass in how densely this treatise reads its own base text: a single phrase like "again, passing beyond five hundred yojanas" (from the conjured-city parable) is read as encoding, in miniature, the entire technical claim that the perfect teaching's severing of affliction does not proceed sequentially. The treatise continues with the perfect teaching's own contemplation that actually enters the real mark through this now fully distinguished gate.

The Contemplation That Enters the Real 明入實觀

二、明入實觀者，上已知四圓門，今依有門修觀，觀則為十(云云)。

對前十二思議之門名不思議境，不思議境即是一實四諦。謂生死苦諦，不可思議，即空、即假、即中。即空故，方便淨；即假故，圓淨；即中故，性淨；三淨一心中得名大涅槃。《淨名》曰：「一切眾生即大涅槃。」故名不可思議四諦也。不可復滅，此即生死之苦諦，是無作之滅，亦是集道也。煩惱集諦不可思議，即空、即假、即中。即空故，名一切智；即假故，名道種智；即中故，一切種智；三智一心中得名大般若。《淨名》曰：「一切眾生即菩提相。不可復得。」此即煩惱之集，而是無作道諦，亦是苦滅，故名不思議一實四諦也。亦是真善妙色，何者？生死即空故名真，生死即假故名善，生死即中故名妙，此名有門不可思議境也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the contemplation that enters the real: having already, above, come to know the perfect teaching's own four gates, one now, relying upon the gate of existence, cultivates contemplation, and this contemplation, too, is treated under ten senses, and so on.

Set against the twelve discursive, conceivable gates discussed previously, this is named the inconceivable object, and the inconceivable object is precisely the one real Four Noble Truths. That is: the truth of the suffering of birth-and-death, inconceivable, is at once empty, at once provisional, and at once the middle. Being at once empty, it is the purity of expedient means; being at once provisional, it is perfect purity; being at once the middle, it is the purity of nature — these three purities, attained within a single mind, are named great nirvāṇa. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says, "Every sentient being is itself great nirvāṇa" — hence this is named the inconceivable Four Noble Truths. Unable to be extinguished any further, this same truth of the suffering of birth-and-death is itself the unconditioned cessation, and is likewise the arising and the path. The truth of the arising of affliction, inconceivable, is at once empty, at once provisional, and at once the middle. Being at once empty, it is named the wisdom that knows all things; being at once provisional, it is named the wisdom of the paths; being at once the middle, it is the wisdom knowing all modes — these three kinds of wisdom, attained within a single mind, are named great prajñā. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says, "Every sentient being is itself the mark of bodhi, unable to be attained any further" — this same arising of affliction is itself the unconditioned truth of the path, and is likewise the cessation of suffering, hence this is named the inconceivable one real Four Noble Truths. This is likewise true, good, wondrous form. Why is this? Birth-and-death, being at once empty, is named "true"; birth-and-death, being at once provisional, is named "good"; birth-and-death, being at once the middle, is named "wondrous" — this is named the gate of existence's own inconceivable object.

二、發真正心者，一切眾生即大涅槃，云何顛倒？以樂為苦，即起大悲，興兩誓願：令未度者度，令未斷者斷。一切煩惱即是菩提，云何愚闇？以道為非道，即起大慈，興兩誓願：令未知者知，未得者得。無緣慈悲，清淨誓願，慈善根力，任運吸取一切眾生也。

三、安心者，既體解成就，發心具足，豈可臨池觀魚，不肯結網，裹糧束脚，安坐不行？修行之要，不出定慧。譬如陰陽調適，萬物秀實，雨旱不節，焦爛豈生？若兩輪均平，是乘能運，二翼具足，堪任飛升。體生死即涅槃名為定，達煩惱即菩提名為慧。於一心中，巧修定慧，具足一切行也。

Second, arousing the true and correct aspiration: every sentient being is itself great nirvāṇa — how, then, could there be such inverted confusion, taking bliss for suffering? One thereby gives rise to great compassion, raising up two vows: to deliver those not yet delivered, to sever what is not yet severed for them. Every affliction is itself bodhi — how, then, could there be such foolish darkness, taking the path for what is not the path? One thereby gives rise to great loving-kindness, raising up two further vows: to cause those who do not yet know to know, and those who have not yet attained to attain. Loving-kindness and compassion with no object, vows pure and undefiled, the power of this wholesome root of loving-kindness spontaneously draws in and gathers every sentient being.

Third, settling the mind: having already embodied and understood this fully, the aspiration already complete, how could one simply stand at the pond's edge admiring the fish, unwilling to knot a net, or pack provisions and bind one's own legs, sitting at ease without ever setting out? The essential point of cultivating practice lies entirely in concentration and wisdom. It is comparable to yin and yang harmoniously adjusted, so that the ten thousand things flourish and bear fruit — should rain and drought fall out of proper measure, how could scorching and rot possibly fail to arise? If a cart's two wheels are evenly balanced, this same vehicle is able to travel; with both wings complete, one is fit to soar in flight. Embodying birth-and-death as itself nirvāṇa is named concentration; penetrating affliction as itself bodhi is named wisdom. Within a single mind, skillfully cultivating concentration and wisdom, one is thereby complete with every practice.

四、破法遍者，以此妙慧，如金剛斧，所擬皆碎；如無翳日，所臨皆朗。若生死即涅槃者，分段、變易，苦諦皆破；若煩惱即菩提者，四住五住，集諦皆破。雖復能破，亦不有所破。何者？生死即涅槃，故無所破也。

五、識通塞者，如主兵寶，取捨得宜，強者綏之，弱者撫之。知生死過患名為塞，即涅槃名為通；煩惱惱亂名為塞，即是菩提名為通。始從外道四見，乃至圓教四門，皆識通塞；節節執著即是塞，節節亡妙名為通。若不識諸法夷嶮，非但行法不前，亦亡去重寶也。

Fourth, thoroughly breaking through the dharma: by means of this same wondrous wisdom, like a vajra axe, whatever it is aimed at is shattered; like the sun unclouded by any haze, whatever it shines upon is made bright. If birth-and-death is itself nirvāṇa, both segmented birth-and-death and transformational birth-and-death — the truth of suffering — are, alike, broken through; if affliction is itself bodhi, both the four and the fifth dwelling-grounds of affliction — the truth of arising — are, alike, broken through. Yet, though it is thus able to break through, there is, in reality, nothing that is broken through at all. Why is this? Because birth-and-death is itself nirvāṇa, there is, in truth, nothing to break through.

Fifth, recognizing passage and obstruction: it is like the treasured general in command of an army, taking and relinquishing exactly as fitting, pacifying the strong and comforting the weak. Knowing birth-and-death's own faults and afflictions is named obstruction; knowing it as itself nirvāṇa is named passage. Affliction's own turmoil and disturbance is named obstruction; knowing it as itself bodhi is named passage. From the very beginning, with the non-Buddhist outsiders' own four views, all the way to the perfect teaching's own four gates, one must recognize passage and obstruction throughout — clinging at every single node is itself obstruction; losing oneself in the wondrous at every single node is named passage. If one fails to recognize the smooth and the perilous within every dharma, not only does one's own practice of the path fail to advance, one also loses the very treasure one carries.

六、善識道品者，觀生死即涅槃，十界生死色陰皆非淨非不淨，乃至識陰非常非不常。能破八顛倒，即法性四念處，念處中具道品、三解脫及一切法。又知涅槃即生死，顯四枯樹；知生死即涅槃，顯四榮樹；知生死涅槃不二，即一實諦，非枯非榮，住大涅槃也。

七、善修對治者，若正道多障，應須助道，觀生死即涅槃，治報障也；觀煩惱即菩提，治業障煩惱障也。

Sixth, skillfully recognizing the factors of the path: contemplating birth-and-death as itself nirvāṇa, the ten dharma-realms' own birth-and-death, the aggregate of form, is neither pure nor impure, even down to the aggregate of consciousness being neither permanent nor impermanent. Being able to break through the eight inverted views, this is precisely the dharma-nature's own four applications of mindfulness, within which the factors of the path, the three liberations, and every dharma are complete. Again, knowing nirvāṇa as itself birth-and-death manifests the four withered trees; knowing birth-and-death as itself nirvāṇa manifests the four flourishing trees; knowing birth-and-death and nirvāṇa as not two is precisely the one real truth, neither withered nor flourishing, abiding in great nirvāṇa.

Seventh, skillfully cultivating the antidote: if the correct path meets with many obstructions, one ought to rely upon the auxiliary path — contemplating birth-and-death as itself nirvāṇa remedies the obstruction of retribution; contemplating affliction as itself bodhi remedies the obstruction of karma and the obstruction of affliction.

Eighth, skillfully knowing the sequence of stages: the dharma of birth-and-death, fundamentally already itself nirvāṇa, is nirvāṇa in principle. Understanding and knowing birth-and-death as itself nirvāṇa is nirvāṇa in name. Diligently contemplating birth-and-death as itself nirvāṇa is nirvāṇa in contemplative practice. Wholesome roots and merit arising is nirvāṇa in resemblance. True and real wisdom arising is nirvāṇa in a portion of the truth. Reaching the very bottom of birth-and-death is nirvāṇa in the ultimate. Contemplating affliction as itself bodhi follows exactly this same pattern.

九、善安忍者，能安內外強軟遮障，不壞觀心。若觀生死即涅槃，不為陰、入、境、病患、業、魔、禪、二乘、菩薩等境所動壞也。若觀煩惱即菩提，不為諸見、增上慢境所動壞也。

十、無法愛者，既過障難，道根成立，諸功德生。觀生死即涅槃，故諸禪三昧功德生。觀煩惱即菩提故，諸陀羅尼無畏不共諸般若生。觀生死涅槃不二，故法身實相生。相似功德順理而生，喜起順道法愛、生名愛法，不上不退，名為頂墮。此愛若起，即當疾滅；愛若滅已，破無明，開佛知見，證實相體。觀生死即涅槃，故證得解脫；煩惱即菩提，故證得般若；此二不二，證得法身。一身無量身，無上寶聚，如意圓珠，眾法具足。是名有門入實，證得經體。餘三門亦如是。

Ninth, skillfully remaining patient: able to remain settled before every inward and outward obstruction, whether forceful or subtle, without one's own contemplating mind being destroyed. If one contemplates birth-and-death as itself nirvāṇa, one is not moved or destroyed by the objects of the aggregates, the sense-fields, meditative states, illness, karma, demons, dhyāna, the two lesser vehicles, or the bodhisattva. If one contemplates affliction as itself bodhi, one is not moved or destroyed by the objects of every view or of overweening pride.

Tenth, no attachment to the Dharma: having already passed through every obstruction and difficulty, the root of the path is thereby established, and every merit arises. Contemplating birth-and-death as itself nirvāṇa, the merit of every dhyāna and samādhi thereby arises. Contemplating affliction as itself bodhi, every dhāraṇī, fearlessness, and the prajñās unshared with others thereby arise. Contemplating birth-and-death and nirvāṇa as not two, the Dharma-body's own real mark thereby arises. Merit of resemblance, arising in accordance with principle, gives rise to joy, and, following this same joy, an attachment to the Dharma of the path may arise, named "attachment to the Dharma" — neither advancing nor retreating, this is named "falling at the summit." Should this attachment arise, it ought to be swiftly extinguished; once this attachment is extinguished, ignorance is broken through, the Buddha's own knowledge and vision is opened, and the essence of the real mark is realized. Contemplating birth-and-death as itself nirvāṇa, liberation is thereby realized; affliction as itself bodhi, prajñā is thereby realized; these two, being not two, the Dharma-body is thereby realized. A single body that is also immeasurable bodies, an unsurpassed heap of treasure, the perfect wish-fulfilling jewel, every dharma complete — this is named entering the real through the gate of existence, realizing the sūtra's own essence. The remaining three gates are exactly the same.

Reading note

Set against the Tripiṭaka and distinct teachings' own versions of this same ten-item scheme, every slot here is filled by the identity of opposites rather than their sequential reconciliation: the object contemplated is birth-and-death already being nirvāṇa, not birth-and-death leading toward nirvāṇa; breaking through the dharma finds "nothing that is broken through at all," since there was never a real division to begin with; and the closing realization is not attained after severing affliction but through recognizing affliction as bodhi directly. The "falling at the summit" warning is worth noting precisely because it appears here too, in the perfect teaching's own supposedly non-sequential path: even recognizing birth-and-death as nirvāṇa can itself become an object of attachment, "neither advancing nor retreating," and must be swiftly relinquished like every attainment before it — the perfect teaching removes every other obstacle but not this last, most subtle one.

The Tripiṭaka's own four gates, meeting different faculties with differing teachings, are accordingly spoken of as four; being alike the Buddha's own teaching, they are spoken of as one. Each gate's own skillful means differs, hence spoken of as four; all alike tending toward nirvāṇa, hence spoken of as one. What is led through follows from the agent that leads through, hence spoken of as four; the agent that leads through converges upon what is led through, hence spoken of as one. That there is no bodhi within the written word itself is narrowness discussed in terms of the teaching; it is comparable to a narrow path that admits no two persons walking abreast — this is narrowness discussed in terms of practice. That the teaching-gate and the practice-gate both find the truth difficult to encounter is narrowness discussed in terms of principle, and so on.

The shared teaching is exactly the same: meeting different conditions with differing teachings, hence four; being alike the Buddha's own teaching, hence one. The methods of contemplation differ, hence four; all alike tending toward no-generation, hence one. What is led through follows the agent, hence four; the agent follows what is led through, hence one. The shared teaching, being real precisely within phenomena themselves, has bodhi already present within the written word itself — contemplating good and evil alike, neither can be found — this is precisely "walking abreast," and narrowness is not discussed in these terms here; it is only that the teaching-gate and the contemplation-gate find the real principle difficult to encounter, and hence principle alone is named narrow, and so on.

The distinct teaching's own four gates are exactly the same: taught for the sake of four kinds of faculty, hence four; being alike the Buddha's own teaching, hence one. The contemplation entering the real differs, hence four; all alike tending toward the one real, hence one. What is led through follows the agent, hence four; the agent converges upon what is led through, hence one. The perfect teaching's own four gates are exactly the same: meeting four kinds of faculty, hence four; all alike the Buddha's own word, hence one. The contemplation entering the real differs, hence four; the four contemplations all tend toward the real, hence one. Reading note

The agent/object-led-through analysis makes a point this treatise has been building toward since the sixteen-gate scheme first appeared: a "wondrous" method (the shared teaching's own skillful embodying gate) can still lead only to a coarse destination (the three vehicles' own one-sided realization), while a "coarse" method (the distinct teaching's own laborious, sequential teaching) can lead all the way to the wondrous perfect real — skillfulness of the door and value of the destination are two independent variables, not one. The Q&A's resolution of "narrow and small" is a small masterclass in this treatise's own reading method: rather than deciding once whether a single scriptural phrase means "coarse" or "wondrous," it shows the same words apply at a different level in each of the Four Teachings — narrowness of teaching, of practice, or, for the perfect teaching alone, narrowness of principle itself, since even the perfect teaching still finds ultimate principle "difficult to encounter."

The kālakā fruit's own portion amounts to nine parts, while the tinduka fruit's own portion amounts to but a single part. 圓教四門者，則皆妙無麁。何者？有門為法界，攝一切法不可思議，即是一切法，況復三門！空門即是法界，攝一切法，況復三門！餘二亦如是，法相平等，無復優劣。若爾則無四門之異，但因順根機，赴緣四說，如四指指一月，月一指四。何者？此由眾生世世習此四門，因以成性，昔四門中推理，欲翻無明，即成慧根性。

As for the perfect teaching's own four gates, all alike are wondrous, without any coarseness at all. Why is this? The gate of existence is itself the dharma-realm, gathering in every dharma inconceivably, being itself every dharma — how much more so, then, the remaining three gates! The gate of emptiness is itself the dharma-realm, gathering in every dharma — how much more so, then, the remaining three gates! The remaining two are exactly the same, the mark of every dharma equal throughout, with no superiority or inferiority remaining at all. If this is so, then there is, in fact, no difference among the four gates at all — it is only that, according with each faculty, meeting differing conditions, four accounts are given, like four fingers pointing at a single moon, the moon being one and the pointing fingers four. Why is this? It is because sentient beings, life after life, have become habituated to these same four gates, and, through this habituation, their own natures have formed accordingly — formerly, within the four gates, reasoning to overturn ignorance, this became the nature of wisdom-faculty.

Formerly, within the four gates, cultivating good to overturn evil karma, this became the nature of merit-faculty; the causes and conditions of merit and wisdom draw forth, in the present, name-and-form, contact, and sensation, each, according to its own former habituation, giving rise to craving and grasping — these are the ten dharmas that form the sentient being of perfect nature. Their desires and delights differing, the remedy fitting to each differs accordingly; the Buddha's own wisdom, clearly discerning, illuminates each faculty without any error. By means of the mundane siddhānta, meeting the four kinds of nature and desire, these same four gates are taught; by means of the individual siddhānta, the four kinds of good are given rise to; by means of the therapeutic siddhānta, their own four kinds of clinging are remedied; by means of the siddhānta of ultimate meaning, all four kinds of persons are caused to see the principle. Without these four causes and conditions present, the Buddha does not teach the Dharma at all. Since the conditions are thus not uniform, they are briefly stated as four, all of them being the correct and direct setting-aside of skillful means, teaching solely the unsurpassed path. The gates' own mark perfectly interpenetrating, all four gates alike are wondrous.

Reading note

The tinduka/kālakā fruit image — two fruits so alike in appearance that nine parts poison to one part medicine still look the same to the eye — gives the "success or failure" analysis its most vivid form: none of the ten items in the contemplation scheme are individually optional, and a practice that gets nine of ten right is still, on balance, coarse rather than wondrous, "a thousand catties of folded flowers" never equaling "a single tael of genuine gold." The perfect teaching's own declaration that all four gates are simply wondrous — "like four fingers pointing at a single moon" — resolves the entire sixteen-gate architecture's own deepest tension: distinctions of faculty and disposition are real and must be met differently, yet the reality they are all pointing toward was never actually fourfold to begin with.

Fourth, opening the coarse gates to reveal the wondrous gates.

Question: The Mūlamadhyamakakārikā first clarifies the Mahāyāna's own gate, and only afterward clarifies the two lesser vehicles' own gate. Why, here, is the lesser gate clarified first, and the great gate clarified only afterward?

Answer: The Mūlamadhyamakakārikā addresses the people of its own time, whose affliction lay in views already fixed and formed — hence it first sweeps these away with the great teaching, and only afterward shows the gate that enters the truth. This present sūtra no longer addresses the affliction of fixed views — its hearers merely dwell within the thatched hut — and so it must first open the gate of skillful means, showing the perfect real mark; hence the lesser gate is listed first, and the great gate clarified only afterward. Opening and breaking-through, each suited to its own proper occasion, each carries its own particular excellence.

As for the Lotus Sūtra's own teaching after this point, there is no need for any further opening; as for the Lotus Sūtra's own teaching before this point, wherever gate and principle have already entered the wondrous, what further remains to be opened? Or, though gate and principle may already be wondrous, the person may not yet be wondrous. Where gate and principle are already wondrous, no further opening is needed; where gate, or principle, or the person themselves is not yet wondrous, this must now be opened. That is: opening every craving, view, and affliction as itself bodhi — hence it says, "contemplating every dharma as empty, as the real mark itself." Opening every instance of birth-and-death as itself nirvāṇa — hence it says, "the mark of the world constantly abides." Opening every ordinary person as itself a wondrous person — hence it says, "every sentient being is, without exception, my own child." Opening every teaching born of craving and views as itself the Buddha-dharma — hence it says, "whether expounding worldly scriptures, or worldly occupation and productive undertaking, all of it is never at odds with the real mark itself." Opening every sentient being as itself the wondrous principle — hence it says, "in order to cause sentient beings to open the Buddha's own knowledge and vision" — showing, awakening, and entering are exactly the same. Opening every Hīnayāna dharma as itself the wondrous dharma — hence it says, "decisively resolving the śrāvaka's own dharma, this being the king of every sūtra." Opening every śrāvaka teaching — hence it says, "the Buddha, formerly, before the bodhisattvas, disparaged the śrāvakas, and yet the Buddha, in reality, always taught and transformed by means of the Mahāyāna." Opening every śrāvaka practice as itself wondrous practice — hence it says, "what you yourselves have practiced is itself the bodhisattva path." Opening every śrāvaka principle as itself the wondrous principle — hence it says, "opening the gate of skillful means, showing the true and real mark." Opening every bodhisattva not yet brought to the wondrous, now, every one, attaining the perfect — hence it says, "the bodhisattvas, hearing this dharma, have had every net of doubt entirely removed."

The distinct teaching has its own single kind of bodhisattva; the Tripiṭaka, too, has its own single kind of bodhisattva; the shared teaching has its own single kind of bodhisattva — whatever has not yet been decisively resolved is, here, every one, opened and revealed. Reading note

The opening Q&A is a small piece of literary self-awareness: Zhiyi notices that his own treatise's sequence — lesser gate first, great gate second — inverts Nāgārjuna's own order in the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, and explains the inversion by the difference in audience affliction rather than any disagreement in doctrine: Nāgārjuna's readers clung to fixed views and needed the great teaching's sweeping negation first, while this sūtra's own hearers merely sit passively "within the thatched hut" and need the gentler invitation of skillful means opened for them before the great teaching can land. The eleven-fold list of what gets "opened" — craving, birth-and-death, the ordinary person, worldly speech itself, every śrāvaka's own teaching, practice, and principle — closing with even the distinct teaching's and shared teaching's own bodhisattvas needing this same resolution, is the most exhaustive single catalogue this treatise has given of what "opening the provisional to reveal the real" actually reaches. This closes opening the coarse gates to reveal the wondrous gates, and, with it, the gates that enter the real mark (入實相門) in its entirety — the fourth of the seven headings under Clarifying the Essence. The treatise continues with the fifth heading, the real mark as the essence of every sūtra (實相為諸經作體).
