The Perfect Teaching's Gate

Fascicle 9 opens with the perfect teaching's own gate into the real mark, first distinguishing it sharply from the distinct teaching's own gate — sharing identical vocabulary yet differing in ten separate ways — before giving its own contemplation, continuing Clarifying the Essence (顯體).

Status: in progress Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

Distinguishing the Perfect Gate 簡圓門

Chinese Source

次、明圓門入實觀者，先、簡圓門。次、明圓觀。

上三藏門，滅實色通真，不得意多諍；體門即幻色通真，示人無諍法；別門體滅生死色，次第滅法性色通中，不得意多諍；圓門即生死色是法性色，即法性色而通中，示人無諍法，故文云：「無上道」，又云：「而行深妙道」，即此義也。

上兩門不通中，不俟分別。別、圓兩種俱通中。論其同異略為十：一、融不融。二、即法不即法。三、明佛智非佛智。四、明次行不次行。五、明斷斷惑不斷斷惑。六、明實位不實位。七、果縱果不縱。八、圓詮不圓詮。九、約難問。十、約譬喻。尋此十意，明識八門同異也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, clarifying the perfect teaching's own gate for entering the real: first, distinguishing the perfect gate; next, clarifying the perfect contemplation.

The Tripiṭaka teaching's own gate, discussed above, extinguishes real form to lead through to the truth — failing to grasp this gives rise to much dispute; the embodying gate takes illusory form itself as leading through to the truth, showing people a dharma free of contention; the distinct teaching's own gate embodies the extinguishing of birth-and-death's own form, sequentially extinguishing the dharma-nature's own form to lead through to the middle — failing to grasp this, too, gives rise to much dispute; the perfect teaching's own gate takes birth-and-death's own form as itself the dharma-nature's own form, this same dharma-nature's own form itself leading through to the middle, showing people a dharma free of contention — hence the text says, "the unsurpassed path," and again, "yet practices the profound and wondrous path" — precisely this same sense.

The two gates discussed above do not lead through to the middle, and need no further distinguishing here. The distinct and perfect teachings' own two kinds both lead through to the middle. Discussing their sameness and difference, this is briefly treated under ten senses: first, merged or unmerged; second, identical with the dharma or not identical with the dharma; third, clarifying the buddha's own wisdom versus wisdom that is not the buddha's own; fourth, clarifying sequential practice versus non-sequential practice; fifth, clarifying severing-that-still-severs versus severing-that-does-not-sever the afflictions; sixth, clarifying the real stage versus the non-real stage; seventh, the fruit permitted freely versus the fruit not permitted freely; eighth, perfect exposition versus non-perfect exposition; ninth, in terms of difficult questions; tenth, in terms of illustration. Examining these same ten senses, the sameness and difference of the eight gates may be clearly recognized.

一、明融不融者，別教四門，所據決定妙有善色，不關於空；據畢竟空，不關於有，乃至非空非有門亦如是。四門歷別，當分各通。

不得意者，作定相取，似同性實，殆濫冥初生覺(云云)。前、三藏有門，已破外道邪計先盡；次、空等三門，破邪則少；又、通巧四門，破三藏之拙；又、別教之門，破通門之近，已不與二乘共，何況外道冥覺而濫妙有！妙有依如來藏分判四門，何得同彼尼犍性實？如周璞、鄭璞，名同質異，貴賤天懸。今時學地論人，反道還俗，竊以此義，偷安莊老，金石相糅，遂令邪正混淆，盲暝之徒不別涇渭。若得諸四門意，精簡真偽，偷盜不生。

然別門雖作定說，如是諍論，諸佛境界，二乘不知，豈同外道耶？

圓門虛融微妙，不可定執，說有不隔無，約有而論無；說無不隔有，約無而論有。有無不二，無決定相，假寄於有，以為言端。而此有門亦即三門，一門無量門，無量門一門，非一、非四，四一一四，此即圓門相也。

First, clarifying merged or unmerged: the distinct teaching's own four gates, in what they rely upon, fix wondrous being and good form as decisively determined, having nothing to do with emptiness; and fix ultimate emptiness as decisively determined, having nothing to do with existence — and so on, likewise, for the gate of neither emptiness nor existence. The four gates, taken sequentially and separately, each lead through only within its own particular division.

For one who fails to grasp this, taking up a fixed mark and clinging to it, this resembles the claim that "the same nature is the real," and comes dangerously close to "the primordial darkness first gives rise to awareness," and so on. The Tripiṭaka's own gate of existence, discussed earlier, has already broken through the non-Buddhist outsiders' own deviant conceptions entirely beforehand; the three gates of emptiness and the rest that follow break through relatively little further deviance; and the shared teaching's own skillful four gates break through the Tripiṭaka's own clumsiness; and the distinct teaching's own gate breaks through the shared teaching's own remaining proximity to it, no longer shared even with the two lesser vehicles — how much more so, then, could it possibly be confused with the non-Buddhist outsiders' own "primordial darkness" and their claim to "wondrous being"! Wondrous being, distinguished into its four gates by way of the tathāgatagarbha, how could it possibly be the same as the Nirgrantha's own "real nature"? It is like the uncut jade of Zhou and the uncut jade of Zheng — sharing an identical name, yet differing utterly in substance, as far apart as the noble and the base, heaven from earth. Students of the Dilun school in recent times, turning against the path and reverting to worldly ways, secretly borrow this very sense, stealthily seeking refuge in Zhuangzi and Laozi, mixing gold together with mere stone — causing, as a result, the deviant and the correct to become thoroughly confused, so that the blind and benighted can no longer tell one river's own waters from another's. If one grasps the true sense of these same four gates, distinguishing the true from the false with precision, no such theft can ever occur.

Yet, though the distinct teaching's own gate makes fixed, definite statements, and disputes of this kind arise, this is still the very domain of every buddha's own state, which the two lesser vehicles do not know — how, then, could it possibly be the same as the non-Buddhist outsiders?

The perfect teaching's own gate, empty, merged, and subtle, cannot be fixed or clung to at all: speaking of existence, it is never cut off from non-existence, discussing non-existence in terms of existence itself; speaking of non-existence, it is never cut off from existence, discussing existence in terms of non-existence itself. Existence and non-existence are not two, with no fixed, determinate mark at all, provisionally lodging itself within "existence" merely as a starting point for speech. And this very gate of existence is, moreover, itself the other three gates as well — one gate being immeasurable gates, immeasurable gates being one gate, neither one nor four, four being one and one being four — this, precisely, is the mark of the perfect teaching's own gate.

復次更約破會明融不融相。若破外道邪見，不破二乘邪曲，亦不破大乘方便。又會不圓者，如《淨名》中會凡夫反復，聲聞無也。會塵勞之儔為如來種，無為入正位，不能反復。生死惡人，煩惱惡法而皆被會；二乘善法，四果聖人而不被會。又般若中，明二乘所行，念處、道品，皆摩訶衍。貪欲、無明、見愛等，皆摩訶衍。善惡之法，悉皆被會。亦不會惡人及二乘人等，不辨其作佛，此即別門攝也。

若圓破者，從別教已去，皆是方便。故迦葉自破云：自此之前，我等皆名邪見人也。既言邪見之人，即無圓正道法，則人法俱被破也。別教人法尚爾，何況草庵人法！二乘尚爾，何況凡夫人法！是則圓破，無所固留。

圓會者，會諸凡夫著法之眾，「汝等皆當作佛」，「我不敢輕於汝等」。五逆調達亦與受記，龍畜等亦與受記，況二乘菩薩等！「世間治生產業，皆與實相不相違背」，即會一切惡法也。「汝等所行是菩薩道」，析法二乘尚被會，況通況別！「汝是我子，我則是父」，無有人法而不被會，俱皆融妙，此即圓門攝也。

Again, further clarifying merged or unmerged in terms of breaking-through and gathering-together. If it breaks through the non-Buddhist outsiders' own deviant views, but does not break through the two lesser vehicles' own deviant crookedness, nor break through the Mahāyāna's own skillful means, and if the gathering-together is not perfect — as when, within the Vimalakīrti Sūtra, the ordinary being caught in reversal and repetition is gathered in, while the śrāvaka is not; the companions of worldly toil and affliction are gathered in as the Tathāgata's own seed, yet the unconditioned who have already entered the correct stage cannot themselves fall into reversal and repetition; the evil person of birth-and-death, afflicted with every evil dharma, is, alike, gathered in, while the two lesser vehicles' own wholesome dharmas, the sages of the four fruits, are not gathered in. Again, within the Perfection of Wisdom teachings, clarifying that the two lesser vehicles' own practice — the applications of mindfulness, the factors of the path — are all, alike, the Mahāyāna; craving and desire, ignorance, views and craving, and the rest, are all, alike, the Mahāyāna; wholesome and evil dharmas are, every one, gathered in. Yet the evil person and the two lesser vehicles' own persons and the rest are not gathered in, their own future buddhahood left undistinguished — this is precisely what belongs to the distinct teaching's own gate.

As for the perfect breaking-through: from the distinct teaching downward, all of it is merely skillful means. Hence Kāśyapa himself breaks through his own former position, saying: "Before this very moment, all of us were, alike, named persons of deviant view." Having already called them persons of deviant view, there is, then, no perfect and correct dharma of the path present in them at all — and hence both person and dharma alike are broken through. If the distinct teaching's own person and dharma are treated this way, how much more so the person and dharma of the thatched hut! If the two lesser vehicles are treated this way, how much more so the person and dharma of the ordinary being! This, then, is the perfect breaking-through, holding nothing back at all.

As for the perfect gathering-together: it gathers in every ordinary being's own multitude, still attached to dharmas — "every one of you shall, without exception, attain buddhahood," "I dare not look down upon any one of you." Even Devadatta, guilty of the five heinous deeds, is granted his own prediction; nāgas and beasts, too, are granted their own predictions — how much more so, then, the two lesser vehicles and bodhisattvas! "Every worldly occupation and productive undertaking is never at odds with the real mark itself" — this is gathering in every evil dharma. "What you yourselves have practiced is itself the bodhisattva path" — even the two lesser vehicles of the analytical dharma are, thereby, gathered in, how much more so the shared or the distinct teachings! "You are truly my own children, and I am your father" — there is no person and no dharma that is not, thereby, gathered in, all of it, alike, merged and made wondrous — this is precisely what belongs to the perfect teaching's own gate.

復次，更約經文前後明圓不圓相。若先明不融門，此說地前；後明不融門而言證融，此說向後。或先明證融門，此說向後；後明不融門，此說地前者，此皆別門攝。若先明融門證亦融，此說信後；後明證不融，此說住前。或先明證不融，此說住前；後明證融，此說信後者，此皆屬圓門攝(云云)。

Again, further clarifying perfect versus non-perfect in terms of the sequence of the sūtra's own text: if the unmerged gate is clarified first, this speaks of the stage before the bodhisattva grounds; and if the unmerged gate is clarified afterward, yet spoken of as having realized merging, this speaks of what comes later still. Or, if the gate of realized merging is clarified first, this speaks of what comes later still; and if the unmerged gate is clarified afterward, this speaks of the stage before the grounds — all such cases belong to the distinct teaching's own gate. If the merged gate is clarified first, its realization likewise merged, this speaks of what comes after the ten faiths; and if unmerged realization is clarified afterward, this speaks of the stage before the ten abidings. Or, if unmerged realization is clarified first, this speaks of the stage before the abidings; and if merged realization is clarified afterward, this speaks of what comes after the faiths — all such cases belong, instead, to the perfect teaching's own gate, and so on.

Reading note

The jade-of-Zhou-and-jade-of-Zheng image (identical name, wholly different substance) is this treatise's sharpest warning yet against a specific contemporary error: Dilun scholars mistaking the distinct teaching's own "wondrous being" for the non-Buddhist Sāṃkhya's "primordial darkness giving rise to awareness," simply because both use similarly grand-sounding language about a foundational reality. The break/gather distinction that follows gives real teeth to what "merged" and "unmerged" mean in practice: the distinct teaching's own gathering, however generous, still stops short of certain persons (the two lesser vehicles' own sages, the unconditioned already settled in the correct stage); the perfect teaching's own gathering, demonstrated through Devadatta's prediction and "every worldly occupation... is never at odds with the real mark," holds absolutely nothing back — the difference between the two teachings is not a difference in generosity of vocabulary but in whether any exception clause survives at all.

Chinese Source

二、即法、不即法者，若說有為門，此有非生死有，出生死外，別論真善妙有。空門者，出二乘真外，別論畢竟空，乃至非有非無門亦如是。是為別四門相。若有為門，即生死之有是實相之有，一切法趣有，有即法界，出法界外，更無法可論；生死即涅槃，涅槃即生死，無二無別，舉有為門端耳；實具一切法，圓通無礙，是名有門。三門亦如是，此即生死之法，是圓四門相也。復次，即法有遍不遍，判圓別相，例前分別(云云)，約五住遍不遍。復次，即法不即法，或前或後，判別圓相，如前分別(云云)。

三、約佛智、非佛智者，若有為門，分別一切智，了達空法；分別道種智，照恒沙佛法。差別不同者，是菩薩智即別四門相也。若有為門，分別一切種智，五眼具足，圓照法界正遍知者，即諸佛之智，是圓四門相也。復次，別門說圓智，圓門說別智，或前或後，分別別、圓相，例如前(云云)。復次，別門證圓智，圓門證別智，或前或後，分別別、圓，如前分別(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, identical with the dharma or not identical with the dharma: if the gate of existence is taught such that this "existence" is not birth-and-death's own existence, but, departing beyond birth-and-death, discusses separately a true, good, wondrous being — and the gate of emptiness, departing beyond the two lesser vehicles' own truth, discusses separately an ultimate emptiness — and so on, likewise, for the gate of neither existence nor non-existence — this is the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates. If, as the gate of existence, birth-and-death's own existence is itself the real mark's own existence, every dharma tending toward existence, existence itself being the dharma-realm, with no further dharma to discuss beyond the dharma-realm at all; birth-and-death is itself nirvāṇa, and nirvāṇa is itself birth-and-death, with no duality and no distinction — the gate of existence is here raised merely as a starting point, yet in reality complete with every dharma, perfectly interpenetrating and unobstructed — this is named the gate of existence. The remaining three gates are exactly the same — this same dharma of birth-and-death is the mark of the perfect teaching's own four gates. Again, whether being identical with the dharma pervades fully or not pervades fully determines the perfect versus the distinct mark, following the same pattern of distinction as before, and so on, in terms of whether the five dwelling-grounds of affliction are pervaded fully or not. Again, whether identical with the dharma or not identical with the dharma comes first or comes later determines the distinct versus the perfect mark, exactly as distinguished before, and so on.

Third, in terms of the buddha's own wisdom versus wisdom that is not the buddha's own: if, as the gate of existence, one distinguishes the wisdom that knows all things, thoroughly penetrating the dharma of emptiness, and distinguishes the wisdom of the paths, illuminating every buddha-dharma numerous as the sands of the Ganges — differentiated and distinct from one another — this is the bodhisattva's own wisdom, and is the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates. If, as the gate of existence, one distinguishes the wisdom knowing all modes, complete with the five eyes, perfectly illuminating the dharma-realm's own correct and universal knowing — this is precisely every buddha's own wisdom, and is the mark of the perfect teaching's own four gates. Again, whether the distinct teaching's own gate teaches the perfect wisdom, or the perfect teaching's own gate teaches the distinct wisdom, coming first or coming later, distinguishing the distinct from the perfect mark, follows the same pattern as before, and so on. Again, whether the distinct teaching's own gate realizes the perfect wisdom, or the perfect teaching's own gate realizes the distinct wisdom, coming first or coming later, distinguishing the distinct from the perfect, follows the same distinction as before, and so on.

四、約次第、不次第者，若以有為門，依門修行，漸次階差，從微至著，不能一行中即無量行，乃至非空非有門亦如是，是別四門相。若以有為門，一切法趣有門，依門修行，亦一切行趣有行，一行無量行名為遍行，乃至非空非有門亦如是，是圓四門相。復次，別門圓行，圓門別行，或前或後，分別別圓，例如前(云云)。

五、約斷斷、不斷斷者，夫至理虛無，無明體性本自不有，何須智慧？解惑既無，安用圓別？《涅槃》云：「誰有智慧？誰有煩惱？」《淨名》曰：「婬、怒、癡性即是解脫」，又「不斷癡愛起於明脫」，此則不論斷不斷。《大經》云：「闇時無明，明時無闇。有智慧時，則無煩惱。」此用智慧斷煩惱也。

若別有門，多就定分割截，漸次斷除五住，即是思議智斷也，乃至三門亦如是，是為別四門相。若圓有門，解惑不二，多明不斷斷，五住皆不思議，即是不思議斷，乃至三門亦如是，是為圓四門相。

復次，圓門說斷，別門說不斷斷，或前或後，判別圓相者，例如前說(云云)。

Fourth, in terms of sequential or non-sequential: if, taking the gate of existence, one relies upon this gate to cultivate practice, gradually advancing through distinct stages, proceeding from the subtle to the manifest, unable, within a single practice, to already possess immeasurable practices at once — and so on, likewise, for the gate of neither emptiness nor existence — this is the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates. If, taking the gate of existence, every dharma tends toward the gate of existence, and, relying upon this gate to cultivate practice, every practice likewise tends toward the practice of existence, a single practice being immeasurable practices, named the pervading practice — and so on, likewise, for the gate of neither emptiness nor existence — this is the mark of the perfect teaching's own four gates. Again, whether the distinct teaching's own gate teaches the perfect practice, or the perfect teaching's own gate teaches the distinct practice, coming first or coming later, distinguishing the distinct from the perfect, follows the same pattern as before, and so on.

Fifth, in terms of severing-that-severs or severing-that-does-not-sever: the ultimate principle itself is empty and without substance, ignorance's own essential nature fundamentally never having existed at all — what need, then, is there for wisdom? Understanding and delusion both already being non-existent, what use could there possibly be for "perfect" or "distinct" at all? The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Who, then, possesses wisdom? Who, then, possesses affliction?" The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "The very nature of lust, anger, and delusion is itself liberation," and again, "without severing delusion and craving, illumination and liberation arise" — this, then, does not discuss severing or not-severing at all. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "When it is dark, there is ignorance; when it is light, there is no darkness. When there is wisdom, then there is no affliction" — this uses wisdom to sever affliction.

If, as the distinct teaching's own gate of existence, one for the most part fixes definite divisions and cuts them apart, gradually and sequentially severing away the five dwelling-grounds of affliction — this is precisely a discursive, conceivable severing through wisdom, and so on, likewise, for the remaining three gates — this is the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates. If, as the perfect teaching's own gate of existence, understanding and delusion are not two, for the most part clarifying severing-that-does-not-sever, the five dwelling-grounds of affliction all alike inconceivable — this is precisely an inconceivable severing, and so on, likewise, for the remaining three gates — this is the mark of the perfect teaching's own four gates.

Again, whether the perfect teaching's own gate teaches severing, or the distinct teaching's own gate teaches severing-that-does-not-sever, coming first or coming later, distinguishing the distinct from the perfect mark, follows the same pattern already explained, and so on.

六、約實位、非實位者，若有門明斷界內見思，判三十心位；斷界外見、思、無明，判十地位；等覺後心，斷無明盡；妙覺常果，累外無事，此乃他家之因，將為己家之果，皆方便非實位也。後三門大同小異，皆是別四門相。

Sixth, in terms of the real stage versus the non-real stage: if, as the gate of existence, one clarifies severing the views and thought within the three realms, distinguishing the stages of the thirty minds; severing the views, thought, and ignorance beyond the three realms, distinguishing the stages of the ten grounds; the final mind of equal enlightenment severing ignorance to its very exhaustion; wondrous enlightenment's own eternal fruit, entirely free of entanglement, with nothing further to do — this, however, takes another household's own cause as one's own household's own fruit, all of it merely skillful means, not the real stage at all. The remaining three gates are largely the same, with only minor differences — all of these, too, are the mark of the distinct teaching's own four gates.

Reading note

Items two through six each isolate one further axis along which the distinct and perfect teachings diverge despite sharing the same starting vocabulary — separateness versus identity with birth-and-death itself, graded bodhisattva wisdom versus the buddha's own complete wisdom, sequential practice versus a single practice already containing every practice, severing that still discursively counts what it severs versus an inconceivable severing where understanding and delusion were never two, and a stage-ladder borrowed from elsewhere versus one genuinely one's own. The "another household's own cause taken as one's own household's own fruit" line closing item six is a pointed indictment: the distinct teaching's own final stages, however impressive-sounding, are, in Zhiyi's account, still borrowed furniture — genuine attainments, but not attainments that were ever really the distinct teaching's own to claim.
