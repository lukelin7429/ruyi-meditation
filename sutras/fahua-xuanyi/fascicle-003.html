How the Six Objects Combine

Fascicle 3 opens by cross-referencing all six objects of contemplation from Fascicle 2 against one another — the ten suchnesses, the fourfold dependent origination, the fourfold Four Noble Truths, the sevenfold two truths, the fivefold three truths, and the one truth — showing that each is only a different name for the same underlying structure, closing the wondrous of the object. The fascicle then turns to the second of the ten wondrous aspects, the wondrous of wisdom: a catalogue of twenty kinds of wisdom, sorted, defined, and matched back against every one of the six objects in turn.

Status: in progress Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

Opening and Combining the Six Objects: The Twelve Links 諸境開合

Chinese Source

天台智者大師說

二、諸境開合者，先用十如為首。何者？此經命章絕言稱歎十如。今更說五境，云何同異耶？十二因緣與十如開合者，名異故言開，義同故言合。無明支合如是性。行支合如是相。識、名色、六入、觸、受合如是體。愛合如是緣。取合如是力、作。有合如是因。生、老死合如是果、報等(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Expounded by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai.

Second, the opening and combining of the various objects: first, taking the ten suchnesses as the head. Why is this? Because this present sūtra, in naming this section, singles out the ten suchnesses alone for its unqualified praise. Now, having gone on to teach five further objects, how do they agree and differ with the ten suchnesses? As for the opening and combining of the twelve links and the ten suchnesses: since their names differ, they are said to be "opened"; since their sense is the same, they are said to be "combined." The link of ignorance combines with "such a nature." The link of volitional formation combines with "such a mark." Consciousness, name-and-form, the six sense-fields, contact, and sensation combine with "such an essence." Craving combines with "such a condition." Grasping combines with "such a power" and "such an activity." Becoming combines with "such a cause." Birth, and old age and death, combine with "such a fruit" and "such a retribution," and the rest, and so on.

又總合者，如是相合行、有兩支。如是性合無明、愛、取三支。如是體合識、名色乃至老死七支。如是力還是煩惱道三支，無明、愛、取能生業力。如是作，還是行、有二支，能為苦作業也。如是因還是行、有二支，為七苦作因也。如是緣還是無明、愛、取三支，能潤業取苦也。如是果還是行、有之習果也。如是報還是行、有之業，招名色等報。此兩番通用思議十二因緣。合六道十如是。

Again, combining them differently: "such a mark" combines with the two links of volitional formation and becoming. "Such a nature" combines with the three links of ignorance, craving, and grasping. "Such an essence" combines with the seven links of consciousness, name-and-form, down through old age and death. "Such a power" is, again, the three links of the path of affliction — ignorance, craving, and grasping, able to generate the power of karma. "Such an activity" is, again, the two links of volitional formation and becoming, able to construct the karma that produces suffering. "Such a cause" is, again, the two links of volitional formation and becoming, serving as the cause of the seven kinds of suffering. "Such a condition" is, again, the three links of ignorance, craving, and grasping, able to moisten karma and draw forth suffering. "Such a fruit" is, again, the habituated fruit of volitional formation and becoming. "Such a retribution" is, again, the karma of volitional formation and becoming, summoning the retribution of name-and-form and the rest. These two rounds together, in general, apply to the conceivable twelve links, combined with the ten suchnesses of the six destinies.

次用不思議十二因緣合四聖十如者，無明支轉即變為明，明即了因，成聖人如是性。惡行支轉即變為善行，善行即緣因，成聖人如是相。識、名色等苦道轉，即法身，成聖人如是體。愛、取二支轉成聖人菩提心，即是如是力。有支含果，變成六度行，即成聖人如是作，亦轉成聖人如是因。此有支轉，有二種：正道轉成如是因，助道轉成聖人如是緣。老死支轉成法性常住，成聖人如是果、報(云云)。又，總作者，體、力、作三法秖是煩惱、業、苦，變成法身、菩提心、六度行等。勤習三法，在內成性，在外成相，正意成體，誓願深遠成力，立行成作，牽果成因，相助成緣，剋發成果、報(云云)。若細分四聖，節節有異，今取大槩，故通釋耳。經云：「一切智願，猶在不失。」二乘亦得作通釋也。

Next, using the inconceivable twelve links, combined with the ten suchnesses of the four kinds of sages: the link of ignorance, transformed, becomes clarity — clarity is precisely the revealing cause, forming the sage's own "such a nature." The link of evil action, transformed, becomes good action — good action is precisely the conditioning cause, forming the sage's own "such a mark." Consciousness, name-and-form, and the rest, the path of suffering transformed, become the Dharma-body, forming the sage's own "such an essence." The two links of craving and grasping, transformed, become the sage's own bodhi-mind — this is precisely "such a power." The link of becoming, containing its own fruit, transformed, becomes the practice of the six pāramitās, forming the sage's own "such an activity," and likewise transformed into the sage's own "such a cause." This transformation of the link of becoming is of two kinds: the correct path transformed becomes "such a cause," and the assisting path transformed becomes the sage's own "such a condition." The link of old age and death, transformed, becomes the eternal abiding of the dharma-nature, forming the sage's own "such a fruit" and "such a retribution," and so on. Again, taken all together: the three dharmas of essence, power, and activity are simply affliction, karma, and suffering, transformed into the Dharma-body, the bodhi-mind, the practice of the six pāramitās, and the rest. Diligently cultivating these three dharmas: internally, this forms the nature; externally, this forms the mark; correct intention forms the essence; the vow, deep and far-reaching, forms the power; establishing practice forms the activity; drawing forth the fruit forms the cause; mutual assistance forms the condition; and the eventual manifestation forms the fruit and the retribution, and so on. If the four kinds of sages were finely differentiated, each step would show its own particular differences — here, taking only the broad outline, a general explanation alone is given. The sūtra says: "The vow of omniscience remains present, never lost" — this general explanation may be applied to the two vehicles as well.

Reading note

This opens a long, densely combinatorial stretch (roughly the whole of the first section of Fascicle 3) cross-referencing all six objects introduced in Fascicle 2's "broad exposition of the object" against one another, two at a time, to show they all describe the same underlying reality under different names — "opened" where their names differ, "combined" where their sense is the same. Each subsequent pairing (dependent origination against the Four Noble Truths, the Four Noble Truths against the two truths, and so on) will follow this same pattern.

The Four Noble Truths Combined with the Ten Suchnesses and the Twelve Links

Chinese Source

四種四諦合十如者，生滅、無生兩種苦、集，是六道十如。如是相、如是性是集。如是體是苦。如是作、力、因、緣又是集。如是果、報又是苦(云云)。生滅、無生兩種道、滅，是析、體二乘及通菩薩十如。如是相、性即是道。如是體即是滅。如是力、作、因、緣皆是道。如是果、報又是滅。無量、無作兩種苦、集，即是四聖界外果報十如。集諦即是界外如是相、性、力、作、因、緣也。苦諦即是界外如是體、果、報等(云云)。無量、無作兩種道、滅，即是四聖界外涅槃十如。道諦即是涅槃性、相、力、作、因、緣等，亦是般若、解脫也。滅諦即是涅槃體、果、報等，亦成常住法身也(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the four kinds of Four Noble Truths combined with the ten suchnesses: the arising-and-ceasing and non-arising kinds of suffering and its arising are the ten suchnesses of the six destinies. "Such a mark" and "such a nature" are the arising. "Such an essence" is suffering. "Such an activity," "such a power," "such a cause," and "such a condition" are, again, the arising. "Such a fruit" and "such a retribution" are, again, suffering, and so on. The arising-and-ceasing and non-arising kinds of the path and cessation are the ten suchnesses of the analytical and embodying two vehicles and the shared teaching's bodhisattva. "Such a mark" and "such a nature" are precisely the path. "Such an essence" is precisely cessation. "Such a power," "such an activity," "such a cause," and "such a condition" are all the path. "Such a fruit" and "such a retribution" are, again, cessation. The immeasurable and uncreated kinds of suffering and its arising are precisely the ten suchnesses of the four kinds of sages' fruit-retribution beyond the three realms. The truth of arising is precisely the mark, nature, power, activity, cause, and condition beyond the three realms. The truth of suffering is precisely the essence, fruit, and retribution beyond the three realms, and so on. The immeasurable and uncreated kinds of the path and cessation are precisely the ten suchnesses of the four kinds of sages' nirvāṇa beyond the three realms. The truth of the path is precisely nirvāṇa's own nature, mark, power, activity, cause, and condition, and the rest — this is also prajñā and liberation. The truth of cessation is precisely nirvāṇa's own essence, fruit, and retribution, and the rest — this also forms the eternally abiding Dharma-body, and so on.

四種四諦合四種十二因緣者，生滅、無生兩苦集，即是兩種思議十二因緣。生滅、無生兩種道滅，即是兩種思議十二因緣，無明滅乃至老死滅也。無量、無作兩苦集，即是兩不思議十二因緣也。無量、無作兩道滅，即是兩不思議十二因緣，無明滅乃至老死滅，此可解。

As for the four kinds of Four Noble Truths combined with the four kinds of twelve links: the arising-and-ceasing and non-arising kinds of suffering and its arising are precisely the two conceivable kinds of the twelve links. The arising-and-ceasing and non-arising kinds of the path and cessation are precisely the two conceivable kinds of the twelve links read as the extinguishing of ignorance down through the extinguishing of old age and death. The immeasurable and uncreated kinds of suffering and its arising are precisely the two inconceivable kinds of the twelve links. The immeasurable and uncreated kinds of the path and cessation are precisely the two inconceivable kinds of the twelve links read as the extinguishing of ignorance down through the extinguishing of old age and death — this may be understood accordingly.

The Seven Kinds of Two Truths Combined with the Ten Suchnesses, Twelve Links, and Four Noble Truths

Chinese Source

七種二諦合十如者，藏、通、別、圓入通凡四俗，皆是六道十如也。藏、通兩真是二乘十如也。別、圓入別兩俗：有邊是六道十如；無邊是二乘十如。圓俗此通九法界十如。別入通、圓入通、別、圓入別、圓，凡五種真，皆是佛法界十如也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the seven kinds of two truths combined with the ten suchnesses: the Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, and perfect-entering-shared teachings' four conventional truths are all, alike, the ten suchnesses of the six destinies. The Tripiṭaka and shared teachings' two real truths are the ten suchnesses of the two vehicles. The distinct and perfect-entering-distinct teachings' two conventional truths: their "existent" side is the ten suchnesses of the six destinies; their "nonexistent" side is the ten suchnesses of the two vehicles. The perfect teaching's own conventional truth here extends generally through all nine dharma-realms. The distinct-entering-shared, perfect-entering-shared, distinct, perfect-entering-distinct, and perfect teachings' five real truths are all, alike, the ten suchnesses of the buddha-dharma-realm.

七種二諦合四種十二因緣者，藏、通、別、圓入通凡四俗，即是思議兩種十二因緣。藏、通兩真即是思議十二因緣，無明滅乃至老死滅也。別、圓入別兩俗：有邊是思議十二因緣；無邊是思議無明滅乃至老死滅。圓俗即通界內、外四種十二因緣也。別入通、圓入通、別、圓入別、圓，凡五種真，即是界外不思議十二因緣，無明滅乃至老死滅也。

As for the seven kinds of two truths combined with the four kinds of twelve links: the Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, and perfect-entering-shared teachings' four conventional truths are precisely the two conceivable kinds of the twelve links. The Tripiṭaka and shared teachings' two real truths are precisely the conceivable twelve links read as the extinguishing of ignorance down through the extinguishing of old age and death. The distinct and perfect-entering-distinct teachings' two conventional truths: their "existent" side is the conceivable twelve links; their "nonexistent" side is the conceivable twelve links read as extinguishing. The perfect teaching's own conventional truth extends generally through all four kinds of the twelve links, within and beyond the three realms alike. The distinct-entering-shared, perfect-entering-shared, distinct, perfect-entering-distinct, and perfect teachings' five real truths are precisely the inconceivable twelve links beyond the three realms, read as the extinguishing of ignorance down through the extinguishing of old age and death.

七種二諦合四種四諦者，實有二諦，即生滅四諦也。幻有二諦，即無生四諦也。別入通、圓入通兩俗，還是無生苦集也。別入通真，是無量道滅也。圓入通真，是無作道滅也。別俗、圓入別俗，此是無量苦集。圓俗是無作苦集。別真是無量道滅。圓入別真、圓真，是無作道滅也。

As for the seven kinds of two truths combined with the four kinds of Four Noble Truths: the really-existent two truths are precisely the arising-and-ceasing Four Noble Truths. The illusorily-existent two truths are precisely the non-arising Four Noble Truths. The distinct-entering-shared and perfect-entering-shared teachings' two conventional truths are, again, the non-arising suffering and its arising. The distinct-entering-shared's real truth is the immeasurable path and cessation. The perfect-entering-shared's real truth is the uncreated path and cessation. The distinct teaching's conventional truth, and the perfect-entering-distinct's conventional truth, are the immeasurable suffering and its arising. The perfect teaching's own conventional truth is the uncreated suffering and its arising. The distinct teaching's real truth is the immeasurable path and cessation. The perfect-entering-distinct's real truth, and the perfect teaching's own real truth, are the uncreated path and cessation.

Reading note

This dense combinatorial stretch is easier to follow as a lattice than as prose: the seven kinds of two truths, the four kinds of Four Noble Truths, the four kinds of twelve links, and the five kinds of three truths (following below) all trace out the identical Tripiṭaka → shared → distinct → perfect progression, just measured by a different set of terms each time. Zhiyi's point in walking through every pairing explicitly, rather than asserting the equivalence once, is that a reader trained on any one of these six schemes should be able to locate their own understanding precisely within any of the others.

The Five Kinds of Three Truths Combined with the Other Objects

Chinese Source

五種三諦合十如者，別入通、圓入通兩俗，是六道十如。別俗、圓入別俗，有邊是六道十如；無邊是二乘十如。圓俗，意通九界(云云)。五種真諦皆是二乘、菩薩等十如；五種中諦皆是佛界十如也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the five kinds of three truths combined with the ten suchnesses: the distinct-entering-shared and perfect-entering-shared teachings' two conventional truths are the ten suchnesses of the six destinies. The distinct teaching's conventional truth and the perfect-entering-distinct's conventional truth: their "existent" side is the ten suchnesses of the six destinies; their "nonexistent" side is the ten suchnesses of the two vehicles. The perfect teaching's own conventional truth, in sense, extends generally through all nine realms, and so on. The five real truths are all, alike, the ten suchnesses of the two vehicles and bodhisattvas; the five middle truths are all, alike, the ten suchnesses of the buddha-realm.

五種三諦合四種十二因緣者，別入通、圓入通兩俗，是六道思議十二因緣。別、圓入別兩俗：有邊是思議六道十二因緣生；無邊是思議十二因緣滅。圓俗，義通(云云)。今且用是四種十二因緣，五種真諦即是思議十二因緣滅，亦即是不思議十二因緣生。五種中諦即是不思議十二因緣滅。

As for the five kinds of three truths combined with the four kinds of twelve links: the distinct-entering-shared and perfect-entering-shared teachings' two conventional truths are the conceivable twelve links of the six destinies. The distinct and perfect-entering-distinct teachings' two conventional truths: their "existent" side is the conceivable arising of the twelve links of the six destinies; their "nonexistent" side is the conceivable extinguishing of the twelve links. The perfect teaching's own conventional truth, in sense, extends generally, and so on. Here, taking up these four kinds of twelve links: the five real truths are precisely the conceivable extinguishing of the twelve links, and are likewise precisely the inconceivable arising of the twelve links. The five middle truths are precisely the inconceivable extinguishing of the twelve links.

五種三諦合四種四諦者，別入通、圓入通兩俗，即無生苦集也。別俗、圓入別俗、圓俗，通是無生之苦集，亦是無生之道滅，亦是無量之苦集也。別入通、圓入通兩真，本取但空邊，是無生道滅也。別真、圓入別真，即是無生之道滅，於無量是苦集。圓真，於無生是道滅，於無量、無作是苦集。別入通中，是無量道滅。圓入通中，是無作道滅。別中，是無量道滅。圓入別中，是無作道滅。圓中，正是無作道滅。

As for the five kinds of three truths combined with the four kinds of Four Noble Truths: the distinct-entering-shared and perfect-entering-shared teachings' two conventional truths are precisely the non-arising suffering and its arising. The distinct teaching's conventional truth, the perfect-entering-distinct's conventional truth, and the perfect teaching's own conventional truth are, in general, the non-arising suffering and its arising, and are also the non-arising path and cessation, and are also the immeasurable suffering and its arising. The distinct-entering-shared and perfect-entering-shared teachings' two real truths, taken originally from their bare-emptiness side, are the non-arising path and cessation. The distinct teaching's real truth, and the perfect-entering-distinct's real truth, are precisely the non-arising path and cessation, while, in terms of the immeasurable, they are suffering and its arising. The perfect teaching's own real truth, in terms of the non-arising, is the path and cessation, while, in terms of the immeasurable and the uncreated, it is suffering and its arising. The distinct-entering-shared's middle truth is the immeasurable path and cessation. The perfect-entering-shared's middle truth is the uncreated path and cessation. The distinct teaching's middle truth is the immeasurable path and cessation. The perfect-entering-distinct's middle truth is the uncreated path and cessation. The perfect teaching's own middle truth is precisely the uncreated path and cessation.

五種三諦合七種二諦者，簡前兩二諦不被合也。次、二種二諦，二俗，即是五種三諦家五種俗。二真，空邊即是五種三諦家真；不空邊即是五種三諦家中。後三種二諦，三俗，空邊即是五種三諦家真；有邊即是五種三諦家俗。三真，即是五種三諦家中。又作一種說，如後簡前二諦不被合，後五俗，有真、有俗；後五真，有真、有中。

As for the five kinds of three truths combined with the seven kinds of two truths: the first two kinds of two truths are set aside, not combined here. As for the next two kinds of two truths: their two conventional truths are precisely the five kinds of three truths' own five conventional truths. Of their two real truths, the bare-emptiness side is precisely the five kinds of three truths' own real truth; the not-empty side is precisely the five kinds of three truths' own middle truth. As for the last three kinds of two truths: their three conventional truths, on the empty side, are precisely the five kinds of three truths' own real truth; on the existent side, they are precisely the five kinds of three truths' own conventional truth. Their three real truths are precisely the five kinds of three truths' own middle truth. Stated in yet another way: just as the first two kinds of two truths are set aside and not combined, the latter five conventional truths contain both a real and a conventional aspect within them, and the latter five real truths contain both a real and a middle aspect within them.

The One Real Truth Combined with Every Other Object

Chinese Source

一實諦合十如者，一一法界皆具十界，簡却九界，但與佛法界同也。簡三種十二因緣，但與一種十二因緣滅同。簡三種四諦，但與一實四諦同。簡七種二諦，但與五真諦有同、不同。簡五種三諦，但與五中諦同(云云)。言無諦不可說者，合十如，如名不異，即是空寂。言辭相寂滅，不可說示，即是十種皆如義也。諸無明滅乃至老死滅，其義甚深，甚深即無諦同也。生生不可說，乃至不生不生不可說，即與無諦同也。七種真諦皆不可說。最初真諦不可說者，如身子云：「吾聞解脫之中，無有言說。」況後六耶？非生死、非涅槃，既非二邊亦無中道，即五種中諦與無諦同也。一實名虛空，虛空無一，云何有實？即無諦同也。無諦自無所存，平等大慧無若干也。雖無若干，若干無量，舒之充滿法界，不知從何而來。無量無若干，收之莫知所有，不知從何而去。不來不去，即是法佛(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the one real truth combined with the ten suchnesses: each single dharma-realm already possesses all ten realms within it; setting aside the other nine, it is identical only with the buddha-dharma-realm. Setting aside the three kinds of twelve links, it is identical only with the single kind of the twelve links read as extinguishing. Setting aside the three kinds of Four Noble Truths, it is identical only with the one real truth's own Four Noble Truths. Setting aside the seven kinds of two truths, it is both identical and not identical with the five real truths. Setting aside the five kinds of three truths, it is identical only with the five middle truths, and so on. As for saying that the truth of no-truth cannot be spoken at all: combined with the ten suchnesses, since the name "thus" admits no distinction, this is itself empty quiescence; that "the marks of speech are themselves quiescent and extinct," unable to be shown or spoken of, is precisely the sense that all ten are, alike, "thus." That ignorance is extinguished, down through old age and death being extinguished — this sense is exceedingly deep, and this very depth is identical with the truth of no-truth. "Arising-arising cannot be spoken," down to "non-arising-non-arising cannot be spoken," is likewise identical with the truth of no-truth. All seven kinds of real truth cannot be spoken at all. That the very first real truth cannot be spoken is as Śāriputra says: "I have heard that, within liberation, there is nothing that can be spoken" — how much more so, then, the remaining six! Neither birth-and-death nor nirvāṇa, being neither of the two extremes, and likewise without any middle way either — this is precisely the five middle truths being identical with the truth of no-truth. The one real truth is named empty space; empty space has no "one" within it — how, then, could there be any "real" at all? This, too, is identical with the truth of no-truth. The truth of no-truth has, of itself, nothing that abides within it — the equal, great wisdom has nothing manifold about it at all. Yet, though without anything manifold, its manifold immeasurability, unfolded, fills the entire dharma-realm, and one cannot know from where it comes. Immeasurable and without anything manifold, gathered in, none can know what it possesses, and one cannot know to where it goes. Neither coming nor going — this is precisely the Dharma-buddha, and so on.

復次，七種二諦，赴緣開合，轉轉相入，一一又各有隨情、隨情智、隨智等。餘五義例亦應有，今不具載。何者？佛以一音演說法，眾生隨類各得解。自思之！

Moreover, the seven kinds of two truths, responding to conditions, opening and combining, turn and enter into one another in succession, and each single one, in turn, likewise carries its own senses of according with feeling, feeling-and-wisdom, and wisdom alone. The remaining five objects, by the same pattern, ought likewise to carry these senses — I do not set them all down here in full. Why is this? Because the Buddha, with a single voice, expounds the Dharma, and sentient beings, each according to their own kind, attain their own understanding accordingly. Reflect on this for yourself!

This closes the entire opening-and-combining exercise across all six objects, ending, fittingly, with the one truth and the "no-truth" that both cancel and complete it — every scheme, pushed far enough, collapses into the same wordless equal wisdom "neither coming nor going." Zhiyi's closing line is a deliberate invitation rather than an evasion: having modeled the method exhaustively for the two truths, he leaves the reader to complete the same cross-referencing for the remaining five objects independently.

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Since the principle of all these objects is already fused together as one, why does it still appear so multifariously varied, each further restricting and entering into the others? Answer: The Tathāgata observes and knows the nature and mark of the ten realms, and finds among them those already ripened and those not yet ripened. For a great capacity not yet ripe, he does not let slander arise; for a small capacity, once ripe, he does not let the moment be lost. The Avataṃsaka, though it, too, surveys all ten realms, ripens beings through the distinct and perfect teachings alone, since only these two realms are ripe at that time. The Tripiṭaka teaching, too, surveys all ten realms, ripening beings through the arising-and-ceasing scheme, since the two vehicles' own nature and mark are ripe. The Vaipulya period, too, surveys all ten realms, ripening beings through four kinds of mutual entering, since four realms are ripe. The Prajñā period, too, surveys all ten realms, ripening beings through three kinds of mutual entering, likewise since four realms are ripe. The Lotus, too, surveys all ten realms, ripening beings through the single, perfect truth alone, since a single nature and mark is ripe. It is like a painter, who is able to blend the five colors together to produce every kind of image — how much more so, then, the Buddha, the Dharma-king, sovereign and at ease with respect to the Dharma, should be unable to interweave his teaching in every variety of way to tame and ripen sentient beings!

Reading note

This answers a question the whole preceding combinatorial exercise might naturally raise: if every scheme of objects ultimately says the same thing, why did the Buddha teach so many different, seemingly incompatible versions? Zhiyi's answer is pedagogical rather than metaphysical — the multiplicity tracks which of the ten dharma-realms are ripe for a given assembly at a given moment, not any real plurality in the underlying principle itself.

This closes the wondrous of the object of contemplation (境妙) in its entirety — the very first, and by far the longest, of the ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate, spanning the whole of Fascicle 2's Lower Half plus this opening stretch of Fascicle 3. The Four Good Roots: Warmth, Summit, Forbearance, and the Supreme Worldly Dharma

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The dharma of warmth: taking the Four Noble Truths as its object, wisdom arises; subduing affliction, this wisdom further increases and forms the sixteen kinds of contemplative wisdom. It is like a fire-drill, upper and lower resting upon one another, giving rise to fire that burns the firewood. Because there is wisdom that knows there is an object, it is able to generate the wisdom of warmth, which causes what exists to wither and fade — like gathering flowers in summer into a heap: the flowers generate their own warmth, which, in turn, causes them to wither by themselves. Because it stands at the summit of the dharma of warmth, it is called "summit"; because it stands below the dharma of forbearance, it is called "lower." If there is no difficulty, one is bound to pass over it and reach the other side; if one encounters difficulty, one at once turns back and retreats. The practitioner, too, abides at the summit only briefly: if there is no difficulty, he is bound to reach forbearance; if there is difficulty, he turns back and retreats to warmth. It is just like the summit of a mountain — hence it is called "summit."

云何為觀？於佛、法、僧生下小信。小信者，此法不久停，故言下小。此信緣佛生小信，是緣道諦。緣法生下小信，是緣滅諦。問：應能緣四諦，云何言緣二諦？答：道、滅勝故。清淨無過，是妙、是離。能生信處，為生受化者信樂心故。若世尊說苦、集是可信敬者，則無受化者。此煩惱、惡行，邪見、顛倒，云何可敬信？我常為此逼迫。受化者於道、滅生欣樂，是故說二也。復有說者，信佛、僧是緣道，信法是緣三諦，則盡信四諦也。

What, then, is meant by contemplation at the stage of summit? A small measure of faith arises with respect to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. It is called a "small" measure of faith because this state of faith does not remain for long, hence it is said to be lesser and small. This faith, arising with respect to the Buddha, generates a small faith that takes the truth of the path as its object; arising with respect to the Dharma, it generates a small faith that takes the truth of cessation as its object. Question: This faith should properly be able to take all Four Noble Truths as its object — why, then, does it speak only of taking two truths as its object? Answer: Because the path and cessation are superior. They are pure and without fault; they are wondrous; they are freedom. They are able to give rise to a basis for faith, because they generate a mind of faith and joy in those who are to be converted. If the World-Honored One had taught that suffering and its arising were what one ought to believe in and revere, then there would be no one to be converted at all — for how could this affliction, evil conduct, wrong view, and inversion possibly be revered and believed in? "I am forever oppressed by these," one would say. Those who are to be converted give rise to joy and delight with respect to the path and cessation — this is why only these two are spoken of. There is also another account, which holds that faith in the Buddha and the Saṅgha takes the path as its object, while faith in the Dharma takes all three remaining truths as its object — in which case, all Four Noble Truths are, in the end, encompassed by faith.

問：住頂亦信陰，亦信寶，亦信諦。何故但說信三寶？答：三寶是生信敬處。但隨行者意，於陰生悅適，是名為煖。於寶生悅適，是名為頂。於諦生悅適，是名為忍。問：何故頂退，不說煖退？答：頂既退，亦應說煖退。行者在頂時，多煩惱業留難。煩惱等作是念：若行者到忍，我復於誰身中當作果報？若離欲界時，亦念：行者出欲界，我復於誰身中生果報？離非想非非想處時，亦念：行者離彼欲已，更不受身，我復於誰身中生於果報？於此三時，多諸留難。留難退，故大憂惱。如人見寶藏大喜，欲取即失。住頂法者自念：不久當得於忍，永斷惡道，獲大重利，猶如聖人。而忽退失，故大憂惱，是故言頂退也。

Question: One who abides at the summit has faith in the aggregates, faith in the Three Jewels, and faith in the truths alike — why, then, does the text speak only of faith in the Three Jewels? Answer: The Three Jewels are the basis from which faith and reverence arise. It is simply according to the practitioner's own disposition: delight arising with respect to the aggregates is called warmth; delight arising with respect to the Three Jewels is called summit; delight arising with respect to the truths is called forbearance. Question: Why does the text speak of regression at the summit, but not of regression at warmth? Answer: Since there is regression at the summit, regression at warmth ought likewise to be spoken of. It is simply that, while the practitioner is at the summit, affliction and karma present him with a great many obstacles and difficulties. Afflictions and the like think to themselves: "If this practitioner reaches forbearance, then in whose body shall we produce our fruit and retribution?" At the moment of leaving the desire realm, they likewise think: "If this practitioner departs from the desire realm, then in whose body shall we give rise to our fruit and retribution?" At the moment of leaving the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, they likewise think: "If this practitioner, having left that desire, no longer receives a body at all, then in whose body shall we give rise to our fruit and retribution?" At these three moments, there are a great many such obstacles and difficulties. When one regresses because of these obstacles and difficulties, one suffers great distress and anguish — like a man who, seeing a treasure-store, rejoices greatly, but loses it the very moment he tries to take it. One who abides in the dharma of summit thinks to himself: "Before long, I shall attain forbearance, forever cut off the evil destinies, and gain a great and weighty benefit, becoming just like a sage" — and yet, when he suddenly regresses and loses this attainment, he suffers great distress and anguish. This is why it is called "regression at the summit."

若能親近善友，從其聞隨順方便法，內心正觀，信佛菩提，信善說法，信僧清淨功德，是說信寶。說色無常，乃至說識無常，是說信陰。知有苦、集、滅、道，是說信諦。若如是即住頂，若不如是即頂退。

If one is able to draw near to good friends, and, hearing from them the accordant and skillful teachings, rightly contemplates within one's own mind, having faith in the Buddha's awakening, faith in the well-taught Dharma, and faith in the Saṅgha's pure merit — this is called faith in the Jewels. Teaching that form is impermanent, down through teaching that consciousness is impermanent — this is called faith in the aggregates. Knowing that there is suffering, its arising, its cessation, and the path — this is called faith in the truths. If one proceeds in this way, one abides at the summit; if one does not proceed in this way, one regresses from the summit.

忍法觀者，正觀欲界苦，色、無色界苦。欲界行集，色、無色界行集。欲界行滅，色、無色界行滅。斷欲界行道，斷色、無色界行道。如是三十二心，是名下忍。行者後時，漸漸減損行及緣，復更正觀欲界苦，色、無色界苦，乃至觀斷欲界行道，除觀斷色、無色界行道，從是中名中忍。復更正觀欲界苦，觀色、無色界苦，乃至觀色、無色界行滅，除滅一切道。復正觀欲界苦，色、無色界苦，乃至觀欲界行滅，除色、無色界行滅。復正觀欲界苦，乃至觀色、無色界行集，除滅一切滅。復正觀欲界苦，乃至觀欲界行集，除色、無色界行集。復正觀欲界、色、無色苦，除一切集。復正觀欲界苦，除色、無色界苦。復正觀欲界常，相續不斷，不遠離。如是觀時，深生厭患，復更減損，但作二心觀於一行，如似苦法忍、苦法智。如是正觀，是名中忍。復以一心觀欲界苦，是名上忍。

As for the contemplation of the dharma of forbearance: one rightly contemplates the suffering of the desire realm, and the suffering of the form and formless realms; the arising of the formations of the desire realm, and the arising of the formations of the form and formless realms; the cessation of the formations of the desire realm, and the cessation of the formations of the form and formless realms; cutting off the path of the formations of the desire realm, and cutting off the path of the formations of the form and formless realms. These thirty-two mind-moments are called the lower forbearance. Afterward, the practitioner gradually reduces both the objects and the aspects of contemplation: he again rightly contemplates the suffering of the desire realm and the suffering of the form and formless realms, down through contemplating the cutting off of the path of the formations of the desire realm, but omits contemplating the cutting off of the path of the formations of the form and formless realms — from this point on, this is called the middle forbearance. He then again rightly contemplates the suffering of the desire realm, contemplates the suffering of the form and formless realms, down through contemplating the cessation of the formations of the form and formless realms, but omits contemplating the cessation of all remaining paths. He then again rightly contemplates the suffering of the desire realm and the suffering of the form and formless realms, down through contemplating the cessation of the formations of the desire realm, but omits the cessation of the formations of the form and formless realms. He then again rightly contemplates the suffering of the desire realm, down through contemplating the arising of the formations of the form and formless realms, but omits contemplating the cessation of all remaining cessations. He then again rightly contemplates the suffering of the desire realm, down through contemplating the arising of the formations of the desire realm, but omits the arising of the formations of the form and formless realms. He then again rightly contemplates the suffering of the desire, form, and formless realms, but omits all remaining arisings. He then again rightly contemplates the suffering of the desire realm, but omits the suffering of the form and formless realms. He then again rightly contemplates the desire realm as constant, its continuity unbroken, without any further departure from it. Contemplating in this way, a deep sense of weariness and aversion arises, and he further reduces still more, forming, at last, only two mind-moments contemplating a single aspect — resembling the forbearance regarding the dharma of suffering and the wisdom regarding the dharma of suffering of the path of vision. This right contemplation is called the middle forbearance. He then contemplates the suffering of the desire realm with a single mind-moment alone — this is called the upper forbearance.

復次，生世第一法，世第一法後，次生苦法忍。譬如人欲從己國適他國，多財產，不能持去，以物易錢；猶嫌錢，易金；嫌金，易多價寶，往適他國。行者乃至漸捨，相續不離，生於上忍。上忍後，生第一法。第一法後，生苦忍。

Next, the supreme worldly dharma arises; and after the supreme worldly dharma, the forbearance regarding the dharma of suffering arises next in turn. It is like a man who wishes to travel from his own country to another country: having many possessions, unable to carry them all with him, he exchanges his goods for money; still finding money burdensome, he exchanges it for gold; still finding gold burdensome, he exchanges it for a single treasure of great value, and travels on to the other country with that alone. In just this way, the practitioner, gradually relinquishing more and more, in unbroken continuity, gives rise to the upper forbearance. After the upper forbearance, the supreme worldly dharma arises. After the supreme worldly dharma, the forbearance regarding the dharma of suffering arises.

問：世第一法有三品不？答：一人無，多人有。身子上，目連中，餘皆下。就佛、支佛、聲聞為三品。

Question: Does the supreme worldly dharma come in three grades? Answer: In a single person, no; but among many people, yes. Śāriputra's was of the upper grade, Maudgalyāyana's of the middle grade, and all others' of the lower grade. Taken with respect to buddhas, pratyekabuddhas, and śrāvakas, it forms three grades.

世第一法者，此心心數法，於餘法為最、為勝、為長、為尊、為上、為妙。亦分、亦都。分者，勝世間法，不勝見諦。見諦眷屬不相離，慧力偏多故、熏禪不與凡夫同生一處故。盡智時，一切善根永離一切諸垢障故。三三昧乃至惡賤無漏，何況有漏，不應都勝？分勝彼煖、頂、忍法，亦應言第一，應言分勝，勝煖、頂、忍、一切凡夫所得禪，無量解脫除入也。或言都勝，非謂一切事業中勝，但以能開聖道門故。彼見諦等，不能開聖道門，以世第一法開聖道門，彼見諦等法得修見諦等法。得修者，皆是世第一法功用。是世第一法名義者，最勝義是第一義；得妙果是第一義，如高幢頂，更無有上，是第一義。

As for the supreme worldly dharma: this mental factor and its associated mental factors are, with respect to all other dharmas, the foremost, the superior, the exalted, the venerable, the highest, and the wondrous. It is superior both partially and wholly. As for partial superiority: it is superior to worldly dharmas, but not superior to the vision of the truths. This is because the vision of the truths and its retinue are inseparable from one another, because the power of wisdom is especially abundant in it, and because the meditative practice it perfumes does not arise together with ordinary beings in the same place. At the moment of the wisdom of exhaustion, all wholesome roots are forever separated from every defiling obstruction. As for the three samādhis, and even the untainted states that ordinary beings disdain as lowly — how much more so, then, the tainted states — how could it not be superior to all of these? Being partially superior to the dharmas of warmth, summit, and forbearance, it, too, ought to be called "supreme" — one should properly say it is "partially superior," superior to warmth, summit, forbearance, and every meditative attainment, immeasurable liberation, and sphere of mastery attained by ordinary beings. Some, however, say it is wholly superior — not meaning that it is superior in every activity and undertaking, but only that it alone is able to open the gate to the path of the sages. The vision of the truths and the like are not able to open the gate to the path of the sages; it is the supreme worldly dharma that opens the gate to the path of the sages, so that the vision of the truths and its associated dharmas are then able to be cultivated. What is thus able to be cultivated is entirely the functional effect of the supreme worldly dharma. As for the name and meaning of this supreme worldly dharma: the sense of being "most excellent" is the sense of "foremost"; attaining a wondrous fruit is the sense of "foremost" — like the very summit of a tall banner, above which there is nothing higher at all — this, too, is the sense of "foremost."

問：前諸義有差別邪？答：此皆歎說上妙之義，亦有差別。於不淨安般名最，於聞慧名勝，於思慧名長，於煖為尊，於頂為上，於忍為妙。又，依未至為最，依初禪為勝，中間為長，二禪為尊，三禪為上，四禪為妙。如是種種說，此依《毘婆沙》釋，欲委知，向彼尋。

Question: Do these various senses carry any distinction among themselves? Answer: These are all terms of praise extolling its supreme and wondrous quality, yet they do carry a distinction. With respect to the contemplation of impurity and mindfulness of the breath, it is called "most excellent"; with respect to the wisdom of hearing, it is called "superior"; with respect to the wisdom of reflection, it is called "exalted"; with respect to warmth, it is "venerable"; with respect to summit, it is "highest"; with respect to forbearance, it is "wondrous." Again, relying on the stage of approach to the first dhyāna, it is "most excellent"; relying on the first dhyāna, it is "superior"; relying on the intermediate stage, it is "exalted"; relying on the second dhyāna, it is "venerable"; relying on the third dhyāna, it is "highest"; relying on the fourth dhyāna, it is "wondrous." Such various accounts as these follow the explanation given in the Vibhāṣā — those who wish to know them in full detail should consult that text directly.

Ru-Yi Translation

The first fruit involves the eight forbearances and eight forms of wisdom. The third fruit, reconsidering and taking the real as its object once more, involves nine uninterrupted paths and nine forms of the wisdom of liberation. The pratyekabuddha makes use of both the general characteristic and the particular characteristics — as when, taking the three time periods, one clarifies suffering and its arising, and differentiates the twelve links, this is precisely the contemplation of particular characteristics. In the six pāramitās, the wisdom that takes principle as its object is weak, subduing affliction without yet cutting it off, while the wisdom that takes phenomena as its object is strong, able to relinquish body, life, and property without the least regret or attachment. Although the śrāvaka is able to give rise to the real and accomplish sagehood, he still speaks of "my robe" and "my bowl" — hence the mutual discussion of their relative strength and weakness, and so on.

The shared teaching's śrāvaka, through the single gate of the general characteristic, penetrates the conventional as identical with the real. The shared teaching's pratyekabuddha is able, within the single gate, to contemplate both the general characteristic and the particular characteristics, and thereby penetrates the conventional as identical with the real. The shared teaching's bodhisattva is able, throughout all four gates, to contemplate both the general characteristic and the particular characteristics, penetrating the conventional as identical with the real; he is also able, comprehensively through all four gates, to emerge into the provisional and teach and transform sentient beings. The ten stages of faith have faith in the true suchness and true mark at the very culmination of the fruit, and, in seeking this principle, give rise to the mind of the ten stages of faith. The ten abidings correctly cultivate entry into emptiness, while incidentally cultivating the provisional and the middle. The ten practices correctly cultivate the provisional, while incidentally cultivating the middle. The ten transferences correctly cultivate the middle. The first ground realizes the middle; from the second ground upward, one reconsiders the middle once more. The Tripiṭaka teaching's buddha, in a single sitting, employs the thirty-four mind-moments — the eight forbearances, the eight forms of wisdom, the nine uninterrupted paths, and the nine paths of liberation — cutting off both the primary afflictions and their habituated residue entirely. The shared teaching's buddha, seated beneath the bodhi-tree, with the wisdom corresponding to a single thought-moment, cuts off the remaining habituated tendencies. The distinct teaching's buddha, with the final mind of the vajra-like samādhi, cuts off the last remaining portion of ignorance and, in the end, exhaustively becomes a buddha. Some say: the moment of this cutting-off is itself the stage of virtual enlightenment, and the buddha himself has nothing further to cut off, but simply realizes and attains perfect and complete bodhi in its fullness. The perfect teaching's disciples of the five ranks, without cutting off the five desires, purify their sense-faculties; possessing the very nature of affliction, they are yet able to know the Tathāgata's own secret treasury. The stage of the purification of the six sense-faculties attains a resemblance to the wisdom of the middle way. The initial abiding attains the Tathāgata's own single body as immeasurable bodies, entering the ocean-like stream of the Dharma and moving within it, flowing forth spontaneously and without obstruction. The later stages may be understood accordingly, and are not further recorded here.

Reading note

This entire "characterization" step works through the twenty-item catalogue from the previous section one by one, in far greater technical detail than the enumeration and classification alone required — especially for items three and four, the four good roots (warmth, summit, forbearance, and the supreme worldly dharma) and the four fruits, where Zhiyi draws directly and extensively on Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma sources, above all the *Vibhāṣā* (as he names explicitly), including citing its compiler Ghoṣaka by name. The progressive "reducing the objects and the aspects" scheme in the forbearance stage (thirty-two mind-moments narrowing step by step to a single mind-moment) and the thirty-four mind-moments of the Tripiṭaka buddha's own awakening (eight forbearances, eight forms of wisdom, nine uninterrupted paths, nine paths of liberation) are standard technical sequences from that same source literature, reproduced here largely intact rather than reworked into Zhiyi's own Tiantai vocabulary — a reminder that the twenty kinds of wisdom, as a catalogue, deliberately spans from pre-Buddhist "worldly wisdom" through the full technical apparatus of Abhidharma scholasticism before ever reaching the shared, distinct, and perfect teachings proper.

The perfect teaching's four kinds of wisdom illuminate the inconceivable non-arising-and-non-ceasing twelve links as their object.

二十智照四種四諦者，前、三藏等七智，照生滅四諦境。次、通教五智，照無生滅四諦境。次、別教四智，照無量四諦境。次、圓教四智，照無作四諦境。

As for how the twenty kinds of wisdom illuminate the four kinds of Four Noble Truths: the preceding seven kinds of wisdom belonging to the Tripiṭaka teaching and the rest illuminate the arising-and-ceasing Four Noble Truths as their object. Next, the shared teaching's five kinds of wisdom illuminate the non-arising-and-non-ceasing Four Noble Truths as their object. Next, the distinct teaching's four kinds of wisdom illuminate the immeasurable Four Noble Truths as their object. Next, the perfect teaching's four kinds of wisdom illuminate the uncreated Four Noble Truths as their object.

次、二十智照二諦者，前、七智是照析空之二諦。次、五智是照體空之二諦。次、八智照顯中之二諦。其間別、圓相入者，可以意得(云云)。

Next, as for how the twenty kinds of wisdom illuminate the two truths: the preceding seven kinds of wisdom illuminate the two truths of emptiness reached through analysis. Next, the five kinds of wisdom illuminate the two truths of emptiness reached by embodying dharmas directly. Next, the eight kinds of wisdom illuminate the two truths of revealing the middle. Where the distinct and perfect teachings interpenetrate one another within this, it may be grasped by reflection, and so on.

次、明二十智照三諦者，前、七智照無中之二諦，是因緣所生法，皆屬俗攝。次、五智照含中之二諦，即空一句，皆屬真諦攝也。次、別圓八智照顯中之二諦，即是假名，亦名中道，二句皆屬中道諦攝也。

Next, clarifying how the twenty kinds of wisdom illuminate the three truths: the preceding seven kinds of wisdom illuminate the two truths without the middle — this is the dharmas produced through causes and conditions, all of which belong under the conventional. Next, the five kinds of wisdom illuminate the two truths containing the middle — the single phrase "empty" here belongs entirely under the real truth. Next, the distinct and perfect teachings' eight kinds of wisdom illuminate the two truths of revealing the middle — this is precisely the provisional name, also called the middle way; both phrases belong entirely under the truth of the middle way.

次、二十智照一實諦者，此須引《釋論》明四悉檀，皆名為實。世界故實，乃至第一義故實。當知實語亦通四諦，生滅故實，無生滅故實，無量故實，無作故實。前、三藏七智照生滅之實。次、通教五智照無生滅之實。次、別教四智照無量之實。次、圓教四智照無作之實。前後諸實(云云)。

Next, as for how the twenty kinds of wisdom illuminate the one real truth: this requires citing the Treatise's clarification of the four siddhāntas, all of which are called "real" — real by virtue of the mundane, real all the way to real by virtue of ultimate meaning. One should know that the word "real" likewise extends through all four kinds of the Four Noble Truths: real by virtue of arising-and-ceasing, real by virtue of non-arising-and-non-ceasing, real by virtue of the immeasurable, real by virtue of the uncreated. The preceding seven kinds of wisdom belonging to the Tripiṭaka teaching illuminate the real of arising-and-ceasing. Next, the shared teaching's five kinds of wisdom illuminate the real of non-arising-and-non-ceasing. Next, the distinct teaching's four kinds of wisdom illuminate the real of the immeasurable. Next, the perfect teaching's four kinds of wisdom illuminate the real of the uncreated. The various senses of "real," earlier and later, and so on.

次、二十智無諦、無照者，無諦無別理，若於四種四諦得悟，不復見諦與不諦，故無諦亦通也。前、七智照生滅之無諦，生生不可說故。次、五智照無生滅之無諦，生不生不可說故。次、四智照無量之無諦，不生生不可說故。次、四智照無作之無諦，不生不生不可說故。前、無諦是權，後、無諦是實，此就言教。若就妙悟同於聖人心中所照者，則不見有權實，故非權非實。空拳誑小兒，誘度於一切。方便說權，方便說實。會理之時，無復權實，故稱非權非實為妙也。

Next, as for how the twenty kinds of wisdom relate to the truth of no-truth, which is without illumination: the truth of no-truth has no separate principle of its own — if one attains awakening with respect to the four kinds of Four Noble Truths, one no longer sees any distinction between truth and non-truth, and so the truth of no-truth, too, extends throughout. The preceding seven kinds of wisdom illuminate the no-truth of arising-and-ceasing, because "arising-arising cannot be spoken." Next, the five kinds of wisdom illuminate the no-truth of non-arising-and-non-ceasing, because "arising-non-arising cannot be spoken." Next, the four kinds of wisdom illuminate the no-truth of the immeasurable, because "non-arising-arising cannot be spoken." Next, the four kinds of wisdom illuminate the no-truth of the uncreated, because "non-arising-non-arising cannot be spoken." The earlier no-truth is the provisional; the later no-truth is the real — this is spoken from the standpoint of verbal teaching. But if spoken from the standpoint of the wondrous awakening identical to what is illuminated within the sage's own mind, then one no longer sees any provisional or real at all — hence it is neither provisional nor real. An empty fist is used to fool a small child, in order to draw all beings onward through it: the provisional is taught as a skillful means, and the real, too, is taught as a skillful means. At the moment principle is fused together, there is no longer any provisional or real remaining — hence it is called "neither provisional nor real," and this is the wondrous.

The Coarse and the Wondrous

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous: the preceding twelve rounds of wisdom are coarse; the latter eight rounds of wisdom are wondrous. Why is this? The buddhas of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings are themselves impermanent, and do not themselves teach the eternal — how, then, could the two vehicles and the bodhisattvas of those teachings ever hear of the eternal, believe in the eternal, or cultivate the eternal? Hence, this is coarse. The distinct teaching's ten stages of faith, from their very outset, have already heard of the eternal, and have faith in and cultivate the eternal — already surpassing those buddhas, how much more so, then, everything that follows! Hence, this is wondrous. The common account holds that the Lotus does not clarify the eternal, but this is merely the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching. Here, clarifying that the ten stages of faith's own knowledge of the middle has already surpassed the Muni, the latter eight rounds are, accordingly, wondrous.

又，別教四智，三麁一妙。圓教四智，悉皆稱妙。何者？地人云：「中道乃是果頭能顯，初心學者仰信此理，如藕絲懸山。」故說信、行皆非圓意也。故十信智為麁。十住正修空，傍修假、中。十行正修假，傍修中。十迴向始正修中。此中但理，不具諸法，是故皆麁。登地智破無明，見中道，證則為妙。類如通、藏兩種，俱得道，而三藏門拙。今別教亦爾，教門皆權，而證是妙(云云)。圓教四智皆妙者，如法相說，如說而信，如理而行，始論五品，終竟妙覺，實而非權，是故皆妙。是名待麁智說妙智也。

Again, among the distinct teaching's four kinds of wisdom, three are coarse and one is wondrous; among the perfect teaching's four kinds of wisdom, all without exception are called wondrous. Why is this? A master of the Dilun school says: "The middle way is something the fruition alone is able to reveal; the beginning student, looking up, has faith in this principle only as a lotus-thread hung from a mountain." Hence it is said that both faith and practice in the distinct teaching fall short of the perfect teaching's own intent. Therefore, the wisdom of the ten stages of faith is coarse. The ten abidings correctly cultivate emptiness, while incidentally cultivating the provisional and the middle. The ten practices correctly cultivate the provisional, while incidentally cultivating the middle. The ten transferences begin to correctly cultivate the middle. Within all of this, there is principle alone, without the dharmas being fully present within it — hence, all of this is coarse. The wisdom of ascending to the grounds breaks through ignorance and sees the middle way; once realized, this becomes wondrous. This resembles the case of the shared and Tripiṭaka teachings: both attain the path, and yet the Tripiṭaka teaching's own approach is clumsy. So, too, here with the distinct teaching: its teaching-gate is entirely a skillful means, and yet its realization is wondrous, and so on. As for why the perfect teaching's four kinds of wisdom are all, without exception, wondrous: as the dharma's own mark is spoken of, so one has faith in it; as principle itself is, so one practices it — beginning with the discussion of the five ranks, and ending with subtle awakening, it is real and not provisional, and hence all of it is wondrous. This is what is called speaking of the wondrous wisdom in relation to the coarse wisdom.

又、約知見明麁妙者，知與見云何？然分別有四：不知不見、知非見、見非知、亦知亦見。先、約三藏釋，後、約圓釋，中間例可解。凡夫不聞故不知，不證故不見。五停、四念至世第一法，聞故名知，未證故非見。辟支佛不聞故非知，自然證故是見。四果聞故亦是知，證故亦是見。傳傳判麁妙，可解。約圓教釋者，七方便不聞故不知，未證故不見。五品、六根，聞故知，未證故不見。發宿習者名見，不從聞故不知。稟教證入者，亦知、亦見。此節節傳為麁妙。

Again, as for clarifying the coarse and the wondrous in terms of knowing and seeing: what is the difference between knowing and seeing? There are, in fact, four distinctions: neither knowing nor seeing; knowing but not seeing; seeing but not knowing; and both knowing and seeing. First explained from the standpoint of the Tripiṭaka teaching, and afterward from the standpoint of the perfect teaching, with the intervening cases to be understood by the same pattern. The ordinary being, not having heard the teaching, does not know; not having realized it, does not see. From the five methods and four applications of mindfulness up through the supreme worldly dharma, having heard the teaching, this is called knowing; not yet having realized it, this is not seeing. The pratyekabuddha, not having heard the teaching, is said not to know; realizing it spontaneously, this is seeing. The four fruits, having heard the teaching, are likewise knowing; having realized it, are likewise seeing. Judging the coarse and the wondrous by successive transmission in this way may be understood accordingly. Explained from the standpoint of the perfect teaching: the seven expedient stages, not having heard the perfect teaching, do not know; not yet having realized it, do not see. The five ranks and the six sense-faculties, having heard it, know; not yet having realized it, do not see. One who brings forth a habituation from a former life is called seeing, though, not arising from hearing in this life, is called not knowing. One who, having received the teaching, realizes and enters it, both knows and sees. This, too, is judged as coarse and wondrous, step by step in succession.

究竟而論，前來二十種智，略而言之，不出權、實二智。如經：「如來方便知見，波羅蜜皆悉具足」，即總束得前來諸權智也。「如來知見廣大深遠」，即是總束前來實智也。方便既其具足，何所不該？知見既其廣大深遠，何所不攝？境淵無邊，智水莫測，唯佛與佛乃能究盡。如此知見，即是眼智。眼即五眼具足，智即三智一心。一切種智知於實，兩智知於權。佛眼見於實，四眼見於權。此知即是見，此見即是知。對前諸智，諸智是麁，此之知見名之為妙也。

In the final analysis, the twenty kinds of wisdom discussed above, spoken of in brief, do not go beyond the two kinds of wisdom, the provisional and the real. As the sūtra says: "The Tathāgata's skillful-means knowledge and vision, and the pāramitās, are all, alike, fully complete" — this gathers up, in total, all the preceding kinds of provisional wisdom. "The Tathāgata's knowledge and vision are vast, great, deep, and far-reaching" — this gathers up, in total, all the preceding kinds of real wisdom. Since the skillful means are themselves already complete, what is there that is not encompassed by them? Since the knowledge and vision are themselves already vast, great, deep, and far-reaching, what is there that is not gathered within them? The depths of the object are without bound; the waters of wisdom cannot be fathomed; only buddha together with buddha is able to fathom them fully. Such knowledge and vision as this is precisely the eye of wisdom: the eye is the full complement of the five eyes; the wisdom is the three kinds of wisdom in a single mind. The wisdom knowing all modes knows the real; the remaining two kinds of wisdom know the provisional. The buddha-eye sees the real; the remaining four eyes see the provisional. This very knowing is precisely this very seeing; this very seeing is precisely this very knowing. Set against all the preceding kinds of wisdom, which are coarse, this very knowledge and vision is what is called the wondrous.

若得知見中意，不復論五眼。迷者未了，更約眼明麁妙。如肉眼盲閉，何由見色？徒聞人說，起種種想，終非真見。欲令眼開，應須治膜，那得閉眼，執諍何益耶？閉眼想則麁，眼開見則妙。天眼未開，不見障外為麁，修禪定願智之力，能發得淨色，徹障內外，明闇無隔。慧眼未開，常行死逕，假令情想，亦復非實，故為麁；無漏豁發，故稱為妙。諦理明了，故稱妙。法眼未開，差機說法。如身子僻教，滿願穢器，名為麁。破障通無知，分別藥病名之為妙。佛眼不開，不見實相。故文云：「二乘之人及新發心者、不退菩薩所不能知」，故四眼皆麁。除諸菩薩眾信力堅固者，以信得入，相似佛眼，能開真佛知見，乃名為妙。諸教多說四眼，或帶四眼說佛眼，是故為麁。今經獨說佛眼，是故為妙。是為待麁為妙也。

If one grasps the sense within this knowledge and vision, one need no longer discuss the five eyes separately at all. But for those who remain confused and have not yet understood, the coarse and the wondrous are clarified once more in terms of the eyes themselves. It is like the physical eye when blind and closed — by what means could it see forms at all? To merely hear another person's account and give rise to one imagining or another is, in the end, never true seeing. If one wishes the eye to open, one must treat the film over it — how could keeping the eye closed and clinging to disputation be of any benefit at all? An eye closed, imagining, is coarse; an eye opened, seeing, is wondrous. When the heavenly eye has not yet opened, not seeing beyond obstructions is coarse; but cultivating the power of meditative concentration and the wisdom of aspiration, one is able to bring forth the pure form, penetrating through obstruction both within and without, its brightness and darkness no longer separated. When the wisdom-eye has not yet opened, one forever treads the path toward death; even supposing one has feelings and imaginings, these, too, are not yet real, and hence this is coarse; but when the taintless bursts suddenly forth, this is called wondrous, and because the principle of the truths is fully clear, this, too, is called wondrous. When the dharma-eye has not yet opened, one teaches the Dharma mismatched to the capacity at hand — as when Śāriputra taught wrongly, or Pūrṇa mistook a foul vessel — this is called coarse; but breaking through the obstruction so as to penetrate what was formerly unknown, and discerning which remedy fits which illness, this is called wondrous. When the buddha-eye has not yet opened, one does not see the true mark — hence the text says: "What is not knowable by those of the two vehicles, by those newly resolved in mind, or even by bodhisattvas who have not yet regressed" — and so all four remaining eyes are coarse. Except for those bodhisattvas among the great assembly whose power of faith is firm and resolute, who, through faith, are able to enter, and who resemble the buddha-eye, able to open the true buddha's own knowledge and vision — only this may be called wondrous. The various teachings, for the most part, speak only of the four eyes, or speak of the buddha-eye while still bearing the four eyes along with it — hence, this is coarse. The present sūtra alone speaks of the buddha-eye by itself — hence, this is wondrous. This is what is meant by the wondrous in relation to the coarse.

Opening the Coarse to Reveal the Wondrous

Sixth, clarifying opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous: as for the preceding sixteen rounds of wisdom, if they are not opened up and made fully clear, they remain merely coarse wisdom; but once they are opened up and made fully clear, all of them become wondrous wisdom. Why is this? It is like King Wonderful Adornment, who was, at first, possessed only of the worldly wisdom of an outside path; upon hearing the Lotus Sūtra, he was at once opened up and made fully clear — taking his very deviant marks and entering them into the correct marks, he cultivated the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment without shifting from any of his former views, and entered the eightfold correct path without abandoning the eightfold deviant path. As for the wisdom of the five methods as a skillful means, down through the shared teaching's buddha-wisdom and the rest — if they are not opened up and made fully clear, they remain simply coarse wisdom. Each and every one of the twelve rounds of wisdom must be brought, one by one, into the perfect teaching's own wondrous four kinds of wisdom, or brought into the wisdom of the five ranks, the resemblance stage, the stage of partial attainment, and the rest, and so on.

Again, opening up and making fully clear the distinct teaching's wisdom, distinguished stage by stage, brings it into wondrous wisdom: as it stands in its own essence, it is such-and-such a stage; as it advances further, it becomes such-and-such a further stage, worked out in fine detail, and so on. All sixteen kinds of coarse wisdom become wondrous wisdom, with no coarse remaining against which to set it — this is precisely the wondrous of wisdom read in the absolute sense.

復次，開麁眼為妙眼者，餘經雖說為五眼，五眼不融，是故為麁。今經決了四眼，令入佛眼。文云：「父母所生眼，遂得清淨。」學大乘者，雖有肉眼，名為佛眼。即是決了肉眼，名為佛眼也。《淨名》云：「世孰有真天眼者，有佛世尊，不以二相見諸佛國。」此即是決了天眼，即是佛眼也。「願得如世尊慧眼第一淨」，即是決了慧眼，能得入妙。決法眼入妙者，邊際智滿是也。四眼融入佛眼，寂而常照，故文云：「決了聲聞法，是諸經之王。」五眼具足成菩提，開佛知見，故稱為妙。

Moreover, as for opening the coarse eyes to reveal the wondrous eye: although the other sūtras speak of the five eyes, the five eyes remain unfused with one another there — hence, this is coarse. The present sūtra opens up and makes fully clear the four lesser eyes, causing them to enter into the buddha-eye. The text says: "The eyes given by one's father and mother thereupon attain purity." For one who studies the Mahāyāna, though he still has the physical eye, it is named the buddha-eye — this is precisely opening up and making fully clear the physical eye, naming it the buddha-eye. The Vimalakīrti [Sūtra] says: "Who among the worldly truly possesses the heavenly eye? Only the buddhas, the world-honored ones, who see all buddha-lands without any duality of appearance" — this is precisely opening up and making fully clear the heavenly eye, which is itself the buddha-eye. "I wish to attain a wisdom-eye as pure as the World-Honored One's own, the foremost of all" — this is precisely opening up and making fully clear the wisdom-eye, able to enter the wondrous. As for opening up the dharma-eye to enter the wondrous, this is the fullness of the wisdom of the utmost limit. The four eyes are fused and enter into the buddha-eye, quiescent and yet ever illuminating — hence the text says: "Opening up and making fully clear the dharma of the śrāvakas, this is the king of all sūtras." The five eyes, fully complete, accomplish bodhi, opening the Buddha's own knowledge and vision — hence, this is called wondrous.

問：佛眼開，乃名為妙。六根雖淨，云何為妙？答：佛眼雖未開，已能圓學圓信。如迦陵頻伽鳥，雖在㲉中，音聲已勝諸鳥，即是假名、相似等妙。若開，即是分妙、究竟妙(云云)。

Question: When the buddha-eye is opened, this alone is called wondrous — but if the six sense-faculties, though purified, have not yet reached the buddha-eye, how can this, too, be wondrous? Answer: Even though the buddha-eye has not yet opened, one is already able to study comprehensively and have faith comprehensively. It is like the kalaviṅka bird: although still within its eggshell, its call already surpasses that of every other bird — this is precisely the wondrous of the nominal and the resemblance stages, and the like. Once it does open, this is precisely the wondrous of a partial attainment, and the wondrous of the ultimate, and so on.

次、對四種十二因緣明智者，《大經》云：「十二因緣有四種觀：下智觀故，得聲聞菩提。中智觀故，得緣覺菩提。上智觀故，得菩薩菩提。上上智觀故，得佛菩提。」何者？十二因緣本是一境，緣解不同，開成四種。今以四教意釋之：三藏具有三人，而皆以析智觀界內十二因緣事，為初門。然析智淺弱，三人之中，聲聞最劣。以劣人標淺法，故名下智。通教亦有三人，同以體智觀界內十二因緣理，體法雖深，望藏為巧，望別未巧。三人之中，緣覺是中。以中人名通法，故言中智。別教佛與菩薩，俱知界外十二因緣事，次第菩薩比佛，猶未是上，比於通、藏則是上法，故以上智當名也。圓教佛與菩薩，俱觀界外十二因緣理，初心即事而中。此法最勝故，以佛當名故，言上上智觀也。以四教釋四觀，於義允合(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, discussing wisdom in relation to the four kinds of twelve links: the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "There are four kinds of contemplation of the twelve links: through the contemplation of lower wisdom, one attains the bodhi of the śrāvaka. Through the contemplation of middle wisdom, one attains the bodhi of the pratyekabuddha. Through the contemplation of upper wisdom, one attains the bodhi of the bodhisattva. Through the contemplation of the highest wisdom, one attains the bodhi of the buddha." Why is this so? The twelve links are, in their basis, a single object, but since the understanding brought to bear upon them differs, they open out into four kinds. Now, explaining this according to the sense of the Four Teachings: the Tripiṭaka teaching possesses all three kinds of persons, and all of them contemplate the phenomena of the twelve links within the three realms through the wisdom of analysis, as their initial gate; yet the wisdom of analysis is shallow and weak, and, among the three kinds of persons, the śrāvaka is the most inferior — since an inferior person marks out a shallow dharma, it is called the lower wisdom. The shared teaching likewise possesses three kinds of persons, who alike contemplate the principle of the twelve links within the three realms through the wisdom of embodying dharmas directly; though the embodying of dharmas is deep, compared to the Tripiṭaka teaching it is skillful, yet compared to the distinct teaching it is not yet skillful — and, among these three kinds of persons, the pratyekabuddha occupies the middle position; since a middling person names a shared dharma, it is called the middle wisdom. The distinct teaching's buddha and bodhisattva both know the phenomena of the twelve links beyond the three realms; the bodhisattva, progressing in sequence, compared to the buddha, is not yet upper, yet compared to the shared and Tripiṭaka teachings, is indeed an upper dharma — hence it is properly named the upper wisdom. The perfect teaching's buddha and bodhisattva both contemplate the principle of the twelve links beyond the three realms, and, from the very outset of their initial resolve, phenomena themselves are identical with the middle; since this dharma is the most excellent of all, it is properly named after the buddha, and is called the contemplation of the highest wisdom. Explaining the four contemplations by means of the Four Teachings in this way accords properly with their sense, and so on.

所言下智觀者，觀受由觸，觸由入，入由名色，名色由識，識由行，行由無明。無明顛倒不善思惟，致不善行，感四趣識、名色等。若善思惟，致善行，感人天識、名色等。觀此無明，念念無常，前後不住。所生善惡，遷變速朽。所受名色，衰損代謝。煩惱、業、苦，更互因緣，都無暫停。過去二因，現在五果；現在三因，未來二果。三世迴復，猶若車輪。癡惑之本，既無常、苦、空、無我，則無明滅。無明滅故，諸行滅，乃至老死滅。若不然火，是則無煙，是名子縛斷。無子則無果，滅智灰身，離二十五有，是名果縛斷。則是下智觀十二因緣，得聲聞菩提也。

As for what is called the contemplation of lower wisdom: one contemplates that sensation arises from contact, contact from the six sense-fields, the six sense-fields from name-and-form, name-and-form from consciousness, consciousness from volitional formation, and volitional formation from ignorance. Ignorance, being inverted and lacking wholesome reflection, brings about unwholesome action, which draws forth the consciousness, name-and-form, and the rest of the four evil destinies; if reflection is wholesome, it brings about wholesome action, which draws forth the consciousness, name-and-form, and the rest of humans and gods. Contemplating this ignorance, moment after moment it is impermanent, never abiding either before or after; the good and evil it produces shift and change, swiftly decaying; the name-and-form it receives declines and passes away in succession. Affliction, karma, and suffering, each in turn a cause and condition for the other, never pause even for a moment. The two causes of the past bring forth the five fruits of the present; the three causes of the present bring forth the two fruits of the future — the three periods of time turning and returning, just like the wheel of a cart. Since the root of delusion and confusion is itself impermanent, suffering, empty, and without self, ignorance is thereby extinguished; and because ignorance is extinguished, all the formations are extinguished, down through old age and death being extinguished. If the fire is no longer kindled, there is then no more smoke — this is called the severance of the bondage of the child. Where there is no child, there is no fruit either — the wisdom that brings extinction and the body reduced to ash, departing from the twenty-five states of existence — this is called the severance of the bondage of the fruit. This, then, is the contemplation of the twelve links through lower wisdom, attaining the bodhi of the śrāvaka.

中智者，觀受由觸，乃至行由無明。無明秖是一念癡心，心無形質，但有名字。內、外、中間求字不得。是字不住，亦不不住。猶如幻化，虛誑眼目。無明體相本自不有，妄想因緣和合而生。無所有故，假名無明。不善思惟，心行所造，以不達無明如幻化故，起善、不善思惟，則有善、不善行，受善、不善名色、觸、受。今達無明如幻故，則諸行亦如化。從幻生識、名色等，皆如幻。愛、取、有、生，三世輪轉，幻化遷改，都無真實。有智之人，不應於中而生愛恚。無明既不可得，則無明不生，不生則不滅。諸行、老死亦不生不滅。不生故，則非新；不滅故，則非故。非故者，無故可畢；非新者，無新可造。無新者，子縛斷；無故者，果縛斷。是名中智觀十二因緣，得緣覺菩提。

As for the middle wisdom: one contemplates that sensation arises from contact, down through volitional formation arising from ignorance. Ignorance is simply a single deluded thought-moment of mind; mind has no form or substance, but only a name. Sought within, without, or in between, this name cannot be found at all — this name neither abides nor does it fail to abide; it is just like a magical illusion, deceiving the eye in vain. The essential mark of ignorance is, from the outset, itself without any being at all, arising only through the coming-together of false thought as cause and condition; because it has no being whatsoever, it is provisionally named "ignorance." Unwholesome reflection is fashioned by the activity of mind; because one fails to penetrate that ignorance is like a magical illusion, one gives rise to wholesome or unwholesome reflection, and there is then wholesome or unwholesome action, receiving wholesome or unwholesome name-and-form, contact, and sensation. Now, penetrating that ignorance is like an illusion, the formations, too, are all like a transformation; consciousness, name-and-form, and the rest, arising from illusion, are all likewise like an illusion. Craving, grasping, becoming, and birth, turning through the three periods of time, are illusory transformations, shifting and changing, entirely without any reality. A person of wisdom ought not to give rise to craving or aversion in the midst of any of this. Since ignorance is itself unattainable, ignorance does not arise; and, not arising, it does not cease either. The formations, and old age and death, likewise neither arise nor cease. Because they do not arise, they are not new; because they do not cease, they are not old. Since they are not old, there is no old thing that need come to an end; since they are not new, there is no new thing that need be constructed. Since there is nothing new, the bondage of the child is severed; since there is nothing old, the bondage of the fruit is severed. This is called the contemplation of the twelve links through the middle wisdom, attaining the bodhi of the pratyekabuddha.

上智觀者，觀受由觸，乃至行由無明。無明秖是癡一念心。心癡故，派出煩惱；由煩惱派出諸業；由業派出諸苦。觀此煩惱，種別不同；不同故，業不同；業不同故，苦不同。諸行若干，名色各異。種種三道，無量無邊，分別不濫。知因此煩惱，起此業，得此苦，不關彼業及彼煩惱。如是三道，覆障三德。破障方便，亦復無量。無明若破，顯出般若；業破，顯出解脫；識、名色破，顯出法身。愛、取、有、老死，亦復如是。自既解已，復能化他。於一切種，知一切法，起道種智，導利眾生。是名上智觀十二因緣也。

As for the contemplation of the upper wisdom: one contemplates that sensation arises from contact, down through volitional formation arising from ignorance. Ignorance is simply a single deluded thought-moment of mind; because the mind is deluded, it branches out into affliction; from affliction, it branches out into the various karmas; from karma, it branches out into the various forms of suffering. Contemplating this affliction, its kinds and distinctions are not the same; because they are not the same, the karma they produce is not the same; and because the karma is not the same, the suffering is not the same. The formations are manifold, and name-and-form differ from one case to the next; the various kinds of the three paths are immeasurable and boundless, distinguished without confusion. Knowing that, because of this particular affliction, this particular karma arises, and this particular suffering is obtained, entirely unrelated to any other karma or any other affliction — contemplating the three paths in this way, one sees that they cover over and obstruct the three virtues. The skillful means for breaking through this obstruction are likewise immeasurable: if ignorance is broken through, prajñā is thereby revealed; if karma is broken through, liberation is thereby revealed; if consciousness and name-and-form are broken through, the Dharma-body is thereby revealed — and craving, grasping, becoming, and old-age-and-death are, in just the same way, broken through as well. Having thus liberated himself, one is then further able to transform others; with respect to every kind, knowing every dharma, one gives rise to the wisdom of the paths, guiding and benefiting sentient beings. This is called the contemplation of the twelve links through the upper wisdom.

上上智觀者，觀受由觸，乃至行由無明，知十二支三道即是三德，豈可斷破三德，更求三德？則壞諸法相。煩惱道即般若，當知煩惱不闇；般若即煩惱，般若不明。煩惱既不闇，何須更斷？般若不明，何所能破？闇本非闇，不須於明。如耆婆執毒成藥，豈可捨此取彼？業道即是解脫者，當知業道非縛；解脫即業者，脫非自在。業非縛故，何所可離？脫非自在，何所可得？如神通人，豈避此就彼耶？苦道即法身者，當知苦非生死；法身即生死，法身非樂。苦非生死，何所可憂？法身非樂，何所可喜？如彼虛空，無得無失，不忻不戚。如是觀者，三道不異三德，三德不異三道，亦於三道具一切佛法。何者？三道即三德，三德是大涅槃，名祕密藏，此即具佛果；深觀十二因緣，即是坐道場，此即具佛因。佛因佛果，皆悉具足，餘例可知。是名上上智觀十二因緣，得佛菩提。

As for the contemplation of the highest wisdom: one contemplates that sensation arises from contact, down through volitional formation arising from ignorance, and knows that the three paths of the twelve links are themselves precisely the three virtues — how, then, could one cut off and break through the three virtues, only to seek the three virtues once more? This would destroy the very mark of all dharmas. The path of affliction is itself prajñā — one should know that affliction is not itself dark; prajñā is itself affliction — prajñā is not itself bright. Since affliction is not itself dark, what further need is there to cut it off? Since prajñā is not itself bright, what is there for it to break through? What is dark was, from the outset, never truly dark, and has no need of brightness at all — it is like Jīvaka, who took poison itself and made of it a medicine: how, then, could one abandon this and grasp at that? As for the path of karma being itself liberation, one should know that the path of karma is not itself bondage; liberation is itself karma — liberation is not itself freedom. Since karma is not itself bondage, what is there to be freed from? Since liberation is not itself freedom, what is there to be attained? It is like a person with supernormal powers — how could he possibly avoid the one and go toward the other? As for the path of suffering being itself the Dharma-body, one should know that suffering is not itself birth-and-death; the Dharma-body is itself birth-and-death — the Dharma-body is not itself happiness. Since suffering is not itself birth-and-death, what is there to grieve over? Since the Dharma-body is not itself happiness, what is there to rejoice in? It is like empty space itself — without gain and without loss, neither delighting nor grieving. Contemplating in this way, the three paths are no different from the three virtues, and the three virtues are no different from the three paths; and, within the three paths themselves, every buddha-dharma is already fully present. Why is this? The three paths are themselves the three virtues, and the three virtues are precisely the great nirvāṇa, named the secret treasury — this is itself the fullness of the buddha-fruit; and the deep contemplation of the twelve links is itself the very sitting beneath the bodhi-tree — this is itself the fullness of the buddha-cause. The buddha-cause and the buddha-fruit are both, alike, entirely fulfilled — the remaining cases may be understood by the same pattern. This is called the contemplation of the twelve links through the highest wisdom, attaining the bodhi of the buddha.

約此應判麁妙、開麁顯妙，意可解故，不委記耳。又四智照四境，境若不轉，其智則麁。四境轉成妙境，麁智即成妙智，仍是待絕之意(云云)。

According to this, one ought to judge the coarse and the wondrous, and open the coarse to reveal the wondrous — the sense of this may be understood, and so it is not recorded in detail here. Again, the four kinds of wisdom illuminate the four objects: if the object is not transformed, the wisdom that illuminates it remains coarse; once the four objects are transformed into the wondrous object, the coarse wisdom, too, becomes wondrous wisdom — this, too, carries the sense of the relative and the absolute, and so on.

二、對四種四諦明智者，《大經》云：「知聖諦智，則有二種：中智、上智。中智者，聲聞、緣覺。上智者，諸佛、菩薩。」若依此文，束於體、析，合稱為中。束大乘利、鈍，合稱為上。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, discussing wisdom in relation to the four kinds of Four Noble Truths: the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "As for the wisdom that knows the noble truths, there are two kinds: the middle wisdom and the upper wisdom. The middle wisdom belongs to the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha; the upper wisdom belongs to the buddhas and bodhisattvas." If one follows this text, then gathering together embodying and analysis, they are jointly called the middle; gathering together the sharp and dull faculties of the Mahāyāna, they are jointly called the upper.

今若約根緣利、鈍，內外事、理，開即成四：聲聞根鈍，緣四諦事，即生滅四諦智；緣覺根利，緣四諦理，即無生四諦智；菩薩智淺，緣不思議事，即無量四諦智；諸佛智深，緣不思議理，即無作四諦智也。此乃《大經》之一文。又云：「凡夫有苦無諦，聲聞有苦、有苦諦。」凡夫不見苦理，故言無諦。聲聞能見無常、苦、空，故言有諦。即是生滅四諦智也。又云：「菩薩之人解苦無苦，而有真諦。」即是體苦非苦，故言無苦；即事而真，故言有諦。乃是摩訶衍門無生四諦智也。又云：「知諸陰是苦，知諸入為門，亦名為苦。知諸界為分，亦名為性，亦名為知，是名中智。」依前說者，即屬聲聞也。分別諸苦、諸入、界等有無量相，「我於彼經，竟不說之」，是名上智。受、想、行、識，亦復如是。非諸聲聞、緣覺境界，此則異前兩意。既稱上智，又非二乘境界，豈非別教菩薩觀恒沙佛法如來藏理耶？是為無量四諦智。又云：「如來非苦、非集、非滅、非道、非諦，是實；虛空非苦、非諦，是實。」非苦者，非虛妄生死。非諦者，非二乘涅槃。是實者，即是實相、中道佛性也。又云：「有苦、有苦因、有苦盡、有苦對，如來非苦乃至非對，是故為實。」如此明義，既異上三番，豈非無作四諦智耶？

Now, if one instead opens this out according to sharp and dull faculties, and phenomenal and principial objects both within and beyond the three realms, it forms four: the śrāvaka's faculty is dull, and takes the phenomena of the Four Noble Truths as its object — this is the arising-and-ceasing wisdom of the Four Noble Truths. The pratyekabuddha's faculty is sharp, and takes the principle of the Four Noble Truths as its object — this is the non-arising wisdom of the Four Noble Truths. The bodhisattva's wisdom is shallow, and takes the inconceivable as phenomena as its object — this is the immeasurable wisdom of the Four Noble Truths. The buddhas' wisdom is deep, and takes the inconceivable as principle as its object — this is the uncreated wisdom of the Four Noble Truths. This is one passage from the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra. It also says: "The ordinary being has suffering but no truth; the śrāvaka has suffering, and also has the truth of suffering." The ordinary being does not see the principle of suffering, hence it is said he has no truth; the śrāvaka is able to see impermanence, suffering, and emptiness, hence it is said he has the truth — this is precisely the arising-and-ceasing wisdom of the Four Noble Truths. It also says: "The bodhisattva understands suffering as without suffering, and yet has the real truth" — this is precisely embodying suffering as not suffering, hence it is said he has no suffering; and, being identical with phenomena, real, hence it is said he has the truth — this is precisely the non-arising wisdom of the Four Noble Truths belonging to the gate of the Mahāyāna. It also says: "Knowing that the aggregates are suffering, knowing that the sense-fields are a gateway, this, too, is called suffering; knowing that the elements are a differentiation, this, too, is called nature, and is also called knowing — this is called the middle wisdom." According to the earlier account, this belongs to the śrāvaka. Differentiating the immeasurable marks of suffering, the sense-fields, the elements, and the rest — "in that other sūtra, I did not, in the end, teach this" — this is called the upper wisdom. Sensation, perception, volitional formations, and consciousness are likewise so; this is not the province of any śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha, and thus differs from the two preceding senses. Since it is called the upper wisdom, and yet is not the province of the two vehicles, is this not precisely the distinct teaching's bodhisattva contemplating the principle of the tathāgatagarbha, as numerous as the sands of the Ganges? This is the immeasurable wisdom of the Four Noble Truths. It also says: "The Tathāgata is neither suffering, nor its arising, nor its cessation, nor the path, nor a truth at all — this is real; empty space is neither suffering nor a truth — this is real." "Not suffering" means not the false birth-and-death; "not a truth" means not the two vehicles' own nirvāṇa; "real" means precisely the true mark, the middle way, buddha-nature itself. It also says: "There is suffering, there is the cause of suffering, there is the exhaustion of suffering, there is the counter to suffering; the Tathāgata is neither suffering nor any of the rest — hence, he is real." Since this clarification of the sense already differs from the preceding three rounds, is this not precisely the uncreated wisdom of the Four Noble Truths?

例此一諦為四，餘三亦應爾。謂有集、有集果，有集盡、有集對，有盡、有盡因，有盡障、有盡障相，有對、有對果，有對障、有對障相。如來非此四四十六種，但是於實(云云)。

Following this pattern for the one truth of suffering made into four, the remaining three truths ought likewise to be so: that is, there is arising, there is the fruit of arising, there is the exhaustion of arising, there is the counter to arising; there is cessation, there is the cause of cessation, there is the obstruction to cessation, there is the mark of the obstruction to cessation; there is the path, there is the fruit of the path, there is the obstruction to the path, there is the mark of the obstruction to the path. The Tathāgata is neither any of these four sets of four, sixteen in all — he is simply the real, and so on.

如是等智，觀於四諦。諦既未融，智、諦皆麁。獨有非苦、非對、有實，為妙。若諦圓，智亦隨圓，皆是如來非苦、非諦、是實之妙智也。此即待絕兩意(云云)。

Kinds of wisdom such as these contemplate the Four Noble Truths; as long as the truths themselves are not yet fused together, both the wisdom and the truths remain coarse. Only "not suffering," "not a truth," and "real" alone are wondrous. If the truth itself is perfect, the wisdom, too, follows it into perfection, and all of this becomes the Tathāgata's own wondrous wisdom of "not suffering," "not a truth," and "real" — this, too, carries the double sense of the relative and the absolute, and so on.

Reading note

The twelve-links passage cites the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s own four-tiered scheme of lower/middle/upper/highest wisdom and maps it directly onto the Four Teachings, reusing the tathāgatagarbha-style rhetoric of the shared/distinct boundary (the highest round in particular — "the three paths are themselves the three virtues" — is close in both vocabulary and argument to the *Mohe Zhiguan*'s own treatment of the three obstructions as identical with the three virtues). Wisdom in Relation to the Two Truths: The Twenty-One Kinds of the Provisional and the Real 三、對二諦論智

Chinese Source

天台智者大師說

三、對二諦境明智者，權、實二智也。上真、俗二諦既開七種，今權、實二智亦為七番：內外相即、不相即四也，三相接合七也。若對上數之：析法權實二智，體法二智，體法含中二智，體法顯中二智，別二智，別含圓二智，圓二智。上七番各開隨情、隨情智、隨智，合二十一種諦。今七番二智，亦各開三種：謂化他權實、自行化他權實、自行權實，合二十一權實也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Expounded by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai.

Third, discussing wisdom in relation to the object of the two truths: this is the provisional and real, the two kinds of wisdom. Since the real and conventional two truths, as discussed above, were already opened into seven kinds, the provisional and real two kinds of wisdom likewise form seven rounds: four in which the inward and the outward are either identical or not identical with one another, and three more formed by their mutual reception, making seven in all. Matched against the numbering given above: the analytical two kinds of wisdom, provisional and real; the embodying two kinds of wisdom; the embodying two kinds of wisdom that contain the middle; the embodying two kinds of wisdom that reveal the middle; the distinct teaching's two kinds of wisdom; the distinct teaching's two kinds of wisdom containing the perfect; and the perfect teaching's two kinds of wisdom. The seven rounds discussed above for the two truths were each opened into according-with-feeling, feeling-and-wisdom, and wisdom-alone, forming, in total, twenty-one kinds of truth. So, too, these seven rounds of the two kinds of wisdom are each opened into three kinds: that used in teaching others, that of one's own practice set against teaching others, and that of one's own practice alone — forming, in total, twenty-one kinds of provisional and real wisdom.

若析法權實二智者，照森羅分別為權智，盡森羅分別為實智。說此二智，逗種種緣，作種種說，隨種種欲、種種宜、種種治、種種悟，各隨堪任，當緣分別。雖復種種，悉為析法權實所攝，故有化他二智。化他二智，隨緣之說，皆束為權智；若內自證得，若權、若實，俱是實證，束為實智。內外相望，共為二智，故有自行化他權實二智也。就自證權實，唯獨明了，餘人不見，更判權實，故有自行二智也。今更約三藏重分別之。此佛化二乘人，多用化他實智。二乘稟此化他之實，修成自行之實，故佛印迦葉云：「我之與汝，俱坐解脫床。」即此義也。若化菩薩，多用化他權智。其稟化他之權，修學得成自行之權，佛亦印言：「我亦如汝(云云)。」此三種二智，若望體法二智，悉皆是權。故龍樹破云：「豈有不淨心中修菩提道？猶如毒器，不任貯食，食則殺人。」此正破析法意也。故皆是權(云云)。

As for the analytical two kinds of wisdom, provisional and real: illuminating the manifold array of phenomena through differentiation is the provisional wisdom; exhausting the manifold array of phenomena through differentiation is the real wisdom. Teaching these two kinds of wisdom, meeting each of the various conditions, one makes each of the various kinds of explanation, according with each of the various desires, each of the various fitting circumstances, each of the various remedies, and each of the various awakenings, each according to what a given being can bear, differentiated according to the conditions at hand. Though manifold in this way, all of it is subsumed under the analytical provisional and real wisdom — hence, this forms the two kinds of wisdom used in teaching others. This wisdom used in teaching others, being explanation that follows conditions, is entirely gathered together as the provisional wisdom; whereas the realization attained inwardly, whether provisional or real, is, in either case, a real realization, and is gathered together as the real wisdom. Setting the inward against the outward, together they form the two kinds of wisdom — hence, this forms the provisional and real two kinds of wisdom of one's own practice set against teaching others. With respect to the provisional and real attained through one's own realization alone, understood by oneself alone, unseen by anyone else, this is further judged into provisional and real — hence, this forms the two kinds of wisdom of one's own practice alone. Now, explaining this once more specifically in terms of the Tripiṭaka teaching: this buddha, in transforming the two vehicles, for the most part uses the real wisdom in teaching others; the two vehicles, receiving this real wisdom taught to others, cultivate it into the real wisdom of their own practice — hence the Buddha sealed Kāśyapa's own attainment, saying: "You and I alike sit upon the very same bed of liberation" — this is precisely this sense. If transforming bodhisattvas, he for the most part uses the provisional wisdom in teaching others; they, receiving this provisional wisdom taught to others, cultivate and study it until they attain the provisional wisdom of their own practice, and the Buddha likewise seals this, saying: "I, too, am just as you are," and so on. These three kinds of the two wisdoms, set against the embodying two kinds of wisdom, are all, alike, provisional. Hence Nāgārjuna refutes this, saying: "How could one possibly cultivate the path to bodhi within an impure mind? It is just like a poisoned vessel, unfit to hold food — whoever eats from it is killed." This is precisely a refutation of the analytical approach's own intent — hence, all of it is provisional, and so on.

體法權實二智者，體森羅之色，即是於空。即色是權智，即空是實智。《大品》云：「即色是空，非色滅空。」正是此義。為緣說二，緣別不同，說亦種種；雖復異說，悉為化他權實所攝，故有化他二智。化他二智既是隨情，皆束為權；內證權實既是自證，悉名為實。以自之實對他之權，故有自行化他二智也。就自證得又分權實，故有自行二智也。此三二智望含中二智，復皆名權。何者？無中道故(云云)。

As for the embodying two kinds of wisdom, provisional and real: embodying the manifold array of form directly, it is itself precisely emptiness. Taking form as it is to be provisional wisdom; taking emptiness as it is to be real wisdom. The Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra says: "Form as it is, is itself emptiness — it is not that form is extinguished into emptiness." This is precisely this very sense. Teaching these two kinds of wisdom for the sake of conditions, since the conditions differ from case to case, the teaching, too, takes many forms; though the explanations differ, all of them are subsumed under the provisional and real wisdom used in teaching others — hence, this forms the two kinds of wisdom used in teaching others. Since this wisdom used in teaching others already follows feeling, all of it is gathered as the provisional; the provisional and real wisdom attained through inward realization, being self-realized, is entirely named the real. Setting one's own real against another's provisional, this forms the two kinds of wisdom of one's own practice set against teaching others. With respect to what is attained through self-realization, this is further divided into provisional and real — hence, this forms the two kinds of wisdom of one's own practice alone. These three kinds of the two wisdoms, set against the two kinds of wisdom that contain the middle, are, once again, all named provisional. Why is this? Because they lack the middle way, and so on.

體法含中權實二智者，體色即空不空。照色是權智；空不空是實智。說此二智，赴無量緣，隨情異說；雖復無量，悉是含中二智所攝，故有化他二智。化他二智本是逗機，皆名為權；自證二智皆名為實。於自證二智更分權、實，故有自行二智。此三二智望顯中二智，悉皆是權。何者？帶於空真及教道方便故。

As for the embodying two kinds of wisdom, provisional and real, that contain the middle: embodying form, it is itself both empty and not empty. Illuminating form is the provisional wisdom; emptiness-and-not-emptiness is the real wisdom. Teaching these two kinds of wisdom, meeting immeasurable conditions, one explains them variously according to feeling; though immeasurable, all of this is subsumed under the two kinds of wisdom that contain the middle — hence, this forms the two kinds of wisdom used in teaching others. This wisdom used in teaching others is, from the outset, meeting particular capacities, and so is entirely named provisional; the two kinds of wisdom of self-realization are entirely named real. Within the two kinds of wisdom of self-realization, these are further divided into provisional and real, forming the two kinds of wisdom of one's own practice. These three kinds of the two wisdoms, set against the two kinds of wisdom that reveal the middle, are all, alike, provisional. Why is this? Because they still carry the true truth of emptiness and the skillful means of the path of teaching.

又，體法顯中權實二智者，體色即空不空，一切法趣空不空。了色是權智；空不空、一切法趣空不空是實智。為緣說二，緣既無量，說亦無量；無量之說悉為顯中二智所攝，故有化他二智。化他二智既是隨緣，悉名為權；自證二智既是證得，悉名為實。以自望他故，有自行化他二智。就自證二智，更分權實。此三二智望別權實二智，悉皆是權。何者？帶即空及教道方便故。

Again, as for the embodying two kinds of wisdom, provisional and real, that reveal the middle: embodying form, it is itself both empty and not empty, and all dharmas converge upon emptiness and not-emptiness. Fully understanding form is the provisional wisdom; emptiness-and-not-emptiness, and all dharmas converging upon emptiness and not-emptiness, is the real wisdom. Teaching these two for the sake of conditions, since the conditions are themselves immeasurable, the teaching, too, is immeasurable; the immeasurable teachings are all subsumed under the two kinds of wisdom that reveal the middle — hence, this forms the two kinds of wisdom used in teaching others. This wisdom used in teaching others, following conditions as it does, is entirely named provisional; the two kinds of wisdom of self-realization, being what is attained through realization, are entirely named real. Setting oneself against another, this forms the two kinds of wisdom of one's own practice set against teaching others. Within the two kinds of wisdom of self-realization, these are further divided into provisional and real. These three kinds of the two wisdoms, set against the distinct teaching's two kinds of wisdom, provisional and real, are all, alike, provisional. Why is this? Because they still carry identity-with-emptiness and the skillful means of the path of teaching.

別權實二智者，體色即空、不空。色、空俱是權智；不空是實智。以此二智，隨百千緣，種種分別；分別雖多，悉為次第二智所攝，故有化他二智。化他二智悉是為緣，皆名為權；自證二智既是證得，悉名為實。以自對他故，有自他二智。就自證權實，自分二智故，有自行二智。此三二智望別含圓二智，悉復是權。何者？以次第故、帶教道故。

As for the distinct teaching's two kinds of wisdom, provisional and real: embodying form, it is itself both empty and not empty; form and emptiness together are the provisional wisdom; not-emptiness alone is the real wisdom. Using these two kinds of wisdom, according with a hundred and a thousand conditions, one differentiates them variously; though the differentiations are many, all of them are subsumed under the two kinds of wisdom taught in sequence — hence, this forms the two kinds of wisdom used in teaching others. This wisdom used in teaching others is entirely for the sake of conditions, and so is entirely named provisional; the two kinds of wisdom of self-realization, being what is attained through realization, are entirely named real. Setting oneself against another, this forms the two kinds of wisdom of self and other. Within the provisional and real wisdom of self-realization, this is itself further divided into two kinds of wisdom, forming the two kinds of wisdom of one's own practice. These three kinds of the two wisdoms, set against the distinct teaching's two kinds of wisdom containing the perfect, are, once again, all provisional. Why is this? Because they proceed in sequence, and because they still carry the path of teaching.

別含圓權實二智者，色空、不空，一切法趣不空。色空名權智；一切法趣不空為實智。以此二智隨百千緣，種種分別；分別雖多，悉為別含圓二智所攝，故有化他二智。化他二智既是為緣，悉皆是權；自證二智既是證得，悉名為實。自他相望，共為二智。就自證得，更分權實，故有自行二智。此三二智望圓二智，悉復是權。何者？帶次第及教道故。

As for the distinct teaching's two kinds of wisdom, provisional and real, containing the perfect: form is empty and not empty, and all dharmas converge upon not-emptiness. Form-and-emptiness is named the provisional wisdom; all dharmas converging upon not-emptiness is the real wisdom. Using these two kinds of wisdom according with a hundred and a thousand conditions, one differentiates them variously; though the differentiations are many, all of them are subsumed under the distinct teaching's two kinds of wisdom containing the perfect — hence, this forms the two kinds of wisdom used in teaching others. This wisdom used in teaching others, being for the sake of conditions, is entirely provisional; the two kinds of wisdom of self-realization, being what is attained through realization, are entirely named real. Setting self against other, together they form the two kinds of wisdom. Within self-realization, this is further divided into provisional and real, forming the two kinds of wisdom of one's own practice. These three kinds of the two wisdoms, set against the perfect teaching's own two kinds of wisdom, are, once again, all provisional. Why is this? Because they still carry sequence and the path of teaching.

圓權實二智者，即色是空、不空，一切法趣色、趣空、趣不空。一切法趣色、趣空是權智；一切法趣不空是實智。如此實智即是權智，權智即實智，無二無別。為化眾生，種種隨緣、隨欲、隨宜、隨治、隨悟。雖種種說，悉為圓二智所攝，故有化他二智。化他二智既是隨情，悉復是權；自證二智，悉名為實。就自證中，更分二智，故有三種不同也。此之二智，不帶析法等十八種二智方便，唯有真權、真實，名佛權實。如經：「如來知見廣大深遠，方便波羅蜜皆悉具足。」獨稱為妙，待前為麁。

As for the perfect teaching's own two kinds of wisdom, provisional and real: form itself is empty and not empty, and all dharmas converge upon form, upon emptiness, and upon not-emptiness. All dharmas converging upon form and upon emptiness is the provisional wisdom; all dharmas converging upon not-emptiness is the real wisdom. In this case, the real wisdom is itself precisely the provisional wisdom, and the provisional wisdom is itself precisely the real wisdom — without duality and without distinction. In order to transform sentient beings, one follows the various conditions, the various desires, the various fitting circumstances, the various remedies, and the various awakenings; though the teaching is manifold, all of it is subsumed under the perfect teaching's own two kinds of wisdom — hence, this forms the two kinds of wisdom used in teaching others. This wisdom used in teaching others, following feeling as it does, is, once again, entirely provisional; the two kinds of wisdom of self-realization are entirely named real. Within self-realization, this is further divided into two kinds of wisdom, and so there are three kinds that are not the same. This pair of wisdoms does not carry along the eighteen kinds of two-wisdom skillful means from the analytical approach onward — there is only the true provisional and the true real, named the buddha's own provisional and real wisdom. As the sūtra says: "The Tathāgata's knowledge and vision are vast, great, deep, and far-reaching; the skillful means and the pāramitās are all, alike, fully complete." This alone is called wondrous; set against what precedes it, this is coarse.

又，從析法二智至顯中二智，凡十二種二智，待前皆名為麁，顯中為妙。何以故？此妙不異後妙故。又從次第二智，凡九種二智，待前為麁，不次第為妙。又前十八種二智皆麁，唯不次第三種為妙。又不次第二種為麁，一種為妙。

Again, from the analytical two kinds of wisdom up through the two kinds of wisdom that reveal the middle — twelve kinds of the two wisdoms in all — set against what precedes them, all are called coarse, while revealing the middle is wondrous. Why is this? Because this wondrous is no different from the wondrous that follows it. Again, from the two kinds of wisdom taught in sequence — nine kinds of the two wisdoms in all — set against what precedes them, they are coarse, while what is not taught in sequence is wondrous. Again, the preceding eighteen kinds of the two wisdoms are all coarse, and only the three kinds not taught in sequence are wondrous. Again, of the two kinds not taught in sequence, two are coarse, and the one that remains is wondrous.

又歷五味教者，乳教具三種、九種二智。酪教一種、三種二智。生蘇四種、十二種二智。熟蘇具三種、九種二智。此經但二種、三種二智。若酪教中，權實皆麁；醍醐教中，權實皆妙。餘三味中，權實有麁有妙，可以意推。

Again, passing through the Five Flavors of teaching: the milk teaching possesses three kinds, or nine kinds, of the two wisdoms. The cream teaching possesses one kind, or three kinds. The curdled-butter teaching possesses four kinds, or twelve kinds. The clarified-butter teaching possesses three kinds, or nine kinds. The present sūtra possesses only two kinds, or three kinds. In the cream teaching, the provisional and the real are both, alike, coarse; in the ghee teaching, the provisional and the real are both, alike, wondrous. In the remaining three flavors, the provisional and the real each have their coarse and their wondrous aspects, which may be worked out by reflection.

若不作如上釋諸智者，經論異說，意則難解。何者？《華嚴》解初住心云：「三世諸佛不知初住智。」世人釋云：「如實智，佛自不知佛如實智，亦不知初住如實智。」此釋自謂於理為通，其實不允。若藏、通等佛不論如實智，云何於自如實智不知耶？別教初住不得如實智，云何言不知？若得前來諸智意者，三世三藏佛不知圓教初住智，此則事理二釋俱無滯也。此中義兼二種：一、分別二十一種權實，二、待麁論妙。如上說。

If one does not explain the various kinds of wisdom as has been done above, the differing accounts found across the sūtras and treatises become difficult to understand. Why is this? The Avataṃsaka Sūtra, explaining the mind of the initial abiding, says: "The buddhas of the three periods of time do not know the wisdom of the initial abiding." Worldly interpreters explain this, saying: "As for the wisdom of thusness-as-real, even the buddha himself does not know his own wisdom of thusness-as-real, nor does he know the wisdom of thusness-as-real belonging to the initial abiding." This explanation considers itself, in principle, to be a coherent reading, but it is, in fact, not correct. If the buddhas of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings do not even discuss the wisdom of thusness-as-real at all, how could it be said that they fail to know their own wisdom of thusness-as-real? And if the distinct teaching's own initial abiding does not attain the wisdom of thusness-as-real at all, how could it be said that it is unknown to it? If one grasps the sense of the various kinds of wisdom discussed above, then it is the buddhas of the Tripiṭaka teaching across the three periods of time who do not know the wisdom of the perfect teaching's own initial abiding — and, read this way, both the phenomenal and the principial explanations proceed without any obstruction. Within this, the sense embraces two things: first, the differentiation of the twenty-one kinds of the provisional and the real; second, discussing the wondrous in relation to the coarse, as explained above.

若開麁顯妙者，諸方便諦既融成妙諦，對諦立智，悉非復麁。如賤人舍，王若過者，舍則莊嚴；如眾流入海，同一鹹味。開諸麁智，即是妙智也。

As for opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous: since all the skillful-means truths have already been fused together into the wondrous truth, the wisdom established in relation to the truth, too, is no longer coarse at all. It is like the dwelling of a lowly person: if a king should happen to pass through it, that very dwelling becomes adorned and magnificent; or like the many streams flowing into the sea, all alike becoming of a single salty taste. Opening up all the various kinds of coarse wisdom, they become, in just this way, wondrous wisdom.

二智多有所關，須商略類通。今對七種二諦，明二十一種權實，以為章門。若得此意，約因緣境，亦應如此。謂析因緣智、體因緣智、含中因緣智、顯中因緣智、次第因緣智、帶次第因緣智、不次第因緣智，一一各有化他、自行化他、自行等三種分別，合有二十一種；分別麁妙、判五味多少、論待絕等。四諦、三諦、一諦等，亦應如是。當自思之，何煩具記也。

The two kinds of wisdom bear upon a great many matters, and their categories must be worked out and cross-referenced with care. Here, matching them against the seven kinds of two truths, the twenty-one kinds of the provisional and the real have been clarified, to serve as the governing chapter-outline. If one grasps this sense, then, taken in relation to the object of dependent origination, the same pattern ought likewise to apply: that is, the analytical wisdom of dependent origination, the embodying wisdom of dependent origination, the wisdom of dependent origination that contains the middle, the wisdom of dependent origination that reveals the middle, the wisdom of dependent origination taught in sequence, the wisdom of dependent origination that carries sequence along with it, and the wisdom of dependent origination not taught in sequence — each and every one of these, in turn, has its own threefold differentiation of that used in teaching others, that of one's own practice set against teaching others, and that of one's own practice alone, forming, in total, twenty-one kinds; and the coarse and the wondrous are to be differentiated among them, the proportion among the Five Flavors judged, and the relative and the absolute discussed, just as before. The Four Noble Truths, the three truths, the one truth, and the rest ought likewise to be treated in just this way. One should reflect on this for oneself — why should it need to be recorded here in its entirety?

問：隨情諦及化他智，何意無量？隨智諦及自行智，何意不多？答：秖約一人，未得道時，見心橫起，邪執無窮。何況多人，種種各異？為是義故，隨情則多。智見於理，理唯一種，不得有異(云云)。夫二諦差別已如上說。

Question: Why is it that the truths that accord with feeling, and the wisdom used in teaching others, are said to be immeasurable, while the truth that accords with wisdom, and the wisdom of one's own practice, are said not to be many? Answer: Simply take a single person: before he has attained the path, his deluded mind arises awry, and his wrong attachments are endless — how much more so, then, among many people, each differing from the others in every way! For this reason, according with feeling is many. Wisdom, however, sees principle directly, and principle is only ever of a single kind — it cannot admit of any variation, and so on. The distinctions among the two truths have already been explained as above.

說此七權實、二十一權實，頗用世人所執義不？頗同世人所說語不？頗用諸論所立義不？既不從世人，亦不從文疏，特是推大小乘經，作此釋耳。若破若立，皆是《法華》之意。若巧、拙相形，以通經二智，破三藏經二智；乃至次第、不次第相形，以圓經二智，破別經二智。方便諸經，明智既麁，通經之論，豈得為妙？經論既爾，弘經論人，何勞擊射？任其所說，自有所墮。

In setting forth these seven kinds of the provisional and real, and these twenty-one kinds of the provisional and real, does this in any way make use of the senses the worldly cling to? Does it in any way match what the worldly themselves say? Does it in any way make use of the senses established by the various treatises? It follows neither the worldly, nor any commentarial literature — it is, specifically, an explanation reached by directly weighing the Mahāyāna and Hīnayāna sūtras against one another. Whether refuting or establishing, all of it carries the Lotus's own intent. Where the skillful and the clumsy are set in contrast, the shared teaching's own two kinds of wisdom refute the Tripiṭaka teaching's own two kinds of wisdom; and on down to where the sequential and the non-sequential are set in contrast, the perfect teaching's own two kinds of wisdom refute the distinct teaching's own two kinds of wisdom. Since the various skillful-means sūtras' own clarification of wisdom is itself already coarse, how could the treatises expounding the shared teaching's sūtras possibly be wondrous? Since the sūtras and treatises are already thus, what need is there for those who propagate these sūtras and treatises to attack one another? Let each account stand as it is spoken — each will, of itself, fall where it falls.

若作生滅解權實者，墮在初番；若作不生不滅解者，墮第二番；乃至第七番，亦可知。又縱廣引經論莊嚴己義者，亦不得出初番隨情二諦化他權實，況出初番第二、第三權實！尚不出初番三種權實，況第七番三種權實！若但以初番二智，破一切世間情執略盡，假令得入化城，秖是自行實智，尚不得化他之權，何況能得後番諸智！

If one interprets the provisional and the real according to arising-and-ceasing, one falls into the first round; if one interprets them according to non-arising-and-non-ceasing, one falls into the second round; and so on, down through the seventh round, which may likewise be understood accordingly. And even if one broadly cites the sūtras and treatises to adorn and support one's own position, one still cannot go beyond the first round's own provisional and real wisdom, used in teaching others according with feeling within the two truths — how much less, then, could one go beyond the first round's own second and third kinds of the provisional and real! If one cannot even go beyond the first round's own three kinds of the provisional and real, how much less, then, the seventh round's own three kinds! If one merely uses the first round's two kinds of wisdom to refute, in broad outline, all worldly attachment born of feeling, then, even granting that one thereby enters the phantom city, this is still merely the real wisdom of one's own practice — one has not even attained the provisional wisdom used in teaching others, how much less, then, could one attain any of the later rounds' own kinds of wisdom!

若尋二十一種二智，凡破幾外見，凡破幾權經論，復顯幾是，立幾權經論，然後方稱妙權妙實。世人全不識一兩種權實之意，而情中即計為智。若是智者，破何惑？見何理？未見理、未破惑，生死浩然，非情何謂？今若待前諸麁智而明妙智者，法華破待之意也。若其會者，一切權經論所明諦理皆成妙理，無非智地。會一切權經論所明二智無非妙智，悉是大車。如此破會深廣，莫以《中論》相比，可熟思之(云云)。

If one examines the twenty-one kinds of the two wisdoms, one must ask: how many outside views does each, in total, refute? How many skillful-means sūtras and treatises does each, in total, refute? How many does it, in turn, reveal to be correct, and how many skillful-means sūtras and treatises does it, in turn, establish? Only then may one properly speak of the wondrous provisional and the wondrous real. The worldly do not recognize even the sense of one or two kinds of the provisional and real at all, and yet, within their own feeling, they at once reckon themselves to possess wisdom. If one is truly wise, what delusion has one refuted? What principle has one seen? Not yet having seen principle, and not yet having refuted delusion, birth-and-death remains vast and boundless — if this is not feeling, then what is it? Now, if one speaks of the wondrous wisdom in relation to all the preceding kinds of coarse wisdom, this is precisely the Lotus's own sense of refutation-in-relation. But if one gathers them all together, then every truth and principle clarified by all the skillful-means sūtras and treatises becomes, itself, the wondrous principle, and there is nothing that is not the very ground of wisdom; gathering together all the two kinds of wisdom clarified by all the skillful-means sūtras and treatises, there is nothing that is not wondrous wisdom — all of it is the great cart. Such refutation and such gathering-together as this, deep and vast as it is, should not be compared with the Middle Treatise's own approach — one ought to reflect on this carefully, and so on.

Reading note

This opens Fascicle 3, Lower Half (卷三下). The sevenfold provisional-and-real wisdom scheme mapped here — analytical / embodying / embodying-containing-the-middle / embodying-revealing-the-middle / distinct / distinct-containing-the-perfect / perfect — runs parallel to, but is not identical with, the seven kinds of two truths from earlier in this fascicle: there the seven were named by teaching (Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, perfect, and their "entering" combinations), while here the same sevenfold structure is named by method (analysis versus direct embodying of dharmas, and whether the middle is merely contained within a teaching's own two truths or fully revealed by it) — the *Fahua Wenju*'s own "received into a higher teaching" (接) doctrine describes the same underlying transition between adjacent rungs of this ladder. Zhiyi's closing insistence that none of this vocabulary derives from "the worldly" or from "commentarial literature" but is reached by directly weighing sūtra against sūtra is a recurring authorial signature already seen in the Guangzhai rebuttal of Fascicle 1.
