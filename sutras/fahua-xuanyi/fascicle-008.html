Explaining the Common Name

Fascicle 8 opens by explaining the word "sūtra" itself (通名) — the name shared by every scripture's own title, closing Explaining the Name (釋名) as a whole — before turning to the treatise's second Section, Clarifying the Essence (顯體).

Status: in progress Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

Explaining the Common Name: "Sūtra" 釋通名

Chinese Source

第二、釋通名者，「經」一字也，具存胡音，應云「薩達磨分陀利脩多羅」。薩達磨，此翻妙法；分陀利，此翻蓮華，已如上釋；脩多羅，或云脩單蘭，或云脩妬路。彼方楚夏，此土翻譯不同，或言無翻，或言有翻。釋此為五：一、明無翻。二、明有翻。三、和融有無。四、歷法明經。五、觀心明經。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining the common name: "sūtra" is a single word. Preserving the foreign pronunciation in full, it ought to be rendered "saddharma-puṇḍarīka-sūtra." "Saddharma" is rendered here as "wondrous dharma"; "puṇḍarīka" is rendered here as "lotus flower," already explained above; "sūtra" is sometimes rendered "sūtrānta," sometimes "sūtra-patha." Just as the various regions of that foreign land have their own dialects, so too, in translating into this land, renderings differ: some say it is untranslatable, some say it is translatable. This is explained under five headings: first, clarifying its untranslatability; second, clarifying its translatability; third, reconciling the translatable and the untranslatable; fourth, explaining "sūtra" through the various dharmas; fifth, explaining "sūtra" through the contemplation of mind.

Untranslatability 明無翻

Chinese Source

言無翻者，彼語多含，此語單淺，不可以單翻複，應留本音。而言「經」者，開善云：非正翻也，但以此代彼耳。此間聖說為經，賢說子史；彼聖稱經，菩薩稱論；既不可翻，宜以此代彼，故稱經也。既不可翻而含五義：一、法本，亦云出生。二云微發，亦云顯示。三云涌泉。四云繩墨。五云結鬘。今秖作五義不可翻，今於一中作三，三五十五義：一、教本。二、行本。三、義本。今廣釋之。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for saying it is untranslatable: the foreign word carries many senses at once, while the word in this land is single and shallow — one cannot use a single, simple word to translate something so manifold, and the original sound ought to be retained instead. As for calling it "sūtra": Kaishan says this is not a proper translation at all, but merely uses this word to stand in place of that one. In this land, what a sage teaches is called a "canon," and what a worthy teaches is called the "masters and histories"; in that foreign land, what a sage teaches is called "sūtra," and what a bodhisattva teaches is called a "treatise" — since it cannot be properly translated, it is fitting to use this word to stand in place of that one, and hence it is called "sūtra." Since it cannot be translated, it carries five senses: first, dharma-source, also called "giving rise to birth"; second, subtle arising, also called "manifestation"; third, gushing spring; fourth, marking-line; fifth, binding a garland. Here, these are simply the five untranslatable senses — and within each single one of these five, a further three senses are found, making fifteen senses in all: first, the source of teaching; second, the source of practice; third, the source of meaning. This is now explained at length.

言法本者，一切皆不可說，以四悉檀因緣則有言說：世界悉檀說，則為教本；為人、對治，則為行本；第一義悉檀，則為義本。所言教本者，金口所說一言為本，流出無量言教。若通、若別，當時被物，聞即得道。故經言：一一脩多羅，復有無量脩多羅以為眷屬。

若後人不解，菩薩以佛教為本，作通論、別論，申通、別經，令佛意不壅，尋者得道，良由其論有本故也。諸外道等，雖有所說，不與脩多羅合，戲論無本，不能得道。經是行本者，示人無諍法，導達通塞，開明眼目，救治人病，如教脩行，則起通別諸行，從此至彼，入清涼池，至甘露地泥洹真法寶，眾生從種種門入，故知經是行本。經是義本者，尋一句詮於一義，尋無量句詮無量義，或尋一句詮於無量義，尋無量句詮於一義，若通、若別尋詮會入，故經是義本。束此三種為法門者，教本即是聞慧，行本即是思慧，義本是脩慧，見真法本之義，尚已多含，故不可翻也。或言出生，例此可知。

As for "dharma-source": all things without exception are, in themselves, inexpressible, and it is only by the causes and conditions of the four siddhāntas that there comes to be any expression at all — teaching by the mundane siddhānta is the source of teaching; teaching by the individual and therapeutic siddhāntas is the source of practice; teaching by the siddhānta of ultimate meaning is the source of meaning. As for what is called the source of teaching: a single word spoken by the Buddha's own golden mouth serves as the source, from which immeasurable further words and teachings flow forth — whether common or particular, meeting beings at the appropriate time, so that, upon hearing it, they attain the path. Hence the sūtra says: each and every sūtra has, further, immeasurable further sūtras as its own retinue.

If later persons do not understand it, bodhisattvas take the Buddha's own teaching as their source and compose common treatises and particular treatises, expounding both common and particular sūtras, so that the Buddha's own intent is never obstructed, and whoever seeks it out attains the path — precisely because their treatises have this same source. The various non-Buddhist outsiders, though they too have their own teachings, do not accord with the sūtra, and their idle theorizing, having no such source, is unable to bring anyone to the path. As for the sūtra being the source of practice: it shows people the dharma free of contention, guides them through what is passable and what is blocked, opens and clarifies the eye, and heals and remedies the sickness of the person — cultivating practice according to this teaching, one gives rise to every common and particular practice, proceeding from this shore to that one, entering the cooling pond, arriving at the ground of sweet dew, the true Dharma-jewel of nirvāṇa, sentient beings entering through many different gates — hence one knows the sūtra is the source of practice. As for the sūtra being the source of meaning: seeking out a single phrase, one meaning is expounded; seeking out immeasurable phrases, immeasurable meanings are expounded; or seeking out a single phrase, immeasurable meanings are expounded; seeking out immeasurable phrases, a single meaning is expounded — whether common or particular, seeking out what is expounded, one converges upon it — hence the sūtra is the source of meaning. Gathering these three together as the Dharma-gate: the source of teaching is precisely the wisdom of hearing; the source of practice is precisely the wisdom of reflection; the source of meaning is the wisdom of cultivation. Seeing the sense of this true dharma-source is already so manifold that it cannot be translated. It is also said to mean "giving rise to birth," which may be understood by this same pattern.

二、含微發者，佛以四悉檀說，言辭巧妙，次第詮量初、中、後善，圓滿具足，如大海水漸漸轉深。聞教之者，初聞世界悉檀，次第領受，分別法相，微有解生，漸漸增長，明練通達。又遍讀諸異論，廣知智者意，多聞強識，以至成佛，就此即約教論微發。初聞為人、對治，即能起行，始人天小行，次戒定慧入無漏行，見道修道，遂證無學。從小入大，終于妙覺，是約行論微發。初聞第一義悉檀，展轉增廣，入於聞、思，煗、頂、世第一。次、入見諦，得真第一義；次、入脩道至無學，從小入大，見似真中道，起自毫末，終成合抱也。束此三發為法門者，依小乘，即三種解脫發；依大乘初住中，有教發是般若，行發即如來藏，理發是實相。微發已自多含，故不可翻也。

Second, containing "subtle arising": the Buddha teaches by means of the four siddhāntas, his words skillful and wondrous, expounding and measuring, in due sequence, what is good at the beginning, in the middle, and at the end, perfectly complete — like the great ocean's own water, growing gradually ever deeper. One who hears this teaching first hears the mundane siddhānta, receiving it in due sequence, distinguishing the marks of dharmas, a subtle understanding beginning to arise, growing gradually greater, becoming clear, refined, and penetrating. Further reading through every differing treatise, broadly knowing the wise ones' own intent, of vast learning and firm memory, one arrives at last at buddhahood — this, in terms of the teaching, discusses subtle arising. First hearing the individual and therapeutic siddhāntas, one is thereby able to give rise to practice — beginning with the small practices of humans and devas, next entering the untainted practice through precepts, concentration, and wisdom, the path of seeing and the path of cultivation, until at last realizing the stage beyond further training — proceeding from the small into the great, ending at last in wondrous enlightenment: this, in terms of practice, discusses subtle arising. First hearing the siddhānta of ultimate meaning, turning and turning, ever broadening, one enters the wisdom of hearing and of reflection, then warmth, summit, and the supreme worldly dharma; next one enters the path of seeing the truths, attaining the true, ultimate meaning; next one enters the path of cultivation, arriving at the stage beyond further training — proceeding from the small into the great, seeing the resemblance and then the true middle way, arising from the very tip of a hair and ending at last as a tree one's arms cannot encircle. Gathering these three "arisings" together as the Dharma-gate: according to the Hīnayāna, these are the three kinds of liberation's own arising; according to the Mahāyāna's own initial abiding, the arising of teaching is prajñā, the arising of practice is the tathāgatagarbha, and the arising of principle is the real mark. "Subtle arising" is already so manifold in itself that it cannot be translated.

三、含涌泉者，從譬為名也。佛以四悉檀說法，文義無盡，法流不絕。若聞世界，說一句解無量句，月四月至歲，如風於空中，自在無障礙，說初心解，已如涌泉，何況後心！何況如來！猶如石泉流潤遍益也！若聞為人、對治，起無量行，恒沙佛法，種種法門，一行無量行，入善境界，登八正直道。若聞第一義，理若虛空，虛空之法，不可格量、遍一切處，是名義涌泉。束此為法門者，教泉是法無礙辯，行泉即辭無礙辯，義泉即義無礙辯。樂說通三處，涌泉已自多含，故不可翻也。

四、含繩墨者，佛以四悉檀說。初聞世界，裁愛見之邪教，不為邪風倒惑，得入正轍，即教繩墨也。若聞為人、對治，遠離非道，入好正濟道品之路，即行繩墨也。若聞第一義，裁愛見此岸，得至彼岸，不保生死亦不住無為，即義繩墨。束此為法門者，教裁邪即是正語；行裁邪即正業、正精進、正念、正定等；義裁邪即正見、正思惟等。繩墨已自多含，故不可翻。

Third, containing "gushing spring": this name is taken from a metaphor. The Buddha teaches the Dharma by means of the four siddhāntas, its text and meaning inexhaustible, the flow of the Dharma never ceasing. If one hears the mundane siddhānta, expounding a single phrase resolves immeasurable further phrases, a month, four months, up to a year — like the wind moving through open space, sovereign and unobstructed; the understanding of the very first mind, already like a gushing spring — how much more so the later mind! How much more so the Tathāgata himself! Just like a spring within stone, its flowing moisture benefiting everywhere pervasively! If one hears the individual and therapeutic siddhāntas, immeasurable practices arise, every buddha-dharma numerous as the sands of the Ganges, every kind of Dharma-gate, a single practice being immeasurable practices, entering the good state, ascending the eightfold correct and upright path. If one hears the siddhānta of ultimate meaning, its principle is like empty space itself — the dharma of empty space cannot be measured or bounded, pervading every place — this is named the gushing spring of meaning. Gathering this as the Dharma-gate: the spring of teaching is the unobstructed eloquence of dharma; the spring of practice is the unobstructed eloquence of words; the spring of meaning is the unobstructed eloquence of meaning. Eloquence in teaching pervades all three of these places — "gushing spring" is already so manifold in itself that it cannot be translated.

Fourth, containing "marking-line": the Buddha teaches by means of the four siddhāntas. First hearing the mundane siddhānta, one cuts away the deviant teaching of craving and views, no longer overturned and deluded by the deviant wind, and thereby enters the correct track — this is the marking-line of teaching. If one hears the individual and therapeutic siddhāntas, one departs far from the wrong path, entering the good, correct road of the factors that ferry one across — this is the marking-line of practice. If one hears the siddhānta of ultimate meaning, one cuts away craving and views on this shore, and is thereby able to arrive at the far shore, neither clinging to birth-and-death nor abiding in the unconditioned — this is the marking-line of meaning. Gathering this as the Dharma-gate: the teaching's own cutting-away of the deviant is precisely correct speech; the practice's own cutting-away of the deviant is precisely correct action, correct diligence, correct mindfulness, and correct concentration, and the rest; the meaning's own cutting-away of the deviant is precisely correct view and correct intention, and the rest. "Marking-line" is already so manifold in itself that it cannot be translated.

五、含結鬘者，結教行理，如結華鬘，令不零落。世界悉檀結佛言教不零落，為人、對治結眾行不零落，第一義結義理不零落。束此為法門者，結教成口無失，結行即身無失，結義即意無失，亦是三種共智慧行，亦是三陀羅尼。教不零落是聞持陀羅尼，行不零落行陀羅尼，義不零落即總持陀羅尼。若作嚴身釋者，即是約教名智慧莊嚴，約行名福德莊嚴，約義即是所莊嚴。所莊嚴即是法身，為定慧莊嚴也。一切眾生皆有法身，法身體素，天龍之所忽劣，若修學定慧，莊嚴法身，則一切見敬也。

舊云：經含五義，今則經含十五義，豈可單漢翻胡，名含之釋如此(云云)。

訓者訓常，今釋其訓。天魔外道不能改壞，名為教常。真正無雜，無能踰過，名為行常。湛然不動，決無異趣，名為理常。又訓法者，法可軌，行可軌，理可軌。今直釋訓已含六義，況胡言重複，而可單翻耶？

Fifth, containing "binding a garland": binding together teaching, practice, and principle, just as binding a garland of flowers keeps its petals from scattering and falling away. The mundane siddhānta binds the Buddha's own spoken teaching so that it does not scatter and fall away; the individual and therapeutic siddhāntas bind every practice so that it does not scatter and fall away; the siddhānta of ultimate meaning binds meaning and principle so that they do not scatter and fall away. Gathering this as the Dharma-gate: binding the teaching accomplishes the mouth's freedom from fault; binding practice accomplishes the body's freedom from fault; binding meaning accomplishes the mind's freedom from fault — these are also the three kinds of practice shared in common with wisdom, and are also the three dhāraṇīs. The teaching not scattering and falling away is the dhāraṇī of retaining what is heard; practice not scattering and falling away is the dhāraṇī of practice; meaning not scattering and falling away is precisely the dhāraṇī that holds all things together. Taken as an explanation of "adornment of the body": in terms of teaching, this is named the adornment of wisdom; in terms of practice, this is named the adornment of merit; in terms of meaning, this is precisely what is adorned. What is adorned is precisely the Dharma-body, adorned by concentration and wisdom. Every sentient being possesses the Dharma-body, yet the Dharma-body's own substance remains plain and unadorned, disregarded and belittled even by the devas and the nāgas; if one cultivates and studies concentration and wisdom, adorning the Dharma-body, then it is seen and honored by all.

The old teaching says: "sūtra" carries five senses; here, it is shown that "sūtra" carries fifteen senses — how, then, could a single word of this land possibly translate the foreign word? The explanation of what is "carried" within the name is as given here, and so on.

As for glossing "sūtra": it is glossed as "constant" — this now explains that gloss. That the heavenly demons and the non-Buddhist outsiders are unable to alter or destroy it is named the constancy of the teaching. That it is true and correct, without any admixture, and that none can surpass it, is named the constancy of practice. That it is still and unmoving, and certainly admits of no divergent direction, is named the constancy of principle. Again, glossing it as "what can be modeled": the dharma can be modeled upon, practice can be modeled upon, and principle can be modeled upon. Now, simply explaining these two glosses already yields six senses — how much more so, then, the foreign word itself, layered with meaning upon meaning — how could it possibly be rendered by any single, simple translation?

Reading note

The fifteen-meaning scheme is this treatise's own signature move applied to a single word: five base senses (dharma-source, subtle arising, gushing spring, marking-line, binding a garland) are each run through the teaching/practice/principle triad that has structured the whole treatise since Fascicle 1, so that "sūtra" is shown to be untranslatable not because it is vague but because it is *precisely* fifteen-fold — a single Chinese word could only ever capture one of the fifteen strands at a time. The gushing-spring image is worth noting for how it scales without limit: "the understanding of the very first mind, already like a gushing spring — how much more so the later mind! How much more so the Tathāgata himself!" is the same a fortiori pattern this treatise has used throughout the origin gate, now applied to a single sūtra's own inexhaustibility.

The Case for Translatability 明有翻

Chinese Source

二、言有翻者，亦為五：一、翻為經，經由為義，由聖人心口故。今亦隨而釋之，謂教由、行由、理由。一切脩多羅、一切通別論、一切疏記等，皆由聖人心口，是名教由。一切契理行、一切相似行、一切信行法行，皆由聖人心口，故以行為由。一切世間義、一切出世義、一切方便義、一切究竟義，皆由聖人心口，故以義為由。教由世界，行由為人、對治，義由第一義悉檀。

又言經者緯義，如世絹經，以緯織之，龍鳳文章成。佛以世界悉檀說經，菩薩以世界緯織，經緯合故賢聖文章成。又約行論經緯，慧行為經，行行為緯，經緯合故，八正文章成。又約理論經緯者，詮真為經，詮俗為緯，經緯合故，二諦文章成。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, as for saying that it is translatable, this too has five senses. First, rendering it as "sūtra": "sūtra" takes "source" as its own sense, since it derives from the sage's own mind and mouth. Here, too, this is explained accordingly, in terms of the teaching's own source, practice's own source, and principle's own source. Every sūtra, every common and particular treatise, every commentary and record, all derive from the sage's own mind and mouth — this is named the teaching's own source. Every practice that accords with principle, every practice of resemblance, every practice of the one who follows through faith and the one who follows through the dharma, all derive from the sage's own mind and mouth — hence practice, too, has this same source. Every mundane meaning, every world-transcending meaning, every meaning of skillful means, every meaning of the ultimate, all derive from the sage's own mind and mouth — hence meaning, too, has this same source. The teaching's own source is the mundane siddhānta; practice's own source is the individual and the therapeutic siddhāntas; meaning's own source is the siddhānta of ultimate meaning.

Again, "sūtra" also carries the sense of "weft": just as, in ordinary silk-weaving, the warp is woven together with the weft, and thereby the pattern of the dragon and the phoenix is formed — the Buddha, by means of the mundane siddhānta, teaches the sūtra as the warp, and the bodhisattva, by means of the mundane siddhānta, weaves in the weft; warp and weft joined together, the pattern of the worthy and the sage is thereby formed. Again, discussed in terms of practice, warp and weft: the practice of wisdom is the warp, and the practice of conduct is the weft; warp and weft joined together, the pattern of the eightfold correct path is thereby formed. Again, discussed in terms of principle, warp and weft: expounding the real is the warp, and expounding the conventional is the weft; warp and weft joined together, the pattern of the two truths is thereby formed.

二、翻為契者，契緣、契事、契義。世界說是契緣；隨宜說是契生善；隨對治說是契破惡，是為契事；隨第一義說，是契義。

三、翻法本者，即教、行、理本，如前釋(云云)。

四、翻線者，線貫持教、行、理，令不零落，嚴身等義如前釋。又線能縫義，縫教使章句次第，堪可說法。如支佛不值十二部線，不能說法，世智辯聰亦不得經線，正語不成。又線能縫行，依經則行正，違經則行邪。又縫理者，理所不印，墮六十二邪；理所印者，會一究竟道也。

五、翻善語教，亦是善行教，亦是善理教。世界悉檀說即善語教；為人、對治即善行教；第一義說即善理教。是名脩多羅有五種翻也。

Second, rendering it as "according with": according with conditions, according with the matter at hand, according with meaning. Teaching by the mundane siddhānta is according with conditions; teaching according to what is fitting is according with generating good; teaching according to the antidote is according with breaking through evil — these two together are according with the matter at hand; teaching according to the siddhānta of ultimate meaning is according with meaning.

Third, rendering it as "dharma-source": this is precisely the source of teaching, of practice, and of principle, exactly as explained before, and so on.

Fourth, rendering it as "thread": the thread strings together and holds fast the teaching, practice, and principle, so that they do not scatter and fall away — the sense of "adornment" and the rest is as explained before. Again, the thread is able to stitch together meaning: stitching the teaching gives its chapters and verses their proper sequence, fit to teach the Dharma — just as the pratyekabuddha, not having encountered the thread of the twelve divisions of scripture, is unable to teach the Dharma, and worldly wisdom and eloquence, too, without obtaining the sūtra's own thread, cannot achieve correct speech. Again, the thread is able to stitch together practice: relying on the sūtra, practice is correct; departing from the sūtra, practice becomes deviant. Again, as for stitching together principle: what principle does not seal falls into the sixty-two views; what principle does seal converges upon the one, ultimate path.

Fifth, rendering it as "the teaching of good words," which is likewise the teaching of good practice, and likewise the teaching of good principle: teaching by the mundane siddhānta is the teaching of good words; teaching by the individual and therapeutic siddhāntas is the teaching of good practice; teaching by the siddhānta of ultimate meaning is the teaching of good principle. This is what is meant by saying that "sūtra" has these five translatable senses.

Reading note

Where the untranslatability camp reads "sūtra" as a single word too dense with meaning for any one Chinese term to capture, the translatability camp reads it the other way around: "sūtra" itself already carries at least five distinct native senses — source, weft-partner-to-warp, according-with, thread, and the teaching of good words — each of which maps cleanly onto the teaching/practice/principle triad this treatise has used constantly since Fascicle 1. The two positions are not yet reconciled here; the treatise stages them as genuinely competing accounts before turning, in the next section, to show that both are right in their own way.
