Six Similes for One Flower

Guanding opens with his own account of why he risked writing down what he heard Zhiyi teach — a portrait of a teacher who, uniquely among his generation, combined every rare accomplishment at once. Zhiyi's own Preface gives six similes packed into a single flower: three reading "lotus" and "flower" as the real and the provisional, three reading them as origin and trace. Guanding's private preface and glosses follow, then the body of Fascicle 1 opens with the Five Sections — Name, Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, Teaching — treated first in outline through Zhiyi's sevenfold method, closing with an extended discussion of the four siddhāntas and the opening rounds of a lengthy critique of earlier masters' readings of "wondrous."

Guanding's Account of the Text's Transmission 法華私記緣起

Chinese Source

沙門灌頂述

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by the monk Guanding.

大法東漸，《僧史》所載，詎有幾人不曾聽講，自解佛乘者乎？縱令發悟，復能入定得陀羅尼者不？縱具定慧，復帝京弘二法不？縱令盛席，謝遣徒眾隱居山谷不？縱避世守玄，被徵為二國師不？縱帝者所尊，太極對御講《仁王般若》不？縱正殿宣揚，為主上三禮不？縱令萬乘屈膝，百高座百官稱美讚歎，彈指喧殿不？縱道俗顒顒，玄悟《法華》圓意不？縱得經意，能無文字，以樂說辯，晝夜流瀉不？唯我智者，具諸功德。

As the great Dharma spread eastward, among all those recorded in the Monks' Histories, how many were there who understood the Buddha-vehicle by themselves, without ever having heard it lectured on? And even granting that one awakened by himself, could he also enter samādhi and attain dhāraṇī? And even granting he possessed both samādhi and wisdom, could he also propagate both aspects of the Dharma in the imperial capital? And even granting he presided over a flourishing assembly, could he also take leave of his followers and withdraw into the mountains and valleys? And even granting he shunned the world to guard what is profound, could he also be summoned to serve as preceptor to two kingdoms? And even granting he was honored by emperors, could he also lecture the sovereign himself, face to face in the Great Ultimate Hall, on the Scripture for Humane Kings on the Perfection of Wisdom? And even granting he proclaimed the Dharma from the main hall, could he also receive the ruler's own threefold homage? And even granting ten thousand chariots bowed their knees before him, and a hundred high seats and a hundred officials praised him with resounding applause that filled the hall — could he, even so, have penetrated the perfect meaning of the Lotus Sūtra in its full profundity? And even granting he grasped the sūtra's meaning, could he also, without relying on written words, pour it forth day and night in an eloquence of unhindered exposition? Only our Great Master Zhiyi possessed every one of these at once.

幸哉灌頂，昔於建業，始聽經文；次在江陵，奉蒙玄義；晚還台嶺，仍值鶴林。荊揚往復，途將萬里，前後補接，纔聞一遍。非但未聞不聞，亦乃聞者未了，卷舒鑽仰，彌覺堅高，猶恨緣淺，不再不三，諮詢無地，如犢思乳。並復惟念：斯言若墜，將來可悲。

How fortunate I, Guanding, have been. Long ago, in Jianye, I first heard him lecture on the sūtra's text; then, in Jiangling, I was privileged to receive the Profound Meaning; and late in his life, having returned to the Tiantai mountains, I was present once more at his final teaching, by the grove where the cranes gathered at his passing. Traveling back and forth between Jing and Yang, a journey of nearly ten thousand li, piecing together what came before and after, I managed to hear it through only once. Not only did I fail to hear what I never had the chance to hear — even what I did hear, I never fully understood. The more I turned it over and pressed into it with reverent effort, the more its height and firmness struck me, and I could only regret that my connection to it had been too slight: I could not hear it a second or a third time, and had nowhere left to turn and ask. I was like a calf still longing for its mother's milk. And I could not help but think: if these words were allowed to be lost, it would be a sorrow for ages to come.

《涅槃》明若樹若石，今經稱若田若里，聿遵聖典，書而傳之，《玄》、《文》各十卷。或以經論誠言，符此深妙；或標諸師異解，驗彼非圓。後代行者，知甘露門之在茲。

The Nirvāṇa Sūtra speaks of what a tree or a stone can preserve; this present sūtra speaks of what a field or a village can preserve. Following reverently the model of the sacred canon, I have written this down and transmitted it — the Profound Meaning and the Textual Commentary, each in ten fascicles. In places I have matched it against the sincere words of sūtra and treatise, to confirm its own depth and subtlety; in places I have set out the differing interpretations of various masters, to show where they fall short of the perfect teaching. May students of later generations know that the gate of sweet dew stands right here.

Reading note

Guanding (灌頂, 561–632) was Zhiyi's direct disciple and the recorder of nearly all his major works, already a translation project on this site at sutras/mohe-zhiguan/. Jianye and Jiangling are, respectively, the old names for Nanjing and for a city on the middle Yangtze; the "crane grove" (鶴林) alludes to the Buddha's own parinirvāṇa beneath twin śāla trees, whose foliage is said to have turned white like a flock of cranes — Guanding uses it here for the place of Zhiyi's own final teaching near the end of his life. The Profound Meaning (玄義) and the Textual Commentary (文句) are this text and its companion volume, the Fahua Wenju, already translated in full on this site at sutras/fahua-wenju/.

Zhiyi's Preface 序王

Chinese Source

所言「妙」者，「妙」名不可思議也。所言「法」者，十界、十如、權實之法也。「蓮華」者，譬權實法也。良以妙法難解，假喻易彰！況意乃多，略擬前後，合成六也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for what is called "wondrous": the name "wondrous" signifies what is inconceivable. As for what is called "dharma": it names the dharmas of the ten realms, the ten suchnesses, and the provisional and the real. "Lotus flower" is a metaphor for the dharmas of the provisional and the real. It is truly because the wondrous dharma is difficult to understand that a metaphor, borrowed for the purpose, brings it easily to light — and since its sense is in fact manifold, let me set out briefly, in order, six meanings that together make up the whole.

一、為蓮故華，譬為實施權。文云「知第一寂滅，以方便力故，雖示種種道，其實為佛乘。」二、華敷譬開權，蓮現譬顯實。文云「開方便門，示真實相。」三、華落譬廢權，蓮成譬立實。文云「正直捨方便，但說無上道。」

First: it is for the sake of the seed-pod that the flower blooms at all — this is a metaphor for establishing the provisional for the sake of the real. The text says: "Knowing the ultimate quiescence, by the power of skillful means, though he shows various paths, in truth it is all for the sake of the buddha-vehicle." Second: the flower's opening is a metaphor for opening up the provisional, and the seed-pod's appearing within it is a metaphor for revealing the real. The text says: "He opens the gate of skillful means and shows the mark of the real truth." Third: the flower's falling away is a metaphor for setting aside the provisional, and the seed-pod's ripening is a metaphor for establishing the real. The text says: "Directly, without deviation, he casts aside skillful means, and speaks only of the unsurpassed path."

又蓮譬於本，華譬於迹，從本垂迹，迹依於本。文云「我實成佛來，久遠若斯。但教化眾生，作如是說：『我少出家，得三菩提。』」二、華敷譬開迹，蓮現譬顯本。文云「一切世間皆謂今始得道；我成佛來，無量無邊那由他劫。」三、華落譬廢迹，蓮成譬立本。文云「諸佛如來，法皆如是。為度眾生，皆實不虛。」

Again: the seed-pod is a metaphor for the origin, and the flower is a metaphor for the trace; from the origin the trace is let down, and the trace depends upon the origin. The text says: "In truth, since I attained buddhahood, it has been a span of time as vast as this. It is only for the sake of teaching and transforming sentient beings that I speak in this way, saying: 'In my youth I left the household and attained awakening.'" Second [of this second triad]: the flower's blooming is a metaphor for opening the trace, and the seed-pod's appearing is a metaphor for revealing the origin. The text says: "All the world takes it that I have only now, for the first time, attained the path; yet since I attained buddhahood, it has been immeasurable, boundless myriads of kalpas." Third: the flower's falling is a metaphor for setting aside the trace, and the seed-pod's ripening is a metaphor for establishing the origin. The text says: "All buddha-tathāgatas — their Dharma is entirely like this: for the sake of saving sentient beings, all of it is true and never false."

是以先標妙法，次喻蓮華：蕩化城之執教，廢草庵之滯情；開方便之權門，示真實之妙理；會眾善之小行，歸廣大之一乘；上、中、下根，皆與記莂。又發眾聖之權巧，顯本地之幽微，故增道損生，位隣大覺。一期化導，事理俱圓，蓮華之譬，意在斯矣。

It is for this reason that he first names the wondrous dharma, and next gives the simile of the lotus flower: sweeping away attachment to the teaching of the phantom city, setting aside the lingering feeling for the thatched hut; opening the provisional gate of skillful means, showing the wondrous principle of the real truth; gathering the small practices of the many kinds of good into the one vast vehicle; and bestowing predictions of buddhahood on capacities high, middling, and low alike. He further discloses the skillful cleverness of all the sages, and reveals the hidden subtlety of the fundamental ground, so that as the path increases, birth-and-death diminishes, until one's stage draws near to great awakening. That the guidance given over this one lifetime is perfect in both phenomena and principle — this is precisely what the simile of the lotus flower is meant to convey.

「經」者，外國稱修多羅，聖教之都名。有翻、無翻，事如後釋。

As for "sūtra": in the Western lands it is called sūtra, the general name for the sacred teaching. Whether it should be translated or left untranslated is a matter explained later.

記者釋曰：蓋「序王」者，敘經玄意。玄意述於文心，文心莫過迹本。仰觀斯旨，眾義冷然。妙法蓮華，即敘名也；示真實之妙理，敘體也；歸廣大之一乘，敘宗也；蕩化城之執教，敘用也；一期化圓，敘教也；六譬，敘迹本也。文略意周矣。

The recorder explains: this "Preface" narrates the profound sense of the sūtra. The profound sense is set forth in the heart of the text, and the heart of the text is nothing other than trace and origin. Looking up and taking in this purport, its several meanings become clear and distinct of themselves. "Wondrous Dharma Lotus Flower" narrates the name; "showing the wondrous principle of the real truth" narrates the essence; "gathering into the one vast vehicle" narrates the cardinal doctrine; "sweeping away attachment to the teaching of the phantom city" narrates the function; "guidance over one lifetime, perfected" narrates the teaching; and the six similes narrate trace and origin. The text is brief, yet its sense is complete.

Reading note

Zhiyi's six similes fall into two parallel triads sharing the same numbering (一/二/三) in the source text — Guanding does not restart the count for the second triad, since the two sets mirror each other exactly. The first triad reads "lotus" and "flower" as the real and the provisional, within a single lifetime's teaching; the second reads them as origin (本, the Buddha's own awakening in the incalculably distant past) and trace (迹, his teaching career as an apparent historical figure) — the governing distinction of the sūtra's own two halves, worked out in detail by Zhiyi later in this treatise and reused throughout the Fahua Wenju. "The phantom city" and "the thatched hut" both name provisional resting-places the sūtra urges its hearers to give up for the sake of the one real destination — images developed at length in the chapters on the phantom-city parable and the parable of the destitute son, both already translated in the Fahua Wenju.

Guanding's Private Preface 私序王

Chinese Source

夫理絕偏圓，寄圓珠而談理；極非遠近，託寶所而論極。極會圓冥，事理俱寂。而不寂者，良由耽無明酒，雖繫珠而不覺；迷涅槃道，路弗遠而言長。聖主世尊！愍斯倒惑，四華六動，開方便之門，三變千踊，表真實之地，咸令一切普得見聞。發祕密之奧藏，稱之為妙。示權實之正軌，故號為法。指久遠之本果，喻之以蓮。會不二之圓道，譬之以華。聲為佛事，稱之為經。圓詮之初，目之為序。序類相從，稱之為品。眾次之首，名為第一。

Ru-Yi Translation

Principle transcends the partial and the perfect alike, and yet, in dependence on the perfect pearl, principle comes to be spoken of; the ultimate is neither far nor near, and yet, relying on the place of treasure, the ultimate comes to be discussed. Where the ultimate merges with the perfect and the hidden, phenomena and principle are both quiescent. And if they seem not quiescent, it is truly because, intoxicated by the wine of ignorance, one wears the pearl sewn into one's own robe without ever noticing it there; lost on the path to nirvāṇa, though the road is not in fact far, one calls it long. The sagely lord, the World-Honored One, taking pity on this inverted delusion, made the four flowers rain down and the six kinds of earth-tremor occur, opening the gate of skillful means; he made the three transformations of the land occur and the thousandfold multitude spring up from the earth, manifesting the ground of the real truth — causing every being, without exception, to see and hear it fully. His disclosing of the secret and profound treasury is called "wondrous." His showing of the correct track of the provisional and the real is therefore named "dharma." His pointing to the ancient fruit of buddhahood, attained in the far distant past, is likened to the lotus-seed-pod. His bringing together of the non-dual, perfect path is compared to the flower. His voice, performing the Buddha's own work, is called the "sūtra." The very opening of this perfect exposition is termed the "prologue." What follows it in sequence, belonging to the same kind, is called a "chapter." The first among this sequence of chapters is named "Chapter One."

釋曰：談記是敘名；會冥是敘體；圓珠是敘宗；俱寂是敘用；四華六動是敘教，本迹可知。

[Guanding] explains: speaking of the pearl narrates the name; merging with the hidden narrates the essence; the perfect pearl narrates the cardinal doctrine; both being quiescent narrates the function; the four flowers and six tremors narrate the teaching — trace and origin may be understood accordingly.

Reading note

The "perfect pearl" and "place of treasure" both allude to parables told later in the sūtra itself: a poor man who does not know a jewel has been sewn into the lining of his own robe by an old friend, and travelers on a long, fearful road who mistake a magically conjured city for their true destination. Both parables, and Zhiyi's own extended treatment of them, are already translated in the Fahua Wenju at fascicles six and seven.

On "Wondrous," Continued

Chinese Source

此《妙法蓮華經》者，本地甚深之奧藏也。文云「是法不可示，世間相常住。」三世如來之所證得也。文云「是第一寂滅於道場知已，大事因緣出現於世。」始見我身，令入佛慧。為未入者，四十餘年，以異方便，助顯第一義。今正直捨方便，但說無上道。

Ru-Yi Translation

This Wondrous Dharma Lotus Flower Sūtra is the profound and secret treasury of the fundamental ground. The text says: "This dharma cannot be shown; the marks of the world are eternally abiding." It is what the tathāgatas of the three times have realized and attained. The text says: "Having known this ultimate quiescence at the seat of enlightenment, I appeared in the world on account of the one great cause and condition." Some beings first behold my body and are thereby led into the Buddha's wisdom; for those who have not yet entered it, for more than forty years, by various skillful means, I have helped reveal the truth of ultimate meaning. Now, directly and without deviation, I cast aside skillful means and speak only of the unsurpassed path.

所言妙者，褒美不可思議之法也。又妙者，十法界、十如之法。此法即妙，此妙即法，無二無別，故言妙也。又妙者，自行權實之法妙也，故舉蓮華而況之也。又妙者，即迹而本，即本而迹，即非本非迹，或為開廢(云云)。又妙者，最勝脩多羅甘露之門，故言妙也。

As for what is called "wondrous": it is praise extolling the inconceivable dharma. Again, "wondrous" names the dharmas of the ten dharma-realms and the ten suchnesses — this dharma is itself the wondrous, and this wondrous is itself the dharma, without duality, without distinction; hence it is called "wondrous." Again, "wondrous" names the wondrousness of the dharma of one's own practice, the provisional and the real; hence the lotus flower is raised up to give its likeness. Again, "wondrous" means: identical with the trace and yet the origin, identical with the origin and yet the trace, identical with neither origin nor trace — or, alternatively, opening the one and setting aside the other, as will be explained. Again, "wondrous" names the gate of sweet dew of the most supreme sūtra; hence it is called "wondrous."

釋曰：妙無別體，體上褒美者，敘妙名也。妙即法界，法界即妙者，敘體也。自行權實者，敘宗也。本迹六喻者，敘用也。甘露門者，敘教也。

[Guanding] explains: that the wondrous has no essence apart from the dharma itself, and is praise bestowed upon that very essence, narrates the name of the wondrous. That the wondrous is itself the dharma-realm, and the dharma-realm is itself the wondrous, narrates the essence. The provisional and the real of one's own practice narrates the cardinal doctrine. The six similes of origin and trace narrate the function. The gate of sweet dew narrates the teaching.

The Five Sections, in Common and in Particular 妙法蓮華經玄義卷第一上

Chinese Source

天台智者大師說

釋名第一　辨體第二　明宗第三　論用第四　判教第五

釋此五章，有通、有別。通是同義，別是異義。如此五章，遍解眾經，故言同也。釋名名異，乃至判教教異，故言別也。例眾經之初，皆安五事，則同義也。如是詮異，我聞人異，一時感應異，佛住處所異，若干人聽眾異，則別義也。又通者，共義；別者，各義。如此通、別，專在一部。通則七番共解，別則五重各說。例如利鈍，須廣略二門也。眾教通別，今所不論；一經通別，今當辨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Expounded by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai.

Explaining the Name is the first section; Clarifying the Essence, the second; Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine, the third; Discussing the Function, the fourth; Classifying the Teachings, the fifth.

In explaining these five sections, there is a common sense and a particular sense. The common sense is what is shared; the particular sense is what differs. These five sections apply to the exposition of every sūtra alike — hence the common sense is so called. But the name explained under "Explaining the Name" differs from sūtra to sūtra, and likewise the teaching classified under "Classifying the Teachings" differs — hence the particular sense is so called. To take an analogy: every sūtra opens with the same five circumstances — this is the common sense; yet what is expounded differs, who heard it differs, the occasion and its response differ, the Buddha's dwelling-place differs, and the number of the assembled hearers differs — this is the particular sense. Again, the common is the sense of what is shared in general; the particular is the sense of what belongs to each thing individually. Applied here, the common and the particular both concern this one sūtra specifically. In its common aspect, the sevenfold common exposition is used; in its particular aspect, the five sections are each expounded separately — just as, for the sharp-witted and the dull alike, one must provide both a broad approach and a brief one. The common and particular senses across all the teachings [of the whole canon] are not discussed here; only the common and particular senses within this one sūtra are to be examined now.

△就通作七番共解：一、標章；二、引證；三、生起；四、開合；五、料簡；六、觀心；七、會異。

As for the common aspect, a sevenfold common exposition is made: first, marking the section; second, citing proof-texts; third, showing how it arises in sequence; fourth, opening it up and combining it [with other schemes]; fifth, resolving doubts; sixth, the contemplation of mind; seventh, reconciling it with other terms.

標章令易憶持，起念心故。引證據佛語，起信心故。生起使不雜亂，起定心故。開合、料簡、會異等，起慧心故。觀心即聞、即行，起精進心故。五心立，成五根，排五障，成五力，乃至入三脫門。略說七重，共意如此。

Marking the section makes it easy to remember and hold in mind, thereby giving rise to the mind of mindfulness. Citing proof-texts relies on the Buddha's own words, thereby giving rise to the mind of faith. Showing its arising in sequence keeps it from becoming confused and disordered, thereby giving rise to the mind of concentration. Opening it up and combining it, resolving doubts, and reconciling it with other terms give rise to the mind of wisdom. The contemplation of mind is at once hearing and practice, thereby giving rise to the mind of diligence. Once these five minds are established, they become the five faculties; once the five obstructions are cleared away, they become the five powers, leading in turn to entry through the three gates of liberation. This, briefly stated, is the shared purport of these seven aspects.

廣解五章者，一一廣起五心、五根，令開、示、悟、入佛之知見耳。

As for the broad exposition of the five sections: each one broadly gives rise to the five minds and five faculties, so as to open, show, awaken to, and enter into the Buddha's own knowledge and vision.

Reading note

The "five circumstances" (五事) opening every sūtra are the standard formula "Thus have I heard; at one time; the Buddha; dwelling at [a place]; together with [an assembly]" — the same fivefold opening whose exegesis occupies much of Fascicle 1 of the Fahua Wenju. The "three gates of liberation" (三脫門) are emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness. Zhiyi's sevenfold method recurs as the organizing structure for nearly every section of this treatise from here forward; this page marks it once in full and does not relabel it at each recurrence unless the wording itself changes.

Marking the Name 標名

Chinese Source

○初標五章(云云)。標名為四：一、立；二、分別；三、結；四、譬。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the marking of the five sections, as will be explained. The marking of the name has four parts: first, establishing it; second, differentiating it; third, concluding it; fourth, giving a simile for it.

立名者，原聖建名，蓋為開深以進始，咸令視聽，俱得見聞，尋途趣遠，而至於極，故以名名法，施設眾生。

As for establishing the name: tracing back to why the sage established a name at all, it is precisely in order to open up what is profound so as to draw beginners forward, causing all who see and hear it to gain sight and hearing of it together — so that, following the path toward what is distant, they may in the end arrive at the ultimate. It is for this reason that he names the dharma with a name, and sets it forth for the sake of sentient beings.

分別者，但法有麁妙，若隔歷三諦，麁法也；圓融三諦，妙法也。

As for differentiating it: dharmas simply have their coarse and their wondrous. Where the three truths are held apart, separated from one another, that is a coarse dharma; where the three truths are perfectly fused together, that is the wondrous dharma.

此妙諦本有，文云「是法住法位，世間相常住。唯我知是相，十方佛亦然。」尚非不退菩薩、入證二乘所知，況復人天群萌之類。佛雖知是，不務速說。文云「我若讚佛乘，眾生沒在苦。謗法不信故，墜於三惡道。」

This wondrous truth is inherently present from the start. The text says: "This dharma abides in its dharma-position; the marks of the world are eternally abiding. Only I know this mark; the buddhas of the ten directions likewise know it so." Yet this is not something known even by non-retrogressing bodhisattvas, or by the two vehicles who have entered realization — how much less by the multitudes of gods and humans! Though the Buddha knows this, he does not press to speak of it quickly. The text says: "If I were to praise the buddha-vehicle, sentient beings, sunk in suffering, would slander the Dharma and fail to believe in it, and would fall into the three evil destinies."

所以初教建立融不融，小根併不聞；次教建立不融，大根都不用；次教俱建立，以融斥不融，令小根恥不融，慕於融；次教俱建立，令小根寄融向不融，令大根從不融向於融。雖種種建立，施設眾生，但隨他意語，非佛本懷故，言不務速說也。

Hence, in the first teaching he established both the fused and the unfused together, yet those of lesser capacity had no access to either at all. In the next teaching he established only the unfused, and those of great capacity made no use of it. In the next teaching he established both together, using the fused to rebuke the unfused, causing those of lesser capacity to feel ashamed of the unfused and to long for the fused. In the next teaching he again established both together, causing those of lesser capacity to lodge themselves within the fused even while still approaching by way of the unfused, and causing those of great capacity to proceed from the unfused toward the fused. Though he established the teaching in these various ways, setting it forth for the sake of sentient beings, this was still speech accommodated to others' own intentions, and not the Buddha's own original purpose — hence it is said that he did not press to speak of it quickly.

今經正直捨不融，但說於融，令一座席同一道味，乃暢如來出世本懷，故建立此經，名之為妙。

In the present sūtra, however, he directly and without deviation casts aside the unfused, and speaks only of the fused, causing the entire assembly, seated together, to share one and the same taste of the path — thereby giving full expression at last to the Tathāgata's own original purpose in appearing in the world. It is for this reason that this sūtra is established and named "wondrous."

結者，當知《華嚴》兼，《三藏》但，《方等》對，《般若》帶。此經無復兼、但、對、帶，專是正直無上之道，故稱為妙法也。

As for the conclusion: one should know that the Avataṃsaka period is "combined," the Tripiṭaka period is "exclusive," the Vaipulya period is "contrasted," and the Prajñā period is "accompanied." This sūtra no longer has any combining, exclusiveness, contrasting, or accompanying about it — it is solely the direct, unsurpassed path, without deviation. Hence it is called the wondrous dharma.

譬蓮華者，例有麁妙。云何？麁狂華無果，或一華多菓，或多華一菓，或一華一菓，或前菓後華，或前華後菓。初喻外道，空修梵行，無所剋獲。次喻凡夫，供養父母，報在梵天。次喻聲聞，種種苦行，止得涅槃。次喻緣覺，一遠離行，亦得涅槃。次喻須陀洹，却後修道。次喻菩薩，先籍緣修，生後真修。皆是麁華，不以為喻。蓮華多奇：為蓮故華，華實具足，可喻即實而權；又華開蓮現，可喻即權而實；又華落蓮成，蓮成亦落，可喻非權非實。如是等種種義便故，以蓮華喻於妙法也。

As for giving a simile through the lotus flower: flowers in general have their coarse and their wondrous kinds. How so? A coarse, wild flower bears no fruit at all; or a single flower bears many fruits; or many flowers bear a single fruit; or a single flower bears a single fruit; or the fruit comes before the flower; or the flower comes before the fruit. The first is a simile for non-Buddhist paths, who cultivate a pure life in vain, gaining nothing thereby. The next is a simile for ordinary people, whose offerings to their parents bring a reward of rebirth in the Brahmā heavens. The next is a simile for śrāvakas, whose various ascetic practices bring them only to nirvāṇa. The next is a simile for pratyekabuddhas, whose single practice of withdrawal likewise brings them only to nirvāṇa. The next is a simile for stream-enterers, who cultivate the path only afterward, in a later life. The next is a simile for bodhisattvas, who first rely on conditioned cultivation before a later, true cultivation is born. All of these are coarse flowers, and are not used here as the simile. The lotus flower is remarkable in many ways: it is for the sake of the seed-pod that the flower blooms, flower and fruit both complete together — this may serve as a simile for the real that is identical with the provisional. Again, the flower opens and the seed-pod appears within it — this may serve as a simile for the provisional that is identical with the real. Again, the flower falls away and the seed-pod ripens, and the ripened pod, too, eventually falls — this may serve as a simile for what is neither the provisional nor the real. Since it is fitting in such various senses as these, the lotus flower is used as the simile for the wondrous dharma.

Reading note

"Combined," "exclusive," "contrasted," and "accompanied" (兼/但/對/帶) form a standard Tiantai quartet describing how each of the Five Periods relates the perfect teaching to the other three: the Avataṃsaka period teaches the perfect teaching combined with the distinct teaching; the Āgama period teaches the Tripiṭaka teaching exclusively, with no perfect teaching present at all; the Vaipulya period teaches the perfect teaching contrasted against the three lesser teachings, to shame their hearers into aspiring higher; and the Prajñā period teaches the perfect teaching while still accompanied by the shared and distinct teachings. Only the Lotus period, as this passage concludes, has none of these four qualifications.

Marking the Essence 標體

Chinese Source

體者為四：一、釋字。二、引同。三、簡非。四、結正。體字訓禮。禮，法也：各親其親，各子其子，君臣撙節。若無禮者，則非法也。出世法體，亦復如是，善惡、凡聖、菩薩、佛，一切不出法性。正指實相，以為正體也。故〈壽量品〉云「不如三界，見於三界，非如非異。」若三界人見三界為異，二乘人見三界為如，菩薩人見三界亦如亦異，佛見三界非如非異、雙照如異。今取佛所見為實相正體也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The essence has four parts: first, explaining the graph; second, citing parallels; third, sifting out what is not it; fourth, concluding with what is correct. The graph for "essence" is glossed as "propriety." Propriety is what is fitting: each treats his own parent as a parent, each treats his own child as a child, ruler and minister each observe due measure. Where there is no propriety, there is no fittingness. The essence of the dharma that transcends the world is likewise so: good and evil, the ordinary and the sagely, bodhisattva and buddha — none of these ever depart from the dharma-nature. It is precisely the true mark that is pointed to as the correct essence. Hence the "Lifespan" chapter says: "Not as [the three realms themselves appear], he sees the three realms — neither as identical nor as different." If a person of the three realms sees the three realms as different from how they truly are, and a person of the two vehicles sees the three realms as identical with how they truly are, and a bodhisattva sees the three realms as both identical and different, then the Buddha sees the three realms as neither identical nor different, illuminating both the identical and the different at once. It is the Buddha's own seeing that is here taken as the true mark, the correct essence.

金剛藏說「佛甚微智」，辭異意同。其辭曰：「空有、不二、不異、不盡。」空非斷無，故言空有。有即是空，空即是有，故言不二。非離空有外，別有中道，故言不異。遍一切處，故言不盡。此亦與龍樹意同。《中論》云「因緣所生法，即空、即假、即中。」因緣所生法即空者，此非斷無也。即假者，不二也。即中者，不異也。因緣所生法者，即遍一切處也。

Vajragarbha speaks of "the Buddha's most subtle wisdom" — his words differ, but his meaning is the same. His words are: "empty yet existent, not-two, not-different, not-exhausted." Emptiness here is not the emptiness of annihilation and nonexistence — hence it is said "empty yet existent." Existence is itself emptiness, and emptiness is itself existence — hence it is said "not-two." There is no middle way apart from and outside emptiness and existence — hence it is said "not-different." It pervades every place — hence it is said "not-exhausted." This, too, accords with Nāgārjuna's meaning. The Middle Treatise says: "Dharmas arisen through causes and conditions are precisely empty, precisely provisional, precisely the middle." That dharmas arisen through causes and conditions are precisely empty means this is not the emptiness of annihilation. That they are precisely provisional means "not-two." That they are precisely the middle means "not-different." That they are dharmas arisen through causes and conditions at all means they pervade every place.

今言實相體，即權而實，離斷無謗也；即實而權，離建立謗也；權實即非權實，離異謗也；雙照權實，遍一切處，離盡謗也。斯乃總二經之雙美，申兩論之同致，顯二家之懸會，明今經之正體也。

Now, to speak of the essence as the true mark: identical with the provisional and yet the real, it is free of the slander of annihilation and nonexistence; identical with the real and yet the provisional, it is free of the slander of groundless establishment; the provisional and the real being identical with neither the provisional nor the real, it is free of the slander of difference; illuminating both the provisional and the real at once, pervading every place, it is free of the slander of exhaustion. This, then, gathers together the twin excellences of the two sūtras, sets forth the shared purport of the two treatises, and reveals the distant accord between the two houses — making plain the correct essence of the present sūtra.

私謂實相之法，橫破凡夫之四執，竪破三聖之證得。破凡夫可解。

In my own view, the dharma of the true mark refutes, horizontally, the ordinary person's four attachments, and refutes, vertically, the three kinds of sages' attainments. Its refutation of the ordinary person needs no further explanation.

破聖者，三藏二乘，指但空為極。譬頗梨珠，一往似真，再研便偽。身子云：「我等同入法性，失於如來無量知見。」空有之旨，正破此證也。通教人，指但空、不但空共為極。譬雜色裹珠，光隨色變。緣所見之光，亡其本體；逐玄黃之色，墮落二乘。《大經》云「聲聞之人，但見於空，不見不空。」菩薩之人，非但見空，亦見不空。所見既殊，不二之旨正破此證也。別教人，指不但空為極。逈出二邊，如雲外月，棄邊取中，如捨空求空，不異之旨，正破此證。若彼有此無，則正法不遍，不盡之旨，亦破此證也。

As for its refutation of the sages: the two vehicles of the Tripiṭaka teaching point to mere emptiness as the ultimate. This is like a crystal bead — at a first glance it seems genuine, but examined a second time, it proves false. Śāriputra says: "We, too, entered the dharma-nature together with the bodhisattvas, yet we lost the Tathāgata's immeasurable knowledge and vision." The purport of "empty yet existent" is precisely what refutes this attainment. A person of the shared teaching points to both mere emptiness and not-mere emptiness together as the ultimate. This is like a bead wrapped in colored cloth, whose light changes according to the color; fixing on the light one sees, one loses sight of the bead's own true body, and chasing after its dark or yellow hue, one falls into the two vehicles. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Persons of the śrāvaka vehicle see only emptiness, and do not see the not-empty; persons of the bodhisattva path see not only emptiness but also the not-empty." Since what each sees differs, the purport of "not-two" is precisely what refutes this attainment. A person of the distinct teaching points to not-mere-emptiness as the ultimate — utterly transcending the two extremes, like the moon beyond the clouds. But casting aside the extremes to grasp the middle is itself like abandoning one emptiness only to seek another; the purport of "not-different" is precisely what refutes this attainment. And if the middle is present there, beyond the extremes, but absent here, within them, then the correct dharma does not pervade everywhere; the purport of "not-exhausted," too, refutes this attainment.

此等皆非佛甚微智，不與金剛藏意同。非佛證得本有常住，不與〈方便品〉同。不遍一切處，不與〈壽量品〉同。既不會正體，攝屬何法？但空，是化他之實；但不但，是自行化他之實。出二邊中，是自行之權。並他經所說，非今體也。今經體者，體化他之權實，即是自行之權實，如垢衣內身，實是長者。體自行化他之權實，即是自行之權實，如衣內繫珠，即無價寶也。自行之權，即自行之實。如一切世間，治生產業，皆與實相不相違背。一色一香，無非中道。況自行之實而非實耶！

None of these attainments is the Buddha's own most subtle wisdom, and so none accords with Vajragarbha's meaning; none is the Buddha's own realization of what is inherently present and eternally abiding, and so none accords with the "Skillful Means" chapter; none pervades every place, and so none accords with the "Lifespan" chapter. Since none of them accords with the correct essence, to what dharma do they then belong? Mere emptiness is the real teaching used to transform others; both mere and not-mere emptiness together is the real teaching used both for one's own practice and to transform others; transcending the two extremes into the middle is the provisional teaching for one's own practice. All of these belong to what other sūtras teach, and are not the essence of the present sūtra. As for the essence of the present sūtra: it takes the provisional and the real used to transform others as themselves identical with the provisional and the real of one's own practice — like the body hidden within the beggar's grimy robe, which is in truth the wealthy elder himself. It takes the provisional and the real of one's own practice and of transforming others together as themselves identical with the provisional and the real of one's own practice — like the pearl sewn within the robe's own lining, which is itself the priceless jewel. The provisional of one's own practice is itself identical with the real of one's own practice — just as, among all worldly affairs, the management of one's livelihood and trade, none of it ever runs contrary to the true mark; a single color, a single fragrance — none of it is other than the middle way. How much more so, then, that the real of one's own practice should fail to be the real!

Marking the Cardinal Doctrine 標宗

Chinese Source

宗者為三：一、示。二、簡。三、結。宗者，要也。所謂佛自行因果，以為宗也。云何為要？無量眾善，言因則攝；無量證得，言果則攝。如提綱維，無目而不動；牽衣一角，無縷而不來。故言宗要。

Ru-Yi Translation

The cardinal doctrine has three parts: first, showing it; second, sifting it out; third, concluding it. "Cardinal doctrine" means what is essential. What is meant here is the Buddha's own causes and fruits of practice, taken as the cardinal doctrine. How is this "essential"? Immeasurable good deeds — speak of the cause, and they are all gathered in; immeasurable realizations — speak of the fruit, and they are all gathered in. It is like lifting the headrope of a net: not one mesh fails to move; or like pulling one corner of a garment: not one thread fails to come along. Hence it is called the essential cardinal doctrine.

然諸因果，善須明識，尚不取別教因果，況餘因果！餘因果者，昔三因大異，而三果小同；又三因大同，而三果小異。又一因逈出，一果不融。因不攝善，果不收德，則非佛自行之因，非佛道場證得之果。

Yet among all causes and fruits, one must clearly discern the good ones: this cardinal doctrine does not even take the causes and fruits of the distinct teaching, let alone any other causes and fruits! As for other causes and fruits: in some, the three former causes are greatly different from one another while the three fruits are only slightly the same; in others, the three causes are largely the same while the three fruits are only slightly different; in others again, a single cause stands utterly apart, and a single fruit remains unfused. Where the cause does not gather in every good deed, and the fruit does not encompass every virtue, this is not the Buddha's own cause of practice, nor the fruit realized at the Buddha's own seat of enlightenment.

又簡者，諸經明佛往昔所行因果，悉皆被拂，咸是方便，非今經之宗要。取意為言，因窮久遠之實修，果窮久遠之實證，如此之因，竪高七種方便，橫包十法界法。初修此實相之行，名為佛因，道場所得，名為佛果。但可以智知，不可以言具。略舉如此因果，以為宗要耳。

Again, as for sifting: whatever other sūtras teach of the causes and fruits of the Buddha's own past practice, all of it is here swept aside, since all of it belongs to skillful means, and none of it is the cardinal doctrine of the present sūtra. Taking up its true purport: the cause reaches to the very limit of the real cultivation practiced across a vast, distant span of time, and the fruit reaches to the very limit of the real realization attained across that same vast span. A cause of this kind rises, vertically, above all seven kinds of skillful means, and encompasses, horizontally, the dharmas of all ten dharma-realms. The initial cultivation of this practice of the true mark is named the Buddha's cause; what is attained at the seat of enlightenment is named the Buddha's fruit. This can only be known by wisdom — it cannot be fully set out in words. Here I have only briefly indicated such a cause and fruit, to serve as the cardinal doctrine.

Ru-Yi Translation

The function has three parts: first, showing it; second, sifting it out; third, its benefit. "Function" means power-in-action. The three kinds of twin wisdom of the provisional and the real are all power-in-action. Within power-in-action, one may further distinguish: the twin wisdom of one's own practice, whose illumination of principle is complete and all-pervading, is named "power"; the two kinds of twin wisdom for transforming others, whose discernment of capacities reaches every capacity, is named "function." Yet the twin wisdom of one's own practice is itself the twin wisdom for transforming others, and the twin wisdom for transforming others is itself the twin wisdom of one's own practice; illuminating principle is itself discerning capacities, and discerning capacities is itself illuminating principle.

如薩婆悉達彎祖王弓滿，名為力；中七鐵鼓，貫一鐵圍山，洞地徹水輪，名為用。諸方便教，力用微弱，如凡人弓箭。何者？昔緣稟化他二智，照理不遍，生信不深，除疑不盡。今緣稟自行二智，極佛境界，起法界信，增圓妙道，斷根本惑，損變易生。非但生身、及生身得忍兩種菩薩俱益，法身、法身後心兩種菩薩亦俱益。化功廣大，利潤弘深，蓋茲經之力用也。

It is like Prince Sarvārthasiddha drawing his ancestral bow to its full strength — this is named "power"; and his striking through seven iron drums, piercing an iron encircling mountain-range, and penetrating down through the earth to the water-wheel beneath it — this is named "function." The various teachings of skillful means have only a feeble power-and-function, like an ordinary person's bow and arrow. Why is this? Because in the past, beings received the twin wisdom for transforming others, whose illumination of principle did not reach everywhere, whose arising of faith was not deep, and whose removal of doubt was not complete. Now, beings receive the twin wisdom of one's own practice, reaching the very limit of the Buddha's own state, giving rise to faith in the dharma-realm, increasing the perfect and wondrous path, severing the fundamental delusion, and diminishing the birth-and-death of change. Not only do the two kinds of bodhisattva — those still in a born body, and those in a born body who have attained forbearance — both benefit; the two kinds of bodhisattva in the Dharma-body, and in the Dharma-body's final stage, also both benefit. That its transforming power is so vast, and its enriching benefit so deep and far-reaching — this is precisely the power-and-function of the present sūtra.

"Teaching" names the sage's own words directed downward to beings; "mark" means differentiating what is the same and what differs. How is this differentiated? Next the sun shines into the dark valleys, where those of shallow practice and partial illumination gain only a gradual, partial understanding suited to their own portion — this is like the Tripiṭaka teaching. The Tripiṭaka teaching was never meant for the great capacities in the first place; though the great may be present in the assembly, they present themselves toddling and babbling like beginners, and the lesser capacities fail to recognize them — this is the lesser keeping the great apart, the great hidden within the lesser. In terms of the dharma reaching its conditions, this is named the gradual mark of the teaching; in terms of the sequence of its telling, this is named the mark of the flavor of cream.

次照平地，影臨萬水，逐器方圓，隨波動靜，示一佛土，令淨穢不同。示現一身，巨細各異，一音說法，隨類各解，恐畏、歡喜厭離、斷疑、神力不共，故見有淨、穢，聞有褒、貶，嗅有薝蔔、不薝蔔，華有著身、不著身，慧有若干、不若干，此如《淨名》、《方等》。約法被緣，猶是漸教。約說次第，生蘇味相。

Next the sun shines upon level ground, its reflection falling upon the ten thousand waters, following each vessel's shape whether round or square, moving with each wave whether stirring or still: he shows a single buddha-land, yet causes it to appear pure or defiled differently to different eyes; he manifests a single body, yet its size appears large or small differently to different eyes; with a single voice he teaches the Dharma, yet each kind of hearer understands it differently — some with fear, some with joy, some with disgust and the wish to leave, some with their doubts cut off, and his spiritual power appears differently to each as well. Hence some see purity, others defilement; some hear praise, others blame; some smell the fragrance of the campaka flower, others do not; some have flowers cling to their bodies, others do not; some have wisdom in full measure, others do not. This is like the Vimalakīrti and the Vaipulya sūtras. In terms of the dharma reaching its conditions, this is still the gradual teaching; in terms of the sequence of its telling, this is the mark of the flavor of fresh curdled butter.

復有義，大人蒙其光用，嬰兒喪其睛明；夜遊者伏匿，作務者興成。故文云「但為菩薩說其實事，而不為我說斯真要。」雖三人俱學，二乘取證，具如《大品》。若約法被緣，猶是漸教。約說次第，名熟蘇味相。

There is, moreover, this further sense: grown persons receive the benefit of its light, while infants lose the clarity of their sight; those who travel by night hide themselves away, while those engaged in their tasks rise and accomplish them. Hence the text says: "It is only for the bodhisattvas that he speaks of this true matter, and not for our sake that he speaks this truly essential teaching." Though the three kinds of persons study together, the two vehicles alone grasp at realization — this is set out in full in the Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra. In terms of the dharma reaching its conditions, this, too, is still the gradual teaching; in terms of the sequence of its telling, this is named the mark of the flavor of ripened butter.

復有義，日光普照，高下悉均平。土圭測影，不縮不盈。若低頭，若小音，若散亂，若微善，皆成佛道。不令有人獨得滅度，皆以如來滅度而滅度之。具如今經。若約法被緣，名漸圓教。若說次第，醍醐味相。當知《華嚴》之譬，與《涅槃》義同。三子、三田、三馬等譬，皆先菩薩，次及二乘，後則平等凡聖(云云)。

There is, moreover, this further sense: the sunlight shines everywhere equally, level over the high and the low alike; when measured against the gnomon's shadow, it neither shrinks nor overflows. Whether one merely bows one's head, or utters but a small sound, or is scattered and distracted, or performs only the slightest good — all alike come to complete the buddha path. He does not let anyone attain extinction alone, but brings every being to extinction through the Tathāgata's own extinction. This is set out in full in the present sūtra. In terms of the dharma reaching its conditions, this is named the gradual-and-perfect teaching; in terms of the sequence of its telling, this is the mark of the flavor of ghee. One should know that the Avataṃsaka simile accords in meaning with the Nirvāṇa Sūtra. The similes of the three kinds of sons, the three kinds of fields, the three kinds of horses, and the like all proceed first to the bodhisattvas, next reach the two vehicles, and last make ordinary beings and sages equal, as will be explained.

Reading note — after Zhanran

The obscure phrase "多跢婆和" (rendered above as "toddling and babbling") is glossed by Zhanran, Zhiyi's ninth-century sub-commentator, as two separate images: "多跢" describes the unsteady gait of one still learning to walk, and "婆和" the inarticulate sounds of one still learning to speak — together picturing how a great bodhisattva, hidden among the Tripiṭaka assembly, presents himself as an utter beginner, so that Tripiṭaka practitioners, mistaking their own attainment for the real thing, fail to recognize the greatness concealed in their midst (法華玄義釋籤, T1717, fascicle 2).

The Lotus among the Five Flavors: What Sets It Apart

Chinese Source

問：既以五味分別，那同稱漸？答：約漸得明五味耳。又若小不聞大，大一向是頓；若大不用小，小一向是漸；若以大破小，是漸頓並陳；若帶小明大，是漸頓相資；若會小歸大，是漸頓泯合。故《無量義》云「漸頓二法，三道、四果不合。」今時則合，即此義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Since the teaching has just been distinguished by the Five Flavors, why are the last three flavors all called "gradual" together? Answer: It is precisely in terms of the gradual that the Five Flavors can be clarified at all. Moreover: if the lesser capacities never hear the greater teaching at all, the greater teaching is wholly sudden; if the greater capacities make no use of the lesser teaching at all, the lesser teaching is wholly gradual; if the greater teaching is used to refute the lesser, this is the gradual and the sudden set out side by side; if the lesser teaching is carried along in clarifying the greater, this is the gradual and the sudden mutually assisting one another; if the lesser is gathered and brought back into the greater, this is the gradual and the sudden dissolved together into one. Hence the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings says: "The two dharmas of the gradual and the sudden, the three paths and the four fruits, were not united." Now, at this time, they are united — this is precisely that meaning.

問：云何相資？答：小聞於大，恥小而慕大，是為頓資小；佛命善吉轉教，大益菩薩，是為漸資頓。

Question: What is meant by "mutually assisting"? Answer: When the lesser hears of the greater, and feels ashamed of the lesser while longing for the greater, this is the sudden assisting the lesser. When the Buddha commands Subhūti to pass the teaching on, and the great teaching benefits the bodhisattvas through him, this is the gradual assisting the sudden.

如前分別，但約顯露，明漸頓五味之相。若論不定，義則不然。雖高山頓說，不動寂場，而遊化鹿苑；雖說四諦生滅，而不妨不生不滅；雖為菩薩說佛境界，而有二乘智斷；雖五人證果，不妨八萬諸天獲無生忍。當知即頓而漸，即漸而頓。《大經》云「或時說深，或時說淺。」應問即遮，應遮即問。一時、一說、一念之中，備有不定。不同舊義，專判一部。味味中悉如此，此乃顯露不定。

As differentiated above, this only clarifies, in terms of what is open and manifest, the marks of the gradual and the sudden across the Five Flavors. But if the indeterminate is discussed, the sense is not so simple. Though the sudden teaching was given on the high mountain, without the Buddha ever stirring from the seat of quiescent enlightenment, he also wandered and taught at the Deer Park; though he taught the Four Noble Truths of arising and ceasing, this did not obstruct his also teaching the truth of neither arising nor ceasing; though he taught the bodhisattvas the Buddha's own state, there were also present the wisdom and severance of the two vehicles; though the five ascetics realized the fruit, this did not prevent eighty thousand gods from attaining the forbearance of non-arising. One should know that it is precisely in the sudden that the gradual is present, and precisely in the gradual that the sudden is present. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "At times he taught the profound, at times he taught the shallow." Where a question was fitting, he answered by declining to answer; where declining was fitting, he answered by questioning. Within a single occasion, a single teaching, a single thought-moment, the indeterminate is present in full. This is not the same as the old view, which fixes each teaching rigidly to a single scripture-division. It is so within every one of the flavors alike — this is the open-and-manifest indeterminate.

祕密不定，其義不然。如來於法，得最自在，若智、若機、若時、若處，三密四門，無妨無礙。此座說頓，十方說漸、說不定。頓座不聞十方，十方不聞頓座。或十方說頓、說不定，此座說漸。各各不相知聞，於此是顯，於彼是密。或為一人說頓，或為多人說漸、說不定，或為一人說漸，為多人說頓。各各不相知，互為顯密。或一座默，十方說；十方默，一座說。或俱默，俱說。各各不相知，互為顯密。雖復如此，未盡如來於法自在之力。但可智知，不可言辨。雖復甚多，亦不出漸、頓、不定、祕密。

The secret indeterminate has a different sense. The Tathāgata, with respect to the Dharma, has attained the most complete freedom: whether in wisdom, in capacity, in time, or in place, through the three mysteries and the four gates, there is no hindrance and no obstruction whatsoever. In this assembly he teaches the sudden, while in the ten directions he teaches the gradual, or teaches the indeterminate. Those in the assembly of the sudden do not hear what is taught in the ten directions; those in the ten directions do not hear what is taught in the assembly of the sudden. Or the ten directions may teach the sudden, or teach the indeterminate, while this assembly teaches the gradual. Each remains unaware of and unable to hear the other: on this side it is open, on that side it is secret. Or he may teach the sudden to one person, and teach the gradual, or the indeterminate, to many; or he may teach the gradual to one person, and teach the sudden to many. Each remains unaware of the other, and each is, in turn, open to itself and secret to the other. Or one assembly may fall silent while the ten directions teach; or the ten directions may fall silent while one assembly teaches. Or both may fall silent, or both may teach. Each remains unaware of the other, and each is, in turn, open and secret. And even so, this still does not exhaust the Tathāgata's own freedom with respect to the Dharma. It can only be known by wisdom, and cannot be fully accounted for in words. Though it is manifold indeed, it still does not go beyond the gradual, the sudden, the indeterminate, and the secret.

今《法華》是顯露，非祕密；是漸頓，非漸漸；是合，非不合；是醍醐非四味；是定，非不定。如此分別，此經與眾經相異也。

Now, the Lotus Sūtra is open and manifest, not secret; it is the gradual-gathered-into-the-sudden, not simply gradual-after-gradual; it is a uniting, not a non-uniting; it is ghee, not any of the other Four Flavors; it is determinate, not indeterminate. Distinguished in this way, this sūtra differs from all other sūtras.

又異者，餘教當機益物，不說如來施化之意。此經明佛設教元始，巧為眾生作頓、漸、不定、顯密種子，中間以頓、漸五味調伏長養而成熟之，又以頓漸五味而度脫之。並脫、並熟、並種、番番不息，大勢威猛，三世益物，具如〈信解品〉中說，與餘經異也。

Again, it differs in this further way: the other teachings simply benefit beings as suited to the occasion, without explaining the Tathāgata's own intention in setting forth his transforming teaching at all. This sūtra makes clear the very origin of the Buddha's establishing of his teaching: how, with skillful means, he first planted seeds of the sudden, the gradual, the indeterminate, and the open-and-secret in beings; how, in the interval, he tempered, nurtured, and ripened them through the sudden and gradual Five Flavors; and how he then liberated them, again, through the sudden and gradual Five Flavors. Liberating together, ripening together, planting seeds together — round after round without cease, with vast and mighty force, benefiting beings across the three times: this is set out in full in the "Faith and Understanding" chapter, and is where this sūtra differs from all other sūtras.

又眾經咸云「道樹，師實智始滿；起道樹，始施權智」。今經明師之權實，在道樹前久久已滿。諸經明二乘弟子不得入實智，亦不能施權智；今經明弟子入實甚久，亦先解行權。又眾經尚不論道樹之前，師之與弟，近近權實，況復遠遠；今經明道樹之前，權實長遠，補處數世界不知，況其塵數！經云「昔所未曾說，今皆當得聞」，殷勤稱讚，良有以也。當知此經，異諸教也。

Again, all other sūtras alike say that it was beneath the bodhi tree that the teacher's real wisdom was first brought to completion, and that it was on rising from beneath the bodhi tree that he first began to bestow provisional wisdom. This present sūtra makes clear that the teacher's provisional and real wisdom had already, long, long before the bodhi tree, been brought to completion. Other sūtras hold that disciples of the two vehicles cannot enter the real wisdom, nor can they bestow provisional wisdom on others; this present sūtra makes clear that the disciples entered the real a very long time ago, and had already, before that, understood and practiced the provisional. Again, other sūtras do not even discuss what lay before the bodhi tree — the near, recent provisional and real wisdom shared between teacher and disciple — how much less the far, distant past! This present sūtra makes clear that, before the bodhi tree, the provisional and real wisdom of teacher and disciple already stretched back across a vast span of time — a span whose full number even a bodhisattva of the next-to-last stage cannot know the worlds of, how much less its number of dust-motes! The sūtra says: "What was never taught before, all of it shall now be heard" — and it is truly for this reason that it is praised so fervently and repeatedly. One should know that this sūtra differs from all other teachings.

Citing the Proof-Texts 引證

Chinese Source

○二、引證者，如文殊答問偈云「我見燈明佛，本光瑞如此，以是知今佛，欲說《法華經》」。何但二萬億？大通智勝及五佛章中三世佛說，皆名「法華」也。文云「今佛放光明，助發實相義」，又云「諸法實相義，已為汝等說」，又云「無量眾所尊，為說實相印」。此亦今古同以實相為體也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, citing proof-texts: as in Mañjuśrī's verse answering the question, which says: "I saw the Buddha Candrasūryapradīpa; the original light-sign was just like this; by this I know that the present Buddha, too, wishes to teach the Lotus Sūtra." And it was not only for the sake of the twenty thousand koṭis of buddhas in that story alone — in the chapter of Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū and the five hundred buddhas, the buddhas of the three times are all said to have taught the "Lotus." The text says: "The present Buddha emits a ray of light, helping to bring forth the meaning of the true mark," and again: "The meaning of the true mark of all dharmas has already been taught for your sake," and again: "Honored by immeasurable multitudes, he teaches the seal of the true mark." This, too, shows that the present and the ancient buddhas alike take the true mark as the essence.

文云「佛當雨法雨，充足求道者」，即是會三歸一之法雨，令求佛道因者充足，乃至一切皆會令充足。若開近顯遠之法雨，令求佛道果者充足。文云「諸求三乘人，若有疑悔者，佛當為除斷，令盡無有餘」，又云「諸佛法久後，要當說真實」，即是斷三乘、五乘、七方便、九法界等疑，皆令生信。此證經用也。

The text says: "The Buddha shall rain down the rain of the Dharma, satisfying those who seek the path" — this is precisely the rain of the Dharma that gathers the three vehicles back into the one, satisfying those who seek the cause of buddhahood, and indeed gathering and satisfying every being without exception. As for the rain of the Dharma that opens the near to reveal the distant, it satisfies those who seek the fruit of buddhahood. The text says: "Whoever among those seeking the three vehicles harbors doubt and regret, the Buddha shall remove and sever it for them, exhausting it entirely, leaving nothing behind," and again: "Long after teaching the Dharma, the buddhas must in the end speak the truth" — this is precisely the severing of doubts belonging to the three vehicles, the five vehicles, the seven skillful means, the nine dharma-realms, and the like, causing faith to arise in every case. This proves the function of the sūtra.

又〈如來神力品〉云「以要言之，如來一切所有之法，如來一切自在神力，如來一切祕要之藏，如來一切甚深之事，皆於此經宣示顯說」。一切法者，權實一切法皆攝也，此證經名。一切自在神力者，內用名自在，外用名神力，即證用也。一切祕要之藏者，非器莫授為祕，正體為要，多所含容而無積聚名藏，此證體也。一切甚深之事者，實相名甚深，為實相修因名深因，究竟實相名深果。

Again, the "Tathāgata's Supernatural Power" chapter says: "To state it in essence: whatever the Tathāgata possesses, all of it — the Tathāgata's every kind of unhindered spiritual power, the Tathāgata's every secret and essential treasury, the Tathāgata's every deeply profound matter — all of it is proclaimed and openly taught in this sūtra." "Whatever the Tathāgata possesses, all of it" means that all dharmas, both provisional and real, are gathered in without exception — this proves the name of the sūtra. "Every kind of unhindered spiritual power" — its inward function is named "unhindered," its outward function is named "spiritual power" — this proves the function. "Every secret and essential treasury" — what cannot be transmitted except to a fit vessel is "secret"; the correct essence is "essential"; containing much within itself while never accumulating as a fixed possession is named a "treasury" — this proves the essence. "Every deeply profound matter" — the true mark itself is named "profound"; the cause cultivated for the sake of the true mark is named "the profound cause"; the ultimate realization of the true mark is named "the profound fruit."

又〈法師品〉云「若聞此經，乃是善行菩薩之道」，深因也。「求佛道者，咸於我前聞《妙法華經》一句，乃至一念隨喜，我皆與授記。乃至須臾聞之，即得究竟三菩提深果。」此證宗也。

Again, the "Teacher of the Dharma" chapter says: "If one hears this sūtra, this is truly the good practice of the bodhisattva path" — this is the profound cause. "Whoever seeks the buddha path, if they hear even a single phrase of the Wondrous Dharma Lotus Flower Sūtra in my presence, or even give rise to a single thought-moment of rejoicing, I shall bestow upon all of them a prediction of buddhahood. Even hearing it for a mere instant, they at once attain the profound fruit of ultimate, complete awakening." This proves the cardinal doctrine.

所以引二文者，古佛事定，舉要略以釋疑。今佛說竟，舉要略以付囑。中間正當機廣說，故不引證耳。若引者，「開、示、悟、入」，即其文也。「為大事因緣故」，證名；「佛之知見」，證體；「開示悟入」，證宗；「為令眾生」，證用；「此異餘經」，證教也。

The reason these two texts are cited is that the former, concerning a buddha of the past, has its matter already settled, and so raises the essentials briefly to resolve doubt, while the latter, spoken by the present Buddha as his teaching draws to a close, likewise raises the essentials briefly as a final entrustment. What lies between these two — the extended teaching given directly to the assembly at hand — is therefore not cited here as proof. Were it to be cited, "opening, showing, awakening to, and entering" would be exactly the text for it: "on account of the one great cause and condition" proves the name; "the Buddha's own knowledge and vision" proves the essence; "opening, showing, awakening to, and entering" proves the cardinal doctrine; "for the sake of causing sentient beings [to enter]" proves the function; "this differs from other sūtras" proves the teaching.

又〈藥王品〉舉十譬歎教，今引其六：大如海，高如山，圓如月，照如日，自在如梵王、極如佛。海是坎德，萬流歸故，同一鹹故。《法華》亦爾，佛所證得，萬善同歸，同乘佛乘。江湖川流，無此大德，餘經亦爾，故《法華》最大也。

Again, the "Bhaiṣajyarāja" chapter raises ten similes in praise of the teaching; here I cite six of them: vast as the ocean, lofty as a mountain, full as the moon, illuminating as the sun, sovereign as the Brahmā king, and ultimate as the Buddha. The ocean has the virtue of the abyss: the ten thousand streams return to it, and all alike become one and the same in taste, salt. The Lotus is likewise: what the Buddha has realized and attained is that the ten thousand good deeds all return to the same place, all alike riding the one buddha-vehicle. Rivers, lakes, and streams have no virtue so vast as this, nor do the other sūtras — hence the Lotus is the vastest of all.

山王最高，四寶所成故，純諸天居故。《法華》亦爾，在四味教之頂，離四誹謗，開、示、悟、入，純一根一緣，同一道味，純是菩薩，無聲聞弟子故。月能虧盈故，月漸圓故。《法華》亦爾，同體權實故，會漸入頓故。

The king of mountains is the loftiest, being formed of the four treasures, and being the pure dwelling-place of the gods. The Lotus is likewise: standing at the summit of the teaching of the Four Flavors, free of the four kinds of slander, its opening, showing, awakening to, and entering are directed to a single, pure capacity and a single, pure condition, sharing one and the same taste of the path — for it is purely for bodhisattvas, having no disciples of the śrāvaka vehicle within it. The moon can wane and can wax, and the moon grows gradually full. The Lotus is likewise: for the provisional and the real are of the same essence within it, and it gathers the gradual back into the sudden.

燈、炬、星、月與闇共住，譬諸經存二乘道果，與小並立，日能破闇故。《法華》破化城，除草庵故。又日映奪星月，令不現故。《法華》拂迹，除方便故。

The lamp, the torch, the stars, and the moon all dwell together with darkness — this is a simile for how the other sūtras preserve the fruit of the two vehicles' path, standing side by side with the lesser. But the sun is able to break through darkness entirely: the Lotus breaks through the phantom city, and clears away the thatched hut. And the sun's brilliance overwhelms the stars and the moon, causing them no longer to appear: the Lotus sweeps away the trace, and clears away skillful means.

輪王於四域自在，釋王於三十三天自在，大梵於三界自在。諸經或於俗諦自在，或於真諦自在，或於中道自在；但是歷別自在，非大自在。今經三諦圓融，最得自在，譬大梵王。餘經拔眾生出生死，如五佛子，於凡夫第一。或拔眾生出涅槃，如菩薩居無學上。今經拔出眾生，過方便教菩薩上，即成法王，最為第一。

A wheel-turning king is sovereign over the four continents; Śakra is sovereign over the thirty-three heavens; Great Brahmā is sovereign over the three realms. The other sūtras are sovereign, some over the conventional truth, some over the truth of the real, some over the truth of the middle — but this is only a sovereignty held separately over each, not a great, all-encompassing sovereignty. The present sūtra, in which the three truths are perfectly fused, attains the most complete sovereignty of all — comparable to the Great Brahmā king. The other sūtras draw sentient beings out of birth-and-death, as the Tripiṭaka teaching's five kinds of Buddha's disciples do, foremost among ordinary beings; or they draw sentient beings out beyond nirvāṇa itself, as bodhisattvas stand above those beyond further learning. The present sūtra draws sentient beings out and beyond even the bodhisattvas of the skillful-means teachings, bringing them at once to become Dharma-kings, foremost of all.

引諸譬喻，明教相最大。例知用、宗、體、名，亦大如海，境智乃至利益，亦大如海。教相如山，在四味教上，用、宗、體、名、境、智、利益，亦復如是。教相虧盈圓滿，如月；用、宗、體、名、境、智、利益，亦復如是。教破化城，用、宗、體、名、境、智、利益，亦復如是。教相自在，餘亦如是。教相王中王，餘亦如是。非但引文證教，餘義亦成。

Citing these various similes makes clear that the mark of the teaching is the vastest of all. By this same pattern one may know that the function, the cardinal doctrine, the essence, and the name are likewise as vast as the ocean, and that its objects of contemplation, its wisdom, and even its benefits are likewise as vast as the ocean. The mark of the teaching is like the mountain, standing above the teachings of the Four Flavors — and the function, the cardinal doctrine, the essence, the name, the objects of contemplation, the wisdom, and the benefits are likewise so. The mark of the teaching wanes and waxes to full completion, like the moon — and the function, the cardinal doctrine, the essence, the name, the objects of contemplation, the wisdom, and the benefits are likewise so. The teaching breaks through the phantom city — and the function, the cardinal doctrine, the essence, the name, the objects of contemplation, the wisdom, and the benefits are likewise so. The mark of the teaching is sovereign — the rest are likewise so. The mark of the teaching is the king among kings — the rest are likewise so. It is not only by citing the text that the teaching is proven; every other sense, too, is thereby established.

How the Five Sections Arise in Sequence 生起

Chinese Source

○三、生起者，能生為生，所生為起。前後有次第，麁細不相違。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, showing its arising in sequence: what gives rise is called "arising"; what is given rise to is called "coming forth." There is a sequence to what comes before and after, and the coarse and the fine do not conflict with one another.

肇云：「名無召物之功，物無應名之實。無名無物，名、物安在？」蓋第一義中無相意耳。世諦為言，無名無以顯法，故初釋名。名，名於法，法即是體，尋名識體；體非宗不會，會體自行已圓，從體起用，導利含識；利益既多，須分別教相也。

Sengzhao says: "The name has no power to summon the thing; the thing has no reality that answers to the name. Without a name and without a thing — where, then, do name and thing stand?" This is simply the sense of the markless within the truth of ultimate meaning. But speaking in terms of conventional truth, without a name there is no way to make the dharma manifest — hence the name is explained first. The name names the dharma, and the dharma is itself the essence; by seeking out the name one comes to know the essence. But the essence cannot be grasped apart from the cardinal doctrine; once the essence is grasped, one's own practice is already made complete, and from the essence the function arises, guiding and benefiting sentient beings. And since the benefits bestowed are so many, one must further differentiate the mark of the teaching.

〈神力品〉中，約教次第，一切法本皆佛法。《大經》云「一切世諦，若於如來，即是第一義諦。」眾生顛倒，謂非佛法，今明言示之，故言一切法也。

In the "Supernatural Power" chapter, in terms of the sequence of the teaching: all dharmas are, at root, all the Buddha's own dharma. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Every conventional truth, when it is seen in the Tathāgata, is itself the truth of ultimate meaning." Sentient beings, inverted in their views, take it that these are not the Buddha's own dharma — hence the chapter now clearly points this out and shows it, and so says "all dharmas."

欲說此法，先以神力駭動，故言一切自在神力。既見變通，醒悟渴仰，得為說教，教詮實相，故言祕密之藏也。稟教修行，即有因果，故言甚深之事也。欲分別四義與餘經同異，次明教相耳。

Wishing to teach this dharma, he first startles and moves the assembly with his spiritual power — hence it is said "every kind of unhindered spiritual power." Having witnessed this transformation and unhindered display, they awaken and thirst for it eagerly, and so become fit to be taught; the teaching sets forth the true mark — hence it is said "the secret treasury." Receiving the teaching and cultivating practice accordingly, cause and fruit are thereby present — hence it is said "the deeply profound matter." Wishing then to differentiate how these four senses agree with and differ from other sūtras, the chapter next makes clear the mark of the teaching.

〈序品〉約行次第，初從經卷，若善知識，有所聞見，即聞名也。聞故推理體顯，顯體須行，行即因果，宗也。行自排惑，亦利眾生，是用也。分別同異，教相也。

The "Introduction" chapter, in terms of the sequence of practice: at first, whether from a sūtra-scroll or from a good spiritual friend, one has something one hears or sees — this is hearing the name. Having heard it, one presses on to reason out and make the essence manifest; making the essence manifest requires practice, and practice is itself cause and fruit — this is the cardinal doctrine. Practice of itself clears away delusion, and also benefits sentient beings — this is the function. Differentiating what agrees and what differs is the mark of the teaching.

開、示、悟、入，亦約行次第，法本無開閉，今呼為方便門開，此聞名也。示真實相，體也。自迷得悟，悟因也。由因故悟果，宗也。悟故深入，亦令他入，用也。分別同異，教也。今之五義，依〈序品〉扶行次第也。

"Opening, showing, awakening to, and entering," too, follow the sequence of practice: the dharma is, at root, neither opened nor closed, yet it is now called the opening of the gate of skillful means — this is hearing the name. "Showing the mark of the real truth" is the essence. Turning from one's own confusion to awakening is the causal awakening. Awakening to the fruit on the basis of this cause is the cardinal doctrine. Because one has awakened, one enters deeply, and also causes others to enter — this is the function. Differentiating what agrees and what differs is the teaching. These five senses, drawn from the "Introduction" chapter, follow and support the sequence of practice.

Reading note

Sengzhao (僧肇, 384–414), Kumārajīva's disciple, is cited here for a line from his commentary on the Vimalakīrti Sūtra, already noted as a recurring source of etymological glosses in the Fahua Wenju's own translation notes. "Opening, showing, awakening to, and entering" (開示悟入) is the sūtra's own fourfold formula for why buddhas appear in the world at all, from the "Skillful Means" chapter — the single phrase Zhiyi treats as containing, in miniature, all five of his own sections at once.

Opening Up and Combining the Five Sections 開合

Chinese Source

○四、開合者，五章共釋一經，種種分別，令易解故。凡三種開合，謂五種、十種、譬喻。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, opening up and combining: the five sections together explain the one sūtra, differentiated in various ways to make it easier to understand. There are, in general, three kinds of opening-up-and-combining: the fivefold, the tenfold, and the simile.

初、釋名通論事、理，顯體專論理，宗、用但論事，教相分別事、理。釋名通說教行，顯體非教、非行，宗、用但行，教相但教。釋名通說因果，顯體非因、非果，宗自因果，用教他因果，教相分別上法耳。釋名通論自行、化他，體非自、非他，宗是自行，用是化他，教相分別自、他。釋名通論說默，體非說、非默，宗默、用說，教相分別(云云)。

First: Explaining the Name discusses both phenomena and principle in common; Clarifying the Essence discusses principle alone; the Cardinal Doctrine and the Function discuss only phenomena; Classifying the Teachings differentiates phenomena and principle. Explaining the Name discusses both teaching and practice in common; Clarifying the Essence is neither teaching nor practice; the Cardinal Doctrine and the Function are only practice; Classifying the Teachings is only teaching. Explaining the Name discusses cause and fruit in common; Clarifying the Essence is neither cause nor fruit; the Cardinal Doctrine concerns one's own cause and fruit, the Function teaches others' cause and fruit; Classifying the Teachings differentiates the dharmas set out above. Explaining the Name discusses one's own practice and transforming others in common; the Essence is neither one's own nor others'; the Cardinal Doctrine is one's own practice, the Function is transforming others; Classifying the Teachings differentiates one's own from others'. Explaining the Name discusses speaking and silence in common; the Essence is neither speaking nor silence; the Cardinal Doctrine is silence, the Function is speech; Classifying the Teachings differentiates them, as will be explained.

十種者，釋名總論三軌：體、宗、用開對三軌，教相分別三軌。釋名總論三道：體、宗、用開對三道，教相分別三道。乃至第十，釋名總論三德：體、宗、用開對三德，教相分別三德(云云)。

As for the tenfold scheme: Explaining the Name discusses the three tracks as a whole; the Essence, the Cardinal Doctrine, and the Function, opened up, correspond respectively to the three tracks; Classifying the Teachings differentiates the three tracks. Explaining the Name discusses the three paths as a whole; the Essence, the Cardinal Doctrine, and the Function, opened up, correspond to the three paths; Classifying the Teachings differentiates the three paths. And so on up to the tenth: Explaining the Name discusses the three virtues as a whole; the Essence, the Cardinal Doctrine, and the Function, opened up, correspond to the three virtues; Classifying the Teachings differentiates the three virtues, as will be explained.

譬喻者，譬如總名人身，開身則有識、命、煖，分別諸身貴、賤、賢、愚，種種差降。人身譬名，識以譬體，命以譬宗，煖以譬用，分別譬教相(云云)。

As for the simile: it is as when the general name "the human body" is opened up, and it is found to consist of consciousness, life, and warmth, while, beyond this, the differentiation of bodies as noble or base, worthy or foolish, admits of various further gradations. The human body is a simile for the name; consciousness is a simile for the essence; life is a simile for the cardinal doctrine; warmth is a simile for the function; and the further differentiation is a simile for the mark of the teaching, as will be explained.

Reading note

The "tenfold" scheme names ten sets of "three" that recur throughout Tiantai doctrine — the three tracks (三軌: the true nature, the contemplative wisdom, and the liberating deeds — later developed at length as 眞性軌/觀照軌/資成軌), the three paths (三道: affliction, karma, and suffering), and, as this passage's own ellipsis indicates, eight further triads through to the three virtues (三德: the Dharma-body, prajñā, and liberation, already established site-wide via the Mohe Zhiguan) — each mapped the same way onto Essence/Cardinal-Doctrine/Function while Name and Teaching frame them fore and aft. Zhiyi does not spell out all ten sets here; this passage functions as a template for readers already trained in his other works.

Resolving Doubts 料簡

Chinese Source

○五料簡者，若為蓮故華，華果必俱，將不墮因中有果耶？答：因中有果，舊醫邪法，已為初教所破，尚非麁權實義，況是妙因妙果、新醫真乳法邪？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, resolving doubts. Question: If it is for the sake of the seed-pod that the flower blooms, then flower and fruit must always be present together — does this not fall into the view that the fruit is already present within the cause? Answer: That the fruit is present within the cause is the old physician's mistaken teaching, already refuted by the very first of the teachings — it does not even rise to the level of the coarse sense of the provisional and the real, how much less to the wondrous cause and wondrous fruit, the new physician's true-milk method!

問：華以喻權，權是小乘之法，則不應破於草庵。草庵既破，何得以華喻權？答：小乘是化他之權，是故須破。今明自行之權，故以華喻耳。

Question: The flower is a metaphor for the provisional, and the provisional is the dharma of the small vehicle — then it should not be right to break through the thatched hut. But if the thatched hut is indeed broken through, how can the flower still serve as a metaphor for the provisional? Answer: The small vehicle is the provisional used to transform others, and so it must be broken through. What is meant here is the provisional of one's own practice — hence the flower still serves as its metaphor.

問：文內從火宅至醫子，凡七譬，悉不明蓮華，何以取此為題？答：七譬是別，蓮華是總，舉總攝別，故冠篇首也。

Question: Within the text, from the burning house to the physician's sons, there are in all seven similes, none of which mentions the lotus flower — why, then, is the lotus flower taken as the title? Answer: The seven similes are particular; the lotus flower is general. Raising the general gathers in the particular — hence it is placed at the head of the title.

問：一切法皆佛法，何意簡權取實為體？答：若開權顯實，諸法皆體；若廢權顯實，如前所用。

Question: If all dharmas are the Buddha's own dharma, why sift out the provisional and take only the real as the essence? Answer: If the provisional is opened to reveal the real, then all dharmas alike are the essence; if the provisional is set aside to reveal the real, then it is as used above.

問：何故雙用因果為宗？答：由因致果，果為因所辦。若從能辦，以因為宗；若從所辦，以果為宗。二義本是相成，不得單取。又迹本二文，俱說因果故。

Question: Why does the cardinal doctrine make use of both cause and fruit together? Answer: Because the fruit is brought about by the cause, and the fruit is what the cause accomplishes. If one speaks in terms of what accomplishes, the cause is taken as the cardinal doctrine; if one speaks in terms of what is accomplished, the fruit is taken as the cardinal doctrine. These two senses are, at root, mutually completing one another, and neither can be taken alone. Moreover, both the trace-gate and origin-gate texts of the sūtra speak of cause and fruit together.

問：論宗簡化他因果，明用俱取自他權實？答：宗論自行，故須簡他，用是益他，是故雙取。又問：用是化他，亦不須自行權實？答：欲以自利、利他故。

Question: In discussing the cardinal doctrine, the causes and fruits of transforming others are sifted out — yet in clarifying the function, both one's own and others' provisional and real are taken together. Answer: The cardinal doctrine discusses one's own practice, and so what belongs to others must be sifted out; the function is what benefits others, and so both are taken together. A further question: If the function is transforming others, should it not also dispense with the provisional and real of one's own practice? Answer: It is because one wishes both to benefit oneself and to benefit others.

並宗亦應然，欲自行化他因果，是故應取他也？答：化他因果，不能致佛菩提，是故不取。並用他權實，亦不能令他至極，亦不應取？答：他宜須此利，是故取也。

By the same reasoning, should not the cardinal doctrine also be so — wishing for the cause and fruit of both one's own practice and transforming others, and therefore taking in what belongs to others as well? Answer: The cause and fruit of transforming others cannot bring one to the Buddha's own awakening — hence it is not taken. By the same reasoning, the provisional and real used for others, too, cannot bring others to the ultimate — should it, too, not be taken? Answer: What others need is precisely this benefit — hence it is taken.

問：宗、用俱明智斷，云何分別？答：自行以智德為宗，斷德為用，若化他、自行，智斷俱為宗，化他，智斷俱為用。

Question: Both the cardinal doctrine and the function speak of wisdom and severance — how are they differentiated? Answer: For one's own practice, the virtue of wisdom is the cardinal doctrine and the virtue of severance is the function; but if one's own practice and transforming others are both considered, then wisdom and severance together are the cardinal doctrine, and wisdom and severance together, applied to transforming others, are the function.

問：何故五章，不四不六？答：設作四六，亦復生疑，墮無窮問，非也。

Question: Why five sections — not four, and not six? Answer: Were they made four or six, doubt would likewise arise, and one would fall into an endless chain of questioning — this alone shows the number itself is not the point.

問：經經各有異意，那得五義共釋眾經耶？答：若經經別釋，但得別，不得同。今共論五義，得同不失別。

Question: Each sūtra has its own particular sense — how, then, can these five senses together explain every sūtra alike? Answer: If each sūtra were explained separately, one would grasp only the particular, and not the common. By discussing these five senses together now, one grasps the common without losing the particular.

The Contemplation of Mind 觀心

Chinese Source

○六明觀心者，從標章至料簡，悉明觀心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, clarifying the contemplation of mind: from the marking of the section through to the resolving of doubts, all of it clarifies the contemplation of mind.

心如幻焰，但有名字，名之為心。適言其有，不見色質；適言其無，復起慮想。不可以有、無思度故，故名心為「妙」。妙心可軌，稱之為「法」。心法非因、非果，能如理觀，即辦因果，是名「蓮華」。由一心成觀，亦轉教餘心，名之為「經」。釋名竟。

Mind is like an illusory flame — it has only a name, and is named "mind." Just when one says it exists, no form or substance is to be found; just when one says it does not exist, thoughts and considerations arise once more. Since it cannot be measured by thought as either existent or nonexistent, mind is therefore named "wondrous." The wondrous mind can serve as a track to follow, and so is called "dharma." Mind as dharma is neither cause nor fruit, yet, contemplated in accordance with principle, it is able to accomplish cause and fruit — this is named "lotus flower." Because this contemplation is accomplished through the one mind, and is also passed on to teach other minds, it is named "sūtra." Explaining the Name is now complete.

心本無名，亦無無名。心名不生，亦復不滅，心即實相。初觀為因，觀成為果。以觀心故，惡覺不起。心數塵勞，若同若異，皆被化而轉，是為觀心。標五章竟。

Mind, at root, has no name, and yet is not without a name either. The naming of mind neither arises nor ceases — mind is itself the true mark. The initial contemplation is the cause; the contemplation brought to completion is the fruit. Through the contemplation of mind, unwholesome thoughts fail to arise. The mind's associated mental factors and the dust of affliction — whether like the mind or different from it — are all thereby transformed and turned around. This is the contemplation of mind. The marking of the five sections is now complete.

觀心引證者，《釋論》云「一陰名色，四陰名名，心但是名也。」《大經》云「能觀心性，名為上定。上定者，第一義定。」證心是體。《大經》云：「夫有心者，皆當得三菩提。」心是宗也。《遺教》云：「制心一處，無事不辦。」心是用也。《釋論》云：「三界無別法，唯是一心作。」心能地獄、心能天堂，心能凡夫、心能賢聖。覺觀心是語本，以心分別於心，證心是教相也。

Citing proof-texts for the contemplation of mind: the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra says: "One aggregate is called form; the remaining four aggregates are called name — mind is simply this 'name.'" The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "To be able to contemplate the nature of mind is named the supreme concentration; the supreme concentration is the concentration of ultimate meaning" — this proves that mind is the essence. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Whoever has a mind shall, without exception, attain complete awakening" — mind is the cardinal doctrine. The Sūtra of the Buddha's Final Bequest says: "Fix the mind in one place, and there is nothing that cannot be accomplished" — mind is the function. The Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra says: "The three realms have no separate dharma of their own — they are made by the one mind alone." Mind can make a hell, mind can make a heaven; mind can make an ordinary being, mind can make a worthy or a sage. Since discursive and reflective mind is the very root of language, and it is by mind that mind itself is differentiated — this proves that mind is the mark of the teaching.

觀心生起者，以心觀心，由能觀心，有所觀境。以觀契境故，從心得解脫故。若一心得解脫，能令一切數皆得解脫故。分別心王、心數，同起、偏起等，即是教相故。

The arising of the contemplation of mind in sequence: it is by mind that mind is contemplated; because there is a mind that contemplates, there is an object that is contemplated. Because the contemplation accords with its object, liberation is attained by way of mind. If the one mind attains liberation, this can cause every one of its mental factors likewise to attain liberation. The differentiation of the mind-king and its mental factors, of their arising together or arising separately, and so forth, is itself the mark of the teaching.

觀心開合者，心是諸法之本，心即總也。別說有三種心：煩惱心是三支；苦果心是七支；業心是二支。苦心即法身，是心體；煩惱心即般若，是心宗；業心即解脫，是心用：即開心為三也。分別十二因緣心生，即有六道差降。分別心滅，即有四聖高下，是為教相兼於開合也。

Opening up and combining, applied to the contemplation of mind: mind is the root of all dharmas — mind is itself the general. Spoken of separately, there are three kinds of mind: the mind of affliction, which is three of the twelve links; the mind of the fruit of suffering, which is seven of the links; and the mind of karma, which is two of the links. The mind of suffering is itself the Dharma-body, and is the essence of mind; the mind of affliction is itself prajñā, and is the cardinal doctrine of mind; the mind of karma is itself liberation, and is the function of mind — this is the opening of mind into three. Differentiating how mind arises through the twelve links of dependent origination, there are the graded distinctions of the six destinies; differentiating how mind ceases, there are the high and low ranks of the four kinds of sages — this is the mark of the teaching applied together with opening-up-and-combining.

觀心料簡者。問：事解已足，何煩觀心？答：《大論》云「佛為信行人，以樹為喻；為法行人，以身為喻。」今亦如是，為文字人，約事解釋；為坐禪人，作觀心解。

Resolving doubts, applied to the contemplation of mind. Question: If the explanation in terms of phenomena already suffices, why trouble further with the contemplation of mind? Answer: The Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra says: "For the faith-follower, the Buddha uses the tree as a simile; for the dharma-follower, he uses the body as a simile." It is likewise so here: for the person of the written word, the explanation is given in terms of phenomena; for the person who sits in meditation, an explanation is given through the contemplation of mind.

又《論》作四句評「有慧無多聞，是不知實相，譬如大闇中，有目無所見；多聞無智慧，亦不知實相，譬如大明中，有燈而無照；多聞利智慧，是所說應受；無聞無智慧，是名人身牛。」今使聞、慧兼修，義、觀雙舉，《百論》有盲跛之譬，《牟子》有說行之義。

Again, the Śāstra makes a fourfold judgment in verse: "One who has wisdom but no broad learning does not know the true mark — like one with eyes in the midst of great darkness, who has nothing to see. One who has broad learning but no wisdom likewise does not know the true mark — like one with a lamp in the midst of great brightness, whose light illuminates nothing. One who has both broad learning and sharp wisdom — what such a person teaches ought to be received. One who has neither learning nor wisdom is called a human being in body only, an ox in understanding." Now, one should cultivate both learning and wisdom together, raising up doctrinal understanding and contemplative practice side by side — the Śata-śāstra gives the simile of the blind man and the lame man, and the Mouzi gives the principle of teaching and practice going together.

《華嚴》云「譬如貧窮人，日夜數他寶，自無半錢分」，偏聞之失也。下文云「未得謂得，未證謂證」，偏觀之失也。何者？視聽馳散，如風中燈，照物不了。但貴耳入口出，都不治心，自是陵人，增見長非，把刃自傷，解牽惡道，由其不習觀也。若觀心人，謂即心而是，已則均佛。都不尋經論，墮增上慢，此則抱炬自燒，行牽惡道，由不習聞也。

The Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "It is like a poor and destitute man who, day and night, counts another's treasure, while he himself does not possess so much as half a coin" — this is the fault of one-sided learning. A passage below says: "Claiming attainment before one has attained it, claiming realization before one has realized it" — this is the fault of one-sided contemplation. Why is this? Sight and hearing, scattered and dispersed, are like a lamp in the wind, whose illumination of things is never complete. If one merely prizes what enters the ear and leaves the mouth, never bringing it to bear on curing the mind at all, one becomes, of oneself, someone who looks down on others, whose views only increase and whose faults only grow, grasping the blade and wounding oneself, dragged by one's own understanding toward an evil destiny — all this comes from failing to practice contemplation. And if a person of contemplation alone claims that mind, just as it is, is already the truth, and that he is already equal to the Buddha, never once seeking out sūtra or treatise, he falls into overweening pride — this is embracing a torch and burning oneself, dragged by one's own practice toward an evil destiny — all this comes from failing to practice learning.

若欲免貧窮，當勤三觀；欲免上慢，當聞六即。世間相常住，理即也。於諸過去佛，若有聞一句，名字即也。深信隨喜，觀行即也。六根清淨，相似即也。安住實智中，分證即也。唯佛與佛究盡實相，究竟即也。修心內觀，則有法財；正信外聞，無復上慢。眼慧明聞，具足利益，何得不觀解耶？

If one wishes to escape destitution, one should diligently practice the three contemplations; if one wishes to escape overweening pride, one should listen and learn the six identities. That the marks of the world are eternally abiding is identity in principle. Among all the buddhas of the past, if one has heard even a single phrase, this is identity in name. Deep faith and rejoicing is identity in contemplative practice. The purification of the six sense-faculties is identity in resemblance. Abiding securely within real wisdom is identity in a portion of the truth realized. Only buddha together with buddha fully fathoms the true mark to its very limit — this is identity in the ultimate. Cultivating the mind through inward contemplation, one comes to possess the wealth of the Dharma; holding correct faith through outward learning, one is no longer troubled by overweening pride. With the eye of wisdom and clear learning both complete, the benefit is fully provided — how, then, could one fail to practice contemplation and understanding both?

The "six identities" (六即), here given for the first time in this treatise, are the same six stages already established site-wide via the Mohe Zhiguan: identity in principle, in name, in contemplative practice, in resemblance, in a portion of the truth, and in the ultimate — a scale meant to guard equally against the "destitute man's" fault of learning without ever practicing, and the "overweening" fault of claiming attainment through practice alone, without the corrective of study. This closes Fascicle 1, Upper Half.
