The Wondrous of Sympathetic Response

Fascicle 6 opens the wondrous of sympathetic response — the sixth of the ten wondrous aspects, and the first to take up what a buddha accomplishes for the sake of others rather than for one's own practice alone.

Status: in progress Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

The Wondrous of Sympathetic Response 明感應妙

Chinese Source

第六明感應妙者，上來四妙名為圓因，三法祕藏名為圓果：境妙究竟顯，名毘盧遮那；智妙究竟滿，名盧舍那；行妙究竟滿，名釋迦牟尼。三佛不一異、不縱橫，故名妙果。《釋論》云：「稽首智度無子佛」者，果地圓極非復因位，故稱無子。果智寂照，有感必彰，故明感應妙也。即為六：一、釋感應名。二、明相。三、明同異。四、明相對。五、明麁妙。六、明觀心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, clarifying the wondrous of sympathetic response: the four wondrous aspects treated above are named the perfect cause; the secret treasury of the three dharmas is named the perfect fruit. The wondrous of the object, its ultimate manifestation, is named Vairocana; the wondrous of wisdom, its ultimate fulfillment, is named Locana; the wondrous of practice, its ultimate fulfillment, is named Śākyamuni. The three buddhas, neither one nor different, neither vertical nor horizontal, are hence named the wondrous fruit. The Treatise says: "I bow my head to the buddha of wisdom's perfection, who has no further child" — the ground of the fruit, perfect and ultimate, is no longer the stage of the cause, and hence is named "without a child." The fruit's own wisdom, quiescent and yet illuminating, wherever there is a capacity to respond to, invariably manifests — hence, the wondrous of sympathetic response is here clarified. This is treated under six headings: first, explaining the name "sympathetic response"; second, clarifying its mark; third, clarifying sameness and difference; fourth, clarifying mutual correspondence; fifth, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous; sixth, the contemplation of mind.

Explaining the Name 釋名

Chinese Source

釋名又三：一、釋名。二、四悉檀帖解。三、料簡。釋名者，《正法華》云：「無數世界廣說經法」，世尊所為感應如是，今故用為名。而經中機語、緣語，並是感之異目，悉語眾生，且從機釋義則易見，緣感例可解。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the name is itself further treated under three headings: first, explaining the name itself; second, matching it against the four siddhāntas; third, resolving doubts. As for explaining the name: the Saddharmapuṇḍarīka, in its earlier translation, says, "Throughout countless worlds, the sūtra's dharma is broadly proclaimed" — the World-Honored One's own undertaking is precisely this kind of sympathetic response, and this is accordingly used as the name here. Within the sūtra, the word "capacity" and the word "condition" are both alike alternate terms for "that which is responded to," and both alike refer to sentient beings — for now, taking up the explanation in terms of "capacity" makes the sense easiest to see, and "condition" and "responding to" may be understood by the same pattern.

機有三義：一者、機是微義。故《易》云：「機者，動之微，吉之先現。」又《阿含》云：「眾生有善法之機，聖人來應也。」眾生有將生之善，此善微微將動，而得為機。若將生善為機，此結為促；今明可生之善，此語則寬。如弩有可發之機，故射者發之，發之則箭動，不發則不前；眾生有可生之善，故聖應則善生，不應則不生。故言機者微也。二者、《古注楞伽經》云：「機是關義。」何者？眾生有善有惡，關聖慈悲，故機是關義也。三者、機是宜義。如欲拔無明之苦，正宜於悲；欲與法性之樂，正宜於慈，故機是宜義也。

"Capacity" carries three senses. First, capacity carries the sense of the subtle. Hence the Book of Changes says: "The subtle is the very first stirring of movement, appearing before the auspicious sign itself." And the Āgama says: "When sentient beings possess the capacity of a wholesome dharma, the sage comes and responds." When a sentient being possesses a wholesome quality about to arise, this wholesome quality, ever so subtly about to stir, is what constitutes the capacity. If "about to arise" alone were taken as the capacity, this would draw the sense too narrow; here, clarifying it instead as "able to arise," this wording is broader. It is like a crossbow possessing a trigger able to release it — the archer releases it, and, once released, the arrow moves; unreleased, it does not advance — sentient beings possess a wholesome quality able to arise, and so, when the sage responds, this wholesome quality arises, and, unresponded to, it does not arise. Hence it is said that capacity carries the sense of the subtle. Second, the Ancient Commentary on the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra says: "Capacity carries the sense of a hinge." Why is this? Sentient beings possess both good and evil, hinging upon the sage's own compassion — hence capacity carries the sense of a hinge. Third, capacity carries the sense of what is fitting. As, wishing to draw out the suffering of ignorance, precisely compassion is fitting; wishing to bestow the bliss of the dharma-nature, precisely loving-kindness is fitting — hence capacity carries the sense of what is fitting.

次明應者，亦為三義：一者、應是赴義。既言機有可生之理，機微將動，聖人赴之，其善得生，故用赴而釋應。二者、應是對義。如人交關，更相主對，若一欲賣，一不欲買，則不相主對；若賣買兩和，則貿易交決，貴賤無悔。今以眾生譬買，如來譬賣，就機以論關，就應以論對，故以對釋應也。三者、應是應義。既言機是於宜，宜何等法？應以慈悲之法，是善惡所宜。悲則宜救苦，慈則宜與樂。隨以何法，應其所宜，故以應釋應也。

Next, clarifying "response": this, too, carries three senses. First, response carries the sense of hastening toward. Since it has already been said that capacity possesses the principle of being able to arise, and, the capacity subtly about to stir, the sage hastens toward it, and its wholesome quality is thereby able to arise — hence "hastening toward" is used to explain "response." Second, response carries the sense of correspondence. It is like people engaged in trade, each corresponding to the other as principal parties — if one wishes to sell while the other has no wish to buy, then the two do not correspond as principal parties; if buyer and seller are both in agreement, then the exchange is settled, and neither the higher nor the lower price is regretted. Here, sentient beings are compared to the buyer, and the Tathāgata to the seller — discussed in terms of capacity, this is spoken of as a hinge; discussed in terms of response, this is spoken of as correspondence — hence "correspondence" is used to explain "response." Third, response carries the sense of responding. Since it has already been said that capacity lies in what is fitting, fitting for what kind of dharma? One responds with the dharma of loving-kindness and compassion, since this is what is fitting for both good and evil alike: compassion is fitting for rescuing from suffering, and loving-kindness is fitting for bestowing bliss. Responding with whatever dharma fits what is called for — hence "responding" is used to explain "response."

Matching Against the Four Siddhāntas 四悉檀帖釋

Chinese Source

二、明四悉檀帖釋者，機應各有三義，即四悉檀意也。若微以釋機，赴以釋應者，是赴樂欲之心也。何但心善可生名之為欲，如草木無心，亦稱可生、欲生、將生，故知赴此善生，是隨樂欲，即世界悉檀明機應也。若關以釋機，對以釋應，更相對當。以悲對其苦機、以慈對其善機者，即是隨對治悉檀以明機應也。次以宜釋機，以應釋應者，即是為人、第一義也。宜以如此等法，與其機感相宜，宜生事善，即為人悉檀；宜生理善，即是第一義悉檀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the matching against the four siddhāntas: capacity and response each carry three senses, and these are precisely the sense of the four siddhāntas. If "the subtle" explains capacity and "hastening toward" explains response, this is hastening toward the mind's own delight and desire. Why should only a mind possessing goodness be said to be capable of arising? Grass and trees, too, though without mind, are equally spoken of as able to arise, desiring to arise, about to arise — one should therefore know that hastening toward this arising of goodness accords with delight and desire, and this is precisely the mundane siddhānta clarifying capacity and response. If "the hinge" explains capacity and "correspondence" explains response, each corresponds precisely to the other: compassion corresponding to the capacity for suffering, and loving-kindness corresponding to the capacity for goodness — this is precisely clarifying capacity and response by way of the therapeutic siddhānta. Next, "what is fitting" explaining capacity and "responding" explaining response — this is precisely the individual siddhānta together with the siddhānta of ultimate meaning. What is fitting is a dharma of this same kind, fitting to the capacity being responded to: fitting to give rise to phenomenal goodness is precisely the individual siddhānta; fitting to give rise to goodness in principle is precisely the siddhānta of ultimate meaning.

Resolving Doubts 料簡

Chinese Source

三、料簡者。

問：
何意於理善稱第一義悉檀耶？

答：
理善明生，理闇必滅，終不理惡滅，方始理善生，故於理善稱第一義悉檀也。若事善生，事惡未必去；事惡去，事善未必生。事是隔別，對治悉檀正是藥病相對，故不於中開第一義悉檀，其意在此。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, resolving doubts.

Question: Why is it that goodness in principle alone is named the siddhānta of ultimate meaning?

Answer: When goodness in principle arises and becomes manifest, darkness in principle is thereby necessarily extinguished — it is never the case that evil in principle is first extinguished, and only afterward does goodness in principle arise; hence, goodness in principle alone is named the siddhānta of ultimate meaning. If phenomenal goodness arises, phenomenal evil is not thereby necessarily removed; if phenomenal evil is removed, phenomenal goodness is not thereby necessarily arisen. Phenomena are divided and set apart from one another, and the therapeutic siddhānta is precisely a matching of remedy against disease — hence, the siddhānta of ultimate meaning is not opened up within it. This is the very sense intended here.

問：
眾生機，聖人應，為一為異？若一則非機應，若異，何相交關而論機應？

答：
不一不異。理論則同如，是故不異；事論有機應，是故不一。譬如父子天性相關，骨肉遺體，異則不可；若同者，父即子，子即父，同又不可；秖不一不異而論父子也。眾生理性與佛不殊，是故不異；而眾生隱如來顯，是故不一，不一不異而論機應也。又同是非事非理故不異，眾生得事，聖人得理；又聖人得事，凡夫有理。故論異(云云)。

Question: The capacity of sentient beings and the response of the sage — are they one, or are they different? If one, then there would be no capacity and response at all; if different, how could they possibly hinge upon one another, such that capacity and response could even be discussed?

Answer: Neither one nor different. Discussed in terms of principle, they are one and the same suchness — hence, not different. Discussed in terms of the phenomenal, there is capacity and there is response — hence, not one. It is comparable to father and son, related by their very nature, sharing the same flesh and bone — being different is impossible; yet, were they the same, the father would be the son and the son the father — being the same, too, is impossible; it is simply that father and son are discussed as neither one nor different. Sentient beings' own principle-nature is no different from the Buddha's — hence, not different; yet sentient beings remain hidden while the Tathāgata stands revealed — hence, not one — capacity and response are discussed as neither one nor different in just this way. Again, both being neither phenomenal nor principle, they are not different; sentient beings attain the phenomenal while the sage attains principle; and, again, the sage attains the phenomenal while the ordinary being possesses principle — hence, they are discussed as different, and so on.

問：
為用法身應？為用應身應？若應身應，應身無本何能應？若用法身應，應則非法。

答：
至論諸法非去來今，非應非不應而能有應；亦可言法應，亦可言應應。法應則冥益，應應則顯益。分別冥顯有四義。如後說(云云)。

Question: Is it the Dharma-body that responds, or is it the response-body that responds? If it is the response-body that responds, then, the response-body having no root of its own, how could it possibly respond? If it is the Dharma-body that responds, then this response would no longer be dharma-like at all.

Answer: In the ultimate discussion, no dharma is past, future, or present, neither response nor non-response, and yet is able to have a response; one may equally say that it is the Dharma responding, or equally say that it is the response-body responding. The Dharma responding is a hidden benefit; the response-body responding is a manifest benefit. Distinguishing the hidden from the manifest carries four further senses, as explained below, and so on.

Reading note

Zhiyi opens the wondrous of sympathetic response — the first of the ten wondrous aspects concerned with what is accomplished for others rather than for oneself — by grounding "capacity" and "response" in ordinary usage before turning technical: capacity as the crossbow's own trigger (barely perceptible, yet decisive), as a hinge (linking sentient beings' own good and evil to the sage's compassion), and as fittingness (which dharma is called for). The four-siddhānta pass then reads these same three senses as the four siddhāntas in miniature — a move this treatise has now made repeatedly for other threefold or twofold schemes, confirming the siddhāntas as this text's most flexible analytic tool. The closing Q&A's "neither one nor different" resolution of the capacity/response relationship reuses the father-and-son image directly, and previews four still-undefined senses of "hidden" and "manifest" benefit that the fascicle will need before it can answer whether it is the Dharma-body or the response-body that actually responds.

Clarifying the Mark 明相

Chinese Source

第二明機應相者，約善惡明機相，約慈悲論應相。若善惡為機，為單？為共？解者不同。或言單惡為機，引經云：「我為斷一切眾生瘡疣重病。」又云：「如有七子，然於病者心則偏重，如來亦爾，於諸眾生非不平等，然於罪者心則偏重。」又云：「如來不為無為眾生而住於世。」又無記是無明，終屬惡攝，此即單以惡為機。或單以善為機，引《大經》云：「我觀眾生，不觀老少中年貧富貴賤，有善心者即便慈念。」此則單善為機。或云，善惡不得獨為機。何者？如金剛後心即是佛，眾善普會善無過此，此何得為機耶？雖云佛佛相念，此是通語，而無拔無與，故知單善不得為機。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the mark of capacity and response: capacity's own mark is clarified in terms of good and evil, while response's own mark is discussed in terms of loving-kindness and compassion. As to whether good and evil, taken as capacity, are single or joint — interpreters differ. Some say that evil alone is the capacity, citing the sūtra: "I am for the sake of severing the grave illness of every sentient being's own festering wound." And again: "It is like a father with seven children — yet his mind grows especially heavy toward whichever child is sick; the Tathāgata, too, is exactly the same — toward every sentient being, he is not without equal regard, and yet his mind grows especially heavy toward the guilty one." And again: "The Tathāgata does not abide in the world for the sake of those without any deed left undone." Again, the indeterminate is itself ignorance, and, in the end, belongs to the category of evil — this is precisely taking evil alone as the capacity. Others take good alone as the capacity, citing the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra: "I regard sentient beings without regard to old or young, middle-aged, poor or wealthy, humble or noble — whoever possesses a wholesome mind, I am accordingly moved with loving-kindness toward them." This takes good alone as the capacity. Others say that neither good nor evil, taken alone, can serve as the capacity. Why is this? As the final thought-moment of the vajra-like concentration is itself already buddhahood, every wholesome quality gathered together in full, with no goodness surpassing this — how, then, could this possibly serve as a capacity? Though it is said that buddha reflects upon buddha, this is a general statement, and carries neither a suffering to be drawn out nor a bliss to be bestowed — one should therefore know that good alone cannot serve as the capacity.

單惡不得為機者，如闡提極惡不能感佛，《大經》云：「唯有一髮不能升身」，即是性德理善。此是通機終不成感也。或取善惡相帶為機者，從闡提起改悔心，上至等覺，皆有善惡相帶，故得為機。

是故約此善惡明其相也(云云)。

Nor can evil alone serve as the capacity: as the icchantika, given over to the most extreme evil, is unable to move a buddha to response — the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says, "Even a single hair alone cannot lift the whole body," which is precisely the inherent virtue of goodness in principle. This is a capacity in the general sense only, and, in the end, cannot bring about any actual response. Others take good and evil bound together as the capacity: from the icchantika's very first arising of a mind of repentance, all the way up through equal enlightenment, good and evil are, throughout, bound together — hence, this alone is able to serve as the capacity.

The mark of capacity is hence clarified in terms of good and evil in just this way, and so on.

次約慈悲以明應相者，或單以慈為應，經云：「慈善根力象見師子，廣說如涅槃(云云)。」或單以悲為應，如《請觀音》，或遊戲地獄大悲代受苦；或合用慈悲為應。何者？良以悲心熏於智慧能拔他苦，慈心熏於禪定能與他樂。下文云：「定慧力莊嚴，以此度眾生。」論云：「水銀和真金，能塗諸色像。」功德和法身，處處應現往，豈是水銀真金單能塗色像耶？當知慈悲合論應也。

Next, clarifying the mark of response in terms of loving-kindness and compassion: some take loving-kindness alone as the response — the sūtra says, "The power of loving-kindness's own wholesome root causes an elephant to appear as a lion," discussed at length as in the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, and so on. Some take compassion alone as the response, as in the Sūtra on Invoking Avalokiteśvara, where great compassion sports even within the hells, suffering in another's place. Others take loving-kindness and compassion jointly as the response. Why is this? It is precisely because a mind of compassion, perfuming wisdom, is able to draw out another's suffering, and a mind of loving-kindness, perfuming concentration, is able to bestow bliss upon another. The text below says: "Adorned with the power of concentration and wisdom, using this to deliver sentient beings." A treatise says: "Mercury blended with true gold is able to coat every kind of image" — merit blended with the Dharma-body responds and manifests everywhere — how, then, could mercury or true gold, each alone, possibly coat an image? One should therefore know that loving-kindness and compassion are discussed jointly as the response.

問：
眾生善惡有三世，何世為機？聖法亦有三世，何世為應？過去已謝，現在不住，未來未至，悉不得為機，亦不得為應，云何論機應耶？

答：
若就至理窮覈三世，皆不可得，故無機亦無應。故經言：「非謂菩提有去、來、今，但以世俗文字數故。」說有三世，以四悉檀力隨順眾生說。或用過去善為機，故言：「我等宿福慶，今得值世尊。」又如五方便人，過去習方便者，發真則易，不習則難，是故以過去善為機。或可以現在善為機，故言：「即生此念時，佛於空中現。」或可以未來善為機，未生善法為令生故，又如無漏無習因而能感佛也，故《大論》云：「譬如蓮華在水，有已生始生未生者，若不得日光，翳死不疑；眾生三世善，若不值佛，無由得成(云云)。」惡亦如是，或以過去之罪，今悉懺悔；現造眾惡，今亦懺悔。未來之罪，斷相續心，遮未來故，名之為救。

Question: Sentient beings' own good and evil span the three time periods — which period, then, constitutes the capacity? The sage's own dharma likewise spans the three time periods — which period, then, constitutes the response? The past has already passed away, the present does not abide, and the future has not yet arrived — none of them, then, can serve as capacity, nor can any serve as response — how, then, could capacity and response even be discussed at all?

Answer: If, in terms of the ultimate principle, the three time periods are thoroughly examined, none of them can be found at all — hence, there is neither capacity nor response. Hence the sūtra says: "It is not that bodhi possesses a past, a future, and a present — this is spoken of merely by means of the conventional written word and its enumeration." Speaking of the existence of the three time periods is done by the power of the four siddhāntas, accommodating and according with sentient beings. Some take past goodness as the capacity, hence it is said: "Through the blessing of our own past merit, we now come to encounter the World-Honored One." Again, as with the five kinds of expedient practitioners: those who have habituated themselves to the expedient means in the past find giving rise to the real easy, while those who have not find it difficult — hence, past goodness is taken as the capacity. Others may take present goodness as the capacity, hence it is said: "At the very moment this thought arose, the Buddha manifested within the sky." Others may take future goodness as the capacity, since the not-yet-arisen wholesome dharma is thereby caused to arise — and, again, as the taintless itself, without any habituated cause, is nonetheless able to move a buddha to response. Hence the Treatise says: "It is like a lotus flower within the water — some already blossomed, some just beginning to blossom, some not yet blossomed — without the sunlight, they would, without doubt, wither and die; sentient beings' own goodness across the three time periods, without encountering a buddha, has no way to be brought to completion, and so on." Evil is exactly the same: some, with respect to past transgressions, now repent of them fully; some, presently committing every kind of evil, likewise now repent of it. With respect to future transgressions, severing the mind's own continuity, blocking the future itself — this is named "rescue."

何者？過去造惡，障現善不得起，為除此惡，是故請佛。又現在果苦報逼迫，眾生而求救護。又未來之惡，與時相值，遮令不起，故通用三世惡為機。應亦如是，或用過去慈悲為應，故云：「我本立誓願，欲令得此法。」或用現在慈悲為應者，一切天人脩羅，皆應至此，為聽法故，未度令度也；又用未來為應者，即是〈壽量〉中，未來世益物也。亦如〈安樂行品〉中云：「我得三菩提時，引之令得，住是法中。」若通論，三世善惡皆為機；別論，但取未來善惡為正機也。何者？過去已謝，現在已定，秖為拔未來惡，生未來善耳。

Why is this? Evil committed in the past obstructs present goodness from being able to arise — for the sake of removing this evil, the Buddha is accordingly entreated. Again, the fruit of present suffering, its retribution pressing hard, causes sentient beings to seek rescue and protection. Again, future evil, once it meets its due time, is blocked from arising at all — hence, the evil of all three time periods is taken together as the capacity. Response is exactly the same: some take past loving-kindness and compassion as the response, hence it is said, "Long ago, I established this very vow, wishing to cause them to attain this dharma." Some take present loving-kindness and compassion as the response: every god, human, and asura alike ought to arrive here, for the sake of hearing the dharma, so that whoever is not yet delivered may be delivered. Again, some take the future as the response — this is precisely the "Life Span" chapter's own benefiting of beings in future ages. This, too, is as the "Peaceful Practices" chapter says: "When I attain the threefold bodhi, I shall draw them onward, causing them to attain and abide within this very dharma." Discussed in general, the goodness and evil of all three time periods alike serve as the capacity; discussed in particular, only future goodness and evil are taken as the true capacity. Why is this? The past has already passed away, and the present is already fixed — it is precisely for the sake of drawing out future evil and giving rise to future goodness alone.

問：
若未來為正機者，四正勤意云何？

答：
此已屬通意！今更別答者，秖為過去惡遮未來善，故勤斷過去惡；秖為過去善不增長，增長者即是未來善也。是故四正勤中，言雖過去，意實未來(云云)。

問：
未來未有，佛云何照？

答：
如來智鑒，能如是知，非下地所知。仰信而已，何可分別！

Question: If the future alone constitutes the true capacity, then what of the sense of the four right exertions?

Answer: This already belongs to the general sense! Answering now in the particular sense: it is precisely because past evil blocks future goodness that one exerts oneself in severing past evil; it is precisely because past goodness has not yet increased and grown that one exerts oneself, and this very increase and growth is itself future goodness. Hence, within the four right exertions, though the words speak of the past, the sense intended is truly the future, and so on.

Question: The future does not yet exist — how, then, does the Buddha perceive it?

Answer: The Tathāgata's own wisdom and perception are able to know it in just this way — this is not something the lower stages can know. One can only look up to it and place one's faith in it — how could it possibly be further distinguished and analyzed!

問：
為是眾生自能感？由佛故感？如來自能應？由眾生故應？

答：
此應作四句：自、他、共、無因，破是性義悉不可。無此四句故，則無性。無性故，但以世間名字，四悉檀中而論感應能所等。而能應屬佛，所應屬眾生；能感屬眾生，所感屬佛。若更翻疊作諸語言，世諦名字則亂，不可分別。雖作如此名字，是字不住，是字無所有故，如夢如幻(云云)。

Question: Is it that sentient beings, of themselves, are able to move the Buddha to respond? Or is it because of the Buddha that they are so moved? Is it that the Tathāgata, of himself, is able to respond? Or is it because of sentient beings that he responds?

Answer: This ought to be treated as a tetralemma: self, other, both together, without any cause — refuting each of these as possessing any fixed nature is, in every case, impossible to sustain. Since none of these four propositions can be sustained, there is accordingly no fixed nature here at all. Since there is no fixed nature, it is simply by means of worldly names, within the four siddhāntas, that being-moved-to-respond and its own agent and object are discussed. The agent of response belongs to the Buddha; the object responded to belongs to sentient beings; the agent of moving-to-response belongs to sentient beings; the object moved-to-response belongs to the Buddha. If one turns these terms over yet again into further layers of language, the conventional truth's own names grow confused, and can no longer be distinguished at all. Though names of this kind are used, these very names do not abide, and these very names possess nothing of their own — like a dream, like an illusion, and so on.

問：
既善惡俱為機者，誰無善惡？此皆應得益耶？

答：
如世病者延醫，而有差不差。機亦如是，有熟不熟，則應有遠有近。

Question: Since good and evil, taken together, both alike constitute the capacity, who, then, is entirely without good or evil? Does this mean everyone, without exception, ought to obtain this benefit?

Answer: It is like a sick person in the world summoning a physician — some are cured, and some are not. Capacity is exactly the same: it is either ripened or unripened, and, accordingly, the response is either near at hand or far off.

Reading note

Zhiyi resolves the single/joint dispute over capacity and response the way he resolves most such disputes in this treatise: not by choosing a side, but by showing each one-sided position breaks down under its own logic (an already-perfected mind carries no more suffering to draw out; an icchantika's evil, taken alone, is "a single hair" too weak to move a buddha at all), leaving good-and-evil-bound-together as the only workable account. The tetralemma applied to whether sentient beings or the Buddha is the true agent of sympathetic response is a compact instance of this treatise's standard four-cornered refutation (self/other/both/neither), concluding that "capacity" and "response" are conventional names only, "like a dream, like an illusion" — a formula that will recur through the rest of this wondrous aspect. The extended discussion of which of the three time periods constitutes the "true" capacity closes on a practical point: since the past is already fixed and the present already determined, only future good and evil remain genuinely open to being drawn out or given rise to, which is why the four right exertions, though phrased in terms of severing what has already arisen, are read as aimed at the future throughout.

Clarifying Sameness and Difference 明同異

Chinese Source

三、明機感不同者，即為三意：一、就四句論不同。二、就三十六句論不同。三、就十法界論不同。但眾生根性百千，諸佛巧應無量，隨其種種得度不同，故文云：「名色各異，種類若干，如上中下根莖葉等，隨其種性，各得生長。」即是機、應不同意也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the non-sameness of capacity and being-moved-to-respond: this is treated under three headings — first, discussed in terms of a tetralemma; second, discussed in terms of thirty-six propositions; third, discussed in terms of the ten dharma-realms. Sentient beings' own faculties and natures number in the hundreds and thousands, and the buddhas' own skillful responses are without measure, according with each one's differing manner of being delivered — hence the text says: "Each differing in name and form, of however many kinds, like the roots, stems, and leaves of the superior, middling, and inferior plants, each according to its own kind and nature, grows accordingly" — this is precisely the sense of capacity and response not being all the same.

今略言為四：一者、冥機冥應。二者、冥機顯應。三者、顯機顯應。四者、顯機冥應。其相云何？若過去善修三業，現在未運身口，藉往善力名此為冥機也。雖不現見靈應，而密為法身所益。不見不聞而覺而知，是名為冥益也。二冥機顯益者，過去殖善而冥機已成，便得值佛聞法，現前獲利是為顯益。如佛初出世，最初得度之人，現在何甞修行？諸佛照其宿機，自往度之，即其義也。三、顯機顯應者，現在身口，精勤不懈，而能感降。如須達長跪，佛往祇洹，月蓋曲躬，聖居門閫。如即行人，道場禮懺，能感靈瑞，即是顯機顯應也。四者、顯機冥應者，如人雖一世勤苦，現善濃積而不顯感，冥有其利，此是顯機冥益。

Here, this is stated briefly as four kinds: first, hidden capacity with hidden response; second, hidden capacity with manifest response; third, manifest capacity with manifest response; fourth, manifest capacity with hidden response. What are their marks? If, in the past, the three karmic actions were wholesomely cultivated, and, in the present, body and speech have not yet been set in motion, relying on the power of that past goodness — this is named hidden capacity. Though no numinous response is presently seen, one is secretly benefited by the Dharma-body nonetheless. Neither seen nor heard, and yet perceived and known — this is named hidden benefit. Second, hidden capacity with manifest benefit: goodness planted in the past, the hidden capacity already accomplished, one then comes to encounter a buddha and hear the dharma, obtaining benefit here and now — this is manifest benefit. As with the very first people delivered when a buddha first appears in the world — what cultivation, after all, had they undertaken in the present? The buddhas, perceiving their own capacity accumulated of old, go themselves to deliver them — this is precisely this same sense. Third, manifest capacity with manifest response: body and speech in the present, diligent and unflagging, are able to move a response down. As with Sudatta kneeling at length, and the Buddha going to Jetavana; or Candracchattra bowing low, and the sage taking up residence at his very gate — comparably, the practitioner bowing and repenting at the place of practice, able to move a numinous sign — this is precisely manifest capacity with manifest response. Fourth, manifest capacity with hidden response: as when a person, though laboring diligently for a single lifetime, present goodness accumulating thick and deep, and yet no manifest response occurs — the benefit is hidden nonetheless — this is manifest capacity with hidden benefit.

若解四意，一切低頭舉手，福不虛棄，終日無感，終日無悔。若見喜殺壽長，好施貧乏，不生邪見；若不解此者，謂其徒功喪計，憂悔失理。《釋論》云：「今我疾苦，皆由過去；今生修福，報在將來，正念無僻。」得此四意也。

If these four senses are understood, then every bow of the head and every raising of the hand — no merit is ever wasted, and, though a whole day passes with no response perceived, one need never regret it for a whole day either. Seeing one who delights in killing yet lives long, or one fond of giving to the poor yet destitute, no distorted view need arise; if one fails to understand this, one supposes one's own effort squandered and one's calculations lost, grieving and regretting for no true reason. The Treatise says: "My present illness and suffering are entirely due to the past; the merit cultivated in this present life shall bear its fruit in the future — correct mindfulness is thereby free of any distorted view." This is precisely grasping these same four senses.

二、就三十六句論機應不同者，前冥顯互論略舉四句。若具足辨者，用四機為根本，所謂冥機、顯機、亦冥亦顯機、非冥非顯機。

Second, clarifying the non-sameness of capacity and response in terms of thirty-six propositions: the previous discussion of hidden and manifest, taken together, gave only the broad outline of four propositions. If distinguished in full: taking the four kinds of capacity as the root — namely, hidden capacity, manifest capacity, both hidden and manifest capacity, and neither hidden nor manifest capacity —

冥是過去，顯是現在，冥顯是過現，非冥非顯是未來。如佛為闡提說法(云云)。於一句中，復為四句：所謂冥機冥應、冥機顯應、冥機亦冥亦顯應、冥機非冥非顯應；餘三機亦如是，四四即成十六句。機既召應，應亦有十六句。一機而感四應，一應而赴四機，機應各為十六，合成三十二句。就前根本四句，便是三十六句機應也。

hidden is the past, manifest is the present, both hidden and manifest is past-and-present together, and neither hidden nor manifest is the future — as when the Buddha teaches the icchantika, and so on. Within each single proposition, there are, again, four further propositions — namely, hidden capacity with hidden response, hidden capacity with manifest response, hidden capacity with both hidden and manifest response, hidden capacity with neither hidden nor manifest response; the remaining three kinds of capacity are exactly the same, and four times four accordingly makes sixteen propositions. Since capacity summons a response, response, too, has its own sixteen propositions. A single capacity may move four kinds of response, and a single response may hasten toward four kinds of capacity — capacity and response, each having sixteen, together make thirty-two propositions. Adding the four root propositions given before, this accordingly makes thirty-six propositions of capacity and response altogether.

三、就十法界論機應不同者，秖約一人身業機具三十六，約三業即有一百八機。約三世、三業，則有三百二十四。一界既爾，十法界即有三千二百四十機應不同。就自行既爾，化他亦然，合則六千四百八十機應，此就歷別十法界。如此，若就十法界交互則增九倍，都六萬四千八百機應也。

Third, clarifying the non-sameness of capacity and response in terms of the ten dharma-realms: simply in terms of a single person's own bodily karma alone, the capacity already comes to thirty-six; in terms of the three kinds of karma, this makes one hundred and eight kinds of capacity. In terms of the three time periods and the three kinds of karma together, this makes three hundred and twenty-four. If this is so for a single realm alone, then the ten dharma-realms together make three thousand two hundred and forty non-identical kinds of capacity and response. As this holds for one's own practice, so, too, it holds for the transformation of others — combined together, this makes six thousand four hundred and eighty kinds of capacity and response — this discussed in terms of the ten dharma-realms taken separately. If, further, the ten dharma-realms are discussed in their mutual interpenetration with one another, this increases ninefold again, coming to a total of sixty-four thousand eight hundred kinds of capacity and response altogether.

Reading note

The four-way hidden/manifest scheme answers a pastoral worry as much as a doctrinal one: since most practitioners never see any visible sign of a response to their own practice, the "hidden capacity, hidden response" category (illustrated by ice being water rather than by any dramatic vision) guarantees that "no merit is ever wasted," countering the specific anxiety that a killer's long life or a generous person's poverty proves karma doesn't work — the response is real, only hidden. The thirty-six-proposition and ten-dharma-realm expansions that follow are Zhiyi's characteristic combinatorial method, generating a number (sixty-four thousand eight hundred) that functions less as an exact count than as a demonstration that the four-fold hidden/manifest scheme, iterated through the three time periods, the three karmic actions, and the mutual interpenetration of the ten dharma-realms, is exhaustive of every possible configuration.

Clarifying Mutual Correspondence 明相對

Chinese Source

四、明機應相對者，即有四意：一、明諸有苦樂與三昧慈悲相對。

二者、機關等相對。三者、三十六句相對。四者、別圓相對。

諸三昧相對者，諸機乃多不出二十五有；諸應乃多不出二十五三昧。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, clarifying the mutual correspondence of capacity and response: this carries four senses — first, clarifying the mutual correspondence between the suffering and bliss of every state of existence and the samādhis of loving-kindness and compassion.

Second, the mutual correspondence of the hinge and the rest. Third, the mutual correspondence of the thirty-six propositions. Fourth, the mutual correspondence of the distinct and the perfect.

As for the mutual correspondence of the samādhis: every capacity, numerous as they are, never exceeds the twenty-five states of existence; every response, numerous as they are, never exceeds the twenty-five samādhis.

地獄有，有善惡之機，關無垢三昧慈悲之應。論其惡者，即有黑業、惡見、思惡、塵沙惡、無明惡；論善，則有白業善，即空善、即假善、即中善，是名地獄機也。無垢三昧慈悲為應者，初修無垢三昧，觀地獄界。因緣觀慈悲，即空觀慈悲，即假觀慈悲，即中觀慈悲。以因緣觀時，悲拔地獄黑業苦。以因緣觀時，慈與白業樂。

The state of existence that is hell possesses both a good and an evil capacity, hinging upon the response of the stainless samādhi's own loving-kindness and compassion. Discussing its evil: there is the evil of dark karma, the evil of wrong views, the evil of thought, the evil of dust-and-sand, and the evil of ignorance. Discussing its good: there is the good of pale karma — namely, the good of emptiness, the good of the provisional, and the good of the middle — this is named the capacity belonging to hell. As for the stainless samādhi's own loving-kindness and compassion serving as the response: one first cultivates the stainless samādhi, contemplating the realm of hell — the compassion of the contemplation of dependent origination, the compassion of the contemplation of emptiness, the compassion of the contemplation of the provisional, the compassion of the contemplation of the middle. During the contemplation of dependent origination, compassion draws out the suffering of hell's own dark karma; during the contemplation of dependent origination, loving-kindness bestows the bliss of pale karma.

以即空觀時，悲拔見思苦；以即空觀時，慈與無漏樂。以即假觀時，悲拔塵沙苦；以即假觀時，慈與道種智樂。以即中觀時，悲拔無明苦；以即中觀時，慈與法性樂。是為地獄有善、惡之機，以關無垢三昧慈悲之應。拔苦與樂，相對義也。

During the contemplation of emptiness, compassion draws out the suffering of views and thought; during the contemplation of emptiness, loving-kindness bestows the bliss of the taintless. During the contemplation of the provisional, compassion draws out the suffering of dust-and-sand; during the contemplation of the provisional, loving-kindness bestows the bliss of the wisdom of the paths. During the contemplation of the middle, compassion draws out the suffering of ignorance; during the contemplation of the middle, loving-kindness bestows the bliss of the dharma-nature. This, then, is hell's own good and evil capacity, hinging upon the response of the stainless samādhi's own loving-kindness and compassion — the sense of their mutual correspondence in drawing out suffering and bestowing bliss.

二者、機關相對者，地獄界中黑業之惡，有微義、有關義、有宜義。如此三機，即關無垢三昧時，慈悲有赴義、有對義、有應義。

地獄白業，亦有六義相對，即空、見思、塵沙、無明等。善惡皆具六義相對(云云)。

Second, as for the mutual correspondence of the hinge, and the rest: the evil of dark karma within the realm of hell carries the sense of the subtle, the sense of the hinge, and the sense of what is fitting. Corresponding to these same three aspects of capacity, at the very moment of hinging upon the stainless samādhi, loving-kindness and compassion carry the sense of hastening toward, the sense of correspondence, and the sense of responding.

Hell's own pale karma likewise carries these same six senses in mutual correspondence, matched to emptiness, to views and thought, to dust-and-sand, to ignorance, and the rest. Both good and evil are alike complete with these six senses in mutual correspondence, and so on.

三者、三十六句相對者，地獄黑白業具有冥機冥應、冥機顯應、顯機顯應、顯機冥應，即關無垢三昧慈悲，冥顯四應，赴於地獄。見思、即空，塵沙、種智，無明、中道等，皆具四機、四應相對(云云)。又地獄有冥顯三十六機，即對無垢三昧三十六應(云云)。

Third, as for the mutual correspondence of the thirty-six propositions: hell's own dark and pale karma are each complete with hidden capacity/hidden response, hidden capacity/manifest response, manifest capacity/manifest response, and manifest capacity/hidden response — hinging upon the stainless samādhi's own loving-kindness and compassion, hidden and manifest in four kinds of response, hastening toward hell. Views-and-thought matched to emptiness, dust-and-sand matched to the wisdom of the paths, ignorance matched to the middle way, and the rest — each is alike complete with these four kinds of capacity and four kinds of response in mutual correspondence, and so on. Again, hell possesses thirty-six kinds of hidden-and-manifest capacity, matched precisely to the stainless samādhi's own thirty-six kinds of response, and so on.

四者、別圓相對者，若地獄有歷別之機，三昧應即歷別。若有圓普之機，三昧應亦圓普。若歷別機起，三昧別應，一有業謝，餘有業未必謝；三惡思盡，餘有思未必盡；地獄道種智明，餘有未必明；地獄佛性了了，餘有未必了了。若作圓機、圓應者。地獄自在業未究竟，餘有亦未究竟；一有見思未盡，餘有亦未盡；一有道種未明，餘有亦未明；一有佛性未了了，餘有亦未了了。一有了了，餘有亦了了；乃至一有業自在，餘有業亦自在。分別地獄機、應相對如上說。餘二十四有機、應相對，例亦如是。

Fourth, as for the mutual correspondence of the distinct and the perfect: if hell possesses a capacity that is sequentially distinct, the samādhi's own response is likewise sequentially distinct. If it possesses a capacity that is perfectly universal, the samādhi's own response is likewise perfectly universal. If a sequentially distinct capacity arises, the samādhi responds distinctly: once one state of existence's karma is set aside, the karma of the remaining states is not thereby necessarily set aside as well; once the three evil states' own thought is exhausted, the thought of the remaining states is not thereby necessarily exhausted as well; once hell's own wisdom of the paths becomes clear, the remaining states' own is not thereby necessarily clear as well; once hell's own buddha-nature is fully and clearly seen, the remaining states' own is not thereby necessarily fully and clearly seen as well. If, however, this is made a perfect capacity and a perfect response: hell's own sovereign freedom over karma not yet ultimate, the remaining states' own is likewise not yet ultimate; one state's own views and thought not yet exhausted, the remaining states' own are likewise not yet exhausted; one state's own wisdom of the paths not yet clear, the remaining states' own are likewise not yet clear; one state's own buddha-nature not yet fully and clearly seen, the remaining states' own are likewise not yet fully and clearly seen; and, once one state's own is fully and clearly seen, the remaining states' own are likewise fully and clearly seen — down through: once one state's own sovereign freedom over karma is attained, the remaining states' own karma likewise attains this same sovereign freedom. The mutual correspondence of hell's own capacity and response, thus distinguished, is exactly as explained above; the mutual correspondence of capacity and response for the remaining twenty-four states of existence follows this same pattern.

問：
頗有善機惡應、惡機善應，偏機圓應、圓機偏應不？

答：
無方適時，亦有此義。《淨名》云：「或時現風火，照令知無常。」即惡應於善也。妙莊嚴信受邪惡，三菩薩應為妻子，即善應於惡。圓機偏應者，一切智願猶在不失，不失即圓機；教聲聞法，即偏應也。偏機圓應者，先引三車，後與一大。領解云：「無上寶聚，不求自得。」即其義也。拔樂與苦，例此可知(云云)。

Question: Is there ever a good capacity met with an evil response, or an evil capacity met with a good response? A one-sided capacity met with a perfect response, or a perfect capacity met with a one-sided response?

Answer: Adapting without any fixed method to the occasion at hand, this sense, too, is present. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "At times, wind and fire are manifested, illuminating things so as to make impermanence known" — this is precisely an evil response met to a good capacity. King Shubhavyuha, given over in faith to wrong and evil views, his own queen and two sons — three bodhisattvas — responding by becoming his own wife and children — this is precisely a good response met to an evil capacity. A perfect capacity met with a one-sided response: the vow of omniscience remains present, never lost — this never-lost quality is itself the perfect capacity — yet he was taught the śrāvaka's own dharma, which is precisely a one-sided response. A one-sided capacity met with a perfect response: first the three carriages were held out, and only afterward the single great one bestowed — the disciples' own understanding, once received, says, "This unsurpassed heap of treasure, sought for by none of us, has nonetheless been obtained of itself" — this is precisely this same sense. Drawing out suffering and bestowing bliss may be understood by this same pattern, and so on.

Reading note

Having established, in the previous section, that capacity and response come in thirty-six varieties across ten dharma-realms, this section works through hell as a single worked example, correlating its own capacity (dark karma matched to views-and-thought, dust-and-sand, and ignorance; pale karma matched to emptiness, the provisional, and the middle) against the stainless samādhi's fourfold contemplation, each pairing drawing out one specific suffering and bestowing one specific bliss. The closing distinct/perfect correlation makes a point already familiar from this treatise's stage discussions: a sequentially distinct response fixes each state of existence's own liberation apart from the rest, while a perfect response ties them together so tightly that one realm's own buddha-nature being "fully and clearly seen" entails the same for every other realm at once — mutual interpenetration read as a consequence of how a genuinely wondrous response operates, not merely as a cosmological curiosity. The closing Q&A's examples of "mismatched" capacity and response — wind and fire manifested to a good capacity, King Shubhavyuha's own family converting him through wrongdoing met with goodness — show that even apparent mismatches resolve, on inspection, into skillful means suited to the deeper capacity beneath the surface one.

Clarifying the Coarse and the Wondrous 明麁妙

第五明麁妙者，即為三意：一、明機之麁妙。二、明應之麁妙。

三、明開麁顯妙。

一、機麁妙者，如樂間地獄，此樂因微善故。《立世毘曇》云：「人養六畜，飲飴溫清者，在熱地獄得冷間，寒地獄得溫間。」若從此義，樂間得論十法界機。阿鼻無樂間則無事善，云何具十？然阿鼻有性善不斷故。又近世雖無事善，遠劫或有，惡強善弱，冥伏未發，若遇因緣，發亦何定。是故阿鼻得具十機。即判麁妙，九界機為麁，佛界機為妙。麁機召方便應，此機有熟、未熟；方便應有淺、有深。機熟者，被應；未熟者，未應。應淺深者，如從無間得之於間，出地獄至畜生，出畜生至鬼，出三惡至人天，出人天至二乘等，悉是機之生、熟，應之淺、深，悉屬麁機攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous: this carries three senses — first, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous of capacity; second, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous of response;

third, clarifying opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous.

First, as for the coarse and the wondrous of capacity: as with the pleasurable respite within hell — this pleasure arises from a subtle wholesome cause. The Treatise on the Establishment of the World says: "A person who raises the six domestic animals, feeding and watering them, warming and cooling them as needed, obtains, within the hot hells, a respite of coolness, and, within the cold hells, a respite of warmth." Following this same sense, this pleasurable respite may be used to discuss the capacity present within all ten dharma-realms. Avīci, having no pleasurable respite, would seem to have no phenomenal goodness at all — how, then, could it possess all ten? And yet Avīci does possess an unbroken goodness of nature. And, though there is no phenomenal goodness to be found in recent time, there may be some present from a distant eon — evil being strong and goodness weak, lying hidden and dormant, not yet manifest — yet, should it meet the right condition, when it manifests is hardly fixed. Hence, Avīci, too, is able to possess all ten kinds of capacity. Judging the coarse and the wondrous accordingly: the capacity of the nine realms is coarse, while the capacity of the buddha-realm is wondrous. The coarse capacity summons an expedient response — this capacity is either ripened or unripened, and the expedient response is either shallow or deep. When capacity is ripened, it is met with a response; when unripened, it is not yet met with one. As for the response being shallow or deep: as when one is drawn from the uninterrupted hell to a hell with respite, or emerges from hell into the animal realm, or emerges from the animal realm into the realm of hungry ghosts, or emerges from the three evil destinies into the human and heavenly realms, or emerges from the human and heavenly realms into the two vehicles, and so forth — all of this is precisely the capacity's own arising and ripening, and the response's own shallowness and depth, all belonging to the coarse capacity.

妙機召究竟妙應，妙機亦有生、熟；妙應亦有淺、深。如慈童女在於地獄，代人受罪即得生天，此乃妙機淺、熟，近在乎天耳。其餘例可知。

The wondrous capacity summons the ultimate, wondrous response; the wondrous capacity, too, has its own arising and ripening, and the wondrous response, too, has its own shallowness and depth. As with the compassionate youth Maitrakanyaka, who, within hell, took another's punishment upon himself and thereby immediately attained rebirth in heaven — this is precisely a wondrous capacity, shallow yet already ripened, its response near at hand, in heaven itself. The remaining cases may be understood by this same pattern.

二明應有麁妙者，聖人慈悲誓願，願持於行，如物有膠，任運與機相著。故慈善根力，手出師子。若無誓願，雖觀苦、樂，不能拔、與。以慈力故，隨機麁、妙，先熟先應，後熟後應。三藏、通教等聖，亦得有應，但是作意神通，譬如圖寫，經紀乃成，覈論無本。

Second, as for clarifying the coarse and the wondrous of response: the sage's own compassion and the vow sustaining it, the vow upholding the practice, is like glue on an object — spontaneously adhering to whatever capacity it meets. Hence the power of loving-kindness's own wholesome root causes a lion to emerge from one's own hand. Without the vow, though one contemplates suffering and bliss, one would be unable to draw out or bestow either. It is by the power of loving-kindness that, according with the capacity, whether coarse or wondrous, whichever ripens first is responded to first, and whichever ripens later is responded to later. The sages of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings, too, are able to possess a response, but this is merely deliberate spiritual power — comparable to a painted portrait, which, however carefully composed, in the end has no genuine basis in reality.

何者？灰身滅智，無常住本，約何起應？若別接通，別惑未斷，亦不得應，縱令赴物，皆名麁應也。若別、圓兩教，初心伏惑，未能有應。初地、初住三觀現前，證二十五三昧。法身清淨，無染如虛空，湛然應一切，無思無念隨機即對。如一月不降，百水不升，而隨河短長，任器規矩，無前無後一時普現，此是不思議妙應也。又如明鏡，表裏清澈，一像千像，無所簡擇，不須功力，任運像似，是名妙應。此是相待論感應妙也。

Why is this? Ashes of the body and the extinguishing of cognition leave no eternally abiding root — on what basis, then, could a response even arise? If received from the distinct teaching into the shared, the distinct teaching's own confusion has not yet been severed, and a response, too, cannot yet be had — even should one hasten toward beings regardless, this, too, is entirely named a coarse response. As for the distinct and perfect teachings: at the initial mind, confusion is only subdued, and a response is not yet possible. Only once the initial ground or initial abiding is reached, the three contemplations manifest before one, and the twenty-five samādhis are realized. The Dharma-body, pure, untainted, like empty space, quiescent and yet illuminating, responds to everything, without deliberation and without conceptual thought, corresponding to whatever capacity is present at once. It is like a single moon that never descends, and a hundred bodies of water that never ascend to meet it, and yet, according with each river's own length, and fitting each vessel's own shape and measure, without before and without after, manifests everywhere all at once — this is precisely the inconceivable, wondrous response. Again, it is like a bright mirror, clear through and through, inside and out — a single image or a thousand images, with nothing selected or excluded, requiring no exertion of effort at all, spontaneously resembling whatever stands before it — this is named the wondrous response. This is the wondrous of sympathetic response discussed in the relative sense.

三開麁顯妙者：若九界機麁，一界機妙；未得法身應麁，得法身應妙者，諸大乘經《華嚴》等，明麁妙相隔，二乘不聞不解，如瘂如聾。《無量義經》明麁妙，從一理出生無量麁妙機應；一理為妙，生出無量為麁。此則從妙出麁，隔而未合。今經無量還為一，此則開權顯實，秖麁是妙。何者？本顯一理，作諸方便；方便即是真實。故云：凡有所作，唯為一事，未曾暫廢。譬如三草、二木，秖是一地所生，即是同源機一。一雨所潤，即是同受應一。愚者未解，謂草木四微，永非是地；智者了達四微生秖是地變，四微滅秖是地還，豈有草木而非於地耶！此即開權而顯實，決了聲聞法，是諸經之王。九法界機，皆佛界機；四聖之應，無非妙應也。

Third, as for opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous: if the capacity of the nine realms is coarse and the capacity of the one buddha-realm alone is wondrous; if the response of one not yet having attained the Dharma-body is coarse and the response of one who has attained it is wondrous — then, in every great Mahāyāna sūtra, the Avataṃsaka and the rest, the coarse and the wondrous are clarified as mutually set apart, and the two vehicles neither hear nor understand it, as if mute or deaf. The Sūtra of Innumerable Meanings clarifies the coarse and the wondrous as the immeasurable capacities and responses, coarse and wondrous, issuing forth from a single principle — the single principle being the wondrous, and what issues forth, immeasurable, being the coarse. This, then, is issuing the coarse from the wondrous, set apart from it and not yet joined to it. In the present sūtra, however, the immeasurable is returned once more to the one — this, then, is opening the provisional to reveal the real, so that the coarse itself is simply the wondrous. Why is this? What was originally manifesting the one principle produces every expedient means; and the expedient means is itself already the real. Hence it is said: whatever is undertaken is done for the sake of this one matter alone, never for even a moment set aside. It is comparable to the three kinds of grasses and the two kinds of trees — all alike growing from this same single ground — this is precisely their sharing one and the same source of capacity. Moistened by this same single rain — this is precisely their receiving one and the same response. The foolish, not yet understanding, suppose the grass and trees' own four subtle elements to be forever other than the ground itself; the wise fully comprehend that the arising of these four subtle elements is simply the ground's own transformation, and their ceasing is simply the ground's own return — how, then, could there possibly be any grass or tree that is not itself the very ground? This is precisely opening the provisional to reveal the real, fully resolving the śrāvaka's own dharma, this being the king of every sūtra. The capacity of the nine dharma-realms is, every one, already the capacity of the buddha-realm; the response of the four kinds of sages is, without exception, already the wondrous response.

Reading note

The "pleasurable respite" argument for why even Avīci — the most extreme hell, with no interruption in its suffering at all — must still possess all ten dharma-realms' worth of capacity is a small masterpiece of Zhiyi's combinatorial reasoning: since goodness need not be active in the present to count, evil merely "strong" and goodness merely "weak, lying hidden and dormant" is enough, and there is no way to rule it out for any being whatsoever. The moon-in-the-water and mirror similes for the wondrous response — reflecting in every river without descending, mirroring every image without selecting among them — are the same non-action-yet-perfectly-responsive imagery this treatise has used before for the perfect teaching's own effortless function, now applied specifically to why only the Dharma-body itself, and not the sages of the lesser teachings' own "deliberate spiritual power," can genuinely respond. The closing "opening the coarse" section reprises the three-grasses-and-two-trees image from the "Parable of the Medicinal Herbs" one last time, now applied specifically to capacity and response themselves: every dharma-realm's own capacity already shares the identical source, the way every plant shares the identical ground beneath it, so that the apparent hierarchy between coarse (nine-realm) and wondrous (buddha-realm) capacity dissolves once both are seen growing from, and returning to, the same single principle.

Seventh, clarifying the wondrous of spiritual powers: this carries four senses — first, clarifying its sequence; second, its names and number; third, sameness and difference; fourth, the coarse and the wondrous. As for why this comes next: the discussion of capacity and response given above merely distinguished the mark of what is able to arise and what is able to be hastened toward; the direct discussion of the transformative function that benefits others is precisely the three wheels of inconceivable transformation — namely, the wheel of the body, the wheel of speech, and the wheel of another's mind. The "Universal Gate" chapter contains only two passages of text, and yet, together, they yield all three senses. "Wandering throughout the sahā world" is precisely the wheel of the body; "and expounding the Dharma for them" is precisely the wheel of speech. As one sees a lotus flower's own great size and thereby knows the pool's own depth, so, too, seeing the greatness of the dharma expounded, one thereby knows the greatness of the wisdom behind it — hence, these two wheels together also show forth the wheel of another's mind. Moreover, in transforming others, the two wheels are shown most often, while the wheel of mind is shown least — following the majority, only these two are named, and hence the wheel of mind goes unmentioned. The sūtra says: "Whoever sees or hears him is, every one, thereby delivered." As for showing forth the wheel of the body: this is precisely showing the body of the king of medicine trees, the body of the king of wish-fulfilling jewels. As for showing forth the wheel of speech: this is precisely showing the poison drum and the heavenly drum. This is compassion perfuming body and speech, and hence there are these two bodies manifested and these two drums proclaimed. As for showing forth the wheel of mind: this is precisely showing according with one's own intent and according with another's intent, and the rest — this, too, is the same as the practice of the sick and the practice of the infant. The discussion above clarified how capacity and being-moved-to-respond hinge upon one another, and yet this wondrous principle is difficult to manifest — one must therefore rely upon spiritual power to set it into motion, manifesting auspicious signs, secretly signifying this same principle. Ordinary people in the world take a spider hanging down as a sign that joyful news is coming, and a magpie's cry as a sign that a traveler is arriving — if even such small things carry their own signs, how, then, could something as great as this be without any auspicious sign at all? Signifying the far-off by means of the near at hand ought likewise to be understood in just this way.

二、名數者，諸經所出，名數不同。今且依六種：謂天眼、天耳、他心、宿命、如意身通、無漏等也。此六皆稱神通者，如《瓔珞》云：「神名天心，通名慧性。」天心者，天然之心也。慧性者，通達無礙也。《毘曇》亦云障通，無知若去即發慧性也。當知天然慧性與六法相應，即能轉變自在，故名神通。《地持．力品》云：「神謂難測知；通謂無壅礙。」此解與《瓔珞》同。天心即是難測知義；慧性即是無壅礙義。然此六法修無前後、證無次第，用亦任時，故眾經列次不同也。《釋論》云：「幻術事是虛誑法，法於草木，誑惑人眼，物實不變。」神通不爾，實得變法，使物實變：如地有成水之理，水有成地之義。若金、銀得火則融，水遇寒則結；火、寒是融、結法。結則實結，融則實融；若得天然慧性，則實能如此變用自在。所變水、火，令他實得受用，而非其果報，但是神通一時所作耳。

Second, as for names and number: the various sūtras give differing names and numbers. Here, for now, six kinds are followed: namely, the deva-eye, the deva-ear, the knowledge of others' minds, the recollection of past lives, the power of miraculous transformation according to one's wish, and the taintless, and the rest. All six of these are named "spiritual powers" — as the Yingluo Sūtra says: "'Spiritual' is named the heavenly mind; 'power' is named the nature of wisdom." The heavenly mind is the mind that is spontaneously so of itself; the nature of wisdom is penetrating through without obstruction. The Abhidharma likewise says: once the obstruction to spiritual power, namely ignorance, is removed, the nature of wisdom is thereby brought forth. One should therefore know that the heavenly mind's own spontaneous nature of wisdom, corresponding to these six dharmas, is thereby able to transform freely at will — hence, this is named spiritual power. The Yogācārabhūmi's own "Chapter on Powers" says: "'Spiritual' means what is difficult to fathom and know; 'power' means what is free of any hindrance or obstruction." This explanation is the same as the Yingluo Sūtra's own: the heavenly mind is precisely the sense of being difficult to fathom and know; the nature of wisdom is precisely the sense of being free of hindrance or obstruction. And yet, among these six dharmas, their cultivation follows no fixed order, their realization follows no fixed sequence, and their function, too, is exercised as the occasion allows — hence, the various sūtras list them in differing orders. The Treatise says: "The affair of an illusionist's trick is an empty, deceptive dharma — performed with grass and wood, it deceives and confuses the onlooker's own eye, while the thing itself is never truly changed at all." Spiritual power is not like this — it truly transforms the dharma itself, causing the thing itself to be genuinely changed: just as earth carries the principle of becoming water, and water carries the sense of becoming earth. Gold or silver, meeting fire, melts; water, meeting cold, solidifies — fire and cold are the very dharmas of melting and solidifying. What solidifies is genuinely solidified, and what melts is genuinely melted; and, once the heavenly mind's own spontaneous nature of wisdom is attained, one is genuinely able to transform and function with this same freedom. The water or fire thus transformed causes others to genuinely make use of it, though this is not their own karmic fruit — it is simply what spiritual power accomplishes for that one occasion alone.

As for the tongue faculty: this takes the four unimpeded eloquences, able through a single wondrous sound to fill the whole trichiliocosm, and does not take the knowledge of flavor, since knowledge of flavor is a matter of karmic retribution. Here, the spiritual power belonging to the six faculties, spoken of now, is not brought forth by relying on any conditioned dhyāna at all — this is precisely the true reality of the middle way itself. The real possesses spiritual power of itself, spontaneously accomplished, requiring no deliberate exertion at all — hence, this is named the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation. Since no separate deliberate exertion is required, it is named unrecorded; spontaneously and constantly luminous, it is like the asura's own harp; transforming, and yet still able to transform again, hence it is called transformation upon transformation. The middle way's own true spiritual power, spontaneous in just this way, differs from every other kind of spiritual power. Discussing its cultivation: this relies entirely on the eternally abiding principle of the real mark itself. The text says: "Attaining this eternal purity of the eye-faculty." Since this is already called eternal, it is precisely one's own original nature's own purity — an eternal nature free of any taint or defilement. The Vibhāṣā says: "The six sense-gates' own excellence is simply so of itself, from the very beginning." The Aṅgulimālīya Sūtra says: "What is meant is that this very eye-faculty, with respect to every tathāgata, is eternal, complete and entire, its cultivation undiminished, seeing fully and clearly." And so on through the ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind — every one of them, with respect to every tathāgata, is eternal, complete and entire, its cultivation undiminished, hearing and knowing fully and clearly, and the rest. "That" refers to what, for the Buddha, is his own, while, for sentient beings, it is other than themselves; sentient beings suppose it impermanent, while, for the Tathāgata, it is eternal. As for "diminished cultivation": cultivation relying on dhyāna is named diminished cultivation; cultivation relying on the real mark is named undiminished cultivation. Not seeing buddha-nature is named seeing that is not full and clear; seeing buddha-nature is named seeing that is full and clear. Again, seeing the principle of the real mark is named "full and clear"; knowing the very affairs of the dharma-realm is named "clear and distinct."

見有二種：一、相似見。二、分真見。相似者，如六根清淨中辨。

論其真見，如《華嚴》所明，佛眼、耳、鼻、舌、身、意也。此經中亦明真身通相：所謂普現色身，示一切眾生所喜見身，即是外身通也；現身如琉璃，十方諸佛悉於身中現，即是內現身通也。眼、耳、鼻、舌等，內、外示現，亦例如此。是則圓教神通，異於前辨(云云)。

Seeing is of two kinds: first, seeing in resemblance; second, seeing in a portion of the truth. Seeing in resemblance is as distinguished within the purification of the six faculties.

Discussing the true seeing itself: this is as clarified in the Avataṃsaka Sūtra — the buddha's own eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind. This present sūtra, too, clarifies the true mark of bodily spiritual power: namely, universally manifesting a form-body, showing every sentient being the body they themselves most delight to see — this is precisely bodily spiritual power manifested outwardly; manifesting a body like beryl, with every buddha throughout the ten directions manifesting within this very body — this is precisely bodily spiritual power manifested inwardly. The eye, ear, nose, tongue, and the rest, manifested both inwardly and outwardly, follow this same pattern. This, then, is the spiritual power belonging to the perfect teaching, differing from every account given before, and so on.

問：
若以六根為六通者，云何功德有增減？

答：
《大論》四十云：「鼻、舌、身，同稱覺；眼稱見；耳稱聞；意稱知。」三識所知為一；三識所知為別。而三識助道法多，故別說；三識不爾，故合說。又三識但知世間事，故合說；三識亦知世間亦知出世間，故別說。又三識但無記法，三識或緣善、惡、無記等；又三識能生三業因緣，故別說也。若例此義，三根種種義強，故有千二百功德。三根力弱故但八百功德者，蓋一途別說，非經圓意。《正法華》「功德正等等千」。

Question: If the six faculties are taken as the six spiritual powers, why is it that their own merit differs in increase and decrease?

Answer: The Treatise, in its fortieth fascicle, says: "The nose, tongue, and body are alike named 'sensing'; the eye is named 'seeing'; the ear is named 'hearing'; the mind is named 'knowing.'" What these three kinds of consciousness know may be taken as one, or may be taken as distinct. Since these three kinds of consciousness carry many dharmas that assist the path, they are discussed separately; the other three do not, and hence are discussed together. Again, these three kinds of consciousness know only worldly affairs, and hence are discussed together; those three know both the worldly and the world-transcending, and hence are discussed separately. Again, these three kinds of consciousness pertain only to the unrecorded dharma, while those three may take good, evil, or the unrecorded alike as their object; and, again, those three are able to give rise to the causes and conditions of the three kinds of karma, and hence are discussed separately. Following this same sense: three faculties, their own sense especially strong in various ways, thereby possess twelve hundred units of merit. As for why the other three faculties, their power weaker, possess only eight hundred units of merit — this is merely one particular account, and not the sūtra's own perfect sense. The Saddharmapuṇḍarīka, in its earlier translation, says: "The merit is, in fact, equal, equally reaching a thousand for each."

今經顯六根互用，將三根足二百，向三根而互用耳。自在無礙能等，如《正法華》說。能縮，如身眼鼻之八百；能盈，如耳舌意千二百。經云：「若能持是經，功德則無量，如虛空無邊，其福不可限。」互用之意彰矣！

The present sūtra reveals the mutual interchangeability of the six faculties, taking the three faculties that possess only two hundred and applying them, through this mutual interchangeability, to the other three faculties as well. Their sovereign freedom, unobstructed, being thereby equal, as the Saddharmapuṇḍarīka's earlier translation itself explains. They are able to contract, as with the eight hundred belonging to the body, eye, and nose; and they are able to expand fully, as with the twelve hundred belonging to the ear, tongue, and mind. The sūtra says: "If one is able to uphold this sūtra, its merit shall then be immeasurable, like empty space, boundless — its blessing cannot be limited." The sense of their mutual interchangeability is thereby made manifest!

Reading note

The name-and-number analysis reduces "spiritual" and "power" to plain qualities of mind — the difficult-to-fathom and the unobstructed — rather than exotic abilities layered on top of an ordinary mind, and the illusionist simile sharpens what makes genuine spiritual power different from a magic trick: an illusion "deceives the onlooker's own eye" while the object itself never changes, but spiritual power "truly transforms the dharma itself." The perfect teaching's own six spiritual powers, coined "the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation" (already established in Fascicle 4's twenty-five samādhis discussion), are then distinguished from every other kind by requiring no cultivation at all in the ordinary sense — they are simply the real mark's own eternal purity, already complete, misread as impermanent only because sentient beings mistake what is genuinely their own for something other. The mutual-interchangeability argument about the twelve-hundred/eight-hundred merit asymmetry is a characteristically Tiantai move: rather than accepting an unequal count at face value, Zhiyi treats the six faculties' full interchangeability with one another as evidence that the lower count is merely "one particular account," not the sūtra's own perfect sense.

As for responding as a follower of the distinct teaching: this uses the compassion of the provisional-as-such and the middle-as-such, perfuming the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, bringing forth a response both gradual and sudden — showing the cultivation of dharmas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges — each and every one of them genuinely seen, sharing fully in that same karmic condition. As for responding as a follower of the perfect teaching: this uses the compassion of the middle-as-such, perfuming the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, bringing forth a perfect and sudden response — showing the cultivation of the immeasurable within the one and the one within the immeasurable — each and every one of them genuinely seen, sharing fully in that same karmic condition. Responses matching another's direct retribution such as these cannot be reckoned or counted at all — they can be grasped in the mind, but cannot be exhausted in words, and so on. Grasping this same sense, one may look forward, from here, to the gradual and the sudden.

五味教中用神通者，乳教所用神力，若多若少，但表兩意：一麁、一妙。三藏用神力，若多若少，但為一麁。方等用神力，若多若少，三麁一妙。般若用神力，若多若少，二麁一妙。此經神力，若多若少，唯為一妙。所以〈序品〉中瑞相有十，咸皆表妙也：「地皆嚴淨」表理妙。「放眉間光」表智妙。「入于三昧」表行妙。

Whatever spiritual power is used within the Tripiṭaka teaching, whether much or little, is only ever coarse. Whatever spiritual power is used within the vaipulya teachings, whether much or little, is three coarse and one wondrous. Whatever spiritual power is used within the perfection-of-wisdom teachings, whether much or little, is two coarse and one wondrous. Whatever spiritual power is used within the present sūtra, whether much or little, is solely wondrous. This is why the ten auspicious signs within the "Introduction" chapter, every one, without exception, signify the wondrous: "the ground everywhere adorned and pure" signifies the wondrous of the object. "Emitting light from between his brows" signifies the wondrous of wisdom. "Entering samādhi" signifies the wondrous of practice.

"Heavenly rain of four kinds of flowers" signifies the wondrous of stage. "The fragrant wind of sandalwood" signifies the wondrous of the vehicle. "The four assemblies, every one, filled with doubt" signifies capacity, and "beholding eighteen thousand lands" signifies response — these two together clarify the wondrous of sympathetic response. "The ground shaking in six ways" signifies the wondrous of spiritual powers. "The heavenly drum sounding of itself" and "expounding the Dharma" signify the wondrous of teaching the Dharma. "The devas, the nāgas, and the great assembly rejoicing together" signifies the wondrous of the retinue. "And further beholding the buddha's own children cultivating every kind of practice" signifies the wondrous of merit and benefit. This use of spiritual transformation, whether little or much, alike signifies the wondrous throughout. The text says: "The Buddha now enters samādhi — this is inconceivable! Manifesting a rare and extraordinary event!" Manifesting this rare and extraordinary event is precisely the wondrous spiritual power.

若應同依報者，有兩意：若國土苦樂，由於眾生，非佛所作，佛但應同而已。若作折伏攝受者，佛鑒機緣，或作苦國、或作樂國，苦樂由佛，不關眾生。

今且釋初意：《大論》云：「有國土純聲聞僧，或國土純菩薩僧，或菩薩聲聞共為僧。」或淨、或穢，何故差別？皆由乘、戒緩急。

As for responding to match another's dependent retribution, this carries two senses: if a land's own suffering or bliss is due to sentient beings themselves, and not something the Buddha has made, then the Buddha merely responds by matching it. If this is instead done as an act of subduing or of gathering in, then the Buddha, perceiving the capacity and condition at hand, makes either a land of suffering or a land of bliss accordingly — the suffering or bliss then being due to the Buddha, and having nothing to do with sentient beings themselves.

Here, the first sense is explained for now: the Treatise says: "There are lands whose saṅgha is purely made up of śrāvakas, and lands whose saṅgha is purely made up of bodhisattvas, and lands whose saṅgha is a mixture of bodhisattvas and śrāvakas together." Some pure, some impure — why this difference? It is entirely due to how urgently or laxly the vehicle and the precepts are each held.

若戒緩乘亦急亦緩者，即是穢土，以聲聞菩薩共為僧。以戒緩故，五濁土穢；乘亦緩故，是開三乘；乘亦急故，是顯一乘，娑婆是也。戒急乘亦緩亦急者，淨土也。戒急故，土無五濁；乘亦緩故，開三乘；亦急故，顯一，安養是也。乘緩戒急者，即是淨土，純聲聞為僧，此可知也。戒緩乘急，即是穢國，純菩薩為僧，此亦可知。淨穢差別，悉由眾生，高下苦樂，不關佛也。

If the precepts are held laxly while the vehicle is held both urgently and laxly, this is precisely an impure land, with bodhisattvas and śrāvakas together making up the saṅgha. Since the precepts are held laxly, the land is impure with the five turbidities; since the vehicle, too, is held laxly, the three vehicles are opened; and since the vehicle is also held urgently, the one vehicle is revealed — the sahā world is exactly this. If the precepts are held urgently while the vehicle is held both laxly and urgently, this is a pure land. Since the precepts are held urgently, the land is free of the five turbidities; since the vehicle is held laxly, the three vehicles are opened; and, being also held urgently, the one is revealed — the land of Sukhāvatī is exactly this. If the vehicle is held laxly while the precepts are held urgently, this is precisely a pure land with a saṅgha purely made up of śrāvakas — this may be understood accordingly. If the precepts are held laxly while the vehicle is held urgently, this is precisely an impure land with a saṅgha purely made up of bodhisattvas — this, too, may be understood accordingly. The difference between the pure and the impure is entirely due to sentient beings themselves; the difference between the elevated and the lowly, between suffering and bliss, has nothing to do with the Buddha.

As for the sense of this being done as an act of subduing or gathering in: the land is then due to the Buddha, and has nothing to do with sentient beings themselves. The Buddha, through the compassion born of contemplating evil, joined with the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, brings forth an impure land, subduing and gathering in the sentient beings of the four evil destinies. Through the compassion born of wholesome karma, joined with the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, he subdues and gathers in the sentient beings of the remaining two destinies. The Buddha, through the compassion of the analytical emptiness and the six pāramitās, and the rest, joined with the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, brings forth either an impure land or a pure land, subduing and gathering in the sentient beings of the śrāvaka and bodhisattva realms. The Buddha, through the compassion of emptiness-as-such, joined with the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, brings forth either a pure land or an impure land, subduing and gathering in the sentient beings of the shared teaching's own śrāvaka and bodhisattva realms. The Buddha, through the compassion of the sequentially distinct, joined with the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, brings forth either an impure land or a pure land, subduing and gathering in the sentient beings of the distinct teaching's own bodhisattva realm. The Buddha, through the compassion of the middle-as-such, joined with the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, brings forth either a pure land or an impure land, subduing and gathering in the sentient beings of the perfect teaching's own bodhisattva realm. Every such difference in the making of a land is entirely due to the Tathāgata's own transformation through spiritual power.

今將此依正轉變，待三教作意神通，悉名為麁。譬如圖畫，盡思竭力，終不似真，名之為麁。若明鏡寫容，任運相似，名之為妙。方便神通，譬如麁畫；中道任運，即對即應，譬於淨鏡，故為妙也。

就無記化化禪所作神變，自論麁妙。若為九界眾生，用方便神力，作淨作穢，若廣若狹，悉名為麁。若為佛法界眾生，用真實神力，作淨作穢，若廣若狹，悉名為妙。如經：放眉間光照萬八千土，及三變土田。比餘經神力，何足為多？但為開發大事，故言妙也。

Here, matching this transformation of direct and dependent retribution against the three provisional teachings' own deliberate spiritual power, every one of these is named coarse. It is comparable to a painted portrait — however much thought and effort is exhausted upon it, it never truly resembles the real thing, and hence is named coarse. As for a bright mirror reflecting a face — this resembles it spontaneously, and hence is named wondrous. Expedient spiritual power is comparable to a coarse painting; the middle way's own spontaneous spiritual power, corresponding and responding at once, is comparable to a clear mirror — hence, it is named wondrous.

As for the spiritual transformation accomplished by the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, its own coarse and wondrous may be further discussed. If, for the sake of the nine realms' own sentient beings, expedient spiritual power is used, making a land pure or impure, broad or narrow, this is, every time, named coarse. If, for the sake of the buddha-realm's own sentient beings, real spiritual power is used, making a land pure or impure, broad or narrow, this is, every time, named wondrous. As with the sūtra's own emitting light from between the brows, illuminating eighteen thousand lands, and the threefold transformation of the land — compared to the spiritual power of every other sūtra, how could this possibly be considered abundant? It is simply for the sake of opening up this one great matter that it alone is called wondrous.

又約五味論麁妙者，乳教一麁一妙，酪教一麁，生蘇三麁一妙，熟蘇二麁一妙，法華一妙。

又諸經妙同麁異，麁有二種：一、難轉麁；二、易轉麁。易轉者，於諸經中已得為妙。難轉者，今於《法華》，無復兩麁，但有一妙。唯一大佛事因緣，曾無他事，假同九界神通。眾生，自謂他事；於佛，常是佛事。客作自謂賤人；長者審知是子。此即相待神通妙也。

又諸經諸麁神通、隔妙神通者，今經皆開權顯實，同妙神通，是名絕待明妙神通。此略記，不周悉也。

Again, discussed in terms of the Five Flavors, coarse and wondrous: the milk teaching has one coarse and one wondrous; the cream teaching has one coarse; the curdled-butter teaching has three coarse and one wondrous; the clarified-butter teaching has two coarse and one wondrous; the Lotus Sūtra has one wondrous alone.

Again, though every sūtra's own wondrous is the same, their own coarse differs — coarse is of two kinds: first, coarse that is difficult to transform; second, coarse that is easy to transform. What is easy to transform has already, within the other sūtras, been made wondrous. What is difficult to transform is, here, within the Lotus Sūtra, no longer present as either kind of coarse at all — only the one wondrous remains. There is but a single great matter, the one buddha's own undertaking, and never, in truth, any other matter at all — the nine realms' own spiritual power is only ever borrowed and provisional. Sentient beings themselves suppose it to be some other matter; for the Buddha, it is, throughout, always this one same undertaking. The hired laborer supposes himself of humble birth; the elder himself knows for certain that he is his own child. This is precisely the wondrous of spiritual power discussed in the relative sense.

Again, as for every other sūtra's own coarse spiritual power being set apart from the wondrous spiritual power: the present sūtra opens every one of them, revealing the real, making them one with the wondrous spiritual power — this is named the wondrous of spiritual power clarified in the absolute sense. This is recorded only in brief, and is not treated exhaustively here.

Reading note

The ten-realm survey of "responding to match another's direct retribution" pairs each of the Four Teachings' own compassion with the identical dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, so that what varies is never the underlying spiritual power itself but only which compassion — contemplating evil, contemplating good, the analytical emptiness, emptiness-as-such, the provisional-and-middle-as-such, the middle-as-such alone — perfumes it toward a given realm's own capacity, from the black-haired forms of hell up through the perfect teaching's own showing of "the immeasurable within the one and the one within the immeasurable." The correlation of the "Introduction" chapter's own ten auspicious signs to all ten wondrous aspects in sequence is one of this treatise's most compressed and elegant demonstrations: a single opening scene of the Lotus Sūtra — ground made pure, light from the brow, entering samādhi, flowers raining down, fragrant wind, the assembly's doubt, the vision of eighteen thousand lands, the six earthquakes, the heavenly drum, the rejoicing assembly, the bodhisattvas at practice — turns out to prefigure the entire architecture of Fascicles 2 through 9 in miniature, sign by sign. The painting-versus-mirror distinction, applied here specifically to why the three provisional teachings' own "deliberate spiritual power" remains coarse no matter how much effort is exerted, while the perfect teaching's spontaneous response is wondrous however little is used, closes the wondrous of spiritual powers on the same note struck throughout this fascicle: genuine wondrousness is a matter of what a response is grounded in, never of its visible scale.

Eighth, the wondrous of teaching the Dharma: no dharma can be pointed to directly, the mark of every word and phrase being itself quiescent cessation; and yet, given the right cause and condition, it may still be spoken of and pointed to. The two bodies of medicine and jewel, treated above, first set things into motion through concentration; now, the two drums of poison and heaven draw beings out afterward through wisdom, proclaiming the one vehicle, with no difference among the three, every one of them arriving at the ground of omniscience. What is thereby taught is, in every case, real and never empty — hence, the wondrous of teaching the Dharma is next clarified. This is treated under six headings: first, explaining the name of the Dharma; second, distinguishing the great and the small; third, clarifying its difference according to the condition it meets; fourth, judging what it expresses; fifth, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous; sixth, the contemplation of mind.

Chinese Source

釋法名者，三世佛法雖多無量，十二部經收罄無不盡。先出達摩欝多羅，有七種分別：體一、相二、制名三、定名四、差別五、相攝六、料簡七。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for explaining the name of the Dharma: though the buddha-dharma of the three time periods is many, beyond measure, the twelve divisions of the scriptures gather it in fully, with nothing left out. First, Dharmottara's own account is given, distinguishing seven senses: first, the essence, which is one; second, the form, which is two; third, how the name is fixed, which is three; fourth, the settled names, which is four; fifth, their differences, which is five; sixth, their mutual inclusion, which is six; seventh, resolving doubts, which is seven.

As for the essence being one: the sūtra takes names, words, and passages of text as its own essence — every sūtra, without exception, is exactly this — hence, the essence is one.

As for the form being two: there is the direct prose exposition, and there is verse composed in praise and eulogy — these two forms are distinct from one another. Why is this? Since people's own dispositions and delights differ, some favoring plain, straightforward language, others favoring beautifully wrought speech — hence, the form is distinguished as two.

制名三者，脩多羅、祇夜、伽陀，三部就字句為名，不就所表。授記等八部，不就所表，又不就字句，從事立稱。方廣一部，名從所表。何者？脩多羅等三部，直說法相，可即名以顯所表。如苦集滅道，依名即顯所表，故就名以為名也。授記等經，所表之法，不可但以言說，要寄事方乃得顯。如授記經，從事為名，止明行因得果道理，理託事彰，事以言辨。如《法華》中，與聲聞授記，彰一切皆當得成佛。寄授記以彰所顯，故名授記經。無問自說經者，聖人說法皆待請問，然亦為眾生作不請之師，故無問自說。又佛法難知，人無能問，若不自說，眾則不知。為說不說，又復不知為說何法，故無問自說，乃所以彰所說。甚深唯證，是以寄無問自說，以彰所顯也。因緣經者，欲明戒法，必因犯彰過，過相彰現方得立制，此亦託因緣以明所顯也。譬喻經者，法相微隱，要假近以喻遠，故以言借況，寄況以彰理也。本事、本生經者，本事說他事，本生說自生，因現事以說往事，託本生以彰所表，名本事經。託本生以彰所行，名本生經也。未曾有經者，說希奇事，由來未有者，未曾有也。示法有大力，有大利益，託未曾有事，以彰所表也。論義經者，諸部中言義隱覆，往復分別得明所顯，寄論義以明理也。

As for the naming method being three: sūtra proper, geya, and gāthā — these three divisions are named directly from their own wording, not from what they point to. The eight remaining divisions beginning with vyākaraṇa are named neither from what they point to nor from their own wording, but take their name from the matter itself. Vaipulya alone is named from what it points to. Why is this? Sūtra proper and the other two directly state the very mark of the dharma, and so their name may point directly to what they signify — as with suffering, its arising, cessation, and the path: the name itself directly shows what it signifies, and hence they are named from their own wording. As for vyākaraṇa and the rest: what these dharmas signify cannot be shown by mere words alone, but must be manifested through a particular matter before it can appear at all. As with the sūtra of prediction: it is named from the matter itself, showing only the principle that practicing the cause yields the fruit — the principle being manifested through the matter, and the matter being distinguished through words. As in the Lotus Sūtra, where the śrāvakas are given their own prediction of buddhahood, manifesting that every one of them shall, without exception, attain buddhahood — relying on this bestowal of prediction to manifest what is signified, hence it is named the sūtra of prediction. As for the sūtra of spontaneous, unprompted utterance: the sage's own teaching of the Dharma always awaits a request or a question, and yet, for the sake of sentient beings, he also serves as a teacher unbidden — hence, spontaneous, unprompted utterance. Again, the buddha-dharma is difficult to know, and no one is able to ask about it; were it not spoken of spontaneously, the assembly would have no way to know it. Whether to speak or not to speak, and, further, not knowing which dharma to speak of even if one did — hence, spontaneous, unprompted utterance is precisely what manifests what is taught. Since it is profound beyond measure, known only through direct realization, it relies on spontaneous, unprompted utterance to manifest what it signifies. As for the sūtra of causal narrative: wishing to clarify the dharma of the precepts, one must, by way of some transgression, manifest the fault involved — the fault's own mark must be manifested before the rule can be established at all — this, too, relies on cause and condition to clarify what it signifies. As for the sūtra of parable: the mark of the dharma is subtle and hidden, and requires borrowing what is near at hand to illustrate what is far off — hence, words are borrowed as an analogy, relying on this analogy to manifest the principle. As for the sūtras of past events and of birth-stories: past events speak of another's own past deeds, while birth-stories speak of one's own past births — relying on a present matter to speak of a past matter, relying on past events to manifest what is signified is named the sūtra of past events; relying on past births to manifest what was practiced is named the sūtra of birth-stories. As for the sūtra of the marvel: this speaks of a rare and extraordinary matter, one never before present — hence, marvel. Showing that the dharma possesses great power, possesses great benefit, relying on this marvelous matter to manifest what is signified. As for the sūtra of doctrinal discussion: within the various divisions, where the words and their sense lie hidden and obscured, going back and forth in analysis brings what is signified to light — relying on this discussion to clarify the principle.

故授記等八經，從事立稱。方廣一部從所表為名者，方廣之理，雖以名說，而妙出名言。雖寄事以彰，然不可如事而取，故不就名不就事，就所表以為名也。

定名有四，脩多羅名線經。經體是名字，而名從況喻。祇夜、偈陀，當體為名。授記、無問自說、論義等三經，體事合目。自餘從事也。

Hence, the eight divisions beginning with vyākaraṇa take their name from the matter itself. As for vaipulya alone taking its name from what it points to: the principle of vaipulya, though it may be spoken of through a name, is wondrous, transcending name and word altogether. Though it, too, relies on some matter to be manifested, it cannot simply be grasped through that matter as such — hence, it is named neither from its own wording nor from the matter, but takes its name from what it points to.

As for the settled names being four: sūtra proper is named "thread sūtra" — the sūtra's own substance is names and words, yet the name itself is drawn from a metaphor. Geya and gāthā are each named directly from their own substance. Vyākaraṇa, spontaneous unprompted utterance, and doctrinal discussion — these three sūtras are each named jointly by their own substance together with the matter involved. The rest are named from the matter alone.

差別者，脩多羅有九種。經云：從如是至奉行，一切名脩多羅。是則脩多羅名通而體總，皆名為經，故名通。就文字經體分為十二部，故體總也。

第二、就總脩多羅中，隨事分出十一部，即對十一部，餘直說法相者，是別相脩多羅。

三者、論義經，解釋十一部經，是則十一部為經本。當知論所解釋，前十一部皆是脩多羅。又《雜心》中〈脩多羅品〉，亦對論以經為脩多羅。又如婆脩槃馱解提婆〈百論〉，論為經本，亦名論為脩多羅。又經云：除脩多羅，餘四句偈以為偈經。即對四句偈經，餘長行說者是脩多羅。又云：祇夜名偈頌脩多羅。即對祇夜頌偈，所頌即是脩多羅也。又如分別三藏，以敷置理教為脩多羅，對別毘尼、阿毘曇也。又如經說，從佛出十二部經，從十二部經出脩多羅。對十二別教，以通教為脩多羅，是九中、初二偈亦是也。

As for their differences being five: sūtra proper is of nine kinds. The sūtra says: from "thus have I heard" down to "received it with reverence and practiced accordingly," every part is named sūtra proper — this, then, is sūtra proper taken in the general sense, its own substance taken as a whole, every one of the twelve divisions alike named a sūtra, and hence named "general." Distinguished according to the written words that make up the sūtra's own substance, it is divided into the twelve divisions — hence, its substance is taken as a whole.

Second, within sūtra proper taken as a whole, eleven divisions are distinguished out according to the matter involved; matched against these eleven divisions, whatever remains, directly stating the mark of the dharma, is the particular sūtra proper.

Third, the sūtra of doctrinal discussion explains the eleven divisions — the eleven divisions are thereby the very root text of the sūtra. One should therefore know that whatever a treatise explains, the eleven divisions that come before it, are, every one, sūtra proper. Again, the "Chapter on Sūtra Proper" within the Saṃyuktābhidharmahṛdaya likewise matches sūtra against treatise, taking the sūtra as sūtra proper. Again, as when Vasubandhu explains Āryadeva's own Śataśāstra, the treatise itself is taken as the root text, and the treatise, too, is named sūtra proper. Again, the sūtra says: apart from sūtra proper, the remaining four-line verses are taken as gāthā-sūtra — matched against this four-line gāthā-sūtra, whatever is stated in prose is sūtra proper. Again, it is said: geya is named the verse that restates sūtra proper — matched against this geya restated verse, what is restated is itself sūtra proper. Again, as when the Tripiṭaka is distinguished, laying out its own principle and teaching is taken as sūtra proper, matched against the vinaya and abhidharma taken separately. Again, as the sūtra itself says: from the Buddha issue the twelve divisions, and from the twelve divisions issues sūtra proper — matched against the twelve particular teachings, the shared teaching is taken as sūtra proper; and, among the nine, this includes the first two, verse included.

偈陀者，有四種。如言《法華》有阿閦婆等偈，《涅槃》二萬五千偈。是則偈經，復是通總。若四句為偈，一字一句，得名為經。非一字一句，皆名為偈。但以聖言巧妙，章句成就，數句為偈，故通得名偈。二、除脩多羅餘四句為偈。三、偈中重頌者，名祇夜，當知不重頌偈，名為偈經。四、如脩多羅通總，隨事剋分，別為異部，以直說為脩多羅。當知偈中亦隨事剋分，若授記因緣等，別為異部，以不隨事，直爾偈說，名為偈經。

祇夜者，名為重頌。頌有三種：一、頌意。二、頌事。三、頌言。

頌意者，頌聖意所念法相及事。若頌心所念法相，則名偈陀經。若頌心所念授記等事，則隨事別為異經。頌事，謂授記等事，亦隨所頌事，別為異經。頌言者，若頌隨事之言，隨事別為異經。若頌直說脩多羅者，名為重頌祇夜經也。

Gāthā is of four kinds. As it is said, the Lotus Sūtra contains verses such as the Akṣobhya verses, and the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra contains twenty-five thousand verses — this, then, is gāthā-sūtra, taken again in the general sense. If four lines make a verse, then a single word or a single line, too, may be named a sūtra; what is not a single word or a single line is, in every case, named a gāthā. It is simply that, by the sage's own skillful, wondrous speech, sections and passages are brought to completion, and several lines together make a verse — hence, in the general sense, this is named gāthā. Second, apart from sūtra proper, the remaining four-line verses are gāthā. Third, whatever restates a verse already given is named geya; one should therefore know that a verse not restating anything is named gāthā-sūtra. Fourth, just as sūtra proper, taken in the general sense as a whole, is distinguished according to the particular matter into the several divisions, with direct prose statement taken as sūtra proper — one should therefore know that verse, too, is distinguished according to the particular matter: whatever concerns prediction, causal narrative, and the rest is distinguished into its own separate division; whatever does not follow any particular matter, but is simply stated directly in verse, is named gāthā-sūtra.

Geya is named restated verse. Restatement is of three kinds: first, restating the sense; second, restating the matter; third, restating the words.

Restating the sense means restating the very dharma-mark and matter that the sage's own mind has in view. If what the mind has in view, the dharma-mark itself, is restated, this is named gāthā-sūtra. If what the mind has in view, a matter such as prediction and the rest, is restated, this is, according to the matter, distinguished as a separate sūtra. Restating the matter means restating a matter such as prediction and the rest, likewise distinguished, according to the matter restated, as a separate sūtra. Restating the words means restating the words belonging to some particular matter, distinguished, according to that matter, as a separate sūtra. If what is restated is sūtra proper's own direct prose statement, this is named the sūtra of restated verse, geya.

授記者，果為心期名記，聖言說與名授。授記有二種：若與諸菩薩授佛記莂，是大乘中授記。若記近因、近果，是小乘中記也。

無問自說有二種：一、理深意遠，人無能問。二、非不可問，但聽者宜聞，佛為不請之師，不請之師不待問自說也。

方廣有二種：一、語廣。二、理廣。

As for vyākaraṇa: the fruit, held in mind as an expectation, is named "prediction"; the sage's own word, given in speech, is named "bestowal." Vyākaraṇa is of two kinds: bestowing upon the various bodhisattvas the prediction of buddhahood is vyākaraṇa within the Mahāyāna; predicting a nearby cause or a nearby fruit is prediction within the Hīnayāna.

Spontaneous, unprompted utterance is of two kinds: first, the principle is so profound and its sense so far-reaching that no one is able to ask about it. Second, it is not that it cannot be asked about, but rather that it is fitting for the listener to hear it spoken; the Buddha, serving as an unbidden teacher, an unbidden teacher does not wait to be asked before speaking of his own accord.

Vaipulya is of two kinds: first, broad in its own language; second, broad in its own principle.

相攝者，就脩多羅中出十一部。若偈與直說，相對言之，脩多羅中得出九部，但無二偈。偈陀中得出十部，但無直說脩多羅也。祇夜中得出九部，無脩多羅，亦無偈經也。

四、伽陀，此云不重頌，亦略言偈耳。四句為頌，如此間詩頌也。

五、優陀那，此云無問自說。六、尼陀那，此云因緣。七、阿波陀那，此云譬喻。八、伊帝目多伽，此云如是語，亦云本事。九、闍陀伽，此云本生。十、毘佛略，此云方廣。十一、阿浮陀達摩，此云未曾有。十二、優波提舍，此云論議。

As for explaining the name of the Dharma: this has already been discussed above, within the arising of the teachings. Here, the names given differ from one another, and their translation varies considerably. Following the Mahāprajñāpāramitopadeśa's own naming, they are as follows: first, sūtra, rendered "the root of the Dharma," also "concordant scripture," also "thread sūtra." Second, geya, rendered "restated verse," since it restates sūtra proper in verse. Third, vyākaraṇa, rendered "bestowing a prediction."

Fourth, gāthā, rendered "unrestated verse," also spoken of briefly as simply "verse" — four lines making a stanza, comparable to the poetry and verse found here in this land.

Fifth, udāna, rendered "spontaneous, unprompted utterance." Sixth, nidāna, rendered "causal narrative." Seventh, avadāna, rendered "parable." Eighth, itivṛttaka, rendered "thus it was said," also "past events." Ninth, jātaka, rendered "birth-story." Tenth, vaipulya, rendered "expanded teaching." Eleventh, adbhutadharma, rendered "the marvel." Twelfth, upadeśa, rendered "doctrinal discussion."

部者，部別各有類從也。經者，外國云脩多羅，此云線經。線能貫穿，經能經緯，言能持法，如線如經。然《阿毘曇雜心》中，說脩多羅五義者，乃是彼論師解義，非翻名也。世俗亦對緯名經，而訓經為常。如物經亘始終，始終時別，而物無改異，不改異故，名之為常。

脩多羅者，諸經中直說者，謂四《阿含》，及二百五十戒，出三藏外諸摩訶衍經直說者，皆名脩多羅也。

祇夜者，諸經中偈，四、五、七、九言句，少多不定，重頌上者，皆名祇夜也。

As for "division": each division has its own particular kind, gathered together accordingly. As for "sūtra": in the foreign land it is called sūtra, and here it is rendered "thread sūtra." A thread is able to run through and connect; a sūtra is able to weave warp and weft; words are able to sustain the Dharma — like a thread, like a woven fabric. And yet, within the Saṃyuktābhidharmahṛdaya's own account of the five senses of "sūtra," this is that treatise's own author's explanation of the sense, not a rendering of the name itself. In common worldly usage, too, "sūtra" is paired with "weft," and "sūtra" is glossed as meaning "the constant." As a thing runs its course from beginning to end, beginning and end differing in time, and yet the thing itself undergoes no alteration or change — being unaltered and unchanged, it is accordingly named "constant."

As for sūtra proper: whatever, among the various sūtras, is stated directly — namely, the four Āgamas, and the two hundred and fifty precepts, together with whatever, among the Mahāyāna sūtras beyond the Tripiṭaka, is likewise stated directly — is, every one, named sūtra proper.

As for geya: whatever verse, among the various sūtras, in lines of four, five, seven, or nine words, however many or few, restates what has already been given above, is, every one, named geya.

和伽羅那者，說三乘、六趣、九道劫數，當得作佛。若後爾所歲，當得聲聞、支佛。後爾所歲，當受六趣報，皆名授記。夫授記法，面門放五色光，從上二牙出，照三惡道。下二牙出，照人天。光中演說，無常無我，安隱涅槃。遇光聞法者，三途中身心安樂，人中癃殘者差，六欲天厭患欲樂，色天厭禪樂。光照十方，遍作佛事，還遶七匝，從佛足下入，是記地獄道。[跳-兆+專]入、髀入、臍入、胸入、口入、肩間入、頂入者，是記佛道。《論》不見記脩羅光，當是開鬼道出脩羅，從容在於髀臍之間耳。

伽陀者，一切四言、五言、七、九等偈，不重頌者，皆名伽陀也。

As for vyākaraṇa: teaching that the three vehicles, the six destinies, and the nine paths, after so many eons, shall attain buddhahood; or that, after so many further years, one shall attain śrāvakahood or pratyekabuddhahood; or that, after so many further years, one shall receive retribution within the six destinies — every one of these is named a prediction. As for the very method of bestowing a prediction: from the face, five-colored light is emitted; from the two upper teeth it issues, illuminating the three evil destinies; from the two lower teeth it issues, illuminating the human and heavenly realms. Within this light, the teaching of impermanence, no-self, and the peaceful security of nirvāṇa is proclaimed. Whoever encounters this light and hears the Dharma — those within the three lower destinies find body and mind at peace; the crippled and disabled among humans are healed; the six heavens of desire grow weary and disgusted with the pleasures of desire; the form-realm heavens grow weary of the pleasure of dhyāna. The light illuminates the ten directions, performing the Buddha's own work universally, then circles back sevenfold and enters beneath the Buddha's own feet — this predicts the destiny of hell. Entering the sole of the foot, the thigh, the navel, the chest, the mouth, between the shoulders, or the crown of the head, each in turn predicts the path to buddhahood. The Treatise does not mention the light predicting the asura — this must be because the destiny of the hungry ghost, once opened, already includes the asura, its own position lying casually somewhere between the thigh and the navel.

As for gāthā: every four-word, five-word, seven-word, or nine-word verse and the rest, whatever does not restate anything already given, is, every one, named gāthā.

優陀那者，有法佛必應說，而無有問者，佛略開問端。如佛在舍婆提毘舍佉堂上，陰地經行，自說優陀那。所謂無我、無我所，是事善哉！是名優陀那。又如般若中，諸天子讚須菩提所說，善哉！善哉！希有世尊，難有世尊，是名優陀那。乃至佛滅後，諸弟子抄集要偈，諸無常偈作〈無常品〉，乃至〈婆羅門品〉，皆名優陀那也。

尼陀那者，說諸佛本起因緣，佛何因緣說此事？脩多羅中，有人問故為說是事；毘尼中，有人犯是事故結是戒。一切佛語緣起事，皆名尼陀那。

阿波陀那者，與世間相似，柔軟淺語。如《中阿含》長譬喻，《長阿含》大譬喻，億耳、二十億耳譬喻等，無量譬喻皆名阿波陀那。

As for udāna: there is a dharma the Buddha must inevitably speak of, and yet no one has asked about it — the Buddha himself briefly opens the very occasion for the question. As when the Buddha, at Śrāvastī, in the hall of Viśākhā, walking back and forth in a shaded place, spontaneously spoke this udāna: "There is no self, and nothing belonging to a self — this matter is excellent!" — this is named udāna. Again, as within the perfection-of-wisdom teachings, the devas' own sons, praising what Subhūti had said: "Excellent! Excellent! O rare World-Honored One, O World-Honored One so difficult to encounter!" — this, too, is named udāna. And, further, after the Buddha's own final nirvāṇa, the disciples gathered together the essential verses, compiling the verses on impermanence into the "Chapter on Impermanence," down through the "Chapter on Brahmins" — every one of these, too, is named udāna.

As for nidāna: this speaks of the buddhas' own original causal narrative — for what cause and condition did the Buddha speak of this matter? Within sūtra proper, someone asked a question, and this matter was accordingly taught; within the vinaya, someone committed this act, and this precept was accordingly established. Every account of the Buddha's own words' causal arising is named nidāna.

As for avadāna: this resembles worldly speech, gentle and accessible in its own language. As with the extended parables of the Madhyama Āgama, the great parables of the Dīrgha Āgama, the parables of Koṭīviṃśa and Koṭīviṃśatikoṭi, and the rest — every immeasurable parable of this kind is named avadāna.

伊帝目多伽有二種：一者、結句。言我先許說者，今已說竟。二者、更有經，名一目多伽。有人言：因多伽目多伽，名出三藏及摩訶衍，何等是？如淨飯王強逼千釋令出家，佛選堪得道者五百人，將往舍婆提，令離親屬。身子、目連教化，初中後夜，專精不睡，以夜為長。後得道還本國，從迦毘羅婆林五十里入城乞食，覺道路為長。時有師子，來禮佛足，為三因緣說偈(云云)，說此三事本因緣，故名一目多伽也。

闍陀伽者，說菩薩本曾為師子，受獼猴寄，攫脇肉貿猴子。於病世作赤目魚，施諸病者。或作飛鳥，救於泫溺，如是等無量本生，多有所濟，皆名闍陀伽也。

Itivṛttaka is of two kinds: first, a closing statement — saying, "what I promised to speak of before has now been fully spoken." Second, there is a further sūtra, named the Ekottarika. Some say: the terms itaka and itivṛttaka issue from both the Tripiṭaka and the Mahāyāna — what, then, is meant? As when King Śuddhodana forcibly compelled a thousand of the Śākya clan to go forth from home, the Buddha selected five hundred of them capable of attaining the path, and led them to Śrāvastī, causing them to be separated from their own kin. Taught and transformed by Śāriputra and Maudgalyāyana, throughout the first, middle, and last watches of the night, they applied themselves single-mindedly and did not sleep, finding the night long. Once they had attained the path and returned to their own homeland, entering the city to beg for alms fifty li from the Kapilavastu grove, they found the road long. At that time, there was a lion who came and bowed at the Buddha's feet, and, for three causal reasons, the Buddha spoke verses, and so on — speaking of the original causal narrative of these three matters, this is accordingly named ekottarika.

As for jātaka: this speaks of the bodhisattva's own past lives — once, as a lion, taking in a monkey who had come to him for refuge, and, when seized by the flank, offering his own flesh in exchange to ransom the monkey's child. During an age of sickness, becoming a red-eyed fish and giving himself to those who were sick. Or becoming a flying bird, saving those drowning and sinking. Every immeasurable birth-story of this kind, bringing benefit to many, is named jātaka.

毘佛略者，所謂《摩訶衍般若經》、《六波羅蜜經》、《華首》、《法華》、《佛本起》、《因緣雲》、《法雲》、《大雲》，如是等無量諸經，為得阿耨三菩提故，說此毘佛略也。

阿浮陀達摩者，如佛現種種神力，眾生怪未曾有。放光動地，種種異相，皆名阿浮陀達摩也。

優波提舍者，答諸問者，釋其所以，廣說諸義，如是等問答解義，皆名優波提舍也。佛自說論義經、迦栴延所解，乃至像法凡夫人如法說者，亦名優波提舍經也。

As for vaipulya: this refers to the Mahāyāna Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra, the Sūtra of the Six Pāramitās, the Kusumasaṃbhava, the Lotus Sūtra, the Buddha's Own Arising, the Cloud of Causal Narrative, the Cloud of the Dharma, the Great Cloud, and every immeasurable sūtra of this kind — spoken for the sake of attaining unsurpassed, perfect enlightenment — this is named vaipulya.

As for adbhutadharma: as when the Buddha manifests every kind of spiritual power, and sentient beings marvel at what has never before been seen — emitting light, shaking the earth, every extraordinary sign of this kind is named adbhutadharma.

As for upadeśa: answering every questioner, explaining the reason for the answer, expounding every kind of meaning at length — every question-and-answer explanation of meaning of this kind is named upadeśa. The Buddha's own self-spoken sūtra of doctrinal discussion, what Kātyāyana himself explained, and, down through the semblance-dharma age, whatever an ordinary person expounds in accordance with the Dharma, is likewise named the sūtra of upadeśa.

Reading note

Zhiyi works through the twelve divisions of the scriptures (十二部經, dvādaśāṅga) twice over: first abstractly, via Dharmottara's sevenfold analytical apparatus (why each division is named as it is — from its own wording, from the matter it concerns, or from what it ultimately signifies), then concretely, restating all twelve by Kumārajīva's own transliterations from the Mahāprajñāpāramitopadeśa and defining each in turn with a worked example. The prediction-bestowing light show (五色光 issuing from the teeth, circling the assembly, and entering different parts of the Buddha's own body to signify different destinies) is a striking piece of concrete ritual imagery inside an otherwise highly abstract taxonomic exercise. This entire apparatus — twelve genres of scripture, each defined twice — exists to set up the third pass still to come: judging each of the twelve coarse or wondrous when applied specifically to the Lotus Sūtra itself, in the "clarifying the coarse and the wondrous" section below.

Distinguishing the Great and the Small 分大小

Chinese Source

二、明分法大小者，此經指九部為入大之本，則九部是小，三部是大，蓋別語耳。通而為言，小亦有記莂六道因果。又《阿含》中亦授彌勒當作佛記，豈非授記經？亦有自唱善哉！無問而說。聲聞經中，以法空為大空，故《成論》中云：「正欲明三藏中實義。」實義者空，是《阿毘曇》所不申，而《成論》申於空。空即廣經。當知小乘，通具十二部也。故《涅槃》云：「先雖得聞十二部經，但聞名字，不聞其義。今因《涅槃》得聞其義。」又云：「先雖得聞十二部經，我意猶謂故不如是《大涅槃經》。」《大品》亦云：「魔作比丘，為菩薩說聲聞十二部經。」有經言：「大小乘各具十二部。若信六部通大小乘，不信六部互不相通。」按此者，即是大小俱有十二部也。但是小乘中說，非大乘之義。故別讓三存九。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the division of the Dharma into great and small: this present sūtra points to the nine divisions as the very root by which one enters the great vehicle, taking the nine divisions as small and the remaining three as great — yet this is merely a particular way of speaking. Discussed in the general sense, the small vehicle, too, possesses the prediction of cause and fruit across the six destinies. Again, within the Āgamas, too, Maitreya is given the prediction that he shall become a buddha — how, then, could this not be a sūtra of prediction? There is likewise self-proclaimed praise — "excellent!" — spoken without being asked. Within the śrāvaka's own sūtras, the emptiness of dharmas is taken as the great emptiness — hence the Tattvasiddhi says: "This is precisely intended to clarify the real sense within the Tripiṭaka." The real sense is emptiness — this is what the Abhidharma does not fully expound, while the Tattvasiddhi does fully expound emptiness. Emptiness is itself vaipulya. One should therefore know that the Hīnayāna, too, is generally complete with all twelve divisions. Hence the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Though I formerly had the chance to hear the twelve divisions of the scriptures, I heard only their names, and did not hear their sense; now, because of the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, I have come to hear their sense." And again: "Though I formerly had the chance to hear the twelve divisions of the scriptures, my own mind still supposed they were not equal to this Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra." The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra likewise says: "Māra, disguised as a bhikṣu, teaches the bodhisattva the śrāvaka's own twelve divisions of scripture." A certain sūtra says: "The Mahāyāna and Hīnayāna each are complete with all twelve divisions. If one accepts that six divisions run through both the Mahāyāna and Hīnayāna, one does not accept that the other six fail to run through both alike." Examining this passage, both the great and the small alike possess all twelve divisions — it is simply that, spoken of from within the Hīnayāna's own standpoint, this is not the sense intended by the Mahāyāna. Hence, in the particular sense, three are set aside and nine retained.

何者？小乘灰斷，無如意珠身，故無廣經。假令以法空為廣說之文，小乘根鈍說必假緣，無天鼓任鳴，少無問自說，雖有授記，記作佛少。又《涅槃》第七云：「九部中，不明佛性，是人無罪。」例此而言，十二部中不明佛性，是人有罪。有人言：大乘九部，除因緣、譬喻、論義，大乘人根利，不假此三。斯亦別論。通語大乘，何得無此三經耶？有經言：「小乘但讓廣經一部，有十一部。」無方廣者，大乘說如來是常，一切眾生皆有佛性。正理為方，包富為廣。又理融無二，亦名為等。聲聞中所無，但十一部耳。若言小乘定有九，不應復有十一部；既取十一，亦通有十二。為緣別說，或讓三、或讓一，以判大小乘(云云)。

Why is this? The Hīnayāna, its ashes and cessation final, has no wish-fulfilling-jewel body, and hence has no vaipulya text. Even granting that the emptiness of dharmas could be taken as an expanded teaching in its own right, the Hīnayāna's own dull faculties mean its teaching always requires some occasioning condition, with no heavenly drum sounding of itself, little spontaneous unprompted utterance, and, though prediction is present, prediction of buddhahood is rare. Again, the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, in its seventh fascicle, says: "Among the nine divisions, buddha-nature is not clarified — such a person bears no fault for this." By this same pattern, then: among the twelve divisions, buddha-nature is not clarified — such a person does bear fault for this. Some say: the Mahāyāna's own nine divisions exclude causal narrative, parable, and doctrinal discussion, since the Mahāyāna practitioner's own sharp faculties require none of these three. This, too, is a particular account. Discussed in the general sense, the Mahāyāna, how could it possibly lack these three sūtras? A certain sūtra says: "The Hīnayāna sets aside only vaipulya, and possesses eleven divisions." As for lacking vaipulya: the Mahāyāna teaches that the Tathāgata is eternal, and that every sentient being possesses buddha-nature — correct principle is "square," and abundant inclusiveness is "broad." Again, principle fused without duality is likewise named "equal." What the śrāvaka's own teaching lacks is only these eleven divisions. If it were said that the Hīnayāna definitely possesses only nine, it ought not, then, possess these eleven divisions as well; and, since eleven is granted, twelve, too, is generally possessed. According to the differing conditions being addressed, sometimes three are set aside, sometimes only one — this is how the great and the small vehicles are judged, and so on.

Reading note

Zhiyi's argument here follows the same "reject the one-sided count" pattern already used elsewhere in this treatise (the twelve-hundred/eight-hundred merit asymmetry among the six faculties, closed just above in the wondrous of spiritual powers): naming three or nine or eleven or twelve as the Hīnayāna's own count are all "particular accounts," while the general truth is that both vehicles possess the full twelve, differing only in emphasis and depth of realization rather than in raw genre. The single sharpest textual crux is vaipulya, which the Hīnayāna genuinely lacks — not because its own sūtras could never touch on emptiness (the Tattvasiddhi's own reading of emptiness as itself vaipulya is granted), but because the Hīnayāna practitioner, having attained "ashes and cessation," has no wish-fulfilling-jewel body left to speak from at that further register at all. This closes 卷第六上 of the source text.

Clarifying Its Difference According to the Condition Met 明對緣同異

Chinese Source

三、明對緣有異者，緣即是十因緣法所成眾生，而此眾生皆有十界根性，熟者先感。佛知成熟、未成熟者，應不失時。若眾生解脫緣未熟，不可全棄，對此機緣，止作人天乘說，不作脩多羅等名。故天竺外典，無十二部名，亦無其意。此間儒道，亦無斯名，意義皆闕。若法身為王，示十善道，亦不濫用此名。故《地持》中說種性菩薩，能自熟又能熟他。有二乘種性及佛種性者，隨法熟之，無種性者，以善趣熟。善趣熟者，即是其義。種性熟者，如下說。若深觀行者，妙得其意，以邪相入正相，用無礙辯，約邪經外典，作十二部義，胡為不得？而非正對緣說也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the differences according to the condition met: "condition" here refers to the sentient beings brought about by the ten links of dependent origination, and these sentient beings all possess the roots and natures of all ten dharma-realms — whichever is ripened is responded to first. The Buddha, knowing which is ripened and which is not yet ripened, responds without ever missing the moment. If a sentient being's own condition for liberation is not yet ripened, they cannot simply be abandoned entirely — matched against this particular capacity and condition, only the human-and-heavenly vehicle is taught, without using the name "sūtra proper" and the rest at all. Hence, the non-Buddhist texts of India possess no name for the twelve divisions, nor any sense of them; and the Confucian and Daoist traditions here in this land, too, possess no such name, both the name and the sense entirely lacking. Even where the Dharma-body, acting as a king, teaches the ten wholesome paths, this name, too, is not applied indiscriminately. Hence the Bodhisattvabhūmi speaks of the bodhisattva of lineage-nature, able both to ripen himself and to ripen others. Whoever possesses the lineage-nature of the two vehicles or of buddhahood is ripened according to that same dharma; whoever possesses no lineage-nature at all is ripened through the wholesome destinies. Being ripened through the wholesome destinies is precisely this same sense; being ripened through the lineage-nature is as explained below. If one contemplates and practices deeply, wondrously grasping this same sense, one may enter the correct mark by way of the deviant mark, and, with unobstructed eloquence, construct the sense of the twelve divisions even from deviant scriptures and non-Buddhist texts — why should this not be possible? Yet this is not the correct teaching matched directly against the condition itself.

次約十因緣所成眾生，有小乘根性，對此機說通，則十二；別則或九、或十一(云云)。若對十因緣所成眾生，有菩薩機者，不作別說，但明十二部經。

今總論如來對四緣，說十二部法，有兩種四教不同：

一者、就隱、顯共論四教：隱即祕密教，顯即頓、漸、不定教。祕密既隱，非世流布，此置而不論。若對四法界眾生，通說十二部，別說或九部、或十一部，名漸法說也。若對兩法界眾生，通說十二部，此說頓法也。或對四法界，或對兩法界，或作別說、或但通說，此說不定法也。

Next, in terms of the sentient beings brought about by the ten links of dependent origination who possess the Hīnayāna's own roots and nature: matched against this capacity, taught in the general sense, this makes twelve; taught in the particular sense, this makes either nine or eleven, and so on. As for the sentient beings brought about by the ten links of dependent origination who possess the bodhisattva's own capacity: no particular account is given for them — only the twelve divisions of scripture in general are clarified.

Here, discussing in general how the Tathāgata, matched against the four kinds of condition, teaches the twelve-fold dharma: there are two differing fourfold schemes of teaching involved.

First, discussed jointly in terms of the hidden and the revealed, as a fourfold scheme: the hidden is the esoteric teaching, and the revealed is the sudden, the gradual, and the indeterminate teachings. Since the esoteric teaching is hidden, and not circulated openly in the world, it is set aside here and not discussed. Matched against sentient beings of all four dharma-realms, the twelve divisions are taught in the general sense, while, in the particular sense, either nine or eleven divisions are taught — this is named the gradual teaching. Matched against sentient beings of only two dharma-realms, the twelve divisions are taught in the general sense — this is the sudden teaching. Whether matched against four dharma-realms or against only two, whether taught in the particular sense or only in the general sense — this is the indeterminate teaching.

二者直就顯露漸教中更明四教者，即是三藏、通、別、圓也。三藏教直對三法界，別說或九、或十一。通教對四法界，通說十二部法也。別教對兩法界，通說十二部法。圓教對一法界，通說十二部法。前以無記化化禪，與諸慈悲合，示現身輪：或為國師、道士、儒林之宗，父母、兄弟，乃至猴猨鹿馬，同事利益不可稱說。今口輪說者，例如前用諸慈悲，熏無記化化禪，種種不同，百千萬法不可說。不可說故，龍宮象負，渧海研山，八萬四千法藏，不可窮盡。雖復無崖，以十二部往收，罄無不盡也。

Second, discussed directly within the revealed, gradual teaching, further clarifying its own fourfold scheme: this is precisely the Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, and perfect teachings. The Tripiṭaka teaching is matched directly against three dharma-realms, taught in the particular sense as either nine or eleven. The shared teaching is matched against four dharma-realms, teaching the twelve divisions in the general sense. The distinct teaching is matched against two dharma-realms, teaching the twelve divisions in the general sense. The perfect teaching is matched against a single dharma-realm, teaching the twelve divisions in the general sense. Discussed above, the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, joined with every form of compassion, manifests the wheel of the body — becoming, at times, a state preceptor, a Daoist master, a leading figure among Confucian scholars, a parent, a sibling, and even a monkey, an ape, a deer, or a horse, sharing in the same undertaking to bring benefit in ways beyond enumeration. As for what is spoken through the wheel of speech: following this same pattern as before, every form of compassion, perfuming the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, differs in kind without end — a hundred thousand myriad dharmas, beyond what words can express. Being beyond expression, though the dragon-palace's own elephant-borne scriptures, and the drop taken from the ocean, the speck ground from the mountain, and the eighty-four thousand stores of the Dharma, can never be exhausted — even without any limit, gathered together by means of the twelve divisions, they are fully collected, with nothing left out.

Reading note

Zhiyi applies two independent fourfold classifications to the same body of teaching in sequence — first the broader esoteric/sudden/gradual/indeterminate scheme (with the esoteric set aside as beyond discussion, following this treatise's standing convention), then, within the revealed teaching's own gradual branch, the familiar Tripiṭaka/shared/distinct/perfect scheme — so that the same twelve-part apparatus just built up in the previous section is shown flexing to match every possible capacity, from the human-and-heavenly vehicle (which does not even rise to using the name "sūtra" at all) up through the perfect teaching's own single dharma-realm. The closing image — the dragon-palace's own scriptures, a drop from the ocean, a speck ground from a mountain, all standing for what remains uncollected and inexhaustible beyond the eighty-four thousand stores of the Dharma already gathered — makes the same point about scale that this treatise has made before about the wondrous of spiritual powers: what looks like an exhaustive taxonomy is itself only ever a skillful, provisional gathering-in of something genuinely without limit.

Judging What Is Expressed 明所詮

Chinese Source

Fourth, judging what is expressed: were this sense examined in full detail, it belongs within the discussion of the meaning of the Four Teachings; here, only its expressive sense is briefly introduced. If the human-and-heavenly vehicle is taught, this expresses only the conceivable conventional truth within the three realms, and never expresses the real truth at all. As for those taught by the gradual teaching, whether particularly through nine or eleven divisions, or generally through all twelve: at the outset, it correctly expresses the conceivable conventional truth, and incidentally expresses the conceivable real truth; in the middle, it correctly expresses the conceivable real truth, and incidentally expresses the conceivable conventional truth; toward the end, it correctly expresses the inconceivable real truth, and incidentally expresses the inconceivable conventional truth — down through jointly expressing both the inconceivable real and conventional truths together, and so on. As for those taught through the indeterminate teaching, what is expressed here cannot be fixed and judged at all.

As for clarifying what is expressed in terms of the Four Teachings within the gradual teaching itself: the Tripiṭaka teaching correctly expresses the conceivable real, and incidentally expresses the conceivable conventional. As for the Tripiṭaka teaching's own bodhisattva, it correctly expresses the conceivable conventional, and incidentally expresses the conceivable real. As for the shared teaching's own two vehicles, it correctly expresses the conceivable real, and incidentally expresses the conceivable conventional. As for the shared teaching's own bodhisattva at the initial mind, this is the same as the two vehicles; as for the bodhisattva at the later mind, it correctly expresses the conventional, and incidentally expresses the real. As for the distinct teaching's own initial mind, it correctly expresses the real and conventional within the three realms, and incidentally expresses the real and conventional beyond the three realms. As for the middle mind, it correctly expresses the real and conventional beyond the three realms, and incidentally expresses the real and conventional within the three realms. As for the later mind, it jointly expresses the real and conventional both within and beyond the three realms together. As for the perfect teaching, at the initial, middle, and later mind alike, it perfectly expresses the inconceivable real and conventional beyond the three realms, and so on.

Reading note

The same real/conventional (真/俗) pairing that has structured this treatise's account of the two truths since Fascicle 1 is applied here specifically to what a given body of teaching primarily versus secondarily expresses, tracked across each teaching's own internal development from initial to later mind: the shared teaching's bodhisattva starts out expressing exactly what the two vehicles express before pivoting, at the later mind, to correctly expressing the conventional instead — a small but precise marker of how the shared teaching's own bodhisattva diverges from the śrāvaka only gradually, not from the very first moment of practice. The distinct teaching's three-stage progression (within the three realms → beyond them → both jointly) traces the same "resource-and-accomplishment, then contemplative illumination, then real nature" sequential structure already established for the distinct teaching's own three tracks back in Fascicle 5, now applied to the question of what its own teaching expresses at each stage of a practitioner's path.

一、就理妙者，一切諸法無非中道，無離文字而說解脫。文字性離即是解脫，一切所說即理而妙。譬如龍雨雨，而處處不同，或水、或火、或刀杖，理亦如是，理具情乖順耳，乖故為麁，理順為妙。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous: this carries five senses — first, in terms of principle; second, in terms of language; third, in terms of what is expressed; fourth, in terms of every other sūtra; fifth, in terms of the present sūtra directly.

First, as for the wondrous in terms of principle: not a single dharma is anything other than the middle way, and there is no liberation spoken of apart from the written word itself. The written word's own nature, once free of itself, is itself already liberation — everything taught is itself principle, and is itself wondrous. It is comparable to the dragon's own rain, falling everywhere differently — here as water, there as fire, elsewhere as swords and staves — principle is exactly the same: principle is fully present throughout, yet feeling either turns against it or accords with it — turning against it is why it is coarse, and according with it is why it is wondrous.

二、約言辭者，如佛得道夜至泥洹夕，常說般若，常說中道，而一音演法，隨類異解。一音巧說，是則為妙；異類殊解，自有麁妙。

三、就所詮者，若對六道眾生說人天乘者，此詮有為，能詮、所詮俱麁。若對鈍根三藏五門，詮於生滅四諦理，此則能詮、所詮俱麁。若通教體法五門，比於三藏析門，體門能詮雖巧，而所詮猶是真諦，所詮亦麁。若別教五門，能詮為麁，所詮中道為妙。若圓教五門，能詮、所詮俱皆是妙也。

Second, as for language: as the Buddha, from the very night of attaining the path through the very evening of final nirvāṇa, constantly taught the prajñā-teaching and constantly taught the middle way, and yet expounded the Dharma with a single sound, understood differently according to each kind of listener — this single sound, skillfully proclaimed, is itself the wondrous; the differing kinds of listeners, understanding it differently, carry their own distinction of coarse and wondrous.

Third, as for what is expressed: if the human-and-heavenly vehicle is taught to sentient beings of the six destinies, this expresses only the conditioned, and both what expresses and what is expressed are coarse alike. If, for dull-faculty practitioners, the Tripiṭaka's own five gates express the principle of the Four Noble Truths of generation-and-extinction, this, too, has both what expresses and what is expressed coarse alike. If the shared teaching's own five gates of embodying dharmas, compared to the Tripiṭaka's own gate of analysis, though what expresses is more skillful, what is expressed still remains the real truth alone — what is expressed, too, remains coarse. If the distinct teaching's own five gates, what expresses is coarse, while what is expressed, the middle way, is wondrous. If the perfect teaching's own five gates, both what expresses and what is expressed are, alike, wondrous.

四、就眾經者，《華嚴》詮別、詮圓。三藏詮偏。方等四種詮。

《般若》三種詮。《法華》唯一詮。又諸經詮妙，與《法華》不異，而帶麁詮，麁詮不得合妙，是故為麁；《法華》不爾，佛平等說，如一味雨。正直捨方便，但說無上道，純是一詮。又云：昔毀呰聲聞，而佛實以大乘教化。又云：「汝等所行，是菩薩道。」此則融麁令妙。如此兩意，異於眾經，是故言妙。譬如良醫，能變毒為藥。二乘根敗，不能反復，名之為毒。今經得記，即是變毒為藥。故《論》云：「餘經非祕密，法華為祕密」也。復有本地圓說，諸經所無，在後當廣明(云云)。

Fourth, as for every other sūtra: the Avataṃsaka expresses the distinct and expresses the perfect. The Tripiṭaka expresses only the one-sided. The vaipulya teachings express in four ways.

The perfection-of-wisdom teachings express in three ways. The Lotus Sūtra expresses in a single way alone. Again, though what every other sūtra expresses as wondrous is no different from the Lotus Sūtra's own, it carries an admixture of the coarse alongside what it expresses, and this coarse expression cannot be joined to the wondrous — hence, it remains coarse. The Lotus Sūtra is not like this: the Buddha teaches with perfect equality, like a single rain falling with one flavor. Directly and without any expedient means, only the unsurpassed path is taught, purely a single expression alone. Again, it is said: formerly, the śrāvaka was disparaged, and yet the Buddha, in truth, always taught through the Mahāyāna. Again, it is said: "What you yourselves have practiced is itself the bodhisattva path" — this, then, is fusing the coarse and making it wondrous. These two senses together differ from every other sūtra — hence, it alone is called wondrous. It is comparable to a skilled physician, able to transform poison itself into medicine. The two vehicles' own roots being ruined, unable to be reversed, this is named poison. The present sūtra's bestowing of prediction is precisely transforming poison into medicine. Hence the Treatise says: "Every other sūtra is not the secret treasury; the Lotus Sūtra alone is the secret treasury." There is, further, the origin-ground's own perfect teaching, which every other sūtra lacks, to be broadly clarified later on, and so on.

五、正就此經明妙十二部者，如脩多羅名直說，今經直說中道佛之智慧；不說六道、二乘、菩薩等法，唯說佛法，故直說為妙。

祇夜妙者，重頌長行中道之說耳，故知祇夜亦妙。

伽陀者，如龍女獻珠，喜見說偈，孤然特起。此偈明於剎那頃便成正覺，稱歎於佛成菩提事。喜見孤起，歎佛容顏甚奇妙，故知孤起伽陀妙也。

Fifth, directly clarifying, in terms of this present sūtra, the wondrousness of all twelve divisions: as sūtra proper is named for its own direct statement, the present sūtra directly states the middle way, the Buddha's own wisdom — it does not teach the dharma of the six destinies, the two vehicles, or the bodhisattva as such, but teaches the buddha-dharma alone — hence, its own direct statement is wondrous.

As for geya being wondrous: this simply restates in verse the same teaching of the middle way already given in prose — one should therefore know that geya, too, is wondrous.

As for gāthā: as when the dragon girl offers her jewel, and Sarvasattvapriyadarśana speaks a verse, arising all on its own, unprompted — this verse clarifies attaining correct enlightenment within the very span of a single instant, praising the Buddha's own accomplishment of bodhi. Sarvasattvapriyadarśana's verse arising unprompted, praising the Buddha's own countenance as truly rare and wondrous — one should therefore know that gāthā arising unprompted is wondrous.

本事妙者，即是二萬佛所教無上道，不教餘事，即是本事妙也。

本生妙者，明十六王子，生身生為王子，法身生為佛子，即是本生妙也。

因緣者，結緣覆講大乘，繫珠不論小乘人天等緣，是名因緣妙也。

未曾有妙者，天華地動，二眉間光，三變土田等，是不可思議未曾有事妙也。

譬喻妙者，經題以法譬為名，譬於開三顯一，何曾譬於餘事？即譬喻妙也。

As for past events being wondrous: this is precisely the twenty thousand buddhas who taught only the unsurpassed path, and taught nothing else at all — this is precisely past events being wondrous.

As for birth-stories being wondrous: clarifying the sixteen princes — their birth-body born as princes, and their Dharma-body born as buddha's children — this is precisely birth-stories being wondrous.

As for causal narrative: forming a bond, hidden within an earlier lecture on the Mahāyāna, sewing a jewel into the robe, without discussing any Hīnayāna, human, or heavenly condition — this is named causal narrative being wondrous.

As for the marvel being wondrous: heavenly flowers, the earth shaking, light from between the two brows, the threefold transformation of the land, and the rest — these are the wondrous, inconceivable, marvelous matters.

As for parable being wondrous: the sūtra's own title takes its name from a dharma and a parable together, illustrating the opening of the three to reveal the one — when has it ever illustrated anything else at all? This is precisely parable being wondrous.

優波提舍妙者，身子問佛，佛答諸佛智慧門。龍女、智積問答，論法華事。智積云：「我見釋迦經無量劫，方成菩提。不信此女，須臾成佛。」此執別疑圓。龍女云：「佛自證知。」以圓珠獻佛，此以圓答別。此即提舍妙也。

無問妙者，如文云：「無問而自說，稱歎所行道，從三昧安詳而起，告舍利弗，說佛智慧。」又「宿世因緣，吾今當說」，即是無問妙也。

授記妙者，授三根佛記，皆安住實智中，為天人所敬，即授記妙也。

方廣妙者，其車高廣，智慧深遠等，即是方廣妙也。

As for upadeśa being wondrous: Śāriputra questions the Buddha, and the Buddha answers concerning the very gateway of every buddha's own wisdom. The dragon girl and Prajñākūṭa question and answer one another, discussing the very matter of the Lotus Sūtra itself. Prajñākūṭa says: "I have seen Śākyamuni cultivate for immeasurable eons before finally accomplishing bodhi — I do not believe this girl could attain buddhahood in a mere instant." This clings to the distinct teaching, doubting the perfect. The dragon girl says: "The Buddha himself bears witness and knows" — offering her round jewel to the Buddha, this answers the distinct teaching by means of the perfect. This is precisely upadeśa being wondrous.

As for spontaneous, unprompted utterance being wondrous: as the text says, "Without being asked, he spoke of his own accord, praising the path he himself had practiced, rising calmly from his own samādhi, addressing Śāriputra, and teaching the Buddha's own wisdom." And again: "The causal narrative of past ages — I shall now speak of it" — this is precisely spontaneous, unprompted utterance being wondrous.

As for prediction being wondrous: bestowing upon all three roots of capacity the prediction of buddhahood, every one of them abiding securely within real wisdom, honored by devas and humans alike — this is precisely prediction being wondrous.

As for vaipulya being wondrous: "this carriage, tall and broad," "wisdom, deep and far-reaching," and the rest — this is precisely vaipulya being wondrous.

當知此經，從初直說，乃至優波提舍，十二意足，而皆是妙，此即待麁明說法妙也。

開麁顯妙者，昔十二、十一、九部不說實者；今無別實，異昔不實。昔但言廣，不明理廣；今開言廣，即理廣也。開昔之異，顯今之同，即是絕待明說法妙也。

One should therefore know that this present sūtra, from its own direct statement at the outset all the way through upadeśa, is complete in all twelve senses, every one of them wondrous — this is precisely clarifying the wondrous of teaching the Dharma in the relative sense, set against the coarse.

As for opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous: the twelve, eleven, and nine divisions of former times did not teach the real; here, there is no separate real apart from them, differing from their former unreality. Formerly, only the expanded was spoken of, without clarifying the expanded in principle; here, the expanded already spoken of is opened, and is itself already the expanded in principle. Opening the difference of former times, revealing the sameness of the present — this is precisely clarifying the wondrous of teaching the Dharma in the absolute sense.

Reading note

Zhiyi closes the wondrous of teaching the Dharma by running the coarse/wondrous verdict five times over — principle, language, what is expressed, every other sūtra, and finally this sūtra directly — each pass narrowing the focus until the fifth returns to the twelve-division apparatus built up across this whole section and finds every one of the twelve, without exception, wondrous when read in the Lotus Sūtra's own case: sūtra proper because it states the buddha-dharma directly rather than any lesser vehicle, gāthā because Sarvasattvapriyadarśana's own unprompted verse arises "all on its own," upadeśa because the dragon girl's question-and-answer with Prajñākūṭa resolves a doubt about the distinct teaching by means of the perfect. The poison-into-medicine simile for the śrāvaka's own prediction of buddhahood — their "roots ruined, unable to be reversed" by any ordinary means, transformed instead through this sūtra's own bestowal — gives concrete stakes to what could otherwise read as a purely formal exercise in classification. This closes the wondrous of teaching the Dharma (說法妙) in its entirety, the eighth of the ten wondrous aspects.

The Wondrous of the Retinue 明眷屬妙

Chinese Source

○第九、眷屬妙者，就此為五：一、明來意。二、明眷屬。三、明麁妙。四、明法門。五、辨觀心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, clarifying the wondrous of the retinue: this is treated under five headings — first, clarifying why this comes next; second, clarifying the retinue itself; third, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous; fourth, clarifying the Dharma-gate; fifth, distinguishing the contemplation of mind.

Why This Comes Next 明來意

Chinese Source

所言次第者，若無說而已，說必被緣，緣即受道人也。已受道故，即成眷屬。譬如父母遺體，攬此成身，得為天性。天性親愛，故名眷；更相臣順，故名屬。行者亦爾，受戒之時，說此戒法，授於前人，前人聽聞，即得發戒，師、弟所由生也。禪亦如是，授安心法，如教修行，即得發定，是為我師，我是弟子。慧亦如是，說諸法門，轉入人心，由法成親。親故則信，信故則順，是名眷屬也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for what is meant by "sequence": if there were no teaching at all, this would be the end of the matter, but teaching necessarily reaches some condition, and this condition is precisely the person who receives the path. Having already received the path, this person accordingly becomes a member of the retinue. It is comparable to a parent and child, sharing the very substance passed down, taken up to form the body — this becomes their very nature. Being close through nature is why it is named "kin"; being, further, mutually deferential is why it is named "belonging." The practitioner is exactly the same: at the moment of receiving the precepts, this precept-dharma is taught, bestowed upon the person before one, and the person before one, hearing it, thereby brings forth the precepts — this is how teacher and disciple come to arise. Dhyāna is exactly the same: bestowing the method for settling the mind, and, practicing as taught, one thereby brings forth concentration — this one becomes my teacher, and I become the disciple. Wisdom is exactly the same: teaching the various Dharma-gates, turning and entering into another's mind — through the Dharma, closeness is formed. Being close, trust follows; trust following, deference follows — this is named the retinue.

他土餘根皆利，隨所用塵起之，令他得益。此土耳根利故，偏用聲塵。故二萬佛時，教無上道。十六王子，覆講《法華》，從是已來，恒為眷屬。世世與師俱生，或人天眷屬，或三乘眷屬，或一乘眷屬。故身子云：「今日乃知真是佛子。」昔教五人得真無漏，名佛子。菩薩不發真，名為外人。於《法華》發大乘解，自稱昔日非真佛子。今說一實之道：從聞、悟、解，法身得生。從佛口生，是聞慧中法身生。從法化生，是思慧中法身生。得佛法分，是修慧中法身生。三慧成就，是真佛子，定於天性，得成眷屬。故次說法之後，而明眷屬妙也。

In other lands, every other faculty, too, is sharp, and, according to whichever sense-object is used, this benefit is likewise brought forth for others. In this land, the ear-faculty alone is sharp, and hence sound is used preferentially. Hence, at the time of the twenty thousand buddhas, the unsurpassed path was taught; the sixteen princes, restating the Lotus Sūtra, have, from that time onward, constantly remained this same retinue, life after life born together with their own teacher — sometimes as a retinue of humans and devas, sometimes as a retinue of the three vehicles, sometimes as a retinue of the one vehicle. Hence Śāriputra says: "Only today do I know that I am truly a child of the Buddha." Formerly, the five men were taught and attained the real, taintless state, and were named children of the Buddha; the bodhisattvas, not yet having given rise to the real, were named outsiders. Within the Lotus Sūtra, giving rise to Mahāyāna understanding, they themselves proclaim that, in former times, they were not truly children of the Buddha. Here, the path of the one reality is taught: from hearing, awakening, and understanding, the Dharma-body is thereby brought forth. Born from the Buddha's own mouth, this is the Dharma-body born within the wisdom of hearing. Born through the Dharma's own transformation, this is the Dharma-body born within the wisdom of reflection. Attaining a share of the buddha-dharma, this is the Dharma-body born within the wisdom of cultivation. The three kinds of wisdom accomplished, this is the true child of the Buddha, fixed within this very nature, thereby coming to form the retinue. Hence, following directly after teaching the Dharma, the wondrous of the retinue is next clarified.

Clarifying the Retinue Itself 明眷屬

Chinese Source

二、明眷屬者，又為五種：一、明理性眷屬。二、明業生眷屬。三、明願生眷屬。四、明神通生眷屬。五、明應生眷屬。

一、理性眷屬者，眾生如佛如，一如無二如，理性相關，任運是子。故云：「我亦如是，眾聖中尊，世間之父。一切眾生，皆是吾子。」此是理性，不關結緣、不結緣，皆是佛子也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the retinue itself: this is further of five kinds — first, clarifying the retinue in principle; second, clarifying the retinue born through karma; third, clarifying the retinue born through vow; fourth, clarifying the retinue born through spiritual power; fifth, clarifying the retinue born through response.

First, as for the retinue in principle: sentient beings' own suchness and the Buddha's own suchness are the identical suchness, one suchness without a second — hinging on this same principle-nature, one is spontaneously already his child. Hence it is said: "I, too, am exactly this way — honored among every sage, father to the whole world — every sentient being, without exception, is my own child." This is spoken of in terms of principle-nature, and has nothing to do with whether a bond has been formed or not — every one, alike, is a child of the Buddha.

二、業生眷屬者，但眾生理論皆子，而飲他毒藥，有失心者、不失心者。不失心者，拜跪問訊，求索救護，與藥即服，故於大通覆講，說《妙法華》，得結大乘父子。其失心者，雖與良藥而不肯服，流浪生死，逃逝他國。即起方便：或作三藏結緣，說生滅之法；或作通教結緣，說無生之法；或作別教結緣，說不生生恒沙佛法；或作圓教結緣，說不生不生一實相法。若信、若謗，因倒、因起，如喜根雖謗，後要得度。結緣已後，以二十五三昧為二十五有，說三諦法而成熟之。或於中間得度、或于今未度，雖復得度、未度，皆是眷屬。今三藏佛，於分段國，出家成道。往日三藏之緣，或得度、或未得度。得度之者，灰身滅智，不復論生緣。未度者，來牽分段：昔殷重信順，今為親識受道；昔汎汎信順，今為疎外受道；昔時拒謗，今為怨家受道。甘露初降，先得服甞，早斷分段，得出生死。如大象捍群，俱證解脫，如五佛子之流，雖是異姓，則是法親內眷屬也。若不得道，雖是宗族，名外眷屬。佛於其人，則無利益。若入滅度，不復更生，此人緣盡，傳付後佛也。

Second, as for the retinue born through karma: though, discussed in terms of principle, every sentient being is already his child, they have drunk another's own poison, and some have thereby lost their own minds, while others have not. Those who have not lost their minds bow and kneel, inquire and pay respect, seek out rescue and protection, and, given the medicine, take it at once — hence, at the time of Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū, restating and teaching the wondrous Lotus Dharma, they formed the very bond of father and child within the Mahāyāna. Those who have lost their minds, though given the excellent medicine, refuse to take it, wandering adrift through birth-and-death, fleeing into another land. The Buddha thereupon sets forth skillful means: sometimes forming a bond through the Tripiṭaka teaching, teaching the dharma of generation-and-extinction; sometimes forming a bond through the shared teaching, teaching the dharma of no-generation; sometimes forming a bond through the distinct teaching, teaching dharmas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges through generation-without-generation; sometimes forming a bond through the perfect teaching, teaching the one real mark's dharma of generation-without-generation-without-generation. Whether through faith or through slander, whether through being overturned or through being raised up — as with Priyaṅkara, who, though he slandered the Dharma, was in the end still delivered — once the bond has been formed, the twenty-five samādhis are matched to the twenty-five states of existence, and the dharma of the three truths is taught, ripening them accordingly. Some are delivered somewhere in between, and some remain undelivered even now — yet, whether already delivered or not yet delivered, all alike are this same retinue. The present Tripiṭaka buddha, within a land of segmented birth-and-death, goes forth from home and attains the path. Those bonded with him through the Tripiṭaka teaching in former days are either already delivered or not yet delivered. Those already delivered, their ashes and cognition extinguished, are no longer discussed in terms of any further birth-condition. Those not yet delivered continue to be drawn along through segmented birth-and-death: those who, formerly, believed and deferred earnestly, now receive the path as close kin; those who, formerly, believed and deferred only casually, now receive the path as distant outsiders; those who, formerly, resisted and slandered, now receive the path as enemies. The sweet dew, first descending, is tasted first by them — cutting off segmented birth-and-death early, they are thereby able to emerge from birth-and-death itself. Like the great elephant leading its own herd, all together bearing witness to liberation — as with the lineage of the five children of the Buddha, though of a different clan-name, they are nonetheless kin within the Dharma, the inner retinue. If they do not attain the path, though they belong to the same lineage, they are named the outer retinue — for such a person, the Buddha's own teaching brings no benefit. Once he enters final extinction, never to be reborn again, this person's own bond is exhausted, and is handed on to a later buddha.

三、願眷屬者，先世結緣，雖未斷苦，願生內眷屬中、或怨家等，因之受道。若得道者，成法內眷屬；不得者成，法外眷屬。若佛滅度，此人無益，傳付後佛也。

四、神通眷屬者，若先世值佛，發真見諦，生猶未盡，或在上界，或在他方。今佛，分段作佛：或以願力、或以通力來生下界；或為親、中、怨，輔佛行化，斷餘殘惑，而出三界。若殘惑未盡，值佛入滅，亦自能斷，或待後佛(云云)。三藏不說界外生，今以大乘意望之。昔值佛得度，三界生盡，受變易身，緣牽分段，非是業生，但是願通。願通云何異？約自報力名神通，約教名誓願。神通生者，本受報處，猶有報身，以身通力，分形來此；若願生者，報處無身，願力下生耳。三藏不說斷結，誓願受生死身，不約此教論願；通教則有誓扶餘習而生分段，依通明願，於義為便。此等未得法身，故全無應生眷屬。三藏眷屬竟。

Third, as for the retinue born through vow: having formed a bond in a former life, though suffering has not yet been severed, one vows to be born within this retinue, whether as close kin or even as an enemy, and, through this, receives the path. Whoever attains the path becomes the inner retinue within the Dharma; whoever does not attain it becomes the outer retinue of the Dharma. Once the Buddha enters final extinction, this person receives no further benefit, and is handed on to a later buddha.

Fourth, as for the retinue born through spiritual power: if, in a former life, one encountered the Buddha, gave rise to the real, seeing the truths directly, and yet one's own births are not yet exhausted, whether in the higher realms or in another region — the present buddha, becoming a buddha within segmented birth-and-death, either through the power of vow or through the power of spiritual transformation, comes to be born within the lower realm — becoming, as close kin, middling acquaintance, or enemy, assists the Buddha's own teaching and transformation, severing whatever residual confusion remains, and thereby emerges from the three realms. If residual confusion is not yet exhausted upon encountering the Buddha's own entry into extinction, one is nonetheless able to sever it oneself, or else awaits a later buddha, and so on. The Tripiṭaka teaching does not speak of birth beyond the three realms — here, this is viewed through the Mahāyāna's own sense. Those who, formerly, encountered a buddha and were delivered, their own births within the three realms exhausted, receive a body of transformational birth-and-death, and are drawn along by this same condition into segmented birth-and-death — this is not birth through karma, but simply through vow or through spiritual power. How, then, does vow differ from spiritual power? Named in terms of one's own retributive power, this is spiritual power; named in terms of the teaching itself, this is the vow. As for birth through spiritual power: within the very place one's own retribution was originally received, a reward-body still remains, and, through this body's own transformative power, one's own form is divided and comes here. As for birth through vow: within the place of retribution, no body remains at all, and, through the power of the vow alone, one descends to be born here. The Tripiṭaka teaching does not speak of severing the fetters through a vow to receive a body of birth-and-death — the vow is not discussed in terms of this teaching at all; the shared teaching, however, does have the vow to sustain what residual habituation remains and thereby be born within segmented birth-and-death — relying on the shared teaching to clarify the vow fits the sense well. Since these teachings have not yet attained the Dharma-body, they entirely lack any retinue born through response at all. This closes the Tripiṭaka teaching's own retinue.

往昔結無生緣者，或已得道、或未得道，佛於分段作佛。未得道者，當處即有業生；上界向下，即有願通兩生。分別願通，如前說。橫從他土來者，即有願通；竪從方便來者，亦有願通。通教未得法身，故無應生眷屬也。

往昔曾結別教緣者，中間同事說法，種種教詔，成熟未熟。佛今在分段作佛。未得道者。當處即有業生；上界向下，得有願通。橫從他方，得有願通。竪從方便來，亦有願通。竪從實報來者，得有應生，無明先破，已得法身之本，能起應入生死。此則異前(云云)。

往昔結圓教緣者，中間調熟，或得道、或未得道，今於分段作佛。

先緣牽來，差別不同。若未得道，當處有一。上界向下，有二。他方橫來，有二。方便來，亦二。實報來，有一。例如前(云云)。

As for those who, in former times, formed a bond through the dharma of no-generation: whether they have already attained the path or not yet attained it, the Buddha becomes a buddha within segmented birth-and-death. As for those not yet having attained the path: right where they are, there is birth through karma; and, from the higher realms descending, there is birth through both vow and spiritual power. The distinction between vow and spiritual power is as explained before. Coming horizontally from another land, there is birth through vow and spiritual power; coming vertically from the expedient-means realm, there is likewise birth through vow and spiritual power. Since the shared teaching has not yet attained the Dharma-body, it, too, lacks any retinue born through response.

As for those who, in former times, formed a bond through the distinct teaching: in the interval, sharing in the same undertaking, teaching the Dharma, giving every kind of instruction, some ripened and some not yet ripened. The Buddha now becomes a buddha within segmented birth-and-death. As for those not yet having attained the path: right where they are, there is birth through karma; from the higher realms descending, there is birth through vow and spiritual power; coming horizontally from another region, there is birth through vow and spiritual power; coming vertically from the expedient-means realm, there is likewise birth through vow and spiritual power. As for those coming vertically from the realm of real reward: there is, further, birth through response — ignorance already broken through beforehand, the very root of the Dharma-body already attained, one is able to give rise to a response and enter birth-and-death. This differs from what came before, and so on.

As for those who, in former times, formed a bond through the perfect teaching: in the interval, brought to maturity, whether they have already attained the path or not yet attained it, the Buddha now becomes a buddha within segmented birth-and-death.

Drawn along by this same former bond, the differences are not all the same. As for those not yet having attained the path: right where they are, there is one kind. From the higher realms descending, there are two kinds. Coming horizontally from another region, there are two kinds. Coming from the expedient-means realm, there are likewise two kinds. Coming from the realm of real reward, there is one kind. This follows the same pattern as before, and so on.

問：
法身惑除理顯，何故受生？

答：
應身受生，其意有三：一為熟他。二為自熟。三為本緣。

一熟他者，秖為業生，善根微弱，不能自發。諸菩薩等，先雖得度，愍彼迷闇，慈力起應入二十五有，而作師導，引諸實行，令向佛所。若得真道，成內眷屬，同於應生。若得似道，同於願通；不得真似，令增進勝業。皆使利益，無有唐捐。如《華嚴》中，說佛初託胎，法身菩薩皆侍衛下生，如陰雲籠月，散降餘胎，為親、中、怨，引諸業者。當知，諸眷屬非生死人。摩耶是千佛之母，淨飯是千佛之父，羅睺羅千佛之子。諸聲聞等，悉內祕外現，示眾有三毒，實自淨佛土，諸親族等，皆是大權法身上地。豈有凡夫，能懷那羅延菩薩耶？

Question: Once confusion is removed and principle is revealed within the Dharma-body, why does one still take on birth?

Answer: The response-body's own taking on of birth carries three senses: first, for the sake of ripening others; second, for the sake of ripening oneself; third, for the sake of one's own original bond.

First, as for ripening others: it is simply that, since birth through karma comes with a wholesome root too feeble to bring itself forth, various bodhisattvas, though already delivered themselves, take pity on that same confusion and darkness, and, through the power of compassion, give rise to a response, entering the twenty-five states of existence, serving as a guiding teacher, drawing beings toward genuine practice, and turning them toward the Buddha's own presence. If one attains the real path through this, one becomes the inner retinue, the same as birth through response; if one attains only a resembling path, this is the same as birth through vow or spiritual power; and, if one attains neither the real nor its resemblance, one is still caused to advance further in excellent karma. All of this, without exception, brings benefit, none of it ever wasted. As the Avataṃsaka says, when the Buddha first took rebirth in the womb, bodhisattvas of the Dharma-body all attended and guarded his own descent into birth, like a shadowy cloud enveloping the moon, scattering and descending into the remaining wombs as well, becoming close kin, middling acquaintance, or enemy, drawing along those bound by karma. One should therefore know that these members of the retinue are not, in truth, persons caught in birth-and-death at all. Māyā is the mother of a thousand buddhas; Śuddhodana is the father of a thousand buddhas; Rāhula is the child of a thousand buddhas. The various śrāvakas, too, are all, inwardly, secretly accomplished while, outwardly, manifesting an ordinary appearance, showing the assembly the three poisons, while, in reality, purifying their own buddha-land of themselves — every one of these kinsmen and relatives belongs to the great skillful means of the Dharma-body's own higher stage. How, then, could an ordinary being possibly conceive a bodhisattva as mighty as Nārāyaṇa?

復次，外道怨惡，抗拒誹謗，當知皆是法身所為。何者？轉輪小善，出世無怨，豈有無上法王怨仇滿路？若於佛起惡，惡道受罪，何得灼然生生相惱？龍象蹴踏，非驢所堪。調達是賓伽羅菩薩，先世大善知識。阿闍世是不動菩薩。薩遮尼犍是大方便菩薩。波旬是住不思議解脫。故《華嚴》列眾，明諸天、龍、鬼神悉住不可思議法門。如是等，若親、中、怨、好、惡、逆、順，皆是法身，先是法內眷屬，今作應生眷屬。若親、中、怨、好、惡、逆、順，未得法身，先雖結緣，為猶在法外，同稱願業等眷屬。諸餘經典，非不明此權利益眾，咸謂是實內、實外、實好、實惡、實逆、實順，故經云：未曾向人，說如此事。今經，佛自開近權、顯遠實，開諸眷屬迹權、顯本實。故文云：「今當為汝說最實事。」是名應生眷屬，熟他故來也。

Again, the non-Buddhist outsider's own enmity and evil, resisting and slandering — one should know that this, too, is entirely the Dharma-body's own doing. Why is this? Even the small goodness of a wheel-turning king brings no enemy when he goes forth into the world — how, then, could the unsurpassed Dharma-king possibly have enemies filling the very road before him? If someone truly gives rise to hostility against the Buddha, they would receive punishment in an evil destiny — how, then, could there possibly be, so blatantly, life after life of mutual torment? The dragon-elephant's own trampling is not something a mere donkey could ever bear. Devadatta is the bodhisattva Piṅgala, a great spiritual friend from a former life. Ajātaśatru is the bodhisattva Acala. Satyaka Nirgrantha is the bodhisattva of great skillful means. Māra Pāpīyas abides within the inconceivable liberation. Hence, the Avataṃsaka's own listing of the assembly clarifies that every deva, nāga, and spirit alike abides within the inconceivable Dharma-gate. All of these — whether close kin, middling acquaintance, or enemy, whether good, evil, contrary, or compliant — are, every one, the Dharma-body: formerly the inner retinue within the Dharma, now forming the retinue born through response. Whoever, whether close kin, middling acquaintance, or enemy, whether good, evil, contrary, or compliant, has not yet attained the Dharma-body — though a bond was formed with them formerly — remains still outside the Dharma, alike named the retinue of vow, karma, and the rest. Every other sūtra, too, is not without clarifying that this expedient means benefits the assembly, yet each supposes it to be truly inner, truly outer, truly good, truly evil, truly contrary, or truly compliant — hence the sūtra says: "Never before, to anyone, have I spoken of a matter such as this." The present sūtra alone has the Buddha himself open the nearby provisional and reveal the far-off real, opening every member of the retinue's own provisional trace and revealing their own real origin. Hence the text says: "I shall now speak to you of the most real matter." This is named the retinue born through response, come for the sake of ripening others.

二、為自成來者，法身菩薩進道無定，或從生身進道、或從法身進道。故下涌出菩薩云：「我亦自欲得此真淨大法。」〈分別功德品〉中明增道損生，即其義也。

三、為本緣所牽，本從此佛初發道心，亦從此佛住不退地。佛尚自入分段施作佛事，有緣之者何得不來！猶如百川應須朝海，緣牽應生亦復如是。

Second, as for coming for the sake of ripening oneself: the bodhisattva of the Dharma-body has no fixed path of advancement, sometimes advancing along the path from within the birth-body, sometimes advancing along the path from within the Dharma-body. Hence, below, the bodhisattvas who have sprung up from the earth say: "We, too, of our own accord, wish to attain this same real, pure, great Dharma." Within the "Distinguishing Merit" chapter, clarifying the advancement of the path and the diminishing of birth, is precisely this same sense.

Third, as for being drawn along by one's own original bond: originally, it was through this very buddha that one first gave rise to the mind of the path, and, likewise, through this very buddha that one came to abide within the stage of non-retrogression. If the Buddha himself still enters segmented birth-and-death to carry out the buddha's own undertaking, how could one who is bonded to him possibly fail to come? Just as every one of the hundred rivers must, in the end, pay homage to the ocean — being drawn along by this bond to be born through response is exactly the same.

若別說者，業生在分段；願生、通生在方便；應生在寂光。通論一處，具有四種如實報，已得法身，能起應作四種眷屬。就圓結緣者，雖未斷惑，自有三種眷屬。就得道者，即是四也。別眷屬，亦四可知。通、藏結緣，三種可知。雖無應來之應，得論感應之應，就所應得名，四義宛足。

問：
下方涌出，妙音東來。如《大經》中召請十方諸大菩薩集娑羅林，大師子吼，於四眷屬，此是何等？

答：
是神通來，非神通生。是應來，非應生。是大誓願相關，非願生。是因緣相召，如下方聞聲、妙音見光，是諸佛大事業來，非業生。業生者，不能業來；業來，非是業生。願通生者，不能願通來；願通來者，亦能願通生，亦能應來。應來，亦能應生(云云)。

If discussed in the particular sense: birth through karma belongs to segmented birth-and-death; birth through vow and birth through spiritual power belong to the expedient-means realm; birth through response belongs to the realm of quiescent light. Discussed together within a single place, all four kinds are alike complete, according with the real reward — already having attained the Dharma-body, one is able to give rise to a response, forming all four kinds of retinue. As for those bonded through the perfect teaching: though confusion is not yet severed, three kinds of retinue are naturally present of themselves; as for those who have attained the path, all four are present. The distinct teaching's own retinue, too, may likewise be understood as fourfold. Those bonded through the shared and Tripiṭaka teachings may be understood as threefold. Though lacking a response that comes as a response, there remains the response discussed as sympathetic response — named according to what is being responded to, the sense of these four is fully complete.

Question: The bodhisattvas springing up from below, the bodhisattva Wonderful Sound coming from the east — as in the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, where the great bodhisattvas of the ten directions are summoned to gather at the śāla grove, the great lion's roar sounded forth — with respect to the four kinds of retinue, what kind is this?

Answer: This is a coming through spiritual power, not a birth through spiritual power. This is a coming through response, not a birth through response. This is bound up with the great vow, not a birth through vow. This is a mutual summoning through cause and condition — as with those from below hearing the sound, and Wonderful Sound seeing the light — this is every buddha's own great undertaking coming forth, not a birth through karma. Birth through karma is unable to come as karma; coming as karma is not birth through karma. Birth through vow and spiritual power is unable to come as vow and spiritual power; coming as vow and spiritual power is, however, also able to be born through vow and spiritual power, and is also able to come as response. Coming as response is likewise able to be born through response, and so on.

Reading note

The fourfold scheme governing this whole item — karma, vow, spiritual power, and response — reads as a graded ladder of how deeply a member of the retinue has already progressed: birth through karma is the rawest case, a bond formed but not yet ripened; birth through vow adds intention without yet requiring liberation; birth through spiritual power requires a body already free of the three realms; and birth through response requires the Dharma-body itself already attained, so that the very distinction between "self" and "other" starts to dissolve. The extended identification of apparent adversaries — Devadatta, Ajātaśatru, Satyaka Nirgrantha, even Māra himself — as bodhisattvas of the Dharma-body deliberately playing the villain is this treatise's most vivid application of the principle already established in Fascicle 5's "evil is itself the resource for good": genuine enmity toward a buddha is doctrinally impossible (a buddha, unlike even a minor wheel-turning king, "goes forth into the world without enemies"), so any apparent enemy must in fact be playing an assigned, compassionate role. The closing Q&A distinguishing "coming as X" from "being born through X" is a careful piece of technical bookkeeping, making clear that the bodhisattvas summoned from below and from afar in the sūtra's own narrative are a fifth category entirely — visitors already possessing every one of the four modes at once, not members of any single one.

Third, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous: if the Tripiṭaka teaching's own retinue, of an inferior root-nature, formed this bond formerly, this bond, too, is shallow and slight, and, ripened by the Dharma in the interval, this ripening amounts to very little. If they come to be born within the buddha-land, forming the inner and outer retinue, through karma, vow, spiritual power, and the rest, even down through those who come as a mere echo in response to a Tripiṭaka buddha, every one of these is a coarse retinue. The shared and distinct teachings' own root-nature, and even the inner and outer retinue, though more skillfully distinguished, differ in the same way — following this same pattern, every one of these, too, is a coarse retinue. This present sūtra teaches that every sentient being, without exception, is already his own child, never a mere hired hand — discussed in terms of principle-nature, none of them is anything other than his child — this is named the wondrous of the retinue in principle. In former times, restating and teaching, forming a bond and sewing a jewel into the robe, across twenty thousand koṭis of buddhas, the unsurpassed path was taught. The sūtra says: "Whenever I encounter sentient beings, I teach them, every one, the buddha-path in its entirety." If sentient beings possessed no buddha-nature, teaching them the buddha-path would be a fault belonging to the Buddha; but since every sentient being possesses buddha-nature, and it is only their own confusion that refuses this teaching, the fault belongs to sentient beings themselves. Every being that possesses a mind whatsoever shall, without exception, become a buddha — even the icchantika's own mind is never severed, and still has the capacity to be reversed — how, then, could becoming a buddha be difficult? The two vehicles, their own ashes and extinction final — extinguishing cognition exhausts the mind, and reducing the body to ashes exhausts form — form and mind both ruined, no longer capable of anything with respect to the five desires — and yet, life after life, encountering the Buddha, they are taught the buddha-path in its entirety nonetheless — this, then, is the wondrous of ripening carried out across the interval. Now, within the Lotus Sūtra, universally attaining buddhahood — this rare and extraordinary matter, the supreme physician-king transforming poison itself into medicine, able to cure even ruined seeds into buddhahood though they possessed no mind for it at all — this, then, is the wondrous of the inner and outer retinue.

譬如臨陣爭勳，前鋒第一。佛說諸教，収羅眾生，而灰心二乘處處不入，於法華忽然得入，故《涅槃》遙指八千聲聞得授記莂，如秋收冬藏，更無所作。若《法華》不悟佛性，《涅槃》不應遙指。若眾生本無佛性，往昔結緣，不應教以佛道。原始要終，佛性義明，可以意得。

今問《華嚴》師頓極之教，說一切眾生有佛性不？若其有者，二乘何不聞經授記作佛，那忽如聾如瘂？若言二乘本有佛性，而怱怱取小，如閹本根，本根已敗，為可治？為不可治？若可治，何故不治？若不可治，那得復言一切眾生皆有佛性？故知《華嚴》所不能治，是方便之說。《法華》能治，是如實之說。能治、難治，此處則妙。

It is comparable to contending for merit on the field of battle, where the vanguard alone claims first place. The Buddha's every teaching gathers in sentient beings, and yet the two vehicles, their minds turned to ashes, find no entry anywhere at all — until, within the Lotus Sūtra, they suddenly find entry — hence, the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra points toward this from afar, noting that the eight thousand śrāvakas received their own prediction of buddhahood, like the autumn harvest stored away for winter, with nothing further left to accomplish. If the Lotus Sūtra had not awakened them to buddha-nature, the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra ought not to have pointed toward it from afar at all. If sentient beings originally possessed no buddha-nature, then the bond formed with them in former times ought not to have taught them the buddha-path at all. Tracing this from its very beginning through to its very end, the sense of buddha-nature is made clear, and may be grasped directly.

Here, a question is put to the masters of the Avataṃsaka's own sudden, ultimate teaching: does it teach that every sentient being possesses buddha-nature, or not? If it does, then why do the two vehicles never hear this sūtra's own prediction of buddhahood at all, suddenly as if deaf or mute? If it is said that the two vehicles do originally possess buddha-nature, yet hastily took up the small vehicle instead, like a root already castrated — is this original root then curable, or incurable? If curable, why is it not cured? If incurable, then how could it still be said that every sentient being possesses buddha-nature? One should therefore know that what the Avataṃsaka is unable to cure is spoken of there only as an expedient means; what the Lotus Sūtra is able to cure is spoken of there as the real, as things truly are. Being able to cure what is otherwise difficult to cure — this is precisely where the wondrous lies.

所謂結緣妙、成熟妙、業生妙、願生妙、應生妙、內眷屬妙、外眷屬妙，能受妙道、影響妙事，是故稱妙。若將此意，約五味者：乳教，有別、圓兩眷屬，一麁一妙；酪教，但一麁；生蘇，三麁一妙；熟蘇，二麁一妙；法華，無麁但妙。是名相待明眷屬妙也。

又開麁顯妙者，諸經明麁眷屬，皆不見佛性。今《法華》定天性審父子，非復客作，故常不輕深得此意，知一切眾生正因不滅，不敢輕慢。於諸過去佛，現在若滅後，若有聞一句，皆得成佛道，即了因不滅；低頭舉手，皆成佛道，即緣因不滅也。一切眾生，無不具此三德，即是開麁顯妙，絕待明眷屬妙也。

This is named the wondrous of forming a bond, the wondrous of ripening, the wondrous of birth through karma, the wondrous of birth through vow, the wondrous of birth through response, the wondrous of the inner retinue, the wondrous of the outer retinue, being able to receive the wondrous path, echoing the wondrous undertaking — hence, this is named wondrous. Taking up this same sense in terms of the Five Flavors: the milk teaching has both the distinct and perfect teachings' own retinue, one coarse and one wondrous; the cream teaching has only one coarse; the curdled-butter teaching has three coarse and one wondrous; the clarified-butter teaching has two coarse and one wondrous; the Lotus Sūtra has no coarse at all, only the wondrous. This is named clarifying the wondrous of the retinue in the relative sense.

Again, as for opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous: every other sūtra's own account of the coarse retinue fails, in every case, to see buddha-nature. The present Lotus Sūtra alone fixes this nature and confirms father and child as genuinely so, never merely hired hands — hence, Never-Disparaging deeply grasped this same sense, knowing that every sentient being's own correct cause is never extinguished, and so dared not treat anyone with contempt. Among every buddha of the past, whether present now or already passed into extinction, whoever has heard even a single phrase attains, every one, the path to buddhahood — this is precisely the revealing cause never extinguished; every bow of the head and every raising of the hand, every one, accomplishes the path to buddhahood — this is precisely the conditioning cause never extinguished. Every sentient being, without exception, is already complete with these three virtues — this is precisely opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous, clarifying the wondrous of the retinue in the absolute sense.

Reading note

The castration simile pressed against the Avataṃsaka masters is one of this treatise's sharpest polemical moments: if the two vehicles genuinely possess buddha-nature yet that sūtra's own teaching leaves them permanently unable to attain buddhahood, the Avataṃsaka is committed to an incoherent position — a curable condition its own teaching cannot cure — whereas the Lotus Sūtra's own prediction of buddhahood to the śrāvakas is offered as proof that the condition was never incurable in the first place, only untreated by that particular medicine. The whole argument runs on the same logic already used for the wondrous of teaching the Dharma just above: two sūtras describing what looks like the identical fact (buddha-nature universally present) can still differ in coarse/wondrous verdict depending on whether that fact is merely asserted or actually acted upon to the point of transforming even "ruined seeds" into buddhas.

Clarifying the Retinue of the Dharma-Gate 明法門眷屬

Chinese Source

四、明法門眷屬者，此如「普現菩薩問淨名居士：父母、妻子、親戚、眷屬、吏民、知識，悉為是誰？奴婢、僮僕、象馬、車乘，皆在何所？淨名答云：方便為父，智度為母，一切眾導師，無不由此生。法喜為妻，慈悲為女，善心誠實男，畢竟空寂舍，弟子眾塵勞，隨意之所轉，道品善知識，由是成正覺。」此法門以為眷屬。

若爾者，法門不同，深淺有異。

若三藏法門，觀真為實、觀假為權，以此二智滿，即名為佛。佛即導師，慈悲六道即是女，令他善順真諦名為男。得此法時喜，名此為妻。此心中修諸波羅蜜道品等，即是善知識也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, clarifying the retinue of the Dharma-gate: this is as when the bodhisattva Puxian Seshen, "Manifesting Every Form," asks the layman Vimalakīrti: "Your father, mother, wife, children, relatives, retinue, officials, subjects, and spiritual friends — who, in truth, are they? Your servants, attendants, elephants, horses, carriages, and carts — where, in truth, are they to be found?" And Vimalakīrti answers: "Skillful means is my father; the perfection of wisdom is my mother — every guiding teacher throughout the world is, without exception, born from these two. The joy of the Dharma is my wife; compassion is my daughter; a good, sincere mind is my son; ultimate empty quiescence is my dwelling; the disciples are the very dust and toil of the world, turned according to my own intent; the factors of the path are my spiritual friends — through these, correct enlightenment is accomplished." This Dharma-gate is here taken as the retinue.

If this is so, then the Dharma-gate differs according to teaching, its depth or shallowness not all the same.

As for the Tripiṭaka teaching's own Dharma-gate: contemplating the real as ultimate and the provisional as expedient, once these two kinds of wisdom are fulfilled, this alone is named buddhahood. The Buddha himself is the guiding teacher; compassion toward the six destinies is precisely his daughter; causing others to accord well with the real truth is named his son. Attaining this dharma, delighted at the moment, is named his wife. Within this same mind, cultivating the pāramitās and the factors of the path is precisely his spiritual friend.

若通教中法門眷屬者，觀諸法如幻、如化，體達即空為實。分別四門，同異為權。於此二智生解，名導師。慈愛眾生為女，令生善直心為男，行六度道品為知識。是為通教中法門眷屬(云云)。

若別教法門恒沙眷屬，真俗合為權，智是父，中實理為母。無量慈善、無量道品、諸波羅蜜通達無滯，道種智分明，觀機識藥，即是別教中眷屬。故《無量義》云：「諸佛法王父，經教夫人母，和合出生諸菩薩子。」《十住毘婆沙》云：「般舟三昧父，大悲無生母，一切諸如來，從此二法生。」《寶性論》云：「大乘信為子，般若以為母，禪胎大悲為乳母。諸佛如實子。」

As for the shared teaching's own retinue of the Dharma-gate: contemplating every dharma as illusory, as a mere transformation, embodying and penetrating through to emptiness-as-such as the ultimate; distinguishing the four gates, their sameness and difference, as the expedient. Giving rise to understanding through these two kinds of wisdom is named the guiding teacher. Compassionate love toward sentient beings is his daughter; causing a good, upright mind to arise is his son; practicing the six pāramitās and the factors of the path is his spiritual friend. This is the shared teaching's own retinue of the Dharma-gate, and so on.

As for the distinct teaching's own Dharma-gate, its retinue as numerous as the sands of the Ganges: the real and the conventional joined together are the expedient — wisdom is the father, and the principle of the middle's own reality is the mother. Immeasurable loving-kindness and goodness, immeasurable factors of the path, every pāramitā penetrated through without obstruction, the wisdom of the paths made clear and distinct, discerning capacity and knowing the fitting remedy — this is precisely the retinue within the distinct teaching. Hence the Sūtra of Innumerable Meanings says: "Every buddha, the Dharma-king, is the father; the sūtra's own teaching, the consort, is the mother; joined together, they bring forth the bodhisattva children." The Daśabhūmikavibhāṣā says: "The pratyutpanna-samādhi is the father; great compassion, free of generation, is the mother — every tathāgata is born from these two dharmas." The Ratnagotravibhāga says: "Faith in the Mahāyāna is the child; prajñā is the mother; the womb of dhyāna and great compassion is the wet-nurse — every buddha is a child born of the real."

闡提謗大乘障，外道橫計身中有我障，聲聞怖畏生死障，支佛背捨利益眾生障；菩薩修四法為對治：修信、修般若、修虛空定首楞嚴定、修大悲，得清淨法界，到彼岸，見如來性，生如來家，是佛子也。既道見如來性，生如來家，當知用如來為父也。無量法門，不可說不可說，皆能生佛子(云云)。

The icchantika's own slander of the Mahāyāna is an obstruction; the non-Buddhist outsider's own deluded reckoning of a self within the body is an obstruction; the śrāvaka's own dread of birth-and-death is an obstruction; the pratyekabuddha's own turning away from benefiting sentient beings is an obstruction. The bodhisattva cultivates four dharmas as their antidote: cultivating faith, cultivating prajñā, cultivating the samādhi of empty space and the Śūraṅgama samādhi, and cultivating great compassion — thereby attaining the pure dharma-realm, arriving at the far shore, seeing the Tathāgata's own nature, and being born within the Tathāgata's own household — this is a child of the Buddha. Since it is said that one sees the Tathāgata's own nature and is born within the Tathāgata's own household, one should therefore know that the Tathāgata himself is taken as the father. Dharma-gates beyond measure, beyond even what can be spoken of, every one, without exception, is able to give birth to a child of the Buddha, and so on.

若圓教法門明眷屬，自行三諦、一諦為實。化他一諦、三諦為權。

隨情一諦、三諦為權。隨智三諦、一諦為實。從此不思議生解，一心具萬行之善為男，無緣大慈為女。開佛知見生喜為妻，非淨非垢等中道、道品、六波羅蜜為善知識。如是等實相，圓極法門以為眷屬。初住之中，便成正覺；能八相化物，即是導師。前來諸法門既麁，生諸導師亦麁。今法門眷屬既妙，所生導師亦妙也。

As for the perfect teaching's own Dharma-gate, clarifying the retinue: one's own practice takes the three truths and the one truth as the real. Transforming others takes the one truth and the three truths as the expedient.

According with sentiment, the one truth and the three truths are the expedient; according with wisdom, the three truths and the one truth are the real. From this, understanding arises inconceivably — a single mind complete with the goodness of every myriad practice is his son; compassion without any object is his daughter. Opening the Buddha's own knowledge and vision, joy arising, is his wife; the middle way, neither pure nor defiled and the rest, the factors of the path, and the six pāramitās are his spiritual friends. The real mark, thus understood, the ultimate perfect Dharma-gate, is taken here as the retinue. Within the very initial abiding, correct enlightenment is already accomplished; able to transform beings through the eight aspects of a buddha's life, this alone is the guiding teacher. Since every Dharma-gate that came before was coarse, the guiding teacher born from it, too, is coarse. Now that this Dharma-gate's own retinue is wondrous, the guiding teacher born from it, too, is wondrous.

用此意歷五味教者：乳教一麁一妙。酪教但有一麁。生蘇有三麁一妙。熟蘇有二麁一妙。《法華》但有一妙。待麁意竟。

諸經妙者自妙、麁者自麁。今經非但妙者為妙，亦無復有麁，絓是前來諸麁，悉皆決了，為一平等大慧妙法門也。絕待意竟。

Taking up this same sense across the Five Flavors: the milk teaching has one coarse and one wondrous. The cream teaching has only one coarse. The curdled-butter teaching has three coarse and one wondrous. The clarified-butter teaching has two coarse and one wondrous. The Lotus Sūtra has only the one wondrous alone. This closes the sense of the relative reading, set against the coarse.

Every other sūtra's own wondrous is wondrous by itself, and its own coarse is coarse by itself. The present sūtra, however, is not merely wondrous as wondrous, but is also entirely without any coarse remaining at all — every coarse element that came before is, without exception, fully resolved, becoming a single, equal, great wisdom's own wondrous Dharma-gate. This closes the sense of the absolute reading.

Reading note

Zhiyi reuses Vimalakīrti's own "father-mother-wife-children" allegory — skillful means and prajñā as parents, the joy of the Dharma as wife, compassion as daughter, an upright mind as son — as a template applied four times over, once per teaching, tracking how each pair of governing terms shifts: the Tripiṭaka teaching's father-and-mother pair is the real and the provisional; the shared teaching's is emptiness and the four gates; the distinct teaching's is wisdom and the middle's own reality; and the perfect teaching's is the one truth and the three truths read either as expedient (according with sentiment) or as real (according with wisdom), depending on whether it is directed at oneself or at others. The four citations stacked together for the distinct teaching (the Sūtra of Innumerable Meanings, the Daśabhūmikavibhāṣā, and the Ratnagotravibhāga, each naming a different father-mother pair for the same bodhisattva lineage) demonstrate the same point this treatise makes repeatedly about apparently divergent scriptural formulas: different names for the identical underlying birth of a buddha's child.

The Contemplation of Mind 明觀心

Chinese Source

五、觀心眷屬者，即為六：一、愛心；二、見心；四則四教也。

愛心眷屬者，無明為父，癡愛為母，出生煩惱之子孫。以貪著、憶想，欲得心中法門，魔鬼便入。如媚女思想，邪媚媚之。行人亦爾，憶想偏邪，邪物得入，以鬼力故，或生權解，或生實解。邪解生，故鬼導師生，起鬼慈善，著邪法喜，行邪六度道品，得邪辯，心明口利，說諸法門，即愛心眷屬也。邪相既利，發得四見，見心推畫，作諸法門。心所見處為實，同他為權。心起愛為女，心分別為男。如是心中，修六度為道品，是名見心眷屬。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, as for the retinue in the contemplation of mind: this is treated under six headings — first, the mind of craving; second, the mind of views; and then four more, which are the Four Teachings themselves.

As for the retinue of the mind of craving: ignorance is the father, and delusion-craving is the mother, giving birth to the very children and grandchildren of affliction. Through attachment and fixed imagining, wishing to attain some Dharma-gate within the mind, demonic influence thereupon enters. It is like a seductive woman's own fantasies, her own deviant charm charming another — the practitioner is exactly the same: fixed imagining turned deviant, a deviant influence gains entry, and, through this demonic power, either a provisional understanding or a real understanding arises. Once a deviant understanding arises, a demonic guiding teacher arises accordingly, giving rise to a demonic compassion, becoming attached to a deviant joy in the Dharma, practicing a deviant version of the six pāramitās and the factors of the path, attaining a deviant eloquence — the mind sharp and the mouth quick, expounding every kind of Dharma-gate — this is precisely the retinue of the mind of craving. Once this deviant mark has sharpened, the four views are brought forth, and the mind of views, reasoning things out, constructs every kind of Dharma-gate: whatever the mind sees is taken as real, and whatever accords with another is taken as provisional. The mind's own arising craving is his daughter; the mind's own discrimination is his son. Within a mind such as this, cultivating the six pāramitās as the factors of the path — this is named the retinue of the mind of views.

何者？此之見愛，不識己心苦、集，妄謂道、滅！不知字與非字，如蟲食木，偶得法門之名，有名無義，豈非愛見耶？若能觀心，識愛見心皆是因緣生法；無常生滅，即有四番觀心眷屬。如《中論》偈云：「因緣生法，即空、即假、即中。」仍於四觀，各明眷屬，準前可知。

判五觀心為麁，後一觀心為妙。又決麁論妙，人尚不自識己愛見心是因緣生，何能識因緣心，即空、即假？尚不識空、假，何能魔界即佛界，於見不動，修三十七品耶？今觀愛即是法性，觀見不動，修三十七品，魔界見界即是佛界，於非字中而能知字，行於非道，通達佛道，於一切法無不是妙。

Why is this? This same craving-and-views, not recognizing one's own mind's own suffering and its arising, falsely supposes it to be the path and cessation instead! Not knowing the difference between what is genuine and what is not, like an insect gnawing through wood and, by chance, tracing out a character — obtaining only the mere name of a Dharma-gate, with the name present but its sense absent — how could this be anything other than craving and views? If one is able to contemplate the mind, recognizing that the mind of craving and the mind of views are both, alike, dharmas born through causes and conditions, impermanent, arising and ceasing, there are accordingly four rounds of the retinue in the contemplation of mind. As the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā's own verse says: "A dharma born through causes and conditions is at once empty, at once provisional, at once the middle." Within these same four contemplations, each in turn clarifies its own retinue, understood by the same pattern as before.

The first five contemplations of mind are judged coarse, and the final, sixth contemplation of mind alone is wondrous. Again, resolving the coarse to discuss the wondrous: a person who cannot even recognize that his own mind of craving and views is born through causes and conditions — how could he possibly recognize the mind of dependent origination as at once empty, at once provisional? Not even recognizing emptiness and the provisional, how could he possibly recognize Māra's own realm as itself already the buddha-realm, remaining unmoved with respect to views while cultivating the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment? Here, contemplating that craving is itself already the dharma-nature, contemplating views without being moved, cultivating the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment — Māra's own realm and the realm of views are themselves already the buddha-realm; within what is not the genuine character, one is nonetheless able to know the genuine character; walking amid what is not the path, one thoroughly penetrates through to the buddha-path — with respect to every dharma whatsoever, there is nothing that is not the wondrous.

明事眷屬，伏聽學文字人；明法門眷屬，伏行教人；明觀心眷屬，伏觀心坐禪人。三種法門，並過其聞見(云云)。

Clarifying the retinue in phenomena subdues and instructs those who study the mere written word; clarifying the retinue of the Dharma-gate subdues and instructs those who practice and teach; clarifying the retinue in the contemplation of mind subdues and instructs those who contemplate the mind, seated in meditation. These three Dharma-gates each, in turn, surpass what came before them in what they hear and see, and so on.

Reading note

The insect-gnawing-through-wood simile — a classical Chinese image for accidentally producing something that resembles meaning without possessing any — captures precisely what distinguishes the coarse mind of craving-and-views from the wondrous sixth contemplation: both can produce fluent, persuasive Dharma-talk, "the mind sharp and the mouth quick," but only the sixth actually recognizes what is being talked about as dependent origination itself, at once empty, provisional, and the middle. The closing line's own three-tier hierarchy — phenomena for those who merely study words, the Dharma-gate for those who practice and teach, the contemplation of mind for those who sit in meditation — closes out the whole of the wondrous of the retinue by returning, one final time, to this treatise's standing insistence that the deepest version of any doctrine is always the one relocated to a single thought-moment of one's own mind. This closes the wondrous of the retinue (眷屬妙) in its entirety, the ninth of the ten wondrous aspects. The fascicle continues with the wondrous of merit and benefit (利益妙), the tenth and final of the ten wondrous aspects.

The Wondrous of Merit and Benefit 明利益妙

Chinese Source

○第十、功德利益者，秖功德利益，一而無異；若分別者，自益名功德，益他名利益(云云)。此為四：一、利益來意。二、正說中利益。三、流通中利益。四、觀心中利益。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, clarifying merit and benefit: merit and benefit, taken simply as such, are one and the same, without distinction; if distinguished, benefiting oneself is named merit, and benefiting others is named benefit, and so on. This is treated under four headings: first, why benefit comes next; second, benefit within the correct teaching; third, benefit within the propagation section; fourth, benefit within the contemplation of mind.

Why Benefit Comes Next 利益來意

Chinese Source

一、利益來意者，諸佛所為，未甞空過。《釋論》云：「佛入王三昧，前放光度前者，後放光度後者。」譬如網魚，前獲、後獲，見光聞法，皆不唐捐。《淨名》云：「法寶普照而雨甘露。」即身口兩益也。《華嚴》、《思益》並云：放光破慳、破瞋、破癡等。具如彼說。今經四大弟子，領佛開三顯一之益。佛言：如來復有無量功德，汝等說不能盡。譬如大雲起於世間，譬形益也。興雷耀電，譬神通益也。其雨普等，譬說法益也。而諸草木各得生長，即是四種眷屬，皆沾七益。故次明利益妙也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, as for why benefit comes next: every buddha's own undertaking is never carried out in vain. The Treatise says: "The Buddha enters the king of samādhis, emitting light to deliver those in front first, then emitting light to deliver those behind." It is comparable to netting fish — those caught first and those caught later — seeing the light and hearing the Dharma is, in every case, never wasted. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "The jeweled Dharma illuminates everywhere, and rains down the sweet dew" — this is precisely the twin benefit of body and speech. The Avataṃsaka and the Viśeṣacintibrahma-paripṛcchā Sūtra both alike say: emitting light breaks through stinginess, breaks through anger, breaks through delusion, and the rest — fully as explained in those same sūtras. The present sūtra's own four great disciples receive the benefit of the Buddha's opening the three to reveal the one. The Buddha says: the Tathāgata possesses, further, immeasurable merit, which you yourselves could never fully describe even in speaking of it. It is comparable to a great cloud rising up throughout the world — this illustrates the benefit of form; thunder rolling and lightning flashing — this illustrates the benefit of spiritual powers; its rain falling everywhere, equally — this illustrates the benefit of teaching the Dharma; and every kind of grass and tree, each thereby growing — this is precisely the four kinds of retinue, every one moistened by these seven benefits. Hence, the wondrous of merit and benefit is next clarified.

Benefit Within the Correct Teaching: Distant Benefit 正說中利益・遠益

Chinese Source

二、正說利益，又為三：先、論遠益。次、論近益。三、論當文益。

遠益者，即大通佛所十六王子，助化宣揚，雙擊毒天二鼓。善生有淺深，惑死有奢促。始人天善，終至大樹，淺益也。始初心最實，終後心最實，深益也。始破不善，終破塵沙，奢死也。始破無明，終亦破無明，促死也。死之奢、促，是毒鼓之力；善生淺、深，天鼓之力。故文云：破有法王，出現於世，隨眾生欲，而為說法。即二鼓之文義也。破有義如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, correctly teaching benefit: this, too, is treated under three headings — first, discussing distant benefit; second, discussing near benefit; third, discussing benefit within the present text.

As for distant benefit: this refers precisely to the sixteen princes at the place of Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Buddha, assisting in the transformation and proclamation, sounding both the poison drum and the heaven drum together. Wholesome growth carries both shallow and deep degrees; the death of confusion carries both the drawn-out and the swift. Beginning with the goodness of humans and devas and ending at the great tree is shallow benefit; beginning with the initial mind's own most real state and ending at the later mind's own most real state is deep benefit. Beginning by breaking through what is unwholesome and ending by breaking through dust-and-sand is the drawn-out death; beginning by breaking through ignorance and ending, likewise, by breaking through ignorance is the swift death. The drawn-out or swift nature of this death is the poison drum's own power; the shallow or deep degree of wholesome growth is the heaven drum's own power. Hence the text says: the Dharma-king who breaks through existence appears in the world, and, according with sentient beings' own desire, teaches the Dharma for them — this is precisely the sense of the two drums' own text. The sense of "breaking through existence" is as already explained before.

說法益義，今當說，略為七益：一、二十五有果報益，亦名地上清涼益。二、二十五有因華開敷益，亦名小草益。三、真諦三昧析法益，亦名中草益。四、俗諦三昧五通益，亦名上草益。五、真諦三昧體法益，亦名小樹益。六、俗諦三昧六通益，亦名大樹益。七、中道王三昧益，亦名最實事益。若二十五有因果益，堪為業生眷屬。若真諦三昧體析益，堪為願生眷屬。若俗諦三昧五通六通益，堪為神通眷屬。若中道王三昧益，堪為應生眷屬。私謂應有四雙、八益，直是開前、合後，故言七益。若開後合前，亦是七益；前後俱開，即是八益，所謂中道次第益，中道不次第益。若前後俱合，則是六益(云云)。

已略說七益竟，今更廣開為十益：一、果益。二、因益。三、聲聞益。四、緣覺益。五、六度益。六、通益。七、別益。八、圓益。九、變易益。十、實報益。

The sense of the benefit of teaching the Dharma shall now be explained, given briefly as seven benefits: first, the benefit of the fruit and retribution of the twenty-five states of existence, also named the benefit of coolness upon the ground; second, the benefit of the twenty-five states of existence' own cause blossoming open, also named the benefit of the small grass; third, the benefit of the real truth's own samādhi analyzing dharmas, also named the benefit of the middling grass; fourth, the benefit of the conventional truth's own samādhi and the five spiritual powers, also named the benefit of the superior grass; fifth, the benefit of the real truth's own samādhi embodying dharmas, also named the benefit of the small tree; sixth, the benefit of the conventional truth's own samādhi and the six spiritual powers, also named the benefit of the great tree; seventh, the benefit of the middle way's own king of samādhis, also named the benefit of the most real matter. As for the benefit of the twenty-five states of existence' own cause and fruit: this is fit to become the retinue born through karma. As for the benefit of the real truth's own samādhi, embodying or analyzing: this is fit to become the retinue born through vow. As for the benefit of the conventional truth's own samādhi, the five or six spiritual powers: this is fit to become the retinue of spiritual power. As for the benefit of the middle way's own king of samādhis: this is fit to become the retinue born through response. Privately, it might be supposed that there ought to be four pairs, eight benefits — this is simply opening what came before and combining what comes after, and hence it is spoken of as seven benefits. If what comes after is opened and what came before is combined, this, too, makes seven benefits; if both before and after are opened, this makes eight benefits — namely, the middle way taken sequentially, and the middle way taken non-sequentially. If both before and after are combined, this makes six benefits, and so on.

The seven benefits have now been briefly explained; here, they are further broadly opened into ten benefits: first, the benefit of the fruit; second, the benefit of the cause; third, the benefit of the śrāvaka; fourth, the benefit of the pratyekabuddha; fifth, the benefit of the six pāramitās; sixth, the benefit of the shared teaching; seventh, the benefit of the distinct teaching; eighth, the benefit of the perfect teaching; ninth, the benefit of transformational birth-and-death; tenth, the benefit of the realm of real reward.

果益者，即二十五有果報益也。有八大地獄：謂阿鼻、想、黑繩、眾合、叫喚、大叫喚、焦熱、大焦熱。一一各有十六小獄為眷屬，合一百三十六所。此正地獄，在地下二萬由旬。其傍地獄，或在地上、或在鐵圍山間。傍輕正重，重者遍歷百三十六，中者不遍，下者復減。其中眾生，常為熱苦所逼，不可具說，聞者驚怖。《四解脫經》稱為火塗，初入初出，兩時可化。其中罪人，宿世善根，可發關宜。而為聖人赴對應之，或蒙光照、或注雨滅火、或調達婆藪開示說法，熱悶甦醒，身體清涼，獲冥、顯兩益，諸苦得息。八寒氷，謂阿波波等，亦有百三十六所，乃至得冥、顯兩益，溫煖適身，是為地獄果上得清涼益也。

As for the benefit of the fruit: this refers precisely to the benefit of the fruit and retribution of the twenty-five states of existence. There are eight great hells: namely, Avīci, Reviving, Black Rope, Crushing, Screaming, Great Screaming, Heating, and Great Heating. Each of these, in turn, has sixteen minor hells as its own retinue, making one hundred and thirty-six in total. These are the direct hells, lying twenty thousand yojanas beneath the ground. Their adjoining hells lie either upon the ground or between the iron encircling mountains. The adjoining hells are lighter, and the direct hells heavier — the heaviest passes through all one hundred and thirty-six in full; the middling does not pass through all of them; and the lowest is fewer still. The sentient beings within them are constantly pressed by the suffering of heat, beyond what can be fully described — whoever hears of it is struck with terror. The Sūtra of the Four Liberations names this the path of fire; at the very moment of entering and the very moment of leaving, these two moments alone allow for transformation. Among those undergoing punishment there, whoever possesses a wholesome root from a former life may bring forth an occasion fitting for this — and the sage, hastening toward them, responds accordingly: some are graced with illuminating light, some have rain poured down to extinguish the fire, some have Devadatta or Vasu open and expound the Dharma for them — the heat and suffocation revived, the body made cool — obtaining both the hidden and the manifest benefit, every suffering brought to rest. The eight cold hells of ice, such as Apapa and the rest, likewise number one hundred and thirty-six, and, obtaining both the hidden and the manifest benefit in the same way, warmth suits the body once more — this is the benefit of coolness obtained upon the fruit of hell.

畜生者，略有三種：水、陸、空。陸有三品：重者土內，不見光明；中者，山林；輕者，人所畜養。彊者伏弱，飲血噉肉，怖畏百端。《四解脫經》稱為血塗。其中眾生，先世善根，可發關宜。聖人赴對應之，得無所畏，獲冥、顯兩益，是為畜生果上清涼益也。

餓鬼者，或居海渚、或在人間山林中、或似人形、或似獸形。重者飢火節焰，不聞漿水之名；中者伺求蕩滌，膿血糞穢；輕者時薄一飽，加以刀杖驅逼，塞海填河。《四解脫》稱為刀塗。其中眾生，先世善根，可發關宜。聖人赴對應之，手出香乳，施令飽滿，獲冥、顯兩益，是為餓鬼果上清涼益也。

阿脩羅者，或居半須彌巖窟、或大海邊、或大海底。與諸天為憾，恒懷怖畏，雷鳴謂為天鼓，龍雨變成刀劍。此中眾生，先世善根，可發關宜。聖人赴對應之，軟言調伏，獲冥、顯兩益，是為阿脩羅果上清涼益也。

As for animals: there are, broadly, three kinds — of water, of land, and of air. Those of land are of three grades: the heaviest dwell within the earth, never seeing any light at all; the middling dwell within mountains and forests; the lightest are raised and kept by humans. The strong subdue the weak, drinking blood and devouring flesh, terror present in every direction. The Sūtra of the Four Liberations names this the path of blood. Among these beings, whoever possesses a wholesome root from a former life may bring forth an occasion fitting for this; the sage, hastening toward them, responds accordingly, and they attain freedom from fear, obtaining both the hidden and the manifest benefit — this is the benefit of coolness upon the fruit of the animal realm.

As for hungry ghosts: some dwell upon islands in the sea, some among the mountains and forests of the human realm, some resembling a human form, some resembling an animal form. The heaviest are consumed by the very flame of hunger, never even hearing the word "drink" or "water"; the middling seek out only filth to be washed away, pus, blood, and excrement; the lightest occasionally have a single, meager fill, yet, further, are driven and pressed by knife and staff, filling the very sea and the rivers with their own number. The Sūtra of the Four Liberations names this the path of the knife. Among these beings, whoever possesses a wholesome root from a former life may bring forth an occasion fitting for this; the sage, hastening toward them, responds accordingly, his own hand bringing forth fragrant milk, bestowed to fill them, obtaining both the hidden and the manifest benefit — this is the benefit of coolness upon the fruit of the hungry-ghost realm.

As for the asuras: some dwell within caverns halfway up Sumeru, some at the edge of the great ocean, some at the very bottom of the great ocean. Bearing enmity toward the devas, they constantly harbor fear and dread — thunder, to them, is heard as the heavenly drum, and the rain of the dragon transforms, to them, into swords and blades. Among these beings, whoever possesses a wholesome root from a former life may bring forth an occasion fitting for this; the sage, hastening toward them, responds accordingly, with gentle words subduing and taming them, obtaining both the hidden and the manifest benefit — this is the benefit of coolness upon the fruit of the asura realm.

四天下人雖果報勝劣，俱有生、老、病、死，同是輕報泥犁。其中眾生，先世善根，可發關宜。聖人赴對應之，令所離得離，所求得求，獲冥、顯兩益也。

六欲天者，地天別有脩羅鬪戰之難，通有五衰死相苦等地獄。其中諸天，先世善根，可發關宜。聖人赴對應之，令獲冥、顯兩益也。

四禪、梵王、無想、那含等色天，雖無下界諸苦，而為色所籠。若命盡時，不樂入禪，風觸吹身，唯除眼識，餘皆有苦。其中諸天，先世善根，可發關宜。聖人赴對應之，令獲冥、顯兩益也。

四空諸天，雖無欲色界等苦，如瘡、如癰、如癡、如箭入體，成就細煩惱。其中諸天，先世善根，可發關宜。聖人赴對應之，令獲冥顯兩益。

As for the people of the four continents: though their own fruit and retribution differ in excellence or inferiority, all alike possess birth, old age, sickness, and death, being alike a lighter form of hellish retribution. Among these beings, whoever possesses a wholesome root from a former life may bring forth an occasion fitting for this; the sage, hastening toward them, responds accordingly, causing whatever ought to be departed from to be departed from, and whatever ought to be sought to be sought, obtaining both the hidden and the manifest benefit.

As for the six heavens of desire: the earth-bound devas alone have the further calamity of battling the asuras; and all alike carry the suffering of the five signs of decline at death, comparable to a hell of their own. Among the devas there, whoever possesses a wholesome root from a former life may bring forth an occasion fitting for this; the sage, hastening toward them, responds accordingly, causing them to obtain both the hidden and the manifest benefit.

As for the form-realm heavens of the four dhyānas, the Brahmā kings, the heaven of no-thought, and the non-returners' own heavens: though free of the sufferings of the lower realms, they remain confined by form itself. When their own lifespan comes to its end, and they take no delight in entering dhyāna, the wind of contact blows upon the body — apart from the eye-consciousness alone, every other consciousness carries suffering. Among the devas there, whoever possesses a wholesome root from a former life may bring forth an occasion fitting for this; the sage, hastening toward them, responds accordingly, causing them to obtain both the hidden and the manifest benefit.

As for the four formless heavens: though free of the sufferings of the desire and form realms, they are like a boil, like an abscess, like delusion, like an arrow lodged within the body, accomplishing only a subtle affliction of their own. Among the devas there, whoever possesses a wholesome root from a former life may bring forth an occasion fitting for this; the sage, hastening toward them, responds accordingly, causing them to obtain both the hidden and the manifest benefit.

此清涼益，合而言之，蓋由凡聖慈善根力；別而言之，本由菩薩初觀二十五有所防之惡而起於悲，觀二十五能防之善而起於慈，以此慈悲熏王三昧，不捨眾生，赴對關宜，令得利益。

《大經》明二十五三昧破二十五有者，十番之一，意略如此也。

This benefit of coolness, discussed together, is due to the power of the wholesome root of loving-kindness shared by ordinary being and sage alike; discussed separately, it is originally due to the bodhisattva's own first contemplating the evil that the twenty-five states of existence must guard against, giving rise to compassion, and contemplating the goodness able to guard against them, giving rise to loving-kindness — through this very loving-kindness and compassion, perfuming the king of samādhis, never abandoning sentient beings, hastening toward and responding to whatever occasion is fitting, causing them to obtain this benefit.

The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra's own account of the twenty-five samādhis breaking through the twenty-five states of existence, the first of ten rounds — the sense is, in brief, exactly this.

Reading note

The ten-round scheme opening here is this treatise's most systematic application yet of the twenty-five samādhis / twenty-five states of existence correlation established in Fascicle 4, run now not against a single practitioner's own stage but against every possible recipient of a buddha's teaching at once — the coolness benefit given to the realm-by-realm cosmological survey of all twenty-five states of existence (the eight hot hells and eight cold hells given in full, animals graded by whether they ever see light, hungry ghosts by the "path of the knife," and upward through the desire, form, and formless heavens) is only the first of ten rounds, each digging one layer deeper into what a genuinely wondrous response accomplishes. The recurring hidden/manifest ("冥/顯") pairing from the wondrous of sympathetic response (Fascicle 6's own opening item) returns here as the basic unit of benefit at every single stage.

Rounds Two Through Eight: Cause, the Two Vehicles, the Six Pāramitās, and the Four Teachings 因益・聲聞益・緣覺益・六度益・通益・別益・圓益

Chinese Source

二番二十五有修因益者，夫自、他因果，各隨義便，互舉一邊，說之則易。前果報益，處所、時節不同，不得一身備論諸益，從多人多處易顯。若明因益，一人之心起無量業，其義易顯。故約一人明二十五有因益也。云何因益？四因壞益，二十一因成益；或一因壞益，一因成益，二十三亦成亦壞益。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second round, as for the benefit of cultivating the cause across the twenty-five states of existence: as for cause and fruit in terms of self and other, each may be raised according to whichever sense is more convenient, and it is easier to discuss taking up one side at a time. In the previous discussion of the benefit of the fruit, the places and occasions involved differed, and no single body could fully treat every benefit at once — this is easier to show through many different people in many different places. In clarifying the benefit of the cause, however, a single person's own mind gives rise to karma beyond measure, and this sense is easy to show directly — hence, this discusses the benefit of the cause across the twenty-five states of existence in terms of a single person. What, then, is the benefit of the cause? Four causes are broken as benefit; twenty-one causes are accomplished as benefit; or, alternatively, one cause is broken as benefit, one cause is accomplished as benefit, and twenty-three are both accomplished and broken as benefit.

若無戒自制，縱其身口，作四趣業，名地獄人。若捨惡持戒，名見天人。但禁戒嚴峻，遇緣動退，則惡業還興。或四重五逆，焚毀塔寺，此心生時，惡起戒沒，是業熟成，必墮惡道。欲離此心，成就戒、善，此有可發關宜之機，感無垢三昧赴對應之。惡心豁破，地獄因息，得冥顯兩益(云云)。今人雖入道場懺悔，惡心不轉，則惡業不壞。惡業不壞，得繩不斷，罪不得滅也。

If, without any precept to restrain oneself, one gives free rein to body and speech, committing the karma of the four evil destinies, this is named a person of hell. If one abandons evil and upholds the precepts, this is named a person who beholds the heavens. Yet if the precepts, however strict, are shaken and retreat upon meeting some condition, evil karma rises up once more. Or, committing one of the four grave offenses or the five acts of unmediated retribution, burning down a stūpa or a temple — at the very moment this mind arises, evil is aroused and the precepts sink away, this karma ripening to completion, one is bound to fall into an evil destiny. Wishing to be free of this mind, and to accomplish the precepts and goodness instead, there is here an occasion fitting to be brought forth, moving the response of the stainless samādhi, hastening toward and responding accordingly. The evil mind broken through completely, the cause of hell is thereby put to rest, and one obtains both the hidden and the manifest benefit, and so on. People today, though entering the place of practice and repenting, if their evil mind is not turned, their evil karma is not thereby broken. If evil karma is not broken, the very knot obtained is not severed, and the offense cannot be extinguished.

若慳貪諂媚，邀射名聞，內無實德，欲人稱美；此惡起戒伏，墮於鬼中。若無慚愧，負債不還，無恭敬心，憍慢瞋忿，貪婪饕餮；此惡起戒伏，墮畜生道。若嫉賢妬能，為勝他故而修福力，蛆毒惡心，方便墜陷，驚怖於他；是惡起戒伏，墮阿脩羅業。欲離此三惡心，成就戒善，善有可發關宜之機，聖用赴對應之。惡心豁破，戒善完具，此名四趣因壞、人天因成，獲冥顯兩益。此約人道修因，作此釋耳。

若約諸趣，欲出地獄入畜，欲出畜入鬼，欲出鬼入脩羅，欲出脩羅入人道，皆傳傳有因。因成業轉，此例可知(云云)。

If one is stingy, greedy, flattering, and fawning, seeking after fame and renown, possessing no real virtue within while wishing others to praise and admire one — this evil, once aroused, subdues the precepts, and one falls among the hungry ghosts. If, without any sense of shame or conscience, one fails to repay a debt, harbors no reverent mind, and is arrogant, contemptuous, wrathful, and greedy, gorging oneself without restraint — this evil, once aroused, subdues the precepts, and one falls into the animal path. If one is envious of the worthy and jealous of the capable, cultivating merit only for the sake of surpassing others, a mind of maggot-like poison and evil, scheming to bring another down, striking terror into them — this evil, once aroused, subdues the precepts, and one falls into the karma of the asura. Wishing to be free of these three evil minds, and to accomplish the precepts and goodness instead, there is goodness here that carries an occasion fitting to be brought forth, and the sage responds to it accordingly. The evil mind broken through completely, the precepts and goodness fully complete — this is named the cause of the four evil destinies broken and the cause of the human and heavenly realms accomplished, obtaining both the hidden and the manifest benefit. This explanation is given in terms of cultivating the cause within the human path specifically.

As for the other destinies: wishing to emerge from hell and enter the animal realm, wishing to emerge from the animal realm and enter the hungry-ghost realm, wishing to emerge from the hungry-ghost realm and enter the asura realm, wishing to emerge from the asura realm and enter the human path — each, in turn, has its own cause. The cause accomplished, karma turns accordingly — this same pattern may be understood, and so on.

若堅持五戒，兼行仁義，孝順父母，信敬慚愧，即是人業。人業有四品：上、中、下、下下。若就果報，閻浮提為下下。若就入道，欝單越為下下。或時善心歇末，惡念唯強，善有可成，惡有可滅，關宜之機，聖用赴對應之。四惡趣壞，四品善成，獲冥顯兩益(云云)。

If one firmly upholds the five precepts, together with practicing benevolence and righteousness, filial piety toward one's own parents, faith, reverence, shame, and conscience, this is precisely human karma. Human karma is of four grades: superior, middling, inferior, and inferior of the inferior. Discussed in terms of fruit and retribution, Jambudvīpa is the inferior of the inferior; discussed in terms of entering the path, Uttarakuru is the inferior of the inferior. At times, the wholesome mind grows faint and fails, while evil thoughts alone remain strong; goodness may still be accomplished, and evil may still be extinguished — the occasion fitting to be brought forth, the sage responds to it accordingly. The four evil destinies broken through, the four grades of goodness accomplished, one obtains both the hidden and the manifest benefit, and so on.

若修持十善，任運無間，善心成熟即天業。故云：純惡心無善念間者，即惡道業，果時純苦故。善惡相間起即人業，人中果報苦樂相間故。十善任運成是天業，天中果報自然故。若修十善兼起護法心，即四天王業。若修十善兼慈化人，是三十三天業。若修十善，其心細妙任運成熟，行住坐臥不惱眾生，善巧純續，是焰摩業。若修十善兼修禪定攝心，麁住細住者，是兜率業；欲界定是化樂業；未到定破事障，是他化業；四禪是色界業；兼於慈悲喜捨，心數法中得定，是梵王業；滅心修無心定，是無想業。

If one cultivates and upholds the ten wholesome actions, spontaneously and without interruption, the wholesome mind ripened to maturity, this is precisely heavenly karma. Hence it is said: a mind of purely evil thought, with no interval of wholesome thought at all, is the karma of an evil destiny, since its fruit is then purely suffering. Good and evil arising in alternation with one another is human karma, since the human realm's own fruit and retribution alternate between suffering and pleasure. The ten wholesome actions accomplished spontaneously is heavenly karma, since the heavenly realm's own fruit and retribution arise of themselves. If one cultivates the ten wholesome actions, together with giving rise to a mind that protects the Dharma, this is the karma of the Four Heavenly Kings. If one cultivates the ten wholesome actions, together with loving-kindness toward and transforming others, this is the karma of the Thirty-Three Devas. If one cultivates the ten wholesome actions, with a mind subtle and fine, spontaneously ripened, never troubling sentient beings whether walking, standing, sitting, or lying down, skillful means continuing purely without break, this is the karma of Yama. If one cultivates the ten wholesome actions, together with cultivating dhyāna concentration and gathering the mind, whether in a coarse or a subtle abiding, this is the karma of Tuṣita; concentration belonging to the desire realm is the karma of Nirmāṇarati; the concentration of the threshold, breaking through the obstruction of phenomena, is the karma of Paranirmitavaśavartin; the fourth dhyāna is the karma of the form realm; together with loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity, attaining concentration within the mental factors, this is the karma of the Brahmā king; extinguishing the mind and cultivating the concentration of no-mind is the karma of the heaven of no-thought.

無想是邪見天，云何為機感？

答：
《大集》云：菩薩調伏眾生多種，或邪或正，行於非道，通達佛道(云云)。

若舊云：聖人以兩片無漏，夾熏一片有漏，練成無漏。今言：九次第定，熏修有漏成無漏，是那含業。四空定，是無色界業。

Question: The heaven of no-thought is a heaven of wrong views — how, then, could it possibly involve capacity and response?

Answer: The Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra says: the bodhisattva subdues and tames sentient beings in many different ways, whether deviant or correct, walking amid what is not the path, thoroughly penetrating through to the buddha-path, and so on.

As the old masters used to say: the sage, taking two portions of the taintless, perfumes a single portion of the tainted between them, refining it into the taintless. Here, it is said: the nine sequential concentrations, perfuming and cultivating the tainted into the taintless, is the karma of the non-returner. The four formless concentrations are the karma of the formless realm.

如是等二十一有，患自地之苦麁，欲修出要，所求不得，所捨不離；爾時即名，可發關宜之機，感二十一三昧慈悲之力，破其修因，令所離得去，所求得成，拔苦與樂，冥顯兩益。此如經文云：「小草、小根、小莖、小枝、小葉，而得生長。」是此益也。合而言之，蓋由凡聖慈善根力；別而言之，本由菩薩初持二百五十戒，修根本等禪，於一一能防善法之中，皆起慈悲。慈悲本誓，熏王三昧，不捨眾生，赴對關宜，各得利益。《大經》云：「二十五三昧，破二十五有」者，十番之二意，略如此(云云)。

Among these twenty-one states of existence such as these, troubled by the coarse suffering of their own realm, wishing to cultivate a way out, yet what is sought is not attained, and what ought to be relinquished is not left behind — at that very moment, this is named the occasion fitting to be brought forth, moving the compassionate power of the twenty-one samādhis, breaking through the cause being cultivated, causing what ought to be departed from to be departed from, and what is sought to be accomplished, drawing out suffering and bestowing bliss, obtaining both the hidden and the manifest benefit. This is as the sūtra's own text says: "The small grasses, their small roots, small stems, small twigs, and small leaves, thereby grow and flourish" — this is precisely this same benefit. Discussed together, this is due to the power of the wholesome root of loving-kindness shared by ordinary being and sage alike; discussed separately, it is originally due to the bodhisattva's own first upholding the two hundred and fifty precepts, cultivating the root dhyānas and the rest, and, within each and every dharma able to guard wholesomeness, giving rise to loving-kindness and compassion. This loving-kindness and compassion, the original vow, perfuming the king of samādhis, never abandoning sentient beings, hastening toward and responding to whatever occasion is fitting, each thereby obtains this benefit. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra's own account of the twenty-five samādhis breaking through the twenty-five states of existence, the second of ten rounds — the sense is, in brief, exactly this, and so on.

三番、聲聞利益者，若人厭患生死，以死受生，以生歸死，勞累精神，輪轉無際；貪欲自蔽，犛牛愛尾，不得解脫。故言若人遭苦，厭老病死，為說涅槃，盡諸苦際；既厭心內決，志求出離，為是事故，修聲聞道。若持戒時，愛見羅剎，毀損浮囊，令戒不淨。戒不淨故，三昧不現前。既無戒定，無漏不發，是故一心修戒定慧，有可發關宜之機，感無垢等四三昧力加之，令四趣業不起，使戒清淨。若均修定慧，慧若有定，慧不狂；定若有慧，定不愚，名之為賢，賢名隣聖。修此定慧，一心精進如救頭然。願樂禪慧，如渴思飲，而為二十一有漏業之所擾亂，若得諸三昧力加之，定發觀明，四善根成就，伏道純熟，一剎那轉即便發真，成須陀洹，破二十五有見諦煩惱、八十八使。是為二十五三昧通加，令斷見諦惑，而復兼除四思。故云：第十六心即入修道，是其義也。次入修道，若是超人，一時用十三昧力加之，破五下分思惟惑。若鈍人，隨其分分斷思惑，則分分用三昧加之，盡三界惑，究竟真諦三昧益也。此中草利益。合而言之，蓋由凡聖慈善根力；別而言之，由本慈悲，初觀十法界中析空滅色之善，因起弘誓，熏王三昧，不捨眾生，致有中草利益。《大經》明二十五三昧破二十五有者，十番之三，意略如此。

Third round, as for the benefit of the śrāvaka: if a person, weary of and troubled by birth-and-death, takes on birth through death and returns to death through birth, wearing down their own spirit, revolving without end, deluded by their own desire, like a yak clinging to its own tail, unable to attain liberation — hence it is said: if a person meets with suffering, weary of old age, sickness, and death, the Buddha teaches nirvāṇa for their sake, bringing every suffering fully to an end. Having, within, resolved a mind of weariness, aspiring to seek release, for the sake of this same matter, one cultivates the śrāvaka's own path. Yet, at the very time of upholding the precepts, if the rākṣasa of craving and views damages the very float one clings to, this causes the precepts to become impure. Since the precepts are impure, samādhi does not manifest before one. Without precepts and concentration, the taintless is not brought forth — hence, one cultivates precepts, concentration, and wisdom with a single mind, and there is here an occasion fitting to be brought forth, moving the power of the stainless and the other three samādhis to assist one, causing the karma of the four evil destinies not to arise, and the precepts to become pure. If concentration and wisdom are cultivated equally — wisdom, when accompanied by concentration, is not wild and unruly; concentration, when accompanied by wisdom, is not blind and foolish — this is named the worthy, and the worthy is named neighbor to the sage. Cultivating this same concentration and wisdom with a single mind, diligent as though saving one's own head from fire, longing for dhyāna and wisdom as one thirsting longs for drink — and yet troubled by the twenty-one tainted forms of karma — if one obtains the power of these same samādhis to assist one, concentration brings forth a clear contemplation, the four good roots are accomplished, the path of subduing matures fully, and, in a single instant, one is transformed and brings forth the real at once, becoming a stream-enterer, breaking through the affliction of the twenty-five states of existence' own views of the truths, the eighty-eight kinds of latent tendency. This is the twenty-five samādhis' own general assistance, causing the affliction of views of the truths to be severed, while, further, also removing the four kinds of thought. Hence it is said: the sixteenth mind already enters the path of cultivation — this is precisely this same sense. Next, entering the path of cultivation: if one is a person who leaps ahead, the power of thirteen samādhis is used all at once to assist one, breaking through the five lower fetters' own afflictions of thought. If one is a dull-faculty person, according with each portion severed of the affliction of thought, the samādhis are used, portion by portion, to assist one, exhausting the affliction of all three realms, the ultimate benefit of the real truth's own samādhi. This is the benefit of the middling grass. Discussed together, this is due to the power of the wholesome root of loving-kindness shared by ordinary being and sage alike; discussed separately, it is due to the original loving-kindness and compassion, first contemplating, among the ten dharma-realms, the goodness of analytical emptiness that extinguishes form, giving rise, through this cause, to the great vow, perfuming the king of samādhis, never abandoning sentient beings, bringing about this benefit of the middling grass. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra's own account of the twenty-five samādhis breaking through the twenty-five states of existence, the third of ten rounds — the sense is, in brief, exactly this.

四番、緣覺利益者，若人宿善深利，在無佛世厭患生死，樂獨善寂，觀深因緣。文云：「曾供養佛，志求勝法，為說緣覺。」此人大福可發關宜，聖人赴對應之，使其華飛釧動，獲冥顯兩益，悟支佛道。此猶屬中草利益也。

Fourth round, as for the benefit of the pratyekabuddha: if a person's own former goodness is deep and sharp, and, in an age without a buddha, weary of and troubled by birth-and-death, delights in solitary, wholesome quiescence, contemplating the depths of dependent origination — the text says: "Having formerly made offering to buddhas, aspiring to seek the supreme dharma, the Buddha teaches pratyekabuddhahood for their sake." This person's own great merit is able to bring forth an occasion fitting for this, and the sage, hastening toward them, responds accordingly, causing flowers to fly and bracelets to stir of themselves, obtaining both the hidden and the manifest benefit, awakening to the pratyekabuddha's own path. This, too, still belongs to the benefit of the middling grass.

五番、六度菩薩，觀於四諦，行六度行。若行檀時，人從乞頭、索眼、國城、妻子，心或轉動，檀度不成，自知是惡。欲成檀善，可發關宜之機，蒙三昧力，伏其慳蔽，是破餓鬼有。蔽心既去，歡喜布施，如飲甘露。知有為法，危脆無常，是蒙心樂三昧，冥顯之益也。尸羅若成，是伏毀戒蔽，破地獄有，是無垢三昧益也。忍成伏瞋蔽，破畜生有，不退三昧益也。禪成是伏亂蔽，破人有，是四三昧益也。精進成伏懈怠蔽，破脩羅有，歡喜三昧益也。慧成伏愚癡蔽，破天有，十七三昧益也。六蔽是六道業，具出菩薩戒本。以伏六道業故，不為諸蔽所惱，得五神通，遊於六道，成六度行。此即上草益也。通應如前，別而論之，由本觀十法界事中善惡，而起弘誓，熏三三昧，不捨眾生(云云)。

Fifth round, as for the bodhisattva of the six pāramitās, contemplating the Four Noble Truths and practicing the six pāramitās' own practice: at the very time of practicing giving, when someone comes asking for one's own head, one's own eyes, one's kingdom, or one's wife and children, the mind, at times, wavers, and the pāramitā of giving fails to be accomplished — one knows of oneself that this is an evil. Wishing to accomplish the goodness of giving, there is here an occasion fitting to be brought forth, and, graced by the power of samādhi, the obstruction of stinginess is subdued — this is breaking through the state of existence that is the hungry ghost. Once this obstructing mind has departed, one delights in giving as though drinking the sweet dew itself — knowing every conditioned dharma to be precarious, fragile, and impermanent — this is graced by the samādhi of the mind's own joy, the benefit both hidden and manifest. If moral discipline is accomplished, this subdues the obstruction of breaking the precepts, breaking through the state of existence that is hell — this is the benefit of the stainless samādhi. If patience is accomplished, this subdues the obstruction of anger, breaking through the state of existence that is the animal realm — this is the benefit of the non-retrogressing samādhi. If meditative concentration is accomplished, this subdues the obstruction of a scattered mind, breaking through the state of existence that is the human realm — this is the benefit of the fourth samādhi. If diligence is accomplished, this subdues the obstruction of laziness, breaking through the state of existence that is the asura — this is the benefit of the samādhi of joy. If wisdom is accomplished, this subdues the obstruction of stupidity and delusion, breaking through the state of existence that is the heavens — this is the benefit of the seventeenth samādhi. These six obstructions are the karma of the six destinies, fully set out within the bodhisattva's own precept-text. Since the karma of the six destinies is subdued, one is no longer troubled by any of these obstructions, attaining the five spiritual powers, roaming freely through the six destinies, and accomplishing the practice of the six pāramitās. This is precisely the benefit of the superior grass. This applies generally as before; discussed separately, it is due to the bodhisattva's own original contemplation of the good and evil present within the affairs of the ten dharma-realms, giving rise, through this, to the great vow, perfuming the three samādhis, never abandoning sentient beings, and so on.

六番、通人益者，此是三乘共學人也。若乾慧地、性地、八人見地，即是用二十五三昧益。從薄地去至十地，用二十一三昧，加破思惑。又侵除無知，是名小樹益也。總別慈悲，例前可知(云云)。

七番、別人益者，此是次第心中繫緣法界，一念法界入十住，得真諦三昧益；入十行、十迴向，得俗諦三昧益；入十地得中諦三昧益。此即大樹之益。總別慈悲，例前(云云)。

Sixth round, as for the benefit of the shared teaching's own practitioner: this is the practitioner shared in study by all three vehicles. At the ground of dry wisdom, the ground of nature, and the ground of the eight kinds of person and the ground of seeing, this is precisely the benefit using the twenty-five samādhis. From the ground of thinning onward through the tenth ground, the twenty-one samādhis are used, assisting in breaking through the affliction of thought. Further encroaching upon and removing ignorance, this is named the benefit of the small tree. Loving-kindness and compassion, both general and particular, follow this same pattern as before, and so on.

Seventh round, as for the benefit of the distinct teaching's own practitioner: this is the sequential mind binding its own attention to the dharma-realm — a single thought-moment of the dharma-realm entering the ten abidings obtains the benefit of the real truth's own samādhi; entering the ten practices and ten dedications obtains the benefit of the conventional truth's own samādhi; entering the ten grounds obtains the benefit of the middle truth's own samādhi. This is precisely the benefit of the great tree. Loving-kindness and compassion, both general and particular, follow this same pattern as before, and so on.

八番、圓人益者，此是修三諦一實之理。一念法界，繫緣法界。若歷緣對境，舉足下足無非道場，其心念念與諸波羅蜜相應，修四三昧觀十種境。可發關宜，聖人赴對應之，豁然開悟。或似或真，得冥顯兩益。此是圓用二十五三昧圓加破二十五有，顯出我性，得究竟實事之益也。

Eighth round, as for the benefit of the perfect teaching's own practitioner: this is cultivating the three truths as a single, real principle. A single thought-moment of the dharma-realm binds its own attention to the dharma-realm. Whether passing through conditions or facing objects, every step taken, whether raised or set down, is nowhere but the very place of enlightenment; the mind, moment after moment, corresponds to every pāramitā, cultivating the four samādhis and contemplating the ten kinds of objects. An occasion fitting to be brought forth may arise, and the sage, hastening toward it, responds accordingly, awakening suddenly and completely — whether in resemblance or in reality, obtaining both the hidden and the manifest benefit. This is the perfect teaching's own use of the twenty-five samādhis, perfectly assisting in breaking through the twenty-five states of existence, revealing one's own true nature, and obtaining the ultimate benefit of the most real matter.

Reading note

The second round's move from a many-people, many-places account of benefit (the fruit) to a single-person account (the cause) is a deliberate methodological pivot: a single mind, in a single lifetime, can cycle through every one of the twenty-five states of existence' own karmic causes, so that the whole cosmology surveyed in round one collapses into an ordinary person's own vacillation between precept-breaking and repentance, envy and generosity, laziness and diligence. Rounds three through eight then climb the site's established stage-ladders one at a time — the śrāvaka's own path from the sixteenth mind's entry into the path of cultivation through arhatship, the pratyekabuddha, the six-pāramitā bodhisattva's six obstructions mapped one-to-one onto the six destinies, and finally the shared, distinct, and perfect teachings' own versions of the small tree, great tree, and most-real-matter benefit already established via the "Parable of the Medicinal Herbs" back in Fascicle 4 — confirming that the whole apparatus of the wondrous of stage and the wondrous of the three tracks was, from the outset, also an account of benefit received.
