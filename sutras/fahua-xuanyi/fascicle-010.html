Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine

Fascicle 10, the treatise's last, closes Clarifying the Essence (顯體) with the real mark as the essence of every dharma, then turns to the third of the Five Sections, Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine (明宗).

Status: in progress Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

The Real Mark as the Essence of Every Dharma 遍一切法體

Chinese Source

○第七、遍為一切法體者，《觀經》云：「毘盧遮那遍一切處。」一切不出四諦。《大經》云：「佛所不說，如十方土；所說者，如爪上土。」迦葉云：「已說是四諦，其未說者，應有五諦。」佛言：無也，但言是四諦有無量相耳。若然，廣開即成四種四諦，具如境妙中說。當知苦集，世間善惡因果；道滅，出世一切因果；悉用實相為體。《淨名》曰：「從無住本，立一切法」，此之謂乎？

然所依之體，體妙無異；能依之法，法有麁妙。諸法相待，分別可知。歷五味分別，麁妙亦可知。開麁顯妙，亦可知(云云)。略說經體竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, pervading as the essence of every dharma: the Contemplation Sūtra says, "Vairocana pervades every place." Everything, without exception, does not go beyond the Four Noble Truths. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "What the Buddha has not taught is like the soil of the ten directions; what he has taught is like the soil upon a single fingernail." Kāśyapa says: "Since these Four Noble Truths have already been taught, ought there not also be a fifth truth not yet taught?" The Buddha says: there is none — it is simply that these same Four Noble Truths possess immeasurable marks. If this is so, then, opened out broadly, they become the four kinds of Four Noble Truths, exactly as explained in full within the wondrous of the object. One should know that suffering and its arising are the causes and effects, good and evil, of the mundane world; the path and cessation are every cause and effect of what transcends the world — all of these, without exception, take the real mark as their essence. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says, "From the root of non-abiding, every dharma is established" — is this not precisely this same sense?

Yet the essence that is depended upon, this essence's own wondrousness admits of no distinction at all; the dharma that depends upon it, this dharma does indeed have both coarse and wondrous aspects. Every dharma, set against one another in this way, may be distinguished and known accordingly. Traced through the Five Flavors, this same distinction, too, coarse and wondrous, may likewise be known. Opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous, too, may likewise be known, and so on. The sūtra's own essence has now been briefly explained, and is complete.

Reading note

This shortest of the seven headings closes Clarifying the Essence with the broadest possible reach: not merely every sūtra or every practice, but every dharma whatsoever, sorted entirely within the Four Noble Truths taken in their fourfold form already established back in the wondrous of the object. The soil-on-a-fingernail image against the soil-of-the-ten-directions is a striking admission of scale — everything the Buddha has ever taught is, by his own account, a vanishingly small sample of everything there is to teach — yet Kāśyapa's own question, whether a fifth truth remains untaught, is refused outright: there is no fifth truth, only the same four truths under immeasurable further marks. This closes pervading as the essence of every dharma (遍一切法體) in its entirety — the seventh and final heading under Clarifying the Essence, and, with it, Clarifying the Essence (顯體) itself, the second of the treatise's Five Sections, complete in its entirety. The treatise continues with its third Section, Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine (明宗).

Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine 明宗

Chinese Source

△大章第三、明宗。宗者，修行之喉衿，顯體之要蹊，如梁柱持屋，結網綱維，提維則目動，梁安則桷存。釋宗為五：一、簡宗體。二、正明宗。三、眾經同異。四、明麁妙。五、結因果。

簡宗體者，有人言：宗即是體，體即是宗。今所不用。何者？宗致既是因果，因果即二體，非因非果，體即不二。體若是二，體即非體；體若不二，體即非宗。宗若不二，宗即非宗；宗若是二，宗即非體。云何而言：體即是宗，宗即是體？又，柱梁是屋之綱維，屋空是梁柱所取，不應以梁柱是屋空，屋空是梁柱。宗、體若一，其過如是。又，宗、體異者，則二法孤調，宗非顯體之宗，體非宗家之體。宗非顯體之宗，宗則邪倒無印；體非宗家之體，則體狹不周，離法性外，別有因果。宗體若異，其過如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third major chapter, Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine. The cardinal doctrine is the very throat and collar of cultivated practice, the essential path leading to Clarifying the Essence — like the beams and pillars that hold up a house, or the guiding cords that give a net its structure: pull the guiding cord and the net's own mesh moves; let the beam rest secure and the rafters remain in place. Explaining the cardinal doctrine is treated under five senses: first, distinguishing the cardinal doctrine from the essence; second, directly clarifying the cardinal doctrine; third, the sameness and difference across the various sūtras; fourth, clarifying coarse and wondrous; fifth, drawing cause and fruit to a close.

As for distinguishing the cardinal doctrine from the essence: some say that the cardinal doctrine is itself the essence, and the essence is itself the cardinal doctrine. Here, this is not adopted. Why is this? The cardinal doctrine's own aim is precisely cause and fruit, and cause and fruit are themselves two aspects of the essence; yet what is neither cause nor fruit — the essence itself — is not two at all. If the essence were two, the essence would no longer be the essence; if the essence is not two, the essence is not the cardinal doctrine. If the cardinal doctrine were not two, the cardinal doctrine would no longer be the cardinal doctrine; if the cardinal doctrine is two, the cardinal doctrine is not the essence. How, then, could one possibly say that the essence is itself the cardinal doctrine, and the cardinal doctrine is itself the essence? Again, the pillar and the beam are the very guiding structure of the house; the house's own empty space is what the beam and pillar make available — one ought not to take the beam and pillar as themselves the house's own empty space, nor the house's own empty space as itself the beam and pillar. If the cardinal doctrine and the essence were one and the same, this same fault would follow. Again, if the cardinal doctrine and the essence were entirely different, then these two dharmas would stand isolated and unrelated to one another — the cardinal doctrine would no longer be the cardinal doctrine that clarifies the essence, and the essence would no longer be the essence belonging to the cardinal doctrine's own household. If the cardinal doctrine were not the cardinal doctrine that clarifies the essence, the cardinal doctrine would be deviant and inverted, without any seal of authenticity at all; if the essence were not the essence belonging to the cardinal doctrine's own household, then the essence would be narrow and incomplete, with some separate cause and fruit existing apart from the dharma-nature itself. If the cardinal doctrine and the essence were entirely different, this same fault would follow.

Reading note

Opening the treatise's third Section with a rejection of the identity theory (cardinal doctrine and essence as simply the same thing) reprises the beam-and-pillar image from Clarifying the Essence's own opening, but now argues the opposite direction: there, the point was that beam and pillar are not the house's empty space; here, the point is that the essence's own empty space is likewise never simply identical with the beams and pillars — cause and fruit — that the cardinal doctrine is actually about. The dilemma structure (identity fails one way, difference fails the other way) previews the resolution the next section will need to supply: neither collapsing the two together nor severing them apart, but showing exactly how the cardinal doctrine's own cause-and-fruit structure depends upon, without being identical to, the single essence it expresses.

Chinese Source

今言不異而異，約非因非果而論因果，故有宗體之別耳。《釋論》云：「若離諸法實相，皆名魔事。」《普賢觀》云：「大乘因者，諸法實相。大乘果者，亦諸法實相。」即其義也。當知實相體通，而非因果，行始辨因，行終論果。而復偏圓有別者，譬如銅體，非始非終，擬鑄為像，即名像始；治瑩悉畢，即名像終；此譬圓因果。若擬器皿及其成就，器皿始終，譬偏因果也。發七方便心謂偏因，證有餘、無餘名偏果；開佛知見名圓因，究竟妙覺名圓果。若識此喻，不即不離，宗義明矣。

例如正因佛性，非因非果，而言是因非果名佛性，是果非因名大涅槃。又佛性非當非本，而言本自有之，一切眾生即涅槃相，不可復滅。又言一切眾生悉有佛性，而實未有三十二相，未來當得金剛之身，以其非當，是故言本；以其非本，是故言當。宗體之義，亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, then, it is said that they are not different and yet are different: in terms of what is neither cause nor fruit, cause and fruit are nonetheless discussed — hence there is this same distinction between the cardinal doctrine and the essence. The Mahāprajñāpāramitopadeśa says, "Whatever departs from the real mark of all dharmas is, in every case, named a demon's own undertaking." The Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra says, "The Great Vehicle's own cause is the real mark of all dharmas; the Great Vehicle's own fruit is likewise the real mark of all dharmas" — precisely this same sense. One should know that the real mark's own essence pervades throughout, and yet is itself neither cause nor fruit — practice, at its very beginning, distinguishes the cause; practice, at its very end, discusses the fruit. And yet, further, the one-sided and the perfect differ in this respect: it is comparable to the very substance of copper, itself neither a beginning nor an end — cast into the mold to form a statue, this is named the statue's own beginning; polished and finished entirely, this is named the statue's own end — this illustrates the perfect teaching's own cause and fruit. If, instead, one casts it as a vessel or bowl, and this same vessel or bowl is brought to completion, the vessel's own beginning and end illustrate the one-sided teaching's own cause and fruit. Arousing the mind of the seven skillful means is called the one-sided cause; realizing nirvāṇa with residue or without residue is named the one-sided fruit; opening the Buddha's own knowledge and vision is named the perfect cause; the ultimate, wondrous enlightenment is named the perfect fruit. If one recognizes this same illustration, neither identical nor separate, the sense of the cardinal doctrine becomes clear.

It is comparable to the correct-cause buddha-nature, itself neither cause nor fruit, and yet spoken of as: being the cause, not the fruit, it is named buddha-nature; being the fruit, not the cause, it is named great nirvāṇa. Again, buddha-nature is neither something yet to come nor something already possessed from the start, and yet it is said that it is originally, of itself, already possessed — every sentient being is itself already the mark of nirvāṇa, never again to be extinguished. It is also said that every sentient being possesses buddha-nature in full, and yet, in reality, does not yet possess the thirty-two marks, only to attain, in the future, the vajra body — precisely because it is not yet come, it is accordingly spoken of as already possessed from the start; and precisely because it is not already possessed from the start, it is accordingly spoken of as yet to come. The sense of the cardinal doctrine and the essence is exactly the same.

遠師以一乘為宗，所謂妙法。引文云：「是乘微妙，為無有上。」私謂為破三故一，待麁非妙因，而不該始末。

龍師云：但以果為宗。妙法者，是如來靈智體也。眾麁斯盡為妙，動靜軌物為法，法既真妙，借蓮華譬之，所以果智為宗也。私謂果不孤立，云何棄其因，又乖文也。

慧觀序云：「會三歸一，乘之始也；慧覺成滿，乘之盛也；滅影澄神，乘之終也。」什師歎云：「若非深入經藏，豈能作如此說？」印師云：諸法實相是一乘妙境，用境智為宗，境無三偽，故稱實相也。今謂加境而闕果，腫不益肥。光宅用一乘因果為宗，前段為因，後段為果。私謂二文各有因果，若互存互沒，則害經文。

Master Huiyuan takes the one vehicle as the cardinal doctrine, namely the wondrous dharma itself, citing the text: "This vehicle is subtle and wondrous, having nothing above it." Here, I would say: this "one" exists only for the sake of breaking through the "three" — set against the coarse, it is not yet the wondrous cause, and does not encompass the whole from beginning to end.

Master Long says: the cardinal doctrine is the fruit alone. "Wondrous dharma" is precisely the very essence of the Tathāgata's own numinous wisdom — every coarseness exhausted is "wondrous"; moving and stilling, modeling every thing, is "dharma"; the dharma being truly wondrous, the lotus flower is borrowed to illustrate it — hence the fruit of wisdom is taken as the cardinal doctrine. Here, I would say: the fruit does not stand in isolation — how, then, could its own cause be discarded? This, too, runs contrary to the sūtra's own text.

Master Huiguan's own preface says: "Gathering the three into the one is the vehicle's own beginning; wisdom and awakening brought to full completion is the vehicle's own flourishing; extinguishing the shadow and clarifying the spirit is the vehicle's own end." Master Kumārajīva praised this, saying: "Were one not deeply versed in the sūtra-treasury itself, how could one possibly speak in such a way?" Master Yin says: the real mark of all dharmas is the one vehicle's own wondrous object; taking the object and the wisdom that knows it as the cardinal doctrine, the object being free of the three falsehoods, it is accordingly named "the real mark." Here, I would say: adding the object while lacking the fruit is like swelling without adding true flesh. Guangzhai takes the one vehicle's own cause and fruit together as the cardinal doctrine, the earlier portion of the text being the cause, and the later portion being the fruit. Here, I would say: each of the two portions of the text has its own cause and fruit — if one is retained while the other is discarded in each case, this does violence to the sūtra's own text.

有人用權實二智為宗。私謂用權，應明三是經宗；三是今經所棄，云何取所棄為宗？

又師云：此名「妙法蓮華」，即以名為宗，妙法是佛所得根本真實法性，此性不異惑染，不與惑同，故稱妙，即宗為名耳。此是地師所用，據八識是極果，今《攝大乘》破之，謂是生死根本。

有師云：常住為宗，但未極上，是覆相明常。私謂都非經意；常若被覆，宗何所顯？常不被覆，常則非宗。

有師云：是顯了明常，與《涅槃》為廣略耳。私謂常為宗者，常無因果，常亦無宗(云云)。

有言：萬善為宗，但使是善皆得作佛。私謂若作佛即是果，何不取果為宗？

有言：萬善中取無漏為宗。私謂太局，又濫小涅槃。

Some take the two kinds of wisdom, provisional and real, as the cardinal doctrine. Here, I would say: if the provisional is used, one ought to clarify that the three vehicles are this sūtra's own cardinal doctrine — yet the three vehicles are precisely what this present sūtra sets aside — how, then, could what is set aside be taken as the cardinal doctrine?

Another master says: this is named "wondrous dharma lotus flower," and hence the very name itself is taken as the cardinal doctrine — "wondrous dharma" being the fundamental, true, and real dharma-nature the Buddha himself has attained, this nature being no different from affliction and defilement, and yet not identical with affliction either, and hence called "wondrous" — the cardinal doctrine is here simply the name itself. This is what the Dilun masters make use of, holding that the eighth consciousness is the ultimate fruit; the Mahāyānasaṃgraha, in our own time, refutes this, holding it to be, instead, the very root of birth-and-death.

Another master says: eternal abiding is the cardinal doctrine, though not yet at the very summit — this is clarifying permanence while its own mark still remains concealed. Here, I would say: this is entirely not the sūtra's own intent at all — if permanence remains concealed, what, then, does the cardinal doctrine actually reveal? And if permanence is not concealed, then permanence is not the cardinal doctrine either.

Another master says: this is clarifying permanence openly and plainly, differing from the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra only in being broader or more concise. Here, I would say: if permanence itself were the cardinal doctrine, permanence would have no cause and fruit, and permanence, too, would then have no cardinal doctrine at all, and so on.

Some say: the ten thousand forms of good are the cardinal doctrine, since whatever is good, in every case, attains buddhahood. Here, I would say: if attaining buddhahood is itself the fruit, why not simply take the fruit as the cardinal doctrine?

Some say: among the ten thousand forms of good, the untainted alone is taken as the cardinal doctrine. Here, I would say: this is far too narrow, and, moreover, risks being confused with the Hīnayāna's own lesser nirvāṇa.

有人言：若稟斯異說各蒙益者，眾釋無可為非；聞而不悟，眾師無可為是。一師之意，唯貴在悟，宜以悟為經宗。《大經》云：「若有定相是生死法，是魔王相；佛法無定相。」是故如來非道說道、道說非道，當知唯悟是從。私謂若悟為宗，乃是果證，非謂行因。

問南指北，方隅料亂。又定以悟為宗，是為定定，何謂不定？說者甚多，不能具出也。

Some say: if one receives these various differing accounts and each, in turn, benefits from them, then none of these interpretations can be called wrong; yet if one hears them and remains unawakened, then none of these masters can be called right either. This one master's own intent values solely awakening itself, and holds that awakening alone ought to be taken as the sūtra's own cardinal doctrine. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Whatever bears a fixed mark is the dharma of birth-and-death, the mark of the demon-king himself; the Buddhadharma bears no fixed mark at all." Hence the Tathāgata sometimes speaks of what is not the path as the path, and sometimes speaks of the path as what is not the path — one should know that awakening alone is what is truly followed. Here, I would say: if awakening itself is taken as the cardinal doctrine, this is, in fact, the realized fruit, not what is meant by the cultivated cause.

Asking about the south while pointing toward the north — the very directions themselves become confused and disordered. And, further, if one fixedly takes awakening as the cardinal doctrine, this is itself a fixed position — how, then, could it be called "without fixed mark" at all? The various accounts given are exceedingly numerous, and cannot all be listed here in full.

Reading note

The copper-cast-as-statue-versus-copper-cast-as-vessel image is this treatise's clearest illustration yet of why "essence" and "cardinal doctrine" must be distinguished without being separated: the same copper (essence) becomes a statue or a vessel (cardinal doctrine, cause-and-fruit) depending on the mold it is cast into, and asking whether the copper "is" the statue mistakes a material for what is made from it. The survey of prior masters that follows is this treatise's most extensive single catalogue of named, individually rebutted rivals in the entire work — nine distinct positions (one vehicle alone, fruit alone, object-and-wisdom, cause-and-fruit split across the text, the two wisdoms, the title's own name, concealed permanence, unconcealed permanence, the ten thousand goods, awakening itself) each given a real citation and a real one-line refutation, none dismissed lightly. The recurring pattern of rebuttal is worth noting: nearly every rival theory fails not because it is false but because it captures only half of a pair (fruit without cause, object without wisdom, the "three" without the "one") that this sūtra insists on holding together.

Directly Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine 正明宗

Chinese Source

二、正明宗者，此經始從〈序品〉訖〈安樂行品〉，破廢方便，開顯真實佛之知見，亦明弟子實因實果，亦明師門權因權果。文義雖廣，撮其樞要，為成弟子實因，因正果傍，故於前段明迹因迹果也。從〈涌出品〉訖〈勸發品〉，發迹顯本，廢方便之近壽，明長遠之實果。亦明弟子實因實果，亦明師門權因權果，而顯師之實果，果正因傍，故於後段明本因本果。合前因果共為經宗，意在於此。所以經分二文，論本論迹，雙題法譬，舉蓮舉華，師弟權實，總在其間也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, directly clarifying the cardinal doctrine: this sūtra, beginning from the "Introduction" chapter through to "Peaceful Practices," breaks through and sets aside skillful means, opening and disclosing the Buddha's own true and real knowledge and vision, while also clarifying the disciples' own real cause and real fruit, and also clarifying the teacher's own provisional cause and provisional fruit. Though its text and sense are broad, gathering their essential point, it is for the sake of bringing to completion the disciples' own real cause — the cause being correct, and the fruit peripheral — hence, within this earlier portion, the trace's own cause and the trace's own fruit are clarified. From the "Emerging from the Earth" chapter through to "Samantabhadra's Encouragement," the trace is disclosed to reveal the origin, setting aside the near life-span taken as a mere skillful means, and clarifying the vast, distant real fruit — while also clarifying the disciples' own real cause and real fruit, and also clarifying the teacher's own provisional cause and provisional fruit — yet, here, it is the teacher's own real fruit that is revealed, the fruit being correct, and the cause peripheral, hence, within this later portion, the origin's own cause and the origin's own fruit are clarified. Bringing together the cause and fruit discussed in both portions as, together, this sūtra's own cardinal doctrine — the intent lies precisely here. This is why the sūtra is divided into two portions of text, discussing the origin and discussing the trace, doubly titled by both dharma and metaphor, citing both the lotus and the flower — the teacher and the disciples, the provisional and the real, are, every one, contained entirely within this same structure.

The Sameness and Difference of Cause and Fruit Across the Various Sūtras 眾經因果同異

Chinese Source

三、眾經因果同異者，謂迹因果或同或異，本因果永異迹。因果者，實相通印諸體，何經不約此論因果？《大品》明非因非果，實相為體，而但因為宗般若遣蕩，正是因意。故云：「菩薩心中名般若，在佛心中名薩婆若。」文中亦說菩薩無生無滅因，獲不斷不常薩婆若果。叡師序云：「啟玄章以不住為始，歸三慧以無得為終。」終始，因果也。文中亦說一切種智佛果，為成般若因，因正果傍。無量義宣說摩訶般若，歷劫修行，故知彼經用因為宗也。

《淨名》用佛國因果兩義為宗，寶積具問因果，佛備答因果。故知雙用因果為宗也。《華嚴》圓頓之教，解宗不同。或言用因為宗，據題言華嚴是萬行莊飾，修因之義。文中多說四十地行相，故用因為宗。又云果為宗，據題云大方廣佛，佛是極果之名，華嚴是定慧萬善，莊嚴佛身，非莊嚴因。文中多說舍那法身之事，即用果為宗也。又解云：因果合為宗，如言佛即是果，華嚴即是因，文中具說法身，亦說諸地，俱用因果為宗。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the sameness and difference of cause and fruit across the various sūtras: this refers to the trace's own cause and fruit, sometimes the same, sometimes differing; the origin's own cause and fruit are permanently different from the trace's. As for cause and fruit: the real mark, its seal running commonly through every essence — what sūtra does not discuss cause and fruit in terms of this same seal? The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra clarifies what is neither cause nor fruit, the real mark, as the essence, and yet takes the cause alone as the cardinal doctrine — prajñā's own sweeping away and dispelling is precisely the sense of the cause. Hence it says, "Within the bodhisattva's own mind, it is named prajñā; within the buddha's own mind, it is named the wisdom that knows all things." The text likewise speaks of the bodhisattva's own unborn, unceasing cause, obtaining the wisdom that knows all things as a fruit neither cut off nor eternal. Master Sengrui's own preface says: "Opening the profound chapter, it begins with non-abiding; returning to the three wisdoms, it ends with attainment-of-nothing" — this "end" and "beginning" are precisely cause and fruit. The text likewise speaks of the buddha's own fruit, the wisdom knowing all modes, as brought about for the sake of accomplishing prajñā's own cause — the cause being correct, and the fruit peripheral. The Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings proclaims the great prajñā, cultivated across countless eons — one thereby knows that that sūtra takes the cause as its own cardinal doctrine.

The Vimalakīrti Sūtra takes both the buddha-land's own cause and fruit together as its cardinal doctrine — Ratnākara fully questions both cause and fruit, and the Buddha fully answers both cause and fruit. One thereby knows that this sūtra takes both cause and fruit jointly as its own cardinal doctrine. The Avataṃsaka's own perfect and sudden teaching is explained differently as to its cardinal doctrine. Some say the cause alone is taken as the cardinal doctrine, basing this on the title itself: "Flower Adornment" being the sense of the ten thousand practices' own adorning splendor, the cultivation of the cause. The text speaks extensively of the conduct-marks of the forty grounds, hence the cause is taken as the cardinal doctrine. Others say the fruit is the cardinal doctrine, basing this on the title's own "great, vast, and expansive Buddha" — "Buddha" being the very name of the ultimate fruit, and "Flower Adornment" being concentration, wisdom, and the ten thousand forms of good adorning the buddha's own body, not adorning the cause. The text speaks extensively of Vairocana's own Dharma-body, hence the fruit is taken as the cardinal doctrine. Yet another interpretation says: cause and fruit together form the cardinal doctrine, as when it is said that "Buddha" is itself the fruit, and "Flower Adornment" is itself the cause; the text fully speaks of the Dharma-body, and likewise speaks of the various grounds, both cause and fruit together taken as the cardinal doctrine.

諸經對緣不同，故明宗互異耳。《般若》通對三人，傍真因果，此義則異。別對菩薩，復有利鈍，為鈍明因，此義亦異。利人因者，此義則同。《淨名》佛國義兼，若三種佛國因果，此義則異；一種佛國因果者則同。《華嚴》亦對兩緣鈍異利同，如前分別。又將此意歷五味因果，例可知。是為眾經因果與迹門同異之相也。

二、本門因果永異眾經者，若三藏菩薩始行實因果，無權因果，乃至明佛道樹始成，非久遠本迹。通教菩薩亦始行因，神通變化而論本迹，非久遠本迹也。《大品》說菩薩有本迹，二乘則無，說佛始得生法二身本迹，不說久遠。《淨名》不說聲聞有本迹，但明菩薩住不思議之本迹。說佛有淨土，螺髻所見亦非久遠。《華嚴》說舍那釋迦為本迹，菩薩亦有本迹，聲聞尚不聞不解，云何自有本迹？

The various sūtras, addressing differing conditions, accordingly clarify their own cardinal doctrine differently. The Perfection of Wisdom teachings address the three kinds of persons together, the peripheral real cause and fruit — in this sense, they differ. Addressed particularly to the bodhisattva, there is, further, both the sharp and the dull faculty — for the dull faculty, the cause is clarified — in this sense, too, they differ; for the sharp faculty's own cause, in this sense, they are the same. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra's own sense of the buddha-land is combined — where there are three kinds of buddha-land cause and fruit, in this sense, they differ; where there is a single kind of buddha-land cause and fruit, they are the same. The Avataṃsaka, too, addresses two conditions, differing for the dull and the same for the sharp faculty, exactly as distinguished before. Taking up this same sense and tracing it through the Five Flavors' own cause and fruit, the pattern may thereby be known. This, then, is the mark of sameness and difference between the various sūtras' own cause and fruit and this present sūtra's own trace gate.

Second, the origin gate's own cause and fruit permanently differ from every other sūtra: if the Tripiṭaka teaching's own bodhisattva only just begins to practice the real cause and fruit, with no provisional cause and fruit at all, and clarifies only that the Buddha first accomplished the path beneath the bodhi tree — this is not the vast, distant trace and origin. The shared teaching's own bodhisattva likewise only just begins to practice the cause, and, in terms of spiritual transformation, discusses trace and origin — this, too, is not the vast, distant trace and origin. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra speaks of the bodhisattva as possessing trace and origin, while the two lesser vehicles do not, and speaks of the Buddha as only just now attaining the birth-body and Dharma-body's own trace and origin — it does not speak of anything vast or distant. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra does not speak of the śrāvaka as possessing trace and origin at all, but clarifies only the bodhisattva's own abiding within the inconceivable trace and origin — it speaks of the Buddha as possessing a pure land, yet what the Curly-Haired One sees, too, is not anything vast or distant. The Avataṃsaka speaks of Vairocana and Śākyamuni as trace and origin, and the bodhisattva, too, as possessing trace and origin — yet even the śrāvaka has not heard of it and does not understand it — how, then, could he possibly possess his own trace and origin?

今經發聲聞有本，本有因果，示為二乘迹中因果。發佛之迹，王宮生身生，道樹法身生，乃至中間生法二身，悉皆是迹。但取最初先得真應，名之為本。故師弟本因本果，與餘經永異。今經迹中師弟因果，與眾經有同有異，本中師弟因果，眾經所無，正以此之因果為經妙宗也。

This present sūtra discloses the śrāvaka as, too, possessing an origin, this origin itself possessing cause and fruit, shown as the two lesser vehicles' own cause and fruit within the trace. Disclosing the Buddha's own trace: the birth-body born within the royal palace, the Dharma-body born beneath the bodhi tree, and, further, every birth-body and Dharma-body born within the interval between — all of these, without exception, are trace. Only the very first attainment of the true and the response-body is taken and named the origin. Hence the teacher's and disciples' own original cause and original fruit are permanently different from every other sūtra. This present sūtra's own teacher-and-disciple cause and fruit within the trace has both sameness and difference with the various other sūtras; the teacher-and-disciple cause and fruit within the origin is something no other sūtra possesses at all — it is precisely this same cause and fruit that is taken as this sūtra's own wondrous cardinal doctrine.

Reading note

The survey of how other Mahāyāna sūtras locate their own cardinal doctrine — the Perfection of Wisdom teachings in the cause alone, the *Vimalakīrti* in cause and fruit jointly, the *Avataṃsaka* disputed three ways depending on which half of its own title is stressed — sets up the sharpest claim of this whole section: every one of these sūtras' own cause-and-fruit account concerns only the trace, the visible teaching career, however each construes it. What no other sūtra possesses at all is an origin with its own cause and fruit — the vast, distant attainment standing behind the visible trace — and it is precisely this, and not any refinement of the trace-cause-and-fruit debate already rehearsed by every other tradition, that this sūtra alone contributes as its own distinctive cardinal doctrine.
