Dharma › Mahāyāna Scriptures › Fahua Xuanyi
Zhiyi · Tiantai · 妙法蓮華經玄義

The Whole Sūtra, in Five Sections.

The Fahua Xuanyi ("Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sūtra") is Zhiyi's account of the sūtra's own doctrinal architecture — not a line-by-line gloss, as the Fahua Wenju is, but a system built entirely around his own fivefold scheme: Explaining the Name, Clarifying the Essence, Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine, Discussing the Function, and Classifying the Teachings. Together with the Fahua Wenju and the Mohe Zhiguan, it forms the "Tiantai Three Great Works on the Lotus." Ru-Yi's own translation of all ten fascicles is now complete — this page is the entrance for reading it fascicle by fascicle.

How to read this collection

Zhiyi delivered this treatise as oral lectures, recorded and edited by his disciple Guanding into the ten-fascicle text preserved in the Taishō canon. Unlike the Fahua Wenju, it never proceeds sequentially through the sūtra's own chapters at all — it is organized entirely around Zhiyi's fivefold scheme, applied first in brief outline through his own "sevenfold common exposition," then unfolded at enormous length, above all in Explaining the Name through the doctrine of the "ten wondrous aspects" of the trace gate and the origin gate, which alone occupies roughly three-quarters of the entire treatise. Scriptural citations throughout are short, usually a single phrase, functioning as proof-texts embedded within a continuous, tightly argued philosophical exposition — this translation follows the source's own paragraph breaks rather than pairing sentence by sentence, the closer fit for continuous argument rather than verse-by-verse commentary.

This site already carries Ru-Yi's own translation of the Lotus Sūtra itself, and its direct sibling, the Fahua Wenju, Zhiyi's line-by-line commentary on that same sūtra. Terminology here is kept consistent with both, and with this site's other Zhiyi/Tiantai translations — the Mohe Zhiguan, the Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen, and the Xiao Zhiguan Jiangyi — and with the Jiaoguan Gangzong, which cites the Fahua Xuanyi itself repeatedly as an authority.

Read by Fascicle

All 10 fascicles are complete, linking to Ru-Yi's own translation. Use this as the table of contents for the whole treatise.

Fascicle 1 · The treatise opens

Six Similes for One Flower

Guanding's account of how the text was transmitted, Zhiyi's own Preface and Guanding's private preface on the six similes of the lotus flower, the sevenfold common exposition applied in brief to Name, Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, and Teaching, the four siddhāntas, and the opening critique of earlier masters' readings of "wondrous."

Fascicle 2 · "Three thousand in a thought," seeded

The Nine Realms in One

Zhiyi's own positive account of "wondrous" through six senses of cause and fruit, the ten suchnesses across the ten dharma-realms — the seed of "three thousand realms in a single thought" — the threefold division into the dharmas of sentient beings, of the Buddha, and of mind, and the opening of the ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate.

Fascicle 3 · Object and wisdom

How the Six Objects Combine

How the six objects of contemplation open into and combine with one another, closing the wondrous of the object; the wondrous of wisdom in its entirety; and the wondrous of practice's opening enumeration schemes and the distinct teaching's precept holy practice.

Fascicle 4 · The Medicinal Herbs stages

One Rain, Six Stages

Closing the wondrous of practice through the concentration and wisdom holy practices and the perfect teaching's five practices, then opening the wondrous of stage through five of its six "Medicinal Herbs" stages: the human and heavenly vehicle, the two vehicles, the Tripiṭaka bodhisattva, and the shared and distinct teachings.

Fascicle 5 · Stage and the three tracks

The Perfect Teaching's Stage and the Three Tracks

The perfect teaching's own stage, closing the wondrous of stage, followed by the wondrous of the three tracks in its entirety.

Fascicle 6 · The trace gate nears its close

Sympathetic Response, Spiritual Powers, Teaching, and the Retinue

The wondrous of sympathetic response, spiritual powers, teaching the Dharma, and the retinue, closed in full, with the wondrous of merit and benefit begun.

Fascicle 7 · From trace to origin

The Origin Gate and the Lotus Flower

The trace gate's ten wondrous aspects completed; the origin gate's own ten wondrous aspects opened and closed in full; and the lotus-flower exposition that closes the particular name, "wondrous dharma lotus flower," within Explaining the Name.

Fascicle 8 · The real mark

The Real Mark and the Sixteen Gates

Closing Explaining the Name through the word "sūtra" itself, then Clarifying the Essence: the real mark as the sūtra's one essence, distinguishing the false, one dharma under many names, and the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct teachings' gates that enter it.

Fascicle 9 · Coarse and wondrous, judged in full

The Real Mark in Every Sūtra and Practice

The perfect teaching's gate distinguished from the distinct teaching's, coarse and wondrous judged across all sixteen gates, and the real mark established as the essence of every sūtra and every practice.

Fascicle 10 · The treatise concludes

Classifying the Teachings

The treatise's final fascicle: closing Clarifying the Essence and Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine, then establishing the Five Periods and Four Teachings in Classifying the Teachings, completing the entire ten-fascicle treatise.

Translation Notes

Recurring terms, kept consistent as the fascicles grow. See the full translation notes for the complete, fascicle-by-fascicle glossary, and also this site's note on translation method.

名 / 體 / 宗 / 用 / 教 — the five sectionsExplaining the Name, Clarifying the Essence, Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine, Discussing the Function, and Classifying the Teachings — the fivefold scheme that gives this treatise its entire structure. Explaining the Name alone occupies roughly three-quarters of the whole work.
七番共解 — the sevenfold common expositionZhiyi's own method, applied to all five sections at once before each is unfolded separately: marking the section, citing proof-texts, showing its arising in sequence, opening and combining it with other schemes, resolving doubts, the contemplation of mind, and reconciling it with other terms.
十妙 — the ten wondrous aspectsObject, wisdom, practice, stage, the three tracks, sympathetic response, spiritual powers, teaching the Dharma, the retinue, and merit and benefit — applied once to the trace gate (chapters 1–14 of the sūtra) and once to the origin gate (chapters 15–28), the doctrinal heart of Explaining the Name.
迹門 / 本門 — the trace gate and the origin gateThe same division this site's Fahua Wenju translation uses: chapters 1–14 concern the Buddha's trace within this one lifetime; chapters 15–28 concern his origin, awakened since a time immeasurably long past.
十如是 / 十法界 / 一念三千 — the ten suchnesses, the ten dharma-realms, and three thousand realms in a single thoughtFascicle 2's doctrine of the ten suchnesses applied across the ten dharma-realms is the direct seed of "three thousand realms in a single thought," worked out at full length in the Mohe Zhiguan.
四悉檀 — the four siddhāntasThe mundane, the individual, the therapeutic, and the ultimate-meaning siddhānta, following the Fahua Wenju's own phrasing as this treatise's closer sibling.
三藏教 / 通教 / 別教 / 圓教 — the four teachingsThe Tripiṭaka teaching, the shared teaching, the distinct teaching, and the perfect teaching — the classification scheme this treatise's own final fascicle establishes in full, alongside the Five Periods.
實相 — the real markClarifying the Essence's own central term: the single true reality underlying every apparent name, distinguished from what is false and shown to be the essence not merely of this sūtra, but of every sūtra and every practice.
