The Concentration and Wisdom Holy Practices

Fascicle 4 continues the distinct teaching's five practices, opened in Fascicle 3 with the precept holy practice. It works through the remaining two branches of the holy practice — concentration and wisdom — in full.

Status: in progress Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

The Concentration Holy Practice: Mundane Dhyāna 定聖行・世間禪

Chinese Source

定聖行者，略為三：一、世間禪。二、出世禪。三、上上禪。世禪復二：一、根本味禪：隱沒、有垢、無記。二、根本淨禪：不隱沒、無垢、有記。根本者，世、出世法之根本也。《大品》云：「諸佛成道，轉法輪，入涅槃，悉在禪中。」若能深觀根本，出生勝妙上定，故稱根本也。隱沒者，闇證無觀慧也。有垢者，地地生愛味也。無記者，境界不分明也。此有三品：謂禪也、等也、空也。即十二門禪也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the concentration holy practice: this is, in brief, of three kinds — first, mundane dhyāna; second, world-transcending dhyāna; third, the highest dhyāna. Mundane dhyāna is, in turn, of two kinds: first, the root dhyāna of flavor — obscured, tainted, and indeterminate; second, the root pure dhyāna — unobscured, untainted, and determinate. "Root" means the very root of both mundane and world-transcending dharmas. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra says: "Every buddha's attaining the path, turning the wheel of the Dharma, and entering nirvāṇa, all take place within dhyāna." If one is able to contemplate the root deeply, one thereby gives rise to the surpassingly wondrous and superior concentrations — hence, it is called "root." "Obscured" means realizing it in darkness, without the wisdom of contemplation. "Tainted" means giving rise to craving for its flavor at every stage. "Indeterminate" means its objects are not clearly distinguished. This is of three grades — dhyāna, the immeasurable, and emptiness — that is, the twelve dhyānas together.

The Root Dhyāna of Flavor: The Four Dhyānas 根本味禪

Chinese Source

初修方便，當善簡風喘，明識正息，安徐記數，莫令增減。若數微細，善解轉緣，調停得所，當證前方便法。或麁、細住，皆有持身法起，進得欲界定，或未到定。八觸發動，五支成就，是發初禪。

Ru-Yi Translation

In first cultivating the expedient means, one should skillfully distinguish wind and panting from correct breath, clearly recognizing the correct breath itself, and count it calmly and slowly, never letting the count increase or decrease. Once the counting becomes subtle and fine, skillfully understanding how to shift one's focus and adjusting it to what is fitting, one thereby verifies the preceding expedient dharma. Whether one's dwelling is coarse or fine, in either case the dharma of sustaining the body arises, and one advances to attain the concentration of the desire realm, or the concentration on the verge of the first dhyāna. The eight sensations arise and stir, and the five factors are accomplished — this is the arising of the first dhyāna.

《大論》云：「已得離婬火，則獲清涼定。如人大熱悶，入冷池則樂」(云云)。若欲進上離下者，凡夫依六行觀，佛弟子多修八聖種。

The Treatise says: "Having already attained freedom from the fire of lust, one gains the concentration of cool refreshment — like a person greatly oppressed by heat who, entering a cold pool, feels joy," and so on. As for wishing to advance upward, leaving the lower stage behind: the ordinary person relies on the sixfold contemplation of activity, while the Buddha's disciples for the most part cultivate the eight noble lineages.

行者於初禪覺、觀支中，厭離覺、觀，以初禪為苦。麁、障二法動亂定心故苦：從二法生喜、樂，故麁；二法翳上定，故障。二禪異此，名勝、妙、出。總而言之，一、知過不受著，二、訶責，三、析破，得離初禪，是修二禪相。善巧攀厭，則內外皎然，與喜俱發，四支成就。故《論》云：「是故除覺、觀，得入一識處，內心清淨故，定生得喜樂」(云云)。二禪中，既離覺、觀，不得作方便。出定時，修習厭下進上，亦有六行，如棄初禪方法(云云)。爾時，泯然不依內外，與樂俱發，五支成就。故《論》云：「由愛故有苦，失喜則生憂，離苦樂身安，捨念及方便」(云云)。欲厭下進上，亦有六行，如前(云云)。善修故，心豁開明，出入息斷，與捨俱發，空明寂靜，四支成就。若能知樂患，見不動大安。憂喜先已除，苦樂今亦斷(云云)。

Within the first dhyāna's own factors of applied and sustained thought, the practitioner grows weary of and turns away from applied and sustained thought, taking the first dhyāna itself as suffering. The two dharmas of coarseness and obstruction stir and disturb the concentrated mind, and are thus suffering: since joy and bliss arise from these two dharmas, they are coarse; and since these two dharmas obscure the higher concentration, they are an obstruction. The second dhyāna differs from this, and is named superior, wondrous, and transcending. In sum: first, knowing the fault and no longer accepting attachment to it; second, reproaching it; third, analyzing and breaking it apart — through these, one attains freedom from the first dhyāna, and this is the mark of cultivating the second dhyāna. Skillfully clinging to this weariness, one becomes inwardly and outwardly limpid and clear, arising together with joy, and the four factors are accomplished. Hence the Treatise says: "For this reason, once applied and sustained thought are removed, one enters the single sphere of consciousness, and, since the inner mind is pure, concentration arises and joy and bliss are attained," and so on. Within the second dhyāna, having already left applied and sustained thought behind, one can no longer make use of them as an expedient means. On emerging from concentration, cultivating weariness of the lower and advancing toward the higher likewise has its own sixfold contemplation, just as in the method for abandoning the first dhyāna, and so on. At that time, dissolving away all dependence on the inner and outer alike, arising together with bliss, the five factors are accomplished. Hence the Treatise says: "Because of craving, there is suffering; once joy is lost, sorrow arises; being free of suffering, bliss, and the body's ease, one relinquishes mindfulness itself along with any further expedient means," and so on. Wishing to grow weary of the lower and advance toward the higher likewise has its own sixfold contemplation, as before, and so on. Through skillful cultivation, the mind opens wide and becomes bright and clear, the in-and-out breath is cut off, arising together with equanimity, empty, bright, and utterly still, and the four factors are accomplished. If one is able to know the affliction even within bliss, one sees the great ease of the unmoving. Sorrow and joy, already removed before, and suffering and bliss, now likewise cut off, and so on.

行人既內證四禪，欲外修福德，應學四等。此有通修、別修。通修者，《大論》云：「是慈在色界四禪中間得修。」此語則通。別修者，初禪有覺觀分別，修悲則易；喜支修喜易；樂支修慈易；一心支修捨易。復次，初禪修悲易：二禪修喜易：三禪修慈易：四禪修捨易。此則修四無量定之處所也。復次，修時緣前人離苦得樂，歡喜平等之相而入定。發時，內得喜樂平等之法，外見前人離苦得樂；或內得外不見、或外見內不得，分別邪正(云云)。

Once the practitioner has inwardly realized the four dhyānas, wishing to outwardly cultivate merit and virtue, he should study the four immeasurable minds. These may be cultivated either generally or individually. As for the general cultivation: the Treatise says: "This loving-kindness can be cultivated in the interval among the four dhyānas of the form realm" — this statement refers to the general approach. As for individual cultivation: the first dhyāna, having applied and sustained thought as its own discriminating factors, makes compassion easy to cultivate there; the joy-factor makes sympathetic joy easy to cultivate; the bliss-factor makes loving-kindness easy to cultivate; the one-pointedness-factor makes equanimity easy to cultivate. Again: the first dhyāna makes compassion easy to cultivate; the second dhyāna makes sympathetic joy easy to cultivate; the third dhyāna makes loving-kindness easy to cultivate; the fourth dhyāna makes equanimity easy to cultivate — these, then, are the respective places for cultivating the four immeasurable concentrations. Again, at the time of cultivation, one takes as one's object another person's leaving suffering and gaining happiness, and enters concentration upon the mark of joy and equality; and at the moment it is brought forth, one inwardly attains the dharma of joy, bliss, and equality, while outwardly seeing that other person leave suffering and gain happiness — or one may inwardly attain it without outwardly seeing it, or outwardly see it without inwardly attaining it; the correct and the deviant should be distinguished accordingly, and so on.

行人欲出色籠，修四空定。滅色存心，心心相依，故名四空。方便者，須訶色是苦本，饑渴寒熱，色為苦聚；讚空為淨妙，離諸逼迫，過一切色，與空定相應，不苦不樂，倍更增長。於深定中，唯見虛空，無諸色相，心無分散。復次，得空定故，出過色界，故名過一切色相；空法持心，種種諸色不得起，故名滅有對相；已得空定，決定能捨色法，不憶戀故，名不念種種色相(云云)。訶下攀上，皆有方便，委在《禪門》(云云)。根本味禪竟。

The practitioner, wishing to leave the cage of form behind, cultivates the four formless attainments. Extinguishing form while preserving mind, mind depending on mind in succession — hence, they are named the "four formless" attainments. As for the expedient means: one must reproach form as the very root of suffering — hunger, thirst, cold, and heat are all a mass of suffering built from form — and praise emptiness as pure and wondrous, free of every oppression. Passing beyond all form, according with the formless concentration, neither suffering nor pleasant, one's cultivation redoubles and increases all the more. Within this deep concentration, one sees only empty space, with no mark of form remaining at all, and the mind is without any scattering. Again, since the concentration of emptiness is attained, one passes beyond the form realm — hence, it is named "passing beyond every mark of form"; the dharma of emptiness sustains the mind, so that every various mark of form is unable to arise — hence, it is named "extinguishing the mark of resistance"; having already attained the concentration of emptiness, one is decisively able to relinquish the dharma of form, no longer recollecting or longing for it — hence, it is named "no longer thinking of the various marks of form," and so on. Reproaching the lower and clinging toward the higher each has its own expedient means, treated fully in the Dhyāna Gate, and so on. This closes the root dhyāna of flavor.

The Root Pure Dhyāna: The Six Wondrous Gates 根本淨禪・六妙門

Chinese Source

根本淨禪，不隱沒、無垢、有記，與上相違。此又三品，謂六妙門、十六特勝、通明等也。涅槃是妙，此六能通，故言六妙門。此三法為三根性，慧性多，為說六妙門，此一一門，於欲界中即能發無漏。若定性多，為說十六特勝。故下地不發無漏，上地禪滿乃能得悟。定慧性等，為說通明。通明觀慧深細，從下至上皆能發無漏。此是隨機之說，若作對治則復別途(云云)。若廣明修習，則攝一切諸禪。今但次第相生，一轍豎意。修此六門，修、證合論，則有十二法。佛言：「遊止三四，出生十二。」即此修數、證數，乃至修淨、證淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the root pure dhyāna: unobscured, untainted, and determinate, the very opposite of what came before. This, too, is of three grades: the six wondrous gates, the sixteen special excellences, and the penetrating-and-clear dhyāna, and the rest. Nirvāṇa is itself wondrous, and these six are able to lead through to it — hence, they are named the six wondrous gates. These three dharmas are taught according to three kinds of capacity: for one whose capacity for wisdom predominates, the six wondrous gates are taught, since each and every one of these gates is able to bring forth the uncontaminated already within the desire realm itself. For one whose capacity for concentration predominates, the sixteen special excellences are taught, since the lower stages do not bring forth the uncontaminated there, and only once dhyāna is fulfilled in the higher stage can awakening be attained. For one whose capacities of concentration and wisdom are equal, the penetrating-and-clear dhyāna is taught, since its contemplative wisdom, deep and fine, is able to bring forth the uncontaminated from the lowest stage all the way up through the highest. This teaching accords with capacity; if treated instead as an antidote to specific afflictions, a different approach again applies, and so on. If one clarifies its cultivation in full, it gathers in every dhyāna without exception; here, however, only the sequential arising of one gate from another, following a single track, is intended. In cultivating these six gates, when cultivation and verification are discussed together, there are twelve dharmas in all. The Buddha said: "Roaming and resting, three and four, give rise to twelve" — this refers precisely to cultivating numbering and verifying numbering, on through cultivating purifying and verifying purifying.

修數者，行人初調和氣息，不澁、不滑，安詳徐數，從一至十，攝心在數，不令馳散，是名修數。與數相應者，覺心任運，從一至十，不加功力，心自住數，息微心細，是名證數。若患數麁，當放數修隨，乃至淨亦各如是。然觀有三義：一、慧觀觀真。二、得解觀即假想觀。三、實觀。此中初用實觀，後用慧觀。

As for cultivating numbering: the practitioner, first regulating the breath so that it is neither rough nor slippery, counts it calmly and slowly, from one up to ten, gathering the mind upon the count and not letting it scatter and run off — this is called cultivating numbering. As for according with the numbering itself: the aware mind moves of its own accord, from one up to ten, with no further exertion of effort needed, the mind settling naturally upon the count, the breath growing subtle and the mind growing fine — this is called verifying numbering. If one finds the numbering itself becomes a coarse burden, one should set numbering aside and cultivate following instead, and so on through purifying, each following this same pattern. Now, contemplation carries three senses: first, the contemplation of wisdom, contemplating the real; second, the contemplation of attained understanding, that is, the contemplation of imagined likeness; third, the real contemplation. Here, the real contemplation is used first, and the contemplation of wisdom afterward.

修實觀者，於定心中，以心眼諦觀此身，細微入出息相，如空中風；皮、肉、筋、骨三十六物，如芭蕉不實，內外不淨，甚可厭惡。復觀定中喜、樂等受，悉有破壞之相，是苦非樂。又觀定中，心識無常，剎那不住，無可著處。復觀定中善、惡等法，悉屬因緣，皆無自性。如是觀時，能破四倒；不得人我，定何所依？是名修觀。如是修時，覺息出入，遍諸毛孔。心眼開明，徹見身內三十六物，及諸蟲戶，內外不淨；眾苦逼迫；剎那變易；一切諸法，悉見無自性。心生悲喜，無所依倚。得四念處，破四顛倒。是名與觀相應，不能具記(云云)。佛坐樹下，內思安般，一數二隨等，正是此禪。十六特勝者，釋名(云云)。此從因緣得名。

As for cultivating the real contemplation: within the concentrated mind, one carefully contemplates this very body with the mind's own eye, observing the fine and subtle mark of the in-and-out breath, like wind moving through empty space; and the thirty-six substances — skin, flesh, sinew, and bone — like a banana tree, without any solid core, impure within and without, altogether repugnant. One further contemplates that the feelings of joy and bliss and the rest, present within concentration, all carry the mark of breaking apart and being destroyed — that they are suffering, not pleasure. One further contemplates that, within concentration, mind and consciousness are impermanent, never abiding even for a single instant, with no place at all to fasten upon. One further contemplates that the wholesome and unwholesome dharmas present within concentration all belong to causes and conditions, each and every one without any nature of its own. Contemplating in this way, one is able to break through the four inversions; since no person or self is ever found, upon what, then, could concentration itself depend? This is called cultivating contemplation. Cultivating in this way, one becomes aware of the breath entering and leaving, pervading every pore of the body. The mind's own eye opens wide and becomes bright, seeing through directly to the thirty-six substances within the body, and to every worm-hole within it, impure within and without; oppressed on every side by manifold sufferings; changing in an instant; and every dharma whatsoever is seen to be entirely without any nature of its own. Sorrow and joy arise in the mind, with nothing left to depend or lean upon. One attains the four applications of mindfulness, breaking through the four inversions. This is called according with contemplation, and it cannot be recorded here in full. The Buddha, seated beneath the tree, inwardly reflecting on mindfulness of the breath — first numbering, then following, and the rest — was practicing exactly this dhyāna. As for the sixteen special excellences: their name is explained, and so on. This name is derived from its own causes and conditions.

The Sixteen Special Excellences 十六特勝

Chinese Source

修相者，知息入知息出者，此代數息，調息綿細，一心隨息，入時知從鼻至臍，出時知從臍至鼻，隨照不亂。知風、喘、氣為麁；知息為細。入麁即調令細，如守門人，知入知出，惡遮好進。澁滑、輕重、冷煖、久近、難易皆知。知息為命所依，一息不還即便命盡。覺息與命危脆無常，不生愛慢。知息非我，即不生見。若知息長短，對欲界定；知息遍身，對未到地。除諸身行，對初禪覺觀支；受喜對喜支；受樂對樂支；受諸心行對一心支。心作喜，即喜俱禪；心作攝，即二禪一心支。心作解脫，即三禪樂。觀無常，即四禪不動。觀出散，即空處。觀離欲，即識處。觀滅，即對無所有處。觀棄捨，對非想非非想處。觀棄捨時，即便獲得三乘涅槃。若橫論觀慧，即對四念處(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for cultivating its marks: "knowing the in-breath, knowing the out-breath" takes the place of numbering the breath — regulating the breath so that it becomes continuous and fine, following the breath single-mindedly, knowing, on the in-breath, that it travels from the nose down to the navel, and, on the out-breath, that it travels from the navel up to the nose, following and illuminating it without any confusion. Knowing wind, panting, and ordinary breath to be coarse, and knowing the true breath to be fine — where the in-breath is coarse, one regulates it to make it fine, like a gatekeeper who knows what enters and what leaves, barring what is harmful and admitting what is good. Roughness and smoothness, heaviness and lightness, cold and warmth, duration and brevity, difficulty and ease — all of these are known. Knowing that the breath is what life itself depends upon, and that, once a single breath fails to return, life itself is ended; being aware that breath and life alike are precarious, fragile, and impermanent, one no longer gives rise to craving or conceit toward them; and knowing that the breath is not the self, one no longer gives rise to any view of self. Knowing the breath's length and shortness corresponds to the concentration of the desire realm; knowing the breath pervading the whole body corresponds to the concentration on the verge of the first dhyāna; removing every bodily formation corresponds to the first dhyāna's own factors of applied and sustained thought; experiencing joy corresponds to the joy-factor; experiencing bliss corresponds to the bliss-factor; experiencing every mental formation corresponds to the one-pointedness-factor. The mind's producing joy is precisely the dhyāna accompanied by joy; the mind's producing restraint is precisely the second dhyāna's own one-pointedness-factor. The mind's producing liberation is precisely the third dhyāna's own bliss. Contemplating impermanence is precisely the fourth dhyāna's own unmoving state. Contemplating the emerging and the dissolving corresponds to the sphere of infinite space. Contemplating the freedom from desire corresponds to the sphere of infinite consciousness. Contemplating cessation corresponds to the sphere of nothing whatsoever. Contemplating relinquishment corresponds to the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception. At the very moment of contemplating relinquishment, one thereupon attains the nirvāṇa of the three vehicles. If discussed broadly in terms of contemplative wisdom, this corresponds to the four applications of mindfulness, and so on.

The Penetrating-and-Clear Dhyāna 通明禪

Chinese Source

通明禪者，行者觀息、色、心三事無分別。諦觀出入息，入無積聚，出無分散；來無所經，去無履涉，如空中風，性無所有。息本依身，身本不有。先世妄想，招今四大，圍於虛空，假名為身：頭等六分，三十六物、四微一一非身。觀身由心，心由緣起，生滅迅速，不見住處相貌。但有名字，名字亦空。如是觀息、色、心，不得三性別異。既不得三事，即不得一切法，此是修相。證者，內證真諦，空如觀解。次第通達此身色息分明，亦知世間天文地理與身相應，能具三界禪定，能知非想有細煩惱，破惑發真得三乘涅槃。悉在《禪門》。世間禪竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the penetrating-and-clear dhyāna: the practitioner contemplates the three matters of breath, form, and mind as without any distinction between them. Carefully contemplating the in-and-out breath: on entering, it accumulates nowhere; on leaving, it disperses nowhere; coming, it passes through no place; going, it treads upon no path — like wind moving through empty space, its nature is that it possesses nothing at all. The breath fundamentally depends upon the body, yet the body itself is fundamentally without real being: deluded thoughts from former lives summon forth the present four great elements, enclosing empty space, and are only provisionally named "body" — the head and the other five parts, the thirty-six substances, the four subtle elements, each and every one is not, in itself, the body. Contemplating that the body arises from mind, and that mind itself arises from conditions, arising and ceasing in swift succession, with no abiding place or mark ever to be found — it has only a name, and even this name is itself empty. Contemplating breath, form, and mind in this way, one finds no distinct nature separating the three from one another. Since none of the three matters can be found at all, no dharma whatsoever can be found either — this is cultivating its mark. As for verification: one inwardly verifies the real truth, understanding it through the contemplation of emptiness and suchness. Proceeding in sequence, one penetrates through to a clear understanding of this body, form, and breath, and also comes to know that the heavenly patterns and earthly configurations of the world correspond to the body itself; one becomes able to possess the dhyāna concentrations of all three realms in full, and to know that even the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception still harbors subtle affliction, breaking through delusion, bringing forth the real, and attaining the nirvāṇa of the three vehicles. All of this is treated fully in the Dhyāna Gate. This closes mundane dhyāna.

Reading note

Fascicle 4 opens directly with the concentration holy practice (定聖行), the second of the three branches of the holy practice (戒/定/慧) begun in Fascicle 3 with the precepts. This entire section on mundane dhyāna reproduces, in compressed form, the classic Tiantai dhyāna taxonomy worked out at much greater length in the *Mohe Zhiguan*'s own twenty-five expedients material and the *Xiao Zhiguan Jiangyi* — terminology here (the six wondrous gates, the sixteen special excellences, the penetrating-and-clear dhyāna, the four immeasurable minds, the four formless attainments) is kept consistent with those established site renderings. The root dhyāna of flavor works step by step through the four root dhyānas by their classic factor-counts (five factors for the first, four for the second, five for the third, four for the fourth); the root pure dhyāna then re-cultivates the same ground without the taint of craving, sorted by capacity into three approaches — the six wondrous gates for the wisdom-predominant, the sixteen special excellences for the concentration-predominant, and the penetrating-and-clear dhyāna for those with both in balance.

World-Transcending Dhyāna: Observing 定聖行・出世間禪・觀

Chinese Source

二、明出世間禪者，即有四種，謂觀、練、熏、修。觀者，謂九想、八背捨、八勝處、十一切處，通稱觀禪。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying world-transcending dhyāna: this is, in turn, of four kinds — observing, training, permeating, and cultivating. "Observing" refers to the nine contemplations, the eight liberations, the eight bases of mastery, and the ten totalities, all together called the dhyāna of observation.

行人為破婬火，必須增想純熟。隨所觀時，與定相應。想定持心，心無分散，能除世間貪愛，破六種欲：有人著赤、白、黃、黑等色、或著相貌端嚴、或著威儀姿態、或著語言嬌媚、或著細滑肌體、或著可意之人。此六欲淵，沈沒行者。

In order to break through the fire of lust, the practitioner must intensify his contemplation until it becomes thoroughly matured. Whenever he engages in this contemplation, it accords with concentration; the contemplation and the concentration together sustain the mind, so that the mind no longer scatters, and one is thereby able to remove worldly craving and attachment, breaking through the six kinds of desire. There are those who are attached to colors such as red, white, yellow, or black; or attached to a fine and dignified appearance; or attached to graceful bearing and manner; or attached to alluring and flattering speech; or attached to smooth and delicate flesh; or attached to a person simply because they are pleasing to the mind. These six abysses of desire drown the practitioner who falls into them.

能修九想，除此六賊：死想破威儀、言語兩欲。脹想、壞想、噉想，破形貌欲。血塗想、青瘀想、膿爛想，破色欲。骨想、燒想，破細滑欲。九想通除所著人欲。又，噉想、散想，除著意人。此九既除於欲，亦薄嗔、癡，九十八使山動。雖是不淨初門，能成大事。如海中屍，依之得度(云云)。

Being able to cultivate the nine contemplations removes these six thieves: the contemplation of death breaks through the two desires of bearing and speech. The contemplations of swelling, of rupture, and of being devoured break through the desire for physical form. The contemplations of being smeared with blood, of discoloration, and of putrefaction break through the desire for color. The contemplations of bones and of burning break through the desire for smooth and delicate flesh. The nine contemplations together remove desire for any person one might become attached to. Again, the contemplations of being devoured and of scattering apart remove attachment to a person merely because they are pleasing to the mind. Once these nine have removed desire, they likewise thin out hatred and delusion, and the ninety-eight proclivities themselves are shaken like a mountain. Though this is only the first gate of the contemplation of impurity, it is able to accomplish a great task — like a corpse floating in the sea, which one may nonetheless rely upon to cross to the other shore, and so on.

八背捨名(云云)。背淨潔五欲，捨離著心，故名背捨。修者，行人持戒清淨，發大誓願，欲成大事，端身正心，諦觀足大指，想如大豆，黑脹[爾*皮]起。此想成時，更進如狸豆大；更如一指大；更如鷄卵大。次二指，三、四、五指。次觀趺底，踵、踝、[跳-兆+專]、膝、髀、臗，悉見膖脹。次觀右脚，亦如是。復當想大小便道、腰、脊、腹、背、胸、脇，悉見腫脹。又觀右胛、臂、肘、腕、掌、五指，又頭、頷等。從足至頭，從頭至足，循身觀察，唯見腫脹，心生厭惡。復當觀壞、膿爛。大小便道，蟲膿流出，臭劇死狗。己身既爾，觀所愛人，亦復如是。內破見我，外破貪愛，久住觀察，除世貪愛。

The name of the eight liberations is explained, and so on. Turning one's back on the pure and clean five desires, relinquishing and abandoning the attached mind — hence, they are named the liberations. As for their cultivation: the practitioner, upholding the precepts purely and raising up the great vow, wishing to accomplish the great task, holds body and mind upright and correct, and carefully contemplates the big toe, imagining it swollen black like a large bean, blistered and distended. Once this image is accomplished, one advances further, imagining it the size of a wildcat-bean, then the size of a single finger, then the size of a hen's egg. Next the second toe, then the third, fourth, and fifth. Next one contemplates the sole of the foot, the heel, the ankle, the calf, the knee, the thigh, and the hip, all seen as swollen and distended. Next one contemplates the right leg in exactly the same way. One should further imagine the passages of excretion, the waist, the spine, the belly, the back, the chest, and the flanks, all seen as swollen and distended. One further contemplates the right shoulder, the arm, the elbow, the wrist, the palm, and the five fingers, and then the head, the jaw, and the rest. Proceeding from the feet up to the head, and from the head back down to the feet, surveying the body in sequence, one sees nothing but swelling and distension, and the mind grows repelled and disgusted by it. One should further contemplate its rupture and putrefaction: from the passages of excretion, worms and pus flow out, more foul than a dead dog. Having contemplated one's own body in this way, one contemplates the person one loves in exactly the same way. Inwardly, this breaks through the view of self; outwardly, it breaks through craving and attachment. Dwelling long in this contemplation, one removes worldly craving and attachment.

次除卻皮肉，諦觀白骨。見骨色相異，謂青、黃、白、鴿，如是骨相亦復無我。得此觀時，名欲界定。次觀骨青時，見此大地東西南北悉皆青相；黃、白、鴿色，亦復如是。此是未到之相。又觀骨人眉間出光，光中見佛，是初背捨成相。如是次第，乃至八背捨發相，具如《禪門》(云云)。

Next, stripping away the skin and flesh, one carefully contemplates the white bones themselves. Seeing the bones' color and mark differ — blue-black, yellow, white, or dove-gray — one sees that this mark of bone, too, is likewise without any self. When this contemplation is attained, it is named the concentration of the desire realm. Next, when one contemplates the bones as blue-black, one sees this entire great earth, east, west, south, and north, all alike bearing this blue-black mark; and the yellow, white, and dove-gray colors are exactly the same. This is the mark of the stage on the verge of the first dhyāna. One further contemplates light streaming from between the brows of this skeletal figure, and, within the light, sees a buddha — this is the mark of the first liberation accomplished. Proceeding in this same sequence, on through the marks of all eight liberations arising in turn, is treated fully in the Dhyāna Gate, and so on.

八勝處者，初兩勝處位在初禪；三、四兩勝處位在二禪；後四勝處位在四禪。三禪樂多心鈍，故不立也。前背捨，緣中多少不得自在，是故勝處更深細觀察少多好醜，悉使勝知勝見，如快馬能破陣，亦能自制其馬(云云)。十一切處者，以八色兩心，更相淡入，廣普遍滿，轉變無礙，具如《禪門》(云云)。

As for the eight bases of mastery: the first two bases of mastery are situated within the first dhyāna; the third and fourth bases of mastery are situated within the second dhyāna; the remaining four bases of mastery are situated within the fourth dhyāna. The third dhyāna, with its abundance of bliss, dulls the mind, and so no basis of mastery is established there. In the liberations discussed before, one was not yet free with respect to the object's quantity, whether little or much; hence, the bases of mastery contemplate more deeply and finely still, discerning little and much, fine and coarse, so that one masters both the knowing and the seeing of them completely — like a swift horse able to break through the enemy's ranks, and also able to master and control itself. As for the ten totalities: taking the eight physical totality-bases together with the two mental ones of emptiness and consciousness, blending them further into one another, extending them broadly and pervading everywhere without limit, transforming freely and without obstruction — this is treated fully in the Dhyāna Gate, and so on.

Training, Permeating, and Cultivating Dhyāna: The Ground of Forbearance 練・熏・修・堪忍地

Chinese Source

練禪者，即九次第定也。上來雖得八禪，入則有間。今欲純熟，令從初淺極至後深，次第而入，中間無有垢滓間穢，令不次第者次第，故名次第。亦是無漏練於有漏，除諸間穬，故名練禪。亦是均調諸禪，令定、慧齊平無間也。《阿毘曇》明熏練，但言以無漏熏四禪。今以無漏通練八地，即是次第入無間三昧也。熏禪者，即師子奮迅三昧也。前是次第無間入，今亦是次第無間入，亦能次第無間出，除麁間及法愛味塵；猶如師子，能卻能進，奮諸塵土。行者入出此法，能遍熏諸禪悉令通利、轉變自在，如熏皮熟隨意作物。修禪者，超越三昧也。近遠超入，近遠超出，近遠超住，是禪功德最深，故名頂禪。於諸法門，自在出入(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for training dhyāna: this is precisely the nine sequential concentrations. Although the eight dhyānas discussed above have already been attained, there remain gaps between entering each one. Here, wishing to make them thoroughly mature, one causes them to be entered in sequence, from the shallowest at the outset all the way to the deepest at the end, with no impure residue or interval left between them, so that what was not sequential becomes sequential — hence, this is named "sequential." It is also the uncontaminated training the contaminated, removing every interval and coarse residue — hence, this is named training dhyāna. It is also the even harmonizing of every dhyāna, so that concentration and wisdom are made level and continuous without any gap. The Abhidharma clarifies permeating and training only by saying that the uncontaminated permeates the four dhyānas; here, however, the uncontaminated trains all eight stages throughout, which is precisely entering the uninterrupted samādhi in sequence. As for permeating dhyāna: this is precisely the lion's swift-leaping concentration. What came before was entering in sequence without interruption; this, too, is entering in sequence without interruption, but is, in addition, also able to exit in sequence without interruption, removing every coarse interval and the dust of craving for the dharma's own flavor — just as a lion is able to retreat and to advance, shaking off every speck of dust. The practitioner who enters and exits by this method is able to permeate every dhyāna throughout, making each one thoroughly fluent and freely transformable, like leather that has been thoroughly tanned and can then be fashioned into whatever shape one wishes. As for cultivating dhyāna: this is precisely the transcendent concentration. Entering by leaping near or far, exiting by leaping near or far, abiding by leaping near or far — the merit and virtue of this dhyāna is the deepest of all, hence it is named the summit dhyāna, moving freely in and out of every dharma-gate, and so on.

又，九次第定善入八背捨；奮迅善出八背捨；超越善住八背捨。善入、出、住百千三昧，即此意也。譬如畫師，五彩相淡，出無量色；如世間果，但以四大，出一切五陰。定法亦爾，但以觀、練、熏、修，出生一切神通變化，無種不備。《大經》云：「菩薩住禪得堪忍地。」地能持、能生。

Again, the nine sequential concentrations are skilled at entering the eight liberations; the swift-leaping is skilled at exiting the eight liberations; and the transcendent is skilled at abiding within the eight liberations. Being skilled at entering, exiting, and abiding within a hundred thousand samādhis is precisely this same sense. It is like a painter who, blending the five colors into one another, produces countless hues; or like the fruits of the world, which, from the four great elements alone, produce every one of the five aggregates. The dharma of concentration is exactly the same: from observing, training, permeating, and cultivating alone, it gives rise to every spiritual power and transformation, lacking not a single kind. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The bodhisattva, abiding in dhyāna, attains the ground of forbearance." A ground is able both to sustain and to give rise to things.

一一禪中，皆有慈悲、誓願、道品、六度諸行，無不具足。何者？若於戒、定中明觀慧，即共念處；單論觀，是性念處；通取戒、定等境、智、文字等，是緣念處。又不淨觀破淨顛倒，是身念處；觀諸禪中，心受苦樂，三世內外，受不可得，破樂顛倒，是受念處；觀諸禪心，以有心故，造作善惡，無心則無作者，破我顛倒，是法念處；觀心生滅，前後際斷，破常顛倒，是心念處。

Within each and every dhyāna, compassion, the vow, the factors of the path, and the practices of the six pāramitās are all present, lacking nothing at all. Why is this? If contemplative wisdom is clarified within the precepts and concentration themselves, this is the shared application of mindfulness; if contemplation alone is discussed, this is the inherent application of mindfulness; and if the objects, wisdom, scriptural texts, and the rest of the precepts and concentration are all taken together in general, this is the conditioned application of mindfulness. Again, the contemplation of impurity breaking through the inversion of purity is the application of mindfulness to the body; contemplating that, within every dhyāna, the mind's experience of suffering and pleasure, whether across the three times or within and without, can nowhere be found, breaking through the inversion of pleasure, is the application of mindfulness to feeling; contemplating that, within every dhyāna's mind, it is only because mind exists that good and evil are ever brought about, and that, were there no mind, there would be no agent to act at all, breaking through the inversion of self, is the application of mindfulness to dharmas; contemplating the mind's own arising and ceasing, its before and after both cut off, breaking through the inversion of permanence, is the application of mindfulness to mind.

復次，八背捨觀四念處、九次第定練四念處、奮迅熏四念處、超越修四念處。二乘為自滅度，修此五禪，成四枯念處，不名堪忍地。菩薩為化眾生，深觀念處，慈悲誓願，荷負眾生，成四榮念處，是摩訶衍，名堪忍地也。

Again, the eight liberations contemplate the four applications of mindfulness; the nine sequential concentrations train the four applications of mindfulness; the swift-leaping permeates the four applications of mindfulness; the transcendent cultivates the four applications of mindfulness. When the two vehicles cultivate these five dhyānas for the sake of their own extinction alone, they accomplish only the four withered applications of mindfulness, and this is not named the ground of forbearance. When the bodhisattva, for the sake of transforming sentient beings, contemplates the applications of mindfulness deeply, moved by compassion and the vow, bearing the burden of sentient beings upon himself, he accomplishes the four flourishing applications of mindfulness — this is the Mahāyāna itself, and is named the ground of forbearance.

問：無色無身，云何具四念處？答：《毘曇》云：「無色有道共戒。戒是無作色，以無漏緣通故，此戒色隨無漏至無色也。」成論人云：「色是無教法，不至無色。」《舍利弗毘曇》云：「無色有色。」當知小乘明義，即有兩意。《大經》云：「無色界色，非諸聲聞所知。」若爾，四念處通無色，亦復何妨？

Question: Since the formless realm has no body, how can it possess the four applications of mindfulness in full? Answer: The Abhidharma says: "The formless realm possesses the precept accompanying the path. This precept is an unmanifest form, and, since the uncontaminated pervades by way of its object without obstruction, this precept-form follows the uncontaminated all the way into the formless realm." The masters of the Satyasiddhi Śāstra say: "Form is a dharma without any teaching-basis, and does not extend into the formless realm." The Śāriputra-abhidharma says: "The formless realm possesses form." One should therefore know that the Hīnayāna's own clarification of this point already carries two distinct senses. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The form of the formless realm is not something known by any of the śrāvakas." If this is so, then what obstacle could there possibly be to the four applications of mindfulness extending into the formless realm as well?

問：諸禪中，但得明念處，尚無正勤，云何具道品？答：約位為言，念處無後品；修行為義，念處具道品也。《大論》云：「初修善有漏五陰，於有為法中得正憶念，即念處智慧也。四種精進，即是正勤。定心中修，名如意足。五善根生，名為根。根增長，名為力。分別道用，名為七覺。安穩道中行，名八正道。」初善有漏中已能具此，何須見道方有八正？若念處既具三十七品者，煖、頂等例然。觀禪既爾，練、熏、修等亦然。

Question: Within the various dhyānas, if only the application of mindfulness itself can be clarified, without even the right diligences present, how can they possibly possess the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment in full? Answer: Spoken of in terms of rank alone, the application of mindfulness has no later factor following it; but spoken of in terms of actual cultivation and practice, the application of mindfulness already possesses the factors of the path in full. The Treatise says: "In first cultivating the wholesome, contaminated five aggregates, one attains right recollection within conditioned dharmas themselves — this is precisely the wisdom of the application of mindfulness. The four kinds of diligence are precisely the right diligences. Cultivated within the concentrated mind, this is named the bases of supernormal power. The five wholesome roots that arise from this are named the faculties. Once the faculties have grown and increased, they are named the powers. Distinguishing the function of the path is named the seven factors of enlightenment. Practicing safely and securely within the path is named the eightfold correct path." Since the initial wholesome, contaminated stage is already able to possess all of this in full, what further need is there to wait for the path of seeing before the eightfold correct path can exist at all? If the application of mindfulness already possesses the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment in full, then warmth, the summit, and the rest follow this same pattern precisely. Since this holds for the dhyāna of observation, it likewise holds for training, permeating, and cultivating as well.

然菩薩於一一禪中，隨所入法門，慈悲眾生，如父母得食，不忘其子。愍傷痴闇，不從內自求樂，從他外求，耽荒五欲，求苦得怖失憂，諸欲無樂，為此起悲。夫欲患如是，何能去之？得禪定樂，則不為所欺，是故起慈。有四弘誓也。又諸禪中修六度者，眾生縛著世間，生活業務不能暫捨。菩薩棄之，一心入禪，是名檀。若不持戒，禪定不發；又入禪時，雜念不起，任運無惡是尸。拘檢身口，捍勞忍苦，制外塵不著，抑內入不起，是為忍。初中後夜，繫念相續，行住坐臥，心常在定，間念不生是名精進。一心在定，不亂不味，名為定。若一心在定，能知世間生滅法相，深識邪偽，名般若。一切眾行皆於禪中具足，一一禪中，能生諸功德，慈悲荷負。是故得名堪忍之地。

Now, the bodhisattva, within each and every dhyāna, according to whatever dharma-gate he has entered, feels compassion for sentient beings, just as a parent, upon obtaining food, never forgets their own child. Pitying and grieving over their darkened delusion — that they do not seek happiness from within themselves, but seek it instead from without, through others, indulging recklessly in the five desires, seeking pleasure only to meet with dread, and losing it only to meet with sorrow, so that these desires never yield any true happiness — he gives rise to compassion on their account. Since the affliction of desire is such as this, how could it ever be removed? Only by attaining the bliss of dhyāna concentration is one no longer deceived by desire — hence, he gives rise to loving-kindness on their account as well. The four universal vows are present here. Again, as for cultivating the six pāramitās within every dhyāna: sentient beings are bound and attached to the world, unable to set aside their livelihood and daily affairs even for a moment; the bodhisattva abandons all of this, entering dhyāna single-mindedly — this is named giving. Were the precepts not upheld, dhyāna concentration would never be brought forth; and, on entering dhyāna, mixed and scattered thoughts no longer arise, and no evil arises of its own accord — this is moral discipline. Restraining body and speech, enduring hardship and bearing suffering, controlling oneself so that outer objects find no purchase and inner afflictions do not arise — this is patience. Throughout the first, middle, and last watches of the night, holding one's mindfulness in unbroken continuity, whether walking, standing, sitting, or lying down, the mind ever abiding in concentration, with no interrupting thought ever arising — this is named diligence. The mind single-pointedly abiding in concentration, neither scattered nor intoxicated by its flavor, is named concentration itself. If, with the mind single-pointedly abiding in concentration, one is able to know the marks of the world's own arising-and-ceasing dharmas, deeply discerning what is deviant and false, this is named prajñā. Every practice without exception is thus made complete within dhyāna itself; within each and every dhyāna, every merit and virtue is brought forth, compassion and loving-kindness bearing the burden of sentient beings upon oneself — hence, this is what is named the ground of forbearance.

Reading note

World-transcending dhyāna's fourfold scheme (觀/練/熏/修 — observing, training, permeating, and cultivating) works through the nine contemplations, eight liberations, eight bases of mastery, and ten totalities (all gathered under "observing"), then the nine sequential concentrations (training), the lion's swift-leaping concentration (permeating), and the transcendent concentration (cultivating) — terminology matched here to the *Mohe Zhiguan*'s own fascicle 9 renderings, its closest parallel treatment of the same fourfold scheme. The section's real payoff is the distinction between the "four withered" and "four flourishing" applications of mindfulness: the two vehicles, cultivating this same fivefold dhyāna sequence for their own liberation alone, reach only the withered version, while the bodhisattva, cultivating it for the sake of others, reaches the ground of forbearance itself — the same compassion-driven upgrade already seen in Fascicle 3's precept holy practice, now applied to concentration.

World-Transcending Superior Dhyāna: Opening the Coarse to Reveal the Wondrous 出世間上上禪・開麁顯妙

Third, world-transcending superior dhyāna: this is precisely the nine kinds of great dhyāna, as explained in the Bodhisattva-bhūmi, and is not treated here in full. As for the dhyāna of inherent nature: this is precisely contemplating the mind's own real nature, and is named the superior concentration. Among all dharmas whatsoever, is there truly any that does not proceed from mind? Mind gathers in everything, like a wish-fulfilling jewel. These nine great dhyānas are, every one, the dharma-realm itself. Every destination reached through dhyāna, every object it constructs, is itself the real — a single color, a single fragrance, none of it is anything other than the middle way. The two vehicles do not even know its name, let alone realize its concentration!

前根本舊禪如乳，練禪如酪，熏禪如生蘇，修禪如熟蘇，九大禪如醍醐，醍醐為妙也。復次，根本禪，愛味心中修，即成乳；自度心中修，即成酪；慈悲心中修，即成生蘇；慈悲次第心中修，即成熟蘇；實相心中修，即成醍醐；餘四味亦如是，若不以實相心修，皆名為麁。

The root dhyāna discussed before is like milk; training dhyāna is like cream; permeating dhyāna is like curdled butter; cultivating dhyāna is like clarified butter; the nine great dhyānas are like ghee — ghee alone is wondrous. Again: the root dhyāna, cultivated with a mind of craving for its flavor, becomes milk; cultivated with a mind bent only on saving oneself, becomes cream; cultivated with a mind of compassion, becomes curdled butter; cultivated with a mind of compassion proceeding in sequence, becomes clarified butter; cultivated with a mind resting in the real mark, becomes ghee — and the remaining four flavors follow this same pattern: unless cultivated with the mind resting in the real mark, every one of them is named coarse.

若開麁顯妙者，阿那波那即是摩訶衍。法界實相攝持諸法，離此之外更無別妙。故知諸佛成道，轉法輪，入涅槃，皆在四禪。四禪中見實相，名禪波羅蜜，何況餘定耶！此即絕待妙義。定聖行竟。

As for opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous: mindfulness of the breath is itself the Mahāyāna. The real mark of the dharma-realm sustains and holds every dharma within it — beyond this, there is no separate wondrous to be found elsewhere. One should therefore know that every buddha's attaining the path, turning the wheel of the Dharma, and entering nirvāṇa, all take place within the four dhyānas. Seeing the real mark within the four dhyānas is named the pāramitā of dhyāna — how much more so, then, for every other concentration besides! This is precisely the sense of the wondrous read in the absolute sense. This closes the concentration holy practice.

Generation-and-Extinction 生滅四諦慧

Chinese Source

生滅四諦慧者，還觀九想、背捨，依、正兩報，膖脹、爛壞不淨之色，是逼迫相、現相、三苦相，是苦諦慧。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the wisdom of the Four Noble Truths of generation-and-extinction: contemplating once more the nine contemplations and the liberations, the dependent and direct retributions, and the swollen, ruptured, impure forms they reveal — these are the mark of oppression, the manifest mark, and the mark of the three sufferings — this is the wisdom of the truth of suffering.

以不起迷著依、正，作恩愛奴，運動身口，起三品十惡業，感三途等生，生長相、轉相、二十五有相。又知世間因果，不淨過患，深愧厭耻。終不殺他活己，奪彼閏身，耽湎不淨，隱曲求直，離合怨親，間搆榮辱，內諂外佞，引納無度，縱毒傷道，邪僻失真。不為不淨，作十惡業；慚愧羞鄙，行三品十善，感三善道生，亦是生長相、轉相、二十五有相。是名集諦慧。

By failing to refrain from delusion and attachment to the dependent and direct retributions, becoming the slave of affection and craving, setting body and speech into motion, one gives rise to the ten evil acts in their three grades, and thereby draws forth rebirth in the three evil destinies and the rest — the mark of arising and growth, the mark of turning, and the mark of the twenty-five states of existence. Again, knowing the causes and effects of the world, and the faults and calamities of impurity, one feels a deep shame and disgust. In the end, one no longer kills another to sustain oneself, seizing another's very body for one's own gain; no longer indulges immersed in impurity; no longer twists the crooked in search of the straight; no longer separates and reunites out of enmity or affection; no longer schemes between honor and disgrace; no longer flatters within while cheating without; no longer indulges without restraint; no longer unleashes poison to injure the path; no longer strays into deviance and loses the real. Refraining from impurity, one no longer commits the ten evil acts; with shame and a sense of humility, one practices the ten wholesome acts in their three grades, and thereby draws forth rebirth in the three wholesome destinies — this, too, is the mark of arising and growth, the mark of turning, and the mark of the twenty-five states of existence. This is called the wisdom of the truth of the arising of suffering.

觀依、正不淨，破淨顛倒；觀諸受即三苦，破樂顛倒；觀諸行和合，破我顛倒；觀諸心生滅，破常顛倒。別相總相、善巧正勤、如意、根、力、覺、道，向涅槃門。慈悲誓願、六度諸行等，即大乘相。亦是戒、定、慧相，亦是能除相，是名道諦慧。

Contemplating that the dependent and direct retributions are impure breaks through the inversion of purity; contemplating that every feeling is itself one of the three sufferings breaks through the inversion of pleasure; contemplating that every formation is a mere coming-together of conditions breaks through the inversion of self; contemplating the arising and ceasing of every mental state breaks through the inversion of permanence. The particular and the general marks, skillful means, the right diligences, the bases of supernormal power, the faculties, the powers, the factors of enlightenment, and the path — all turn toward the gate of nirvāṇa. Compassion, the vow, and the practices of the six pāramitās and the rest are precisely the mark of the Mahāyāna; they are likewise the mark of the precepts, concentration, and wisdom, and likewise the mark of what is able to remove suffering — this is called the wisdom of the truth of the path.

倒不起則業不起；業不起即因不起；因不起故果不起，是名寂滅相，亦二十五有滅相，亦名除相，是為生滅四諦慧。

Once the inversions no longer arise, karma no longer arises; once karma no longer arises, cause no longer arises; and since cause no longer arises, effect, too, no longer arises — this is named the mark of quiescent extinction, and is likewise the mark of the extinguishing of the twenty-five states of existence, and is likewise named the mark of removal. This is the wisdom of the Four Noble Truths of generation-and-extinction.

No Generation 無生四諦慧

Chinese Source

無生四諦慧者，觀不淨色，色性自空，非色滅空，如鏡中像，無有真實。洞達五受陰空無所有，解苦無苦而有真諦，是苦諦慧。知集由心，心如幻化，所起之集亦如幻化，一切愛見與虛空等，是名集諦慧。道本治集，所治既如幻化，能治亦如幻化，是名道諦慧。法若有生，亦可有滅，法本不生，今則不滅，若有一法過涅槃者，我亦說如幻化，是名滅諦慧。雖知五陰眾生如虛空，而誓度如空之眾生。雖知集無所有，而斷諸妄想，如與空共鬪。雖知道不二相，而勤於空中種樹。雖無眾生得滅度者，而滅度無量眾生。約此即事而真，論道品、六度等(云云)，是名無生四諦慧。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the wisdom of the Four Noble Truths of no generation: contemplating impure form, its nature is empty of itself — it is not that form ceases and then becomes empty — like a reflection in a mirror, possessing no reality at all. Penetrating through to see that the five aggregates of clinging are themselves empty, possessing nothing at all, one understands suffering as no-suffering and yet possessing the real truth — this is the wisdom of the truth of suffering. Knowing that the arising of suffering originates from mind, and that mind itself is like an illusion or a conjuring, the arising it gives rise to is likewise like an illusion or a conjuring, and every craving and view is equal to empty space itself — this is called the wisdom of the truth of the arising of suffering. The path fundamentally treats the arising of suffering; and since what is treated is itself like an illusion or a conjuring, what treats it, too, is likewise like an illusion or a conjuring — this is called the wisdom of the truth of the path. If a dharma truly has arising, it may likewise have cessation; but since a dharma fundamentally never arises, it accordingly never ceases either — and were there even a single dharma that surpassed nirvāṇa itself, I would say that this, too, is like an illusion or a conjuring — this is called the wisdom of the truth of cessation. Though one knows that the five aggregates and sentient beings are like empty space, one nonetheless vows to save sentient beings who are themselves like empty space. Though one knows that the arising of suffering possesses nothing at all, one nonetheless cuts off every false thought, as though wrestling with empty space itself. Though one knows that the path bears no mark of duality, one nonetheless labors to plant trees within empty space itself. Though there is, in reality, no sentient being who attains extinction, one nonetheless brings immeasurable sentient beings to extinction. Discussing the factors of the path, the six pāramitās, and the rest according to this same principle — namely, that the very phenomenon itself is already the real — this is named the wisdom of the Four Noble Truths of no generation.

The Immeasurable 無量四諦慧

Chinese Source

無量四諦慧者，《大經》云：「佛說四諦，若攝法盡，則不應言：所不說者，如十方土。攝法不盡，應有五諦。」佛言：「四諦攝盡，無第五諦。但苦有無量相，集滅道等皆有無量相。我於彼經，竟不說之。」若是空者，空尚無空，云何無量？當知出假分別之慧也。此慧遍知十法界假、實差別，名苦諦慧；遍知五住煩惱不同，名集諦慧；遍解半、滿、正、助等行，名道諦慧；解半滿十六門，諸滅門不同，是滅諦慧。二乘但服四諦藥，治見、思病，自出生死，於分別則閑。菩薩作大醫王，須解診種種脈，識種種病，精種種藥，得種種差。約此起種種慈悲，行種種行，諸度道品，成種種眾生，淨種種佛土，廣說如《止觀》(云云)。是名無量四諦慧。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the wisdom of the Four Noble Truths of the immeasurable: the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "If the Buddha's teaching of the Four Noble Truths gathers in every dharma completely, then one ought not to say that what remains untaught is as vast as the lands of the ten directions; and if it does not gather in every dharma completely, there ought to be a fifth truth." The Buddha said: "The Four Noble Truths gather in every dharma completely; there is no fifth truth. It is only that suffering carries immeasurable marks, and its arising, its cessation, and the path likewise each carry immeasurable marks — and these I have, in that sūtra, in the end simply not taught." If this were merely emptiness, then emptiness itself would not even possess emptiness — how, then, could it be immeasurable? One should therefore know that this is the wisdom of discrimination in emerging into the provisional. This wisdom fully knows the provisional and real distinctions across the ten dharma-realms — this is named the wisdom of the truth of suffering; fully knows the differences among the five categories of affliction — this is named the wisdom of the truth of the arising of suffering; fully understands the partial and the complete, the correct and the auxiliary practices, and the rest — this is named the wisdom of the truth of the path; understands the partial and complete sixteen gates and the differences among the various gates of cessation — this is the wisdom of the truth of cessation. The two vehicles take only the medicine of the Four Noble Truths, treating the sickness of views and thought alone, escaping birth-and-death for themselves, and remain unconcerned with further discrimination beyond this. The bodhisattva, however, acts as the great physician-king: he must understand how to diagnose every manner of pulse, recognize every manner of sickness, refine every manner of medicine, and achieve every manner of cure. According to this, he gives rise to every manner of compassion, practices every manner of practice, the pāramitās and the factors of the path, brings every manner of sentient being to maturity, and purifies every manner of buddha-land — set out at length in the Mohe Zhiguan, and so on. This is named the wisdom of the Four Noble Truths of the immeasurable.

The Unconditioned 無作四諦慧

Chinese Source

無作四諦慧者，解、惑因緣而成四也。《大經》云：「寶珠在體，謂呼失去，憂愁啼哭。但見其體及瘡，不見寶珠及鏡。唯有憂悲，無復歡喜。」此迷道、滅，而起苦、集。若解瘡體即是寶珠，則喜不哭。因滅無明，即得熾然三菩提燈。此解悟因緣，即是道、滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the wisdom of the Four Noble Truths of the unconditioned: understanding and delusion, through causes and conditions, together form these four. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "A jewel is present within one's own body, yet, believing it lost, one weeps and wails in sorrow — seeing only one's own body and its wound, but never the jewel itself, nor the mirror that would reveal it, one has only sorrow and grief left, and joy no longer." This is being deluded about the path and cessation, and thereby giving rise to suffering and its arising. Once one understands that the body's very wound is itself the jewel, one rejoices and weeps no more. Through the cessation of ignorance, one thereupon attains the blazing lamp of the threefold bodhi. This understanding and awakening, through causes and conditions, is precisely the path and cessation themselves.

道、滅即苦、集。苦、集即道、滅。若爾，則四非四。四既非四，無量亦非無量；無量既非無量，則假非假；假非假故，則空非空。何但即空非空，亦即假非假，雙亡正入，即寂照雙流。《大品》云：「一切種智即寂滅相。」種種行類相貌皆知，名一切種智。寂滅相，即是雙遮、雙亡。行類相貌皆知，即是雙流雙照。無心亡照，任運寂知，故名不可思議，即無作四諦慧。

The path and cessation are precisely suffering and its arising; suffering and its arising are precisely the path and cessation. If this is so, then the four are not four at all. Since the four are not four, the immeasurable, too, is not immeasurable; and since the immeasurable is not immeasurable, the provisional, too, is not provisional; and since the provisional is not provisional, emptiness, too, is not empty. How could this be limited to emptiness being not empty alone? It is likewise the provisional being not provisional — both negated together, one enters correctly, and quiescence and illumination flow together as one. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra says: "The wisdom knowing all modes is itself the mark of quiescent extinction." Knowing every manner of category, kind, and mark is named the wisdom knowing all modes. The mark of quiescent extinction is precisely the double negation, both cast aside together; knowing every category, kind, and mark is precisely the double flowing, both illuminating together. With no mind left to cast anything aside or illuminate, quiescence and knowing arise of their own accord — hence, this is named the inconceivable, and is precisely the wisdom of the Four Noble Truths of the unconditioned.

《大經》云：「無苦、無諦，有實。無集、道、滅、無諦，有實。」實即中道、如來、虛空、佛性。如此觀時，無緣慈悲，拔二邊苦，與中道樂。修色非淨、非不淨，即空、即假、即中、非枯、非榮，中間論滅，一切道品無不具足。遍捨十法界依、正，名檀；中道、道共，到尸彼岸，名戒；住寂滅忍，二邊不動名忍；二邊不間，名牢強精進；入王三昧，住首楞嚴，名禪；實相般若名智慧；無謀巧用名方便；八自在我名力；無記化化禪名願；三智一心中得名智。一波羅蜜具十，亦具一切佛法。一行無量行、無量行一行，是如來行，是名無作四諦慧。

The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "There is no suffering, no truth of suffering, yet there is the real; there is no arising, no path, no cessation, no truth of these, yet there is the real." The real is precisely the middle way, the Tathāgata, empty space, and buddha-nature. Contemplating in this way, with the compassion that takes no object, one draws out the suffering of both extremes and bestows the bliss of the middle way. Cultivating form as neither pure nor impure, at once empty, at once provisional, at once the middle, neither withered nor flourishing, discussing cessation in the very midst between them, every factor of the path is thereby made complete without exception. Universally relinquishing the dependent and direct retributions across all ten dharma-realms is named giving; the middle way and the precept accompanying the path, reaching the far shore of śīla, is named the precepts; abiding in the forbearance of quiescent extinction, unmoved by either extreme, is named forbearance; unbroken between the two extremes, is named firm and unyielding diligence; entering the king of samādhis, abiding in the Śūraṅgama, is named meditative concentration; the prajñā of the real mark is named wisdom; skillful function without any calculation is named skillful means; the eight sovereign selves are named power; the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation is named the vow; the three kinds of wisdom attained within a single mind are named knowledge. A single pāramitā is thereby complete with all ten, and likewise complete with every buddha-dharma without exception. A single practice is every immeasurable practice, and every immeasurable practice is itself a single practice — this is the Tathāgata's own practice, and is named the wisdom of the Four Noble Truths of the unconditioned.

修此慧時，即得住於無所畏地，即初歡喜地。離五怖畏，謂不活畏、惡名畏、死畏、惡道畏、大眾威德畏。《大經》云：「不畏貪欲、恚、癡。」此內無三毒，外離八風，則無惡名畏；若言「不畏地獄」等，即無惡道畏；若言「不畏沙門、婆羅門」，即無大眾畏；見中道，則無二死畏；實相智慧常命立，無不活畏。

Cultivating this wisdom, one thereupon comes to abide within the ground of fearlessness, that is, the initial ground of joy. One is freed from the five fears — namely, the fear of not surviving, the fear of ill repute, the fear of death, the fear of the evil destinies, and the fear of the awesome authority of the great assembly. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "I do not fear craving, hatred, or delusion." Since one is inwardly free of the three poisons and outwardly free of the eight winds, one is accordingly free of the fear of ill repute; and where it is said, "I do not fear hell" and the rest, one is accordingly free of the fear of the evil destinies; and where it is said, "I do not fear the ascetic or the brahmin," one is accordingly free of the fear of the great assembly; seeing the middle way, one is accordingly free of the fear of the two deaths; and with the wisdom of the real mark, one's own life ever established, one is accordingly free of the fear of not surviving.

The five fears (五怖畏) have no prior rendering elsewhere on this site — coined here for this project; see the translation notes.

The Ground of Joy: The Twenty-Five Samādhis 歡喜地・二十五三昧

Chinese Source

得入此地，具二十五三昧，破二十五有，顯二十五有我性。我性即實性，實性即佛性。開佛之知見，發真中道，斷無明惑，顯真、應二身，緣感即應百佛世界，現十法界身、入三世佛智地，能自利、利他，真實大慶，名歡喜地也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Attaining entry into this ground, one possesses the twenty-five samādhis, breaking through the twenty-five states of existence and revealing the true nature of self within the twenty-five states of existence. This true nature of self is precisely the real nature, and the real nature is precisely buddha-nature. Opening the buddha's own knowledge and vision, one brings forth the real middle way, severs the affliction of ignorance, and reveals both the real body and the responsive body; according with conditions, one responds throughout a hundred buddha-worlds, manifesting a body suited to each of the ten dharma-realms, and enters the wisdom-ground of the buddhas of the three times, able to benefit both oneself and others — this true and great joy is named the ground of joy.

此地具足四德：破二十五有煩惱名淨；破二十五有業名我；不受二十五有報名樂；無二十五有生死名常。常、樂、我、淨，名為佛性顯，即此意也。

This ground is complete with the four virtues: breaking through the affliction of the twenty-five states of existence is named purity; breaking through the karma of the twenty-five states of existence is named self; no longer receiving the retribution of the twenty-five states of existence is named bliss; being free of the birth-and-death of the twenty-five states of existence is named permanence. Permanence, bliss, self, and purity — this is precisely the sense in which buddha-nature is revealed.

《地持》說離五怖畏者，修無我智，我想不生，云何當有我愛、眾具愛？是離不活畏；不於他人有所求欲，常饒益一切眾生，是離惡名畏；於我見、我想，心不生，是離死畏；此身命終，於未來世必與佛菩薩共會，是離惡道畏；觀於世間無與等者，況復過上，是離大眾畏。《十地經》亦同。《十地論》解云：「是中，第一、依身；第二、依口；第三、第四依身；第五、依意。」活者，依身所用眾具，能資於生，名資生；生為活也。此就因中說果，菩薩無此畏。復次，名字言說皆依口失。護名不為利養，心不悕望他人恭敬，故名無惡名。第五依意可解。三、四依身。愛善道，憎惡道，無愛憎身，故無惡道畏。亦不愛憎身，故無死畏。私謂：不畏貪欲等，無作集壞。不畏惡道，此名無作苦壞。不畏大眾，此是無作道立。無不活、無死畏，此是見性得常，無作滅立。

The Bodhisattva-bhūmi explains freedom from the five fears as follows: cultivating the wisdom of no-self, no notion of self arises — how, then, could there be any craving for self, or craving for the requisites that sustain it? This is freedom from the fear of not surviving. Seeking nothing at all from others, ever benefiting all sentient beings — this is freedom from the fear of ill repute. Neither the view of self nor the notion of self ever arising in the mind — this is freedom from the fear of death. When this body's life comes to its end, one is certain, in a future age, to join together with the buddhas and bodhisattvas — this is freedom from the fear of the evil destinies. Contemplating that nothing in the world is equal to oneself, let alone surpasses one — this is freedom from the fear of the great assembly. The Sūtra of the Ten Grounds agrees with this. The Treatise on the Ten Grounds explains: "Among these, the first depends on the body; the second depends on speech; the third and fourth depend on the body; the fifth depends on the mind." "Surviving" means relying on the requisites the body makes use of, able to sustain life — hence, "sustenance," and sustenance is what "surviving" means. This speaks of the fruit already present within the cause — the bodhisattva has no such fear. Again, name and reputation are both lost by way of speech: guarding one's name, not for the sake of gain, with the mind holding no expectation of others' reverence — hence, this is "freedom from ill repute." The fifth, depending on the mind, may be understood without further comment. The third and fourth depend on the body: loving the good destinies and hating the evil ones — but, having no body left to love or hate, one is accordingly free of the fear of the evil destinies; and, likewise having no body to love or hate, one is accordingly free of the fear of death. In my own view: not fearing craving and the rest is the unconditioned breaking apart of the arising of suffering; not fearing the evil destinies is what is named the unconditioned breaking apart of suffering itself; not fearing the great assembly is the unconditioned establishing of the path; being free of both the fear of not surviving and the fear of death is seeing one's own nature and attaining permanence, the unconditioned establishing of cessation.

復次，破二十五有，有能含果。有破，故集諦壞；果破，故苦諦壞。得二十五三昧者，道諦立。見二十五有我性，我性即佛性，滅諦立。破二十五有，則無煩惱，是淨德；破二十五有，果故無苦，是常德；得二十五三昧是樂；見二十五我性是我，四德宛然矣。

Again, breaking through the twenty-five states of existence: existence itself is able to contain the fruit. Once existence is broken, the truth of the arising of suffering is thereby broken; once the fruit is broken, the truth of suffering itself is thereby broken. Attaining the twenty-five samādhis establishes the truth of the path. Seeing the true nature of self within the twenty-five states of existence — this true nature of self being precisely buddha-nature — establishes the truth of cessation. Breaking through the twenty-five states of existence, one is free of affliction — this is the virtue of purity; breaking through the twenty-five states of existence, one is free of the fruit and thus free of suffering — this is the virtue of permanence; attaining the twenty-five samādhis is bliss; seeing the true nature of self within the twenty-five states is self — the four virtues stand out clearly and distinctly.

今釋二十五三昧名，依四悉檀意：一、隨時趣立。如人多子，各立一名，使兄弟不濫。二十五三昧亦復如是，各舉一名，令世諦不亂，豈可定執也。二、隨其義便，各從所以而立一名也。三、隨事對當，各有主治，從對得名也。四、理實無名，而依理立字。雖有四意，多用對治、約理以立二十五三昧也。通釋二十五，各為四意：一、出諸有過患。二、明本法功德。三、結行成三昧。四、慈悲破有。一一皆爾。

As for now explaining the names of the twenty-five samādhis: this follows the sense of the four siddhāntas. First, names established according to circumstance and inclination — as when a person with many children gives each a separate name, so that the brothers are not confused with one another; the twenty-five samādhis, too, are exactly the same, each given its own name so that the mundane truth is not thrown into confusion — how, then, could one insist rigidly on any one of them alone? Second, names established according to whatever meaning is convenient, each name given for its own particular reason. Third, names established by matching to the specific matter at hand, each samādhi having its own principal object of treatment, the name derived from what it is set against. Fourth, in reality, principle itself has no name at all, and a term is established only by relying on principle. Though these four senses all exist, the twenty-five samādhis are named here mostly by way of the therapeutic and the sense of principle. Explaining all twenty-five in general, each is treated according to four senses: first, setting forth the faults and calamities of that particular state of existence; second, clarifying the merit and virtue of the fundamental dharma that treats it; third, concluding the practice by accomplishing the samādhi; fourth, compassion breaking through that state of existence. Each and every one of the twenty-five follows this same fourfold pattern.

Reading note

The wisdom holy practice culminates, as the precept and concentration holy practices did before it, in a named ground — here the "ground of fearlessness," identified with the first bodhisattva ground, the ground of joy. The twenty-five samādhis (each corresponding to one of the twenty-five states of existence: the six destinies, the four dhyānas, four formless attainments, four immeasurables, and further subdivisions of the form and formless realms) are about to be explained one by one, each following the same fourfold template just laid out — faults, the treating dharma's virtue, the resulting samādhi, and compassion's use of it to help others. The five fears are explained twice in close succession, first in the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s own terms and then again via the *Bodhisattva-bhūmi* and *Daśabhūmika-śāstra*, with Zhiyi's own gloss ("in my own view") mapping each fear's removal onto one of the four kinds of Four Noble Truths just discussed — no-arising, no-suffering, the path established, cessation established.
