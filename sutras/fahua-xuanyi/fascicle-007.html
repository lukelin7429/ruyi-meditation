The Origin Gate and the Lotus Flower

Fascicle 7 completes the wondrous of merit and benefit, closing the trace gate's own ten wondrous aspects (迹門十妙) in full; opens and closes the origin gate's own ten wondrous aspects (本十妙) in their entirety; and closes Explaining the Name (釋名) itself through the extensive lotus-flower illustration that gives this sūtra its title.

Status: Fascicle 7 of 10 complete Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

Near Benefit 近利益

Chinese Source

二、近利益者，起於寂滅道場，始成正覺，即轉法輪，擊於毒鼓、天鼓，利益眾生，齊至法華已前；益亦淺、深，死亦奢、促。何者？教本逗緣，緣略為四，教亦有四，教主亦四，皆稱法王，具王三昧，自破二十五有，七益眾生，例如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, as for near benefit: rising from the very place of quiescent extinction, first accomplishing correct enlightenment, immediately turning the wheel of the Dharma, sounding both the poison drum and the heaven drum, benefiting sentient beings up until, but not yet including, the Lotus Sūtra itself — this benefit, too, carries both shallow and deep degrees, and this death, too, carries both the drawn-out and the swift. Why is this? The teaching is fundamentally suited to the condition it meets, and these conditions are, in broad outline, four; the teachings, too, are four; and the masters of these teachings, too, are four — each alike named the Dharma-king, each complete with the king of samādhis, each breaking through the twenty-five states of existence of himself, benefiting sentient beings through the seven benefits — this follows the same pattern as already explained before.

又大小乘經，明佛入王三昧，放光說法，善惡諸趣果苦得益者，如《阿含》中說，見佛光明，蒙佛手觸，六道苦患，悉得除愈。又《大品》云：放光照地獄眾生，苦惱即除，生齊第六天。苦除是果益，生天是因益。《大品》稱為華葉之益也。又佛放光，幽闇之處，皆大明，各作是念：此中云何忽生眾生？此亦是果益。此因果益，四教主佛，通能此益。

Again, both the Mahāyāna and Hīnayāna sūtras clarify that the Buddha, entering the king of samādhis, emits light and teaches the Dharma, and every good or evil destiny's own fruit of suffering thereby obtains benefit — as the Āgamas say, seeing the Buddha's own light and being touched by the Buddha's own hand, the suffering and affliction of the six destinies are, every one, thereby removed and healed. Again, the Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra says: emitting light, illuminating the sentient beings of hell, their suffering and affliction are removed at once, and they are reborn all the way up to the sixth heaven. Suffering removed is the benefit of the fruit; being reborn in heaven is the benefit of the cause. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra names this the benefit of the flower and leaf. Again, when the Buddha emits light, every dark and hidden place becomes greatly bright, and each being there thinks to itself: how could sentient beings suddenly appear here of themselves? This, too, is the benefit of the fruit. This benefit of cause and fruit is a benefit that the masters of all Four Teachings' own buddhas are alike able to bestow.

別論益者，則是淺深不同，謂聲聞斷正，緣覺侵習，同名中草。菩薩伏惑，兼度眾生。故經云：「求世尊處，我當作佛，行精進定。」是名上草益。蓋三藏教主慈善根力利益之相也。經云：「若諸菩薩智慧堅固，了達三界，求最上乘。」即三人同觀無生，非但有前析智之益，別有巧度，即是體真，是為小樹增長益。蓋通教主利益之相。經云：「復有住禪得神通力，聞諸法空，心大歡喜。」住禪者，住九種大禪；心大歡喜者，登歡喜地，度無數億百千眾生，是名大樹增長。非但有前因果析體之益，而別有分別道種智，乃至一切種智益。蓋別教主利益之相。經云：今當為汝說最實事，不啻如前之益，乃有即破無明、顯出佛性究竟實益。蓋圓教主利益之相。

As for discussing benefit in the particular sense, this differs in its own shallow and deep degrees: the śrāvaka severs the correct affliction, and the pratyekabuddha further encroaches upon its habituated residue — both alike named the middling grass. The bodhisattva subdues confusion, together with delivering sentient beings — hence the sūtra says: "Seeking the World-Honored One's own stage, I too shall become a buddha — practicing diligence and concentration" — this is named the benefit of the superior grass. This, then, is the mark of benefit belonging to the Tripiṭaka teaching's own master, the power of his loving-kindness's own wholesome root. The sūtra says: "If the bodhisattvas' own wisdom is firm and unshakable, fully penetrating through the three realms, seeking the supreme vehicle" — this refers to the three kinds of person alike contemplating no-generation, not merely possessing the previous benefit of analytical wisdom, but, further, possessing a skillful crossing-over, precisely embodying the real — this is the benefit of the small tree's own growth. This, then, is the mark of benefit belonging to the shared teaching's own master. The sūtra says: "There are, further, those who, abiding in dhyāna, attain the power of spiritual transformation, and, hearing that every dharma is empty, their minds are greatly delighted" — abiding in dhyāna means abiding in the nine kinds of great dhyāna; the mind greatly delighted means ascending to the ground of joy, delivering countless koṭis of hundreds of thousands of sentient beings — this is named the great tree's own growth. Not merely possessing the previous benefit of analyzing and embodying cause and fruit, but, further, possessing the distinctly discerned wisdom of the paths, down through the benefit of the wisdom knowing all modes. This, then, is the mark of benefit belonging to the distinct teaching's own master. The sūtra says: "I shall now speak to you of the most real matter" — this is not merely the same as the benefit given before, but, further, possesses the benefit of directly breaking through ignorance and revealing buddha-nature, the ultimate real benefit. This, then, is the mark of benefit belonging to the perfect teaching's own master.

復次前三教益，劣不兼勝，勝則兼劣，可解(云云)。

又歷五味者，乳教但因、果、大樹、實事四益，而不明三草一木，以大乘經不入二乘人手，如聾如瘂故。酪教但有三草等四益。生蘇備有七益。熟蘇無析法三草，而有體法等七益。醍醐但有實事益。

前諸益皆麁，今益則妙。近從寂滅訖至法華為生身菩薩，但得八番之益，不得第九、第十益也。又有得義者，即是菩薩從法性身來入分段，作願通應生等眷屬，進破無明，斷除殘品，即得明第九、第十番益也。始從寂滅，終至法華，略言十益也。

Again, the previous three teachings' own benefit — the lesser does not include the greater, while the greater does include the lesser — this may be understood directly, and so on.

Again, discussed across the Five Flavors: the milk teaching possesses only the four benefits of cause, fruit, the great tree, and the most real matter, without clarifying the three grasses or the single tree, since the Mahāyāna sūtra does not fall into the two vehicles' own hands, as if they were deaf or mute. The cream teaching possesses only the three grasses and the rest, four benefits. The curdled-butter teaching is fully complete with all seven benefits. The clarified-butter teaching lacks the analytical dharma's own three grasses, yet possesses the embodying dharma's own seven benefits and the rest. The ghee possesses only the benefit of the most real matter.

Every benefit given before is coarse; the present benefit alone is wondrous. Near benefit, from quiescent extinction all the way up to, but not including, the Lotus Sūtra, applies to the bodhisattva still within the birth-body, who obtains only the first eight rounds' own benefit, and does not yet obtain the ninth or tenth benefit. There is, further, a sense in which it may be obtained: this is precisely the bodhisattva who, coming from the body of the dharma-nature, enters segmented birth-and-death, forming the retinue born through vow, spiritual power, response, and the rest, advancing to break through ignorance, severing and removing the residual grades — this alone is able to clarify the ninth and tenth rounds' own benefit. Beginning from quiescent extinction and ending at the Lotus Sūtra, this is, in brief, spoken of as the ten benefits.

問：
法身菩薩，聞應身佛說法，應身中益，亦令法身得益耶？

答：
譬如磨鏡，鏡轉明，色像亦明。

又問：
應身聞法益、法身亦益者，應身現病，法身亦病耶？

答：
此病若實者，應病法亦病。秖為應病非實，非實故，應身無病，法身亦無病。又若應身現病少，當知法身益亦少。若能應身現病廣，法身益亦廣也。

Question: When a bodhisattva of the Dharma-body hears the response-body buddha teach the Dharma, does the benefit received within the response-body also cause the Dharma-body to obtain benefit?

Answer: It is comparable to polishing a mirror — as the mirror grows clearer, the image reflected within it grows clearer too.

Further question: If, when the response-body hears the Dharma and receives benefit, the Dharma-body, too, receives benefit — then, when the response-body manifests sickness, does the Dharma-body, too, become sick?

Answer: Were this sickness genuinely real, then, when the response-body is sick, the Dharma would be sick too. It is simply that the response-body's own sickness is not real, and, being unreal, the response-body itself has no sickness, and the Dharma-body, too, has no sickness. Again, if the response-body manifests only a slight sickness, one should know that the Dharma-body's own benefit, too, is slight; if the response-body is able to manifest a broad sickness, the Dharma-body's own benefit, too, is broad.

今作諸句料簡：自有果益、因不益，因益、果不益，俱益，俱不益，此即現事可解。

自有壞益、成益、亦壞亦成益、不壞不成益。不壞不成益，是清涼益；四趣因，是壞益；非想因，是成益；中間，是亦成亦壞益(云云)。

自有因益即果益，果益即因益，此變易因移果易意也。自有因益非增道，果益非損生，得是因果益，不得是因果益，分段報因果也。

自有因益是增道，果益是損生，不得是因果益，得是因果益，習因習果也。

自有真益非俗益，二乘也。俗益非真益，六度菩薩也。自有先俗益後真益，六度菩薩也。自有先真益後俗益，通菩薩也。自有真俗益非中益，中益非真俗益，別也。自有真益即俗益，亦即中道益，圓也。

Here, this is resolved through a series of propositions: there is benefit of the fruit without benefit of the cause; benefit of the cause without benefit of the fruit; benefit of both together; and benefit of neither — this may be understood directly from present phenomena.

There is benefit as breaking, benefit as accomplishing, benefit as both breaking and accomplishing, and benefit as neither breaking nor accomplishing. Benefit as neither breaking nor accomplishing is the benefit of coolness; the cause of the four evil destinies is benefit as breaking; the cause of the heaven of neither-perception-nor-non-perception is benefit as accomplishing; the intervening cases are benefit as both accomplishing and breaking, and so on.

There is benefit of the cause that is itself already benefit of the fruit, and benefit of the fruit that is itself already benefit of the cause — this is the sense of transformational birth-and-death, where cause shifts and fruit changes. There is benefit of the cause that does not increase the path, and benefit of the fruit that does not diminish birth — obtaining this benefit of cause and fruit, or not obtaining this benefit of cause and fruit, is the cause and fruit of segmented retribution.

There is benefit of the cause that does increase the path, and benefit of the fruit that does diminish birth — not obtaining this benefit of cause and fruit, or obtaining this benefit of cause and fruit, is the habituated cause and habituated fruit.

There is benefit of the real that is not benefit of the conventional — this is the two vehicles. Benefit of the conventional that is not benefit of the real — this is the bodhisattva of the six pāramitās. There is benefit of the conventional first and benefit of the real afterward — this is the bodhisattva of the six pāramitās. There is benefit of the real first and benefit of the conventional afterward — this is the shared teaching's own bodhisattva. There is benefit of the real and the conventional that is not benefit of the middle, and benefit of the middle that is not benefit of the real and the conventional — this is the distinct teaching. There is benefit of the real that is itself already benefit of the conventional, and is itself already benefit of the middle way as well — this is the perfect teaching.

Reading note

Near benefit narrows the ten-round distant-benefit scheme from Fascicle 6 to a single teaching career — from Śākyamuni's own awakening beneath the bodhi tree up to, but not including, the Lotus Sūtra's own assembly — mapped again onto each of the Four Teachings' own master, each described in a single formulaic phrase (the Tripiṭaka master's "power of loving-kindness's own wholesome root," the perfect master's "revealing buddha-nature, the ultimate real benefit") that echoes the small-grass-to-most-real-matter ladder one final time before it is finally judged wondrous rather than coarse. The mirror-and-reflection simile answering whether a Dharma-body bodhisattva benefits from a response-body's own teaching, and the careful qualification that follows (the response-body's sickness is never real, so neither is any sympathetic sickness in the Dharma-body) heads off a literalism this treatise has guarded against throughout: apparent suffering in a buddha's manifest body is never evidence of genuine suffering in what that body manifests from.

Benefit Within the Present Text 當文利益

Chinese Source

三當文利益，就今經備有七益，雖復差別，即無差別。譬如芽、莖、枝、葉，生長不同，而是一地所生。七益誠復淺深，無非實相，故言差別無差別也。諸經差別麁益，同入此經無差妙益，或進入諸妙益，或按位成妙益。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, benefit within the present text: within the present sūtra, all seven benefits are fully present, and yet, though thus differentiated, they are without any difference at all. It is comparable to the sprout, the stem, the branch, and the leaf, each growing differently, and yet all alike growing from this same single ground. The seven benefits are indeed shallow or deep, yet none of them is anything other than the real mark — hence, it is said, differentiated, and yet without difference.

Every other sūtra's own differentiated, coarse benefit alike enters into this present sūtra's own undifferentiated, wondrous benefit — sometimes advancing into a further wondrous benefit, sometimes remaining fixed at its own stage while still becoming wondrous.

進入益者，本是地上清涼，今則進發大乘，解心明淨，或進觀行妙，相似分真中。本是人天因益，今進入相似分真。本是小乘學無學益，今進破無明分真妙益，譬如迴聲入角轉小為大也。通、別進益，例此可知。

按位益者，本是麁果，地上清涼，即成理妙之益。按於麁因之益，即是觀行妙益。按麁學無學益，即是相似妙益。開麁即妙，不須進入。通、別，例此可知。

進入妙益，即是待麁益明妙益；按位益，即是絕待妙益(云云)。

As for the benefit of advancing further: what was originally coolness upon the ground now advances and gives rise to the Mahāyāna, understanding of mind made clear and pure — sometimes advancing into the wondrous of contemplative practice, into resemblance, or into a portion of the truth. What was originally the benefit of the cause belonging to humans and devas now advances into resemblance or a portion of the truth. What was originally the benefit of the Hīnayāna's own learner or one beyond learning now advances into breaking through ignorance, the wondrous benefit of a portion of the truth — comparable to an echo entering a horn, turning the small into the great. The shared and distinct teachings' own benefit of advancing follows this same pattern, understood accordingly.

As for the benefit of remaining fixed at its own stage: what was originally a coarse fruit, coolness upon the ground, is itself already the wondrous benefit of principle. Fixed at the coarse benefit of cause, this is itself already the wondrous benefit of contemplative practice. Fixed at the coarse benefit of the learner or one beyond learning, this is itself already the wondrous benefit of resemblance. Opening the coarse, it is itself already the wondrous — there is no need to advance any further at all. The shared and distinct teachings follow this same pattern, understood accordingly.

The benefit of advancing further is precisely clarifying the wondrous benefit set against the coarse, in the relative sense; the benefit of remaining fixed at its own stage is precisely the wondrous benefit in the absolute sense, and so on.

諸麁益判眷屬者，果、因二益，堪為業生眷屬。中上二草小樹等，堪為願通眷屬。大樹見性已去，皆應生眷屬攝(云云)。

進入按位者，理妙、假名、觀行妙，堪為業生眷屬。相似妙，堪為願通眷屬。分真，堪為應生眷屬。是名此經利益之相也(云云)。

As for judging the retinue through every coarse benefit: the two benefits of fruit and cause are fit to become the retinue born through karma. The middling and superior grasses, and the small tree and the rest, are fit to become the retinue of vow and spiritual power. The great tree onward, once one's own nature is seen, is, every one, subsumed within the retinue born through response, and so on.

As for advancing further or remaining fixed at one's own stage: the wondrous of principle, of the provisional name, and of contemplative practice are fit to become the retinue born through karma. The wondrous of resemblance is fit to become the retinue of vow and spiritual power. A portion of the truth is fit to become the retinue born through response. This is named the mark of benefit belonging to the present sūtra, and so on.

Reading note

The "differentiated, yet without difference" formula closing this section is the same move this treatise has made for the twelve divisions of scripture, the six spiritual powers, and the ten wondrous aspects themselves: apparent multiplicity (seven benefits, shallow and deep) turns out, on inspection, to grow from a single source, the way sprout, stem, branch, and leaf all grow from one ground. The advancing/fixed-in-place (進入/按位) distinction usefully separates two different senses in which "coarse becomes wondrous": sometimes a benefit genuinely progresses to a higher stage (echo into horn, "turning the small into the great"), and sometimes the very same stage is simply re-read as wondrous once its coarse reading is opened — the relative and absolute readings of "wondrous" applied one final time, now to benefit itself.

Benefit Within the Propagation Section 流通中利益

Chinese Source

三、明流通利益者，為三：一出師。二出法。三出益。

弘經行人，具通凡聖，若法身菩薩誓願莊嚴，令此土、他土、下土、上土得權實七益、九益、十益，化功歸己，還資法身，增道損生也。生身菩薩亦能此土、他土弘經，令他得權實七益，化功歸己，增道損生，而不能上土利益也。凡夫之師亦能此土弘經，令他得權實七益，化功歸己，增益品位。故《無量義》云：「有病導師，在於此岸，而成就船筏，渡人彼岸。」即其義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying benefit within the propagation section: this is treated under three headings — first, bringing forth the teacher; second, bringing forth the method; third, bringing forth the benefit.

Those who propagate the sūtra span, in general, both ordinary being and sage alike. If a bodhisattva of the Dharma-body, adorned with the vow, causes this land, other lands, the lower land, and the higher land to obtain the seven, nine, or ten benefits of both the provisional and the real, the merit of this transformation returns to himself, in turn nourishing the Dharma-body, increasing the path and diminishing birth. A bodhisattva still within the birth-body, too, is able to propagate the sūtra in this land and in other lands, causing others to obtain the seven benefits of both the provisional and the real, the merit of this transformation returning to himself, increasing the path and diminishing birth, yet unable to bring benefit to the higher land. Even an ordinary being's own teacher is able to propagate the sūtra in this land, causing others to obtain the seven benefits of both the provisional and the real, the merit of this transformation returning to himself, increasing his own rank. Hence the Sūtra of Innumerable Meanings says: "A guiding teacher who is himself sick, standing upon this shore, nonetheless builds a boat or a raft, and ferries others across to the further shore" — this is precisely this same sense.

問：
凡夫但能為凡夫弘經，使凡夫得益，亦能令聖人得益耶？

答：
聖有二種：一小乘聖，二大乘聖。如經云：「若有實得阿羅漢，生滅度想，若遇餘佛，便得決了。」南岳師云：初依名餘佛，無明未破名之為餘。能知如來祕密之藏，深覺圓理，名之為佛。佛滅後實得羅漢者，於權實未了。若遇初依，即能決了，成相似益，成進入分真益。此文往證凡夫之師，得為小乘聖人弘經得益也。經云：「六根清淨人說法，十方諸佛皆樂見之，皆向其處說法，一切天龍聞其所說，皆大歡喜。」此亦是凡夫師為大聖說法之明文也。

Question: An ordinary being is able only to propagate the sūtra for the sake of other ordinary beings, causing them to obtain benefit — is he also able to cause a sage to obtain benefit?

Answer: The sage is of two kinds — first, the Hīnayāna sage; second, the Mahāyāna sage. As the sūtra says: "If there is one who has genuinely attained arhatship, giving rise to the notion that he has already reached extinction — should he encounter a further buddha, he shall thereupon come to full resolution." Nanyue says: the first stage of reliance is named "a further buddha," since ignorance, not yet broken through, is why he is named "further"; being able to know the Tathāgata's own secret treasury, deeply awakening to the perfect principle, is why he is named "buddha." One who, after the Buddha's own extinction, has genuinely attained arhatship, has not yet fully resolved the provisional and the real. Should he encounter the first stage of reliance, he is thereby able to come to full resolution, accomplishing the benefit of resemblance, accomplishing the benefit of advancing into a portion of the truth. This text serves to prove, in turn, that an ordinary being's own teacher is able to propagate the sūtra for the sake of a Hīnayāna sage and obtain this same benefit. The sūtra says: "A person of purified six faculties who teaches the Dharma — every buddha throughout the ten directions delights to behold him, and each turns toward that very place to teach the Dharma there; every deva and nāga, hearing what he teaches, is greatly delighted" — this, too, is explicit textual proof of an ordinary being's own teacher teaching the Dharma for the sake of a great sage.

二出法者，通經方法，明出聖言。文云：「若眾生不信受者，當於餘深法中，示教利喜。」餘者，帶方便也。深者，明中道也。帶方便明中道者，即別教也。若但方便，不明中道，即通、藏等教也。

經文許用別助圓，而例推亦應用通助圓。又文云：「更以異方便，助顯第一義。」豈隔藏、通耶？但菩薩已得實慧，亦得權意，不以實濫權，亦不謂權是實；但為弘實而眾生不信，須為實施權，以淺助深，無虛妄也。此則雙用權實而弘經也。安樂行云：「若有難問，不以小乘法答，但以大乘而為解說，令得一切種智。」此則但令用實弘經也。又云：「隨宜為說」，此亦不隔於權也。今時人弘法，或一向用大，或一向用小，皆不得佛意。善弘經者，用與適時，口雖說權，而內心不違實法。但使眾生得權實七益，於弘經暢矣。

Second, bringing forth the method: the method for propagating the sūtra is clarified by bringing forth the sage's own words. The text says: "If sentient beings do not believe and accept it, one ought to instruct, teach, benefit, and delight them within some further, deeper dharma." "Further" here bears the sense of expedient means; "deeper" here clarifies the middle way. Bearing expedient means while clarifying the middle way is precisely the distinct teaching. If it is merely expedient means, without clarifying the middle way, this is precisely the shared or Tripiṭaka teaching and the rest.

The sūtra's own text permits using the distinct teaching to assist the perfect; by this same reasoning, the shared teaching, too, ought to be used to assist the perfect. Again, the text says: "Further, by means of a different expedient means, assisting to reveal the truth of ultimate meaning" — how, then, could this exclude the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings? It is simply that the bodhisattva, having already attained real wisdom, likewise attains the sense of the provisional as well, neither confusing the real with the provisional, nor supposing the provisional to be the real; it is simply that, propagating the real, and sentient beings not believing it, one must set forth the provisional for the sake of the real, using the shallow to assist the deep, without any falsehood involved. This, then, is using both the provisional and the real together to propagate the sūtra. The "Peaceful Practices" chapter says: "Should there be a difficult question, do not answer with the Hīnayāna's own dharma, but explain it only through the Mahāyāna, causing them to attain the wisdom knowing all modes" — this, then, causes the real alone to be used in propagating the sūtra. And it is also said: "Teach according to what is fitting" — this, too, does not exclude the provisional at all. People today, propagating the Dharma, either use the great exclusively, or use the small exclusively — both alike fail to grasp the Buddha's own intent. One who propagates the sūtra well uses each according to what the moment calls for — though his mouth may speak the provisional, his inner mind never departs from the real dharma. So long as sentient beings are caused to obtain the seven benefits of both the provisional and the real, this alone brings propagation of the sūtra to its fullest expression.

三出益者，然流通利益，不待第三流通段方明利益。秖正說文中，已指未來弘經之利。〈譬喻品〉後、〈授記品〉末、〈法師品〉中，皆明弘經功德利益：能於如來滅後聞一句偈者，亦與受三菩提記，況弘宣者！竊為一人說者，功德尚多，況處眾廣說者！展轉至第五十人隨喜功德，尚非二乘境界，況最初會中聞隨喜者！常不輕流通一句，尚得六根清淨，況具足流通者！初品弟子弘經功德，無量億劫行五波羅蜜不得為喻。況第五品，十方虛空，寧有邊表？五品弘經，尚叵窮盡，皆云：「入如來室，著如來衣，坐如來座。」如來之法，皆非數量，況八萬大士、千界微塵菩薩而當可說耶？而當可知耶？唯除如來，無能盡知者也。

Third, bringing forth the benefit: yet the benefit of propagation does not wait for the third, propagation section, before benefit can be clarified — already, within the correct-teaching section itself, the future benefit of propagating the sūtra has been pointed to. After the "Parable" chapter, at the end of the "Prediction" chapter, and within the "Teacher of the Dharma" chapter, the merit and benefit of propagating the sūtra is, in every case, clarified: whoever, after the Tathāgata's own extinction, hears even a single verse is given, in turn, the prediction of the threefold bodhi — how much more, then, one who propagates and proclaims it! One who quietly teaches it to a single person alone already possesses abundant merit — how much more, then, one who broadly teaches it before an assembly! Passing it along, turn by turn, until the fiftieth person's own sympathetic joy is already beyond the very domain of the two vehicles — how much more, then, one who hears it with joy at the very first assembly! Never-Disparaging, propagating a single phrase, already attained the purification of the six faculties — how much more, then, one who propagates it in full! The merit of a disciple of the first rank propagating the sūtra cannot be illustrated even by immeasurable koṭis of eons spent practicing the five pāramitās. How much more, then, the fifth rank — could the empty space of the ten directions possibly have any edge or limit? The five ranks' own propagation of the sūtra is already beyond exhaustion, each of them said to "enter the Tathāgata's own room, don the Tathāgata's own robe, and sit upon the Tathāgata's own seat." The Tathāgata's own dharma is, in every case, beyond number and measure — how much more, then, the eighty thousand great beings, the bodhisattvas numerous as motes of dust across a thousand worlds — could this possibly be spoken of in full? Could this possibly be known in full? Only the Tathāgata himself, and none other, is able to know it entirely.

凡師弘經，令凡七益。經云：「此經是閻浮提人病之良藥，若聞是經，不老不死」者，於老死中，識老死實相。老死，是果報法；識實相，即得清涼理妙益也，亦果報益也。能持此經故，生安樂土，處蓮華中，不為貪欲所惱，亦是離十惱亂，是善行菩薩道；亦名名字益，亦是觀行妙，亦是修因妙。得陀羅尼，能旋假入空，即是下、中、上藥草等益，亦是小樹益；得百千旋陀羅尼，即大樹益；得法音方便陀羅尼，是相似實益；若有須臾聞，即得究竟三菩提，即是真實益。復次，如人穿鑿高原見乾土，是下、中、上藥草益。見泥，是小樹大樹益。得水，是最實益。後五百歲尚獲此益，況復今時，弘經利他，寧無七益耶？

An ordinary being's own teacher, propagating the sūtra, causes an ordinary benefit of the seven kinds. The sūtra says: "This sūtra is the excellent medicine for the sickness of the people of Jambudvīpa; whoever hears this sūtra shall neither age nor die" — within old age and death, one recognizes the real mark of old age and death itself. Old age and death are the very dharma of fruit and retribution; recognizing their real mark is itself obtaining the wondrous benefit of coolness in principle, and is likewise the benefit of the fruit. Being able to uphold this sūtra, one is thereby born within the land of peace and bliss, dwelling within a lotus flower, no longer troubled by craving and desire — this, too, is being free of the ten disturbances, this being the bodhisattva path skillfully practiced; this, too, is named the benefit in name, and is likewise the wondrous of contemplative practice, and is likewise the wondrous of cultivating the cause. Attaining the dhāraṇī, able to turn the provisional and enter emptiness, this is precisely the benefit of the inferior, middling, and superior medicinal herbs and the rest, and is likewise the benefit of the small tree; attaining the dhāraṇī of a hundred thousand revolutions, this is the benefit of the great tree; attaining the dhāraṇī of the sound of the Dharma's own skillful means, this is the real benefit of resemblance; if one hears it for even a single instant, one thereby attains the ultimate threefold bodhi at once — this is precisely the real benefit itself. Again, it is like a person digging into a high plateau: seeing dry earth is the benefit of the inferior, middling, and superior medicinal herbs. Seeing mud is the benefit of the small tree and the great tree. Reaching water is the benefit of the most real matter. Even in the final five hundred years, this same benefit is still obtained — how much more, then, in the present age, propagating the sūtra to benefit others — how could the seven benefits possibly be absent?

Reading note

The three-tiered teacher hierarchy — Dharma-body bodhisattva, birth-body bodhisattva, ordinary being — grades not whether one can propagate the sūtra effectively (all three can) but how far the resulting benefit reaches: only a Dharma-body bodhisattva's own propagation touches "the higher land" as well as this one. The Q&A defending an ordinary teacher's ability to benefit even a Hīnayāna sage turns on Nanyue Huisi's own gloss of "a further buddha" as the first stage of the fifty-two-stage reliance scheme (already an unusually concrete citation of Zhiyi's own teacher by name, matching this treatise's earlier such moments). The "bringing forth the method" section's defense of using every one of the Four Teachings, provisional and real together, as needed — "though his mouth may speak the provisional, his inner mind never departs from the real dharma" — reads as practical guidance as much as doctrine: a warning against propagating "the great exclusively" or "the small exclusively," aimed as much at Zhiyi's own contemporaries as at any hypothetical reader.

The Contemplation of Mind 觀心中利益

Chinese Source

第四觀心者，小乘明心起，未動身口，不名為業。大乘明剎那造罪，殃墜無間。無間是大苦報處，剎那促起業處。促心暫起，重業已成，況九法界而不具足。若能淨心，諸業即淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the contemplation of mind: the Hīnayāna clarifies that, when a mind arises but has not yet set body or speech into motion, this is not yet named karma. The Mahāyāna clarifies that, in a single instant, an offense is committed, and its calamity plunges one into the uninterrupted hell. The uninterrupted hell is the place of the greatest suffering's own retribution, and a single instant is the swiftest place at which karma may arise. A hasty mind, stirring only briefly, already accomplishes grave karma — how much more, then, the nine dharma-realms, none of which is left incomplete? If one is able to purify the mind, every karma is thereby purified at once.

淨心觀者，謂觀諸心，悉是因緣生法，即空、即假、即中，一心三觀。以是觀故，知心非心，心但有名；知法非法，法無有我；知名無名，即是我等；知法無法，即涅槃等。此解起時，於我、我所，如雲如幻，即是地上清涼益。信敬慚愧，諸善心生，於空、假、中，意而有勇，即是因益。念念與即空相應，是中、上草、小樹等益。念念與即假相應，是大樹益。念念與即中相應，是最實事益。

於一念益心，七種分別(云云)。

As for the contemplation of the pure mind: this means contemplating every mind as, in every case, a dharma born through causes and conditions, at once empty, at once provisional, at once the middle — the single mind's own threefold contemplation. Through this contemplation, one knows the mind to be no mind at all, the mind possessing only a name; one knows the dharma to be no dharma at all, the dharma possessing no self; one knows the name to be without any name, and this itself is the very meaning of "self" and the rest; one knows the dharma to be without any dharma, and this itself is the very meaning of "nirvāṇa" and the rest. When this understanding arises, with respect to the self and what belongs to the self, it is like a cloud, like an illusion — this is precisely the benefit of coolness upon the ground. Faith, reverence, shame, and conscience, every wholesome mind arising, and, with respect to emptiness, the provisional, and the middle, the mind possessing courage — this is precisely the benefit of the cause. The mind, moment after moment, corresponding to emptiness-as-such, is the benefit of the middling and superior grasses, the small tree, and the rest. The mind, moment after moment, corresponding to the provisional-as-such, is the benefit of the great tree. The mind, moment after moment, corresponding to the middle-as-such, is the benefit of the most real matter.

Within a single thought-moment's own beneficial mind, these seven distinctions are present, and so on.

夫一向無生觀人，但信心益，不信外佛威加益，此墮自性癡。又一向信外佛加，不內心求益，此墮他性癡。共癡、無因癡，亦可解。

自性癡人，眼見世間牽重不前者，傍力助進。云何不信罪垢重者，佛威建立，令觀慧得益耶？又汝從何處，得是無生內觀耶？從師耶從經耶？從自悟耶？師與經，即是汝之外緣。若自悟者，必被冥加，汝不知恩，如樹木不識日月風雨等恩。又三事，汝不知外加：一不信教。二不自行求外加。三不教人。直是汝之不信，非無外益也。經曰：非內非外，而內而外。而內故，諸佛解脫，於心中求。

Now, a person who contemplates no-generation exclusively, believing only in the mind's own benefit and not believing in the external buddha's own majestic assistance, falls into the delusion of self-nature alone. Again, a person who believes exclusively in the external buddha's own assistance, without seeking benefit within his own mind, falls into the delusion of other-nature alone. The delusion of both together, and the delusion of neither cause at all, may likewise be understood accordingly.

As for the person deluded by self-nature alone: one's own eyes see, in the world, that whoever is burdened too heavily to advance receives assistance from some external force to help them onward — how, then, could one fail to believe that the one whose own offenses and defilements are heavy likewise receives the Buddha's own majestic power, established to cause the contemplative wisdom to obtain benefit? Again, from where, precisely, did you yourself obtain this very inner contemplation of no-generation? From a teacher? From a sūtra? From your own awakening alone? A teacher and a sūtra are both, precisely, your own external condition. If it came from your own awakening alone, you must nonetheless have been assisted in a hidden way, and you simply fail to recognize this debt of gratitude — like a tree that does not recognize its own debt to the sun, the moon, the wind, and the rain. Again, there are three matters through which you fail to recognize this external assistance: first, not believing the teaching; second, not personally seeking this external assistance through one's own practice; third, not teaching others. This is simply your own failure to believe — it is not that this external benefit is absent. The sūtra says: neither internal nor external, and yet both internal and external. Being internal, every buddha's own liberation is to be sought within the mind itself.

而外故，諸佛護念。云何不信外益耶！(云云)他、共、無因癡，例可解。即假故，無自性；即空故，無他性；即中故，無共性；雙照故，無無因性(云云)。

Being external, every buddha's own mindful protection is present. How, then, could one fail to believe in this external benefit! And so on. The delusion of other-nature, of both together, and of neither cause at all, may likewise be understood by this same pattern. Being provisional-as-such, there is no self-nature; being empty-as-such, there is no other-nature; being the middle-as-such, there is no shared nature; illuminating both at once, there is no natureless-cause nature either, and so on.

Reading note

The self-nature/other-nature/both/neither tetralemma closing this section applies this treatise's standard four-cornered refutation to a genuinely practical spiritual question: does grace come from within or without? Zhiyi's answer refuses the false choice directly — "neither internal nor external, and yet both internal and external" — and backs it with an argument from ordinary experience (a burdened traveler visibly helped by an outside force) alongside a pointed challenge: even one's own "self-awakened" insight into no-generation had to come from somewhere, a teacher or a sūtra at minimum, and failing to recognize that debt is compared to a tree failing to recognize what it owes the sun, moon, wind, and rain. This closes the wondrous of merit and benefit (利益妙) in its entirety, the tenth and final of the ten wondrous aspects — and, with it, the entire ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate (迹門十妙) that have structured this treatise since the opening of Fascicle 2. The fascicle continues with a closing synthesis of the whole ten-wondrous-aspects scheme, resolving it into the provisional and the real (結成權實).

Closing by Resolving into the Provisional and the Real 結成權實

Chinese Source

第五結成權實者，光宅云：「照三三之境為權，照四一之境為實。」今不用此解。既以大乘果為大理，何不用小乘果為小理？彼救云：小果非真，故不以其果為理。若爾，權教及權行人，何嘗是實？既立權教行人，何不立權理？又權若無理，俗不應稱諦，既言俗諦，權不應止三也。實有四者，夫因果是二法，云何以二法為理一耶？經云：「觀一切法如實相，不行不分別。」云何分別因果為理一？若爾，便無實相，則魔所說，故不用彼釋。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, closing by resolving into the provisional and the real: Guangzhai says: "Illuminating the object of the threefold-threefold is the provisional; illuminating the object of the fourfold-one is the real." Here, this explanation is not adopted. Since the Mahāyāna's own fruit is taken as the great principle, why should the Hīnayāna's own fruit not likewise be taken as a lesser principle? That position defends itself by saying: the lesser fruit is not real, and hence its fruit is not taken as principle. If this is so, then the provisional teaching and its provisional practitioners — how could they ever be the real at all? Since a provisional teaching and its practitioners are already established, why should a provisional principle not likewise be established? Again, if the provisional possessed no principle at all, the conventional ought not to be named a truth — yet, since it is already spoken of as the conventional truth, the provisional ought not to be confined to three alone. As for the real possessing four: cause and fruit are two distinct dharmas — how, then, could these two dharmas be taken as a single principle? The sūtra says: "Contemplating every dharma as the real mark, neither acting nor discriminating" — how, then, could cause and fruit be discriminated as a single principle? If this is so, there would then be no real mark at all, and this would be what Māra himself teaches — hence, that same explanation is not adopted.

今明照十麁之境為權，照十妙之境為實。十麁者即前九法界，三因緣等諸麁諦智，乃至麁利益，皆稱權也。照十妙者，即是理妙，乃至利益妙，妙故為實。復次為十妙故，開出十麁，如為蓮故華，意在於蓮，而蓮隱不現。於餘深法，示教利喜，餘法有實，而實不顯。文云：「如來方便意趣難解也」。又華開蓮現，譬開十麁顯十妙，則無復十麁，唯一大事不可思議境界，乃至利益。肇師云：「始從佛國，終法供養，皆明不可思議。」今亦例爾，既開麁已，始終皆妙。

Here, it is clarified that illuminating the object of the ten coarse aspects is the provisional, and illuminating the object of the ten wondrous aspects is the real. The ten coarse aspects are precisely the previous nine dharma-realms, the three causal conditions, and every other coarse truth and wisdom, down through coarse benefit — all of these, alike, are named the provisional. Illuminating the ten wondrous aspects — namely, the wondrous of principle, down through the wondrous of benefit — being wondrous, these are named the real. Again, it is for the sake of the ten wondrous aspects that the ten coarse aspects are opened forth in the first place — comparable to the flower existing for the sake of the lotus seed-pod, the true intent lying in the seed-pod, though the seed-pod itself remains hidden, not yet manifest. Within some further, deeper dharma, instructing, teaching, benefiting, and delighting — this further dharma possesses the real, though the real itself remains unrevealed. The text says: "The Tathāgata's own skillful intent is difficult to fathom." Again, once the flower opens, the seed-pod is revealed — this illustrates opening the ten coarse aspects to reveal the ten wondrous aspects: there is then no further coarse remaining at all, only the one great matter, the inconceivable state, down through benefit itself. Sengzhao says: "From the very buddha-land at the start through to the offering of the Dharma at the end, every one of these clarifies the inconceivable." Here, too, this same pattern applies: once the coarse has already been opened, beginning to end, all is wondrous.

又約五味者，乳味則有為十妙明十麁，開十麁顯十妙，則成一權一實；若就四悉檀，則有六權二實；若約四門，則十二權四實也。

若約三藏一向是權，化城楊葉，還就三藏，約化他為權，就自行為實；約四悉檀，三權一實；約四門，十二權四實。

若方等既備有四教，故三十種權一十種實；若約四悉檀，十四權二實；對四門五十六權八實。

若約摩訶般若，既廢三藏，但用三教，通別二十種為權，一十種圓為實；若約悉檀，十權二實；若歷四門，四十權八實。

Again, discussed in terms of the Five Flavors: within the milk flavor, the ten wondrous aspects, taken as conditioned, clarify the ten coarse aspects; opening the ten coarse aspects to reveal the ten wondrous aspects thereby makes one provisional and one real. If discussed in terms of the four siddhāntas, this makes six provisional and two real. If discussed in terms of the four gates, this makes twelve provisional and four real.

As for the Tripiṭaka teaching, taken as entirely provisional — the phantom city, the willow leaf — returning to the Tripiṭaka teaching itself: discussed in terms of transforming others, this is the provisional; discussed in terms of one's own practice, this is the real. Discussed in terms of the four siddhāntas, this makes three provisional and one real. Discussed in terms of the four gates, this makes twelve provisional and four real.

As for the vaipulya teachings, since they are already complete with all Four Teachings, this makes thirty kinds of provisional and ten kinds of real. Discussed in terms of the four siddhāntas, this makes fourteen provisional and two real. Matched against the four gates, this makes fifty-six provisional and eight real.

As for the Mahāprajñāpāramitā, since the Tripiṭaka teaching is already set aside, only the remaining three teachings are used — the shared and distinct teachings, twenty kinds, are the provisional; the perfect teaching, ten kinds, is the real. Discussed in terms of the siddhāntas, this makes ten provisional and two real. Passing through the four gates, this makes forty provisional and eight real.

若至法華，前來一向皆廢，但說一實，實中非無方便，但是實相方便，同稱為實。今約悉檀者，未悟之前三權，悟即一實；若歷四門，十二權四實，名數一往同三藏，而意有天懸而地殊。彼教十二權四實，一向是權；法華一向是實。料簡異方等、般若(云云)。故云：「但說無上道，示真實相。」此之謂也。

若約涅槃，涅槃備釋四教，亦是三十權一十實，一往似同方等，而意逈異。彼則二入實，二不入實。今涅槃四俱入實，因中則有三權一實，在果則四實而無權。若約四悉檀，十四權二實；歷四門，五十六權八實；若更約三因門，五十六權；若果門四實但是實，仍其本因，故說四耳。是則四門入實，約果四實十二權，法華義齊也。

As for arriving at the Lotus Sūtra: everything that came before is now, without exception, set aside, and only the one real is taught. Within this real, expedient means are not entirely absent, but this is precisely the real mark's own expedient means, alike named the real. Here, discussed in terms of the siddhāntas: before awakening, there are three provisional; once awakened, this is the one real. Discussed across the four gates, this makes twelve provisional and four real — the very name and number, at a first glance, the same as the Tripiṭaka teaching's own, and yet the sense involved differs as widely as heaven from earth. That teaching's own twelve provisional and four real are, throughout, entirely provisional; the Lotus Sūtra's own are, throughout, entirely real. This distinction differs from the vaipulya and perfection-of-wisdom teachings, and so on. Hence it is said: "Only teaching the unsurpassed path, revealing the real mark itself" — this is precisely what is meant here.

As for the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra: the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, too, is fully complete with an explanation of all Four Teachings, and is likewise thirty kinds of provisional and ten kinds of real — at a first glance, seemingly the same as the vaipulya teachings, and yet the sense involved is entirely different. There, two enter the real and two do not enter the real; here, in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, all four alike enter the real — within the cause, there are three provisional and one real; within the fruit, there are four real and no provisional at all. Discussed in terms of the four siddhāntas, this makes fourteen provisional and two real; passing through the four gates, this makes fifty-six provisional and eight real; if, further, discussed in terms of the threefold cause-gate, this makes fifty-six provisional; and, as for the fruit-gate, its four real are simply real, following their own original cause, and hence spoken of as four. This, then, is the four gates entering the real; discussed in terms of the fruit, four real and twelve provisional — the Lotus Sūtra's own sense is equal to this.

故知諸教，雖同有權實，權實不同，或一向實、或一向權、或權實相兼，皆是稱當機情，緣理未融。今總就教判權實，若約三藏、通、別三教是權，圓教為實。又諸教權實，未融為權；既融，開權顯實為實。今法華是一圓故為實，又開權故為實。若就圓教為語，照前三教三十麁為權，照十妙為實。若就開權圓融為語，決於三十麁皆成妙，但稱為實，是故稱妙。若取悟理者，理即非權非實，不見一法，空拳誑小兒。說權說實，是則為麁，理則非權非實，是故為妙也。

One should therefore know that, though every teaching alike possesses the provisional and the real, the provisional and the real are not the same across them — some are entirely real, some are entirely provisional, some combine the provisional and the real together — all of this according with the capacity and disposition met at that moment, the condition and principle not yet fused together. Here, discussed altogether in terms of the teachings, judging the provisional and the real: discussed in terms of the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct teachings, these are the provisional; the perfect teaching is the real. Again, every teaching's own provisional and real, not yet fused together, are the provisional; once fused, opening the provisional to reveal the real is the real. The Lotus Sūtra, being the one perfect teaching itself, is hence the real; and, further, since it opens the provisional, it is hence the real. Discussed in terms of the perfect teaching's own standpoint: illuminating the previous three teachings' own thirty coarse aspects is the provisional; illuminating the ten wondrous aspects is the real. Discussed in terms of opening the provisional and perfect fusion: once the thirty coarse aspects are all resolved as having become wondrous, they are simply named the real — hence, they are named wondrous. If one takes up the awakening to principle itself: principle is neither provisional nor real, and sees not even a single dharma — an empty fist deceiving a small child. Speaking of the provisional, speaking of the real — this, then, remains coarse; principle itself is neither provisional nor real — hence, this alone is the wondrous.

Reading note

This section performs, one final time and now across the entire sweep of the treatise's own Five Flavors scheme, the same "count the combinations" method already used for the twelve divisions of scripture and the six spiritual powers' own merit-counts — provisional-and-real ratios climbing from one-provisional-one-real in the milk teaching up through fifty-six-provisional-eight-real in the vaipulya and *Nirvāṇa* teachings — but the real payoff is the warning embedded at the very center: the *Lotus Sūtra*'s own "twelve provisional, four real" carries the identical name and number as the Tripiṭaka teaching's, "and yet the sense involved differs as widely as heaven from earth," since the Tripiṭaka's provisional is provisional straight through while the *Lotus*'s own provisional has already been opened to reveal the real beneath it. The flower-and-seed-pod image (蓮, the lotus itself, hidden inside the flower whose real purpose is only to protect and eventually reveal it) gives this treatise's own title-text a final, literal payoff — the sūtra of the "wondrous dharma" is, quite directly, the sūtra of the lotus flower opening to show what it always contained. This closes the section resolving the entire ten-wondrous-aspects doctrine into the provisional and the real, and, with it, the whole account of the trace gate's own ten wondrous aspects (迹門十妙) from every angle the treatise intends to take on it. The fascicle continues by opening an entirely new major division: the origin gate (本門), beginning with the sixfold distinction between trace and origin (本迹) themselves.

Explaining Trace and Origin 釋本迹

Chinese Source

○第二、約本明十妙者，為二：先、釋本迹。二、明十妙。

釋本迹為六：本者理本，即是實相一究竟道；迹者，除諸法實相，其餘種種皆名為迹。又理之與事，皆名為本；說理說事，皆名教迹也。又理事之教，皆名為本；稟教修行，名為迹，如人依處，則有行跡，尋跡得處也。又行能證體，體為本；依體起用，用為迹。又實得體用，名為本；權施體用，名為迹。又今日所顯者為本；先來已說者為迹。約此六義，以明本迹也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the ten wondrous aspects in terms of the origin: this is treated under two headings — first, explaining trace and origin; second, clarifying the ten wondrous aspects themselves.

Explaining trace and origin is treated under six senses. The origin is principle as origin — precisely the one ultimate path of the real mark itself; the trace is whatever, apart from the real mark of every dharma, is otherwise of every kind — all of this is named the trace. Again, principle together with phenomena are both alike named the origin; speaking of principle, speaking of phenomena, are both alike named the teaching's own trace. Again, the teaching of principle and phenomena is alike named the origin; receiving the teaching and cultivating practice is named the trace — as when a person, depending on some place, thereby leaves footprints of their own passage, and, tracing these footprints, one arrives at that same place. Again, practice is able to realize the essence — the essence is the origin; depending on the essence, function arises — function is the trace. Again, genuinely attaining essence and function is named the origin; provisionally bestowing essence and function is named the trace. Again, what is revealed today is the origin; what has already been spoken of before is the trace. In terms of these same six senses, trace and origin are thereby clarified.

一、約理事明本迹者，從無住本立一切法，無住之理，即是本時實相真諦也；一切法，即是本時森羅，俗諦也。由實相真本垂於俗迹，尋於俗迹即顯真本，本迹雖殊，不思議一也。故文云：「觀一切法空如實相，但以因緣有，從顛倒生(云云)。」

二、理教明本迹者，即是本時所照二諦，俱不可說，故皆名本也。

昔佛方便說之，即是二諦之教，教名為迹。若無二諦之本，則無二種之教，若無教迹，豈顯諦本？本迹雖殊，不思議一。文云：「是法不可示，言辭相寂滅，以方便力故，為五比丘說。」

First, clarifying trace and origin in terms of principle and phenomena: from the rootless root, every dharma is established — the principle of non-abiding is precisely the original real mark, the real truth; every dharma is precisely the original manifold array, the conventional truth. Through the real mark's own true origin descending into the conventional trace, tracing this same conventional trace, the true origin is thereby revealed. Trace and origin, though differing, are inconceivably one and the same. Hence the text says: "Contemplating that every dharma is empty, just as the real mark itself, existing only through causes and conditions, arising from inverted views, and so on."

Second, clarifying trace and origin in terms of principle and teaching: this refers precisely to the two truths as illuminated in the original moment — both alike beyond all speech, and hence both alike named the origin.

Formerly, the Buddha, through skillful means, spoke of them — this is precisely the teaching of the two truths, and this teaching is named the trace. Without the origin of the two truths, there would be no teaching of these two kinds at all; and, without the teaching's own trace, how could the truth's own origin possibly be revealed? Trace and origin, though differing, are inconceivably one and the same. The text says: "This dharma cannot be pointed to directly, the mark of every word and phrase itself quiescent cessation — yet, through the power of skillful means, it was taught for the sake of the five bhikṣus."

三、約教行為本迹者，最初稟昔佛之教以為本，則有修因致果之行。由教詮理而得起行，由行會教而得顯理；本迹雖殊，不思議一也。文云：「諸法從本來，常自寂滅相，佛子行道已，來世得作佛(云云)。」

四、約體用明本迹者，由昔最初修行契理，證於法身為本，初得法身本故，即體起應身之用，由於應身得顯法身；本迹雖殊，不思議一。文云：「吾從成佛已來，甚大久遠若斯，但以方便教化眾生，作如此說。」

Third, clarifying trace and origin in terms of teaching and practice: at the very outset, receiving a former buddha's own teaching as the origin, there is thereby the practice of cultivating the cause and bringing about the fruit. Through the teaching expressing principle, practice is thereby brought forth; through practice according with the teaching, principle is thereby revealed. Trace and origin, though differing, are inconceivably one and the same. The text says: "All dharmas, from their very origin, are forever, of themselves, marked by quiescent cessation; once the buddha's own children have finished walking the path, in a future age they shall attain buddhahood, and so on."

Fourth, clarifying trace and origin in terms of essence and function: through cultivating practice in former times, according with principle, realizing the Dharma-body as the origin — having first attained this original Dharma-body, the very function of the response-body arises from this same essence; through the response-body, the Dharma-body comes to be revealed. Trace and origin, though differing, are inconceivably one and the same. The text says: "Since my own attainment of buddhahood, an extremely vast span of time such as this has already passed; yet, through the power of skillful means, I teach and transform sentient beings, and speak in this way."

五、約實權明本迹者，實者，最初久遠實得法、應二身，皆名為本；中間數數唱生、唱滅，種種權施法、應二身，故名為迹。非初得法、應之本，則無中間法、應之迹。由迹顯本，本迹雖殊，不思議一也。文云：「是我方便，諸佛亦然。」

六、約今已論本迹者，前來諸教已說事理，乃至權實者，皆是迹也。今經所說久遠事理，乃至權實者，皆名為本。非今所明久遠之本，無以垂於已說之迹；非已說迹，豈顯今本？本迹雖殊，不思議一也。文云：「諸佛法久後，要當說真實。」

Fifth, clarifying trace and origin in terms of the real and the provisional: the real refers to the two bodies, Dharma-body and response-body, genuinely attained at the very outset, in the vast distant past — these are both alike named the origin; the provisional refers to the two bodies, Dharma-body and response-body, provisionally bestowed again and again in the interval, repeatedly proclaiming birth and proclaiming extinction, in every kind of way — hence, these are named the trace. Without the origin of the Dharma-body and response-body first attained, there would be no trace of the Dharma-body and response-body in the interval. Through the trace, the origin is revealed. Trace and origin, though differing, are inconceivably one and the same. The text says: "This is my own skillful means, and every buddha does likewise."

Sixth, clarifying trace and origin in terms of the present and what came before: every prior teaching's own already-spoken principle and phenomena, down through the provisional and the real, are, without exception, the trace; the present sūtra's own teaching of the vast, distant principle and phenomena, down through the provisional and the real, is, without exception, named the origin. Without the vast, distant origin clarified in the present, there would be nothing to descend into the already-spoken trace; without the already-spoken trace, how could the present origin possibly be revealed? Trace and origin, though differing, are inconceivably one and the same. The text says: "The buddhas' own dharma, after a long span of time, must at last speak the truth."

若約已今論本迹者，指已為迹，攝得釋迦寂滅道場已來十麁十妙，悉名為迹。指今為本，總遠攝最初本時諸麁諸妙，皆名為本。

若約權實明本迹者，指權為迹，別攝得中間種種異名佛十麁十妙，皆名為權。指實為本，攝得最初十麁十妙，悉名為實。

若約體用明本迹者，指用為迹，攝得最初感應、神通、說法、眷屬、利益等五妙。指體為本，攝得最初三法妙也。

As for discussing trace and origin in terms of the present and what came before: pointing to what came before as the trace gathers in everything from Śākyamuni's own place of quiescent extinction onward — the ten coarse and ten wondrous aspects already treated — all of this, without exception, named the trace. Pointing to the present as the origin gathers together, at the greatest distance, every coarse and every wondrous aspect belonging to the very original moment itself — all of this, without exception, named the origin.

As for discussing trace and origin in terms of the provisional and the real: pointing to the provisional as the trace, in the particular sense, gathers in the various differently named buddhas' own ten coarse and ten wondrous aspects present in the interval — all of this, without exception, named the provisional. Pointing to the real as the origin gathers in the ten coarse and ten wondrous aspects of the very original moment itself — all of this, without exception, named the real.

As for discussing trace and origin in terms of essence and function: pointing to function as the trace gathers in the original moment's own five wondrous aspects — sympathetic response, spiritual powers, teaching the Dharma, the retinue, and merit and benefit. Pointing to essence as the origin gathers in the original moment's own wondrous of the three tracks.

若約教行為本迹者，指行為迹，攝得最初行妙、位妙；指教為本，攝得最初本時智妙。

若理教為本迹者，指理為本，攝得本初之境妙；指教為迹，攝得本時之師教妙，兼得本師十妙。

若理事為本迹者，指事為迹，攝得本時諸麁境；指理為本，攝得本時諸妙境。最初之本為本，但本而非迹；最後已說，但迹而非本；中間亦迹亦本。若無本時之本，不能垂得中間、最後之迹；若無已說之迹，不能顯得今說之本。本迹雖殊，不思議一也。

As for discussing trace and origin in terms of teaching and practice: pointing to practice as the trace gathers in the original moment's own wondrous of practice and wondrous of stage; pointing to teaching as the origin gathers in the original moment's own wondrous of wisdom.

As for discussing trace and origin in terms of principle and teaching: pointing to principle as the origin gathers in the original moment's own wondrous of the object; pointing to teaching as the trace gathers in the original moment's own wondrous of the teacher's teaching, and, together with it, the original teacher's own ten wondrous aspects.

As for discussing trace and origin in terms of principle and phenomena: pointing to phenomena as the trace gathers in the original moment's own every coarse object; pointing to principle as the origin gathers in the original moment's own every wondrous object. The very original moment's own origin is origin alone, never trace; what was already spoken of at the very end is trace alone, never origin; what lies in the interval is both trace and origin at once. Without the origin belonging to the original moment, there would be nothing to descend into the trace of the interval and the very end; without the already-spoken trace, the origin spoken of in the present could never be revealed. Trace and origin, though differing, are inconceivably one and the same.

Reading note

This opens the treatise's second great division — the origin gate (本門), standing alongside the trace gate (迹門) whose own ten wondrous aspects have just closed — with a sixfold analytical apparatus that reuses, at a new level, nearly every polarity this treatise has built up over six fascicles: principle/phenomena, principle/teaching, teaching/practice, essence/function, real/provisional, and finally the present/what-came-before. Each pairing resolves the same way: trace and origin are "inconceivably one and the same," neither reducible to the other, the trace never merely arbitrary and the origin never accessible except through it — a structural echo of the wish-fulfilling jewel simile from the wondrous of the three tracks (Fascicle 5), where light and substance were "neither one with the jewel itself nor different from it." The sixth sense's own present/already-spoken distinction is the pivot the rest of this new division will build on: everything covered so far in this treatise — the entire trace gate's own ten wondrous aspects — is now reclassified, in a single stroke, as itself only ever a trace, with a vaster, more original story about the Buddha's own attainment yet to be told.

釋此十妙，又為十重：一、略釋十意。二、生起次第。三、明本迹開合。四、引文證成。五、廣解。六、三世料簡。七、論麁妙。八、結成權實。九、利益。十、觀心。

Second, clarifying the origin's own ten wondrous aspects: first, the wondrous of the original cause; second, the wondrous of the original fruit; third, the wondrous of the original land; fourth, the wondrous of the original sympathetic response; fifth, the wondrous of the original spiritual powers; sixth, the wondrous of the original teaching of the Dharma; seventh, the wondrous of the original retinue; eighth, the wondrous of the original nirvāṇa; ninth, the wondrous of the original lifespan; tenth, the wondrous of the original merit and benefit.

Briefly Explaining the Ten Senses 略釋十意

Chinese Source

○一、略釋者，本因妙者，本初發菩提心，行菩薩道所修因也。若十六王子在大通佛時弘經結緣，皆是中間所作，非本因也。若娑婆為墨，東行千界方下一點，點不點等，盡抹為塵，一塵一劫，復過於是百千萬億那由他劫，彌勒補處，以出假種智直數世界，尚不能知，況數其塵，寧當得盡？特是如來巧喻，顯其長遠之相；況以世智，巧歷算數耶！文云：「我以佛眼觀彼久遠，猶若今也。」唯佛能知如此久遠，皆是迹因，非本因也。若留中間之因，於後難信，是故《法華》拂迹除疑，權而非實，我本行菩薩道時，不在中間，過是已前所行道者，名之為本，即是本因妙也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, briefly explaining: as for the wondrous of the original cause, this is the cause cultivated at the very original arousing of the bodhi-mind, practicing the bodhisattva path. As for the sixteen princes, at the time of Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Buddha, propagating the sūtra and forming bonds — all of this was accomplished within the interval, and is not the original cause. As for taking the sahā world's own worlds as ink, traveling a thousand worlds eastward before setting down a single dot; whether dotted or undotted, all ground down into dust, a single mote of dust for a single eon, and passing beyond even this number, hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of nayutas of eons — Maitreya himself, the very buddha-to-be, using the wisdom of the paths that emerges into the provisional, could not directly enumerate even the worlds themselves, let alone enumerate the motes of dust — how, then, could this possibly be exhausted? This is simply the Tathāgata's own skillful simile, revealing the mark of this vast, distant span — how much less could worldly wisdom, however skillfully calculating, ever reckon it! The text says: "I, with the buddha-eye, behold that vast, distant time as though it were the present moment itself." Only the Buddha is able to know a span so vast and distant as this — every one of these, however, remains the trace cause, not the original cause. If the cause belonging to the interval were left as it stands, it would, afterward, be difficult to believe — hence, the Lotus Sūtra wipes away the trace and removes this doubt: it is provisional, not real. "When I myself, in the past, practiced the bodhisattva path" — this does not refer to the interval, but to what was practiced even prior to it — this alone is named the origin, and this is precisely the wondrous of the original cause.

二、明本果妙者，本初所行圓妙之因，契得究竟常、樂、我、淨，乃是本果。不取寂滅道場，舍那成佛，為本果也。尚不取中間之果以為本果，況舍那始成云何是本？但取成佛已來，甚大久遠，初證之果，名本果妙也。

三、本國土者，本既成果，必有依國。今既迹在同居、或在三土，中間亦有四土，本佛亦應有土，復居何處？文云：「自從是來，我常在此娑婆世界說法教化。」按此文者，實非今日迹中娑婆，亦非中間權迹處所，乃是本之娑婆，即本土妙也。

Second, clarifying the wondrous of the original fruit: the original, perfect, wondrous cause first practiced, according with and attaining the ultimate eternity, bliss, self, and purity — this alone is the original fruit. This does not take the place of quiescent extinction, where Locana attains buddhahood, as the original fruit. If even the fruit belonging to the interval is not taken as the original fruit, how much less could Locana's own newly attained buddhahood possibly be the origin? Only the fruit first realized, since the vastly distant attainment of buddhahood, is taken here and named the wondrous of the original fruit.

Third, as for the original land: since the origin has already accomplished its fruit, there must necessarily be some land depended upon. Now, since the trace lies within the realm of common dwelling, or within one of the three lands, and the interval likewise possesses four lands, the original buddha, too, ought to possess his own land — but where, then, does he dwell? The text says: "From that time onward, I have constantly abided within this very sahā world, teaching the Dharma and transforming beings." Examining this text: this is, in truth, neither the present-day sahā world of the trace, nor any place belonging to the provisional trace of the interval, but is precisely the sahā world of the origin itself — this is precisely the wondrous of the original land.

四、本感應者，既已成果，即有本時所證二十五三昧，慈悲誓願，機感相關，能即寂而照，故言本感應也。

五、本神通者，亦是昔時所得無記化化禪，與本因時諸慈悲合，施化所作神通，駭動最初可度眾生，故言本神通也。

六、本說法者，即是往昔，初坐道場，始成正覺，初轉法輪，四辯所說之法，名本說法也。

Fourth, as for the original sympathetic response: since the fruit has already been accomplished, there is accordingly the twenty-five samādhis realized in the original moment, and the vow of loving-kindness and compassion — capacity and response hinging upon one another, quiescent and yet illuminating at once — hence, this is spoken of as the original sympathetic response.

Fifth, as for the original spiritual powers: this, too, refers to the dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation attained in former times, joined with every form of loving-kindness and compassion present at the time of the original cause, the spiritual power thereby exercised in transforming beings, stirring and moving the very first sentient beings able to be delivered — hence, this is spoken of as the original spiritual powers.

Sixth, as for the original teaching of the Dharma: this refers precisely to the dharma taught, through the four unimpeded eloquences, at the very outset, first sitting at the place of enlightenment, first accomplishing correct enlightenment, first turning the wheel of the Dharma — this is named the original teaching of the Dharma.

七、本眷屬者，本時說法，所被之人也。如下方住者，彌勒不識，即本之眷屬也。

八、本涅槃者，本時所證斷德涅槃，亦是本時應處同居、方便二土，有緣既度，唱言入滅，即本涅槃也。

九、本壽命者，既唱入滅，則有長短、遠近壽命也。

十、本利益者，本業、願、通、應等眷屬，八番、十番饒益者是也。

Seventh, as for the original retinue: this refers to the persons covered by the teaching given in the original moment. As with those dwelling below, whom even Maitreya himself does not recognize — these are precisely the original retinue.

Eighth, as for the original nirvāṇa: this refers to the nirvāṇa of the virtue of severance realized in the original moment — and, likewise, in the original moment, responding within the realm of common dwelling and the expedient-means land, those with whom a bond existed having already been delivered, proclaiming entry into extinction — this is precisely the original nirvāṇa.

Ninth, as for the original lifespan: since entry into extinction is proclaimed, there is accordingly a lifespan, long or short, distant or near.

Tenth, as for the original benefit: this refers to the original retinue born through karma, vow, spiritual power, response, and the rest, richly benefited across the eight and ten rounds — this is precisely what is meant.

Their Arising in Sequence 生起次第

Chinese Source

○二、生起者，此十種義，赴緣直說，散在經文，今欲編次，故須生起。所以本因居初者，必由因而致果。果成，故有國。極果居國，即有照機。機動則施化，施化則有神通。神通竟，次為說法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, as for their arising in sequence: these ten senses, taught directly according to the condition met, are scattered throughout the sūtra's own text — here, wishing to arrange them in order, their arising in sequence must therefore be explained. The reason the original cause comes first is that the fruit must necessarily come about through the cause. Once the fruit is accomplished, there is accordingly a land. The ultimate fruit dwelling within this land, there is accordingly the illumination of capacity. Once capacity stirs, transformation is thereupon set forth; transformation set forth, there is accordingly spiritual power. Once spiritual power is complete, teaching the Dharma comes next.

What teaching the Dharma covers accordingly forms the retinue. The retinue already delivered, the bond exhausted, nirvāṇa follows. Because of nirvāṇa, the lifespan's own length, whether long or short, is accordingly discussed, together with the benefit accomplished through this same lifespan, long or short, down through the benefit belonging to the correct and semblance ages after the Buddha's own extinction. The sense involved is without measure, yet only ten items are given here, gathering it all in from beginning to end, thereby forming this same sequence once more.

Trace and Origin: Their Sameness and Difference 迹本同異

Chinese Source

○三、迹本同異者，迹中因開而果合，合習果、報果為三法妙也。

本中因合而果開，開習果，出報果，明本國土妙也。作此同異者，依於義便，互有去取。迹中委悉明境、智、行、位；本文語略，通束為因妙，得意知是開合耳。果妙者，即是迹中三軌妙也。感應、神通、說法、眷屬，名同上也。本開涅槃壽命妙者，久遠諸佛，如燈明、迦葉佛等，皆於法華即入涅槃。義推本佛，必是淨土淨機。

又往事已成，故開出涅槃等妙也。迹中無此二義者，釋迦雖於《法華》唱言涅槃，而未滅度，此事方在涅槃，故迹中不辨。利益同上也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, as for the sameness and difference between trace and origin: within the trace, the cause is opened out while the fruit is combined, combining the habituated fruit and the retributive fruit into the wondrous of the three tracks.

Within the origin, the cause is combined while the fruit is opened out, opening the habituated fruit and bringing forth the retributive fruit, clarifying the wondrous of the original land. This same sameness and difference is arranged according to whichever sense proves more convenient, taken up or set aside as fits the case. Within the trace, the object, wisdom, practice, and stage are clarified in full detail; the origin's own text is given more briefly, generally gathered together as the wondrous of the cause — grasping this same sense, one knows this to be a matter of opening and combining. The wondrous of the fruit is precisely the trace's own wondrous of the three tracks. Sympathetic response, spiritual powers, teaching the Dharma, and the retinue are named the same as above. As for the origin opening out the wondrous of nirvāṇa and lifespan: buddhas of the vastly distant past, such as Candrasūryapradīpa and Kāśyapa Buddha and the rest, every one of them, within the Lotus Sūtra itself, enters nirvāṇa. Reasoning from this to the original buddha, this must likewise be a pure land, a pure capacity.

Again, since the past matter has already been accomplished, the wondrous of nirvāṇa and the rest are accordingly opened forth. As for why the trace lacks these two senses: though Śākyamuni, within the Lotus Sūtra, proclaims nirvāṇa, he has not yet actually entered final extinction — this matter still lies within the realm of nirvāṇa itself, and hence is not distinguished within the trace. Benefit is the same as above.

Reading note

The origin gate's own ten wondrous aspects replace the trace gate's opening triad (境妙/智妙/行妙, the object/wisdom/practice a practitioner works through) with a single, compressed "wondrous of the original cause," and replace the trace gate's own culminating three tracks (三法妙) with an opened-out "wondrous of the original land" — a structural move Zhiyi explains directly here as opening what the trace gate combined, and combining what the trace gate opened, "according to whichever sense proves more convenient." Two items have no trace-gate counterpart at all — the wondrous of the original nirvāṇa and the wondrous of the original lifespan — for a reason that is itself doctrinally loaded: Śākyamuni, within the Lotus Sūtra's own trace-gate narrative, only proclaims his coming nirvāṇa without yet actually entering it, so the trace has no occasion to discuss nirvāṇa as an accomplished fact, while the origin gate's own vastly more ancient buddha-attainment has already run its full course, nirvāṇa included, many times over. The "arising in sequence" section's own cause-to-benefit chain is the identical logical skeleton already used to justify the trace gate's ten-item order back in Fascicle 2, now simply run again for the origin.

Citing Texts as Proof 引文證

Fourth, as for citing texts as proof: this does not reach out to draw upon some other sūtra, nor does it generally draw upon anything beyond this same division — it simply proves these same ten senses directly from within the origin gate itself. Yet, as for the former buddhas' own Lotus Sūtra, with its verses as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, and the present buddha's own eight years of teaching at Vulture Peak — how could the matters within the Sanskrit source text possibly be exhausted? In this remote, outlying land of China, only the broad outline has been heard; people, seeing these seven fascicles, suppose this to be a minor sūtra — yet the Sanskrit text is vast and expansive, and what does it not distinguish? Here, within the space of only a few pages, the ten proof-texts stand plainly before us.

The text says: "The lifespan I accomplished at the time I myself, in the past, practiced the bodhisattva path, remains, even now, not yet exhausted" — this is precisely the origin's own wondrous of the cause of practice. The text says: "Since my own genuine attainment of buddhahood, immeasurable, boundless koṭis of nayutas of eons have passed." And again: "Since my own genuine attainment of buddhahood, an extremely vast span such as this has already passed; yet, through the power of skillful means, I teach and transform sentient beings, and speak in this way" — this is precisely the wondrous of the original fruit. The text says: "Since I myself attained the threefold bodhi within the sahā world, I have taught, transformed, shown, and guided these very bodhisattvas." And again: "From that time onward, I have constantly abided within this very sahā world, teaching the Dharma and transforming beings, and, in other places besides, guiding and benefiting sentient beings as well" — this same land is no longer the present-day sahā world at all — this is precisely the wondrous of the original land.

文云：「若有眾生來至我所，我以佛眼觀其信等諸根利鈍。」此即本時照機之智，是感應妙也。文云：「如來祕密神通之力。」又中間文云：「或示己身、或示他身；或示己事、或示他事。」即是垂形十界，作種種像，驗本亦然，是本神通妙也。文云：「是諸菩薩悉是我所化，令發大道心，今皆住不退，修學我道法。」又中間，或說己事、或說他事，驗本亦然，即本說法妙。

The text says: "If there are sentient beings who come to where I am, I, with the buddha-eye, observe whether their faculties of faith and the rest are sharp or dull" — this is precisely the original moment's own wisdom illuminating capacity, the wondrous of sympathetic response. The text says: "The Tathāgata's own secret power of spiritual transformation." And, further, within the interval, the text says: "Sometimes showing his own body, sometimes showing another's body; sometimes showing his own affairs, sometimes showing another's affairs" — this is precisely manifesting a form within each of the ten dharma-realms, taking on every kind of image; testing this same pattern against the origin, it, too, is exactly the same — this is the wondrous of the original spiritual powers. The text says: "Every one of these bodhisattvas has been transformed by me, causing them to give rise to the mind of the great path; every one of them now abides in non-retrogression, cultivating and studying my own path and dharma." And, further, within the interval, sometimes speaking of his own affairs, sometimes speaking of another's affairs; testing this same pattern against the origin, it, too, is exactly the same — this is precisely the wondrous of the original teaching of the Dharma.

文云：「此諸菩薩身皆金色，下方空中住，此等是我子，我從久遠來，教化是等眾。」即本眷屬妙也。文云：「又復言其入於涅槃，如是皆以方便分別。」又云：「今非實滅，而便唱言當取滅度。」往緣既訖而唱入滅，中間既唱涅槃，例本亦有涅槃，即本涅槃妙。

The text says: "These bodhisattvas, their bodies every one golden in color, dwelling in the empty sky below — these are my own children; I, since a vastly distant time, have taught and transformed this same assembly" — this is precisely the wondrous of the original retinue. The text says: "And, further, speaking of his entry into nirvāṇa — every one of these is spoken through skillful means, distinguished accordingly." And again: "Now, this is not truly extinction, and yet he thereupon proclaims that he shall attain extinction." The past bond already concluded, entry into extinction is proclaimed; since the interval, too, proclaims nirvāṇa, by this same pattern, the origin, too, must possess its own nirvāṇa — this is precisely the wondrous of the original nirvāṇa.

文云：「處處自說名字不同，年紀大小。」年即壽命，大小即長短常無常也。中間既爾，本壽亦然，即本壽命妙也。文云：「又以方便說微妙法，能令眾生發歡喜心。」即中間利益。又云：「聞佛說壽命劫數長遠如是，無量無邊阿僧祇眾生得大饒益。」即迹中之益。迹與中間既爾，例本亦然，即是本利益妙也。十據在經非人造也。

The text says: "Everywhere, speaking of himself under differing names, his own age either great or small" — age here is precisely the lifespan; great or small is precisely long or short, eternal or impermanent. Since the interval is exactly this way, the original lifespan, too, is exactly this way — this is precisely the wondrous of the original lifespan. The text says: "And, further, through skillful means, teaching the subtle and wondrous dharma, able to cause sentient beings to give rise to a joyful mind" — this is precisely the benefit within the interval. And again: "Hearing the Buddha teach that the number of eons of his own lifespan is vast and distant such as this, sentient beings immeasurable and boundless, incalculable in number, obtained great and abundant benefit" — this is precisely the benefit within the trace. Since the trace and the interval are already exactly this way, by this same pattern, the origin, too, must be exactly this way — this is precisely the wondrous of the original benefit. These ten proofs rest within the sūtra's own text itself, and are not any person's own fabrication.

Reading note

The ten citations that follow are deliberately drawn only "from within the origin gate itself" (the "Life Span of the Tathāgata" chapter and its immediate context), each one matched to exactly one of the ten origin-gate wondrous aspects in the same order already given in the brief explanation — a discipline this treatise has followed for the trace gate's own ten wondrous aspects as well, insisting that the doctrinal architecture is discovered in the sūtra's own text, "not any person's own fabrication."

The Broad Explanation 廣解

Chinese Source

○五、廣釋者，夫非本無以垂迹，若能解迹則亦知本，為未解者更重分別。但本極法身，微妙深遠，佛若不說，彌勒尚闇，何況下地！何況凡夫！雖然父母之年不可不知，如來功德何容不識？今略依經旨，髣髴推尋。

Fifth, the broad explanation: were there no origin, there would be nothing for the trace to descend from; if one is able to understand the trace, one thereby also knows the origin — for those who have not yet understood it, this is distinguished once more, in greater detail. Yet the origin, the very ultimate Dharma-body, is subtle, wondrous, profound, and far-reaching — had the Buddha himself not spoken of it, even Maitreya would remain in the dark, let alone those of the lower stages, let alone ordinary beings! And yet, though the very age of one's own parents cannot go unknown, how could the Tathāgata's own merit possibly be left unrecognized? Here, following the sūtra's own intent in broad outline, this is traced out as best as can be approximated.

The Wondrous of the Original Cause 本因妙

Chinese Source

本因妙者，經言：「我本行菩薩道時所成壽命」者，慧命，即本時智妙也。我本行者，行是進趣，即本行妙也。菩薩道時者，菩薩是因人，復顯位妙也。一句文證成三妙，三妙即本時因妙，非迹因也。迹因多種，或言昔為陶師，值先釋迦佛，三事供養，藉草然燈、石蜜漿，發口得記，父母名字，弟子侍人，皆如先佛，即是初阿僧祇發心。既不明斷惑，知是三藏行因之相也。或言昔為摩納，值然燈佛，五華奉散，布髮掩泥，躍身虛空，得無生忍，佛與受記，號釋迦文。《大品》亦云：「華嚴城內得記。」義與此同。並云斷惑，故知通佛行因之相也。或言昔為寶海梵志，刪提嵐國，寶藏佛所，行大精進，十方佛送華供養。既為寶藏佛父，又是彌陀之師，稱其功德不可思議，故知是別圓行因之相。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the wondrous of the original cause: the sūtra says, "The lifespan I accomplished at the time I myself, in the past, practiced the bodhisattva path" — the wisdom-life here is precisely the original moment's own wondrous of wisdom. "I myself, in the past, practiced" — practice is advancing forward, and this is precisely the original wondrous of practice. "At the time of the bodhisattva path" — the bodhisattva is a person of the cause, and this, again, reveals the wondrous of stage. This single phrase of text thus proves all three wondrous aspects at once — these three wondrous aspects are precisely the original moment's own wondrous of the cause, not the trace cause. The trace cause is of many kinds: some say that, in a former life, he was a potter, and, encountering an earlier Śākyamuni Buddha, made three kinds of offering — a bed of grass, a burning lamp, and stone-honey syrup — and, opening his mouth, received the prediction; his parents' own names, and his disciples and attendants, were all exactly the same as the earlier buddha's own — this is precisely the very first arousing of the mind at the start of the first incalculable eon. Since this account does not clarify any severing of affliction, one knows this to be the mark of the Tripiṭaka teaching's own cause of practice. Some say that, in a former life, he was a young brahmin student, and, encountering Dīpaṃkara Buddha, scattered five flowers in offering, spread his own hair over the mud, and leapt into the empty sky, attaining the forbearance of no-generation, and the Buddha bestowed upon him the prediction, naming him Śākyamuni. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra likewise says: "Received the prediction within the city of the Avataṃsaka" — the sense is the same as this. Both accounts speak of severing affliction — hence, one knows this to be the mark of the shared teaching's own cause of practice. Some say that, in a former life, he was the brahmin Samudrareṇu, in the land of Saṃdilya, at the place of the buddha Ratnagarbha, practicing with great diligence, and buddhas throughout the ten directions sent flowers as an offering to him. He was already the father of the buddha Ratnagarbha, and, moreover, was the teacher of Amitābha — his merit is spoken of as inconceivable — hence, one knows this to be the mark of the distinct and perfect teachings' own cause of practice.

以三義故，知此諸因悉是迹因：一、近故。二、淺深不同故。三、被拂故。今世已前，本來已後，中間行行，悉是方便，故知是迹因也。若執迹因為本因者，斯不知迹，亦不識本。如不識天月，但觀池月，若光、若桂、若輪，準下知上。光譬智妙，桂譬行妙，輪譬位妙。若識迹中三妙，拂迹顯本，即知本地因妙，如撥影指天，云何臨盆而不仰漢？嗚呼！聾騃，若為論道耶？若得斯意，迹本非本，本迹非迹，本迹雖殊，不思議一也。

For three reasons, one knows that every one of these causes is entirely the trace cause: first, because they are near at hand; second, because they differ in shallowness and depth; third, because they are wiped away. Everything before the present age, and everything after the original moment itself, every practice carried out in the interval, is, without exception, an expedient means — hence, one knows this to be the trace cause. If one clings to the trace cause as though it were the original cause, this is failing to know the trace, and likewise failing to recognize the origin. It is like failing to recognize the moon in the sky itself, and looking only at the moon reflected in a pond — whether its light, its cassia tree, or its disk — inferring the moon above from what is seen below. The light is a simile for the wondrous of wisdom; the cassia tree is a simile for the wondrous of practice; the disk is a simile for the wondrous of stage. If one recognizes the trace's own three wondrous aspects, wiping away the trace to reveal the origin, one thereby knows the original ground's own wondrous of the cause — like brushing aside a reflection to point toward the sky itself — how, then, could one gaze down into a basin of water and fail to look up at the Milky Way above? Alas — how could the deaf and the dull-witted possibly discuss the path in this way? If one grasps this same sense, the trace-as-origin is not the origin, and the origin-as-trace is not the trace; trace and origin, though differing, are inconceivably one and the same.

問：
經稱本行菩薩道時者，應是初住得真道時也。中間，應是諸地增道損生。今之寂場，應是妙覺。妙覺顯本，應指昔初住。此一途為允？

答：
文義不可。文云：「盡行諸佛所有道法」，又云：「具足行諸道」，悉具足因，乃是本因。初住，不得稱悉具，故非所指本因也。又中間之果悉拂是權，況今寂場之果，何得為實？又中間之果尚被拂者，中間之因，寧實因也？故爾問，非也。

Question: The sūtra speaks of "the time I myself, in the past, practiced the bodhisattva path" — this ought to be the very moment of attaining the real path at the initial abiding. The interval ought to be the various grounds' own advancing of the path and diminishing of birth. The present place of quiescent extinction ought to be wondrous enlightenment. Wondrous enlightenment revealing the origin ought, then, to point back to that same former initial abiding. Is this one single account correct?

Answer: Neither the text nor its sense permits this. The text says: "Fully practicing every path and dharma possessed by every buddha," and again: "Practicing every path in its entirety" — being entirely complete with the cause in every respect is precisely the original cause. The initial abiding cannot be called "entirely complete" — hence, it is not what is pointed to as the original cause. Again, the fruit belonging to the interval is, every bit of it, wiped away as provisional — how much more, then, could the present place of quiescent extinction's own fruit possibly be the real? And if even the fruit belonging to the interval is thus wiped away, how could the cause belonging to the interval possibly be the real cause? Hence, this question, as posed, is mistaken.

The Wondrous of the Original Fruit 本果妙

Chinese Source

二、明本果妙者，經言：「我成佛已來，甚大久遠。」我者，即真性軌。佛者，覺義，即觀照軌。已來者，乘如實道，來成正覺，即是起應資成軌也。如此三軌，成來已久，即本果妙也。本果圓滿，久在於昔，非今迹成，迹成又非一種：或言道樹草座、三十四心、見思俱斷、朗然大悟，覺知世間出世間一切諸法，名之為佛。唯有此佛、無十方佛，三世佛者悉是他佛，非我分身，此即三藏佛果相也。或言道樹天衣為座，以一念相應慧斷餘殘習氣而得成佛。《大品》中說：「共般若時，十方有千佛現，問難人皆字須菩提、釋提桓因等。」亦是他佛，非我分身，此即通佛果成相也。

Second, clarifying the wondrous of the original fruit: the sūtra says, "Since my own attainment of buddhahood, an extremely vast span of time has already passed." "I" here is precisely the track of real nature. "Buddha" carries the sense of awakening, precisely the track of contemplative illumination. "Since" refers to riding the very path of suchness itself, arriving at correct enlightenment — this is precisely the track of resource-and-accomplishment giving rise to response. These same three tracks, accomplished for so very long a time, are precisely the wondrous of the original fruit. The original fruit, perfect and complete, present already in the distant past, is not accomplished now, within the trace; and this accomplishment within the trace, too, is not of a single kind. Some say: seated upon a grass mat beneath the bodhi tree, in thirty-four mental moments, views and thought both severed at once, awakening brilliantly, knowing every dharma of the mundane and the world-transcending alike — this is named buddhahood. There is only this one buddha, and no buddhas throughout the ten directions; the buddhas of the three time periods are, every one, some other buddha, not my own divided body — this is precisely the Tripiṭaka teaching's own mark of the fruit of buddhahood. Some say: seated upon a heavenly robe beneath the bodhi tree, through the wisdom corresponding to a single thought-moment, severing every remaining habituated residue, thereby attaining buddhahood. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra, within its own "shared" portion, says: "At the very moment of the shared prajñā-teaching, a thousand buddhas appear throughout the ten directions, and every one of those who raise questions and objections is named Subhūti, Śakra, and the rest" — these, too, are some other buddha, not my own divided body — this is precisely the shared teaching's own mark of the fruit of buddhahood accomplished.

或言寂滅道場，七寶華為座，身稱華臺，千葉上一一菩薩，復有百億菩薩，如是則有千百億菩薩，十方放白毫及分身光，白毫入華臺菩薩頂，分身光入華葉菩薩頂，此名受法王職位，窮得諸佛法底而得成佛。華臺名報佛，華葉上名應佛，報應但是相關而已，不得相即，此是別佛果成相也。或言道場以虛空為座，一成一切成。毘盧遮那遍一切處，舍那、釋迦成亦遍一切處，三佛具足無有缺減，三佛相即，無有一異。法華八方，一一方各四百萬億那由他國土，安置釋迦，悉是遮那。《普賢觀》云：「釋迦牟尼名毘盧遮那。」此即圓佛果成相也。

Some say: at the very place of quiescent extinction, seated upon a lotus made of the seven treasures, his own body itself named the lotus platform, upon each and every one of its thousand petals a bodhisattva, and, further, a hundred koṭis of bodhisattvas upon each — thus, a thousand hundred koṭis of bodhisattvas altogether — the ten directions emitting light from the white curl and from every divided body, the white curl's own light entering the crown of the bodhisattva upon the lotus platform, and the divided bodies' own light entering the crown of the bodhisattva upon each petal — this is named receiving the very office of the Dharma-king, exhausting and attaining the very depths of every buddha's own dharma, and thereby attaining buddhahood. The lotus platform is named the reward-body buddha; what stands upon the petals is named the response-body buddha — reward and response are simply mutually related, and cannot be identified as one and the same — this is precisely the distinct teaching's own mark of the fruit of buddhahood accomplished. Some say: at the place of enlightenment, taking empty space itself as the seat, one accomplishment is every accomplishment at once. Vairocana pervades every place; Locana and Śākyamuni, too, in their own accomplishment, likewise pervade every place; the three buddhas are complete, with nothing lacking or diminished, the three buddhas mutually identical, with neither oneness nor difference between them. Throughout the Lotus Sūtra's own eight directions, each and every direction possessing four hundred myriad koṭis of nayutas of lands, Śākyamuni is placed within every one of them, and every one of these is entirely Vairocana himself. The Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra says: "Śākyamuni is named Vairocana" — this is precisely the perfect teaching's own mark of the fruit of buddhahood accomplished.

有三義故，知此諸果皆是迹果：一、今世始成故。二、淺深不同故。三、拂中間故。若是本果，何得今日始成？本果，一果一切果，何得前後差別不同？自從今世之前，本成之後，百千萬億行因得果，唱生唱滅，悉是中間，拂為方便，寂滅樹王，何得非迹？若執迹果為本果者，斯不知迹，亦不識本。從本垂迹，如月現水，拂迹顯本，如撥影指天。當撥始成之果皆迹果，指久成之果是本果也。如此解者，中間果疑，颯然皆盡，長遠之信，其義明焉。迹本非本，本迹非迹，迹本雖殊，不思議一也。

For three reasons, one knows that every one of these fruits is the trace fruit: first, because it is only newly accomplished within the present age; second, because these differ in shallowness and depth; third, because the interval is wiped away. If this were the original fruit, how could it possibly be only newly accomplished today? The original fruit is one fruit that is every fruit at once — how, then, could there be any difference of before and after, not all the same? From before the present age, and after the original accomplishment itself, hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of causes practiced and fruits attained, proclaiming birth and proclaiming extinction — all of this, without exception, lies within the interval, wiped away as an expedient means — how, then, could the very king of trees beneath which quiescent extinction was attained possibly fail to be a trace as well? If one clings to the trace fruit as though it were the original fruit, this is failing to know the trace, and likewise failing to recognize the origin. From the origin descending into the trace is like the moon appearing within the water; wiping away the trace to reveal the origin is like brushing aside a reflection to point toward the sky itself. One ought to brush aside every newly accomplished fruit as, every one, the trace fruit, and point instead to the fruit accomplished so very long ago as the original fruit. Understood in this way, every doubt concerning the fruit belonging to the interval is swept away entirely, and the very sense of this vastly distant faith becomes clear. The trace-as-origin is not the origin, and the origin-as-trace is not the trace; trace and origin, though differing, are inconceivably one and the same.

The Wondrous of the Original Land 本國土妙

Third, as for the wondrous of the original land: the sūtra says, "From that time onward, I have constantly abided within this very sahā world, teaching the Dharma and transforming beings, and, in other places besides, guiding and benefiting sentient beings as well." The sahā world here is precisely the original moment's own realm of common dwelling. The other places here are precisely the original moment's own three lands. This refers to the very land dwelt in by the real and response bodies at the original moment, not the land within the trace. Within the trace, land is likewise clarified in more than one way: some say that, taken together, these three thousand hundred koṭis of suns and moons make up the impure realm of common dwelling. Some say that, in the west, there is a land named Unsurpassed, whose every adornment resembles that of Sukhāvatī itself — the pure realm of common dwelling. Some say that the World of the Flower King, the ocean of the lotus-treasury, is precisely the realm of real reward. The realm of quiescent light, its principle penetrating through everywhere, is comparable to a mirror, or to a vessel; the various lands, each distinct, are comparable to an image, or to food served — separated by the power of one's own karma, what is perceived differs accordingly. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "My own buddha-land is pure, and yet you fail to see it" — this is precisely the difference in what sentient beings themselves perceive, and has nothing to do with the buddha-land itself.

若言今此三界皆是我有，諸土淨穢調伏攝受，皆佛所為。譬如百姓居土，土非其有；如父立舍，父去舍存。

如來亦爾，為眾生故而取佛土，化訖入滅，佛去土存，此乃佛土，不關眾生也。復次三變土田者，或是變同居之穢，令見同居之淨。

As for saying that these very three realms are all, in fact, my own possession, and that every land, pure or impure, subduing or gathering in, is entirely the Buddha's own doing — it is comparable to the common people dwelling upon the land, though the land itself is not their own possession; it is like a father building a house — the father departs, and the house remains standing nonetheless.

The Tathāgata is exactly the same: for the sake of sentient beings, he takes up a buddha-land, and, once the transformation is complete, enters extinction — the buddha departs, and the land remains, this land belonging to the buddha alone, having nothing to do with sentient beings themselves. Again, as for the threefold transformation of the land: this is sometimes transforming the impurity of the realm of common dwelling, causing it to be seen as the purity of the realm of common dwelling.

Sometimes it is seeing the purity of the expedient-means land with residue remaining — comparable to the "Life Span" chapter's own saying, "If there is one who believes and understands with a deeply sincere mind, he shall behold the Buddha constantly present upon Vulture Peak, together with great bodhisattvas and the assembly of śrāvakas" — this is precisely this same sense. Sometimes it is seeing the purity of the realm of real reward — comparable to seeing the sahā world's own land entirely made of dark blue beryl, filled purely with bodhisattvas — this is precisely this same sense. Sometimes it is seeing quiescent light itself, and the rest. It is the very power of the Lotus samādhi that causes what is seen to differ accordingly.

有三義故，得知諸土悉迹土也：一、今佛所栖故。二、前後修立故。三、中間所拂故。若是本土，非今佛所栖，今佛所栖，即迹土也。若是本土，一土一切土，不應前、後修立，深、淺不同；今土已前，本土已後，皆名中間，中間悉稱方便，況今之土，寧得非迹？從本垂迹，執迹為本者，此不知迹亦不識本。今拂迹指本，本時所栖四土者，是本國土妙也。迹本非本，本迹非迹，非迹非本，即不思議一也。

For three reasons, one is able to know that every one of these lands is entirely the trace land: first, because it is the land the present buddha himself dwells within; second, because it is cultivated and established before and after; third, because the interval is wiped away. If this were the original land, it would not be a land the present buddha himself dwells within — the land the present buddha himself dwells within is precisely the trace land. If this were the original land, one land would be every land at once, and there ought to be no cultivation and establishment before and after, no difference in depth and shallowness; everything before the present land, and everything after the original land itself, is named the interval, and the interval, entirely, is spoken of as an expedient means — how much more, then, could the present land possibly fail to be a trace? From the origin descending into the trace, clinging to the trace as though it were the origin — this is failing to know the trace, and likewise failing to recognize the origin. Here, wiping away the trace and pointing to the origin, the four lands dwelt within at the original moment are precisely the wondrous of the original land. The trace-as-origin is not the origin, and the origin-as-trace is not the trace; being neither trace nor origin, this is precisely inconceivably one and the same.

Reading note

Each of these three items applies the identical three-part argument — newness, internal variation, and the fact of being "wiped away" (拂) as provisional elsewhere in the sūtra — to strip away every candidate for "the origin" that turns out, on inspection, to still be a trace: the potter's and young brahmin's own jātaka stories, the various accounts of the bodhi tree awakening (grass mat, thirty-four mental moments; heavenly robe; the lotus-platform cosmology of the *Avataṃsaka*; Vairocana pervading every place), and even the sahā world's own multiple cosmological readings (impure common dwelling, Sukhāvatī-like purity, the lotus-treasury ocean, quiescent light itself) are all, without exception, sorted into "trace" once the same test is applied. The moon-in-water and reflection-versus-sky similes, already used for the trace/origin distinction in the wondrous of the original cause, recur here as this section's own controlling image: every account of "the Buddha's own attainment" offered by any prior teaching is a genuine reflection of something real, yet mistaking the reflection for the thing reflected is the single error this whole "broad explanation" exists to correct.

Fourth, as for the wondrous of the original sympathetic response: the sūtra says, "If there are sentient beings who come to where I am, I, with the buddha-eye, observe whether their faculties of faith and the rest are sharp or dull." Sentient beings coming to him is capacity hinging upon the Dharma-body. His own observing with the buddha-eye is loving-kindness and compassion going forth to respond. Faculties sharp or dull refers to the ten dharma-realms' own differing degrees of hidden or manifest, delight or aversion. Some say that the king of samādhis illuminates the ten dharma-realms' own capacity all at once, in a single moment — this is the perfect teaching's buddha illuminating the capacity within the ten dharma-realms' own land of quiescent light, the perfect sympathetic response.

At the place of quiescent extinction, beneath the tree, this fullness first begins, one-sidedly — hence, one knows this to be the trace. Some cultivate it earlier, some study it later, differing in depth and shallowness — hence, one knows this to be the provisional.

Since the interval onward, all of this is wiped away as expedient means — how, then, could this fail to be the trace? Issuing from the origin into the trace, how could one possibly cling to the trace as though it were the origin? Wiping away the trace to reveal the origin, one ought to set the trace aside and point to the origin instead — origin and trace, trace and origin, are inconceivably one and the same, and so on.

Again, sometimes the original response is coarse while the trace response is wondrous; sometimes the original response is wondrous while the trace response is coarse; both wondrous, both coarse — this, too, ought to be understood in the same way. Again, the original response is sometimes broad while the trace response is narrow; or the trace response is broad while the original response is narrow; both narrow, both broad — this, too, ought to be understood in the same way. This distinguishes trace from origin only in terms of the present versus what came before, not in terms of coarse versus wondrous, or broad versus narrow, and so on.

Showing his own body and his own affairs is the perfect teaching's own spiritual power; showing another's body and another's affairs is the one-sided spiritual power. "Secret" means wondrous — whether one-sided or perfect, both alike are wondrous. This refers to the spiritual power of the original moment, not the trace spiritual power. The trace's own spiritual power is of many kinds: some say that, relying on the liberations, the bases of mastery, and the totalities, and the fourteen kinds of transformation, one attains the six spiritual powers, surpassing the non-Buddhist outsiders and excelling the two vehicles — this is precisely the Tripiṭaka buddha's own spiritual power. Some say that, relying on the taintless wisdom that directly embodies dharmas, one attains the six spiritual powers, surpassing those who rely on the liberations — this is the shared teaching's buddha's own spiritual power. Some say that the previous six spiritual powers, gathered together, are made five, and, relying on the middle way, the taintless spiritual power is brought forth — this sixth is the distinct teaching's buddha's own spiritual power. Some say that the middle way's own dhyāna of unrecorded transformation upon transformation, complete with the six spiritual powers and every kind of transformation, never rising from the concentration of cessation, manifesting every deportment, speech and silence never obstructing one another, movement and quiescence sharing a single, non-dual principle — and, further, as in the present sūtra, the six auspicious signs, the transformation of the land, and the rest — every one of these is the perfect teaching's buddha's own spiritual power.

For three reasons, every one of these spiritual powers is reasoned to be trace, not origin: newly attained, cultivated near at hand, wiped away to remove doubt, and the rest, exactly as explained above. The fourfold set of propositions, too, is resolved exactly as above. Yet, from the origin descending into the trace, the trace is never the origin; wiping away the trace to reveal the origin, one ought to set the trace aside and point to the origin instead — origin and trace, trace and origin, are inconceivably one and the same.

These two items apply the identical trace/origin machinery already established, but their closing riders are worth noting on their own terms: the original sympathetic response is explicitly said sometimes to be coarse where the trace response is wondrous, and sometimes the reverse — the trace/origin distinction, unlike the earlier coarse/wondrous distinction that has structured this treatise since Fascicle 1, tracks only "the present versus what came before," not quality. A trace response can, in principle, outshine its own origin in vividness or breadth without that origin ceasing to be more fundamental — a genuinely different axis of evaluation than anything this treatise has used before.

Sixth, as for the wondrous of the original teaching of the Dharma: the sūtra says, "These, every one, have been transformed by me, causing them to give rise to the mind of the great path; every one of them now abides in non-retrogression." "Transformed by me" is precisely the teaching of the Dharma. "Causing them to give rise to the mind of the great path" distinguishes this from the teaching of the small vehicle. This refers to the teaching distinguished at the original moment, not the trace teaching. The trace's own teaching is of many kinds. Following the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, the first and last flavors alone are clarified as issuing from the cow; yet, reasoning by the same sense, the three intervening flavors, too, ought likewise to issue from the cow. Why is this? An ordinary calf, grazing on ordinary grass, is able only to yield milk — not grazing on the grass of forbearance, it does not yield the further four flavors. The well-trained calf grazing on the grass of forbearance is a striking elaboration of the milk-to-ghee Five Flavors scheme this treatise has used constantly since Fascicle 1: rather than five sequentially produced substances, all five flavors are "already present within the cow itself," waiting only on what the calf is fed — the Buddha's own single, complete dharma-mind, "listening for whichever capacity comes knocking," differing only in which capacity strikes it. The closing lines' own rapid inversion (what was already taught is the trace, what is taught now is the origin, and then, in the very next breath, "what was already the origin is now the trace") is this treatise's own warning against treating even the trace/origin distinction itself as a fixed hierarchy rather than a perspective that shifts with what is being asked.

Reading note

The governing image here — a life of wisdom that is itself "neither long nor short" yet capable of becoming either — extends the wondrous of the original cause's own logic (a cause identified with wisdom rather than any specific practice) into the register of time itself: length of life turns out to be no more fixed a property of buddhahood than any of the other categories this treatise has spent seven fascicles relativizing. This shortest of the ten items closes the detailed exposition (廣解) of all ten origin-gate wondrous aspects by doing exactly what the ninth item did for lifespan: taking a category already built up at length for the trace gate — the wondrous benefit of remaining fixed at one's own stage and of advancing into a further one, first established back in Fascicle 7's account of near benefit — and simply asking what the same category must look like at the level of the origin. The answer is characteristically economical: the bodhisattvas emerged from beneath, already dwelling within quiescent light rather than any lesser land, are themselves the proof that an original benefit exists, without requiring the same encyclopedic unpacking already given its trace counterpart back in Fascicle 6. This closes the detailed exposition (廣解) of the origin gate's own ten wondrous aspects in their entirety — the fifth of the tenfold analysis applied to the origin gate's ten-item list. The treatise continues with the sixth item of that same tenfold analysis, resolving doubts through the three time-periods (三世料簡).

Resolving Doubts Through the Three Time-Periods 約三世料簡

Chinese Source

○第六、約三世料簡者，文云：「如來自在神通之力，如來大勢威猛之力，如來師子奮迅之力。」即是三世益物之文。若過去最初所證權實之法，名為本也，從本證已，後方便化他。開三顯一、發迹顯本者，還指最初為本；中間示現、發迹顯本，亦指最初為本；今日發迹顯本，亦指最初為本；未來發本顯迹，亦指最初為本。三世乃殊，毘盧遮那一本不異，如百千枝葉，同趣一根(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, resolving doubts by taking up the three time-periods: the text says, "the Tathāgata's own power of sovereign spiritual transformation, the Tathāgata's own power of great, awe-inspiring might, the Tathāgata's own power of the lion's swift-leaping" — this is precisely the text on benefiting beings across the three time-periods. If the provisional and real dharma realized at the very first, in the past, is named the origin, then, once realization has been attained from this origin, expedient transformation of others follows afterward. Whether it is opening the three to reveal the one and revealing the trace to disclose the origin, the middle period's own manifestation revealing the trace to disclose the origin, the present day's own revealing the trace to disclose the origin, or the future's own disclosing the origin to reveal the trace — in every case, it points back to this very first moment as the origin. Though the three time-periods differ, Vairocana's single origin is no different across them, like a hundred thousand branches and leaves all converging upon a single root, and so on.

問：
現見無量佛悉是釋迦分身，為當猶有餘佛，餘佛復有分身不？

答：
《普賢觀》云：「東方有佛名曰善德，彼佛亦有分身諸佛。」若爾，亦有諸佛，諸佛亦有分身。又〈神力品〉云：「彈指、謦欬是二音聲，遍至十方諸佛世界，彼佛四眾遙伸供養，所散諸物從十方來，譬如雪聚，遍覆此間諸佛之上。」故知有諸佛，諸佛亦有分身也。

問：
三世諸佛皆有分身者，云何復言多寶如來全身不散，如入禪定？若全身不散，云何復言遊於十方，證《法華經》？二意云何通？

答：
《釋論》解念佛中云：多寶無人請說法，便入涅槃，後化佛身及七寶塔，證《法華經》。若從《論釋》，乃是化作全身，非無分身也。師云：若言不得說法，那告四眾，我滅後起一大塔？非都不說法，應是不說《法華》。故發大誓願，不碎生身之骨，全身不散，出證圓經。如入禪定者，表於不滅；出證常經，表於不偏；不偏不滅，圓常義顯。口唱真淨大法，真是常，略舉二德，我樂可知，鈍者讀文，猶自不覺也。

問：
三世諸佛皆顯本者，最初實成若為顯本？

答：
不必皆顯本。今作有義者，最初妙覺指初住為本，若初住被加作妙覺，亦指初住為本。初住之前，竪無所指，橫有體用，即指體豈非本耶？又發願故，說壽長遠，如文(云云)。又解，最初之佛，雖無長遠、已今、權實等本迹之可顯，而有體用、教行、理教、事理等本迹之可顯(云云)。若作無義，若最初始成佛，既始得本，未論垂迹，無久迹可發，無久本可顯(云云)。若久成佛，如釋迦之例，以東方為譬。若久此者，即以四方為譬。又久者，十方為譬。若近此者，則減東方為譬。若都無者，則無所譬(云云)。

Question: What is presently seen — the immeasurable buddhas, every one an emanation body of Śākyamuni — are there, then, still further buddhas besides these, and do those further buddhas likewise have their own emanation bodies?

Answer: The Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra says, "In the east there is a buddha named Wholesome Virtue, and that buddha, too, has his own emanation buddhas." If this is so, then there are indeed other buddhas, and those buddhas, too, have their own emanation bodies. Again, the "Spiritual Powers" chapter says: "These two sounds — the snap of fingers and the clearing of the throat — reach throughout the buddha-worlds of the ten directions; the fourfold assembly of those buddhas, from afar, extend their own offerings, and the scattered offerings, coming from every direction, gather like a mass of snow, covering over every one of the buddhas present here." One therefore knows that there are indeed other buddhas, and that those buddhas, too, have their own emanation bodies.

Question: If the buddhas of the three time-periods all have their own emanation bodies, then why does it also say that the Tathāgata Prabhūtaratna's whole body does not disperse, as though he had entered meditative concentration? And if his whole body does not disperse, then why does it also say that he travels throughout the ten directions bearing witness to the Lotus Sūtra? How can these two senses be reconciled?

Answer: The Mahāprajñāpāramitopadeśa, explaining the recollection of the Buddha, says: since Prabhūtaratna had no one to request that he teach the Dharma, he simply entered nirvāṇa, and it was only afterward that his transformation body and his seven-jeweled stūpa bore witness to the Lotus Sūtra. Following this reading from the Treatise, this is precisely a transformed whole body, not a case of having no emanation bodies at all. The Master says: if it were said that he is unable to teach the Dharma, then why does he tell the fourfold assembly, "After my extinction, erect for me a single great stūpa"? It is not that he does not teach the Dharma at all — it must rather be that he does not teach the Lotus Sūtra specifically. Hence, having brought forth his great vow, his physical relics remain unbroken, his whole body undispersed, and he emerges to bear witness to this perfect sūtra. His entering into meditative concentration signals that he has not been extinguished; his emerging to bear witness to this eternal sūtra signals that he holds to no one-sided view; being neither one-sided nor extinguished, the sense of the perfect and the eternal is thereby made manifest. Speaking aloud the true and pure great Dharma is itself the true and eternal — these two virtues are only briefly indicated here, yet what I myself take delight in may already be known from them; a reader of dull faculties, reading this very text, may still fail to perceive it.

Question: If the buddhas of the three time-periods all disclose their own origin, then, as for the very first buddha who was really accomplished — how could his origin possibly be disclosed?

Answer: It is not necessarily the case that every buddha discloses an origin. Taking up the position that there is such a sense: the very first wondrous enlightenment points to the initial abiding as its own origin; and if the initial abiding is, in turn, elevated to become wondrous enlightenment, this, too, points to the initial abiding as its origin. Before the initial abiding, there is, vertically, nothing further to point to, yet, horizontally, there is essence and function — and does pointing to this very essence not itself amount to an origin? Again, because of the vow he made, his life-span is said to be vast and distant, exactly as the text states, and so on. Another interpretation: even the very first buddha, though he has no long-and-distant, already-and-now, or provisional-and-real sense of trace and origin capable of being disclosed, nonetheless has an essence-and-function, teaching-and-practice, principle-and-teaching, phenomena-and-principle sense of trace and origin that can indeed be disclosed, and so on. Taking up the position that there is no such sense: if the very first buddha only just attained buddhahood, then, having only just attained this origin, no descent into any trace is yet even at issue — there is no long-past trace that could be disclosed, and no long-past origin that could be revealed, and so on. If some other buddha attained buddhahood long ago, as in Śākyamuni's own case, this is illustrated by the east alone; if attained even longer ago than this, it is illustrated by all four directions; and if longer still, it is illustrated by the ten directions entire. If nearer than this, then even the east alone is diminished as the illustration; and if there is no such attainment at all, then there is nothing whatsoever to illustrate it by, and so on.

問：
若實初成，無久本可顯，云何經言：「是我方便，諸佛亦然」？

答：
雖無長久之本，若須用方便者，佛有延促劫智，能演七日為無量劫義(云云)。

問：
佛若有久成、始成，有發迹、不發迹，亦應有開三顯一、不開三不顯一耶？

答：
若菩薩、聲聞共為僧者，則有開三顯一；若純菩薩為僧者，何須開顯耶？

問：
若不開三顯一，五佛章云何？

答：
同是聲聞、菩薩共為僧，出五濁世可如此，出淨土佛則不然。

Question: If some buddha really did only just attain buddhahood for the first time, with no long-past origin capable of being disclosed, then why does the sūtra say, "This is my own skillful means, and it is exactly the same for every buddha"?

Answer: Even lacking any long-past origin, if the use of skillful means is still required, a buddha nonetheless possesses the wisdom of extending or contracting an eon, able to expand seven days into the sense of immeasurable eons, and so on.

Question: If a buddha may either have attained buddhahood long ago or only just now, and may either disclose his trace or not disclose it, then should there not likewise be cases of opening the three to reveal the one, and cases of not opening the three and not revealing the one?

Answer: Where bodhisattvas and śrāvakas together make up the saṅgha, there is indeed the opening of the three to reveal the one; where the saṅgha consists purely of bodhisattvas, what need is there for any such opening and disclosing?

Question: If some buddhas do not open the three to reveal the one, then what of the chapter of the five buddhas?

Answer: These are alike cases where śrāvakas and bodhisattvas together make up the saṅgha, appearing amid the five turbidities of a defiled age — of this kind, it may indeed be so; but a buddha appearing within a pure land is not the same.

問：
破十麁，顯十妙，則無明惑盡，一實理彰。今更破迹妙為麁，顯本為妙，破何惑？顯何理？

答：
無明重數甚多，實相海深無量，如此破顯無咎。

又問：
若爾，還以妙破妙，所破之妙，妙而更麁，亦應還以麁破麁，所破之麁，例更是妙；所破四住，例亦應妙？

答：
就頓明義，秖四住即是於妙，況破四住智，寧非妙耶？

又問：
若爾，但有頓義，應無漸義？

答：
若分漸、頓，漸之能、所俱麁；頓之能、所俱妙(云云)。

Question: Breaking through the ten coarse aspects to reveal the ten wondrous aspects — this is precisely fundamental ignorance and delusion exhausted, and the single, real principle made manifest. Now, breaking through the trace's own wondrous aspects yet again, taking them as coarse, and revealing the origin as wondrous — what delusion is broken through here? What principle is revealed?

Answer: The layers of fundamental ignorance are exceedingly many, and the ocean of the real mark is immeasurably deep — breaking through and revealing in this way carries no fault.

A further question: If this is so, then wondrous is here used to break through wondrous, and the wondrous thus broken through, though wondrous, becomes coarse once more — should it not, by the same logic, likewise be the case that coarse is used to break through coarse, and the coarse thus broken through becomes, by the same pattern, wondrous once more? And should the four dwelling-grounds of affliction broken through, by the same pattern, likewise become wondrous?

Answer: Speaking in terms of the sudden teaching's own sense, the four dwelling-grounds of affliction themselves are precisely wondrous already — how much more so, then, the wisdom that breaks through the four dwelling-grounds of affliction! How could this possibly fail to be wondrous?

A further question: If this is so, then there is only the sudden teaching's own sense — should there then be no sense of the gradual at all?

Answer: If the gradual and the sudden are distinguished, then, within the gradual, both what breaks through and what is broken through are coarse alike; within the sudden, both what breaks through and what is broken through are wondrous alike, and so on.

問：
中間有偏圓、權實，而同稱是權者，亦應同稱為偏耶？

答：
通義則爾，別義不然。偏圓約法，法則已定，故偏非圓、圓非偏。權實約教，迹中施設，同皆是假，故就假論權耳。

問：
既有帶麁妙，復有不帶麁妙，亦應有帶妙麁、不帶妙麁？

答：
此應四句：帶麁妙，即別教也；不帶麁妙，即圓教也；帶妙麁，即通教也；不帶妙麁，即三藏也。又帶麁妙，如通；不帶麁妙，如圓；亦帶麁亦不帶麁，如別；非帶非不帶，如圓接別、如圓別接通。又約五味：麁不帶妙如酪；妙不帶麁如醍醐；亦帶麁亦不帶麁如生、熟蘇；非帶麁非不帶麁如乳(云云)。

問：
二麁既不同，那忽同呼為麁？

答：
事有淺、深，故為二；俱非妙理，故同是麁。

問：
應帶方便實？不帶方便實？

答：
例。

問：
亦應有帶二一？不帶二一？

答：
例。通論本迹，秖是權實；別論高下，宜用本迹；橫論真偽，宜用權實；本迹約身、約位，權實約智、約教(云云)。

問：
本地十妙約六重本迹，攝屬何耶？

答：
非已今、非中間，乃是體用、教行、理教等，共論十妙也。

Question: Within the interval, there is the one-sided and the perfect, and the provisional and the real; and where both alike are named provisional, should both alike also be named one-sided?

Answer: In terms of the sense held in common, this is indeed so; but in terms of their particular sense, it is not. The one-sided and the perfect are determined in terms of the dharma itself, and the dharma is already fixed — hence the one-sided is not the perfect, and the perfect is not the one-sided. The provisional and the real are determined in terms of the teaching, and, within the trace, whatever is set forth is alike a mere provisional device — hence it is only in terms of this provisional device that "the provisional" here is discussed.

Question: Given that there is both wondrous accompanied by coarse and wondrous unaccompanied by coarse, should there not likewise be coarse accompanied by wondrous, and coarse unaccompanied by wondrous?

Answer: This calls for a fourfold set of propositions: wondrous accompanied by coarse is precisely the distinct teaching; wondrous unaccompanied by coarse is precisely the perfect teaching; coarse accompanied by wondrous is precisely the shared teaching; coarse unaccompanied by wondrous is precisely the Tripiṭaka teaching. Again, accompanied by coarse is like the shared teaching; unaccompanied by coarse is like the perfect teaching; both accompanied and unaccompanied at once is like the distinct teaching; neither accompanied nor unaccompanied is like the perfect teaching receiving the distinct teaching, or like the perfect-and-distinct teaching receiving the shared teaching. Again, in terms of the Five Flavors: coarse unaccompanied by wondrous is like cream; wondrous unaccompanied by coarse is like ghee; both accompanied and unaccompanied by coarse at once is like curdled and clarified butter; neither accompanied by coarse nor unaccompanied by coarse is like milk, and so on.

Question: Since the two kinds of coarse already differ from one another, how can they suddenly both be called by the same name, "coarse"?

Answer: The matter itself has both shallow and deep aspects, hence there are two; yet neither is the wondrous principle itself, hence both alike are coarse.

Question: Should there, then, likewise be a real accompanied by expedient means, and a real unaccompanied by expedient means?

Answer: The same pattern applies.

Question: Should there likewise be a one accompanied by the two, and a one unaccompanied by the two?

Answer: The same pattern applies. Discussed generally, trace and origin are simply the provisional and the real; discussed particularly, in terms of higher and lower, trace and origin is the fitting vocabulary to use; discussed crosswise, in terms of the true and the false, provisional and real is the fitting vocabulary to use. Trace and origin are determined in terms of the body and the stage; provisional and real are determined in terms of wisdom and the teaching, and so on.

Question: The original ground's own ten wondrous aspects, considered in terms of the sixfold sense of trace and origin — under which of the six does it properly belong?

Answer: It belongs to neither the already-and-now sense nor the interval sense — it is, rather, the essence-and-function, teaching-and-practice, principle-and-teaching senses, and the rest, that together discuss the ten wondrous aspects.

Reading note

This long chain of fifteen questions is this item's own way of stress-testing the trace/origin machinery built up across the whole of Fascicle 7: emanation bodies within emanation bodies (are there buddhas even further back than the ones already disclosed?), Prabhūtaratna's own paradox of an undispersed body that still travels the ten directions, and, most pointedly, whether a buddha who really did just now attain buddhahood for the first time could have any origin at all to disclose — answered by falling back on essence-and-function and teaching-and-practice as senses of "origin" that do not require any literal antiquity. The closing run of propositions on coarse-accompanied-by-wondrous and wondrous-accompanied-by-coarse extends the Four Teachings' own four-cornered logic, already used throughout this treatise, one level higher — to the trace/origin distinction itself — while the very last question closes the loop by asking where this whole account of the ten wondrous aspects belongs within the sixfold trace/origin scheme given back at this fascicle's own opening (釋本迹): neither in time (already-and-now, the interval) but in the structural senses (essence/function, teaching/practice, principle/teaching) alone.

Judging Coarse and Wondrous 判麁妙

Chinese Source

○第七判麁妙者，若迹中已得十麁為麁，十妙為妙；未開十麁為麁，開十成妙，具如前說。迹中若待麁妙、若開麁妙，此妙不異本妙，而言始得，始得為麁。本中先成，若麁若妙、若開麁妙，亦不異迹妙，而是先得，先得稱妙。又迹中事理始得為麁，本中事理先得為妙，迹中理教、教行、體用、權實等，亦如是。又若未發迹顯本者，但解迹中事理之麁妙，終不能解本中之事麁，況解本中之理妙！彌勒尚不達，何況餘人！若發迹中之事理，即顯本中之事理。

亦知由本中之事理，能垂迹中之事理。迹既由本，則本妙迹麁，既有本迹之殊，故言麁妙。妙理則非迹非本，不思議一也。理教、教行、體用、權實、已今等，亦如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, judging coarse and wondrous: within the trace, what has already attained the ten coarse aspects is coarse, and the ten wondrous aspects are wondrous; what has not yet opened the ten coarse aspects is coarse, and opening the ten makes them wondrous — exactly as explained before. Within the trace, whether relative coarse-and-wondrous or opened coarse-and-wondrous, this wondrous is no different from the original wondrous, and yet it is said to be only now first attained — being only now first attained, it is coarse. Within the origin, already accomplished from before, whether coarse or wondrous, whether relative or opened coarse-and-wondrous, this too is no different from the trace's own wondrous, and yet it was already attained beforehand — having already been attained beforehand, it is called wondrous. Again, within the trace, phenomena and principle only now first attained are coarse; within the origin, phenomena and principle already attained beforehand are wondrous; within the trace, principle-and-teaching, teaching-and-practice, essence-and-function, provisional-and-real, and the rest, are all exactly the same. Again, for one who has not yet disclosed the trace to reveal the origin, one is able only to understand the coarse-and-wondrous of phenomena and principle within the trace, and is entirely unable to understand the coarse of phenomena within the origin — how much more so the wondrous of principle within the origin! Even Maitreya himself did not penetrate this — how much more so everyone else! If one discloses the phenomena and principle within the trace, this itself reveals the phenomena and principle within the origin.

One likewise thereby knows that it is by means of the phenomena and principle within the origin that the phenomena and principle within the trace are able to descend as they do. Since the trace derives from the origin, the origin is wondrous and the trace is coarse; and since there is indeed this distinction between origin and trace, coarse and wondrous are spoken of accordingly. Yet the wondrous principle itself is neither trace nor origin — it is inconceivably one and the same. Principle-and-teaching, teaching-and-practice, essence-and-function, provisional-and-real, already-and-now, and the rest, are all exactly the same.

Reading note

This item's central move is to show that "coarse" and "wondrous" never named an intrinsic property of either trace or origin to begin with — the very same content, whether trace or origin, is judged wondrous by having already been attained, and coarse by being only now first attained. This is why "even Maitreya himself did not penetrate this": the closing line's insistence that the wondrous principle itself is "neither trace nor origin" is this treatise's own reminder, restated one level up, that every technical distinction it has built across seven fascicles — coarse/wondrous no less than trace/origin — is scaffolding laid over a single reality that does not, in itself, divide.

Clarifying the Provisional and the Real 明權實

Chinese Source

○第八、明權實者，照迹中十麁之境為權，照迹中十妙之境為實，乃至中間三世所照十麁之境為權，十妙之境為實。若權若實，悉皆是迹，迹故稱權。如是中間無量，無量不可說節節權實，餘經尚無中間一番之權，況一番之實！尚無中間一番權實，況無量番！尚無中間權實，況有本地權實！中間權實皆名為權，本初照十麁十妙皆名為實。迹權本實俱不思議，不思議即是法性，法性之理，非古非今，非本非迹，非權非實，但約此法性論本迹權實麁妙耳。但以世俗文字有去來今，非謂菩提有去來今也。

復次，分別權實，則有三種：謂自行、化他、自行化他，具如境妙中說。本地自行所契權實二智，名佛自行權實；從本已來，乃至鹿苑，種種方便，隨他意語，說此二智，迴轉無方，名佛化他權實二智。化他雖有二種，皆名為權；自行雖二種，皆名為實，是名自行化他合說權實。

復次，迹中約實施權，意在於實，而實意難測。何者？化城是權，而人作實解，是不識權，亦不知實。若廢權顯實，意在於權，權則易測，何者？既知化城一事是佛權施，則遍達恒沙佛法，遠通久劫方便。故《華嚴》中明，為阿鞞跋致多明事數，即其義也。若開權顯實者，達事法已，權意即息，亦不離權遠求於實。權即是實，無復別權，故言開權顯實也。

迹中既有三意，如此迹由本垂，本亦如是，本迹雖殊，不思議一也(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, clarifying the provisional and the real: illuminating the ten coarse aspects' own objects within the trace is the provisional; illuminating the ten wondrous aspects' own objects within the trace is the real; and, further, the objects of the ten coarse aspects illuminated across the three time-periods within the interval are the provisional, and the objects of the ten wondrous aspects are the real. Whether provisional or real, both alike are entirely trace — hence, being trace, both are named provisional. In this way, the interval is immeasurable, and its immeasurable, inexpressible, node-by-node instances of provisional and real — other sūtras do not even possess a single such instance of the provisional within the interval, let alone a single instance of the real! They do not even possess a single such instance of provisional-and-real within the interval, let alone immeasurable instances! They do not even possess the interval's own provisional-and-real, let alone the original ground's own provisional-and-real! Every provisional-and-real of the interval is named provisional; the very first illumination of the ten coarse and ten wondrous aspects at the original ground is named real. The trace's provisional and the origin's real are both alike inconceivable, and this very inconceivability is precisely the dharma-nature. The principle of the dharma-nature is neither ancient nor present, neither origin nor trace, neither provisional nor real — it is only in terms of this same dharma-nature that origin and trace, provisional and real, coarse and wondrous, are discussed at all. It is only in terms of worldly, conventional writing that there is a past, a future, and a present — this is not to say that bodhi itself possesses any past, future, or present.

Again, distinguishing the provisional and the real, there are three kinds: namely, of one's own practice, used in teaching others, and of one's own practice set against teaching others — exactly as explained within the wondrous of the object. The two kinds of wisdom, provisional and real, realized through one's own practice at the original ground, are named the buddha's own provisional and real wisdom of his own practice; from the origin onward, even down to the Deer Park, every kind of skillful means, speech following the intention of others, expounding these same two kinds of wisdom, turning about without any fixed pattern — this is named the buddha's own provisional and real wisdom used in teaching others. Though teaching others has these same two kinds, both alike are named provisional; though one's own practice likewise has these same two kinds, both alike are named real — this is what is meant by discussing the provisional and the real jointly, in terms of one's own practice set against teaching others.

Again, within the trace, setting forth the provisional in terms of the real, the intent lies in the real, and yet this real intent is difficult to fathom. Why is this? The conjured city is the provisional, and yet, if a person takes it for the real, this is failing to recognize the provisional, and likewise failing to know the real. If, instead, the provisional is set aside to reveal the real, the intent lies in the provisional, and the provisional then becomes easy to fathom. Why is this? Once one knows that this one matter, the conjured city, is the Buddha's own provisional device, one thereby penetrates every buddha-dharma numerous as the sands of the Ganges, reaching all the way back to the skillful means of eons long past. Hence, the Avataṃsaka Sūtra's own clarification, teaching in detail the numerical categories of things for the sake of those never turning back, carries precisely this same sense. As for opening the provisional to reveal the real: once the phenomenal dharma is penetrated, the provisional intent itself comes to rest, and yet one does not depart from the provisional to seek the real somewhere far off. The provisional is itself the real, with no further, separate provisional remaining — hence this is called opening the provisional to reveal the real.

Since the trace already has these three senses in just this way, and the trace descends from the origin, the origin, too, is exactly the same; though origin and trace differ, they are inconceivably one and the same, and so on.

Reading note

The opening move here reprises, at the level of the origin gate, the identical logic already used to close the trace gate's own ten wondrous aspects back at this fascicle's start: what other sūtras cannot even offer once — a single instance of the provisional and the real — this treatise offers in "immeasurable, inexpressible, node-by-node" instances, escalating from the interval's own countless provisional-and-real pairs to the qualitatively different provisional-and-real of the original ground itself. The threefold self-practice/teaching-others/both-combined division, reused from the wondrous of the object all the way back in Fascicle 2, and the conjured-city image, reused from the *Lotus Sūtra*'s own parable, both serve the same closing point: "the provisional is itself the real, with no further, separate provisional remaining" — opening the provisional never uncovers some other, separate real hiding behind it, but simply sees the provisional correctly for what it always was.

Benefit 利益

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, discussing benefit: first, clarifying the benefit to the birth-body; next, clarifying the benefit to the Dharma-body. The birth-body attains benefit in two places — within the trace gate, where the three are gathered into the one, and the provisional is opened to reveal the real. As for the bodhisattva of the birth-body attaining benefit: among the ten wondrous aspects, five wondrous benefits are attained. Why is this? The wondrous of the object is common, possessed fully by everyone already; the wondrous of the vehicle is particular, ultimately residing in the buddha alone. Sympathetic response, spiritual powers, and teaching the Dharma are all benefits belonging to the level of the fruit — if the fruit has not yet been realized, this benefit is not yet discussed.

Considered within the six identities, four identities' worth of benefit is attained; identity in principle and identity in the ultimate follow the same pattern as before — only within identity in name are wisdom, practice, stage, retinue, and merit attained, and, further, within identity in a portion of the truth, wisdom, practice, stage, retinue, and merit are likewise attained. Just as when Śāriputra received his prediction, the fourfold assembly, the devas, and the nāgas all rejoiced, speaking a verse: "Śāriputra, of great wisdom, has now received the venerable prediction; we too, exactly the same, shall each attain buddhahood as well" — this is precisely the mark of the birth-body bodhisattva hearing the teaching within the trace gate and attaining benefit.

Disclosing the origin to reveal the trace, teaching that the Buddha's own life-span is vast and distant, the samādhi of contemplating the Buddha attains great increase — from this, there likewise exist birth-body bodhisattvas who attain five benefits among the ten wondrous aspects, and four benefits among the six identities, diminishing birth and advancing the path, and so on.

From the second abiding onward, up through the stage of one more birth remaining, all of these are cases of the Dharma-body attaining five benefits. Why is this? Those born through response, hearing of the merit of the original ground, find the samādhi of contemplating the Buddha turning ever deeper and broader, beyond all measure, incomparable to the benefit that came before within the trace. Why is this? The Buddha's own state grows ever deeper, and his merit ever greater accordingly. Hence the "Discrimination of Merits" chapter says: "The Buddha has taught a rare dharma, never heard before — the World-Honored One possesses great power, his life-span beyond all measure." Of those who are said to gain benefit from the Dharma, joy fills their entire bodies; some abide at the stage of non-retrogression, some attain dhāraṇī. This is precisely the mark of both the birth-body and the Dharma-body attaining benefit.

若論實道得益，兩處不殊，而權智事用，不得相比。喻如慧解脫、俱解脫，無漏不二，而功德優劣。前迹門得道，止齊無生法忍；本門得道，齊餘一生在，以塵為數，多小深淺，豈同於前？當揀彼文，從發心處即是六根淨位，乃至一生在，即是最後分真(云云)。

又流通利益者，前流通迹門，是諸發誓菩薩及諸羅漢得授記者，此土、他土弘經，論其功德。觀文，但明冥利，不說顯益。今說本門，付囑一切諸佛所有之法，兼得迹門法也。秘奧之藏，即是本迹中實相也。一切甚深之事，即是本迹中因果也。如此等法，付囑千世界微塵菩薩，法身地弘經，何但如生身，此土、他土弘經耶？十法界身遊諸國土，則有冥、顯兩益也。疑者云：法身常有佛，何須菩薩弘？但弘之在人，待時待伴，如佛雖在世，而文殊入龍宮，法身處雖有佛，復須外緣。故佛付囑，吐舌摩頂，種種相貌，殷勤付囑，令弘此法得無量微妙功德。其有聞者，妙益不可稱數。故文云：若有聞佛說壽量，一切皆歡喜，得無量無漏功德之果報。即此意也。

As for discussing the attainment of the real path, the two places do not differ; yet, in terms of the functioning of provisional wisdom's own affairs, they cannot be set side by side. It is comparable to liberation through wisdom alone and liberation in both respects: the taintless itself is not two, and yet their merit differs, superior and inferior. The attainment of the path within the trace gate, as discussed before, reaches only as far as the forbearance of the dharma of no-generation; the attainment of the path within the origin gate reaches as far as one more birth remaining, counted by motes of dust — how could their abundance or scarcity, depth or shallowness, possibly be the same as what came before? One ought to distinguish that text carefully: from the point where the aspiration is first raised, this is precisely the stage of the six purified faculties, and, going further, one more birth remaining is precisely the very last portion of the truth, and so on.

Again, as for the benefit within the propagation section: the propagation of the trace gate, discussed earlier, concerns the bodhisattvas who have made their vows and the arhats who have received their predictions, propagating the sūtra in this land and in other lands, and discusses their merit. Examining the text, only the hidden benefit is made clear there — the manifest benefit is not spoken of. Now, in speaking of the origin gate, every dharma possessed by every buddha is entrusted, together with the dharma of the trace gate as well. The secret and profound treasury is precisely the real mark within both origin and trace; every deeply profound matter is precisely the cause and fruit within both origin and trace. Dharmas of this kind are entrusted to the bodhisattvas numerous as motes of dust across a thousand worlds, propagating the sūtra from the very ground of the Dharma-body itself — how could this be limited merely to the birth-body's own propagating the sūtra in this land and in other lands? A body pervading the ten dharma-realms, traveling throughout every land, thereby possesses both the hidden and the manifest benefit. One who doubts this says: since the Dharma-body permanently possesses the Buddha, what need is there for a bodhisattva to propagate it? Yet propagation itself depends upon the person, awaiting the right time and the right companions — just as, though the Buddha was in the world, Mañjuśrī still entered the dragon-king's palace to propagate the teaching there; though the domain of the Dharma-body already possesses the Buddha, an external condition is still required. Hence the Buddha entrusts the teaching, extending his tongue and laying his hand upon the crown, in every kind of manifest form, earnestly entrusting it, so that propagating this Dharma may attain immeasurable, subtle merit. Whoever hears it attains a wondrous benefit beyond all reckoning. Hence the text says: if there are those who hear the Buddha teach the measure of his life-span, all alike rejoice, and attain the resultant fruit of immeasurable, taintless merit — this is precisely this same sense.

Reading note

This ninth item is easy to confuse with the fifth wondrous aspect's own "wondrous of the original merit and benefit" (本利益妙) covered earlier in this same fascicle, but the scope differs: that item asked what benefit belongs to the origin gate specifically, while this item asks what benefit accrues from the whole account of trace-and-origin together, tracked through the birth-body versus the Dharma-body and through the six identities. The doubt raised near the end — since the Dharma-body already permanently possesses the Buddha, why would a bodhisattva's own propagation still be needed? — is answered by the same logic already used for Mañjuśrī entering the dragon-king's palace back in Fascicle 6's wondrous of the retinue: presence in principle is never a substitute for the actual, contingent work of a particular person propagating the teaching at a particular time, to particular companions.

The Contemplation of Mind 觀心

Chinese Source

○十、觀心者，本妙長遠，豈可觀心？雖不即是，亦不離心。何者？佛如、眾生如，一如無二如，佛既觀心，得此本妙，迹用廣大，不可稱說。我如如佛如，亦當觀心，出此大利。亦願我如，速如佛如。故文云：「聞佛壽無量，深心須臾信，其福過於彼。願我於未來，長壽度眾生，如今日世尊，諸釋中之王，道場師子吼，說法無所畏。我等於未來，一切所尊敬，坐於道場時，說壽亦如是。」此即觀心本妙，得六即利益之相(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, the contemplation of mind: the original wondrous aspects are vast and distant — how could they possibly be found through the contemplation of mind? Though not identical with mind, they are not separate from mind either. Why is this? The suchness of the Buddha and the suchness of sentient beings are a single suchness, without a second suchness; since the Buddha himself contemplated mind and thereby attained this original wondrous, his trace's own function became vast and great, beyond all telling. My own suchness is the Buddha's own suchness — I, too, ought to contemplate mind, and bring forth this same great benefit. May my own suchness, too, swiftly become the Buddha's own suchness. Hence the text says: "One who hears of the Buddha's immeasurable life-span, and, with a deeply sincere mind, believes it even for a single instant — that person's own blessing surpasses even the offerings described before. May I, too, in the future, live long and deliver sentient beings, just as the World-Honored One does today, king among all the Śākyas, roaring the lion's roar at the place of enlightenment, teaching the Dharma without any fear. May we, too, in the future, honored and revered by all, when we ourselves sit at the place of enlightenment, teach of our own life-span in exactly this same way." This is precisely the mark of the contemplation of mind attaining the original wondrous, and, through it, the benefit of the six identities, and so on.

A Closing Question on "Great" and "Wondrous" 大妙料簡

Chinese Source

△問：
大妙云何？

答：
此應三雙六句分別(云云)。文云：「佛自住大乘。」又云：「如是大果報。」又云：「有大車。」而題稱為妙。如《涅槃》云：「大般涅槃，微妙經典。」而題稱為大，即妙是大，即大是妙也。《大品》云：「色非深非妙，乃至識非深非妙。」此是大破妙。此文云：「一切法空寂，無漏、無為、無大、無小。」此是妙破大。如大阿羅漢，此大猶修於妙，如滅、止、妙、離，此妙猶更修大(云云)。

若大妙一等，餘經俱應稱妙。

答：
餘經通論，約理大妙不殊，而別帶方便；此經不帶方便，故別稱妙。小乘得入，發迹顯本，故別稱妙。

Ru-Yi Translation

A further question: What, then, is meant by "great" as opposed to "wondrous"?

Answer: This calls for a threefold pairing, discussed as six propositions, and so on. The text says, "The Buddha himself abides in the Great Vehicle." Again it says, "such a great fruit of retribution." Again it says, "there is a great cart" — and yet the title itself is named "wondrous." The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says, "great final nirvāṇa, a subtle and wondrous scripture" — and yet its title is named "great." Wondrous is thus precisely great, and great is thus precisely wondrous. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra says, "Form is neither profound nor wondrous, and so on down to consciousness, neither profound nor wondrous" — this is great breaking through wondrous. This present text says, "All dharmas are empty and quiescent, without taint, unconditioned, without great and without small" — this is wondrous breaking through great. It is like the great arhat, whose greatness still requires the cultivation of the wondrous; and like cessation, stilling, subtlety, and freedom, whose wondrousness still requires, further, the cultivation of the great, and so on.

Question: If great and wondrous are thus equal, then every other sūtra ought equally to be called wondrous.

Answer: Considered generally, other sūtras, too, are, in terms of principle, no different in their greatness and wondrousness; yet, particularly, they still carry an accompanying element of skillful means. This sūtra carries no accompanying skillful means, and so it alone is specially called wondrous. Even the Hīnayāna is able to enter it, disclosing the trace to reveal the origin — and so, for this reason too, it alone is specially called wondrous.

問：
大小俱稱妙，大小俱明常，一往斥之(云云)。小乘滅止妙離，名同理異，不得是常，並云不得是妙？

答：
妙名不可思議，小乘真諦，亡言絕慮，通得是不思議，通名為妙耳。

次當縱之，亦得以三無為稱常而常異。又並既俱稱常，亦俱會一？

答：
會諸見同入真，而會異。又並俱無常俱麁，俱不會耶！例通而義異，云何大乘無常？大乘非但無無常，亦無於常，以無於常，故言無常，云何大乘是麁！夫有言說即名為麁，云何大乘不會耶！一切諸法皆是佛法，更何所會(云云)。

Question: If both the Mahāyāna and the Hīnayāna alike are called wondrous, and both alike make permanence clear, then, taking this at face value, one might reject the distinction between them, and so on. Yet the Hīnayāna's own "cessation, stilling, subtlety, freedom" shares the same name but differs in principle — it cannot be permanence, and, likewise, ought it not also be said that it cannot be wondrous?

Answer: The name "wondrous" means the inconceivable. The Hīnayāna's own truth of ultimate meaning, cutting off both speech and thought, generally also attains this same inconceivability — hence it, too, is generally named wondrous.

Next, granting this position for the sake of argument: one may also, by means of the three unconditioned dharmas, name both permanence, and yet their permanence differs. And further, since both alike are named permanent, are both alike, too, "gathered together as one"?

Answer: Gathering the various views so that they alike enter the real is indeed a "gathering," and yet the gathering itself differs in each case. And further, if both alike were impermanent and both alike coarse, would both alike then also fail to be gathered? The pattern holds in general, yet the sense differs in particular — how, then, could the Mahāyāna be called impermanent? The Mahāyāna is not merely without impermanence — it is also without permanence itself; and it is precisely because it is without any fixed permanence that it is spoken of, in this sense, as "without permanence." How, then, could the Mahāyāna be coarse? Now, wherever there is verbal expression at all, that alone is named coarse — by this logic, how could the Mahāyāna's own teaching, too, then fail to be gathered together as one? All dharmas without exception are themselves the Buddha-dharma — what further "gathering into one" could there possibly be, and so on.

Reading note

This closing exchange resolves a tension that has been implicit since the very first fascicle: if "great" (大, as in Mahāyāna, or the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s own "great final nirvāṇa") and "wondrous" name the identical reality, why does this sūtra's own title choose "wondrous" specifically, rather than "great"? The answer turns on what each title excludes: other Mahāyāna sūtras remain, in Zhiyi's judgment, entangled with an accompanying element of skillful means even where their own principle is equally great and wondrous, while this sūtra alone carries no such accompaniment, and alone opens a path by which even a Hīnayāna hearer can enter and have his own trace disclosed to reveal its origin. The final reductio — that treating the Mahāyāna's own use of words as automatically "coarse" would just as easily disqualify the Mahāyāna's own teaching, which equally uses words — closes the "wondrous dharma" (妙法) portion of Explaining the Name on the same note struck throughout: technical distinctions are tools for teaching, not partitions within reality itself, since "all dharmas without exception are themselves the Buddha-dharma."

Explaining "Lotus Flower" 釋蓮華

Settling Whether It Is Dharma or Metaphor 定法譬

Chinese Source

次、釋蓮華者，為四意：一、定法譬。二、引舊釋。三、出經論。四、正解釋。

定法譬者，權實難顯，借喻蓮華，譬於妙法。又七喻文多，故以譬標題。又解云：蓮華非譬，當體得名。類如劫初，萬物無名，聖人觀理，準則作名：如蛛羅引絲，倣之結網；蓬飛獨運，依而造車；浮槎汎流而立舟；鳥跡成文而寫字；皆法理而制事耳。今蓮華之稱，非是假喻，乃是法華法門；法華法門清淨，因果微妙，名此法門為蓮華，即是法華三昧當體之名，非譬喻也。餘經多自釋名，此經無解，或是其文未度耳。而此兩釋皆有道理，今融二意。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining "lotus flower": this has four senses: first, settling whether it is dharma or metaphor; second, citing prior interpretations; third, presenting sūtra and treatise sources; fourth, the correct explanation.

As for settling whether it is dharma or metaphor: the provisional and the real are difficult to make manifest, and so the lotus flower is borrowed as a metaphor, likening it to the wondrous dharma. Again, the seven parables' own text is extensive, and so the title itself is set forth by way of metaphor. Another interpretation says: the lotus flower is not a metaphor at all, but a name obtained from its very own essence. This is comparable to the very beginning of the eon, when the ten thousand things had no names yet, and the sages, contemplating principle, established names by taking it as their standard: just as the spider's spinning of its web inspired the knotting of nets in imitation of it; the tumbleweed's own unaided rolling inspired the making of the cart, modeled after it; a raft floating and drifting upon the current inspired the establishing of the boat; and a bird's footprints forming a pattern inspired the writing of characters — in every case, phenomena were fashioned by taking principle as their model. So too, the name "lotus flower" here is not a borrowed metaphor at all, but is precisely the Dharma-gate of the Lotus; the Dharma-gate of the Lotus, pure, its cause and fruit subtle and wondrous, is itself named "lotus flower" — this is precisely the name obtained from the very essence of the Lotus samādhi, not a metaphor at all. Other sūtras mostly explain their own names directly, while this sūtra offers no such explanation — perhaps this portion of the text was simply never translated. Yet both of these interpretations have their own reasoning, and the two senses are here brought together as one.

問：
蓮華定是法華三昧之蓮華？定是華草之蓮華？

答：
定是法蓮華，法蓮華難解，故草華為喻。利根即名解理，不假譬喻，但作法華之解。中、下未悟，須譬乃知，以易解之蓮華，喻難解之蓮華。故有三周說法，逗上、中、下根。約上根是法名，約中、下是譬名；三根合論，雙標法譬。如此解者，與誰為諍耶！今且依法譬為釋也。

Question: Is the lotus flower definitely the lotus flower of the Lotus samādhi? Or is it definitely the lotus flower of the plant-and-grass kind?

Answer: It is definitely the dharma-lotus-flower. The dharma-lotus-flower is difficult to understand, and so the plant-flower is used as its illustration. One of sharp faculties understands the principle directly from the very name itself, without needing any metaphor at all, taking it simply as the understanding of the Lotus itself. Those of middling and lesser faculties, not yet awakened, require the metaphor before they can know it — the easily understood lotus flower is thus used to illustrate the difficult-to-understand lotus flower. Hence there are the three cycles of teaching the Dharma, meeting the upper, middling, and lower faculties in turn. In terms of the upper faculty, it is a dharma-name; in terms of the middling and lower faculties, it is a metaphor-name; discussed jointly across all three faculties, both dharma and metaphor are indicated at once. Understood in this way, with whom is there any dispute left to have? Here, then, the explanation shall proceed in terms of both dharma and metaphor together.

Reading note

The opening question — dharma or metaphor? — is resolved the same way this treatise resolves most such either/or questions: not by choosing, but by showing each answer suits a different hearer, sharp-faculty and middling-or-lesser alike, so that "with whom is there any dispute left to have?" Guangzhai's rejected reading is worth pausing on for its own sake: his flower-and-seed-pod-are-simultaneous image is a genuinely elegant illustration of this sūtra's own simultaneous cause-and-fruit teaching, and Zhiyi does not discard the image, only its "one-sided" application — dividing cause to the disciples and fruit to the teacher, when in fact the trace gate's own text shows teacher and disciple each possessing a complete cause-and-fruit pair of their own, and the origin gate mirrors this exactly one level up. The four-subtle-elements analogy that follows is a quietly important methodological point: just as the four elements underlying both flower and seed-pod belong properly to neither one alone, the real mark underlying trace and origin, or provisional and real, is not itself partitioned by the distinctions built upon it — the same warning against reifying the treatise's own scaffolding already sounded at the close of the immediately preceding items.

Reading note

This ten-suchness sequence is the treatise's most sustained single-image exposition since the milk-to-ghee Five Flavors scheme: every one of the ten technical categories established for the wondrous of the object back in Fascicle 2 — mark, nature, essence, power, activity, cause, condition, fruit, retribution, and the ultimate equality of the fundamental and the derivative — receives its own distinct anatomical correlate in the lotus seed's own growth, from the black, unstainable skin (mark) through the dais that outlasts the flower entirely (retribution) to the identity between the lotus's buried and blooming forms (the equality of fundamental and derivative). The choice of the *stone lotus* specifically — a seed capable of remaining dormant for centuries yet still viable — sharpens the "unstained by adventitious dust" image already used site-wide for the tathāgatagarbha: buddha-nature is not damaged by however long it lies buried, only temporarily unexpressed.

Digging up and transplanting that same lotus root, or gathering that same lotus seed, and planting it within this pond, so that lotus flowers flourish in abundance — this illustrates the wondrous retinue born through vow. From that other pond, flying here like a drifting gossamer thread or a thin mist, entering into this pond, so that lotus flowers flourish in abundance — this illustrates the wondrous retinue born through response.

Fish and turtles gasping for air beneath it, bees and butterflies flying and gathering above it, illustrate the wondrous benefit of sentient beings' own cooling, refreshing retribution; whoever sees it and rejoices illustrates the benefit of the cause; gathering and using its leaves illustrates the benefit of the three kinds of grasses; gathering and using its flower illustrates the wondrous benefit of the small tree; gathering and using its seed-pod illustrates the wondrous benefit of the great tree; gathering and using its root illustrates the wondrous benefit of the most real matter. All of these illustrate merit and benefit.

Illustrations of this kind, and every other immeasurable illustration besides, serve to illustrate the ten wondrous aspects within the trace, and so on.

Reading note

Having spent the ten suchnesses on a single organism's own anatomy, this second sweep turns to the lotus pond as an entire ecosystem in motion — its reflection in still or troubled water for sympathetic response, its swaying in the wind for the "Introduction" chapter's own six auspicious signs, its opening and closing for teaching the Dharma, and its own reproductive cycle (root sending up a new flower, a fallen seed sprouting elsewhere, a transplanted root, wind-borne spores drifting in) for the fourfold retinue scheme established back in Fascicle 6. The closing image — bees, butterflies, fish, and turtles all drawing benefit from the same pond according to their own nature — closes the trace gate's own ten wondrous aspects on the note this whole extensive illustration has been building toward: one single, undivided reality yielding entirely different benefits depending only on what draws near it and how.

Chinese Source

次、譬本者，譬如一池蓮華始熟，熟已墮落，投於泥水，方復生長，乃至成熟，如是展轉更生熟，歲月既積，遂遍大池，華田布滿。佛亦如是，本初修因證果已竟，為眾生故更起方便，在生死中，示初發心，復示究竟。數數生滅，無數百千，本地垂應，俯同凡俗，更修五行。烏蓮更生莖葉，譬更修聖行；蓮子四微，稍稍增長，譬更修天行；荷葉始生，譬更修梵行；蓮子墮泥，譬同諸惡更修病行；蓮芽始萌，譬同小善，更修嬰兒行。如是三世益物，不可稱計，遍滿法界，無非分身、垂迹、開迹、廢迹等益(云云)。若非蓮華，何由遍喻上來諸法？法、譬雙辨，故稱妙法蓮華也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, illustrating the origin: it is like a single pond's own lotus flowers first ripening; once ripened, they fall, cast into the mud and water, and grow once more, all the way to ripening again — turning and turning in this way, generation after generation, as months and years accumulate, they come at last to fill the whole pond, the flower-field spread out in full. The Buddha is exactly the same: having, at the very beginning, cultivated the cause and realized the fruit already, he further raises up skillful means for the sake of sentient beings, and, within birth-and-death, shows the first arousing of the aspiration, and shows, too, its ultimate accomplishment. Arising and ceasing again and again, countless hundreds and thousands of times, he descends in response from the original ground, bending low to match the ordinary and the worldly, and cultivates the five practices once more: the black lotus growing further stalk and leaf illustrates cultivating the holy practice once more; the lotus seed's own four subtle elements gradually increasing illustrates cultivating the heavenly practice once more; the lotus-leaf just beginning to sprout illustrates cultivating the brahma practice once more; the lotus seed falling into the mud illustrates matching every evil and cultivating the sick practice once more; the lotus sprout just beginning to bud illustrates matching every small good and cultivating the infant practice once more. Benefiting beings across the three time-periods in this way is beyond all reckoning, filling the whole dharma-realm completely, nothing that is not the benefit of emanation bodies, descending into the trace, opening the trace, and abolishing the trace, and so on. Without the lotus flower, how could every dharma discussed above possibly be illustrated so pervasively? Dharma and metaphor both made clear together — hence this sūtra is named the Wondrous Dharma Lotus Flower.

Reading note

The closing image reuses the very same reproductive cycle just given for the retinue — a fallen seed sprouting elsewhere, generation upon generation, until an entire pond is filled — but now applied one level up, to the Buddha's own repeated arising and ceasing across the three time-periods, cultivating the five practices anew each time "in accordance with the ordinary and the worldly." The treatise's own closing line makes explicit what has organized this entire "explaining the name" exposition since Fascicle 2: it is precisely because dharma and metaphor are argued out jointly, at equal length and with equal seriousness, that the sūtra earns its own title. This completes "Explaining Lotus Flower" (釋蓮華) and, with it, Explaining the Name (釋名) in its entirety — the first of the treatise's Five Sections, and the doctrinal core that has structured this treatise since Fascicle 1. This also closes Fascicle 7 of the source text in its entirety (both 卷第七上 and 卷第七下). Fascicle 8 continues by turning from the particular name just completed (別名, "wondrous dharma lotus flower") to the common name shared by every sūtra's own title (通名) — explaining the word "sūtra" (經) itself.
