The Perfect Teaching's Stage and the Three Tracks

Fascicle 5 closes the wondrous of stage with the perfect teaching's own "most real matter," then opens and completes the wondrous of the three tracks — real nature, contemplative illumination, and resource-and-accomplishment — the fifth of the ten wondrous aspects.

Status: Fascicle 5 of 10 complete Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

The Most Real Stage: The Perfect Teaching's Own Stage 明最實位・圓教位

Chinese Source

明最實位者，即圓教位也。此為十意：一、簡名義。二、明位數。三、明斷伏。四、明功用。五、明麁妙。六、明位興。七、明位廢。八、開麁顯妙。九、引經。十、妙位始終。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for clarifying the most real stage: this is precisely the perfect teaching's own stage. This is treated under ten headings: first, distinguishing the name and the sense; second, clarifying the number of stages; third, clarifying severing and subduing; fourth, clarifying its function; fifth, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous; sixth, clarifying the rise of the stage; seventh, clarifying the abolition of the stage; eighth, opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous; ninth, citing scripture; tenth, the wondrous stage from beginning to end.

Distinguishing the Name and the Sense 簡名義

Chinese Source

一、簡名義者，若圓別不同自有十意，下辨體中說。今約通、別、圓，三句料簡：一、名通義圓。二、名別義圓。三、名義俱圓。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, as for distinguishing the name and the sense: as to how the perfect differs from the distinct, this carries its own ten senses, explained below within the clarification of the Essence. Here, this is distinguished according to three statements — the shared, the distinct, and the perfect: first, the name shared while the sense is perfect; second, the name distinct while the sense is perfect; third, both the name and the sense perfect.

名通義圓者，下文云：「我等今日，真阿羅漢。普於其中，應受供養。」又云：「我等今日，真是聲聞，以佛道聲，令一切聞。」此名與通、藏同而義異。何者？彼但殺四住之賊，無明尚在，此不生義偏，故天女曰：「結習未盡，華則著身。」今殺通、別兩惑，得如來滅度，故殺賊義圓。又彼是分段不生，界外猶生。《寶性論》云：「二乘於無漏界，生三種意陰。」今則分段、變易二俱不生，不生義圓。彼是界內應供，非界外應供。《淨名》曰：「其供汝者，不名福田。」則應供義偏。今則普於其中應受供養，則應供義圓。彼但小乘，從他聞四諦聲，則聲偏、聞偏。今能令一切法界，聞一實四諦、佛道之聲，使一切聞，則聲聞義圓。故知依義不依語，從圓判位也。

As for the name being shared while the sense is perfect: the text below says, "We, today, are true arhats, and ought, everywhere within it, to receive offerings." And again: "We, today, are truly śrāvakas, and, by the very sound of the buddha-path, cause everyone to hear it." These names are the same as those of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings, yet their sense differs. Why is this? Those teachings' own arhat merely slays the thief of the four abiding afflictions, while ignorance still remains — this is a one-sided sense of "no longer arising"; hence the heavenly maiden says, "The habitual residue of the fetters not yet exhausted, the flowers still cling to the body." Here, however, one slays both the shared and distinct teachings' own confusion at once, attaining the Tathāgata's own extinction — hence, the sense of "slaying the thief" is perfect. Again, those teachings' own arhat is free of segmented birth-and-death, yet is still born beyond the three realms. The Ratnagotravibhāga says: "The two vehicles, within the uncontaminated realm, still give rise to three kinds of mental aggregates." Here, however, one is free of both segmented birth-and-death and transformational birth-and-death together — hence, the sense of "no longer arising" is perfect. Those teachings' own arhat is worthy of offerings only within the three realms, not worthy of offerings beyond them. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "Whoever makes offering to you is not, thereby, named a field of merit." This is a one-sided sense of "worthy of offerings." Here, however, one is everywhere within it worthy of receiving offerings — hence, the sense of "worthy of offerings" is perfect. Those teachings' own arhat is merely the Hīnayāna, hearing the sound of the Four Noble Truths from another — this is a one-sided sense of both "sound" and "hearing." Here, however, one is able to cause the entire dharma-realm to hear the sound of the one real Four Noble Truths, the very sound of the buddha-path, making everyone hear it — hence, the sense of "śrāvaka" itself is perfect. One should therefore know that this relies on the sense, not on the mere words, in judging the stage according to the perfect teaching.

名別義圓者，如五十二位，名與別同。而初、中、後位，圓融妙實，隨自意語，非是教道方便，依義不依語，應從圓判位也。

As for the name being distinct while the sense is perfect: as with the fifty-two stages, whose names are the same as the distinct teaching's own. Yet the initial, middle, and final stages are perfectly fused, wondrous, and real, spoken according to one's own intent rather than as the teaching's own expedient path — relying on the sense, not on the mere words, one ought to judge the stage according to the perfect teaching.

名義俱圓者，文云：「開示悟入，皆是佛之知見。」佛一切種智知、佛眼見，此之知見，無有缺減。又，入如來室，坐如來座，以如來莊嚴，此則名義俱圓，判於圓位也。

As for both the name and the sense being perfect: the text says, "Opening, showing, awakening, and entering are all alike the buddha's own knowledge and vision." Knowing through the buddha's own wisdom knowing all modes, seeing through the buddha-eye — this knowledge and vision lacks nothing at all, diminished in no respect. Again, entering the Tathāgata's room, sitting in the Tathāgata's seat, adorned with the Tathāgata's own adornment — this, then, is both the name and the sense perfect, judged as the perfect stage.

Reading note

Fascicle 5 opens the sixth and final stage of the "Medicinal Herbs" parable's six-stage scheme — the perfect teaching's own "most real matter," announced already in Fascicle 4. Zhiyi lays out a ten-part outline for the whole discussion before beginning, then opens with a three-way distinction (name shared but sense perfect / name borrowed from the distinct teaching but sense perfect / both name and sense perfect) that recurs as a methodological pattern throughout this treatise: the same word — arhat, śrāvaka, the fifty-two stages — can mean something categorically different depending on which teaching is speaking it, and the treatise insists on judging by sense rather than surface vocabulary. The three proof-texts illustrating "name shared, sense perfect" — the heavenly maiden's flowers sticking to the unliberated (from the *Vimalakīrti Sūtra*), the *Ratnagotravibhāga* on the two vehicles' subtle remaining birth, and the *Vimalakīrti Sūtra* again on who truly constitutes a field of merit — are all classic proof-texts for exactly this kind of "same word, deeper meaning" argument in Mahāyāna polemic.

Clarifying the Number of Stages 明位數

Chinese Source

二明位數者，又為三：一、明數。二、引證多少。三、料簡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, as for clarifying the number of stages: this, too, is of three parts — first, clarifying the number itself; second, citing evidence for how many there are; third, resolving doubts.

數者，人解不同。有言：頓悟即佛，無復位次之殊。引《思益》云：「如此學者，不從一地至一地。」又有師言：頓悟初心即究竟圓極，而有四十二位者，是化鈍根方便，立淺深之名耳。引《楞伽》云：「初地即二地，二地即三地，寂滅真如，有何次位？」又有師言：初頓悟至十住，即是十地；而說有十行、十迴向、十地者，此是重說耳。今謂諸解，悉是偏取。然平等法界，尚不論悟與不悟，孰辨淺深？既得論悟與不悟，何妨論於淺深？究竟大乘，無過《華嚴》、《大集》、《大品》、《法華》、《涅槃》，雖明法界平等、無說無示，而菩薩行位終自炳然。

As for the number itself: people's understandings differ. Some say: sudden awakening is itself buddhahood, with no further distinction of sequential stages at all — citing the Viśeṣacintibrahma-paripṛcchā Sūtra: "Such a student as this does not proceed from one ground to another ground." Others say: sudden awakening at the initial mind is itself the ultimate, perfect extreme, and the existence of forty-two stages is merely an expedient means for transforming the dull-faculty practitioner, establishing names of shallow and deep only for their sake — citing the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra: "The first ground is itself the second ground, the second ground is itself the third ground — within quiescent extinction and suchness itself, what sequential stage could there possibly be?" Still others say: the initial sudden awakening, up through the ten abidings, is itself already the ten grounds; and the further speaking of the ten practices, the ten dedications, and the ten grounds is merely a repeated restatement of the same thing. Here, it is held that every one of these interpretations is one-sided. Yet, within the equality of the dharma-realm itself, one does not even discuss awakened versus unawakened at all — how, then, could one possibly distinguish shallow from deep? But since one is already able to discuss awakened versus unawakened, what obstacle could there be to also discussing shallow versus deep? Among the ultimate Mahāyāna scriptures, none surpasses the Avataṃsaka, the Mahāsaṃnipāta, the Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra, the Lotus Sūtra, and the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra — and though these clarify the dharma-realm's own equality, beyond all speech and beyond all demonstration, the bodhisattva's own practice-stages remain, in the end, perfectly evident of themselves nonetheless.

又有人言：平等法界，定無次位。今例難此語：真諦有分別耶？真諦無分別耶？見真之者，判七賢、七聖、二十七賢聖等。今實相平等，雖無次位，見實相者，判次位何咎？《大論》云：「譬如入海，有始入者，到中者，至彼岸者。」若見真判位，如江河深淺，若實相判位，如入海深淺。故《普賢觀》云：「大乘因者，諸法實相。」大乘果者，亦諸法實相，論諸次位，非徒臆說。隨順契經，以四悉檀明位無妨，還約七種以明階位，謂十信、十住、十行、十迴向、十地、等覺、妙覺。

Again, some say: the equality of the dharma-realm admits, with certainty, of no sequential stage whatsoever. Here, this statement may be challenged by the same example: does the real truth admit of discrimination, or does the real truth admit of no discrimination at all? Those who see the real distinguish the seven worthies, the seven sages, the twenty-seven worthies and sages, and the rest. If the equality of the real mark, though admitting of no sequential stage, may nonetheless be distinguished into sequential stages by whoever sees the real mark — what fault could there possibly be in this? The Treatise says: "It is like entering the sea — there is the one who has only just entered, the one who has reached its middle, and the one who has arrived at the far shore." If one distinguishes stages in terms of seeing the real, this is like the differing depths of a river; if one distinguishes stages in terms of the real mark itself, this is like the differing depths of the sea itself. Hence the Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra says: "The cause of the Mahāyāna is the real mark of every dharma." The fruit of the Mahāyāna, too, is the real mark of every dharma — discussing these various sequential stages is not mere idle speculation. According with the scriptures themselves, clarifying the stages by means of the four siddhāntas presents no obstacle at all; here, too, the stage is clarified according to seven kinds, namely the ten faiths, the ten abidings, the ten practices, the ten dedications, the ten grounds, equal enlightenment, and wondrous enlightenment.

The Five Ranks, Before the Ten Faiths 十信之前・五品位

Chinese Source

今於十信之前，更明五品之位(云云)。若人宿殖深厚，或值善知識、或從經卷，圓聞妙理，謂一法一切法，一切法一法，非一非一切，不可思議，如前所說，起圓信解，信一心中具十法界，如一微塵有大千經卷。欲開此心而修圓行。圓行者，一行一切行。略言為十：謂識一念，平等具足，不可思議。傷己昏沈，慈及一切。又知此心，常寂常照。用寂照心，破一切法，即空、即假、即中。又識一心諸心，若通、若塞。能於此心，具足道品，向菩提路。又解此心，正、助之法。又識己心，及凡、聖心。又安心不動、不墮、不退、不散。雖識一心，無量功德，不生染著，十心成就。舉要言之，其心念念悉與諸波羅蜜相應，是名圓教初隨喜品位。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, before the ten faiths, the stage of the five ranks is further clarified, and so on. If a person's past cultivation has been deep and abundant, whether through encountering a good spiritual friend or through a scriptural text, hearing the perfect, wondrous principle — that one dharma is every dharma, that every dharma is one dharma, that it is neither one nor every, inconceivable, exactly as explained before — one gives rise to perfect faith and understanding, believing that the single mind is already complete with all ten dharma-realms, just as a single mote of dust contains within it a scroll as vast as the great trichiliocosm itself. Wishing to open this mind and cultivate the perfect practice — the perfect practice being one in which a single practice is every practice — this may be stated briefly as ten: knowing this single thought to be, of itself, equally complete, inconceivable; grieving over one's own confusion and torpor, extending loving-kindness to all; further knowing that this mind is ever quiescent and ever illuminating; using this mind of quiescent illumination to break through every dharma, at once empty, at once provisional, at once the middle; further knowing that, within this single mind, every mind is either penetrating or obstructed; being able, within this mind, to become complete with the factors of the path, turning toward the road of bodhi; further understanding, within this mind, the correct and auxiliary dharmas; further knowing one's own mind, and the mind of the ordinary being and of the sage alike; further settling the mind so that it is unmoving, unfalling, unretreating, and unscattered. Though one knows that this single mind possesses immeasurable merit and virtue, one gives rise to no defiled attachment toward it — the ten minds are thereby accomplished. To state the essential point: this mind, thought after thought, corresponds entirely to every pāramitā — this is named the perfect teaching's own initial rank of sympathetic joy.

行者圓信始生，善須將養。若涉事紛動，令道芽破敗。唯得內修理觀，外則受持、讀誦大乘經典，聞有助觀之力。內外相藉，圓信轉明，十心堅固。《金剛般若》云：「一日三時，以恒河沙身布施，不如受持一句功德。」初品觀智如目，次品讀誦如日。日有光故，目見種種色。《論》云：「於實名了因，於餘名生因。福不趣菩提，二能趣菩提。」聞有巨益，意在於此，是名第二品位。

The practitioner, this perfect faith just having arisen, must skillfully nourish and sustain it. Should one become entangled in worldly affairs and their agitation, this would cause the sprout of the path to break and wither. One should therefore only cultivate the contemplation of principle inwardly, while, outwardly, upholding, reading, and reciting the Mahāyāna scriptures, since hearing them carries a power that assists this contemplation. Inward and outward mutually reinforcing one another, this perfect faith grows ever more distinct, and the ten minds become firm and secure. The Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā says: "Giving one's own body as gifts, as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, three times a day, does not equal the merit of upholding a single phrase." The first rank's own contemplative wisdom is like the eye; the second rank's own reading and reciting is like the sun — since the sun possesses light, the eye is thereby able to see every manner of form. The Treatise says: "With respect to the real, this is named the revealing cause; with respect to what remains, this is named the generating cause. Merit alone does not proceed toward bodhi; only these two together are able to proceed toward bodhi." The great benefit of hearing the scriptures lies precisely in this sense — this is named the stage of the second rank.

行者內觀轉強，外資又著，圓解在懷，弘誓熏動，更加說法，如實演布。《安樂行》云：「但以大乘法答。設以方便隨宜，終令悟大。」《淨名》云：「說法淨，則智慧淨。」《毘曇》云：「說法解脫，聽法解脫。」說法開導，是前人得道全因緣，化功歸己十心則三倍轉明，是名第三品位。

As the practitioner's own inward contemplation grows stronger still, and outward support grows firmer as well, perfect understanding held within, the universal vow stirred and moved, one further takes up teaching the dharma, proclaiming and spreading it exactly as it truly is. The Peaceful Practices chapter says: "Answering only by means of the Mahāyāna dharma; and, even where skillful means are employed as fitting, in the end always causing awakening to the great vehicle." The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "Where the teaching of the dharma is pure, wisdom, too, is pure." The Abhidharma says: "Teaching the dharma is liberation; hearing the dharma is liberation." Teaching the dharma to guide others is the very complete cause and condition by which another person attains the path, and, since this transformative merit returns to oneself as well, the ten minds accordingly grow three times more distinct — this is named the stage of the third rank.

上來前熟觀心，未遑涉事。今正觀稍明，即傍兼利物。能以少施，與虛空法界等。使一切法趣檀，檀為法界。《大品》云：「菩薩少施，超過聲聞、辟支佛上，當學般若。」即此意也。餘五亦如是。事相雖少，運懷甚大。此則理觀為正，事行為傍，故言兼行布施。事福資理，則十心彌盛，是名第四品位。

Up to this point, one has first thoroughly cultivated the contemplation of mind, without yet having leisure to engage in worldly affairs. Now, as this correct contemplation grows somewhat more distinct, one incidentally also takes up benefiting others. Being able, through even a small act of giving, to equal empty space and the dharma-realm itself, causing every dharma to converge upon giving, giving itself becoming the dharma-realm. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra says: "The bodhisattva's small act of giving surpasses even the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha — one should study prajñā" — this is precisely this same sense. The remaining five pāramitās are exactly the same. Though the outward phenomenal act is small, the mind it carries is vast indeed. Here, the contemplation of principle remains the primary practice, and worldly action remains only incidental to it — hence, this is called "incidentally practicing giving." As this worldly merit nourishes principle, the ten minds grow ever more abundant — this is named the stage of the fourth rank.

行人圓觀稍熟，理事欲融，涉事不妨理，在理不隔事，故具行六度。若布施時，無二邊取著，十法界依、正，一捨一切捨，財、身及命，無畏等施。若持戒時，性重、譏嫌等無差別；五部重輕，無所觸犯。若行忍時，生、法寂滅，荷負安耐。若行精進，身心俱淨，無間無退。若行禪時，遊入諸禪，靜散無妨。若修慧時，權實二智，究了通達；乃至世智，治生產業，皆與實相不相違背。具足解釋佛之知見，而於正觀，如火益薪。此是第五品位。

The practitioner's own perfect contemplation, growing somewhat mature, causes principle and worldly action to fuse together — engaging in worldly action no longer obstructs principle, and abiding in principle no longer sets itself apart from worldly action; hence, one now practices the six pāramitās directly and completely. When giving, one is free of attachment to either extreme; the dependent and direct retributions across all ten dharma-realms, relinquishing one is relinquishing all — wealth, body, and life itself, and the giving of fearlessness, are all given equally. When upholding the precepts, there is no distinction between grave inherent wrong and what the world finds objectionable; the grave and light offenses across the five divisions of the vinaya are none of them ever transgressed. When practicing patience, birth and dharmas alike are seen as quiescent extinction, bearing every burden with calm endurance. When practicing diligence, body and mind alike are purified, without interruption and without retreat. When practicing dhyāna, one roams freely into every dhyāna, whether stillness or dispersal presenting no obstacle. When cultivating wisdom, both the provisional and the real wisdom are thoroughly penetrated and understood; even worldly wisdom itself, and one's own livelihood and occupation, are none of them ever in conflict with the real mark. Complete with the full explanation of the buddha's own knowledge and vision, and with respect to correct contemplation, this is like fire fed further with firewood. This is the stage of the fifth rank.

Comparing the Five Ranks 五品位料簡

Chinese Source

如此五品圓信功德，東西八方不可為喻。雖是初心，而勝聲聞無學功德，具如經說。若欲比決取解，類如三藏家別、總四念處位。義推如通教乾慧地位，亦如伏忍位。義推亦得是別教十信位(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

The merit and virtue of this perfect faith across these five ranks cannot be compared to anything in the eight directions of east and west alike. Though this is still the initial mind, it already surpasses the merit and virtue of the śrāvaka beyond further learning — set out fully as the sūtra itself explains. If one wishes to compare this against other schemes to aid understanding, it is comparable to the Tripiṭaka teaching's own particular and general applications of mindfulness; reasoned by sense, it is like the shared teaching's own ground of dry wisdom, and likewise like the stage of subduing forbearance; reasoned by sense, it may also be taken as the distinct teaching's own stage of the ten faiths, and so on.

私謂：五品位是圓家方便，初欲令易解準小望大，如三藏之五停心。初品圓信法界，上信諸佛，下信眾生，皆起隨喜，是圓家慈停心，遍對治法界上嫉妬。第二品讀誦大乘文字，文字是法身氣命，讀誦明利是圓家數息停心，遍治法界上覺觀。說法品能自淨心，亦淨他心，是圓家因緣停心，遍治法界上自、他癡。癡去故，諸行去乃至老死去，兼行六度品，是圓家不淨停心。六蔽初名貪欲，若捨貪欲，欲因、欲果皆捨。捨故無復報身，非淨非不淨也。正行六度品，是圓家念佛停心。正行六度時，即事而理，理不妨道，事妨於道。即事而理，無障可論。大意如此(云云)。

In my own view: the stage of the five ranks is the perfect teaching's own expedient means, meant at the outset to make understanding easier by measuring the small against the great, just as the Tripiṭaka teaching has its own five methods of stilling the mind. The first rank's perfect faith in the dharma-realm — believing upward in every buddha, believing downward in every sentient being, giving rise to sympathetic joy toward both alike — is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through loving-kindness, universally treating jealousy and envy throughout the dharma-realm. The second rank's reading and reciting of the Mahāyāna's own text — the text being the very vital breath of the Dharma-body — reading and reciting it with clarity and sharpness is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through counting the breath, universally treating applied and sustained thought throughout the dharma-realm. The rank of teaching the dharma, able to purify one's own mind and likewise purify the minds of others, is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through causes and conditions, universally treating delusion, both one's own and others', throughout the dharma-realm. Since delusion departs, every formation departs, on through old age and death departing as well; the rank of incidentally practicing the six pāramitās is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through the contemplation of impurity. The first of the six coverings is named craving desire; if one relinquishes craving desire, both its cause and its fruit are relinquished together. Since this is relinquished, no further retribution-body remains — neither pure nor impure. The rank of directly practicing the six pāramitās is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through mindfulness of the Buddha. When directly practicing the six pāramitās, the very phenomenon itself is already principle; principle presents no obstacle to the path, while worldly phenomena alone obstruct the path — yet, since the very phenomenon itself is already principle, there remains no obstacle whatsoever to discuss. This is, in general outline, the sense of it, and so on.

The five ranks (五品位) are among the most celebrated schemes in all of Tiantai practice — Zhiyi's own answer to what an ordinary, still-unenlightened practitioner in this present age can actually do: sympathetic joy (perfect faith alone, before any external practice), reading and reciting the Mahāyāna scriptures, teaching the dharma to others, incidentally practicing the six pāramitās, and finally practicing the six pāramitās directly, principle and worldly action now fully fused. Zhiyi's own "personal view" closing this section maps all five ranks onto the Tripiṭaka teaching's five methods of stilling the mind one-to-one — a deliberate small-to-great pedagogical bridge, since the five methods of stilling the mind were the very first, most elementary stage translated at the start of Fascicle 4's "middle medicinal herb." The five ranks precede the ten faiths in the perfect teaching's own count, and answer the "how many stages" survey above by showing that even the earliest rungs of the perfect ladder already outstrip the arhat's own final attainment in the lesser teachings.

The Ten Faiths 十信位

Chinese Source

The Renwang Prajñāpāramitā and the Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra are cited as before. The text below, on entering the Tathāgata's room, seat, and robe, and the rest, is precisely cultivating the four peaceful practices' own sphere of action and sphere of intimacy. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "There is, further, a single practice that is the Tathāgata's own practice, namely, the Mahāyāna." The Treatise says: "The bodhisattva, from the very first arousing of the mind, already contemplates nirvāṇa while walking the path. The Ten Abidings 十住位

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the stage of the ten abidings: proceeding from the resembling understanding of the ten faiths, one is thereby able to enter the ten abidings' own real wisdom of the middle. At the very moment the initial abiding of the arousing of the mind is brought forth, three kinds of mind are brought forth together: first, the wholesome mind of the conditioning cause; second, the wisdom-mind of the revealing cause; third, the mind of principle of the correct cause — this is precisely the threefold opening and unfolding of the object, wisdom, and practice discussed before. "Abiding" means abiding in the nirvāṇa of the three virtues. The mind of the conditioning cause brought forth is precisely abiding in the inconceivable liberation, the Śūraṅgama samādhi; the mind of wisdom brought forth is precisely abiding in the great prajñā's own ultimate emptiness; the mind of the correct cause brought forth is precisely abiding in the real mark's own Dharma-body, the middle way's own ultimate meaning. To state the essential point: this is precisely abiding in the three virtues, every buddha-dharma at once.

又住清淨圓滿菩提心，無緣慈悲，無作誓願，普覆法界。又住一念中，成就一切萬行諸波羅蜜。又住一切種智，圓斷法界見、思、無明。又住得佛眼，圓見十法界三諦之法。又住圓入一切法門，所謂二十五三昧，冥益眾生。又成就菩薩圓滿業，能顯一切神通，謂三輪不思議化彌滿法界，顯益眾生。又能成就開權顯實，入一乘道。又能嚴淨一切佛土，能起三業，供養一切十方佛，得圓滿陀羅尼，受持一切佛法，如雲持雨。又住能從一地，具足一切諸地功德，心心寂滅，自然流入薩婆若海。

Again, one abides in the pure and perfectly complete bodhi-mind, the compassion that takes no object, the unconditioned vow, universally covering the entire dharma-realm. Again, one abides within a single thought, accomplishing every one of the myriad practices, every pāramitā. Again, one abides in the wisdom knowing all modes, perfectly severing the views, thought, and ignorance of the entire dharma-realm. Again, one abides in attaining the buddha-eye, perfectly seeing the dharma of the three truths across all ten dharma-realms. Again, one abides in perfectly entering every dharma-gate, namely the twenty-five samādhis, secretly benefiting sentient beings. Again, one accomplishes the bodhisattva's own perfectly complete karma, able to manifest every spiritual power, namely the inconceivable transformation of the three wheels, filling the entire dharma-realm, manifestly benefiting sentient beings. Again, one is able to accomplish opening the provisional to reveal the real, entering the path of the one vehicle. Again, one is able to adorn and purify every buddha-land, able to raise up the three karmic activities, making offering to every buddha of the ten directions, attaining the perfectly complete dhāraṇī, upholding every buddha-dharma, like a cloud holding rain. Again, one abides in being able, from a single ground, to become complete with the merit and virtue of every ground at once, thought after thought quiescent and extinct, flowing naturally into the ocean of omniscience itself.

《華嚴》云：「初住菩薩所有功德，三世諸佛歎不能盡。若具足說，凡人聞，迷亂心發狂。」私謂：初住成就十德，應是十信中十法，轉似為真，一住具十。細意尋之，對當相應。何者？十信百法為一切法本，豈不得作此釋耶？初住既爾，三觀現前，無功用心斷法界無量品無明，不可稱計。一往大分，略為十品智斷，即是十住故。《仁王》云：「入理般若名為住。」即是十番進發無漏，同見中道佛性第一義理。以不住法，從淺至深，住佛三德及一切佛法，故名十住位。

The Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "The merit and virtue possessed by the bodhisattva of the initial abiding cannot be exhausted even by the buddhas of the three times praising it; were it explained in full, an ordinary person hearing it would become confused and deranged, their mind driven mad." In my own view: the ten virtues accomplished at the initial abiding ought to correspond to the ten dharmas among the ten faiths, turned from resembling to real, a single abiding already complete with all ten. Examined carefully, they correspond exactly to one another. Why is this? Since the ten faiths' own hundred dharmas are already the very root of every dharma, how could this correlation not be made? The initial abiding being thus, the three contemplations manifest before one, and the mind of effortless function severs the immeasurable grades of ignorance throughout the dharma-realm, beyond all reckoning or calculation. Taken broadly as a whole, this is, in brief, divided into ten grades of wisdom-and-severance, and this is precisely why there are ten abidings. The Renwang Jing says: "Entering the prajñā of principle is named abiding" — this is precisely the tenfold advance in bringing forth the uncontaminated, alike seeing the middle way's own buddha-nature, the principle of ultimate meaning. Since one no longer abides in any dharma, proceeding from the shallow to the deep, one abides in the buddha's own three virtues and in every buddha-dharma — hence, this is named the stage of the ten abidings.

此位，諸經出處不同。《華嚴》云：「初發心時，便成正覺。」了達諸法真實之性，所有聞法，不由他悟。是菩薩成就十種智力，究竟離虛妄，無染如虛空。清淨妙法身，湛然應一切，當知即是發真無漏，斷無明初品也。《淨名》云：「一念知一切法，是為坐道場，成就一切智故。」亦是入不二法門，得無生忍也。《大品》明「從初發心，即坐道場、轉法輪、度眾生。」當知，此菩薩為如佛，亦是阿字門，所謂一切法初不生也。即是今經為令眾生開佛知見，亦是龍女於剎那頃發菩提心，成等正覺。即是《涅槃》明發心、畢竟二不別，如是二心，前心難。此諸大乘，悉明圓初發心住位也。乃至第十住(云云)。

This stage is set out differently in the different sūtras' own sources. The Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "At the very moment the mind is first aroused, one thereby already accomplishes true and correct enlightenment" — fully penetrating the real, true nature of every dharma, whatever dharma one hears, one awakens to it without relying on another. This bodhisattva accomplishes the ten kinds of power of wisdom, ultimately free of every falsehood, untainted like empty space itself; the pure, wondrous Dharma-body, tranquil and clear, responds to everything — one should know that this is precisely bringing forth the real, the uncontaminated, severing the first grade of ignorance. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "Knowing every dharma within a single thought is precisely sitting at the very seat of enlightenment, since it accomplishes the wisdom that knows all things" — this is likewise entering the gate of non-duality, attaining the forbearance of no-generation. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra clarifies: "From the very first arousing of the mind, one already sits at the seat of enlightenment, turns the wheel of the Dharma, and saves sentient beings." One should know that this bodhisattva is equivalent to a buddha, and is likewise the gate of the letter "a" — namely, that every dharma, from the very outset, never arises at all. This is precisely why the present sūtra causes sentient beings to open the buddha's own knowledge and vision, and is likewise why the dragon girl, in the space of a single instant, arouses the bodhi-mind and accomplishes perfect, complete enlightenment. This is precisely what the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra clarifies as the arousing of the mind and its ultimate attainment being, in the end, not two different things — though, of these two minds, the first mind is the more difficult. All of these Mahāyāna scriptures alike clarify the perfect teaching's own initial abiding of the arousing of the mind, on through the tenth abiding, and so on.

Reading note

The initial abiding (初住) is where the perfect teaching's practitioner first genuinely breaks through ignorance and sees the middle way directly — the moment the *Avataṃsaka Sūtra*'s own famous line "at the very moment the mind is first aroused, one thereby already accomplishes true and correct enlightenment" describes, and the moment the *Lotus Sūtra*'s own dragon girl passage (from the "Devadatta" chapter) dramatizes as instantaneous buddhahood. Zhiyi is careful to qualify this: the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s own point that "the arousing of the mind and its ultimate attainment are not two different things" is true, yet "of these two minds, the first mind is the more difficult" — the initial breakthrough, not the long unfolding after it, is the genuinely hard step. The correlation of the ten abidings' own ten virtues with the ten faiths' own ten minds (resembling turned real, "a single abiding already complete with all ten") repeats the same pattern already used to correlate the five ranks with the five methods of stilling the mind.

Chinese Source

三、明十行位者，即是從十住後，實相真明，不可思議。更十番智斷，破十品無明，一行一切行，念念進趣，流入平等法界海。諸波羅蜜任運生長，自行、化他，功德與虛空等，故名十行位也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the stage of the ten practices: proceeding from the ten abidings onward, the real mark's own real clarity becomes inconceivable. Undergoing ten further rounds of wisdom-and-severance, breaking through ten grades of ignorance, a single practice is every practice, advancing thought after thought, flowing into the ocean of the equality of the dharma-realm. Every pāramitā arises and grows of its own accord, one's own practice and the transformation of others alike, their merit and virtue equal to empty space itself — hence, this is named the stage of the ten practices.

As for the stage of the ten dedications: this is precisely the path of effortless function following the ten practices — the inconceivable real clarity opening and unfolding the entire dharma-realm thought after thought, the vow and its practice, phenomena and principle, naturally fused together, turning to enter the ocean of the equality of the dharma-realm. Realizing ten further rounds of wisdom-and-severance, breaking through ten grades of ignorance, this is accordingly named dedication.

十地位者，即是無漏真明入無功用道，猶如大地能生一切佛法，荷負法界眾生，普入三世佛地。又證十番智斷，破十品無明，故名十地位也。

As for the stage of the ten grounds: this is precisely the uncontaminated real clarity entering the path of effortless function, just like the great earth itself, able to give rise to every buddha-dharma, bearing the burden of every sentient being throughout the dharma-realm, universally entering the buddha-ground of the three times. Severing the last, most subtle ignorance at the very source itself, one ascends to the very summit of the mountain of the middle way, taking leave at last of ignorance, one's own father and mother — this is named one who still has something left to sever, and is named a superior person still with something above.

七、明妙覺地者，究竟解脫無上佛智故，言無所斷者，名無上士。此即三德，不縱不橫，究竟後心，大涅槃也。一切大：理大、誓願大、莊嚴大、智斷大、遍知大、道大、用大、權實大、利益大、無住大。即是前十觀成乘，圓極竟在於佛，過茶無字可說(云云)。故盧舍那佛名為淨滿，一切皆滿也。

Seventh, clarifying the ground of wondrous enlightenment: since this is the ultimate liberation, the unsurpassed wisdom of the buddha, this is spoken of as one with nothing further left to sever, and is named the unsurpassed person, with nothing above. This is precisely the three virtues, neither vertically arranged nor horizontally arranged, the ultimate, final mind, the great nirvāṇa itself. Every kind of greatness is present: the greatness of principle, the greatness of the vow, the greatness of adornment, the greatness of wisdom-and-severance, the greatness of complete knowledge, the greatness of the path, the greatness of function, the greatness of the provisional and the real, the greatness of benefit, and the greatness of non-abiding. This is precisely the ten contemplations discussed earlier accomplishing the vehicle, perfect and ultimate at last in the buddha himself, beyond the letter "dha," with no further letter left to speak of, and so on. Hence Vairocana Buddha is named "pure and full" — everything, without exception, is thereby fulfilled.

Reading note

This completes the perfect teaching's own seven-stage ladder in outline — ten faiths, ten abidings, ten practices, ten dedications, ten grounds, equal enlightenment, wondrous enlightenment — each of the last four stages severing ten further grades of ignorance in turn, for forty grades total across the four "ten"-stages plus equal enlightenment's own final, subtlest grade. "Neither vertically nor horizontally arranged" recalls the Siddhaṃ-letter-*i* image already used in Fascicle 3 for the three virtues; "beyond the letter 'dha,' with no further letter to speak of" recalls the fifty-letter analogy just used in Fascicle 4 to explain why even the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s own nirvāṇa was still only the bodhisattva's practice — here, reaching that same final letter is what wondrous enlightenment itself actually is. What follows next turns to how many stages there really are — cross-checking this same seven-stage count against further scriptural evidence, including the famous "moon-love samādhi" and "forty-two letters" correlations.

Citing Further Scripture: The Moon-Love Samādhi and the Forty-Two Letters 引眾經明位數・月愛三昧・四十二字門

Chinese Source

二次引眾經，明位數多少者，《大涅槃》云：「月愛三昧，從初一日至十五日，光色漸漸增長。又從十六日至三十日，光色漸漸損減。」光色增長，譬十五智德，摩訶般若。光色漸減，譬十五斷德，無累解脫。三十心為三智斷，十地為十智斷，等覺、妙覺各為一智斷，合十五智斷，月體譬法身。《大經》云：「月性常圓，實無增減。」因須彌山，故有虧盈。不增而增，白月漸著；不減而減，黑月稍無。法身亦爾，實無智斷，因無明故，約如論智，如實不智；約如論斷，如實不斷。雖無智而智，般若漸漸明；雖無斷而斷，解脫漸漸離。舉月為喻，知是圓教智斷位也。《大經》云：「從初安置諸子祕密之藏，三德涅槃，然後我當於此祕藏而般涅槃。」此即最後智斷也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, next citing many sūtras to clarify how many stages there really are: the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The moon-love samādhi: from the first day up through the fifteenth day, the moon's own light and color gradually increase and grow; and, from the sixteenth day up through the thirtieth day, the moon's own light and color gradually diminish and wane." The increase and growth of light and color is a metaphor for the fifteen virtues of wisdom, the great prajñā; the gradual diminishing of light and color is a metaphor for the fifteen virtues of severance, liberation free of all encumbrance. The three sets of ten minds count as three units of wisdom-and-severance; the ten grounds count as ten units of wisdom-and-severance; equal enlightenment and wondrous enlightenment each count as a single unit of wisdom-and-severance — together, fifteen units of wisdom-and-severance in all. The very body of the moon itself is a metaphor for the Dharma-body. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The moon's own nature is ever full and round; in reality, it neither increases nor decreases at all." It is only because of Mount Sumeru that it appears to wax and wane. Without truly increasing, it increases — the bright moon growing gradually more visible; without truly decreasing, it decreases — the dark moon growing gradually less visible. The Dharma-body is exactly the same: in reality, it possesses no wisdom and no severance at all; it is only on account of ignorance that, spoken of in terms of suchness, wisdom is discussed — though, in reality, suchness is not wisdom at all; and, spoken of in terms of suchness, severance is discussed — though, in reality, suchness has nothing at all to sever. Though there is no wisdom, wisdom is spoken of nonetheless, and prajñā grows gradually more distinct; though there is no severance, severance is spoken of nonetheless, and liberation grows gradually more complete. Raising up the moon as a metaphor, one thereby knows this to be the perfect teaching's own stage of wisdom-and-severance. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Having, from the very outset, placed my disciples within the secret treasury, the nirvāṇa of the three virtues, only afterward shall I myself enter parinirvāṇa within this same secret treasury" — this is precisely the final wisdom-and-severance.

問：何得知月喻譬位耶？答：《仁王》明十四忍：三十心為三般若，十地為十般若，等覺為一般若，十四般若在菩薩心中，皆名為忍。轉至佛心，名之為智。此與十五日明智位同。《勝天王》明十四般若位，正用十四日月為譬。故作此釋也。

Question: How can one know that the moon simile is truly a metaphor for the stages? Answer: The Renwang Jing clarifies fourteen forbearances: the three sets of ten minds count as three kinds of prajñā; the ten grounds count as ten kinds of prajñā; equal enlightenment counts as a single kind of prajñā — these fourteen kinds of prajñā, while still present within the bodhisattva's own mind, are all alike named "forbearance"; only once turned into the buddha's own mind are they named "wisdom." This corresponds exactly to the fifteen-day waxing period clarifying the stage of wisdom just discussed. The Śrīmahādevī Prajñāpāramitā clarifies the fourteen stages of prajñā, using precisely the fourteen days of the moon's own waxing as its own metaphor — hence, this same interpretation is made here.

《大品》明四十二字門，語等、字等，南岳師云：「此是諸佛密語，何必不表四十二位？」諸學人執《釋論》，云無此解，多疑不用。但《論》本文千卷，什師作九倍略之，何必無此解耶？今謂：此解深應冥會。何者？《經》云：「初阿後茶，中有四十。初阿字門，具四十一字；後茶亦爾。」《華嚴》云：「從初一地具足一切諸地功德。」此義即同。《經》云：「若聞阿字門，則解一切義，所謂諸法初不生故。」此豈非圓教初住，初得無生法忍？過茶無字可說，豈非妙覺無上、無過？〈廣乘品〉明一切法皆是摩訶衍竟，即說四十二字門，豈非圓教菩薩從初發心，得諸法實相、具一切佛法，故名阿字；至妙覺地，窮一切法底，故名茶字。此義，其數與圓位，甚自分明。又，四十二字後，即說菩薩十地，此是顯別教方便之次位也。又，次十地之後，說三乘共十地，此顯通教方便位也。經文次比，三義宛然。今取四十二字，以證圓位也。

The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra clarifies the gate of the forty-two letters, where speech is equal and letters are equal; the master of Nanyue says: "This is the secret language of every buddha — why must it not represent the forty-two stages?" The various students who cling to the Treatise, saying no such interpretation exists there, mostly doubt this and refuse to accept it. Yet the Treatise's own original text runs to a thousand fascicles, and Master Kumārajīva condensed it down to a ninth of its length — how could one be certain no such interpretation exists within it? Here, it is held that this interpretation deeply and rightly accords with the truth. Why is this? The sūtra says: "Beginning with 'a' and ending with 'dha,' there are forty in between; the initial letter 'a' gate is already complete with all forty-one letters, and the final letter 'dha' is exactly the same." The Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "From the very first ground onward, one is already complete with the merit and virtue of every ground" — this sense is exactly the same. The sūtra says: "If one hears the gate of the letter 'a,' one thereby understands every meaning, namely that every dharma, from the very outset, never arises at all" — is this not precisely the perfect teaching's own initial abiding, first attaining the forbearance of the dharma of no-generation? And beyond the letter "dha," with no further letter left to speak of — is this not precisely wondrous enlightenment itself, unsurpassed, with nothing beyond it? The "Chapter on the Vast Vehicle" clarifies that every dharma is, in its entirety, the Mahāyāna, and only then speaks of the gate of the forty-two letters — is this not precisely the perfect teaching's own bodhisattva, from the very first arousing of the mind, attaining the real mark of every dharma, complete with every buddha-dharma, and hence named the letter "a"; and, reaching the ground of wondrous enlightenment, exhausting the very depths of every dharma, and hence named the letter "dha"? This sense, and this number, correspond to the perfect stage with perfect clarity of themselves. Again, after the forty-two letters, the bodhisattva's own ten grounds are spoken of next — this reveals the distinct teaching's own expedient sequence of stages. And, next, after these same ten grounds, the three vehicles' own shared ten grounds are spoken of — this reveals the shared teaching's own expedient stage. The sūtra's own text places these in this very sequence, and the three senses stand out clearly of themselves. Here, the forty-two letters are taken as proof of the perfect stage.

Reading note

Two of Tiantai literature's most famous numerological proof-passages appear back to back here. The moon-love samādhi reads the waxing and waning of a single lunar month as a metaphor for wisdom and severance across the same fifteen units already derived from the seven-stage count (three sets of ten minds + ten grounds + equal enlightenment + wondrous enlightenment), with the crucial caveat, drawn directly from the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra* itself, that the moon "in reality neither increases nor decreases at all" — the Dharma-body, like the moon, only appears to gain wisdom and lose affliction from the standpoint of ignorance, which is itself unreal. The forty-two letters (the *arapacana* Sanskrit syllabary, from "a" to "dha") are defended against skeptics who doubted Nanyue Huisi's own reading of them as secretly encoding the forty-two stages, on the grounds that Kumārajīva's translation of the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra* condensed a thousand-fascicle original ninefold — an argument from textual compression rather than direct citation. Both passages converge independently on the same forty-two-stage count (ten faiths not included, since the letters begin only at the initial abiding) that the seven-stage scheme and the moon simile already gave.

The Lotus Sūtra's Own Evidence, and a Caution Against Rigid Literalism 引今經證・料簡

Chinese Source

此經〈分別功德品〉明初心五品弟子之位，文甚分明。〈法師功德品〉明六根清淨相。〈方便品〉云：「諸佛為一大事因緣故，出現於世，為令眾生開佛知見。」四句，南岳師解云：「開佛知見是十住位；示佛知見是十行位；悟佛知見是十迴向位；入佛知見是十地等覺位。皆言佛知者，得一切種智也。皆言佛見者，悉得佛眼也。」又《經》云：「是為諸佛一大事因緣」者，同入一乘諸法實相也。又云：「唯佛與佛，乃能究盡諸法實相」者，即是妙覺位也。

Ru-Yi Translation

This present sūtra's own "Distinctions in Merit" chapter clarifies the initial-mind stage of the five ranks of disciples, its own text entirely clear on this point. The "Merits of the Dharma Teacher" chapter clarifies the mark of the purification of the six sense-faculties. The "Skillful Means" chapter says: "Every buddha, on account of the one great cause and condition, appears in the world, in order to cause sentient beings to open the buddha's own knowledge and vision." The master of Nanyue explains these four phrases: "Opening the buddha's own knowledge and vision is the stage of the ten abidings; showing the buddha's own knowledge and vision is the stage of the ten practices; awakening to the buddha's own knowledge and vision is the stage of the ten dedications; entering the buddha's own knowledge and vision is the stage of the ten grounds and equal enlightenment. Wherever 'the buddha's own knowledge' is spoken of, this is attaining the wisdom knowing all modes; wherever 'the buddha's own vision' is spoken of, this is attaining the buddha-eye in full." Again, the sūtra says, "this is every buddha's own one great cause and condition," meaning alike entering the one vehicle's own real mark of every dharma. Again, it says, "only a buddha together with a buddha is able to fathom the real mark of every dharma to its very end," meaning precisely the stage of wondrous enlightenment.

又〈譬喻品〉，諸子門外索車，長者各賜等一大車。是時諸子乘是寶乘，遊於四方。嬉戲快樂，自在無礙，直至道場。言四方者，即譬開、示、悟、入四十位也。直至道場，即是究盡實相，妙覺位也。〈序品〉中，天雨四華，表此四十因位也。如上所引，眾經為證。及引今文，明四十二位炳然，皆是無次位之次位，達於實相，增道損生，論次位耳。

Again, in the "Parable" chapter, the children, seeking carriages outside the gate, are each given by the elder an equally great carriage; at that time, the children, riding upon this treasure-vehicle, roam throughout the four directions, sporting joyfully, free and unobstructed, arriving directly at the very seat of enlightenment. Speaking of "the four directions" is precisely a metaphor for the forty stages of opening, showing, awakening, and entering; "arriving directly at the seat of enlightenment" is precisely fathoming the real mark to its very end, the stage of wondrous enlightenment. In the "Introduction" chapter, the heavens rain down four kinds of flowers, representing these same forty causal stages. As cited above, the many sūtras together provide the proof; and, citing the present text as well, the forty-two stages are made perfectly clear — all of them being the sequential stage of no sequential stage at all, reaching the real mark, while yet discussing sequential stages in terms of advancing the path and diminishing birth.

Resolving Doubts 料簡

Chinese Source

三、料簡者。問：無明覆佛性中道，止作四十二品斷耶？答：無明雖無所有，不有而有，不無階品。一往大分為四十二品。然其品數無量無邊。《大論》云：「無明品類，其數甚多，是故處處說破無明三昧。」又云：「法愛難盡，處處重說般若也。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, resolving doubts. Question: Does the ignorance that covers buddha-nature and the middle way only admit of being severed into these forty-two grades? Answer: Though ignorance possesses nothing at all, it is, without truly possessing being, nonetheless present, and does not lack its own gradations. Taken broadly as a whole, it is divided into forty-two grades; yet the true number of its grades is immeasurable and boundless. The Treatise says: "The categories of ignorance are so numerous that, for this very reason, the samādhi that breaks through ignorance is spoken of again and again, in one place after another." It also says: "Attachment to the dharma is difficult to exhaust completely, and so prajñā is taught again and again, in one place after another."

此諸圓位，不可思議。若專對法門，尋者失意，多別解、別執，則乖圓融之道。如此等位，莫以凡情局取，不以凡心能宣。《華嚴》云：「諸地不可說，何況以示人！」且置是事。若大乘懺悔，發初隨喜圓信之心，獲一旋陀羅尼，已不可向人說；雖種種分別，亦不可解。況後諸位，二乘尚不聞其名，豈凡人能說？此語有意，大師自說己證也。又且置是事：聲聞學四念處，發得煗法，亦不可向外凡說盡。設種種解，亦不能知。又置是事。如人坐禪，初發五支，不可為未證者說。設方便說，彼亦不解。又置是事。斲輪人不能以其術授其子，況諸深法而可說耶？

All of these perfect stages are inconceivable. If one insists on correlating them rigidly with specific dharma-gates, one who searches for them in this way will lose their true sense, falling into one separate interpretation and rigid attachment after another, thereby departing from the very path of perfect fusion. With stages such as these, one must never grasp at them narrowly with an ordinary mind's own sentiment, nor can an ordinary mind ever fully proclaim them. The Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "The various grounds cannot even be spoken of — how much less could they be shown to another!" Let this matter be set aside for a moment. Even at the level of raising up the initial mind of sympathetic joy in Mahāyāna repentance and perfect faith, once one attains a single dhāraṇī of complete revolution, this already cannot be spoken of to another; though one may discriminate it in every manner of way, it still cannot be understood. How much more so, then, the later stages beyond this — even the two vehicles have never so much as heard their names, so how could an ordinary person possibly speak of them? This remark carries a real point: the Great Master is here speaking of his own personal realization. Let this matter, too, be set aside for a moment: the śrāvaka who studies the four applications of mindfulness and brings forth the dharma of warmth likewise cannot fully explain it to an exterior ordinary person; though one offers every manner of explanation, that person still cannot know it. Let this, too, be set aside. It is like a person practicing seated meditation who first brings forth the five factors of the first dhyāna — this cannot be explained to one who has not yet realized it; even if explained through skillful means, that person still would not understand it. Let this, too, be set aside. The wheelwright is unable to transmit his own skill to his very son through words alone — how much less, then, could these profound dharmas possibly be spoken of directly?

末代學者，多執經論，方便斷伏、諍鬪(云云)。如水性冷，不飲安知？此乃諸佛赴緣不思議語，隨機增減，位數不同。爾未證得，空諍何為？普願法界眾生，歸僧息諍論，入大和合海。

Students of this later age mostly cling rigidly to the sūtras and treatises, disputing and quarreling over the expedient means of severing and subduing affliction, and so on. It is like the coldness of water's own nature — without drinking it oneself, how could one truly know it? This is simply the inconceivable language by which every buddha responds to conditions, increasing or decreasing according to capacity, so that the number of stages differs from one account to the next. Since you yourselves have not yet realized any of this, what is the use of empty disputation? May it be universally wished that every sentient being throughout the dharma-realm return to the saṅgha, put an end to contentious argument, and enter together the great ocean of harmonious accord.

Reading note

Nanyue Huisi's reading of the "opening, showing, awakening, entering" fourfold gate from the "Skillful Means" chapter onto the ten abidings/practices/dedications/grounds-and-equal-enlightenment is one of the most influential single sentences in Tiantai exegesis, cited constantly across the tradition afterward. Having spent several sections triangulating the forty-two-stage count from every angle available, Zhiyi then turns and warns against treating that same count too literally — "if one insists on correlating them rigidly with specific dharma-gates, one who searches for them in this way will lose their true sense." The aside "the Great Master is here speaking of his own personal realization" is a rare moment where Zhiyi steps outside the text to gloss his own teacher Huisi's words as testimony rather than doctrine — the wheelwright analogy (from the *Zhuangzi*) makes the same point once more: some things cannot be handed down in words at all, only realized directly.

Four Ways to Open and Combine the Stages 四句料簡圓位

Chinese Source

又以四句料簡圓位，或開初合後，或開後合初，或初後俱開，或初後俱合。如《大經》明三十三天不死甘露，將、臣共服。此譬諸位，開前為三十心，合十地為一，等覺為一，譬三十二臣，喻於因位；妙覺為主，譬於果位。君之與臣，同服甘露；因之與果，俱證常樂。若不以圓位釋之，此文難會。是為開初合後，以明圓位也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, the perfect stage may be resolved according to four statements: opening the earlier and combining the later; opening the later and combining the earlier; opening both the earlier and the later; or combining both the earlier and the later. As the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra clarifies: the deathless ambrosia of Trāyastriṃśa is drunk together by the ruler and his ministers alike. This is a metaphor for the various stages: opening the earlier portion into the three sets of ten minds, while combining the ten grounds into one and equal enlightenment into one, is a metaphor for the thirty-two ministers, representing the causal stage; wondrous enlightenment as the ruler is a metaphor for the fruit-stage. The ruler and his ministers alike drink the same ambrosia; the cause and the fruit alike realize the same permanence and bliss. Without interpreting this passage according to the perfect stage, it would be difficult to make sense of at all. This is opening the earlier and combining the later, used to clarify the perfect stage.

若十四般若，合三十心為三般若，開十地為十般若，就等覺為十四般若，皆是因位；轉入薩婆若，即是果位。是為合前開後，以明圓位。若四十二字門，即是初後俱開，以明圓位。若天雨四華，表開、示、悟、入；遊於四方者，此即前後俱合。諸經開合不同，皆是悉檀方便，而圓位宛然矣。

As for the fourteen kinds of prajñā: combining the three sets of ten minds into three kinds of prajñā, while opening the ten grounds into ten kinds of prajñā, and reaching equal enlightenment as the fourteenth kind — all of these together are the causal stage; turning to enter omniscience itself is precisely the fruit-stage. This is combining the earlier and opening the later, used to clarify the perfect stage. As for the gate of the forty-two letters: this is precisely opening both the earlier and the later together, used to clarify the perfect stage. As for the heavens raining down the four kinds of flowers, representing opening, showing, awakening, and entering, and roaming through the four directions — this is precisely combining both the earlier and the later together. The various sūtras differ in how they open and combine these stages, yet all of this is simply the expedient means of the siddhāntas, and the perfect stage stands out clearly and distinctly throughout.

Clarifying Severing and Subduing in the Perfect Stage 明圓位斷伏

Chinese Source

三、明圓位斷伏者，五品已圓解一實四諦，其心念念與法界諸波羅蜜相應，遍體無邪曲偏等倒，圓伏枝、客、根本惑，故名伏忍。諸教初心，無此氣分。《大經》云：「學大乘者，雖有肉眼，名為佛眼。」㲉中鳴，勝諸鳥。例如：小乘伏煗，佛法則有，外道則無。今此伏忍，圓教則有，三教則無。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the severing and subduing of the perfect stage: at the five ranks, one has already perfectly understood the one real Four Noble Truths, this mind, thought after thought, corresponding to every pāramitā throughout the dharma-realm, its entire embodiment free of every deviant distortion and one-sided inversion, perfectly subduing the branch afflictions, the guest afflictions, and the root affliction alike — hence, this is named subduing forbearance. The initial mind of the other teachings possesses no trace of this quality at all. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "One who studies the Mahāyāna, though still possessing the physical eye, is named to possess the buddha-eye" — like the cry still within the eggshell, already surpassing every other bird. This is comparable to the Hīnayāna's own dharma of warmth: the buddha's own teaching possesses it, while the non-Buddhist path does not. Here, too, this subduing forbearance is possessed by the perfect teaching, while the other three teachings do not possess it.

十信之位，伏道轉強。發得似解，破界內見、思，界內、界外無知塵沙。如經文云「得三陀羅尼」，但名似道，未是真道。旋陀羅尼是旋假入真。百千旋陀羅尼是旋真入俗。法音方便正是伏道，未得入中。如《纓珞》從假入空觀，雖斷見、思，但離虛妄，名為解脫，其實未得一切解脫。當知，六根雖淨，圓教煗、頂四善根，柔順忍，伏道位耳。

At the stage of the ten faiths, the subduing path grows ever stronger. Bringing forth the resembling understanding, one breaks through the views and thought within the three realms, and the ignorance-of-particulars and dust-and-sand both within and beyond the three realms. As the sūtra's own text says, "attaining the three dhāraṇīs" — yet these are only named the resembling path, not yet the real path. The dhāraṇī of revolution is revolving from the provisional to enter the real. The dhāraṇī of a hundred thousand revolutions is revolving from the real to enter the conventional. The dhāraṇī of skillful means in the sound of the Dharma is precisely the subduing path itself, not yet having entered the middle. Just as the Yingluo Sūtra's own contemplation of entering emptiness from the provisional, though it severs views and thought, only frees one from falsehood, and is named liberation — yet, in reality, one has not yet attained liberation in every respect. One should therefore know that, though the six sense-faculties are purified, this is still only the perfect teaching's own dharma of warmth and the summit, the four good roots, accordant forbearance — merely the stage of the subduing path.

若入初住，得真法音陀羅尼，正破無明，始名斷道。見佛性常住第一義理，名圓教無生忍。十行、十迴向、十地、等覺，皆破無明，同是無生忍位。妙覺，斷道已周，究竟成就，名為寂滅忍。

Once one enters the initial abiding, one attains the dhāraṇī of the real sound of the Dharma, directly breaking through ignorance — only now is this named the path of severance. Seeing buddha-nature's own permanently abiding, ultimate-meaning principle is named the perfect teaching's own forbearance of no-generation. The ten practices, the ten dedications, the ten grounds, and equal enlightenment all break through ignorance, alike belonging to the stage of the forbearance of no-generation. Wondrous enlightenment, the path of severance already complete all the way around, its ultimate accomplishment fulfilled, is named the forbearance of quiescent extinction.

若約位別判，伏、順二忍但伏不斷，例如無礙道。妙覺一忍，斷而不伏，例如解脫道。無生一忍，亦伏、亦斷，亦無礙，亦解脫。若論通義，妙覺、寂滅忍，亦名無生忍。《大經》云：「涅言不生；槃言不滅。不生不滅，名大涅槃。」亦名伏忍。《仁王》云：「從初發心至金剛頂，皆名伏忍。」伏是賢義。普賢菩薩居眾伏之頂。伏忍既通，順忍可解。伏、順既其通上，寂滅、無生亦應通下。

Judged according to the stages taken separately: the two forbearances of subduing and of according only subdue without severing, comparable to the uninterrupted path. Wondrous enlightenment's own single forbearance severs without subduing, comparable to the path of liberation. The single forbearance of no-generation both subdues and severs, both is uninterrupted and is liberated. Discussed in terms of the shared sense, however, wondrous enlightenment's own forbearance of quiescent extinction is likewise named the forbearance of no-generation. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "'Nir' is spoken of as no-arising; 'vāṇa' is spoken of as no-ceasing — no-arising and no-ceasing together are named the great nirvāṇa" — and this, too, is named subduing forbearance. The Renwang Jing says: "From the very first arousing of the mind all the way to the vajra summit, all of this is named subduing forbearance" — "subduing" carries the sense of "worthy." The bodhisattva Samantabhadra dwells at the very summit of the multitude of subduers. Since subduing forbearance already extends throughout in this shared sense, according forbearance may be understood the same way; and since subduing and according forbearance already extend upward in this shared sense, quiescent-extinction and no-generation forbearance ought likewise to extend downward in the same way.

《思益》云：「一切眾生即滅盡定。」《淨名》云：「一切眾生皆如也。」如即無生忍。又就事為無生，就理為寂滅。又分證為寂滅，讓果為無生。若約因果，亦有通別。通者，一切眾生即大涅槃，即是約因論果。佛性者，名之為因，此即約果論因。《大經》云：「是果非因，名大涅槃；是因非果，名為佛性。」了見佛性，乃是於佛。故亦得是因(云云)。等覺望妙覺為因，望菩薩為果。自下已去，亦因、亦因因，亦果、亦果果。約分別義者，伏、順二忍未是真因；無生一忍，未是真果。從十住去，名真因；妙覺，名真果。云何伏、順非真因？例如，小乘方便之位，不名修道；見諦已去，約真修道。此義可知。今順忍中，斷除見、思，如水上油，虛妄易吹。無明是同體之惑，如水內乳，唯登住已去菩薩鵝王，能唼無明乳、清法性水，從此已去，乃判真因。

The Viśeṣacintibrahma-paripṛcchā Sūtra says: "Every sentient being is already the concentration of cessation itself." The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "Every sentient being is already suchness itself" — this suchness is precisely the forbearance of no-generation. Again, spoken of in terms of the phenomenal, this is no-generation; spoken of in terms of principle, this is quiescent extinction. Again, a partial realization is named quiescent extinction; yielding to the fruit itself is named no-generation. Discussed in terms of cause and fruit, this, too, admits of both a shared and a particular sense: in the shared sense, every sentient being is already the great nirvāṇa itself — this discusses the fruit in terms of the cause. Buddha-nature is named the cause — this discusses the cause in terms of the fruit. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "What is fruit and not cause is named the great nirvāṇa; what is cause and not fruit is named buddha-nature." Fully seeing buddha-nature is already being a buddha — hence, this, too, may be taken as the cause, and so on. Equal enlightenment, measured against wondrous enlightenment, is the cause; measured against the bodhisattva stages below it, it is the fruit. From this point downward, each stage is both cause and the cause of a cause, both fruit and the fruit of a fruit. Discussed in terms of careful distinction: the two forbearances of subduing and according are not yet the real cause; the single forbearance of no-generation is not yet the real fruit. From the ten abidings onward is named the real cause; wondrous enlightenment alone is named the real fruit. Why are subduing and according forbearance not the real cause? This is comparable to the Hīnayāna's own stage of expedient means, which is not yet named the path of cultivation — only from the seeing of the truths onward is this named, in the real sense, the path of cultivation; this same sense may be understood here. Within according forbearance, the severing and removal of views and thought is like oil floating upon water — this falsehood is easily blown away. Ignorance, however, is an affliction of one and the same essence as the real itself, like milk mixed within water — only the bodhisattva who is the king of geese, already having ascended to the abidings and beyond, is able to sip out the milk of ignorance and leave the clear water of the dharma-nature behind; only from this point onward, therefore, is the real cause properly judged to begin.

Against the Distinct Teaching's Sequential Severing of Views and Thought 難別教見思前後斷

Chinese Source

復次別教，判三地或四地，斷見盡；六地或七地，斷思盡。此不應爾。何者？無明見、思，同體之惑，何得前後斷耶？當是別教附傍小乘方便說耳。若見先盡，則實理無復有障，云何十地見不了了？《地持》云：「第九、離一切見清淨淨禪。」第九是等覺地，入離見禪，乃成大菩提果。若見先斷，等覺復何所離？若思前盡，後地應無果報及諸禪定。何者？《華嚴》明阿僧祇香雲、華雲，不可思議充塞法界者，此是菩薩勝妙果報所感五塵，呼此為欲界思惑。一切菩薩皆入出無量百千三昧禪定心塵之法，呼此為色、無色界思惑。若七地思盡，上地應絕六塵，何故復言：「三賢、十聖住果報」？若住果報，思不前盡？今明如此見、思，通至上地，至佛方盡。故云：「唯佛一人居淨土，唯佛一人能盡源。」是故伏斷，如前分別(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, the distinct teaching judges that the third or fourth ground exhausts the severing of views, and the sixth or seventh ground exhausts the severing of thought. This ought not to be so. Why is this? Ignorance, views, and thought are afflictions of one and the same essence — how could they possibly be severed one before the other in sequence? This must be the distinct teaching borrowing, in a roundabout way, from the Hīnayāna's own expedient explanation. If views were exhausted first, then the real principle would no longer carry any obstruction at all — why, then, would the tenth ground's own seeing still not be perfectly clear? The Bodhisattva-bhūmi says: "The ninth is the pure dhyāna free of every view." The ninth is the ground of equal enlightenment; entering the dhyāna free of views is precisely what accomplishes the great fruit of bodhi. If views were already severed beforehand, what, then, would equal enlightenment still be freeing itself from? If thought were exhausted before this, then the later grounds ought to possess no retribution-fruit or dhyāna concentration at all. Why is this? The Avataṃsaka Sūtra clarifies clouds of incalculable fragrance and clouds of flowers, inconceivably filling the entire dharma-realm — these are the five sense-objects called forth by the bodhisattva's own superior and wondrous retribution-fruit, and this is named the affliction of thought within the desire realm. Every bodhisattva enters and emerges from immeasurable hundreds of thousands of samādhis, the very dharma of concentration's own mind-dust, and this is named the affliction of thought within the form and formless realms. If the seventh ground already exhausted thought, the higher grounds ought to be entirely free of the six sense-objects — why, then, does it further say, "the three worthy stages and the ten sage-stages still abide in retribution-fruit"? If one still abides in retribution-fruit, has thought truly been exhausted beforehand at all? Here, it is clarified that views and thought such as these extend all the way through to the higher grounds, only exhausted upon reaching buddhahood itself. Hence it is said: "Only the buddha alone dwells in the pure land; only the buddha alone is able to exhaust the very source." This is accordingly why subduing and severing are distinguished as explained before, and so on.

問：界內必先斷見，次思，後無知。界外何意不爾？答：界內為三途苦重，先斷見，次思，後及無知。界外苦輕，故先枝，後本。又，思無知，不障偏真，為見真理，故先除見。界外塵沙是體上惑，遠能障理，先却遠障，次除近障(云云)。復次，三藏中，後身菩薩及超果二乘，見、思同斷，亦先斷思(云云)。不超果者，前後斷耳。通教亦有超、不超二義。別教前後斷，圓教同斷。前後之問，但見一途耳(云云)。

Question: Within the three realms, one must necessarily sever views first, then thought, and only afterward ignorance-of-particulars — why is it not the same beyond the three realms? Answer: Within the three realms, since the suffering of the three evil destinies is severe, one severs views first, then thought, then, afterward, ignorance-of-particulars as well. Beyond the three realms, suffering is light, and so the branch is severed first, the root only afterward. Again, thought and ignorance-of-particulars do not obstruct the one-sided real, so, for the sake of seeing the true principle, views are removed first. Dust-and-sand beyond the three realms is an affliction upon the very essence itself, able to obstruct principle from a distance — hence, the distant obstruction is set aside first, and the near obstruction removed only afterward, and so on. Again, within the Tripiṭaka teaching, the bodhisattva in his final body and the two vehicles who leap over a fruit sever views and thought together at once, and, even then, sever thought first, and so on. Those who do not leap over a fruit sever them in sequence, one after the other. The shared teaching, too, admits of both the leaping and the non-leaping senses. The distinct teaching severs them in sequence, one after the other; the perfect teaching severs them together at once. The question of sequence versus simultaneity, then, is simply a matter of which single path one happens to be looking at, and so on.

Reading note

The four forbearances (伏忍/subduing, 順忍/according, 無生忍/no-generation, 寂滅忍/quiescent extinction) map the five ranks and ten faiths (subduing only), the initial abiding through equal enlightenment (severing ignorance directly, hence "no-generation"), and wondrous enlightenment alone (severance complete) — with the geese-and-milk simile (a king goose able to separate milk from water, leaving the water pure) vividly illustrating why only genuine severance of ignorance, not mere suppression of views and thought, counts as the "real cause." The polemic against the distinct teaching's sequential ground-by-ground severing scheme is sharp and specific: if views and thought are truly exhausted at an early, fixed ground, later grounds' own continued dhyāna and retribution-fruit (drawn from the *Avataṃsaka Sūtra*'s own descriptions) become inexplicable. Zhiyi's own conclusion — that the schemes differ only in "which single path one happens to be looking at" — is characteristic of how this treatise handles apparent contradictions between texts and teachings throughout.

Clarifying Function 明功用

Chinese Source

四、明功用者，若分字解義，功論自進，用論益物。合字解者，正語化他。五品之位，理雖未顯，觀慧已圓，具煩惱性，能知如來祕密之藏，堪為世間作初依止。依止此人，猶如如來，當知不久，詣於道樹，近三菩提。一切世間，皆應向禮；一切賢聖，皆樂見之。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, clarifying function: if the two characters are parsed and their sense explained separately, "merit" discusses one's own advancement, while "function" discusses benefiting others. Taken together as a compound, this speaks precisely of transforming others. At the stage of the five ranks, though principle has not yet been made manifest, contemplative wisdom is already perfect and complete; still possessed of the nature of affliction, one is nonetheless able to know the Tathāgata's own secret treasury, and is already fit to serve the world as its first reliance. Relying upon such a person is just like relying upon the Tathāgata himself — one should know that, before long, this person will arrive beneath the tree of enlightenment, drawing near to the threefold bodhi. Everyone in the entire world ought to bow toward such a person; every worthy and sage alike delights in seeing them.

若六根似解，圓觀轉明，長別苦海。能以一妙音，遍滿三千界，隨意之所至，一切天龍皆向其處聽法。其人有所說法，能令大眾歡喜，猶是第一依止。《涅槃》標四依，義通圓、別。人師多約別判。地前通名初依。登地至三地，斷見盡，名須陀洹。至五地侵思，名斯陀含，是第二依。至七地思盡，名阿那含，是第三依。八地至十地，欲色心三習盡，名阿羅漢，是第四依。若推圓望別，應約十住明三依，對住前為四依。若始終判者，五品、六根為初依；十住為二依；十行十迴向為三依；十地等覺為四依。

As for the six sense-faculties' own resembling understanding: perfect contemplation grows ever more distinct, and one forever departs the sea of suffering. Being able, with a single wondrous sound, to fill the entire trichiliocosm, wherever one's own intention reaches, every heavenly being and dragon alike turns toward that place to hear the Dharma; whatever this person teaches is able to bring the whole assembly joy — this, too, is still the first reliance. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra sets out the four reliances, and their sense extends to both the perfect and the distinct teachings. Teachers commonly judge them in terms of the distinct teaching. Before the ground is reached, this is generally named the initial reliance. Once the ground is reached, up through the third ground, severing views completely, this is named stream-entry. Up through the fifth ground, encroaching upon thought, this is named once-returning, the second reliance. Up through the seventh ground, thought is exhausted, this is named non-returning, the third reliance. From the eighth ground through the tenth, the three kinds of habitual residue with respect to the desire realm, form, and mind are exhausted, this is named arhatship, the fourth reliance. Reasoning the perfect teaching measured against the distinct, one ought to take the ten abidings as the basis for clarifying three of the four reliances, with what precedes the abidings serving as the remaining one. Judged from beginning to end: the five ranks and the six sense-faculties' own purification are the initial reliance; the ten abidings are the second reliance; the ten practices and ten dedications are the third reliance; the ten grounds and equal enlightenment are the fourth reliance.

從初住已上，總論功用；若豎功未深，橫用不廣；豎功若深，橫用必廣。譬如諸樹，根深則枝闊，華葉亦多。初住豎破一分無明，獲一分二十五三昧，顯一分我性。論其實處，不可思議，依於教門。橫則百佛世界，分身散影，作十法界像，利祐眾生。如是住住豎入，倍倍轉深，無明漸漸盡、三昧轉轉增，我性分分顯、橫用稍稍廣。千佛界、萬佛界、恒沙佛界、不可說不可說佛界，遍如是界，八相成道，教化眾生，況餘九法界身耶？諸行、諸地，亦復如是。論其滿足，唯佛與佛，乃能究盡無明之源。故經言：「如佛心中無無明，唯佛法王住究竟王三昧。毘盧遮那法身，橫周法界，豎極菩提，大功圓滿，勝用具足」(云云)。

From the initial abiding onward, function is discussed in general: if the vertical merit is not yet deep, the horizontal function is not yet broad; once the vertical merit grows deep, the horizontal function necessarily grows broad. It is like every kind of tree: the deeper the root, the broader the branches, and the more abundant the flowers and leaves as well. At the initial abiding, one vertically breaks through a single portion of ignorance, obtaining a single portion of the twenty-five samādhis, revealing a single portion of the true nature of self. Discussed in terms of its own real place, this is inconceivable, and is here explained only by relying on the teaching's own gateway. Horizontally, one manifests across a hundred buddha-worlds, dividing the body and scattering its shadow, taking the form of every one of the ten dharma-realms, benefiting and protecting sentient beings. In just this way, abiding after abiding, one enters ever more vertically, growing doubly deeper each time — ignorance gradually exhausted further, the samādhis increasing further still, the true nature of self revealed portion by portion further, the horizontal function gradually broadening further. A thousand buddha-worlds, ten thousand buddha-worlds, buddha-worlds as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, buddha-worlds beyond all telling — pervading every such world, accomplishing the path through the eight aspects, transforming sentient beings — how much more so, then, the bodies taken in the remaining nine dharma-realms as well? Every practice, every ground, is exactly the same. Discussed in terms of its full completion, only a buddha together with a buddha is able to fathom the very source of ignorance to its very end. Hence the sūtra says: "Just as the buddha's own mind holds no ignorance at all, only the Dharma-king buddha abides in the ultimate king samādhi. Vairocana's own Dharma-body, horizontally pervading the entire dharma-realm, vertically reaching the very summit of bodhi, its great merit perfectly complete, its supreme function fully provided," and so on.

Reading note

The four reliances (四依 — figures the community can rely on as authoritative teachers, from the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*) are here correlated with the perfect teaching's own stages just as they conventionally are with the distinct teaching's ten grounds, giving the five ranks and six-root purification the striking status of "first reliance" despite occurring entirely before any genuine breakthrough. The vertical/horizontal image (豎/橫) — depth of one's own severance of ignorance versus breadth of one's function benefiting others, tied together like a tree's root and its canopy — recurs throughout later Tiantai literature as a basic way of describing how a single bodhisattva's local progress translates into cosmic-scale activity across "buddha-worlds beyond all telling."

Clarifying the Coarse and the Wondrous Across Every Stage 通諸位論麁妙

Chinese Source

五、通諸位論麁妙者，小草止免四趣，不動、不出。中草雖復動、出，智不窮源，恩不及物。上草雖能兼濟，滅色為拙。小樹雖巧，功齊界內，故其位皆麁。大樹實事，同緣中道，皆破無明，俱有界外功用，故此位為妙。而別教從方便門，曲逕紆迴，所因處拙，其位亦麁。圓教直門，是故為妙。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, discussing coarse and wondrous across all the stages together: the small herb only escapes the four evil destinies, neither unmoved nor emerged from the three realms. The middle herb, though already unmoved and emerged, has a wisdom that does not fathom the source, and a kindness that does not reach every being. The superior herb, though able to benefit both self and others together, is still clumsy in extinguishing form. The small tree, though skillful, has its merit confined within the three realms — hence, all of these stages are coarse. The great tree's own real matter, alike taking the middle way as its object, all breaking through ignorance, all alike possessing function beyond the three realms — hence, this stage is wondrous. Yet the distinct teaching, proceeding through the gate of expedient means, its path winding and circuitous, the very ground it relies upon still clumsy, its own stage, too, remains coarse. The perfect teaching's own gate is direct — hence, it alone is wondrous.

又，三藏菩薩，全不斷惑，望圓教五品，有齊有劣：同不斷惑，是故言齊；五品圓解常住，彼全不聞常住，是故為劣。若三藏佛位，斷見、思盡，望六根清淨位，有齊、有劣：同除四住，此處為齊；若伏無明，三藏則劣。佛尚為劣，二乘可知。當知，三草蒙籠，生用淺短，故其位皆麁。若乾慧地、性地，望五品位，有齊有劣，例前(云云)。若八人、六地，見、思盡，七地修方便，至佛斷習盡，望圓教似解，有齊、有劣，例前可解當知。小樹之位，未有干雲婆娑之能，是故皆麁。

Again, the Tripiṭaka teaching's own bodhisattva, who does not sever affliction at all, measured against the perfect teaching's own five ranks, is both equal to it and inferior to it: alike not severing affliction, they are accordingly equal; yet the five ranks perfectly understand the permanently abiding, while that bodhisattva has never even heard of the permanently abiding at all — hence, he is inferior. As for the Tripiṭaka teaching's own buddha-stage, severing views and thought completely, measured against the stage of the purification of the six sense-faculties, it is both equal to it and inferior to it: alike removing the four abiding afflictions, this much is equal; yet, with respect to subduing ignorance, the Tripiṭaka teaching's own buddha is inferior. If even the buddha of that teaching is thus inferior, the two vehicles may be known accordingly. One should therefore know that the three herbs, still wrapped in obscurity, produce only a shallow and limited function — hence, all of these stages are coarse. As for the ground of dry wisdom and the ground of nature, measured against the stage of the five ranks, they are both equal and inferior, following the same pattern as before, and so on. As for the eighth ground of the eight kinds of person, views and thought being exhausted, the seventh ground cultivating expedient means, and reaching the buddha with habitual residue exhausted — measured against the perfect teaching's own resembling understanding, they are both equal and inferior, to be understood following the same pattern as before. The stage of the small tree has not yet the capacity to soar into the clouds and spread wide like a great canopy — hence, all of it remains coarse.

若別教十信望五品位，有齊、有劣：同未斷惑，是故為齊；十信歷別，五品圓解，此則為優。別教十住斷通見、思，十行破塵沙，十迴向伏無明，秖與圓家十信位齊優劣(云云)。若登地破無明，秖與圓家初住齊。何者？若十地十品破無明，圓家十住亦十品破無明。設開十地為三十品，秖是圓家十住三十品齊。若與為論，圓家不開十住，合取三十心為三十品，與別家十地三十品等者，則十地與圓家十迴向齊。若奪而為論，別家佛地與圓家初行齊；與而為論，別家佛地與圓家初地齊。故知，別教權說判佛則高；望實為言，其佛猶下。

As for the distinct teaching's own ten faiths, measured against the stage of the five ranks, they are both equal and inferior: alike not yet severing affliction, they are accordingly equal; yet the ten faiths proceed through separate, distinct stages, while the five ranks understand perfectly — in this respect, the five ranks are superior. The distinct teaching's own ten abidings sever the views and thought shared with the shared teaching; the ten practices break through dust-and-sand; the ten dedications subdue ignorance — these correspond only to the perfect teaching's own stage of the ten faiths, equal in some respects and superior in others, and so on. As for breaking through ignorance upon reaching the ground, this corresponds only to the perfect teaching's own initial abiding. Why is this? If the ten grounds break through ignorance across ten grades, the perfect teaching's own ten abidings likewise break through ignorance across ten grades. Even were the ten grounds opened out into thirty grades, this would correspond only to the perfect teaching's own ten abidings' own thirty grades equally. If discussed comparatively, since the perfect teaching does not open out its own ten abidings this way, but instead takes the three sets of ten minds together as thirty grades, and these are equal to the distinct teaching's own ten grounds' thirty grades, then the ten grounds correspond to the perfect teaching's own ten dedications instead. If discussed by taking away, the distinct teaching's own ground of buddhahood corresponds only to the perfect teaching's own initial practice; if discussed by granting, the distinct teaching's own ground of buddhahood corresponds to the perfect teaching's own initial ground. One should therefore know that the distinct teaching's own provisional account judges its buddha to be exalted, yet, measured in terms of the real, this same buddha remains, in fact, still lower.

譬如邊方未靜，授官則高；定爵論勳，置官則下。別教權說，雖高而麁；圓教實說，雖低而妙，此譬可解。以我之因，為汝之果，別位則麁。當知大樹雖巨圍，要因於地，方漸生長。是知圓位從初至後，皆是實說。實伏、實斷，俱皆稱妙(云云)。《大論》云：「譬如有樹，名曰好堅。在地百歲，一出即長百丈，蓋眾樹頂。」此譬圓位也。

It is like a border region not yet pacified, where an official is granted a high rank at the outset; but once the realm is settled and merit properly assessed, that same official's rank is set lower than before. The distinct teaching's own provisional account, though high, remains coarse; the perfect teaching's own real account, though seemingly lower, is wondrous — this metaphor may be understood accordingly. Taking my own cause as your own fruit, the distinct teaching's own stage remains coarse in this respect. One should know that, though the great tree grows to an enormous girth, it must still depend upon the earth in order to grow gradually. One thus knows that the perfect teaching's own stage, from the very first to the very last, is entirely a real account throughout — its subduing real, its severing real, all alike named wondrous, and so on. The Treatise says: "It is like a tree named 'Excellent and Firm': remaining in the earth for a hundred years, the very moment it emerges, it grows a hundred zhang tall in an instant, its canopy covering the crown of every other tree." This is a metaphor for the perfect stage.

Reading note

This section works systematically back through every stage-scheme translated so far in this treatise — the Tripiṭaka bodhisattva and buddha, the shared teaching's ground of dry wisdom through the eighth ground, the distinct teaching's ten faiths through the ground of buddhahood — cross-matching each against its perfect-teaching counterpart with a deliberately double-edged verdict: "equal in some respects, inferior in others" (有齊、有劣). The border-official simile makes the treatise's real point vivid: a provisional rank granted generously during an unsettled campaign looks impressive at the time, but is revealed as lower once true merit is finally assessed — exactly how the distinct teaching's own buddha, though nominally the highest attainment that teaching offers, corresponds only to the perfect teaching's own tenth ground once measured honestly. The closing "Excellent and Firm" tree (from the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra*) — a legendary tree said to grow a hundred *zhang* the instant it finally breaks the surface after a century underground — caps the extended tree-metaphor running through this whole "Medicinal Herbs" section: the perfect teaching's own apparent suddenness is not really sudden at all, but the visible culmination of depth already present all along.

Seventh, clarifying the abolition of the stages: principle itself fundamentally has no stage at all; stages arise only in response to conditions. Since conditions arise one after another in succession, stages, too, are set aside one after another in succession — this is not something that only began with the Lotus Sūtra's own abolition of them now. One must recognize the intent behind each act of refuting and establishing, and must not refute or establish carelessly, at random. Why is this? Originally, when the Tathāgata established the Tripiṭaka teaching's own stage, this was a provisional means for generating phenomenal goodness. Once this phenomenal goodness had already been generated, and its function of salvation was already sufficient, it accordingly had to be set aside. The shared and distinct teachings' own stages are exactly the same. This is the Tathāgata's own intent in refuting and establishing. As for the ordinary Mahāyāna master who fails entirely to properly order the Tripiṭaka teaching's own material — this, too, is losing sight of the Buddha's own skillful means. As for the ordinary Hīnayāna master who searches out the sense of the sūtras to explain the very treatise he expounds, distinguishing the meaning of the bodhisattva — the Mahāvibhāṣā itself already states its own account of the bodhisattva's meaning, yet he refuses to make use of it, instead taking up Mahāyāna sūtras to interpret the Tripiṭaka teaching's own twin gates of emptiness and existence — how could these possibly be made to agree with one another? This carries two faults: first, it buries the Buddha's own skillful means; second, it exposes the treatise's own author as not understanding the meaning of the bodhisattva at all — hence, this must be refuted. Even granting that sūtras are cited to explain the sense of the Mahāyāna, what kind of Mahāyāna is this meant to be? If it is meant as the shared teaching's own Mahāyāna, then the three vehicles alike enter the real truth, and this remains so all the way to buddhahood — how, then, could the eighth ground contemplate the middle way and break through ignorance? Constructed as the shared teaching's own sense, this fails to hold together — hence, it must be refuted. If it is meant as the distinct teaching's own sense of the Mahāyāna, then, from the very first mind onward, it already differs from the two vehicles — how, then, could the sixth ground be made equal to the arhat? Constructed as the distinct teaching's own sense, this, too, fails to hold together — hence, it must be refuted. Moreover, the distinct teaching is itself only an expedient means; clinging to the provisional while slandering the real must accordingly be refuted as well. In the past, once a person had passed away, their doctrine became fixed; now, seeing through to its own faults, it must accordingly be refuted. Yet, in order to extend the Buddha's own skillful means, it must accordingly also be established once more — this is the very intent behind refuting and establishing in the present age. The perfect teaching, however, arises from a single master alone, transcending the three provisional teachings and being itself the one real teaching — its object, wisdom, practice, and stage are not the same as what came before. If the text and its principle can be reconciled, let us together walk this level road; but where the aim is lost and the track departed from, please follow a worthy guide instead. Having first set forth this intent, the abolition of the stages is next clarified.

As for the Buddha's own responding to capacity in raising up, abolishing, refuting, and establishing these teachings: as the Sūtra of Innumerable Meanings says, "The innumerable dharmas arise from a single dharma — namely, the two paths, the three vehicles, and the four fruits." The two paths are the sudden and the gradual. The three dharmas are the three vehicles. The four fruits are the four stages. These innumerable dharmas all arise from this single dharma. Why is this? Since the two paths are already the sudden and the gradual, and the sudden is precisely the great path, like the sun illuminating the high mountains — let this be set aside for now, not yet discussed. Here, the very beginning of the gradual path is clarified, which is precisely the Tripiṭaka teaching. Its teaching says: whoever seeks buddhahood must cultivate the six pāramitās across three incalculable eons, planting the seeds of the auspicious marks across a hundred further eons, before buddhahood can finally be attained. This is spoken in order to cause phenomenal goodness to arise. For whoever wishes to seek buddhahood, reforming evil and following goodness, once this goodness is established, the teaching is accordingly set aside. It is thereupon refuted, saying: how could there possibly be a bodhisattva who attains bodhi without severing confusion and affliction at all? A poisoned vessel cannot be trusted to hold food — this teaching is accordingly abolished, and its practices and stages are abolished along with it. Since this teaching originally looked to the fruit in order to practice the cause, once there is no fruit left to look toward, the buddha-wisdom and the buddha-stage it promised are both abolished together.

For the sake of these various senses, it is accordingly said that the Tripiṭaka teaching is abolished while the shared teaching is established. Those who originally received only the shared teaching and never studied the Tripiṭaka teaching at all are not the ones this discussion of abolition concerns. The very purpose of establishing the shared teaching was to give rise to goodness of principle, embodying dharmas directly to sever confusion, entering the real by way of the skillful method; once this, too, the teaching's own intent is already fulfilled. The wise person, having seen emptiness, ought then also to see the not-empty — how could one remain forever fixed within emptiness alone? The shared teaching is accordingly abolished, its bodhisattva's own practice and wisdom abolished along with it, and the buddha-wisdom and buddha-stage it promised abolished as well, and so on. For the two vehicles, only the teaching itself is abolished; the rest, and so on. This shared teaching is itself shared both with the shared-teaching-proper reading and with the shared-teaching-as-received-by-the-distinct reading: in the sense shared with the Perfection of Wisdom teachings, it is as explained above; in the sense not shared with the Perfection of Wisdom teachings, there is, in that respect, no abolition at all, and so on. One should therefore know that the masters of the Satyasiddhi Śāstra and the Daśabhūmika Śāstra see only the sense shared with the Perfection of Wisdom teachings, and fail to see the sense not shared with them; the masters of the Middle Treatise grasp the sense not shared with them, but lose the sense shared with them. It accomplishes goodness for the person who came before, while accommodating goodness for the person who comes after — hence, it carries both an abolished and an unabolished aspect. The shared teaching, accomplishing for the one before and accommodating the one after, is exactly the same. The distinct teaching, accomplishing for the one before and accommodating the one after, is exactly the same as well. The perfect teaching itself carries both an establishing and a not-yet-establishing aspect: its initial illumination of the high mountains is already, of itself, an establishing, while, with respect to the Tripiṭaka teaching's own audience, it is not yet established. The text says, "only now do they first see my own body and enter the Tathāgata's own wisdom" — this is precisely the earlier establishing; those who studied the lesser teachings, now entering the buddha's own wisdom, is precisely the later establishing — what lies between these two may be understood accordingly. Every practice and every wisdom carries both an abolished and an unabolished aspect. Every fruit and every stage carries both an abolished and an unabolished aspect.

The clarified-butter flavor has three teachings: two teachings' own practice and stage carry both an abolished and an unabolished aspect; one teaching's own practice and stage carries no abolition at all. In the Lotus Sūtra, the practice and stage of three teachings are all abolished, while the practice and stage of the one remaining teaching are not abolished at all. It simply teaches the unsurpassed path, all riding together upon the one treasure-vehicle alike, arriving directly at the very seat of enlightenment together — hence, all three senses alike remain unabolished. The Sūtra of Innumerable Meanings says that the two paths, the three vehicles, and the four fruits do not yet accord with one another; by the time of the Lotus Sūtra, they are all brought into accord — hence, no further abolition is discussed. From the accomplishment of the path onward, across more than forty years, the real had not yet been revealed; the Lotus Sūtra is where the real is first revealed. It has been handed down that the Buddha was seventy-two years of age when he taught the Lotus Sūtra, and so on.

How is it that a stage is abolished and a further stage entered afterward? The Tripiṭaka teaching's own bodhisattva abolishes the stage of not severing affliction and enters the stage of severing affliction. How is it that a stage is abolished without any further stage entered? Whoever abolishes this stage and secretly awakens to the sudden teaching does not enter any stage in sequence at all. How is it that a stage is not abolished and no further stage entered? The two vehicles who abide in the fruit are such a case. How is it that a stage is not abolished yet a further stage is still entered? Whoever is accommodated for a later condition, likewise not yet having abolished anything, secretly awakens and enters a higher stage nonetheless. The shared and distinct teachings' own wisdom and stage may be resolved in exactly the same way, and so on.

問：廢更修，可有益，廢不更修，有何益？答：自有廢修得益。自有訶廢，聞雖不修，而有恥小鄙劣，折其取證之心，亦是有益。又，齊其斷結，謂言無益，迴心入大，即是得益(云云)。

Question: Abolishing and then further cultivating may indeed bring benefit — but what benefit is there in abolishing without further cultivation? Answer: There is indeed abolishing-and-cultivating that brings benefit. But there is also reproving the abolition itself: though one hears it without cultivating further, one is nonetheless made ashamed of one's own base and inferior smallness, breaking one's own mind's grasping at premature realization — this, too, is a benefit. Again, though one is equal in having severed the fetters, and it might be said this brings no benefit, turning the mind to enter the Mahāyāna is itself already the benefit gained, and so on.

Reading note

Zhiyi opens the abolition discussion with an unusually direct polemic against contemporary Abhidharma-based scholars who claimed to find bodhisattva teaching within Hīnayāna treatises using borrowed Mahāyāna sūtra language — a real historical debate (echoing Nāgārjuna's own critique of Sarvāstivāda "bodhisattva" claims) rather than an abstract hypothetical. The chapter's real payoff is the Five-Flavors correlation: each of the first four flavors abolishes some but not all of the teachings active within it, while the *Lotus Sūtra* alone, as the ghee, abolishes all three provisional teachings while leaving the one real teaching entirely unabolished — because, unlike the earlier periods, it was never itself a still-further provisional stepping stone. The sixteen-fold permutation of teaching/practice/stage/wisdom each independently abolished-or-not, cultivated-or-not, is a systematic way of covering every kind of listener the *Lotus Sūtra* actually depicts (the resting-in-the-fruit śrāvaka, the secretly-awakened sharp-faculty listener, the bodhisattva accommodated for a still-later teaching) rather than a single simple rule.

Eighth, opening the coarse stage to reveal the wondrous stage: as for breaking through the three to reveal the one, this is the sense of the relative reading, and may be understood as explained before. As for the three being themselves already one, this is the sense of the absolute reading, and its meaning is not the same. Why is this? Formerly, the provisional held the real concealed within it, like a flower still containing its lotus-seed; opening the provisional to reveal the real is like the flower opening to reveal the lotus-seed within. Apart from this same flower, there is no separate lotus-seed to be found elsewhere; apart from this same coarse, there is no separate wondrous to be found elsewhere. What need, then, is there to break through the coarse in order to arrive at the wondrous? Simply opening the provisional stage is itself already revealing the wondrous stage.

As for opening the coarse mind of birth-and-death: this clarifies that the ordinary being possesses the capacity to turn back and reconsider, easily able to arouse the bodhi-mind. Birth-and-death is itself already nirvāṇa, neither two nor separate — the coarse itself is already the wondrous. If, beginning from the ordinary being, one arouses any of the four minds — the analytical, the embodying, the distinct, or the perfect — these are, every one, the initial mind of one of the four stages. All are minds arisen from causes and conditions, and this very arising through causes and conditions is at once empty, at once provisional, at once the middle, neither two nor separate from the perfect teaching's own initial mind. Every one of these initial minds, then, reveals the wondrous already within the milk itself — this is precisely placing poison within milk, and it is already able to kill a person. The killing may be swift or slow: if measured according to the stage while still nominally wondrous, this becomes the merely nominal wondrous; if advanced further into expedient means, this becomes the resembling wondrous; if advanced further still into principle itself, this becomes the wondrous of a partial realization of the real, and so on.

若開六度權位行者，檀即因緣生法，即空、即假、即中。開檀得見佛性，乃至般若亦復如是。亦名置毒乳中，即能殺人。按位即假名妙。若進方便，成相似妙。若進入理，成分真妙(云云)。方便聲聞未入位者，開權顯實亦如是。

As for opening the six pāramitās' own provisional stage and practice: giving itself is a dharma arisen from causes and conditions, at once empty, at once provisional, at once the middle. Opening giving in this way, one attains the seeing of buddha-nature; and prajñā, too, is exactly the same. This, too, is named placing poison within milk, already able to kill a person. Measured according to the stage, this is the merely nominal wondrous; advanced further into expedient means, this becomes the resembling wondrous; advanced further still into principle, this becomes the wondrous of a partial realization of the real, and so on. For the śrāvaka of expedient means who has not yet entered any stage, opening the provisional to reveal the real is exactly the same.

三藏斷結位，若未開權，永無反復，如焦種無芽。今開析空，即假、即中，如置毒酪中，亦能殺人。按麁即妙，是相似位。若進入，隨位判妙也。次、開通教二乘、菩薩，亦如是。出假菩薩位者，決了此假，假即是中，如置毒生蘇，而能殺人。按麁即妙，是相似位。若進入，隨位判妙。

As for the Tripiṭaka teaching's own stage of severing the fetters: if the provisional has not yet been opened, there can never be any turning back and reconsidering at all — like a scorched seed that can never sprout. Here, opening the analytical emptiness of this teaching to reveal that it, too, is at once provisional, at once the middle, is like placing poison within cream — this, too, is already able to kill a person. Measured according to the coarse, this is already the wondrous, and is the resembling stage; advanced further, the wondrous is judged according to whatever stage is reached. Next, opening the shared teaching's own two vehicles and bodhisattva is exactly the same. As for the stage of the bodhisattva emerging into the provisional: resolving and fully understanding this same provisional, the provisional is itself already the middle — like placing poison within curdled butter, which is likewise able to kill a person. Measured according to the coarse, this is already the wondrous, and is the resembling stage; advanced further, the wondrous is judged according to whatever stage is reached.

若開別教十信位者，同前。若開十住者，同二乘(云云)。若開十行位者，同通教出假菩薩。若開十迴向、伏無明位，即此而中，是名置毒熟蘇，即能殺人。按麁即妙，是相似位。若進入，隨位判妙。若登地之位不決了者，秖是拙度之位。今決此權，令得顯實，即是置毒醍醐而殺於人。

As for opening the distinct teaching's own stage of the ten faiths: this is exactly the same as before. As for opening the ten abidings: this is exactly the same as the two vehicles, and so on. As for opening the stage of the ten practices: this is exactly the same as the shared teaching's own bodhisattva emerging into the provisional. As for opening the ten dedications, the stage of subduing ignorance: this very provisional is itself already the middle — this is named placing poison within clarified butter, already able to kill a person. Measured according to the coarse, this is already the wondrous, and is the resembling stage; advanced further, the wondrous is judged according to whatever stage is reached. As for the stage of reaching the ground without yet having fully resolved it: this remains merely the stage of the clumsy method. Here, resolving this same provisional, causing the real to be revealed, is precisely placing poison within ghee itself, and killing a person thereby.

按麁即妙，是十住位。若進入，隨位判妙。若決諸權，或按位妙，或進入妙，無麁可待，同成一妙，其義已顯。今更譬說，譬如小國大臣，來朝大國，失本位次，雖預行伍，限外空官。若大國小臣，心膂憑寄，爵乃未高，他所敬貴。諸教諸位，決麁入妙，雖得入流，欲比圓教八妙，猶是從鈍中來。圓教發心，雖未入位，能知如來祕密之藏，即喚作佛。初心尚然，何況後位乎！(云云)。

Measured according to the coarse, this is already the wondrous — this is the stage of the ten abidings. Advanced further, the wondrous is judged according to whatever stage is reached. Once every provisional teaching is thus resolved, whether measured as wondrous according to its own stage, or wondrous through further advancement, there remains no coarse left to set against it — all alike become the one single wondrous, and this sense has already been made clear. Here, a further metaphor may be given: it is like a great minister from a small country coming to pay court at a great country — he loses his own original rank, and, though placed among its own ranks of office, is limited to an honorary post outside its actual structure. But a lesser minister of the great country itself, trusted with its very heart and sinew, though his own title may not yet be high, is honored and prized by everyone else nonetheless. Every teaching, every stage, once its own provisional is resolved and it enters the wondrous, though it may thereby enter the same stream, still, measured against the perfect teaching's own eight kinds of wondrous, remains something that has come from dullness after all. The perfect teaching's own arousing of the mind, though not yet having entered any stage at all, is already able to know the Tathāgata's own secret treasury, and is already called "buddha" outright. If the initial mind is already thus, how much more so, then, the later stages themselves! And so on.

Reading note

This is the absolute reading of "opening the coarse" applied specifically to stages: rather than breaking through provisional stages to arrive at a separate wondrous one, the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s own poison-in-milk simile shows the wondrous already lethal-potent within the very first, coarsest stage (an ordinary being's mind of birth-and-death), and remaining just as potent through every subsequent transformation — cream, curdled butter, clarified butter, ghee — exactly paralleling the Five Flavors sequence used throughout this treatise for the Four Teachings. Each teaching's own furthest stage (the Tripiṭaka teaching's severing of the fetters, the shared teaching's bodhisattva emerging into the provisional, the distinct teaching's subduing of ignorance, and finally the perfect teaching's own ground-stage) is mapped onto milk through ghee in turn, so that resolving any one teaching's own provisional stage already reveals the wondrous, without needing to discard it for something else entirely.

九、引《涅槃》五譬，成四教位。若不將四教釋譬，譬不可解。若非五譬判四教位，取信為難。若信經文，則位義易曉。解諸位意，彼譬冷然。彼此相須，可謂兼美者也。彼文云：「凡夫，如乳。須陀洹，如酪。斯陀含，如生蘇。阿那含，如熟蘇。阿羅漢、支佛、佛，如醍醐。」此譬三藏五位。何者？凡夫全生，未能除惑，菩薩亦爾，但得如乳。須陀洹破見，革凡成聖，如乳變為酪。斯陀含侵六品思，故如生蘇。阿那含欲界思盡，故如熟蘇。阿羅漢、支佛、佛，皆斷三界見思盡，故同稱醍醐。故《釋論》云：「聲聞經中，稱阿羅漢地為佛地。故共為一味也。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, citing the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra's own five similes to establish the stages of the Four Teachings: without using the Four Teachings to interpret these similes, the similes themselves cannot be understood; and without these five similes to judge the stages of the Four Teachings, it would be difficult to gain confidence in them. If one has faith in the sūtra's own text, then the sense of the stages becomes easy to grasp; and, once the sense of the various stages is understood, that same simile becomes perfectly clear and cool to the touch. Each depends upon the other, and together they may be said to bring out the best in both. That text says: "The ordinary being is like milk. The stream-enterer is like cream. The once-returner is like curdled butter. The non-returner is like clarified butter. The arhat, the pratyekabuddha, and the buddha are like ghee." This is a simile for the Tripiṭaka teaching's own five stages. Why is this? The ordinary being, entirely unripened, has not yet been able to remove confusion at all, and the bodhisattva, too, is exactly the same — attaining only the equivalent of milk. The stream-enterer breaks through views, transforming from the ordinary into the sage, like milk transformed into cream. The once-returner encroaches upon six grades of thought, and hence is like curdled butter. The non-returner exhausts thought within the desire realm, and hence is like clarified butter. The arhat, the pratyekabuddha, and the buddha all alike sever the views and thought of the three realms completely, and hence are alike named ghee. Hence the Treatise says: "Within the śrāvaka's own scriptures, the arhat's own ground is named the buddha-ground — hence, they are together named a single flavor."

問：此經以三藏菩薩為上草。彼經云何以菩薩為乳味？答：經取化他邊強，喻之上草。此中自證力弱，同凡夫為乳味(云云)。

Question: The present sūtra takes the Tripiṭaka teaching's own bodhisattva as the superior herb — why, then, does that other sūtra take the bodhisattva as the milk flavor? Answer: The sūtra takes the strength of his transformation of others as the basis for the metaphor of the superior herb; here, however, the weakness of his own personal realization places him, alike with the ordinary being, at the milk flavor, and so on.

三十二云：「凡夫如雜血乳；須陀洹、斯陀含如淨乳；阿那含如酪；阿羅漢如生蘇；支佛、菩薩如熟蘇；佛如醍醐。」此譬通教五位也。凡夫不斷惑，乳猶雜血。二果侵思未多，同初果如乳。三果欲思已盡，故如酪。四果見思俱盡，如生蘇。支佛智利侵習，小勝聲聞，故共菩薩如熟蘇。十地名佛地，即是醍醐。

Fascicle thirty-two says: "The ordinary being is like milk mixed with blood; the stream-enterer and once-returner are like pure milk; the non-returner is like cream; the arhat is like curdled butter; the pratyekabuddha and the bodhisattva are like clarified butter; the buddha is like ghee." This is a simile for the shared teaching's own five stages. The ordinary being, not yet having severed affliction, remains milk mixed with blood. The second fruit, not yet encroaching heavily upon thought, is grouped with the first fruit as mere milk. The third fruit has already exhausted desire-realm thought, and hence is like cream. The fourth fruit has exhausted both views and thought together, and is like curdled butter. The pratyekabuddha, sharp of wisdom and encroaching further upon habitual residue, slightly surpassing the śrāvaka, is accordingly grouped together with the bodhisattva as clarified butter. The tenth ground, named the buddha-ground, is precisely ghee.

前以菩薩同凡味，故知是三藏。今以菩薩同支佛，故知是通。若不作通釋譬義，何由可解(云云)。

Earlier, the bodhisattva was grouped together with the ordinary being's own flavor — hence, one knows this to be the Tripiṭaka teaching. Here, the bodhisattva is grouped together with the pratyekabuddha instead — hence, one knows this to be the shared teaching. Without interpreting the sense of these similes according to the shared teaching specifically, how could this possibly be understood otherwise? And so on.

Reading note

The *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra* itself gives several different five-flavor similes scattered across its own fascicles, using the very same ordinary-being/stream-enterer/once-returner/non-returner/arhat-pratyekabuddha-buddha vocabulary each time — yet grouping them differently, so that the bodhisattva lands at "milk" in one passage and at "clarified butter" alongside the pratyekabuddha in another. Zhiyi's method throughout this ninth section is to treat these divergences not as inconsistency but as evidence: each simile only makes sense once matched to a specific one of the Four Teachings, and, reciprocally, each teaching's own stage-scheme is confirmed by having an independent scriptural simile that fits it exactly — "each depends upon the other, and together they may be said to bring out the best in both." This closes 卷五上 (Fascicle 5's upper half); 卷五下 continues with the distinct and perfect teachings' own similes, including the famous "cow that eats the forbearance-grass and thereby obtains ghee directly" passage for the perfect teaching.

The Distinct and Perfect Teachings' Own Similes: The Cow That Eats the Forbearance-Grass 別教・圓教五譬・忍辱草喻

Chinese Source

第九卷：「凡夫佛性，如牛新生，血乳未別。聲聞佛性，如清淨乳。支佛如酪。菩薩如生、熟蘇。佛如醍醐。」此譬別教五位。乳譬無明，血譬四住。凡夫具此，故言雜血。十住已斷四住之血，與二乘齊，故言聲聞如乳。十住後心，理明智利，類支佛侵習，故言如酪。十行破塵沙，如生蘇。十迴向，破界外塵沙，如熟蘇。故言菩薩如生、熟蘇。登地破無明，顯佛性，得一身無量身，百佛世界八相作佛，故言佛如醍醐。

The ninth fascicle says: "The ordinary being's own buddha-nature is like a newborn calf, its blood and milk not yet distinguished. The śrāvaka's own buddha-nature is like pure milk. The pratyekabuddha is like cream. The bodhisattva is like curdled and clarified butter. The buddha is like ghee." This is a simile for the distinct teaching's own five stages. Milk is a metaphor for ignorance; blood is a metaphor for the four abiding afflictions. The ordinary being possesses both together, and hence is spoken of as mixed with blood. The ten abidings have already severed the blood of the four abiding afflictions, equal in this respect to the two vehicles — hence, it is said that the śrāvaka is like milk. The ten abidings' own final mind, its principle clear and its wisdom sharp, resembling the pratyekabuddha's own encroachment upon habitual residue, and hence is said to be like cream. The ten practices break through dust-and-sand, and are like curdled butter. The ten dedications break through the dust-and-sand beyond the three realms, and are like clarified butter — hence, it is said that the bodhisattva is like curdled and clarified butter. Reaching the ground, breaking through ignorance, revealing buddha-nature, attaining a single body that is immeasurable bodies, accomplishing buddhahood through the eight aspects across a hundred buddha-worlds — hence, it is said that the buddha is like ghee.

二十五云：「雪山有草，名為忍辱。牛若食者，即得醍醐。」牛喻凡夫，草喻八正。能修八正，即見佛性，名得醍醐。此譬圓教，行大直道，觀一切眾生，即涅槃相，不復可滅。圓信、圓行不由歷別，於一生中，即入初住，得見佛性。如牛食忍草，不歷四味，卓出醍醐，故知圓教意也。忍草譬境妙，牛譬智妙，食者譬行妙，出醍醐譬位妙，此圓意也。牛食餘草，血乳轉變，歷四味已，方成醍醐。餘方便教，境、智、行、位，皆麁意也。

Fascicle twenty-five says: "On the Snowy Mountains, there is a grass named forbearance; if a cow eats it, ghee is obtained directly." The cow is a metaphor for the ordinary being; the grass is a metaphor for the eightfold correct path. Being able to cultivate the eightfold correct path, one thereby sees buddha-nature directly, named obtaining ghee. This is a simile for the perfect teaching: practicing the great, direct path, contemplating that every sentient being already bears the mark of nirvāṇa itself, never again to be extinguished. Perfect faith and perfect practice do not proceed through separate, distinct stages one by one — within a single lifetime, one enters directly into the initial abiding, attaining the sight of buddha-nature. Just as the cow that eats the forbearance-grass obtains ghee directly, without passing through the four intervening flavors at all, standing out and reaching ghee outright — one thereby knows this to be the perfect teaching's own sense. The forbearance-grass is a metaphor for the wondrous of the object; the cow is a metaphor for the wondrous of wisdom; the eating of it is a metaphor for the wondrous of practice; and obtaining ghee is a metaphor for the wondrous of stage — this is the perfect teaching's own sense. A cow that eats any other grass has its blood and milk gradually transformed, passing through the four intervening flavors before finally becoming ghee. Every other, expedient teaching's own object, wisdom, practice, and stage are, all of them, a sense of the coarse.

前四譬，即有四處明醍醐。四教明佛智各異，俱既稱佛，同指佛智以為醍醐。藏、通二佛不明中道，但取果頭佛二諦智為醍醐。別教登地破無明，即能作佛，以中道理智為醍醐。圓教初住得中道智，亦稱為醍醐。《纓珞》云：「頓悟世尊」，即此初住智為醍醐也。前兩醍醐是權非實，故有教而無人。別教醍醐名權理實，圓教醍醐名理俱實。以是義故，前三位、五味皆麁，圓教一味皆妙。

In the four similes discussed before, there are, accordingly, four separate places where ghee is clarified. Though the Four Teachings each clarify buddha-wisdom differently, all being alike named "buddha," they alike point to buddha-wisdom itself as ghee. The Tripiṭaka and shared teachings' own two buddhas do not clarify the middle way at all, and take only the fruit-stage buddha's own wisdom of the two truths as ghee. The distinct teaching, reaching the ground and breaking through ignorance, is thereby able to become a buddha, taking the principle-wisdom of the middle way as ghee. The perfect teaching, at the initial abiding, attains the wisdom of the middle way, and this, too, is named ghee. The Yingluo Sūtra says, "the World-Honored One suddenly awakened" — this refers precisely to this same initial abiding's own wisdom as ghee. The first two kinds of ghee are provisional, not real — hence, there is a teaching there, but no person who has actually attained it. The distinct teaching's own ghee is named provisional in name but real in principle. The perfect teaching's own ghee is named both principle and name together real. For this reason, the stages and Five Flavors of the first three teachings are, every one, coarse, while the perfect teaching's own single flavor is, every one, wondrous.

Reading note

卷五下 (Fascicle 5's lower half) opens by completing the five-similes survey: the distinct teaching's own scheme names blood (the four abiding afflictions) and milk (ignorance) as the two things the ordinary being carries mixed together, so that its own "milk" stage is already the śrāvaka rather than the ordinary being. The forbearance-grass simile that follows is one of the single most quoted images in the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra* for the perfect teaching's own directness — a cow that eats this one grass obtains ghee immediately, never having to pass through cream, curdled butter, or clarified butter at all, exactly as the perfect teaching's own practitioner reaches the initial abiding's genuine breakthrough within a single lifetime rather than across incalculable eons. Zhiyi maps all four wondrous aspects already translated onto the single image at once — the grass is the object, the cow is wisdom, the eating is practice, and the ghee obtained is the stage — showing this one simile to already contain the whole architecture of the ten wondrous aspects in miniature. The closing comparison of the four teachings' own "ghee" is worth sitting with: only the perfect teaching's ghee is named "real" in both name and principle — the distinct teaching's is real in principle but still provisional in name, and the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings' own ghee, resting on a buddha who never actually sees the middle way, is a teaching with, strictly speaking, no one who has ever truly attained it.

Poison in Milk, Across Every Teaching's Own Stages 置毒五味・遍歷四教

Chinese Source

第二十七卷云：「譬如有人，置毒乳中，則能殺人。乃至醍醐，亦能殺人。」此譬兩用：一、通約漸、頓，明不定教，處處皆得見佛性也。二、約行不定。行人心行，譬之如乳。實相智譬之以毒，毒有殞命之能，此智有破無明之力。久遠劫來，說實相毒，置於凡夫心乳，毒慧開發，不可為定，或於初味發，或於後味發，不得次第往判，故言置毒乳中，乃至醍醐，遍五味中，悉有殺義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fascicle twenty-seven says: "It is like a person who places poison within milk — this is already able to kill a person; and, on through ghee, this, too, is able to kill a person." This simile has two uses: first, in terms of the gradual and the sudden together, it clarifies the indeterminate teaching, whereby buddha-nature may be seen in any and every place at once; second, in terms of the practitioner's own indeterminacy. The practitioner's own mind and practice are likened to the milk; the wisdom of the real mark is likened to the poison — poison carries the power to end a life, and this wisdom carries the power to break through ignorance. From distant eons past, the poison of the real mark has been taught, placed within the ordinary being's own mind, which is the milk; this poisoned wisdom's own opening and unfolding cannot be fixed in advance — it may open at the first flavor, or it may open only at the last flavor, and cannot be judged by sequence alone — hence, it is said that poison is placed within milk, and, on through ghee, throughout all five flavors, there is, everywhere, this same sense of killing.

若眾生始於凡地，得聞《華嚴》，即便見理，入佛慧者，此是血乳殺人。若先得十住，今《華嚴》得悟，即是酪中殺人。十行悟者，是生蘇殺人。十迴向悟者，是熟蘇殺人。諸地更悟，是醍醐中殺人。若過去，先是圓教中假名、相似之位，今聞《華嚴》得悟者，亦是乳中殺人，亦是酪、生熟等蘇中殺人。若先是諸住、諸行等位，今更聞《華嚴》，得增道損生，即是於醍醐中殺人(云云)。

If a sentient being, beginning still at the ordinary stage, hears the Avataṃsaka Sūtra and thereby directly sees principle, entering the buddha's own wisdom, this is killing within blood-and-milk. If one had already attained the ten abidings, and now, through the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, attains awakening, this is killing within cream. Whoever awakens at the ten practices, this is killing within curdled butter. Whoever awakens at the ten dedications, this is killing within clarified butter. Whoever awakens further at any of the grounds, this is killing within ghee. If, in a past life, one had already reached the perfect teaching's own nominal or resembling stage, and now, hearing the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, attains awakening, this is likewise killing within milk, and likewise killing within cream, curdled butter, or clarified butter. If one had already reached any of the abidings or practices, and now, further hearing the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, attains the advancing of the path and the diminishing of birth, this is killing within ghee itself, and so on.

若歷三藏教中，凡夫及方便位，及菩薩位，聞三藏教，於中即能密見中道，即是乳中殺人。若四果位，密見中道，即是酪中殺人。顯露教中，無此事也。若通教中，凡夫及三乘方便位，若聞通教密見佛性，即是乳中殺人。若入位者，祕密而去，即是酪中殺人。若菩薩道種智中去，即是生蘇殺人。九地中去，即是熟蘇殺人。十地中去，即是醍醐中殺人。通教聲聞，但有祕密中殺人，無顯露不定殺也。

Traced through the Tripiṭaka teaching: if the ordinary being, or one at the stage of expedient means, or one at the bodhisattva stage, hears the Tripiṭaka teaching and, within it, is secretly able to see the middle way, this is killing within milk. If one at the stage of the four fruits secretly sees the middle way, this is killing within cream. Within the revealed teaching itself, no such thing occurs. As for the shared teaching: if the ordinary being, or one at the three vehicles' own stage of expedient means, hears the shared teaching and secretly sees buddha-nature, this is killing within milk. If one who has already entered a stage proceeds secretly onward, this is killing within cream. If one proceeds within the bodhisattva's own wisdom of the paths, this is killing within curdled butter. If one proceeds within the ninth ground, this is killing within clarified butter. If one proceeds within the tenth ground, this is killing within ghee. The shared teaching's own śrāvaka has only this secret killing, and no killing through the revealed, indeterminate teaching.

若歷別教中，十信聞教去，即是乳中殺人。三十心中去，即是酪殺人，生蘇熟蘇等殺人。登地去，即是醍醐殺人也。若圓教中，發始聞經即破無明、見佛性，是乳中殺人。六根清淨去，是酪生熟蘇等殺人。若初住去，是醍醐殺人。

Traced through the distinct teaching: whoever proceeds from hearing the teaching at the ten faiths, this is killing within milk. Whoever proceeds within the three sets of ten minds, this is killing within cream, and killing within curdled and clarified butter. Whoever proceeds upon reaching the ground, this is killing within ghee. Traced through the perfect teaching: whoever, upon first hearing the sūtra, immediately breaks through ignorance and sees buddha-nature, this is killing within milk. Whoever proceeds from the purification of the six sense-faculties, this is killing within cream, curdled butter, and clarified butter. Whoever proceeds from the initial abiding, this is killing within ghee.

若有行人，歷諸教四譬五味過已，方得入圓教醍醐中殺人者，此是破三顯一，相形待為妙。若置毒乳中，味味悉殺人者，此是開權顯實，於一切法中即見中道。故文云：「汝等所行，是菩薩道。」不須更改途易轍而求真實，即麁見妙，故以置毒為喻。諸經悉有祕密置毒之妙，而未有顯露歷味入妙，亦無顯露決麁即妙。至此《法華》方有二意。同乘寶乘，皆開佛知見，顯露事彰，是故獨稱為妙，其意在此。次第入妙、開麁即妙，各有兩意：按位開入、有增進開入。若言決了聲聞法，是諸經之王，聞已諦思惟，得近無上道，即是按位顯妙。增道損生，即是升進入妙。故《法華》獨稱妙也。

If there is a practitioner who, having passed entirely through the four similes and Five Flavors of the various teachings, only then attains entry into the perfect teaching's own killing within ghee — this is breaking through the three to reveal the one, the wondrous read in relation to something else, the relative sense. If poison is placed within milk, and every single flavor, one after another, is equally able to kill — this is opening the provisional to reveal the real, seeing the middle way directly within every dharma whatsoever. Hence the text says: "What you yourselves have been practicing is already the bodhisattva path" — there is no need to change course or alter one's own track in search of the real elsewhere; the coarse itself is seen as the wondrous directly — hence, the placing of poison is used as this same metaphor. Every other sūtra already possesses this same wondrous sense of secretly placed poison, yet none of them yet possess entering the wondrous openly, through the revealed teaching, across the flavors in sequence; nor do any of them possess resolving the coarse to reveal the wondrous openly, through the revealed teaching either. Only with the present Lotus Sūtra are both of these senses finally present together. Riding together upon the one treasure-vehicle alike, all alike opening the buddha's own knowledge and vision, this matter is made openly and clearly manifest — hence, the Lotus Sūtra alone is named wondrous, and this is precisely the sense of it. Entering the wondrous in sequence, and opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous directly, each carry two senses in turn: revealing the wondrous according to one's own present stage, and entering the wondrous through further advancement. As it is said, resolving and fully understanding the śrāvaka's own dharma is itself the very king of all the sūtras — having heard it, reflecting upon it carefully, one draws near to the unsurpassed path; this is precisely revealing the wondrous according to one's own present stage. Advancing the path and diminishing birth is precisely entering the wondrous through further advancement. Hence, the Lotus Sūtra alone is named wondrous.

Reading note

This extends the poison-in-milk simile from the previous section across every stage of every one of the Four Teachings individually, distinguishing "secret" benefit (a practitioner nominally still following the Tripiṭaka or shared teaching's own gradual path who is, unbeknownst to themselves, actually awakening directly to the middle way) from "revealed" benefit (openly taught as such). Zhiyi's key claim is that only the *Lotus Sūtra* combines both the sequential entry into the wondrous (working up through the flavors in order) and the direct opening of the coarse into the wondrous (any flavor already lethal) as openly, revealed teachings at once — every earlier sūtra offers one or the other only secretly. This sets up the tenth and final item of the wondrous-of-stage discussion, the stage read "from beginning to end."

The Wondrous Stage, From Beginning to End 十、明妙位始終

Chinese Source

十、明妙位始終者，真如法中無詮次，無一地、二地，法性平等，常自寂然，豈應分別初後始終？良由平等大慧，觀於法界無有若干，能破若干無明，顯出無若干智慧。約此智慧，無始而始，即是初阿；無終而終，即是後茶；無中而論中，即是四十心。雖復差別，則無差別，故名不思議位也。如下文云：「聲聞、緣覺如竹林，新發、不退菩薩等，皆不能知，除諸菩薩眾信力堅固者。」聲聞、緣覺所不能知，此簡三藏、通教兩種二乘也。三藏菩薩，緣真不及聲聞，聲聞尚不知，此菩薩那得知？通教菩薩入真之智，與二乘不殊，二乘不知，彼菩薩亦不知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, clarifying the wondrous stage from beginning to end: within the dharma of suchness itself, there is no sequence of exposition at all — no first ground, no second ground; the dharma-nature is equal throughout, ever quiescent of itself — how, then, could one possibly distinguish a beginning and an end, a first and a last? It is simply that, by means of the equal, great wisdom, contemplating the dharma-realm as possessing no distinctions of any kind whatsoever, one is able to break through ignorance of every kind, and thereby reveal wisdom possessing no distinctions of any kind. In terms of this same wisdom: without any beginning, and yet beginning nonetheless, this is precisely the initial letter "a"; without any end, and yet ending nonetheless, this is precisely the final letter "dha"; without any middle, and yet a middle discussed nonetheless, this is precisely the forty minds in between. Though distinctions are, once again, present, there is, in reality, no distinction at all — hence, this is named the inconceivable stage. As the text below says: "The śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha are like a bamboo grove; the newly arisen and the unretreating bodhisattva, and the rest, are none of them able to know this, except for those bodhisattvas whose power of faith is firm and secure." That the śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha are unable to know this distinguishes the two kinds of the two vehicles found within the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings. The Tripiṭaka teaching's own bodhisattva, whose grasp of the real does not even equal the śrāvaka's own, cannot possibly know this if even the śrāvaka does not know it. The shared teaching's own bodhisattva, whose wisdom of entering the real is no different from that of the two vehicles, likewise does not know this if the two vehicles themselves do not know it.

今標二乘不知，兩處菩薩亦不能測。發心不知，即指別教十信；不退不能知，即指別教三十心。十住，位不退。十行，行不退。十迴向，念不退。此三不退皆不能知。三藏中不退，尚不及二乘；通教中不退，止等二乘，二乘不知，豈重舉菩薩？今標發心不退者，則擬別教中人也。信力者，是假名位。堅固者，是鐵輪位。如此等位，聞經即解，故得為妙，似位之始也。初開佛知見，乘是寶乘，遊於東方，即是真位之始也。三方是中位，直至道場，過茶無字可說，即是終位也。

Here, marking that the two vehicles do not know this, two further groups of bodhisattvas are likewise unable to fathom it. Those unable to know it as "newly arisen" refer precisely to the distinct teaching's own ten faiths; those unable to know it as "unretreating" refer precisely to the distinct teaching's own three sets of ten minds — the ten abidings being unretreating in rank, the ten practices unretreating in practice, and the ten dedications unretreating in mindfulness. All three of these unretreating stages are equally unable to know this. Since even the unretreating of the Tripiṭaka teaching does not equal the two vehicles, and the unretreating of the shared teaching only equals the two vehicles — if the two vehicles themselves do not know this, why would the bodhisattva be raised again as a separate case? Here, marking "newly arisen" and "unretreating" together is meant to point specifically to the persons within the distinct teaching. "Power of faith" is the nominal stage; "firm and secure" is the iron-wheel stage. Stages such as these, hearing the sūtra, immediately understand it — hence, they are able to be named wondrous, the very beginning of the resembling stage. Opening the buddha's own knowledge and vision for the first time, riding upon this treasure-vehicle, roaming through the eastern direction, is precisely the beginning of the real stage. The three remaining directions are the middle stage; arriving directly at the very seat of enlightenment, beyond the letter "dha," with no further letter left to speak of, is precisely the final stage.

如此諸位，乘何等乘？乘有三種：謂教、行、證。若言是乘，從三界出，到薩婆若中住。住有二義：一、取證故住，即通教意也。二、所乘極故住，即別、圓意也。初心憑教所詮，信教立行，得出三界。無明未破，未有所證，故不見真。但乘教乘，來至此耳。我圓教中，其誰是耶？謂五品弟子，能善發大心，長別三界苦輪海，即其人也。教乘既息，證乘未及，以似解慧，進修眾行，則以行為乘，從方便三界中出，到初住薩婆若中住。我圓教中，其誰是耶？謂十信心六根淨者，即其人也。初住乃至等覺，更增道損生者，此以證為乘，從因緣三界乃至無後三界中出，到妙覺中，過茶無字可說，故言到薩婆若中住。前來諸乘，猶有上法，不得稱住。茶無上法，是故言住。住無住處，即妙位之終。

As for these various stages — which vehicle does each ride upon? There are three kinds of vehicle: the teaching, the practice, and the realization. As it is said, this vehicle emerges from the three realms and comes to abide within omniscience itself. This "abiding" carries two senses: first, abiding because realization has been attained — this is the shared teaching's own sense; second, abiding because the vehicle ridden has reached its own limit — this is the distinct and perfect teachings' own sense. At the initial mind, relying on what the teaching itself expounds, believing the teaching and establishing practice upon it, one is able to emerge from the three realms; yet, ignorance not yet broken through, there is not yet any realization attained, and so the real is not yet seen — one has merely ridden the vehicle of the teaching, arriving only this far. Within our own perfect teaching, who corresponds to this? The disciples of the five ranks, able skillfully to arouse the great mind, forever departing the sea of suffering's wheel across the three realms — these are precisely such persons. The vehicle of the teaching having already come to rest, while the vehicle of realization has not yet been reached, one uses the resembling understanding-wisdom to advance in cultivating every practice, taking practice itself as the vehicle, emerging from the three realms of expedient means and coming to abide within the initial abiding's own omniscience. Within our own perfect teaching, who corresponds to this? Those of the ten faiths' own mind whose six sense-faculties are purified — these are precisely such persons. From the initial abiding all the way through equal enlightenment, further advancing the path and diminishing birth, this takes realization itself as the vehicle, emerging from the three realms of causes and conditions all the way through the three realms with no further remainder, arriving within wondrous enlightenment itself, beyond the letter "dha," with no further letter left to speak of — hence, it is said to arrive and abide within omniscience itself. Every vehicle discussed before this still had some further dharma still above it, and so could not yet be named "abiding." The letter "dha" has no further dharma above it at all — hence, it alone is spoken of as "abiding." Abiding in the place of no abiding at all is precisely the very end of the wondrous stage.

復次，別教十住破見思，是行三百由旬，十行破塵沙為四百，十迴向伏無明為五百。十地斷無明，此分見中道，即為寶所也。

Again, within the distinct teaching, the ten abidings' own breaking through views and thought is traveling three hundred yojanas; the ten practices' own breaking through dust-and-sand is four hundred; the ten dedications' own subduing of ignorance is five hundred. The ten grounds' own severing of ignorance, this partial seeing of the middle way, is precisely the place of treasure itself.

圓教六根清淨時，是行四百。破無明入初住，是行五百。二乘聞經，破無明惑，開佛知見，得記作佛者，即是決了諸麁位，過五百由旬，來入初住。即是妙位之始，得於證乘，遊於東方也。若至本門中增道損生，更乘證乘，遊於南方，是進入十行位也。西方是進入十迴向。北方是進入十地也。又如文云：「說是如來壽命長遠時，六百八十萬億那由他恒河沙人得無生法忍」，即是十住。「復千倍菩薩，得聞持陀羅尼」，即十行。「復有一世界微塵數菩薩，得樂說辯才」，即十迴向。「復有一世界微塵數，得旋陀羅尼」，即初地。「三千大千微塵，得不退」，即二地。「二千國土微塵，能轉清淨法輪」，即三地。「小千國土微塵，八生當得菩提」，即四地。七生當得，即五地。六生當得，即六地。五生當得，即七地。「四生當得」，即八地。「三生當得」，即九地「二生當得」，即十地。「一生當得」，即等覺。過此一生，即是過茶無字，即是妙覺地，妙位之終也。

In the perfect teaching, the time of the purification of the six sense-faculties is traveling four hundred yojanas. Breaking through ignorance to enter the initial abiding is traveling five hundred. As for the two vehicles who, hearing the sūtra, break through the confusion of ignorance, open the buddha's own knowledge and vision, and receive the prediction of buddhahood — this is precisely resolving every coarse stage fully, passing beyond the five hundred yojanas, and arriving at the initial abiding. This is precisely the very beginning of the wondrous stage, attaining the vehicle of realization, roaming through the eastern direction. As for reaching the origin gate and there further advancing the path and diminishing birth, riding the vehicle of realization further still, roaming through the southern direction — this is advancing into the stage of the ten practices. The western direction is advancing into the ten dedications. The northern direction is advancing into the ten grounds. Again, as the text says: "At the time when the Tathāgata's own lifespan, vast and distant, was proclaimed, six hundred and eighty ten-thousand koṭis of nayutas of people, as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, attained the forbearance of the dharma of no-generation" — this is precisely the ten abidings. "A further thousand times that number of bodhisattvas attained the dhāraṇī of hearing and upholding" — this is the ten practices. "A further number of bodhisattvas, equal to the motes of dust in an entire world-system, attained eloquence delighting in teaching" — this is the ten dedications. "A further number equal to the motes of dust in an entire world-system attained the dhāraṇī of revolution" — this is the first ground. "A number equal to the motes of dust in a great trichiliocosm attained non-retrogression" — this is the second ground. "A number equal to the motes of dust in two thousand lands were able to turn the pure wheel of the Dharma" — this is the third ground. "A number equal to the motes of dust in a small thousand-world-system will attain bodhi after eight further rebirths" — this is the fourth ground. Those who will attain it after seven further rebirths are the fifth ground. After six further rebirths, the sixth ground. After five further rebirths, the seventh ground. "After four further rebirths" is the eighth ground. "After three further rebirths" is the ninth ground; "after two further rebirths" is the tenth ground. "After one further rebirth" is equal enlightenment. Beyond this one further rebirth is precisely passing beyond the letter "dha," with no further letter to speak of — this is precisely the ground of wondrous enlightenment, the very end of the wondrous stage.

將前列位中，引《法華經》文，入此中共作一科者，即不煩也。

Taking up the Lotus Sūtra's own text already cited within the stages listed before, and gathering it together here as a single unified section, spares any further repetition.

Reading note

This closes the wondrous of stage (位妙) in its entirety — the fourth of the ten wondrous aspects, running from the opening of the "Medicinal Herbs" six-stage scheme back near the end of Fascicle 4 through this final item, "the stage from beginning to end." The three vehicles of teaching, practice, and realization (教乘/行乘/證乘) give the five ranks, the ten faiths, and the initial-abiding-through-equal-enlightenment span of the perfect teaching's own stage-scheme a structural backbone independent of any particular teaching's own vocabulary. The correlation with the four directions of the burning-house parable's ox-cart, and, still more strikingly, with the *Lotus Sūtra*'s own "Life Span of the Tathāgata" chapter — where the numbers of beings attaining each stage upon hearing the Buddha's revelation of his own eternal lifespan are read as a precise, one-to-one map of the entire stage-scheme from the ten abidings through equal enlightenment — is among this treatise's most systematic uses of the sūtra's own narrative details as doctrinal evidence. The wondrous of the three tracks (三法妙), the fifth of the ten wondrous aspects, follows immediately.

The Wondrous of the Three Tracks ○第五、三法妙

Chinese Source

○第五、三法妙者，斯乃妙位所住之法也。言三法者，即三軌也。軌名軌範，還是三法可軌範耳。此即七意：一、總明三軌。二、歷別明三軌。三、判麁妙。四、開麁顯妙。五、明始終。六、類三法。七、悉檀料簡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, as for the wondrous of the three tracks: this is precisely the dharma abided within by the wondrous stage itself. Speaking of the three dharmas, this refers to the three tracks. "Track" names a guiding rule and pattern — it is simply that these same three dharmas are themselves able to serve as this guiding rule and pattern. This is treated under seven headings: first, clarifying the three tracks in general; second, clarifying the three tracks in particular; third, judging the coarse and the wondrous; fourth, opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous; fifth, clarifying the beginning and the end; sixth, classifying other schemes as instances of the three dharmas; seventh, resolving doubts by way of the siddhāntas.

Clarifying the Three Tracks in General 總明三軌

Chinese Source

一、總明三軌者：一、真性軌。二、觀照軌。三、資成軌。名雖有三，秖是一大乘法也。經曰：「十方諦求，更無餘乘，唯一佛乘」。一佛乘即具三法，亦名第一義諦，亦名第一義空，亦名如來藏。此三不定三，三而論一。一不定一，一而論三，不可思議，不並不別，伊字、天目。故《大經》云：「佛性者，亦一、非一、非一非非一。亦一者，一切眾生悉一乘故。」此語第一義諦。「非一者，如是數法故。」此語如來藏。「非一非非一，數、非數法，不決定故。」此語第一義空。而皆稱亦者，鄭重也。秖是一法，亦名三耳。故不可單取，不可複取，不縱不橫，而三而一。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying the three tracks in general: first, the track of real nature; second, the track of contemplative illumination; third, the track of resource-and-accomplishment. Though named as three, this is simply the one single dharma of the Mahāyāna. The sūtra says: "Seeking earnestly throughout the ten directions, there is no other vehicle at all — only the one buddha-vehicle." This one buddha-vehicle is already complete with all three dharmas, also named the truth of ultimate meaning, also named the emptiness of ultimate meaning, also named the tathāgatagarbha. These three are not fixed as three — three, and yet discussed as one. The one is not fixed as one — one, and yet discussed as three — inconceivable, neither juxtaposed nor separate, the Siddhaṃ letter i, the three eyes upon a single face. Hence the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Buddha-nature is at once one, not one, and neither one nor not one. It is one because every sentient being alike belongs to the one vehicle" — this speaks of the truth of ultimate meaning. "It is not one because it consists of dharmas that can be numbered" — this speaks of the tathāgatagarbha. "It is neither one nor not one, since the dharma that can be numbered and the dharma that cannot be numbered are both indeterminate" — this speaks of the emptiness of ultimate meaning. And each of these is spoken of with the qualifier "at once," out of careful emphasis. This is simply the one single dharma, merely also named three. Hence, it cannot be taken singly, nor can it be taken as multiple — neither vertically arranged nor horizontally arranged, and yet three, and yet one.

前明諸諦，若開、若合，若麁、若妙等，已是真性軌相也。前明諸智，若開、若合，若麁、若妙，是觀照軌相也。前明諸行，若開、若合，若麁、若妙，已是資成軌相也。前明諸位，秖是修此三法所證之果耳。若然，何以重說？重說有三義：一者、前境、智、行，是因中所乘之三軌。今明乘是大乘，已至道場，證果所住之三軌也。二者、前作境、智等名別說，今作法名合說。三者、前直爾散說，不論本末。今遠論其本，即是性德三軌，亦名如來之藏；極論其末，即是修德三軌，亦名祕密藏。本末含藏一切諸法。從性德之三法，起名字之三法；因名字之三法，修觀行之三法；因觀行之三法，發相似之三法；乃至分證之三法，究竟之三法。自成三法化他三法，為是義故，宜應重說也。

The various truths discussed before, whether opened out or combined, whether coarse or wondrous, and the rest, are already the mark of the track of real nature. The various kinds of wisdom discussed before, whether opened out or combined, whether coarse or wondrous, are the mark of the track of contemplative illumination. The various practices discussed before, whether opened out or combined, whether coarse or wondrous, are already the mark of the track of resource-and-accomplishment. The various stages discussed before are simply the fruit realized through cultivating these same three dharmas. If this is so, why discuss them again? Discussing them again carries three senses: first, the object, wisdom, and practice discussed before are the three tracks ridden upon within the cause; here, it is clarified that the vehicle ridden is the Mahāyāna itself, already arrived at the very seat of enlightenment, the three tracks abided within as the realized fruit. Second, what was discussed before, under the separate names of object, wisdom, and so on, is discussed separately; here, it is discussed together under the single name "dharma." Third, what was discussed before was simply set out dispersedly, without discussing its own root and branch; here, tracing it back to its very root, this is precisely the three tracks of inherent virtue, also named the tathāgatagarbha; and following it out to its furthest branch, this is precisely the three tracks of cultivated virtue, also named the secret treasury. Root and branch together contain every dharma whatsoever. From the three dharmas of inherent virtue arises the three dharmas at the level of mere name; from the three dharmas at the level of mere name, one cultivates the three dharmas of contemplative practice; from the three dharmas of contemplative practice, one brings forth the three dharmas of resembling realization; on through the three dharmas of partial realization, and the three dharmas of ultimate realization. There is the threefold dharma accomplished for oneself, and the threefold dharma that transforms others — for the sake of these senses, it is fitting that they be discussed once again.

私謂一句即三句，三句即一句，名圓佛乘。記中既從如來藏一句出諸方便，此乃別判，例應通開。非一者，數法故。指此為如來藏，開出三藏中三乘事相方便。非一非非一，不決定故。指此一句，為第一義空，開出通教三人即事而真。亦一者，一切眾生悉一乘故。指此一句，為第一義諦，開出別教獨菩薩乘。此諸方便，悉從圓出。故經言：「於一佛乘，分別說三。」即此義也。

In my own view: a single phrase is already all three phrases, and the three phrases are already a single phrase — this is named the perfect buddha-vehicle. Since the commentary already derives the various expedient means from the single phrase of the tathāgatagarbha, this is a particular judgment that ought, by the same example, to be opened out in general as well. "Not one," since it consists of dharmas that can be numbered — pointing to this as the tathāgatagarbha opens out the phenomenal, expedient means of the three vehicles within the Tripiṭaka teaching. "Neither one nor not one," since it is indeterminate — pointing to this same single phrase as the emptiness of ultimate meaning opens out the shared teaching's own three kinds of persons, the very phenomenon itself already the real. "At once one," since every sentient being alike belongs to the one vehicle — pointing to this same single phrase as the truth of ultimate meaning opens out the distinct teaching's own bodhisattva-only vehicle. Every one of these expedient means issues, in every case, from the perfect teaching itself. Hence the sūtra says: "Within the one buddha-vehicle, I distinguish and teach three" — this is precisely this same sense.

Reading note

The wondrous of the three tracks (三法妙), the fifth of the ten wondrous aspects, restates the wondrous of the object, wisdom, and practice already unfolded across Fascicles 2 through 4 — but now discussed as the fruit realized rather than the cause cultivated, under the single name "dharma" rather than three separate names, and traced to its own root (inherent virtue, already present) and branch (cultivated virtue, brought forth through practice). The track of real nature (真性軌), the track of contemplative illumination (觀照軌), and the track of resource-and-accomplishment (資成軌) map directly onto the truth of ultimate meaning, the emptiness of ultimate meaning, and the tathāgatagarbha respectively — the same three terms already used for the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s own three-fold buddha-nature formula ("at once one, not one, neither one nor not one") quoted here. Zhiyi's own "personal view" closing this section performs the treatise's characteristic move once more: a single phrase of scripture is shown to already contain, latently, the seeds from which each of the three provisional teachings can be derived as a particular opening-out of the one perfect teaching.

From what dharma does the Buddha open out the various provisional vehicles? As the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra clarifies, buddha-nature is not one, consisting of dharmas that can be numbered, and teaches the three vehicles accordingly. Why is this? Since the various vehicles are already skillful means, while the tathāgatagarbha is itself the very substance — proceeding from the substance to bring forth skillful means — hence, it is said that these various provisional vehicles are gathered within the tathāgatagarbha. This is also grounded in the sūtra itself: the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says, "The śrāvaka's own saṅgha is named the conditioned saṅgha." It also says, "the six pāramitās are the adornment of merit." It also says, "the śrāvaka, since his power of concentration predominates, does not see buddha-nature." One should therefore know that this power of concentration is precisely merit, and merit itself is simply the conditioned. The Śrīmālādevī Sūtra names this the contaminated. Comparably: since views and thought within the three realms are not yet broken through, there remains something still being brought about — hence, this is named the conditioned; since grasping principle is not yet correct, this is named the contaminated; since it is not the dharma of wisdom, this is named merit. Here, reasoning from below upward, the same pattern ought to apply: since the two vehicles have not yet broken through transformational change, this, too, remains the conditioned; since ignorance is not yet cast off, this, too, is named the contaminated; since it is not the wisdom of the middle way, this, too, is named merit. For this reason, one should know that every expedient vehicle is, in every case, gathered within the track of resource-and-accomplishment, each issuing forth as a partial derivation from the single phrase of the Mahāyāna itself, not the ultimate dharma — hence it is said, "within the one buddha-vehicle, I distinguish and teach three." This is precisely this same sense. This is likewise, within the one buddha-vehicle, distinguishing and teaching five; likewise distinguishing and teaching seven; likewise distinguishing and teaching nine. According to this interpretation, the single phrase of the tathāgatagarbha opens out every manner of skillful means, every provisional vehicle's own dharma.

Yet the Mahāyānasaṃgraha clarifies three kinds of vehicle: the vehicle of principle, the vehicle of according, and the vehicle of attainment. "Principle" is precisely suchness prior to the path. "According" is precisely contemplating suchness, wisdom according with its object. "Attainment" is every practice and vow permeating through habituation, this habituation permeating the wisdom free of discrimination, uniting with the object free of discrimination, corresponding to suchness itself. These three senses, taken all at once, do indeed correspond to the three tracks — yet, in sequence, they are not truly fused together. Why is this? The ninth consciousness is suchness after the path; suchness itself has no phenomenal activity, and the root seeds of wisdom and practice are all accomplished through habituation stored within the ālaya-consciousness, from which the light of discrimination-free wisdom is attained, accomplishing the real, true nature. This, then, makes the vehicle of principle originally already present, while the vehicle of attainment is only now present, and suchness after the path only then becomes able to transform beings — is this not precisely a vertical arrangement? And if the three vehicles are all, every one, gathered within the ālaya-consciousness, this is likewise a horizontal arrangement, and moreover comes dangerously close to the doctrine of an original, primordially arisen awareness. Being already both vertical and horizontal at once, this stands opposed to the true Siddhaṃ letter i itself.

Originally, when the Tathāgata first appeared in the world, he wished to teach the real directly; yet, for the sake of those unable to bear it, he first used impermanence to dispel inversion, next used the purity of emptiness to sweep away attachment, next used the arousing of the mind through distinct, separate stages — and only afterward did he finally clarify permanence, bliss, self, and purity. Nāgārjuna, composing his treatise, extended the Buddha's own intent in just this way, using the emptiness of the unobtainable to wash away sealed-in attachment, habituating oneself to correspond with the emptiness of every dharma — this is named according with prajñā itself. How could this emptiness fail to empty ignorance as well? If ignorance itself is empty, where, then, could any seed possibly remain? Once every dharma has already been purified in this way, only then is emptiness itself pointed to and the teaching given, forming the mark of the fourfold statement. This language is empty and profound, holding to nothing at all. It is like a sickness already cured, only then may one take food, and this food, too, is properly digested. How, then, could one begin from the outset by grounding everything in the ālaya-consciousness to bring forth every dharma? Rooted still in the view of pride, never yet truly subdued at all, sealing oneself within this new text is like adding water to already-lengthening ice. One should therefore know that this treatise was not addressed to the heavily attached beings of a later age, but was rather only a single particular dharma-gate for beyond the three realms.

Moreover, if the ālaya-consciousness is truly complete with every dharma, how could it fail to be complete with suchness after the path as well? And if it is said to be complete with it, how, then, can it be said that suchness is not the eighth consciousness? It is to be feared that this remains only an expedient means, itself opened out from within the tathāgatagarbha. If one clings to it as merely expedient, this greatly obstructs the real; and if one takes it as the real itself, clinging to it in turn becomes a view fixed in mere words, containing much that is still infantile and unripe, and this may well endanger one's own life. If one is able to skillfully understand the intent behind refuting and establishing in this way, one may remain, with respect to every sūtra and treatise, entirely pure and free of any obstruction or attachment.

Reading note

The Tripiṭaka and shared teachings both display the same pattern already familiar from the wondrous of practice: each is "a teaching with no person who has actually attained it," since the very moment realization is reached, the vehicle that carried the practitioner there — the teaching's own words — has nothing further to say, so that "the children seeking a carriage outside the gate" (the burning-house parable) never quite receive one within these teachings' own terms. The critique of the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*'s three-vehicles scheme is a substantial piece of doctrinal polemic in its own right: Zhiyi's objection is that grounding "suchness" in the ālaya-consciousness as a prior substrate makes it either a sequential (vertical) or a merely co-located (horizontal) structure — precisely the two failure modes the Siddhaṃ letter *i* image is meant to rule out for a genuinely non-dual threefold reality. The historical aside on Nāgārjuna is presented as a corrective already built into the Madhyamaka tradition: emptiness, properly understood, already empties ignorance itself, so there is no need for a Yogācāra-style further substrate to explain how wisdom "gets stored." Zhiyi's own conclusion is notably even-handed — not a wholesale rejection of the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*, but a caution that its own scheme is best read as one more expedient opening-out from the tathāgatagarbha, dangerous only if mistaken for the final word.

Once the track of real nature is revealed, this is named the Dharma-body. Once contemplative illumination is revealed, this is named prajñā. Once resource-and-accomplishment is revealed, this is named liberation. These latter two are precisely the adornment of concentration and wisdom, adorning the Dharma-body itself. The Dharma-body is the vehicle's own substance; concentration and wisdom are its equipment. The text below says: "This carriage is tall and broad, adorned all over with every kind of jewel" — this is named the very vehicle ridden by the perfect teaching's own practitioner, arriving at omniscience itself, beyond the letter "dha," with no further letter left to speak of.

無字可說，亦應無乘可運。若自行運畢，乘義則休。若權化未畢，運他不休。故文云：「佛自住大乘，如其所得法，定慧力莊嚴，以此度眾生。」即其義也。譬如御者，運車達到，猶名為車。果乘亦爾，猶名為運。

Since there is no further letter left to speak of, ought there not also be no further vehicle left to convey anything at all? If one's own practice has already finished conveying itself, the sense of the vehicle accordingly comes to rest. Yet, if the transformation of others through skillful means is not yet finished, conveying others never comes to rest. Hence the text says: "The Buddha himself abides in the Mahāyāna, and, according to the dharma he himself has attained, adorned with the power of concentration and wisdom, uses this very dharma to save sentient beings" — this is precisely this same sense. It is like a charioteer: once the carriage has already conveyed him to his destination, it is nonetheless still named a carriage. The fruit-vehicle is exactly the same — it, too, is nonetheless still named "conveying."

復次，何必一向以運義釋乘？若取真性，不動、不出，則非運非不運。若取觀照、資成，能動、能出，則名為運。秖動出即不動出；即不動出是動出。即用而論體，動出是不動出；即體而論用，即不動出是動出。體用不二而二耳。例如：轉、不轉，皆阿鞞跋致；動、不動，皆是毘尼。以是義故，發趣、不發趣，皆名為乘也(云云)。

Again, why must one always interpret "vehicle" entirely in terms of the sense of conveyance? If one takes real nature itself, unmoving and not emerging, then this is neither conveying nor not conveying. If one takes contemplative illumination and resource-and-accomplishment, able to move and able to emerge, then this alone is named conveying. Yet moving-and-emerging is itself already unmoving-and-not-emerging; and unmoving-and-not-emerging is itself already moving-and-emerging. Discussed in terms of function, moving-and-emerging is unmoving-and-not-emerging; discussed in terms of substance, unmoving-and-not-emerging is moving-and-emerging. Substance and function are not two, and yet are two. Comparably: whether turning or not turning, both alike are avaivartika, the unretreating stage; whether moving or unmoving, both alike are vinaya. For this same reason, whether advancing or not advancing, both alike are named "vehicle," and so on.

Reading note

This closes the survey of all Four Teachings' own three tracks. Where the earlier three teachings each turned out to be, in Zhiyi's own phrase, "a teaching with no person who has actually attained it" — the vehicle running out of road exactly where realization begins — the perfect teaching's own vehicle is, from the outset, already unmoving and not emerging: the wish-fulfilling jewel simile (light and substance neither identical to nor separate from the jewel itself) gives contemplative illumination and resource-and-accomplishment the same non-dual relationship to real nature that the three virtues (Dharma-body/prajñā/liberation) and the Siddhaṃ letter *i* have carried throughout this treatise. The closing paradox — that a vehicle already arrived can still be "named a carriage," and that "moving is itself unmoving" — resolves the apparent tension between the perfect teaching's vehicle needing no further conveyance for its own sake, and its continuing, tirelessly, to convey others.

Judging the Coarse and the Wondrous 判麁妙

Chinese Source

三、明麁妙者，三藏於有為福德，論三法為乘。四念處是聞慧，乘於教乘，到四善根。四善根乘於行乘，到見諦。見諦乘於證乘，到無學。既是權法，出三界外，以真為證。證則不運、不見實乘。嗚呼自責，欲問世尊，為失、為不失？即此意也。半字漸引，非究竟義，是故三法皆麁也。通教即空慧，三法為乘巧，餘意大同。乾慧地乘於教乘，性地乘於行乘，八人見地乘於證乘。此亦偏說，是故為麁。別教以資成資於觀照，觀照開於真性，三法為乘。十信乘教、十住乘行、十地乘證，到妙覺薩婆若中住。緣，修成即謝，唯真修在。若爾，資成在前，觀照居次，真性在後。此三豎別，縱非大乘，此三並異，橫非大乘，是方便法，是故為麁也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous: the Tripiṭaka teaching, discussing its own conditioned merit, treats the three dharmas as its vehicle. The four applications of mindfulness are the wisdom of hearing, riding the vehicle of the teaching, arriving at the four good roots. The four good roots ride the vehicle of practice, arriving at the seeing of the truths. The seeing of the truths rides the vehicle of realization, arriving at the stage beyond further learning. Since this is already a provisional dharma, emerging beyond the three realms and taking the real as its own realization — once realization is reached, there is no further conveyance, and the real vehicle itself is never actually seen. "Alas!" one reproaches oneself, wishing to ask the World-Honored One whether it has been lost, or not lost — this is precisely this same sense. Drawn along gradually by half-words, this is not yet the ultimate sense — hence, all three dharmas here remain coarse. The shared teaching, taking the wisdom that form is itself empty, treats the three dharmas as its own skillful vehicle, its remaining sense largely the same. The ground of dry wisdom rides the vehicle of the teaching; the ground of nature rides the vehicle of practice; the ground of the eight kinds of person and the ground of seeing ride the vehicle of realization. This, too, is a one-sided account — hence, it remains coarse. The distinct teaching takes resource-and-accomplishment as the resource for contemplative illumination, and contemplative illumination opens out into real nature, treating the three dharmas as its vehicle: the ten faiths ride the teaching, the ten abidings ride the practice, the ten grounds ride the realization, arriving at wondrous enlightenment's own abiding within omniscience. Yet, once what is cultivated through conditions is accomplished, it is thereupon set aside, and only the cultivation of the real itself remains. If this is so, then resource-and-accomplishment comes first, contemplative illumination follows next, and real nature comes last — these three, vertically distinct one from another, though not the Mahāyāna in a sequential sense, and these three, juxtaposed as separate from one another, though not the Mahāyāna in a parallel sense either, remain, in either case, an expedient dharma — hence, this, too, remains coarse.

圓教點實相為第一義空，名空為縱。第一義空即是實相，實相不縱，此空豈縱？點實相為如來藏，名之為橫。如來藏即實相，實相不橫，此藏豈橫？故不可以縱思、不可以橫思，故名不可思議法，即是妙也。秖點空、藏為實相，空縱、藏橫，實相那不縱橫？秖點空為如來藏，空既不橫，藏那得橫？點如來藏為空，藏既不縱，空那得縱？點實相為空、藏，實相非縱非橫，空、藏亦非縱非橫，宛轉相即，不可思議，故名為妙。秖點如來藏為廣，點第一義空為高，故言其車高廣。如來藏即實相，故其車非廣。第一義空即實相，故其車非高。秖實相是空，那得非高？秖實相是如來藏，那得非廣？又點實相為如來藏，故言眾寶莊校，又多僕從而侍衛之。點實相為第一義空，故言有大白牛，肥壯多力，行步平正，其疾如風，智慧無染，名為白。能破惑，故名多力。中道慧，名平正。入無功用，故其疾如風。不思議三法，共成大車，豈有縱橫並別之異？

As for the perfect teaching: pointing to the real mark as the emptiness of ultimate meaning names this emptiness as vertical. Yet the emptiness of ultimate meaning is itself already the real mark, and the real mark is not vertical at all — how, then, could this emptiness be vertical? Pointing to the real mark as the tathāgatagarbha names this as horizontal. Yet the tathāgatagarbha is itself already the real mark, and the real mark is not horizontal at all — how, then, could this treasury be horizontal? Hence, it cannot be thought of as vertical, nor can it be thought of as horizontal — hence, it is named the inconceivable dharma, which is itself the wondrous. Simply pointing to emptiness and the treasury as the real mark, with emptiness vertical and the treasury horizontal — how could the real mark itself be either vertical or horizontal? Simply pointing to emptiness as the tathāgatagarbha — since emptiness is already not horizontal, how could the treasury become horizontal? Pointing to the tathāgatagarbha as emptiness — since the treasury is already not vertical, how could emptiness become vertical? Pointing to the real mark as both emptiness and the treasury, the real mark is neither vertical nor horizontal, and emptiness and the treasury, too, are neither vertical nor horizontal, turning and interpenetrating one another completely, inconceivable — hence, this is named the wondrous. Simply pointing to the tathāgatagarbha as breadth, and pointing to the emptiness of ultimate meaning as height — hence, it is said, "this carriage is tall and broad." The tathāgatagarbha is itself the real mark — hence, this carriage is not merely broad. The emptiness of ultimate meaning is itself the real mark — hence, this carriage is not merely tall. Yet, since the real mark is itself emptiness, how could it fail to be tall? And since the real mark is itself the tathāgatagarbha, how could it fail to be broad? Again, pointing to the real mark as the tathāgatagarbha, it is accordingly said, "adorned all over with every kind of jewel," and "attended and guarded by many servants." Pointing to the real mark as the emptiness of ultimate meaning, it is accordingly said, "there is a great white ox, fat and sturdy, of great strength, its gait level and even, swift as the wind" — wisdom free of every taint is named "white"; being able to break through confusion is named "of great strength"; the wisdom of the middle way is named "level and even"; entering effortless function is why it is "swift as the wind." The inconceivable three dharmas together accomplish this one great carriage — how, then, could there possibly be any distinction of vertical, horizontal, juxtaposed, or separate remaining at all?

如是教乘，不縱不橫，五品所乘，到於似解。如是行乘，不縱不橫，似解所乘，到於十住。如是證乘，不縱不橫，十住所乘，到於妙覺薩婆若中住，故名妙乘。又云：「是乘微妙，清淨第一。」故瓦官建講，人夢聽者駕乘闐門而出；彼處建講，人夢黃衣滿路。以相則之，邪正明矣！若將此麁妙等乘，約五味者，乳教一麁一妙。酪教一麁。生蘇三麁一妙。熟蘇二麁一妙。眾經悉帶縱橫方便，說不縱、不橫之真實，故言為麁。今經正直捨方便，故加之以妙(云云)。

This same teaching-vehicle, neither vertical nor horizontal, is ridden by the five ranks, arriving at the resembling understanding. This same practice-vehicle, neither vertical nor horizontal, is ridden by the resembling understanding, arriving at the ten abidings. This same realization-vehicle, neither vertical nor horizontal, is ridden by the ten abidings, arriving at wondrous enlightenment's own abiding within omniscience — hence, this alone is named the wondrous vehicle. It is also said: "This vehicle is subtle and wondrous, pure and supreme." Hence, when a lecture was once held at the Wa-guan Monastery, people dreamed that the listeners rode out through the crowded gate upon their own carriages; while, at a place where a different lecture was held, people dreamed that the road was filled with figures in yellow robes instead. Judged by these very signs, the deviant and the correct are made perfectly clear! If one takes this scheme of coarse and wondrous vehicles and correlates it with the Five Flavors: the milk teaching has one coarse and one wondrous; the cream teaching has one coarse; the curdled-butter teaching has three coarse and one wondrous; the clarified-butter teaching has two coarse and one wondrous. Every other sūtra carries some admixture of the vertical or horizontal as an expedient means, teaching the neither-vertical-nor-horizontal real only indirectly through it — hence, they are spoken of as coarse. The present sūtra alone, directly and without any expedient means, sets these aside entirely — hence, it alone is further named wondrous, and so on.

Reading note

The coarse/wondrous verdict on the three provisional teachings repeats the pattern already found throughout this fascicle: each keeps its own three tracks either sequential (the distinct teaching's resource-and-accomplishment, then contemplative illumination, then real nature, one after another) or separable, and both failure modes are named directly as "vertical" and "horizontal." The perfect teaching's own demonstration is a small masterpiece of circular, mutually-implicating argument: emptiness cannot be "vertical" because it is itself already the real mark, and the tathāgatagarbha cannot be "horizontal" for the identical reason — each of the three terms turns out, on inspection, to already be each of the others. The mapping of the burning-house parable's ox-cart onto this same structure (height = emptiness, breadth = the tathāgatagarbha, the ox's own qualities = the wisdom that drives it) is a vivid, concrete payoff for what is otherwise a highly abstract argument. The dueling-dream anecdote about Wa-guan Monastery is a piece of local lore about Zhiyi's own teaching career, offered as informal, almost folkloric confirmation that the perfect teaching's own lectures were recognized, even in dreams, as the one that actually delivers a vehicle.

Opening the Coarse to Reveal the Wondrous 開麁顯妙

Chinese Source

四、開麁顯妙者，約《大經》三句也。經言：「佛性亦一」者，一切眾生悉一乘故。此是不動、不出之一乘，故具足三法，不縱、不橫。夫有心者皆備此理，而其家大小都無知者，是故為麁。今示眾生諸覺寶藏，耘除草穢，開顯藏金。一切無礙人，一道出生死。十方諦求，更無餘乘，唯一佛乘，是故為妙。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous: this follows the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra's own three statements. The sūtra says: "Buddha-nature is at once one," since every sentient being alike belongs to the one vehicle. This is the one vehicle that is unmoving and does not emerge, and hence is already complete with the three dharmas, neither vertical nor horizontal. Now, everyone who possesses a mind at all already possesses this same principle in full — yet the members of this same household, great and small alike, remain entirely unaware of it — hence, this is coarse. Here, the treasury of every sentient being's own awakening is revealed, weeding away the coarse growth to open and reveal the buried gold within. Every unobstructed person walks the single path that leaves birth-and-death behind. Seeking earnestly throughout the ten directions, there is no other vehicle at all — only the one buddha-vehicle — hence, this is wondrous.

經言：「佛性亦非一非非一。」數、非數法，不決定故。若執緣修智慧，定能顯理；慧自非理，則照用不明，不見佛性，是故為麁。今開定執之慧，即不決定慧，即慧而理、即理而慧，不執著數，定三定一，不著非數，非三非一，如此乃名無著妙慧。能破一切定相及不定相，亦無能破、所破。如輪王能破能安，如日除闇生物，如醫除膜養珠，即是大乘不縱不橫之妙慧也。

The sūtra says: "Buddha-nature is also neither one nor not one," since the numbered dharma and the unnumbered dharma are both indeterminate. If one clings to the wisdom cultivated through conditions as though it could, by itself, fix and determine the revealing of principle, then, since this wisdom is not itself principle, its own illuminating function remains unclear, and buddha-nature is not seen — hence, this is coarse. Here, the wisdom clung to as fixed and determinate is opened out into the indeterminate wisdom itself — wisdom is itself already principle, and principle is itself already wisdom, clinging neither to number, fixing it as three or fixing it as one, nor clinging to the unnumbered, as neither three nor one — this alone is named the wondrous wisdom free of attachment. It is able to break through every fixed mark and every indeterminate mark alike, with no breaker and nothing broken remaining either. Like the wheel-turning king, able both to conquer and to bring peace; like the sun, dispelling darkness and giving rise to life; like the physician, removing the cataract to nurture the pearl-like eye beneath — this is precisely the Mahāyāna's own wondrous wisdom, neither vertical nor horizontal.

經言：「佛性亦非一」說三乘故。即是三乘、五乘、七乘等諸方便乘。若住諸乘，但是事善，及以偏真，通入處近，是故為麁。今若決了諸乘即是如來藏，藏名佛性。從人天善乃至別乘，皆不動本法，即是於妙。當知，三句攝一切法，無非佛性，悉皆是妙。無麁可待，即絕待妙也。

The sūtra says: "Buddha-nature is also not one," since it teaches the three vehicles. This refers to the three vehicles, the five vehicles, the seven vehicles, and every other expedient vehicle. If one abides fixedly within any of these vehicles, this remains merely phenomenal goodness, or a one-sided real, its own penetration reaching only a nearby place — hence, this is coarse. Here, if one fully resolves that every one of these vehicles is itself already the tathāgatagarbha, and this treasury is itself named buddha-nature — then, from the goodness of humans and gods all the way through the bodhisattva-only vehicle, none of it ever departs from this same fundamental dharma — this alone is the wondrous. One should therefore know that these three statements together gather in every dharma whatsoever, none of it anything other than buddha-nature itself, every one of them, alike, already wondrous. With no coarse left to set against it, this is precisely the wondrous read in the absolute sense.

Reading note

This maps the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s own threefold buddha-nature formula, already used to open this whole discussion of the three tracks, onto the absolute reading directly: "at once one" opens the coarse of never having recognized one's own buried treasure; "neither one nor not one" opens the coarse of clinging to cultivated wisdom as though it could fix principle on its own; "also not one" opens the coarse of stopping short within any single provisional vehicle — three, five, or seven vehicles alike. Each dissolves once its object is seen as already the tathāgatagarbha itself, so that even the goodness of humans and gods turns out never to have departed from the one fundamental dharma. The three similes closing the second statement — the wheel-turning king, the sun, and the physician removing a cataract — are stock images for wisdom that acts by revealing rather than by imposing, consistent with the "absolute" reading's basic claim throughout this treatise: nothing new is added to reach the wondrous, only what was obscuring it is cleared away.

Clarifying Beginning and End 明始終

Chinese Source

五、明始終者，不取五品教乘為始，乃取凡地一念之心，具十法界、十種相性，為三法之始。何者？十種相性秖是三軌。如是體即真性軌；如是性，性以據內，即是觀照軌；如是相者，相以據外，即是福德，是資成軌。力者是了因，是觀照軌；作者是萬行精勤，即是資成；因者是習因，屬觀照。緣者是報因，屬資成；果者是習果，屬觀照；報者是習報，屬資成。本末等者，空等，即觀照；假等即資成；中等即真性。直就一界十如論於三軌。今但明凡心一念，即皆具十法界，一一界悉有煩惱性相、惡業性相、苦道性相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, clarifying beginning and end: this does not take the five ranks' own vehicle of the teaching as the beginning, but rather takes the single thought-moment of the mind at the ordinary stage — already complete with the ten dharma-realms and the ten kinds of mark-and-nature — as the beginning of the three dharmas. Why is this? The ten kinds of mark-and-nature are simply the three tracks themselves. Such an essence is precisely the track of real nature. Such a nature — nature being based on what is internal — is precisely the track of contemplative illumination. Such a mark — mark being based on what is external — is precisely merit and virtue, the track of resource-and-accomplishment. Such a power is the revealing cause, the track of contemplative illumination. Such an activity is the diligent exertion of every practice, precisely resource-and-accomplishment. Such a cause is the habituated cause, belonging to contemplative illumination. Such a condition is the retributive cause, belonging to resource-and-accomplishment. Such a fruit is the habituated fruit, belonging to contemplative illumination. Such a retribution is the habituated retribution, belonging to resource-and-accomplishment. As for the equality of beginning and end: the equality of emptiness corresponds to contemplative illumination; the equality of the provisional corresponds to resource-and-accomplishment; the equality of the middle corresponds to real nature. This discusses the three tracks directly in terms of a single dharma-realm's own ten suchnesses. Here, however, only the ordinary mind's single thought-moment is being clarified — this alone already possesses all ten dharma-realms within it, and each and every realm already possesses the mark-and-nature of affliction, the mark-and-nature of evil karma, and the mark-and-nature of the path of suffering.

若有無明煩惱性相，即是智慧觀照性相。何者？以迷明故起無明。

If there is the mark-and-nature of ignorance and affliction, this is itself already the mark-and-nature of wisdom's own contemplative illumination. Why is this? It is precisely because clarity itself is deluded that ignorance arises.

若解無明，即是於明。《大經》云：「無明轉，即變為明。」《淨名》云：「無明即是明。」當知，不離無明而有於明，如氷是水，如水是氷。

If ignorance is understood, this is itself already clarity. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Ignorance, transformed, is itself changed into clarity." The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "Ignorance is itself clarity." One should therefore know that clarity is never apart from ignorance — it is like ice being water, and water being ice.

又凡夫心，一念即具十界，悉有惡業性相，秖惡性相即善性相，由惡有善，離惡無善，翻於諸惡，即善資成。如竹中有火性，未即是火事，故有而不燒，遇緣事成，即能燒物。惡即善性，未即是事。

Again, the ordinary being's own mind, in a single thought-moment, is already complete with the ten realms, each and every one already possessing the mark-and-nature of evil karma. Yet this very mark-and-nature of evil is itself already the mark-and-nature of good: it is by means of evil that good exists at all, and, apart from evil, there is no good — turning evil around is itself the resource-and-accomplishment of good. It is like the nature of fire already present within bamboo — this is not yet the actual event of fire, and so it is present without burning; once it meets the right condition and the event is accomplished, it becomes able to burn things. Evil is itself the nature of good, though not yet the actual event of good.

遇緣成事，即能翻惡。如竹有火，火出還燒竹。惡中有善，善成還破惡。故即惡性相是善性相也。凡夫一念，皆有十界識、名色等苦道性相。迷此苦道，生死浩然，此是迷法身為苦道，不離苦道別有法身。如迷南為北，無別南也。若悟生死，即是法身，故云：苦道性相即是法身性相也。

Once it meets the right condition and the event is accomplished, it becomes able to turn evil around. It is like the fire already present within bamboo: once the fire emerges, it burns the very bamboo that held it. Good is present within evil, and, once good is accomplished, it in turn breaks through evil. Hence this very mark-and-nature of evil is the mark-and-nature of good. In the ordinary being's own single thought-moment, there is already present, in every one of the ten realms, the mark-and-nature of the path of suffering — consciousness, name-and-form, and the rest. Deluded with respect to this path of suffering, birth-and-death surges on vast and unending — yet this is simply the Dharma-body itself, mistaken for the path of suffering; apart from the path of suffering, there is no separate Dharma-body to be found. It is like mistaking south for north — there is no separate south to be found elsewhere. If birth-and-death is awakened to, this is itself already the Dharma-body — hence it is said: the mark-and-nature of the path of suffering is itself the mark-and-nature of the Dharma-body.

夫有心者，皆有三道性相，即是三軌性相。故《淨名》云：「煩惱之儔，為如來種。」此之謂也。若言如是力、如是作者，菩提心發也，即是真性等萌動。如是因者，即是觀照萌動。如是緣者，即是資成萌動。如是果者，由觀照萌動，成習因，感得般若習果滿也。

Whoever possesses a mind at all already possesses the mark-and-nature of the three paths, which is itself already the mark-and-nature of the three tracks. Hence the Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "The very company of afflictions is itself the seed of the Tathāgata" — this is precisely what is meant here. As for such a power and such an activity: these are the arousing of the bodhi-mind, precisely the first stirring of real nature and the rest. Such a cause is precisely the stirring of contemplative illumination. Such a condition is precisely the stirring of resource-and-accomplishment. Such a fruit, arising from the stirring of contemplative illumination, becomes the habituated cause, and, through this, the fulfillment of prajñā's own habituated fruit is obtained.

Such a retribution, arising from the stirring of resource-and-accomplishment, becomes the conditioning cause, and, through this, the fulfillment of liberation's own retributive fruit is obtained. Since the fruit and its retribution are both fulfilled, the Dharma-body, too, is fulfilled — this is precisely the ultimate fulfillment of the three virtues, named the secret treasury. As for the equality of beginning and end: the three tracks of inherent virtue lie hidden and dormant, neither vertical nor horizontal; the three tracks of cultivated virtue stand revealed and manifest, neither vertical nor horizontal. Hidden and dormant, they are equal in their suchness, equal in their number, equal in their wondrousness; revealed and manifest, they are equal in their suchness, equal in their number, equal in their wondrousness — hence it is said, "equal." This, too, is the equality of emptiness, the equality of the provisional, and the equality of the middle, and so on.

Reading note

This section relocates the three tracks from a doctrinal scheme judged teaching by teaching to the single thought-moment of an unenlightened mind, using the ten suchnesses already established in Fascicles 2–3 as the bridge. Every one of the ten dharma-realms, present at once within that single thought, already carries the mark-and-nature of affliction, of evil karma, and of the path of suffering — and each of these three is worked through in turn as already identical with its own opposite: ignorance is itself clarity (as ice is water), evil is itself the resource for good (as the fire-nature already latent in bamboo needs only the right condition to burn), and the path of suffering is itself already the Dharma-body (as mistaking south for north creates no second, missing south). The chapter then re-derives the three tracks' own arising and fulfillment — the bodhi-mind's first stirring, through habituated cause and fruit, to the "ultimate fulfillment of the three virtues" — entirely from within this same single thought, closing with the same inherent/cultivated (性德/修德) distinction already used to open this whole discussion of the three tracks.

Seventh, correlating and connecting the three bodies: the track of real nature is precisely the Dharma-body; contemplative illumination is precisely the reward-body; resource-and-accomplishment is precisely the response-body. As the Suvarṇaprabhāsottama Sūtra, in its new translation, says: "In dependence upon the Dharma-body, the reward-body is obtained to exist; in dependence upon the reward-body, the response-body is obtained to exist" — this is precisely as already clarified above, that, in dependence upon the wondrous of the object, the wondrous of wisdom is obtained to exist, and, in dependence upon the wondrous of wisdom, the wondrous of practice is obtained to exist. That same text says: "The Buddha's own true Dharma-body is just like empty space; responding to beings, it manifests a form, like the moon reflected in water." The reward-body is precisely the moon in the sky itself. This present text says: "The Buddha himself abides in the Mahāyāna" — this is precisely the body of the real mark, just like empty space.

「定慧力莊嚴」慧如天月，定如水月。又云：「唯佛與佛，乃能究盡諸法實相。」即是法身。「我所得智慧，微妙最第一。」即是報身。「名稱普聞」即是應身。又，非生、現生等，是應身也。或示己身，即法身、報身。或示他身，即報、應。「我以相嚴身，光明照十方，為說實相印。」實相印即法身；照十方即應身；相嚴身即報身。又「深達罪福相，遍照於十方。」即報身；「微妙淨法身」即法身；「具相三十二」即應身。三軌名異，義即三身，故《普賢觀》云：「佛三種身，從方等生。」《法界性論》云：「水銀和真金，能塗諸色像，功德和法身，處處應現往。」若此三身不縱不橫，妙決了三身入法身妙。歷七位妙(云云)。

"Adorned with the power of concentration and wisdom" — wisdom is like the moon in the sky, and concentration is like the moon reflected in the water. And again: "Only a buddha, together with another buddha, is able to fathom the real mark of every dharma fully" — this is precisely the Dharma-body. "The wisdom that I myself have attained is subtle and wondrous, supreme above all else" — this is precisely the reward-body. "Renown heard everywhere" is precisely the response-body. Again, neither being born nor manifesting birth, and the rest, are the response-body. Sometimes manifesting one's own body is the Dharma-body together with the reward-body; sometimes manifesting another's body is the reward-body together with the response-body. "I adorn my own body with these very marks, its light illuminating the ten directions, and, for this, teach the seal of the real mark" — the seal of the real mark is precisely the Dharma-body; illuminating the ten directions is precisely the response-body; the body adorned with these marks is precisely the reward-body. Again: "Penetrating deeply into the mark of transgression and merit, illuminating everywhere throughout the ten directions" is precisely the reward-body; "the subtle and wondrous, pure Dharma-body" is precisely the Dharma-body; "complete with the thirty-two marks" is precisely the response-body. Though the three tracks bear different names, their sense is precisely the three bodies — hence the Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra says: "The buddha's three kinds of body arise from the vaipulya teachings." The Treatise on the Nature of the Dharma-realm says: "Mercury blended with true gold is able to coat every kind of image; merit blended with the Dharma-body responds and manifests everywhere." If these three bodies are neither vertical nor horizontal, the wondrous fully resolves the three bodies into the wondrous of the Dharma-body itself. Passing through the seven stages, the wondrous, and so on.

The Three Nirvāṇas 三涅槃

Chinese Source

八、類通三涅槃者，地人言：「但有性淨、方便淨。實相名為性淨涅槃；修因所成為方便淨涅槃。」今以理性為性淨涅槃，修因所成為圓淨涅槃，此則義便；薪盡火滅為方便淨涅槃，此文便。若將修因所成為方便涅槃者，以薪盡火滅為何等涅槃？故知應有三涅槃，三涅槃即是三軌。文云：「是法不可示，言辭相寂滅。」又云：「諸法從本來，常自寂滅相。」是性淨涅槃。又云：「皆以如來滅度而滅度之」即圓淨涅槃。又云：「我成佛已來，甚大久遠。」久修業所得，慧光照無量，亦是圓淨涅槃。數數唱生，處處現滅，於此夜滅度，如薪盡火滅，豈非方便淨涅槃？

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, correlating and connecting the three nirvāṇas: the Dilun masters say, "There are only the nature-pure and the expedient-means pure. The real mark is named the nature-pure nirvāṇa; what is accomplished through cultivated cause is the expedient-means pure nirvāṇa." Here, however, principle-in-itself is taken as the nature-pure nirvāṇa, and what is accomplished through cultivated cause is taken as the perfect pure nirvāṇa — this fits the sense better; the firewood exhausted and the fire extinguished is taken as the expedient-means pure nirvāṇa — this fits the text better. If what is accomplished through cultivated cause were instead taken as the expedient-means nirvāṇa, then to what nirvāṇa would the firewood exhausted and the fire extinguished then belong? One should therefore know that there must indeed be three nirvāṇas, and these three nirvāṇas are themselves precisely the three tracks. The text says: "This dharma cannot be pointed to directly; the mark of every word and phrase is itself quiescent cessation." And again: "All dharmas, from their very origin, are forever, of themselves, marked by quiescent cessation" — this is the nature-pure nirvāṇa. And again: "Every one of them, I cause to attain extinction through the Tathāgata's own extinction" — this is precisely the perfect pure nirvāṇa. And again: "Since my own attainment of buddhahood, an extremely vast span of time has already passed" — attained through long cultivation of practice, the light of wisdom illuminating without measure — this, too, is the perfect pure nirvāṇa. Repeatedly proclaiming birth, manifesting extinction everywhere, attaining extinction on this very night, like the firewood exhausted and the fire extinguished — is this not precisely the expedient-means pure nirvāṇa?

《大經》題稱「大般涅槃」，翻為大滅度。大者，其性廣博，即據性淨。度者，到於彼岸，智慧滿足，即據圓淨。滅者，煩惱永盡，斷德成就，即據方便淨。此三涅槃，即是三軌也。

The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra's own title, "mahāparinirvāṇa," is translated as "great extinction and crossing-over." "Great" refers to its nature being vast and expansive — this is precisely based on the nature-pure. "Crossing-over" refers to arriving at the further shore, wisdom fulfilled in its entirety — this is precisely based on the perfect pure. "Extinction" refers to affliction permanently exhausted, the virtue of severance accomplished — this is precisely based on the expedient-means pure. These three nirvāṇas are themselves precisely the three tracks.

The Three Jewels as a Single Essence 一體三寶

Chinese Source

九、類通一體三寶者，真性即法寶；觀照即佛寶；資成即僧寶。故法性不動名不覺；佛智契理，故佛名為覺。事和、理和，故僧名和合。《思益》云：「知覺名為佛；知離名為法；知無名為僧。」此是一體三寶。故下文云：「佛自住大乘」佛是佛寶；大乘是法寶；「如其所得法，以此度眾生。」即是與理和，復與眾生和，即是僧寶。「世間相常住」，名法寶；「於道場知已」，名佛寶；「導師方便說」上與理和，下與眾生和，名僧寶。一體三寶，非一之一，不三之三，此之三一，不縱不橫，稱之為妙。歷七位(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, correlating and connecting the Three Jewels as a single essence: real nature is precisely the Dharma Jewel; contemplative illumination is precisely the Buddha Jewel; resource-and-accomplishment is precisely the Saṅgha Jewel. Hence the Dharma-nature, unmoving, is named "not awakened"; the Buddha's own wisdom, in accord with principle, is why the Buddha is named "awakened." Harmony in practice and harmony in principle are why the Saṅgha is named "harmonious assembly." The Viśeṣacintibrahma-paripṛcchā Sūtra says: "Knowing awakening is named the Buddha; knowing freedom is named the Dharma; knowing there is nothing is named the Saṅgha" — this is the Three Jewels as a single essence. Hence the text below says: "The Buddha himself abides in the Mahāyāna" — the Buddha here is the Buddha Jewel, and the Mahāyāna is the Dharma Jewel; "according to the dharma he himself has attained, he uses this very dharma to save sentient beings" — this is precisely being in harmony with principle, and, further, being in harmony with sentient beings, which is precisely the Saṅgha Jewel. "The mark of the world abides eternally" is named the Dharma Jewel; "having already known this, seated at the place of enlightenment" is named the Buddha Jewel; "the guiding teacher expounds it through skillful means" — above, in harmony with principle, and, below, in harmony with sentient beings — is named the Saṅgha Jewel. The Three Jewels as a single essence, being one that is not merely one, and three that are not merely three — this same three-in-one, neither vertical nor horizontal, is named the wondrous. Passing through the seven stages, and so on.

The Three Virtues 三德

Chinese Source

十、類通三德者，《大經》三德，共成大涅槃。此經三軌，共成大乘。彼明法身德，此云實相。彼云：「佛性者亦一」一切眾生悉一乘故，亦是指實相為一乘。彼處明般若德；此經明其智慧門難解難入。「我所得智慧，微妙最第一」乃至「決了聲聞法，是諸經之王」皆是般若。彼經明解脫德；此經明數數示現，現生現滅，隨所調伏眾生之處，自既無累，令他解脫，乃至收取萬善事中功德悉得證果，豈非解脫？二經義合，碌碌之徒，隨名異解，譬聞天帝，不識憍尸。唯知《涅槃》佛性之文，不見雙樹有一乘之旨。彼文親說佛性亦一，一即一乘。而人云：「此乃《涅槃》一乘是佛性，《法華》一乘非佛性。」若言《法華》不明佛性者，《涅槃》不應遙指云：「八千聲聞於法華中，得受記莂，如秋收冬藏，見如來性，更無所作。」而人云：「《涅槃》有遙指之文，此中無佛性之語。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, correlating and connecting the three virtues: the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra's own three virtues together accomplish the great nirvāṇa; this present sūtra's three tracks together accomplish the Mahāyāna. That sūtra clarifies the virtue of the Dharma-body; here, this is called the real mark. That sūtra says, "Buddha-nature is also one," since every sentient being alike belongs to the one vehicle — this, too, points to the real mark as the one vehicle. That sūtra clarifies the virtue of prajñā; this present sūtra clarifies that its own gateway of wisdom is difficult to understand and difficult to enter. "The wisdom that I myself have attained is subtle and wondrous, supreme above all else," down through "fully resolving the śrāvaka's own dharma, this is the king of every sūtra" — all of this is prajñā. That sūtra clarifies the virtue of liberation; this present sūtra clarifies its repeatedly manifesting birth and manifesting extinction, according with wherever sentient beings are to be tamed and subdued — being itself already free of every entanglement, and causing others, too, to attain liberation, down through gathering up the merit accomplished amid every kind of good deed until the fruit itself is fully realized — is this not precisely liberation? These two sūtras' own senses converge — yet mediocre people, following the mere names, interpret them differently, comparable to hearing of the god Indra without recognizing that Kauśika is the very same being. Knowing only the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra's own text on buddha-nature, they fail to see that, beneath the twin śāla trees, the very same sense of the one vehicle is present. That sūtra's own text explicitly says buddha-nature is also one, and this very oneness is precisely the one vehicle. Yet people say: "This 'one vehicle' of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra is buddha-nature, while the Lotus Sūtra's own 'one vehicle' is not buddha-nature." If it is claimed that the Lotus Sūtra does not clarify buddha-nature, then the Nirvāṇa Sūtra itself ought not to have pointed toward it from afar, saying: "The eight thousand śrāvakas, within the Lotus Sūtra, received their own prediction of buddhahood, like the autumn harvest stored away for winter — they saw the Tathāgata's own nature, with nothing further left to accomplish." Yet people say: "The Nirvāṇa Sūtra does indeed contain this text pointing from afar, but the Lotus Sūtra itself contains no word of buddha-nature."

今據此文：「種種性相義，而我皆已見。」既言種種，何獨簡於佛性耶？又，「世間相常住。於道場知已，導師方便說。」豈非佛性之文耶？《論》云：「佛性水常不輕。」知眾生有佛性。又《涅槃》三德為祕密藏。安置諸子祕密藏中，我亦不久當入其中，此即自他俱入祕密。此經云：「佛自住大乘，以此度眾生。終不以小乘濟度諸眾生，悉以如來滅度而滅度之。」如是自他俱入如來滅度。

Here, however, based on this very text: "The sense of every kind of nature and mark, I have already, in every case, seen" — since this speaks of every kind, why should buddha-nature alone be excluded from it? Again: "The mark of the world abides eternally. Having already known this, seated at the place of enlightenment, the guiding teacher expounds it through skillful means" — is this not itself already a text on buddha-nature? The Treatise says: "The water of buddha-nature never disparages" — knowing that sentient beings possess buddha-nature. Again, the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's own three virtues together form the secret treasury: "I place every one of my own children within the secret treasury; I myself, too, before long, shall enter within it as well" — this is precisely self and other both entering the secret treasury together. This present sūtra says: "The Buddha himself abides in the Mahāyāna, and, with this very dharma, saves sentient beings — never, in the end, using the Hīnayāna to ferry sentient beings across, but causing every one of them to attain extinction through the Tathāgata's own extinction." This, too, is self and other both entering the Tathāgata's own extinction together.

滅度秖是涅槃，涅槃秖是祕藏。《釋論》云：「《法華》為祕藏，兩經文義宛宛恒同。」何故諸人，苟欲抗異？若文義舛隔，作同想無罪，今文義本合，離之何福？但《涅槃》以佛性為宗，非不明一乘義；今經以一乘為宗，非不明佛性義。赴機異說，其義常通也。

Extinction is itself simply nirvāṇa, and nirvāṇa is itself simply the secret treasury. The Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom says: "The Lotus Sūtra is itself the secret treasury" — the two sūtras' own text and sense are, in every particular, forever the same. Why, then, do so many people insist on setting them against each other as different? Were their text and sense genuinely at odds, there would be no fault in regarding them as the same; but, since their text and sense are, from the outset, already in agreement, what merit could there be in separating them? It is simply that the Nirvāṇa Sūtra takes buddha-nature as its own cardinal doctrine, without thereby failing to clarify the sense of the one vehicle; and this present sūtra takes the one vehicle as its own cardinal doctrine, without thereby failing to clarify the sense of buddha-nature. Responding to differing capacities with differing accounts, their own sense remains, throughout, mutually consistent.

若三德縱橫即是麁；不縱橫即是妙。歷七位(云云)。

If the three virtues are vertical or horizontal, this is precisely coarse; if neither vertical nor horizontal, this is precisely the wondrous. Passing through the seven stages, and so on.

Reading note

This ten-fold correlation is the payoff of the whole structure built up since the opening of the three tracks: once real nature, contemplative illumination, and resource-and-accomplishment are established as an inherent, non-sequential triad in a single thought-moment, every other threefold scheme in Buddhist doctrine turns out to be the very same triad under a different name, drawn from a different sūtra or treatise for a different audience. The list runs from the most concrete (the three paths of affliction, karma, and suffering — already fully worked out in Fascicles 2–3 of this treatise) through a contested point of contemporary scholastic dispute (the three consciousnesses, staged as a live disagreement between Dilun and Yogācāra masters, resolved the same way Fascicle 5 of the Mohe Zhiguan resolves the parallel Dilun/Shelun dispute over the three thousand realms — not by picking a winner, but by showing each name captures only one side of a single mind) to the most exalted (the three virtues of the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, with Zhiyi's own extended argument that that sūtra's "one vehicle is buddha-nature" and the Lotus Sūtra's own one vehicle are the identical claim, misread as opposed only by readers who, in his own simile, have heard of the god Indra without realizing that "Kauśika" names the very same being). The five-part refrain repeated after each item — principle, then the five ranks, the six faculties, the stages from initial abiding onward, and wondrous enlightenment — ties every one of these ten dharmas back to the six identities already governing the whole treatise, so that no matter which name is used, the same single path from ordinary mind to buddhahood is being described throughout.

Resolving Doubts Through the Siddhāntas 悉檀料簡

Chinese Source

七、悉檀料簡者。

問：
十種三法及餘一切皆是三軌者，唯應三軌，何意異說？

答：
眾生機宜不同，應隨機設逗，悉檀方便引接耳。隨俗故異，稱便宜故異，逐對治故異，令人入道故異。朝三暮四，撫眾狙而皆悅，苦塗水洗，養嬰兒以適時。善巧赴機，故方圓任物，譬千車而同轍，豈守一而疑諸？

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, resolving doubts through the siddhāntas.

Question: If the ten kinds of three dharmas, and everything else besides, are all alike simply the three tracks, then only the three tracks alone ought to be spoken of — why, then, are these differing accounts given?

Answer: Sentient beings' own capacities and what is fitting for them are not all the same, and one ought to respond according to each capacity, setting forth whatever is fitting — the siddhāntas are simply skillful means for drawing beings onward and receiving them. They differ according to what accords with the world; they differ according to what is named for convenience; they differ according to what pursues the antidote; they differ according to what causes a person to enter the path. "Three in the morning and four in the evening" delighted every one of the monkeys alike; bitter medicine and a warm bath, given to nurture an infant, are each administered at the fitting time. Skillfully responding to capacity, the square and the round are each employed as fits the thing at hand — comparable to a thousand carriages all following the very same rut — how, then, could one cling rigidly to one single account and doubt every other?

今通用四悉檀，歷十法，論妙，不妙；具說三軌，共成大乘。大乘之中，備有三法及一切法，不相混亂，即是世界悉檀。資成資發智慧，以生善故，是為人悉檀。觀照破惑，諸惡滅故，是對治悉檀。

真性實理，為第一義悉檀。一段眾生，宜以大乘名說，得四利益也。

Here, the four siddhāntas are used throughout, passing through the ten dharmas, discussing the wondrous and the not-yet-wondrous; the three tracks are set forth fully, together accomplishing the Mahāyāna. Within the Mahāyāna, the three dharmas, and every dharma besides, are all fully present without being confused with one another — this is precisely the mundane siddhānta. Resource-and-accomplishment provides the resource that gives rise to wisdom, since it produces what is wholesome — this is the individual siddhānta. Contemplative illumination breaks through confusion, since every evil is thereby extinguished — this is the therapeutic siddhānta.

Real nature, the real principle itself, is the siddhānta of ultimate meaning. For this one particular group of sentient beings, it is fitting to teach using the very name "Mahāyāna," and thereby obtain all four benefits.

備說三德為大涅槃。雖三點上下而無縱、表裏而無橫，一不相混，三不相離，即世界悉檀。善利殃釁不干，故得挺然累表，是故解脫即為人悉檀。般若如金剛，隨所擬皆碎，即是對治悉檀。法身即第一義也。一段眾生聞三德名，即獲四利矣！舉初、舉後、中間例然。

The three virtues are set forth in full as the great nirvāṇa. Though the three dots of the Siddhaṃ letter i are arranged above and below, there is no verticality to them; though set out front and back, there is no horizontality to them — one, without being confused together; three, without being separated apart — this is precisely the mundane siddhānta. Wholesome benefit, untouched by any calamity or fault, is why it stands out, distinct, transcending every entanglement — hence, liberation is precisely the individual siddhānta. Prajñā is like the vajra, shattering whatever it is aimed at — this is precisely the therapeutic siddhānta. The Dharma-body is precisely the siddhānta of ultimate meaning. This one particular group of sentient beings, upon hearing the names of the three virtues, thereby obtains all four benefits at once! Citing the first case and the last, the middle case follows the identical pattern.

次、明妙不妙。《論》云：「三悉檀是世諦，心所行處，可破可壞。第一義悉檀，是心不行處，諸佛聖人心所得法，不可破壞，即是真諦。」若然者，比四悉檀為二諦所攝。更有中道，復云何攝？

Next, clarifying the wondrous and the not-yet-wondrous. The Treatise says: "The three siddhāntas belong to the conventional truth — they are where the mind actively operates, and can be broken and destroyed. The siddhānta of ultimate meaning is where the mind does not operate at all, the very dharma attained within the minds of every buddha and sage, unable to be broken or destroyed — this is precisely the real truth." If this is so, then the four siddhāntas, matched against this scheme, are gathered under the two truths — yet there remains, beyond this, the middle way as well — under which of the two, then, is it to be gathered?

若不攝中，但是藏、通之意，此悉檀為麁。今言俗有、真無，是隔異法，便是三悉檀心所行處，可破可壞。中道第一義，非有、非無，有無不二則無隔異，無異即真諦。前三悉檀所通，止至化城，化城非實，故可破可壞，可壞為麁。今中道無異，又通至寶所，無能過、無能滅，故不可壞，稱之為妙。若餘經說中道第一義悉檀，與此經不殊。但餘經帶阿羅漢所得，為第一義悉檀，故不稱妙。此經正直捨方便，但有圓實四悉檀，是故為妙。若不決三悉檀入第一義，是復為麁；若決一一悉檀，皆有第一義者，是則為妙。

If the middle is not gathered under either, then this remains merely the sense of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings, and this account of the siddhāntas remains coarse. Here, however, when it is said that the conventional exists while the real is empty, this is a dharma divided and set apart — and this is exactly where the three siddhāntas' own mind actively operates, able to be broken and destroyed. The middle way of ultimate meaning, however, is neither existent nor empty; and, existence and emptiness being non-dual, there is no division or separation at all — being free of any such division is precisely the real truth. What the prior three siddhāntas lead to reaches only as far as the phantom city — the phantom city is not itself real, and so can be broken and destroyed; being able to be destroyed, it remains coarse. The middle way here, free of any division, leads instead all the way to the very place of treasure — nothing can surpass it, and nothing can destroy it — hence, unable to be destroyed, it is named the wondrous. If other sūtras, too, teach a siddhānta of ultimate meaning as the middle way, this does not differ from the present sūtra's own account. It is simply that, in those other sūtras, this is taught bearing traces of what the arhat has attained, taken as the siddhānta of ultimate meaning — hence, it is not there named the wondrous. This present sūtra, however, directly and without any expedient means, sets these aside entirely, possessing only the perfect and real four siddhāntas — hence, this is named the wondrous. If the three siddhāntas are not resolved into the siddhānta of ultimate meaning, this, too, remains coarse; if each and every siddhānta is resolved as already containing the siddhānta of ultimate meaning within it, this, then, is the wondrous.

五品弟子，假名四悉檀。六根淨，相似四悉檀。初住至等覺，分真四悉檀。妙覺，究竟四悉檀，是故稱妙。

At the disciple-stage of the five ranks, this is the four siddhāntas under a provisional name. With the purification of the six faculties, this is the four siddhāntas in resemblance. From the initial abiding through equal enlightenment, this is the four siddhāntas in a portion of the truth. At wondrous enlightenment, this is the four siddhāntas in the ultimate — hence, this is named the wondrous.

此五番明妙，從因至果，以辨自行妙，半如意珠竟。

These five rounds of clarifying the wondrous, from cause through to fruit, distinguishing the wondrous of one's own practice, close out the first half of the wish-fulfilling jewel.

Reading note

Zhiyi closes the wondrous of the three tracks — the fifth of the ten wondrous aspects, and the largest single item covered so far — with a self-aware methodological defense: why give ten different names for one structure at all? The Zhuangzi-derived image of the monkey keeper, whose animals are delighted by "three in the morning, four in the evening" though the total never changes, makes the same point the four siddhāntas make more systematically — that responding to differing capacities with differing names is not inconsistency but skillful accommodation. The harder question, raised in the second half of this item, is how the four siddhāntas relate to the two truths and the middle way: since the three worldly siddhāntas belong to the conventional truth and the siddhānta of ultimate meaning to the real truth, where does the middle way itself fit? Zhiyi's answer — that only a siddhānta of ultimate meaning free of any division between existence and emptiness reaches "the place of treasure" rather than merely the "phantom city" reached by other sūtras' own versions of the same four siddhāntas — repeats the burning-house parable's own two way-stations one last time, now mapped onto the coarse/wondrous distinction between this sūtra's four siddhāntas and every other's. The closing line's own accounting is worth noting directly: these five items (總明三軌 through 悉檀料簡) are named "the wondrous of one's own practice" (自行妙), and their completion is called only "half" the wish-fulfilling jewel — the wondrous of the three tracks concerns what a practitioner attains for themselves, while the remaining five of the ten wondrous aspects, beginning with the wondrous of sympathetic response that opens Fascicle 6, take up the "other half": what is accomplished for the sake of others. This closes the wondrous of the three tracks (三法妙) in its entirety, the fifth of the ten wondrous aspects, and with it Fascicle 5 of the source text.
