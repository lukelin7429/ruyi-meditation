The Perfect Teaching's Stage

Fascicle 5 opens the perfect teaching's own stage — the "most real matter" and sixth stage of the "Medicinal Herbs" parable's scheme, closing the wondrous of stage.

Status: in progress Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

The Most Real Stage: The Perfect Teaching's Own Stage 明最實位・圓教位

Chinese Source

明最實位者，即圓教位也。此為十意：一、簡名義。二、明位數。三、明斷伏。四、明功用。五、明麁妙。六、明位興。七、明位廢。八、開麁顯妙。九、引經。十、妙位始終。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for clarifying the most real stage: this is precisely the perfect teaching's own stage. This is treated under ten headings: first, distinguishing the name and the sense; second, clarifying the number of stages; third, clarifying severing and subduing; fourth, clarifying its function; fifth, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous; sixth, clarifying the rise of the stage; seventh, clarifying the abolition of the stage; eighth, opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous; ninth, citing scripture; tenth, the wondrous stage from beginning to end.

Distinguishing the Name and the Sense 簡名義

Chinese Source

一、簡名義者，若圓別不同自有十意，下辨體中說。今約通、別、圓，三句料簡：一、名通義圓。二、名別義圓。三、名義俱圓。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, as for distinguishing the name and the sense: as to how the perfect differs from the distinct, this carries its own ten senses, explained below within the clarification of the Essence. Here, this is distinguished according to three statements — the shared, the distinct, and the perfect: first, the name shared while the sense is perfect; second, the name distinct while the sense is perfect; third, both the name and the sense perfect.

名通義圓者，下文云：「我等今日，真阿羅漢。普於其中，應受供養。」又云：「我等今日，真是聲聞，以佛道聲，令一切聞。」此名與通、藏同而義異。何者？彼但殺四住之賊，無明尚在，此不生義偏，故天女曰：「結習未盡，華則著身。」今殺通、別兩惑，得如來滅度，故殺賊義圓。又彼是分段不生，界外猶生。《寶性論》云：「二乘於無漏界，生三種意陰。」今則分段、變易二俱不生，不生義圓。彼是界內應供，非界外應供。《淨名》曰：「其供汝者，不名福田。」則應供義偏。今則普於其中應受供養，則應供義圓。彼但小乘，從他聞四諦聲，則聲偏、聞偏。今能令一切法界，聞一實四諦、佛道之聲，使一切聞，則聲聞義圓。故知依義不依語，從圓判位也。

As for the name being shared while the sense is perfect: the text below says, "We, today, are true arhats, and ought, everywhere within it, to receive offerings." And again: "We, today, are truly śrāvakas, and, by the very sound of the buddha-path, cause everyone to hear it." These names are the same as those of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings, yet their sense differs. Why is this? Those teachings' own arhat merely slays the thief of the four abiding afflictions, while ignorance still remains — this is a one-sided sense of "no longer arising"; hence the heavenly maiden says, "The habitual residue of the fetters not yet exhausted, the flowers still cling to the body." Here, however, one slays both the shared and distinct teachings' own confusion at once, attaining the Tathāgata's own extinction — hence, the sense of "slaying the thief" is perfect. Again, those teachings' own arhat is free of segmented birth-and-death, yet is still born beyond the three realms. The Ratnagotravibhāga says: "The two vehicles, within the uncontaminated realm, still give rise to three kinds of mental aggregates." Here, however, one is free of both segmented birth-and-death and transformational birth-and-death together — hence, the sense of "no longer arising" is perfect. Those teachings' own arhat is worthy of offerings only within the three realms, not worthy of offerings beyond them. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "Whoever makes offering to you is not, thereby, named a field of merit." This is a one-sided sense of "worthy of offerings." Here, however, one is everywhere within it worthy of receiving offerings — hence, the sense of "worthy of offerings" is perfect. Those teachings' own arhat is merely the Hīnayāna, hearing the sound of the Four Noble Truths from another — this is a one-sided sense of both "sound" and "hearing." Here, however, one is able to cause the entire dharma-realm to hear the sound of the one real Four Noble Truths, the very sound of the buddha-path, making everyone hear it — hence, the sense of "śrāvaka" itself is perfect. One should therefore know that this relies on the sense, not on the mere words, in judging the stage according to the perfect teaching.

名別義圓者，如五十二位，名與別同。而初、中、後位，圓融妙實，隨自意語，非是教道方便，依義不依語，應從圓判位也。

As for the name being distinct while the sense is perfect: as with the fifty-two stages, whose names are the same as the distinct teaching's own. Yet the initial, middle, and final stages are perfectly fused, wondrous, and real, spoken according to one's own intent rather than as the teaching's own expedient path — relying on the sense, not on the mere words, one ought to judge the stage according to the perfect teaching.

名義俱圓者，文云：「開示悟入，皆是佛之知見。」佛一切種智知、佛眼見，此之知見，無有缺減。又，入如來室，坐如來座，以如來莊嚴，此則名義俱圓，判於圓位也。

As for both the name and the sense being perfect: the text says, "Opening, showing, awakening, and entering are all alike the buddha's own knowledge and vision." Knowing through the buddha's own wisdom knowing all modes, seeing through the buddha-eye — this knowledge and vision lacks nothing at all, diminished in no respect. Again, entering the Tathāgata's room, sitting in the Tathāgata's seat, adorned with the Tathāgata's own adornment — this, then, is both the name and the sense perfect, judged as the perfect stage.

Reading note

Fascicle 5 opens the sixth and final stage of the "Medicinal Herbs" parable's six-stage scheme — the perfect teaching's own "most real matter," announced already in Fascicle 4. Zhiyi lays out a ten-part outline for the whole discussion before beginning, then opens with a three-way distinction (name shared but sense perfect / name borrowed from the distinct teaching but sense perfect / both name and sense perfect) that recurs as a methodological pattern throughout this treatise: the same word — arhat, śrāvaka, the fifty-two stages — can mean something categorically different depending on which teaching is speaking it, and the treatise insists on judging by sense rather than surface vocabulary. The three proof-texts illustrating "name shared, sense perfect" — the heavenly maiden's flowers sticking to the unliberated (from the *Vimalakīrti Sūtra*), the *Ratnagotravibhāga* on the two vehicles' subtle remaining birth, and the *Vimalakīrti Sūtra* again on who truly constitutes a field of merit — are all classic proof-texts for exactly this kind of "same word, deeper meaning" argument in Mahāyāna polemic.

Clarifying the Number of Stages 明位數

Chinese Source

二明位數者，又為三：一、明數。二、引證多少。三、料簡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, as for clarifying the number of stages: this, too, is of three parts — first, clarifying the number itself; second, citing evidence for how many there are; third, resolving doubts.

數者，人解不同。有言：頓悟即佛，無復位次之殊。引《思益》云：「如此學者，不從一地至一地。」又有師言：頓悟初心即究竟圓極，而有四十二位者，是化鈍根方便，立淺深之名耳。引《楞伽》云：「初地即二地，二地即三地，寂滅真如，有何次位？」又有師言：初頓悟至十住，即是十地；而說有十行、十迴向、十地者，此是重說耳。今謂諸解，悉是偏取。然平等法界，尚不論悟與不悟，孰辨淺深？既得論悟與不悟，何妨論於淺深？究竟大乘，無過《華嚴》、《大集》、《大品》、《法華》、《涅槃》，雖明法界平等、無說無示，而菩薩行位終自炳然。

As for the number itself: people's understandings differ. Some say: sudden awakening is itself buddhahood, with no further distinction of sequential stages at all — citing the Viśeṣacintibrahma-paripṛcchā Sūtra: "Such a student as this does not proceed from one ground to another ground." Others say: sudden awakening at the initial mind is itself the ultimate, perfect extreme, and the existence of forty-two stages is merely an expedient means for transforming the dull-faculty practitioner, establishing names of shallow and deep only for their sake — citing the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra: "The first ground is itself the second ground, the second ground is itself the third ground — within quiescent extinction and suchness itself, what sequential stage could there possibly be?" Still others say: the initial sudden awakening, up through the ten abidings, is itself already the ten grounds; and the further speaking of the ten practices, the ten dedications, and the ten grounds is merely a repeated restatement of the same thing. Here, it is held that every one of these interpretations is one-sided. Yet, within the equality of the dharma-realm itself, one does not even discuss awakened versus unawakened at all — how, then, could one possibly distinguish shallow from deep? But since one is already able to discuss awakened versus unawakened, what obstacle could there be to also discussing shallow versus deep? Among the ultimate Mahāyāna scriptures, none surpasses the Avataṃsaka, the Mahāsaṃnipāta, the Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra, the Lotus Sūtra, and the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra — and though these clarify the dharma-realm's own equality, beyond all speech and beyond all demonstration, the bodhisattva's own practice-stages remain, in the end, perfectly evident of themselves nonetheless.

又有人言：平等法界，定無次位。今例難此語：真諦有分別耶？真諦無分別耶？見真之者，判七賢、七聖、二十七賢聖等。今實相平等，雖無次位，見實相者，判次位何咎？《大論》云：「譬如入海，有始入者，到中者，至彼岸者。」若見真判位，如江河深淺，若實相判位，如入海深淺。故《普賢觀》云：「大乘因者，諸法實相。」大乘果者，亦諸法實相，論諸次位，非徒臆說。隨順契經，以四悉檀明位無妨，還約七種以明階位，謂十信、十住、十行、十迴向、十地、等覺、妙覺。

Again, some say: the equality of the dharma-realm admits, with certainty, of no sequential stage whatsoever. Here, this statement may be challenged by the same example: does the real truth admit of discrimination, or does the real truth admit of no discrimination at all? Those who see the real distinguish the seven worthies, the seven sages, the twenty-seven worthies and sages, and the rest. If the equality of the real mark, though admitting of no sequential stage, may nonetheless be distinguished into sequential stages by whoever sees the real mark — what fault could there possibly be in this? The Treatise says: "It is like entering the sea — there is the one who has only just entered, the one who has reached its middle, and the one who has arrived at the far shore." If one distinguishes stages in terms of seeing the real, this is like the differing depths of a river; if one distinguishes stages in terms of the real mark itself, this is like the differing depths of the sea itself. Hence the Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra says: "The cause of the Mahāyāna is the real mark of every dharma." The fruit of the Mahāyāna, too, is the real mark of every dharma — discussing these various sequential stages is not mere idle speculation. According with the scriptures themselves, clarifying the stages by means of the four siddhāntas presents no obstacle at all; here, too, the stage is clarified according to seven kinds, namely the ten faiths, the ten abidings, the ten practices, the ten dedications, the ten grounds, equal enlightenment, and wondrous enlightenment.

The Five Ranks, Before the Ten Faiths 十信之前・五品位

Chinese Source

今於十信之前，更明五品之位(云云)。若人宿殖深厚，或值善知識、或從經卷，圓聞妙理，謂一法一切法，一切法一法，非一非一切，不可思議，如前所說，起圓信解，信一心中具十法界，如一微塵有大千經卷。欲開此心而修圓行。圓行者，一行一切行。略言為十：謂識一念，平等具足，不可思議。傷己昏沈，慈及一切。又知此心，常寂常照。用寂照心，破一切法，即空、即假、即中。又識一心諸心，若通、若塞。能於此心，具足道品，向菩提路。又解此心，正、助之法。又識己心，及凡、聖心。又安心不動、不墮、不退、不散。雖識一心，無量功德，不生染著，十心成就。舉要言之，其心念念悉與諸波羅蜜相應，是名圓教初隨喜品位。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, before the ten faiths, the stage of the five ranks is further clarified, and so on. If a person's past cultivation has been deep and abundant, whether through encountering a good spiritual friend or through a scriptural text, hearing the perfect, wondrous principle — that one dharma is every dharma, that every dharma is one dharma, that it is neither one nor every, inconceivable, exactly as explained before — one gives rise to perfect faith and understanding, believing that the single mind is already complete with all ten dharma-realms, just as a single mote of dust contains within it a scroll as vast as the great trichiliocosm itself. Wishing to open this mind and cultivate the perfect practice — the perfect practice being one in which a single practice is every practice — this may be stated briefly as ten: knowing this single thought to be, of itself, equally complete, inconceivable; grieving over one's own confusion and torpor, extending loving-kindness to all; further knowing that this mind is ever quiescent and ever illuminating; using this mind of quiescent illumination to break through every dharma, at once empty, at once provisional, at once the middle; further knowing that, within this single mind, every mind is either penetrating or obstructed; being able, within this mind, to become complete with the factors of the path, turning toward the road of bodhi; further understanding, within this mind, the correct and auxiliary dharmas; further knowing one's own mind, and the mind of the ordinary being and of the sage alike; further settling the mind so that it is unmoving, unfalling, unretreating, and unscattered. Though one knows that this single mind possesses immeasurable merit and virtue, one gives rise to no defiled attachment toward it — the ten minds are thereby accomplished. To state the essential point: this mind, thought after thought, corresponds entirely to every pāramitā — this is named the perfect teaching's own initial rank of sympathetic joy.

行者圓信始生，善須將養。若涉事紛動，令道芽破敗。唯得內修理觀，外則受持、讀誦大乘經典，聞有助觀之力。內外相藉，圓信轉明，十心堅固。《金剛般若》云：「一日三時，以恒河沙身布施，不如受持一句功德。」初品觀智如目，次品讀誦如日。日有光故，目見種種色。《論》云：「於實名了因，於餘名生因。福不趣菩提，二能趣菩提。」聞有巨益，意在於此，是名第二品位。

The practitioner, this perfect faith just having arisen, must skillfully nourish and sustain it. Should one become entangled in worldly affairs and their agitation, this would cause the sprout of the path to break and wither. One should therefore only cultivate the contemplation of principle inwardly, while, outwardly, upholding, reading, and reciting the Mahāyāna scriptures, since hearing them carries a power that assists this contemplation. Inward and outward mutually reinforcing one another, this perfect faith grows ever more distinct, and the ten minds become firm and secure. The Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā says: "Giving one's own body as gifts, as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, three times a day, does not equal the merit of upholding a single phrase." The first rank's own contemplative wisdom is like the eye; the second rank's own reading and reciting is like the sun — since the sun possesses light, the eye is thereby able to see every manner of form. The Treatise says: "With respect to the real, this is named the revealing cause; with respect to what remains, this is named the generating cause. Merit alone does not proceed toward bodhi; only these two together are able to proceed toward bodhi." The great benefit of hearing the scriptures lies precisely in this sense — this is named the stage of the second rank.

行者內觀轉強，外資又著，圓解在懷，弘誓熏動，更加說法，如實演布。《安樂行》云：「但以大乘法答。設以方便隨宜，終令悟大。」《淨名》云：「說法淨，則智慧淨。」《毘曇》云：「說法解脫，聽法解脫。」說法開導，是前人得道全因緣，化功歸己十心則三倍轉明，是名第三品位。

As the practitioner's own inward contemplation grows stronger still, and outward support grows firmer as well, perfect understanding held within, the universal vow stirred and moved, one further takes up teaching the dharma, proclaiming and spreading it exactly as it truly is. The Peaceful Practices chapter says: "Answering only by means of the Mahāyāna dharma; and, even where skillful means are employed as fitting, in the end always causing awakening to the great vehicle." The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "Where the teaching of the dharma is pure, wisdom, too, is pure." The Abhidharma says: "Teaching the dharma is liberation; hearing the dharma is liberation." Teaching the dharma to guide others is the very complete cause and condition by which another person attains the path, and, since this transformative merit returns to oneself as well, the ten minds accordingly grow three times more distinct — this is named the stage of the third rank.

上來前熟觀心，未遑涉事。今正觀稍明，即傍兼利物。能以少施，與虛空法界等。使一切法趣檀，檀為法界。《大品》云：「菩薩少施，超過聲聞、辟支佛上，當學般若。」即此意也。餘五亦如是。事相雖少，運懷甚大。此則理觀為正，事行為傍，故言兼行布施。事福資理，則十心彌盛，是名第四品位。

Up to this point, one has first thoroughly cultivated the contemplation of mind, without yet having leisure to engage in worldly affairs. Now, as this correct contemplation grows somewhat more distinct, one incidentally also takes up benefiting others. Being able, through even a small act of giving, to equal empty space and the dharma-realm itself, causing every dharma to converge upon giving, giving itself becoming the dharma-realm. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra says: "The bodhisattva's small act of giving surpasses even the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha — one should study prajñā" — this is precisely this same sense. The remaining five pāramitās are exactly the same. Though the outward phenomenal act is small, the mind it carries is vast indeed. Here, the contemplation of principle remains the primary practice, and worldly action remains only incidental to it — hence, this is called "incidentally practicing giving." As this worldly merit nourishes principle, the ten minds grow ever more abundant — this is named the stage of the fourth rank.

行人圓觀稍熟，理事欲融，涉事不妨理，在理不隔事，故具行六度。若布施時，無二邊取著，十法界依、正，一捨一切捨，財、身及命，無畏等施。若持戒時，性重、譏嫌等無差別；五部重輕，無所觸犯。若行忍時，生、法寂滅，荷負安耐。若行精進，身心俱淨，無間無退。若行禪時，遊入諸禪，靜散無妨。若修慧時，權實二智，究了通達；乃至世智，治生產業，皆與實相不相違背。具足解釋佛之知見，而於正觀，如火益薪。此是第五品位。

The practitioner's own perfect contemplation, growing somewhat mature, causes principle and worldly action to fuse together — engaging in worldly action no longer obstructs principle, and abiding in principle no longer sets itself apart from worldly action; hence, one now practices the six pāramitās directly and completely. When giving, one is free of attachment to either extreme; the dependent and direct retributions across all ten dharma-realms, relinquishing one is relinquishing all — wealth, body, and life itself, and the giving of fearlessness, are all given equally. When upholding the precepts, there is no distinction between grave inherent wrong and what the world finds objectionable; the grave and light offenses across the five divisions of the vinaya are none of them ever transgressed. When practicing patience, birth and dharmas alike are seen as quiescent extinction, bearing every burden with calm endurance. When practicing diligence, body and mind alike are purified, without interruption and without retreat. When practicing dhyāna, one roams freely into every dhyāna, whether stillness or dispersal presenting no obstacle. When cultivating wisdom, both the provisional and the real wisdom are thoroughly penetrated and understood; even worldly wisdom itself, and one's own livelihood and occupation, are none of them ever in conflict with the real mark. Complete with the full explanation of the buddha's own knowledge and vision, and with respect to correct contemplation, this is like fire fed further with firewood. This is the stage of the fifth rank.

Comparing the Five Ranks 五品位料簡

Chinese Source

如此五品圓信功德，東西八方不可為喻。雖是初心，而勝聲聞無學功德，具如經說。若欲比決取解，類如三藏家別、總四念處位。義推如通教乾慧地位，亦如伏忍位。義推亦得是別教十信位(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

The merit and virtue of this perfect faith across these five ranks cannot be compared to anything in the eight directions of east and west alike. Though this is still the initial mind, it already surpasses the merit and virtue of the śrāvaka beyond further learning — set out fully as the sūtra itself explains. If one wishes to compare this against other schemes to aid understanding, it is comparable to the Tripiṭaka teaching's own particular and general applications of mindfulness; reasoned by sense, it is like the shared teaching's own ground of dry wisdom, and likewise like the stage of subduing forbearance; reasoned by sense, it may also be taken as the distinct teaching's own stage of the ten faiths, and so on.

私謂：五品位是圓家方便，初欲令易解準小望大，如三藏之五停心。初品圓信法界，上信諸佛，下信眾生，皆起隨喜，是圓家慈停心，遍對治法界上嫉妬。第二品讀誦大乘文字，文字是法身氣命，讀誦明利是圓家數息停心，遍治法界上覺觀。說法品能自淨心，亦淨他心，是圓家因緣停心，遍治法界上自、他癡。癡去故，諸行去乃至老死去，兼行六度品，是圓家不淨停心。六蔽初名貪欲，若捨貪欲，欲因、欲果皆捨。捨故無復報身，非淨非不淨也。正行六度品，是圓家念佛停心。正行六度時，即事而理，理不妨道，事妨於道。即事而理，無障可論。大意如此(云云)。

In my own view: the stage of the five ranks is the perfect teaching's own expedient means, meant at the outset to make understanding easier by measuring the small against the great, just as the Tripiṭaka teaching has its own five methods of stilling the mind. The first rank's perfect faith in the dharma-realm — believing upward in every buddha, believing downward in every sentient being, giving rise to sympathetic joy toward both alike — is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through loving-kindness, universally treating jealousy and envy throughout the dharma-realm. The second rank's reading and reciting of the Mahāyāna's own text — the text being the very vital breath of the Dharma-body — reading and reciting it with clarity and sharpness is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through counting the breath, universally treating applied and sustained thought throughout the dharma-realm. The rank of teaching the dharma, able to purify one's own mind and likewise purify the minds of others, is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through causes and conditions, universally treating delusion, both one's own and others', throughout the dharma-realm. Since delusion departs, every formation departs, on through old age and death departing as well; the rank of incidentally practicing the six pāramitās is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through the contemplation of impurity. The first of the six coverings is named craving desire; if one relinquishes craving desire, both its cause and its fruit are relinquished together. Since this is relinquished, no further retribution-body remains — neither pure nor impure. The rank of directly practicing the six pāramitās is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through mindfulness of the Buddha. When directly practicing the six pāramitās, the very phenomenon itself is already principle; principle presents no obstacle to the path, while worldly phenomena alone obstruct the path — yet, since the very phenomenon itself is already principle, there remains no obstacle whatsoever to discuss. This is, in general outline, the sense of it, and so on.

Reading note

The five ranks (五品位) are among the most celebrated schemes in all of Tiantai practice — Zhiyi's own answer to what an ordinary, still-unenlightened practitioner in this present age can actually do: sympathetic joy (perfect faith alone, before any external practice), reading and reciting the Mahāyāna scriptures, teaching the dharma to others, incidentally practicing the six pāramitās, and finally practicing the six pāramitās directly, principle and worldly action now fully fused. Zhiyi's own "personal view" closing this section maps all five ranks onto the Tripiṭaka teaching's five methods of stilling the mind one-to-one — a deliberate small-to-great pedagogical bridge, since the five methods of stilling the mind were the very first, most elementary stage translated at the start of Fascicle 4's "middle medicinal herb." The five ranks precede the ten faiths in the perfect teaching's own count, and answer the "how many stages" survey above by showing that even the earliest rungs of the perfect ladder already outstrip the arhat's own final attainment in the lesser teachings.
