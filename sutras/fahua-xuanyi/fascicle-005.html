The Perfect Teaching's Stage

Fascicle 5 opens the perfect teaching's own stage — the "most real matter" and sixth stage of the "Medicinal Herbs" parable's scheme, closing the wondrous of stage.

Status: in progress Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T33, no. 1716 (Zhiyi, recorded by Guanding)

The Most Real Stage: The Perfect Teaching's Own Stage 明最實位・圓教位

Chinese Source

明最實位者，即圓教位也。此為十意：一、簡名義。二、明位數。三、明斷伏。四、明功用。五、明麁妙。六、明位興。七、明位廢。八、開麁顯妙。九、引經。十、妙位始終。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for clarifying the most real stage: this is precisely the perfect teaching's own stage. This is treated under ten headings: first, distinguishing the name and the sense; second, clarifying the number of stages; third, clarifying severing and subduing; fourth, clarifying its function; fifth, clarifying the coarse and the wondrous; sixth, clarifying the rise of the stage; seventh, clarifying the abolition of the stage; eighth, opening the coarse to reveal the wondrous; ninth, citing scripture; tenth, the wondrous stage from beginning to end.

Distinguishing the Name and the Sense 簡名義

Chinese Source

一、簡名義者，若圓別不同自有十意，下辨體中說。今約通、別、圓，三句料簡：一、名通義圓。二、名別義圓。三、名義俱圓。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, as for distinguishing the name and the sense: as to how the perfect differs from the distinct, this carries its own ten senses, explained below within the clarification of the Essence. Here, this is distinguished according to three statements — the shared, the distinct, and the perfect: first, the name shared while the sense is perfect; second, the name distinct while the sense is perfect; third, both the name and the sense perfect.

名通義圓者，下文云：「我等今日，真阿羅漢。普於其中，應受供養。」又云：「我等今日，真是聲聞，以佛道聲，令一切聞。」此名與通、藏同而義異。何者？彼但殺四住之賊，無明尚在，此不生義偏，故天女曰：「結習未盡，華則著身。」今殺通、別兩惑，得如來滅度，故殺賊義圓。又彼是分段不生，界外猶生。《寶性論》云：「二乘於無漏界，生三種意陰。」今則分段、變易二俱不生，不生義圓。彼是界內應供，非界外應供。《淨名》曰：「其供汝者，不名福田。」則應供義偏。今則普於其中應受供養，則應供義圓。彼但小乘，從他聞四諦聲，則聲偏、聞偏。今能令一切法界，聞一實四諦、佛道之聲，使一切聞，則聲聞義圓。故知依義不依語，從圓判位也。

As for the name being shared while the sense is perfect: the text below says, "We, today, are true arhats, and ought, everywhere within it, to receive offerings." And again: "We, today, are truly śrāvakas, and, by the very sound of the buddha-path, cause everyone to hear it." These names are the same as those of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings, yet their sense differs. Why is this? Those teachings' own arhat merely slays the thief of the four abiding afflictions, while ignorance still remains — this is a one-sided sense of "no longer arising"; hence the heavenly maiden says, "The habitual residue of the fetters not yet exhausted, the flowers still cling to the body." Here, however, one slays both the shared and distinct teachings' own confusion at once, attaining the Tathāgata's own extinction — hence, the sense of "slaying the thief" is perfect. Again, those teachings' own arhat is free of segmented birth-and-death, yet is still born beyond the three realms. The Ratnagotravibhāga says: "The two vehicles, within the uncontaminated realm, still give rise to three kinds of mental aggregates." Here, however, one is free of both segmented birth-and-death and transformational birth-and-death together — hence, the sense of "no longer arising" is perfect. Those teachings' own arhat is worthy of offerings only within the three realms, not worthy of offerings beyond them. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "Whoever makes offering to you is not, thereby, named a field of merit." This is a one-sided sense of "worthy of offerings." Here, however, one is everywhere within it worthy of receiving offerings — hence, the sense of "worthy of offerings" is perfect. Those teachings' own arhat is merely the Hīnayāna, hearing the sound of the Four Noble Truths from another — this is a one-sided sense of both "sound" and "hearing." Here, however, one is able to cause the entire dharma-realm to hear the sound of the one real Four Noble Truths, the very sound of the buddha-path, making everyone hear it — hence, the sense of "śrāvaka" itself is perfect. One should therefore know that this relies on the sense, not on the mere words, in judging the stage according to the perfect teaching.

名別義圓者，如五十二位，名與別同。而初、中、後位，圓融妙實，隨自意語，非是教道方便，依義不依語，應從圓判位也。

As for the name being distinct while the sense is perfect: as with the fifty-two stages, whose names are the same as the distinct teaching's own. Yet the initial, middle, and final stages are perfectly fused, wondrous, and real, spoken according to one's own intent rather than as the teaching's own expedient path — relying on the sense, not on the mere words, one ought to judge the stage according to the perfect teaching.

名義俱圓者，文云：「開示悟入，皆是佛之知見。」佛一切種智知、佛眼見，此之知見，無有缺減。又，入如來室，坐如來座，以如來莊嚴，此則名義俱圓，判於圓位也。

As for both the name and the sense being perfect: the text says, "Opening, showing, awakening, and entering are all alike the buddha's own knowledge and vision." Knowing through the buddha's own wisdom knowing all modes, seeing through the buddha-eye — this knowledge and vision lacks nothing at all, diminished in no respect. Again, entering the Tathāgata's room, sitting in the Tathāgata's seat, adorned with the Tathāgata's own adornment — this, then, is both the name and the sense perfect, judged as the perfect stage.

Reading note

Fascicle 5 opens the sixth and final stage of the "Medicinal Herbs" parable's six-stage scheme — the perfect teaching's own "most real matter," announced already in Fascicle 4. Zhiyi lays out a ten-part outline for the whole discussion before beginning, then opens with a three-way distinction (name shared but sense perfect / name borrowed from the distinct teaching but sense perfect / both name and sense perfect) that recurs as a methodological pattern throughout this treatise: the same word — arhat, śrāvaka, the fifty-two stages — can mean something categorically different depending on which teaching is speaking it, and the treatise insists on judging by sense rather than surface vocabulary. The three proof-texts illustrating "name shared, sense perfect" — the heavenly maiden's flowers sticking to the unliberated (from the *Vimalakīrti Sūtra*), the *Ratnagotravibhāga* on the two vehicles' subtle remaining birth, and the *Vimalakīrti Sūtra* again on who truly constitutes a field of merit — are all classic proof-texts for exactly this kind of "same word, deeper meaning" argument in Mahāyāna polemic.

Clarifying the Number of Stages 明位數

Chinese Source

二明位數者，又為三：一、明數。二、引證多少。三、料簡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, as for clarifying the number of stages: this, too, is of three parts — first, clarifying the number itself; second, citing evidence for how many there are; third, resolving doubts.

數者，人解不同。有言：頓悟即佛，無復位次之殊。引《思益》云：「如此學者，不從一地至一地。」又有師言：頓悟初心即究竟圓極，而有四十二位者，是化鈍根方便，立淺深之名耳。引《楞伽》云：「初地即二地，二地即三地，寂滅真如，有何次位？」又有師言：初頓悟至十住，即是十地；而說有十行、十迴向、十地者，此是重說耳。今謂諸解，悉是偏取。然平等法界，尚不論悟與不悟，孰辨淺深？既得論悟與不悟，何妨論於淺深？究竟大乘，無過《華嚴》、《大集》、《大品》、《法華》、《涅槃》，雖明法界平等、無說無示，而菩薩行位終自炳然。

As for the number itself: people's understandings differ. Some say: sudden awakening is itself buddhahood, with no further distinction of sequential stages at all — citing the Viśeṣacintibrahma-paripṛcchā Sūtra: "Such a student as this does not proceed from one ground to another ground." Others say: sudden awakening at the initial mind is itself the ultimate, perfect extreme, and the existence of forty-two stages is merely an expedient means for transforming the dull-faculty practitioner, establishing names of shallow and deep only for their sake — citing the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra: "The first ground is itself the second ground, the second ground is itself the third ground — within quiescent extinction and suchness itself, what sequential stage could there possibly be?" Still others say: the initial sudden awakening, up through the ten abidings, is itself already the ten grounds; and the further speaking of the ten practices, the ten dedications, and the ten grounds is merely a repeated restatement of the same thing. Here, it is held that every one of these interpretations is one-sided. Yet, within the equality of the dharma-realm itself, one does not even discuss awakened versus unawakened at all — how, then, could one possibly distinguish shallow from deep? But since one is already able to discuss awakened versus unawakened, what obstacle could there be to also discussing shallow versus deep? Among the ultimate Mahāyāna scriptures, none surpasses the Avataṃsaka, the Mahāsaṃnipāta, the Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra, the Lotus Sūtra, and the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra — and though these clarify the dharma-realm's own equality, beyond all speech and beyond all demonstration, the bodhisattva's own practice-stages remain, in the end, perfectly evident of themselves nonetheless.

又有人言：平等法界，定無次位。今例難此語：真諦有分別耶？真諦無分別耶？見真之者，判七賢、七聖、二十七賢聖等。今實相平等，雖無次位，見實相者，判次位何咎？《大論》云：「譬如入海，有始入者，到中者，至彼岸者。」若見真判位，如江河深淺，若實相判位，如入海深淺。故《普賢觀》云：「大乘因者，諸法實相。」大乘果者，亦諸法實相，論諸次位，非徒臆說。隨順契經，以四悉檀明位無妨，還約七種以明階位，謂十信、十住、十行、十迴向、十地、等覺、妙覺。

Again, some say: the equality of the dharma-realm admits, with certainty, of no sequential stage whatsoever. Here, this statement may be challenged by the same example: does the real truth admit of discrimination, or does the real truth admit of no discrimination at all? Those who see the real distinguish the seven worthies, the seven sages, the twenty-seven worthies and sages, and the rest. If the equality of the real mark, though admitting of no sequential stage, may nonetheless be distinguished into sequential stages by whoever sees the real mark — what fault could there possibly be in this? The Treatise says: "It is like entering the sea — there is the one who has only just entered, the one who has reached its middle, and the one who has arrived at the far shore." If one distinguishes stages in terms of seeing the real, this is like the differing depths of a river; if one distinguishes stages in terms of the real mark itself, this is like the differing depths of the sea itself. Hence the Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra says: "The cause of the Mahāyāna is the real mark of every dharma." The fruit of the Mahāyāna, too, is the real mark of every dharma — discussing these various sequential stages is not mere idle speculation. According with the scriptures themselves, clarifying the stages by means of the four siddhāntas presents no obstacle at all; here, too, the stage is clarified according to seven kinds, namely the ten faiths, the ten abidings, the ten practices, the ten dedications, the ten grounds, equal enlightenment, and wondrous enlightenment.

The Five Ranks, Before the Ten Faiths 十信之前・五品位

Chinese Source

今於十信之前，更明五品之位(云云)。若人宿殖深厚，或值善知識、或從經卷，圓聞妙理，謂一法一切法，一切法一法，非一非一切，不可思議，如前所說，起圓信解，信一心中具十法界，如一微塵有大千經卷。欲開此心而修圓行。圓行者，一行一切行。略言為十：謂識一念，平等具足，不可思議。傷己昏沈，慈及一切。又知此心，常寂常照。用寂照心，破一切法，即空、即假、即中。又識一心諸心，若通、若塞。能於此心，具足道品，向菩提路。又解此心，正、助之法。又識己心，及凡、聖心。又安心不動、不墮、不退、不散。雖識一心，無量功德，不生染著，十心成就。舉要言之，其心念念悉與諸波羅蜜相應，是名圓教初隨喜品位。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, before the ten faiths, the stage of the five ranks is further clarified, and so on. If a person's past cultivation has been deep and abundant, whether through encountering a good spiritual friend or through a scriptural text, hearing the perfect, wondrous principle — that one dharma is every dharma, that every dharma is one dharma, that it is neither one nor every, inconceivable, exactly as explained before — one gives rise to perfect faith and understanding, believing that the single mind is already complete with all ten dharma-realms, just as a single mote of dust contains within it a scroll as vast as the great trichiliocosm itself. Wishing to open this mind and cultivate the perfect practice — the perfect practice being one in which a single practice is every practice — this may be stated briefly as ten: knowing this single thought to be, of itself, equally complete, inconceivable; grieving over one's own confusion and torpor, extending loving-kindness to all; further knowing that this mind is ever quiescent and ever illuminating; using this mind of quiescent illumination to break through every dharma, at once empty, at once provisional, at once the middle; further knowing that, within this single mind, every mind is either penetrating or obstructed; being able, within this mind, to become complete with the factors of the path, turning toward the road of bodhi; further understanding, within this mind, the correct and auxiliary dharmas; further knowing one's own mind, and the mind of the ordinary being and of the sage alike; further settling the mind so that it is unmoving, unfalling, unretreating, and unscattered. Though one knows that this single mind possesses immeasurable merit and virtue, one gives rise to no defiled attachment toward it — the ten minds are thereby accomplished. To state the essential point: this mind, thought after thought, corresponds entirely to every pāramitā — this is named the perfect teaching's own initial rank of sympathetic joy.

行者圓信始生，善須將養。若涉事紛動，令道芽破敗。唯得內修理觀，外則受持、讀誦大乘經典，聞有助觀之力。內外相藉，圓信轉明，十心堅固。《金剛般若》云：「一日三時，以恒河沙身布施，不如受持一句功德。」初品觀智如目，次品讀誦如日。日有光故，目見種種色。《論》云：「於實名了因，於餘名生因。福不趣菩提，二能趣菩提。」聞有巨益，意在於此，是名第二品位。

The practitioner, this perfect faith just having arisen, must skillfully nourish and sustain it. Should one become entangled in worldly affairs and their agitation, this would cause the sprout of the path to break and wither. One should therefore only cultivate the contemplation of principle inwardly, while, outwardly, upholding, reading, and reciting the Mahāyāna scriptures, since hearing them carries a power that assists this contemplation. Inward and outward mutually reinforcing one another, this perfect faith grows ever more distinct, and the ten minds become firm and secure. The Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā says: "Giving one's own body as gifts, as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, three times a day, does not equal the merit of upholding a single phrase." The first rank's own contemplative wisdom is like the eye; the second rank's own reading and reciting is like the sun — since the sun possesses light, the eye is thereby able to see every manner of form. The Treatise says: "With respect to the real, this is named the revealing cause; with respect to what remains, this is named the generating cause. Merit alone does not proceed toward bodhi; only these two together are able to proceed toward bodhi." The great benefit of hearing the scriptures lies precisely in this sense — this is named the stage of the second rank.

行者內觀轉強，外資又著，圓解在懷，弘誓熏動，更加說法，如實演布。《安樂行》云：「但以大乘法答。設以方便隨宜，終令悟大。」《淨名》云：「說法淨，則智慧淨。」《毘曇》云：「說法解脫，聽法解脫。」說法開導，是前人得道全因緣，化功歸己十心則三倍轉明，是名第三品位。

As the practitioner's own inward contemplation grows stronger still, and outward support grows firmer as well, perfect understanding held within, the universal vow stirred and moved, one further takes up teaching the dharma, proclaiming and spreading it exactly as it truly is. The Peaceful Practices chapter says: "Answering only by means of the Mahāyāna dharma; and, even where skillful means are employed as fitting, in the end always causing awakening to the great vehicle." The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "Where the teaching of the dharma is pure, wisdom, too, is pure." The Abhidharma says: "Teaching the dharma is liberation; hearing the dharma is liberation." Teaching the dharma to guide others is the very complete cause and condition by which another person attains the path, and, since this transformative merit returns to oneself as well, the ten minds accordingly grow three times more distinct — this is named the stage of the third rank.

上來前熟觀心，未遑涉事。今正觀稍明，即傍兼利物。能以少施，與虛空法界等。使一切法趣檀，檀為法界。《大品》云：「菩薩少施，超過聲聞、辟支佛上，當學般若。」即此意也。餘五亦如是。事相雖少，運懷甚大。此則理觀為正，事行為傍，故言兼行布施。事福資理，則十心彌盛，是名第四品位。

Up to this point, one has first thoroughly cultivated the contemplation of mind, without yet having leisure to engage in worldly affairs. Now, as this correct contemplation grows somewhat more distinct, one incidentally also takes up benefiting others. Being able, through even a small act of giving, to equal empty space and the dharma-realm itself, causing every dharma to converge upon giving, giving itself becoming the dharma-realm. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra says: "The bodhisattva's small act of giving surpasses even the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha — one should study prajñā" — this is precisely this same sense. The remaining five pāramitās are exactly the same. Though the outward phenomenal act is small, the mind it carries is vast indeed. Here, the contemplation of principle remains the primary practice, and worldly action remains only incidental to it — hence, this is called "incidentally practicing giving." As this worldly merit nourishes principle, the ten minds grow ever more abundant — this is named the stage of the fourth rank.

行人圓觀稍熟，理事欲融，涉事不妨理，在理不隔事，故具行六度。若布施時，無二邊取著，十法界依、正，一捨一切捨，財、身及命，無畏等施。若持戒時，性重、譏嫌等無差別；五部重輕，無所觸犯。若行忍時，生、法寂滅，荷負安耐。若行精進，身心俱淨，無間無退。若行禪時，遊入諸禪，靜散無妨。若修慧時，權實二智，究了通達；乃至世智，治生產業，皆與實相不相違背。具足解釋佛之知見，而於正觀，如火益薪。此是第五品位。

The practitioner's own perfect contemplation, growing somewhat mature, causes principle and worldly action to fuse together — engaging in worldly action no longer obstructs principle, and abiding in principle no longer sets itself apart from worldly action; hence, one now practices the six pāramitās directly and completely. When giving, one is free of attachment to either extreme; the dependent and direct retributions across all ten dharma-realms, relinquishing one is relinquishing all — wealth, body, and life itself, and the giving of fearlessness, are all given equally. When upholding the precepts, there is no distinction between grave inherent wrong and what the world finds objectionable; the grave and light offenses across the five divisions of the vinaya are none of them ever transgressed. When practicing patience, birth and dharmas alike are seen as quiescent extinction, bearing every burden with calm endurance. When practicing diligence, body and mind alike are purified, without interruption and without retreat. When practicing dhyāna, one roams freely into every dhyāna, whether stillness or dispersal presenting no obstacle. When cultivating wisdom, both the provisional and the real wisdom are thoroughly penetrated and understood; even worldly wisdom itself, and one's own livelihood and occupation, are none of them ever in conflict with the real mark. Complete with the full explanation of the buddha's own knowledge and vision, and with respect to correct contemplation, this is like fire fed further with firewood. This is the stage of the fifth rank.

Comparing the Five Ranks 五品位料簡

Chinese Source

如此五品圓信功德，東西八方不可為喻。雖是初心，而勝聲聞無學功德，具如經說。若欲比決取解，類如三藏家別、總四念處位。義推如通教乾慧地位，亦如伏忍位。義推亦得是別教十信位(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

The merit and virtue of this perfect faith across these five ranks cannot be compared to anything in the eight directions of east and west alike. Though this is still the initial mind, it already surpasses the merit and virtue of the śrāvaka beyond further learning — set out fully as the sūtra itself explains. If one wishes to compare this against other schemes to aid understanding, it is comparable to the Tripiṭaka teaching's own particular and general applications of mindfulness; reasoned by sense, it is like the shared teaching's own ground of dry wisdom, and likewise like the stage of subduing forbearance; reasoned by sense, it may also be taken as the distinct teaching's own stage of the ten faiths, and so on.

私謂：五品位是圓家方便，初欲令易解準小望大，如三藏之五停心。初品圓信法界，上信諸佛，下信眾生，皆起隨喜，是圓家慈停心，遍對治法界上嫉妬。第二品讀誦大乘文字，文字是法身氣命，讀誦明利是圓家數息停心，遍治法界上覺觀。說法品能自淨心，亦淨他心，是圓家因緣停心，遍治法界上自、他癡。癡去故，諸行去乃至老死去，兼行六度品，是圓家不淨停心。六蔽初名貪欲，若捨貪欲，欲因、欲果皆捨。捨故無復報身，非淨非不淨也。正行六度品，是圓家念佛停心。正行六度時，即事而理，理不妨道，事妨於道。即事而理，無障可論。大意如此(云云)。

In my own view: the stage of the five ranks is the perfect teaching's own expedient means, meant at the outset to make understanding easier by measuring the small against the great, just as the Tripiṭaka teaching has its own five methods of stilling the mind. The first rank's perfect faith in the dharma-realm — believing upward in every buddha, believing downward in every sentient being, giving rise to sympathetic joy toward both alike — is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through loving-kindness, universally treating jealousy and envy throughout the dharma-realm. The second rank's reading and reciting of the Mahāyāna's own text — the text being the very vital breath of the Dharma-body — reading and reciting it with clarity and sharpness is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through counting the breath, universally treating applied and sustained thought throughout the dharma-realm. The rank of teaching the dharma, able to purify one's own mind and likewise purify the minds of others, is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through causes and conditions, universally treating delusion, both one's own and others', throughout the dharma-realm. Since delusion departs, every formation departs, on through old age and death departing as well; the rank of incidentally practicing the six pāramitās is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through the contemplation of impurity. The first of the six coverings is named craving desire; if one relinquishes craving desire, both its cause and its fruit are relinquished together. Since this is relinquished, no further retribution-body remains — neither pure nor impure. The rank of directly practicing the six pāramitās is the perfect teaching's own version of stilling the mind through mindfulness of the Buddha. When directly practicing the six pāramitās, the very phenomenon itself is already principle; principle presents no obstacle to the path, while worldly phenomena alone obstruct the path — yet, since the very phenomenon itself is already principle, there remains no obstacle whatsoever to discuss. This is, in general outline, the sense of it, and so on.

Reading note

The five ranks (五品位) are among the most celebrated schemes in all of Tiantai practice — Zhiyi's own answer to what an ordinary, still-unenlightened practitioner in this present age can actually do: sympathetic joy (perfect faith alone, before any external practice), reading and reciting the Mahāyāna scriptures, teaching the dharma to others, incidentally practicing the six pāramitās, and finally practicing the six pāramitās directly, principle and worldly action now fully fused. Zhiyi's own "personal view" closing this section maps all five ranks onto the Tripiṭaka teaching's five methods of stilling the mind one-to-one — a deliberate small-to-great pedagogical bridge, since the five methods of stilling the mind were the very first, most elementary stage translated at the start of Fascicle 4's "middle medicinal herb." The five ranks precede the ten faiths in the perfect teaching's own count, and answer the "how many stages" survey above by showing that even the earliest rungs of the perfect ladder already outstrip the arhat's own final attainment in the lesser teachings.

The Ten Faiths 十信位

Chinese Source

一明十信位者，初以圓聞，能起圓信，修於圓行，善巧增益，令此圓行五倍深明，因此圓行得入圓位。以善修平等法界，即入信心。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying the stage of the ten faiths: at the outset, through perfect hearing, one is able to give rise to perfect faith, cultivating the perfect practice; through skillful means, this is further increased, causing this perfect practice to become five times deeper and more distinct, and, through this perfect practice, one is thereby able to enter the perfect stage. Skillfully cultivating the equality of the dharma-realm, one enters the mind of faith.

善修慈愍，即入念心。善修寂照，即入進心。善修破法，即入慧心。善修通塞，即入定心。善修道品，即入不退心。善修正助，即入迴向心。善修凡聖位，即入護法心。善修不動，即入戒心。善修無著，即入願心。是名入十信位。《纓珞》云：「一信有十，十信有百。百法為一切法之根本也。」是名圓教鐵輪十信位，即是六根清淨，圓教似解，煗、頂、忍、世第一法。《普賢觀》明無生忍前有十境界，即此位也。

Skillfully cultivating compassionate pity, one enters the mind of mindfulness. Skillfully cultivating quiescent illumination, one enters the mind of diligence. Skillfully cultivating the breaking-through of dharmas, one enters the mind of wisdom. Skillfully cultivating what is penetrating and what is obstructed, one enters the mind of concentration. Skillfully cultivating the factors of the path, one enters the mind of non-retrogression. Skillfully cultivating the correct and auxiliary practices, one enters the mind of dedication. Skillfully cultivating the stages of the ordinary being and the sage, one enters the mind of guarding the dharma. Skillfully cultivating the unmoving, one enters the mind of the precepts. Skillfully cultivating non-attachment, one enters the mind of the vow. This is named entering the stage of the ten faiths. The Yingluo Sūtra says: "One faith already possesses ten; the ten faiths already possess a hundred — these hundred dharmas are the very root of every dharma." This is named the perfect teaching's own iron-wheel stage of the ten faiths, which is precisely the purification of the six sense-faculties, the perfect teaching's own resembling understanding, and the dharma of warmth, of the summit, of forbearance, and the supreme worldly dharma. The Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra clarifies ten realms present before the forbearance of no-generation — this is precisely this same stage.

入此信心，能破界內見、思盡，又破界外塵沙無知，能伏無明住地之惑。《仁王般若》云：「十善菩薩發大心，長別三界苦輪海。」亦此位也。此位，經經出之不同。《華嚴》法慧菩薩答正念天子，明菩薩觀十種梵行空，學十種智力，入初住。十種梵行空即一實諦，亦無作之滅諦。學十種智力，即觀無作之道諦，即十信位也。

Entering this mind of faith, one is able to break through the views and thought within the three realms completely, and further break through the ignorance-of-particulars beyond the three realms, and is able to subdue the affliction of the ground of abiding ignorance. The Renwang Prajñāpāramitā says: "The bodhisattva of the ten wholesome acts arouses the great mind, and forever departs from the sea of suffering's wheel across the three realms" — this, too, is this same stage. This stage is set out differently across different sūtras. In the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, the bodhisattva Dharmamati, answering the heavenly son of Right Mindfulness, clarifies the bodhisattva's contemplation of the emptiness of the ten kinds of brahma-practice, studying the ten kinds of power of wisdom, and entering the first abiding — the emptiness of the ten kinds of brahma-practice is precisely the one real truth, and is likewise the unconditioned truth of cessation; studying the ten kinds of power of wisdom is precisely contemplating the unconditioned truth of the path — this is precisely the stage of the ten faiths.

若《大品》云：「譬如入海，先見平相。」亦是是乘從三界中出也。《仁王般若》、《普賢觀》如前引。下文入如來室、座、衣等，即是修四安樂行行處、近處。《涅槃》云：「復有一行，是如來行，所謂大乘。」《大論》云：「菩薩從初發心，即觀涅槃行道。若觀涅槃行道，生相似解，即是一行如來行也。」(云云)。

The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra says: "It is like entering the sea and, at first, seeing only its level surface" — this, too, is this same vehicle emerging from within the three realms. The Renwang Prajñāpāramitā and the Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra are cited as before. The text below, on entering the Tathāgata's room, seat, and robe, and the rest, is precisely cultivating the four peaceful practices' own sphere of action and sphere of intimacy. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "There is, further, a single practice that is the Tathāgata's own practice, namely, the Mahāyāna." The Treatise says: "The bodhisattva, from the very first arousing of the mind, already contemplates nirvāṇa while walking the path. If one contemplates nirvāṇa while walking the path, giving rise to a resembling understanding of it, this is already the single practice that is the Tathāgata's own practice," and so on.

Reading note

The ten faiths (十信) map the ten minds of perfect faith already introduced back in the five ranks onto the *Yingluo Sūtra*'s own naming (faith/mindfulness/diligence/wisdom/concentration/non-retrogression/dedication/guarding the dharma/the precepts/the vow), and are identified outright with the six-root purification (六根清淨) — a famous stage in its own right, treated at length in the *Lotus Sūtra*'s own "Merits of the Dharma Teacher" chapter, cited again below — and with the four good roots' resembling understanding already established from Fascicle 3. The scriptural cross-references gathered here (the *Avataṃsaka*'s ten brahma-practices, the *Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra*'s sea simile, the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s single Tathāgata-practice) are the same proof-texts already used for the perfect teaching's five practices back in Fascicle 4, now redeployed to locate this same material within the stage-scheme instead.

The Ten Abidings 十住位

Chinese Source

二、明十住位者，以從相似十信，能入十住真中智也。初發心住發時，三種心發：一、緣因善心發。二、了因慧心發。三、正因理心發。即是前境、智、行妙三種開發也。住者，住三德涅槃也。緣因心發，即是住不可思議解脫首楞嚴定；慧心發，即是住摩訶般若畢竟之空；正因心發，即是住實相法身中道第一義。舉要言之，即是住三德一切佛法也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the stage of the ten abidings: proceeding from the resembling understanding of the ten faiths, one is thereby able to enter the ten abidings' own real wisdom of the middle. At the very moment the initial abiding of the arousing of the mind is brought forth, three kinds of mind are brought forth together: first, the wholesome mind of the conditioning cause; second, the wisdom-mind of the revealing cause; third, the mind of principle of the correct cause — this is precisely the threefold opening and unfolding of the object, wisdom, and practice discussed before. "Abiding" means abiding in the nirvāṇa of the three virtues. The mind of the conditioning cause brought forth is precisely abiding in the inconceivable liberation, the Śūraṅgama samādhi; the mind of wisdom brought forth is precisely abiding in the great prajñā's own ultimate emptiness; the mind of the correct cause brought forth is precisely abiding in the real mark's own Dharma-body, the middle way's own ultimate meaning. To state the essential point: this is precisely abiding in the three virtues, every buddha-dharma at once.

又住清淨圓滿菩提心，無緣慈悲，無作誓願，普覆法界。又住一念中，成就一切萬行諸波羅蜜。又住一切種智，圓斷法界見、思、無明。又住得佛眼，圓見十法界三諦之法。又住圓入一切法門，所謂二十五三昧，冥益眾生。又成就菩薩圓滿業，能顯一切神通，謂三輪不思議化彌滿法界，顯益眾生。又能成就開權顯實，入一乘道。又能嚴淨一切佛土，能起三業，供養一切十方佛，得圓滿陀羅尼，受持一切佛法，如雲持雨。又住能從一地，具足一切諸地功德，心心寂滅，自然流入薩婆若海。

Again, one abides in the pure and perfectly complete bodhi-mind, the compassion that takes no object, the unconditioned vow, universally covering the entire dharma-realm. Again, one abides within a single thought, accomplishing every one of the myriad practices, every pāramitā. Again, one abides in the wisdom knowing all modes, perfectly severing the views, thought, and ignorance of the entire dharma-realm. Again, one abides in attaining the buddha-eye, perfectly seeing the dharma of the three truths across all ten dharma-realms. Again, one abides in perfectly entering every dharma-gate, namely the twenty-five samādhis, secretly benefiting sentient beings. Again, one accomplishes the bodhisattva's own perfectly complete karma, able to manifest every spiritual power, namely the inconceivable transformation of the three wheels, filling the entire dharma-realm, manifestly benefiting sentient beings. Again, one is able to accomplish opening the provisional to reveal the real, entering the path of the one vehicle. Again, one is able to adorn and purify every buddha-land, able to raise up the three karmic activities, making offering to every buddha of the ten directions, attaining the perfectly complete dhāraṇī, upholding every buddha-dharma, like a cloud holding rain. Again, one abides in being able, from a single ground, to become complete with the merit and virtue of every ground at once, thought after thought quiescent and extinct, flowing naturally into the ocean of omniscience itself.

《華嚴》云：「初住菩薩所有功德，三世諸佛歎不能盡。若具足說，凡人聞，迷亂心發狂。」私謂：初住成就十德，應是十信中十法，轉似為真，一住具十。細意尋之，對當相應。何者？十信百法為一切法本，豈不得作此釋耶？初住既爾，三觀現前，無功用心斷法界無量品無明，不可稱計。一往大分，略為十品智斷，即是十住故。《仁王》云：「入理般若名為住。」即是十番進發無漏，同見中道佛性第一義理。以不住法，從淺至深，住佛三德及一切佛法，故名十住位。

The Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "The merit and virtue possessed by the bodhisattva of the initial abiding cannot be exhausted even by the buddhas of the three times praising it; were it explained in full, an ordinary person hearing it would become confused and deranged, their mind driven mad." In my own view: the ten virtues accomplished at the initial abiding ought to correspond to the ten dharmas among the ten faiths, turned from resembling to real, a single abiding already complete with all ten. Examined carefully, they correspond exactly to one another. Why is this? Since the ten faiths' own hundred dharmas are already the very root of every dharma, how could this correlation not be made? The initial abiding being thus, the three contemplations manifest before one, and the mind of effortless function severs the immeasurable grades of ignorance throughout the dharma-realm, beyond all reckoning or calculation. Taken broadly as a whole, this is, in brief, divided into ten grades of wisdom-and-severance, and this is precisely why there are ten abidings. The Renwang Jing says: "Entering the prajñā of principle is named abiding" — this is precisely the tenfold advance in bringing forth the uncontaminated, alike seeing the middle way's own buddha-nature, the principle of ultimate meaning. Since one no longer abides in any dharma, proceeding from the shallow to the deep, one abides in the buddha's own three virtues and in every buddha-dharma — hence, this is named the stage of the ten abidings.

此位，諸經出處不同。《華嚴》云：「初發心時，便成正覺。」了達諸法真實之性，所有聞法，不由他悟。是菩薩成就十種智力，究竟離虛妄，無染如虛空。清淨妙法身，湛然應一切，當知即是發真無漏，斷無明初品也。《淨名》云：「一念知一切法，是為坐道場，成就一切智故。」亦是入不二法門，得無生忍也。《大品》明「從初發心，即坐道場、轉法輪、度眾生。」當知，此菩薩為如佛，亦是阿字門，所謂一切法初不生也。即是今經為令眾生開佛知見，亦是龍女於剎那頃發菩提心，成等正覺。即是《涅槃》明發心、畢竟二不別，如是二心，前心難。此諸大乘，悉明圓初發心住位也。乃至第十住(云云)。

This stage is set out differently in the different sūtras' own sources. The Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "At the very moment the mind is first aroused, one thereby already accomplishes true and correct enlightenment" — fully penetrating the real, true nature of every dharma, whatever dharma one hears, one awakens to it without relying on another. This bodhisattva accomplishes the ten kinds of power of wisdom, ultimately free of every falsehood, untainted like empty space itself; the pure, wondrous Dharma-body, tranquil and clear, responds to everything — one should know that this is precisely bringing forth the real, the uncontaminated, severing the first grade of ignorance. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "Knowing every dharma within a single thought is precisely sitting at the very seat of enlightenment, since it accomplishes the wisdom that knows all things" — this is likewise entering the gate of non-duality, attaining the forbearance of no-generation. The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra clarifies: "From the very first arousing of the mind, one already sits at the seat of enlightenment, turns the wheel of the Dharma, and saves sentient beings." One should know that this bodhisattva is equivalent to a buddha, and is likewise the gate of the letter "a" — namely, that every dharma, from the very outset, never arises at all. This is precisely why the present sūtra causes sentient beings to open the buddha's own knowledge and vision, and is likewise why the dragon girl, in the space of a single instant, arouses the bodhi-mind and accomplishes perfect, complete enlightenment. This is precisely what the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra clarifies as the arousing of the mind and its ultimate attainment being, in the end, not two different things — though, of these two minds, the first mind is the more difficult. All of these Mahāyāna scriptures alike clarify the perfect teaching's own initial abiding of the arousing of the mind, on through the tenth abiding, and so on.

Reading note

The initial abiding (初住) is where the perfect teaching's practitioner first genuinely breaks through ignorance and sees the middle way directly — the moment the *Avataṃsaka Sūtra*'s own famous line "at the very moment the mind is first aroused, one thereby already accomplishes true and correct enlightenment" describes, and the moment the *Lotus Sūtra*'s own dragon girl passage (from the "Devadatta" chapter) dramatizes as instantaneous buddhahood. Zhiyi is careful to qualify this: the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s own point that "the arousing of the mind and its ultimate attainment are not two different things" is true, yet "of these two minds, the first mind is the more difficult" — the initial breakthrough, not the long unfolding after it, is the genuinely hard step. The correlation of the ten abidings' own ten virtues with the ten faiths' own ten minds (resembling turned real, "a single abiding already complete with all ten") repeats the same pattern already used to correlate the five ranks with the five methods of stilling the mind.

The Ten Practices Through Wondrous Enlightenment 十行・十迴向・十地・等覺・妙覺

Chinese Source

三、明十行位者，即是從十住後，實相真明，不可思議。更十番智斷，破十品無明，一行一切行，念念進趣，流入平等法界海。諸波羅蜜任運生長，自行、化他，功德與虛空等，故名十行位也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the stage of the ten practices: proceeding from the ten abidings onward, the real mark's own real clarity becomes inconceivable. Undergoing ten further rounds of wisdom-and-severance, breaking through ten grades of ignorance, a single practice is every practice, advancing thought after thought, flowing into the ocean of the equality of the dharma-realm. Every pāramitā arises and grows of its own accord, one's own practice and the transformation of others alike, their merit and virtue equal to empty space itself — hence, this is named the stage of the ten practices.

十迴向位者，即是十行之後無功用道，不可思議真明念念開發一切法界，願行事理自然和融，迴入平等法界海。更證十番智斷，破十品無明，故名迴向也。

As for the stage of the ten dedications: this is precisely the path of effortless function following the ten practices — the inconceivable real clarity opening and unfolding the entire dharma-realm thought after thought, the vow and its practice, phenomena and principle, naturally fused together, turning to enter the ocean of the equality of the dharma-realm. Realizing ten further rounds of wisdom-and-severance, breaking through ten grades of ignorance, this is accordingly named dedication.

十地位者，即是無漏真明入無功用道，猶如大地能生一切佛法，荷負法界眾生，普入三世佛地。又證十番智斷，破十品無明，故名十地位也。

As for the stage of the ten grounds: this is precisely the uncontaminated real clarity entering the path of effortless function, just like the great earth itself, able to give rise to every buddha-dharma, bearing the burden of every sentient being throughout the dharma-realm, universally entering the buddha-ground of the three times. Realizing ten further rounds of wisdom-and-severance, breaking through ten grades of ignorance, this is accordingly named the stage of the ten grounds.

等覺地者，觀達無始無明源底，邊際智滿，畢竟清淨。斷最後窮源微細無明，登中道山頂，與無明父母別，是名有所斷者，名有上士也。

As for the ground of equal enlightenment: contemplating and penetrating through to the very source and depth of beginningless ignorance, the wisdom of its outermost limit is fulfilled, ultimately pure and undefiled. Severing the last, most subtle ignorance at the very source itself, one ascends to the very summit of the mountain of the middle way, taking leave at last of ignorance, one's own father and mother — this is named one who still has something left to sever, and is named a superior person still with something above.

七、明妙覺地者，究竟解脫無上佛智故，言無所斷者，名無上士。此即三德，不縱不橫，究竟後心，大涅槃也。一切大：理大、誓願大、莊嚴大、智斷大、遍知大、道大、用大、權實大、利益大、無住大。即是前十觀成乘，圓極竟在於佛，過茶無字可說(云云)。故盧舍那佛名為淨滿，一切皆滿也。

Seventh, clarifying the ground of wondrous enlightenment: since this is the ultimate liberation, the unsurpassed wisdom of the buddha, this is spoken of as one with nothing further left to sever, and is named the unsurpassed person, with nothing above. This is precisely the three virtues, neither vertically arranged nor horizontally arranged, the ultimate, final mind, the great nirvāṇa itself. Every kind of greatness is present: the greatness of principle, the greatness of the vow, the greatness of adornment, the greatness of wisdom-and-severance, the greatness of complete knowledge, the greatness of the path, the greatness of function, the greatness of the provisional and the real, the greatness of benefit, and the greatness of non-abiding. This is precisely the ten contemplations discussed earlier accomplishing the vehicle, perfect and ultimate at last in the buddha himself, beyond the letter "dha," with no further letter left to speak of, and so on. Hence Vairocana Buddha is named "pure and full" — everything, without exception, is thereby fulfilled.

Reading note

This completes the perfect teaching's own seven-stage ladder in outline — ten faiths, ten abidings, ten practices, ten dedications, ten grounds, equal enlightenment, wondrous enlightenment — each of the last four stages severing ten further grades of ignorance in turn, for forty grades total across the four "ten"-stages plus equal enlightenment's own final, subtlest grade. "Neither vertically nor horizontally arranged" recalls the Siddhaṃ-letter-*i* image already used in Fascicle 3 for the three virtues; "beyond the letter 'dha,' with no further letter to speak of" recalls the fifty-letter analogy just used in Fascicle 4 to explain why even the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s own nirvāṇa was still only the bodhisattva's practice — here, reaching that same final letter is what wondrous enlightenment itself actually is. What follows next turns to how many stages there really are — cross-checking this same seven-stage count against further scriptural evidence, including the famous "moon-love samādhi" and "forty-two letters" correlations.

Citing Further Scripture: The Moon-Love Samādhi and the Forty-Two Letters 引眾經明位數・月愛三昧・四十二字門

Chinese Source

二次引眾經，明位數多少者，《大涅槃》云：「月愛三昧，從初一日至十五日，光色漸漸增長。又從十六日至三十日，光色漸漸損減。」光色增長，譬十五智德，摩訶般若。光色漸減，譬十五斷德，無累解脫。三十心為三智斷，十地為十智斷，等覺、妙覺各為一智斷，合十五智斷，月體譬法身。《大經》云：「月性常圓，實無增減。」因須彌山，故有虧盈。不增而增，白月漸著；不減而減，黑月稍無。法身亦爾，實無智斷，因無明故，約如論智，如實不智；約如論斷，如實不斷。雖無智而智，般若漸漸明；雖無斷而斷，解脫漸漸離。舉月為喻，知是圓教智斷位也。《大經》云：「從初安置諸子祕密之藏，三德涅槃，然後我當於此祕藏而般涅槃。」此即最後智斷也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, next citing many sūtras to clarify how many stages there really are: the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The moon-love samādhi: from the first day up through the fifteenth day, the moon's own light and color gradually increase and grow; and, from the sixteenth day up through the thirtieth day, the moon's own light and color gradually diminish and wane." The increase and growth of light and color is a metaphor for the fifteen virtues of wisdom, the great prajñā; the gradual diminishing of light and color is a metaphor for the fifteen virtues of severance, liberation free of all encumbrance. The three sets of ten minds count as three units of wisdom-and-severance; the ten grounds count as ten units of wisdom-and-severance; equal enlightenment and wondrous enlightenment each count as a single unit of wisdom-and-severance — together, fifteen units of wisdom-and-severance in all. The very body of the moon itself is a metaphor for the Dharma-body. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The moon's own nature is ever full and round; in reality, it neither increases nor decreases at all." It is only because of Mount Sumeru that it appears to wax and wane. Without truly increasing, it increases — the bright moon growing gradually more visible; without truly decreasing, it decreases — the dark moon growing gradually less visible. The Dharma-body is exactly the same: in reality, it possesses no wisdom and no severance at all; it is only on account of ignorance that, spoken of in terms of suchness, wisdom is discussed — though, in reality, suchness is not wisdom at all; and, spoken of in terms of suchness, severance is discussed — though, in reality, suchness has nothing at all to sever. Though there is no wisdom, wisdom is spoken of nonetheless, and prajñā grows gradually more distinct; though there is no severance, severance is spoken of nonetheless, and liberation grows gradually more complete. Raising up the moon as a metaphor, one thereby knows this to be the perfect teaching's own stage of wisdom-and-severance. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Having, from the very outset, placed my disciples within the secret treasury, the nirvāṇa of the three virtues, only afterward shall I myself enter parinirvāṇa within this same secret treasury" — this is precisely the final wisdom-and-severance.

問：何得知月喻譬位耶？答：《仁王》明十四忍：三十心為三般若，十地為十般若，等覺為一般若，十四般若在菩薩心中，皆名為忍。轉至佛心，名之為智。此與十五日明智位同。《勝天王》明十四般若位，正用十四日月為譬。故作此釋也。

Question: How can one know that the moon simile is truly a metaphor for the stages? Answer: The Renwang Jing clarifies fourteen forbearances: the three sets of ten minds count as three kinds of prajñā; the ten grounds count as ten kinds of prajñā; equal enlightenment counts as a single kind of prajñā — these fourteen kinds of prajñā, while still present within the bodhisattva's own mind, are all alike named "forbearance"; only once turned into the buddha's own mind are they named "wisdom." This corresponds exactly to the fifteen-day waxing period clarifying the stage of wisdom just discussed. The Śrīmahādevī Prajñāpāramitā clarifies the fourteen stages of prajñā, using precisely the fourteen days of the moon's own waxing as its own metaphor — hence, this same interpretation is made here.

《大品》明四十二字門，語等、字等，南岳師云：「此是諸佛密語，何必不表四十二位？」諸學人執《釋論》，云無此解，多疑不用。但《論》本文千卷，什師作九倍略之，何必無此解耶？今謂：此解深應冥會。何者？《經》云：「初阿後茶，中有四十。初阿字門，具四十一字；後茶亦爾。」《華嚴》云：「從初一地具足一切諸地功德。」此義即同。《經》云：「若聞阿字門，則解一切義，所謂諸法初不生故。」此豈非圓教初住，初得無生法忍？過茶無字可說，豈非妙覺無上、無過？〈廣乘品〉明一切法皆是摩訶衍竟，即說四十二字門，豈非圓教菩薩從初發心，得諸法實相、具一切佛法，故名阿字；至妙覺地，窮一切法底，故名茶字。此義，其數與圓位，甚自分明。又，四十二字後，即說菩薩十地，此是顯別教方便之次位也。又，次十地之後，說三乘共十地，此顯通教方便位也。經文次比，三義宛然。今取四十二字，以證圓位也。

The Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra clarifies the gate of the forty-two letters, where speech is equal and letters are equal; the master of Nanyue says: "This is the secret language of every buddha — why must it not represent the forty-two stages?" The various students who cling to the Treatise, saying no such interpretation exists there, mostly doubt this and refuse to accept it. Yet the Treatise's own original text runs to a thousand fascicles, and Master Kumārajīva condensed it down to a ninth of its length — how could one be certain no such interpretation exists within it? Here, it is held that this interpretation deeply and rightly accords with the truth. Why is this? The sūtra says: "Beginning with 'a' and ending with 'dha,' there are forty in between; the initial letter 'a' gate is already complete with all forty-one letters, and the final letter 'dha' is exactly the same." The Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "From the very first ground onward, one is already complete with the merit and virtue of every ground" — this sense is exactly the same. The sūtra says: "If one hears the gate of the letter 'a,' one thereby understands every meaning, namely that every dharma, from the very outset, never arises at all" — is this not precisely the perfect teaching's own initial abiding, first attaining the forbearance of the dharma of no-generation? And beyond the letter "dha," with no further letter left to speak of — is this not precisely wondrous enlightenment itself, unsurpassed, with nothing beyond it? The "Chapter on the Vast Vehicle" clarifies that every dharma is, in its entirety, the Mahāyāna, and only then speaks of the gate of the forty-two letters — is this not precisely the perfect teaching's own bodhisattva, from the very first arousing of the mind, attaining the real mark of every dharma, complete with every buddha-dharma, and hence named the letter "a"; and, reaching the ground of wondrous enlightenment, exhausting the very depths of every dharma, and hence named the letter "dha"? This sense, and this number, correspond to the perfect stage with perfect clarity of themselves. Again, after the forty-two letters, the bodhisattva's own ten grounds are spoken of next — this reveals the distinct teaching's own expedient sequence of stages. And, next, after these same ten grounds, the three vehicles' own shared ten grounds are spoken of — this reveals the shared teaching's own expedient stage. The sūtra's own text places these in this very sequence, and the three senses stand out clearly of themselves. Here, the forty-two letters are taken as proof of the perfect stage.

Reading note

Two of Tiantai literature's most famous numerological proof-passages appear back to back here. The moon-love samādhi reads the waxing and waning of a single lunar month as a metaphor for wisdom and severance across the same fifteen units already derived from the seven-stage count (three sets of ten minds + ten grounds + equal enlightenment + wondrous enlightenment), with the crucial caveat, drawn directly from the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra* itself, that the moon "in reality neither increases nor decreases at all" — the Dharma-body, like the moon, only appears to gain wisdom and lose affliction from the standpoint of ignorance, which is itself unreal. The forty-two letters (the *arapacana* Sanskrit syllabary, from "a" to "dha") are defended against skeptics who doubted Nanyue Huisi's own reading of them as secretly encoding the forty-two stages, on the grounds that Kumārajīva's translation of the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra* condensed a thousand-fascicle original ninefold — an argument from textual compression rather than direct citation. Both passages converge independently on the same forty-two-stage count (ten faiths not included, since the letters begin only at the initial abiding) that the seven-stage scheme and the moon simile already gave.
