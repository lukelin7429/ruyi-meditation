# Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha — Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha* (金剛頂一切如來真實攝大乘現證大教王經, "Sūtra of the Vast King of Teachings, the Direct Realization of the Great Vehicle That Gathers In the Reality of Every Tathāgata"), Taishō T0865, translated into Chinese by Amoghavajra (不空) sometime after his return from Sri Lanka in 746 CE. They exist so that vocabulary stays consistent across all three parts, and so that terminology without precedent anywhere else on this site is recorded for any later text to inherit rather than re-coin.

## The text, and its place in the Tang esoteric tradition

Amoghavajra (705–774) was the third of the "Kaiyuan Three Masters" (開元三大士) who established the systematic Tang esoteric tradition at the imperial court, following Śubhakarasiṃha and his own teacher Vajrabodhi. This sūtra and the *Mahāvairocana Sūtra*, translated two decades earlier by Śubhakarasiṃha and Yixing, together form that tradition's two root scriptures — the Vajra Realm (Vajradhātu) and Womb (Garbhadhātu) systems whose union, in later Japanese Shingon teaching, constitutes the complete esoteric path (兩部). Amoghavajra's own separate writing on this text's transmission (T1798, 金剛頂經大瑜伽祕密心地法門義訣) records the same striking account his tradition gives of both root sūtras' origin: a Sanskrit original of one hundred thousand verses, kept sealed in an iron stūpa in South India for centuries, opened by a master versed in the Mahāvairocana mantra through seven days of circumambulation and recitation, and transmitted onward in an abridged recension of roughly three thousand verses — the version this sūtra translates. A related, shorter recension of the same underlying corpus survives as T0866 (金剛頂瑜伽中略出念誦經), translated earlier by Amoghavajra's own teacher Vajrabodhi; this collection translates only T0865, Amoghavajra's own fuller translation, per the scope this project was given.

The base text runs to roughly 24,000 Chinese characters across three fascicles (卷上/中/下), all belonging to a single continuous chapter, 金剛界大曼荼羅廣大儀軌品 ("The Chapter on the Vast Ritual of the Vajra Realm Great Maṇḍala"), which the source itself divides into three parts (之一/之二/之三) that align almost exactly with the fascicle boundaries — a genuinely unusual structure among the texts this site has translated, where the three-part division emerged from the text's own internal markers rather than an editorial decision made for this collection.

## Reading-note sourcing: no surviving commentary

Unlike the *Mahāvairocana Sūtra*, no dedicated line-by-line Chinese commentary on this specific text survives. Before beginning translation, this project checked both CBETA and the Obsidian vault library used throughout this site's translation work, searching specifically for any exegesis matched passage-by-passage to T0865. Several texts turned up under "金剛頂" in the search results — T1798 (金剛頂經大瑜伽祕密心地法門義訣, attributed to Amoghavajra himself) and a wide range of ritual manuals (T0869–T1175) drawing on the broader Vajraśekhara corpus this sūtra belongs to — but on inspection, T1798 proved to be Amoghavajra's own separate ritual/practice manual, cross-referenced in its own text to a different scripture (T0867) rather than a commentary keyed to T0865's own passages, and the remaining texts are independent sādhanas for specific deities or practices rather than sustained exegesis of this sūtra itself.

Per this site's practice on the *Hongyi vinaya essays* project when no suitable premodern commentary could be found for a text otherwise worth translating, reading notes on this collection are offered as Ru-Yi Meditation Center's own original commentary, honestly labeled "Ru-Yi Meditation Center" throughout rather than borrowed from an unrelated source or left unattributed. Where a reading note draws a specific historical or doctrinal connection (to Shingon's *gosō jōjin-gan*, to the vajra-name ordination practice, to the flower-casting rite still performed today), that connection rests on general scholarly consensus about this text's later reception rather than a named premodern commentator's own gloss.

## Structure

| Part | Base text covers | Approx. Chinese length |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 金剛界大曼荼羅廣大儀軌品之一 (卷上): the five-stage process; twelve of the sixteen great bodhisattvas | 8,300 characters |
| 2 | 金剛界大曼荼羅廣大儀軌品之二 (卷中): the sixteen completed; four pāramitā bodhisattvas; eight offering goddesses; four gate-guardians; the 108 names; the maṇḍala's construction | 7,330 characters |
| 3 | 金剛界大曼荼羅廣大儀軌品之三 (卷下): the disciple's entry; the flower-casting rite; consecration; four kinds of siddhi; the mudrā catalogue; releasing the seals | 7,990 characters |

Total: roughly 24,000 characters — considerably shorter than the *Mahāvairocana Sūtra*, but far denser per character, with almost no narrative framing beyond the opening scene and a formulaic generation sequence repeated thirty-seven times for the maṇḍala's own deities.

## Core terminology

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 金剛界 | Vajra Realm / Vajradhātu | Kept as "Vajra Realm" in running prose, with "Vajradhātu" given at first mention and wherever the Sanskrit form is useful for cross-reference to the wider scholarly literature. |
| 婆伽梵 | Bhagavān | Kept as the transliteration the source itself consistently uses (rather than "World-Honored One," as this sūtra's own vocabulary prefers the loanword), matching the practice already established for this term on the Mahāvairocana Sūtra collection. |
| 一切義成就 | Sarvārthasiddhi | Kept as the standard Sanskrit reconstruction of this pre-awakening epithet of the historical Buddha, rather than translated as "Accomplisher of Every Aim" in running prose, to mark the sūtra's own deliberate identification of this figure with Śākyamuni. |
| 嗢陀南 | udāna | Kept as the Sanskrit loanword throughout — a spontaneous utterance of realization, distinct from a mantra or a doctrinal verse — since the source itself transliterates rather than translates this term for the paired verses each deity speaks. |
| 三昧耶 | samaya | Kept as the Sanskrit loanword, matching this site's Mahāvairocana Sūtra glossary; refers here both to the abstract vow/bond a disciple takes on and to a specific class of seal (三昧耶印) built from that same concept. |
| 悉地 | siddhi | Kept as the standard Sanskrit loanword, matching this site's Mahāvairocana Sūtra glossary entry for the same term. |
| 嚩日囉 / 金剛 | vajra | Translated "vajra" throughout rather than the older "diamond," matching this site's established convention; kept as a prefix in every deity's own name (Vajrasattva, Vajrapāṇi, Vajrarāja) rather than translated into a purely English compound. |
| 阿尾捨 | āveśa | Kept as the Sanskrit loanword (ritual possession or entry by the deity), a term this sūtra introduces without full explanation in Part 3; rendered in running prose as "entry" or "possession" contextually where the loanword itself would be unfamiliar to a first-time reader. |

## A note on textual gaps preserved in this translation

Per this site's general practice of honest annotation over silent smoothing, the received Chinese text of T0865, as preserved in Taishō, ends mid-sequence at the close of Part 3 — the seal-release formula is given, but no closing entrustment, dedication, or standard sūtra-closing formula follows, unlike nearly every other text this site has translated. This translation records that gap directly rather than supplying an invented conclusion. Readers wanting the fuller ritual sequence a living tradition would use to actually close a Vajra Realm consecration should understand this text as a working liturgical core rather than a complete, self-contained manual — consistent with this sūtra's own repeated assumption, most visible in its densely compressed mudrā verses, that a living teacher's own demonstration fills in what the written text leaves implicit.

## A note for future Tang-esoteric and related texts

Several renderings coined here — "Sarvārthasiddhi" kept untranslated for the pre-awakening Buddha, "udāna" kept as a loanword distinct from "mantra" and "verse," and the consistent "vajra-" prefix convention for the thirty-seven deities' own names — have no prior site precedent outside this collection and its Mahāvairocana Sūtra companion, and were decided with an eye toward reuse in any future text from the same corpus this site may take up (T0866, Vajrabodhi's own earlier and shorter translation of a related recension; the Susiddhikara; or later ritual manuals in the same Vajraśekhara lineage catalogued in this project's own source search, such as T0869's outline of the "eighteen assemblies"). Future translators working on such texts should check this glossary, and the companion Mahāvairocana Sūtra glossary, before coining fresh renderings for the same terms — and should independently re-verify whether a suitable premodern commentary exists for their specific text before assuming Ru-Yi original commentary is the only option, since this collection's own search covered only T0865 specifically, not the wider corpus.
