The Vajra Realm Maṇḍala

Part 2 completes the thirty-seven deities of the Vajra Realm Maṇḍala: the last four of the sixteen great bodhisattvas, the four pāramitā bodhisattvas born from the four buddhas back toward Mahāvairocana, eight goddesses of offering exchanged between them, and four gate-guardians who complete the assembly. Every buddha in existence is then summoned by a single finger-snap, praised through a hundred and eight names for Vajrapāṇi, and the maṇḍala itself is built, measured out in verse.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 2 of 3 complete Source: Taishō T0865, 金剛頂一切如來真實攝大乘現證大教王經 (tr. Amoghavajra) Reading notes: Ru-Yi Meditation Center original commentary

Amoghasiddhi's Family, and the Sixteen Complete

Part 1 closed as Amoghasiddhi's own family of four bodhisattvas began to emerge. The same generation pattern established by Vajrasattva continues here, completing the sixteen great bodhisattvas of the maṇḍala.

Chinese Source

金剛業 Vajrakarma (毘首羯磨, the crossed vajra): 「奇哉佛不空，我一切業多，無功作佛益，能轉金剛業。」「此是一切佛，作種種勝業，授與我掌中，以業安於業。」

金剛護 Vajrarakṣa (難敵精進, the armor): 「奇哉精進甲，我固堅固者，由堅固無身，作金剛勝身。」「此是一切佛，最勝慈甲冑，堅精進大護，名為大慈友。」

金剛牙 Vajrayakṣa (摧一切魔, the fangs): 「奇哉大方便，諸佛之慈愍，由有形寂靜，示作暴怒形。」「此是一切佛，調伏諸難調，金剛牙器仗，方便愍慈者。」

金剛拳 Vajrasandhi (一切如來拳, the binding fist): 「奇哉妙堅縛，我堅三昧耶，成諸意樂故，解脫者為縛。」「此是一切佛，印縛大堅固，速成諸印故，不越三昧耶。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Vajrakarma, "Vajra Action," the Universal Craftsman, the crossed vajra: "How wondrous — the buddha's own unfailing power; my every action is manifold, accomplishing the buddha's own benefit without effort, able to turn the very work of the vajra." "This is every buddha's own working of every kind of supreme deed, placed now into my own palm: action settled within action."

Vajrarakṣa, "Vajra Protection," the Diligence No Foe Can Match, the armor: "How wondrous — this armor of diligence; I am firm, and firmly grounded, and, from firmness itself without a body, have made a supreme vajra body." "This is every buddha's own most supreme armor of loving-kindness, the firm and great protection of diligence — named the great friend of compassion."

Vajrayakṣa, "Vajra Fangs," the Crusher of Every Māra, the fangs: "How wondrous — this great skillful means, every buddha's own compassion; from a form that is itself utterly still, it displays a wrathful form." "This is every buddha's own subjugation of every being hard to subdue — the vajra fangs, skillful means born of loving compassion."

Vajrasandhi, "Vajra Binding," Every Tathāgata's Own Fist, the binding fist: "How wondrous — this wondrous, firm binding; I am the firm samaya, accomplishing every wish — for one already liberated, this very binding is itself freedom." "This is every buddha's own great firmness of seal and bond: swiftly accomplishing every seal, never transgressing the samaya."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Amoghasiddhi's four complete the sixteen as the perfection of skillful action: Vajrakarma as effortless work itself, Vajrarakṣa as the protection diligence provides, Vajrayakṣa as wrath deployed only in service of compassion — "from a form that is itself utterly still, it displays a wrathful form," the same principle this site's Mahāvairocana Sūtra translation notes already traced in Acala and Trailokyavijaya — and Vajrasandhi's paradox that binding, rightly understood, is itself what liberation looks like from the outside. With all sixteen now born, the maṇḍala's structure stands complete at its inner ring: Mahāvairocana at the center, four buddhas around him, and four bodhisattvas surrounding each of those four in turn.

Four Pāramitā Bodhisattvas

Each of the four surrounding buddhas now returns the gesture, generating a bodhisattva of their own that circles back to Mahāvairocana at the center — the four pāramitās (giving, morality, patience, and diligence collapsed here into vajra, jewel, dharma, and action) offered from the buddhas toward the buddha who first empowered them.

Chinese Source (abridged)

金剛波羅蜜 Vajrapāramitā (from Akṣobhya): 「奇哉一切佛，薩埵金剛堅，由堅無身故，獲得金剛身。」

寶波羅蜜 Ratnapāramitā (from Ratnasambhava): 「奇哉一切佛，我名寶金剛，於一切印眾，堅灌頂理趣。」

法波羅蜜 Dharmapāramitā (from Avalokiteśvara's own Tathāgata form): 「奇哉一切佛，法金剛我淨，由自性清淨，令貪染無垢。」

羯磨波羅蜜 Karmapāramitā (from Amoghasiddhi): 「奇哉一切佛，我名業金剛，由一成一切，佛界善作業。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Vajrapāramitā, born from Akṣobhya: "How wondrous — every buddha! The vajra-being's own firmness: from firmness itself without a body, a vajra body is attained."

Ratnapāramitā, born from Ratnasambhava: "How wondrous — every buddha! I am named Jewel-Vajra; among every seal there is, I hold the very principle of firm consecration."

Dharmapāramitā, born from the Tathāgata form of Avalokiteśvara: "How wondrous — every buddha! I, Dharma-Vajra, am pure; through my own intrinsic purity, I make even passion's own defilement stainless."

Karmapāramitā, born from Amoghasiddhi: "How wondrous — every buddha! I am named Action-Vajra; from the one, I accomplish all — throughout the buddha-realm, skillfully carrying out every deed."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

These four bodhisattvas complete a structural symmetry easy to miss on a first pass through this text: Mahāvairocana generates all sixteen great bodhisattvas outward from his own heart toward the four buddhas' families, and the four buddhas answer by each generating one bodhisattva back toward him — a closed circuit of generation running in both directions at once, rather than a single hierarchy flowing outward from a fixed center. With Mahāvairocana, the four buddhas, the sixteen great bodhisattvas, and now these four pāramitā bodhisattvas, the maṇḍala's core twenty-five deities are complete; the offering goddesses and gate-guardians that follow bring the total to thirty-seven.

Eight Goddesses of Offering

Mahāvairocana next generates four goddesses of inner offering — dance, garland, song, and movement — sent out to the four surrounding buddhas; the four buddhas answer with four goddesses of outer offering — incense, flowers, light, and scented paste — sent back toward Mahāvairocana's own dwelling, the jeweled peak pavilion on Sumeru's summit.

金剛嬉戲 Vajralāsyā (dance, to Akṣobhya): 「奇哉無有比，諸佛中供養，由貪染供養，能轉諸供養。」

金剛鬘 Vajramālā (garland, to Ratnasambhava): 「奇哉我無比，稱為寶供養，於三界王勝，教勅受供養。」

金剛歌詠 Vajragītā (song, to Amitāyus): 「奇哉成歌詠，我供諸見者，由此供養故，諸法如響應。」

金剛舞 Vajranṛtyā (movement, to Amoghasiddhi): 「奇哉廣供養，作諸供養故，由金剛舞儀，安立佛供養。」

金剛燒香 Vajradhūpā (incense, from Akṣobhya): 「奇哉大供養，悅澤具端嚴，由薩埵遍入，速疾證菩提。」

金剛花 Vajrapuṣpā (flowers, from Ratnasambhava): 「奇哉花供養，能作諸莊嚴，由如來寶性，速疾獲供養。」

金剛光 Vajrālokā (light, from Amitāyus): 「奇哉我廣大，供養燈端嚴，由速具光明，獲一切佛眼。」

金剛塗香 Vajragandhā (scented paste, from Amoghasiddhi): 「奇哉香供養，我微妙悅意，由如來香故，授與一切身。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Vajralāsyā, "Vajra Dance," sent to Akṣobhya: "How wondrous, beyond compare — this offering amid every buddha; through the offering of desire's own defilement, every offering itself is transformed."

Vajramālā, "Vajra Garland," sent to Ratnasambhava: "How wondrous — beyond compare, I am named the offering of the jewel; supreme among kings of the three worlds, I receive offering by his own command."

Vajragītā, "Vajra Song," sent to Amitāyus: "How wondrous — this song accomplished; I make offering to everyone who sees; through this very offering, every dharma answers back, as an echo answers a sound."

Vajranṛtyā, "Vajra Movement," sent to Amoghasiddhi: "How wondrous — this vast offering, carrying out every kind of offering; through the vajra's own dance, the buddha's offering is established."

Vajradhūpā, "Vajra Incense," returning from Akṣobhya: "How wondrous — this great offering, glossy and finely adorned; through the vajra-being's own pervasive entry, bodhi is swiftly realized."

Vajrapuṣpā, "Vajra Flower," returning from Ratnasambhava: "How wondrous — this offering of flowers, able to accomplish every adornment; through the Tathāgata's own jewel-nature, offering is swiftly obtained."

Vajrālokā, "Vajra Light," returning from Amitāyus: "How wondrous — this vast offering, the lamp's own fine adornment; through swiftly possessing this very light, the eye of every buddha is obtained."

Vajragandhā, "Vajra Scent," returning from Amoghasiddhi: "How wondrous — this offering of incense, subtle and wondrous, delighting the mind; through the Tathāgata's own fragrance, it is bestowed upon every body."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The same closed circuit already noted for the four pāramitā bodhisattvas repeats here at a finer scale: Mahāvairocana's inner offerings (dance, garland, song, movement — arts of the body performed toward the four buddhas) are answered by the four buddhas' outer offerings (incense, flowers, light, scented paste — substances presented back toward Mahāvairocana). Every element of a maṇḍala's own physical offering table — incense burning, flowers arranged, lamps lit, scented paste applied — is, on this text's own account, not simply a devotional gesture borrowed from ordinary ritual custom but the very substance of four goddesses generated by the buddhas themselves, giving material form to a gift-exchange this maṇḍala treats as intrinsic to its own structure rather than added onto it from outside.

Four Gate-Guardians, and the Summoning of Every Buddha

Chinese Source (abridged)

金剛鉤 Vajrāṅkuśa (the hook, east gate): 「奇哉一切佛，鉤誓我堅固，由我遍鉤召，集諸曼荼羅。」

金剛索 Vajrapāśa (the lasso, south gate): 「奇哉一切佛，我堅金剛索，設入諸微塵，我復引入此。」

金剛鎖 Vajrasphoṭa (the chain, west gate): 「奇哉一切佛，大堅金剛鎖，令諸縛脫者，有情利故縛。」

金剛鈴 Vajrāveśa (the bell, north gate): 「奇哉一切佛，我堅金剛入，為一切主宰，亦即為僮僕。」

爾時，世尊為一切如來召集故，作金剛彈指相，說此一切如來召集加持心：「嚩日囉三摩惹」由剎那攞嚩須臾頃，一切如來彈指相警覺已，遍一切世界雲海中一切世界微塵等如來并菩薩集會曼荼羅。

Ru-Yi Translation

Vajrāṅkuśa, "Vajra Hook," east gate: "How wondrous — every buddha! My own vow of the hook is firm; through my own pervasive summoning, every maṇḍala is gathered together."

Vajrapāśa, "Vajra Lasso," south gate: "How wondrous — every buddha! My vajra lasso is firm; though one enter as far as every mote of dust, I shall draw them back in here."

Vajrasphoṭa, "Vajra Chain," west gate: "How wondrous — every buddha! The great and firm vajra chain: it binds those already free of every bond, precisely for the benefit of beings."

Vajrāveśa, "Vajra Bell," north gate: "How wondrous — every buddha! My own firm vajra entry: I am sovereign over all, and yet also the humblest servant."

At that time the World-Honored One, so as to summon every Tathāgata, formed the vajra finger-snap gesture, and spoke this empowering heart-mantra summoning every Tathāgata: Vajra-samāja ["Vajra-assembly"]. In the space of a single finger-snap, every Tathāgata, roused by that very gesture, gathered — every Tathāgata and bodhisattva, as numerous as the motes of dust in every ocean of world-clouds throughout every world-system — into the assembled maṇḍala.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

With these four gate-guardians, the maṇḍala's thirty-seven deities stand complete: Mahāvairocana at the center; the four surrounding buddhas; the sixteen great bodhisattvas of their four families; the four pāramitā bodhisattvas returning toward the center; the eight goddesses of offering exchanged in both directions; and these four gate-guardians stationed at the maṇḍala's own four entrances. Vajrasphoṭa's own paradox — "it binds those already free of every bond, precisely for the benefit of beings" — echoes Vajrasandhi's identical claim about the binding fist earlier in this same Part, a deliberate doubling this text does not treat as redundant: a maṇḍala this systematically constructed can afford to state its central paradoxes more than once, at different scales, without diluting either statement. That every Tathāgata in the universe answers a single finger-snap "in the space of a single finger-snap" gives this text its own image for what the Mahāvairocana Sūtra called the compression of three incalculable kalpas into one lifetime: here, the assembling of the entire cosmos takes no longer than the gesture itself.

The Hundred-and-Eight Names of Vajrapāṇi

Before the maṇḍala itself is built, the assembled Tathāgatas praise Vajrapāṇi — the very bodhisattva this text has followed from the bodhi tree onward — through a hundred and eight epithets, one for each of the maṇḍala's own deities and their qualities, gathered into a single sustained hymn.

Chinese Source (selected stanzas)

「金剛勇大心，　金剛諸如來，普賢金剛初，　我禮金剛手；金剛王妙覺，　金剛鉤如來，不空王金剛，　我禮金剛召；……金剛法善利，　金剛蓮妙淨，世貴金剛眼，　我禮金剛眼；……金剛業教令，　金剛廣不空，業金剛遍行，　我禮金剛巧；……若有持此名，　百八寂靜讚，金剛名灌頂，　彼亦獲如是。若有以此名，　讚大持金剛，正意歌詠者，　彼如持金剛。我等以此名，　一百八名讚，願大乘現證，　遍流大理趣。我等讚汝尊，　願說最勝儀，一切佛大輪，　勝大曼荼羅。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Vajra of valiant, great mind, vajra of every Tathāgata, first vajra of Samantabhadra — I bow to Vajrapāṇi! Vajra-king of wondrous awakening, vajra Tathāgata of the hook, vajra of the unfailing king — I bow to the vajra summoner! …Vajra of good benefit in the Dharma, vajra of the wondrously pure lotus, the world's own treasured vajra eye — I bow to the vajra eye! …Vajra of every commanded deed, vajra vast and unfailing, vajra of action pervading all — I bow to the vajra of skill! …Whoever holds these hundred and eight names, this tranquil hymn of praise, is consecrated by the very name of vajra, and shall obtain the same. Whoever, with a settled and sincere mind, sings these names in praise of the great vajra-holder, is themselves like one who holds the vajra. With these hundred and eight names, we now offer this hymn of praise, and pray that the Great Vehicle's own direct realization may flow forth everywhere, in its supreme principle. We praise you, Honored One — we beg you now to teach the most supreme rite: the great wheel of every buddha, the supreme great maṇḍala."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The hundred and eight names — a number this tradition shares with the hundred and eight beads of a standard mala, and with the hundred and eight afflictions catalogued elsewhere in Buddhist literature — move systematically through the maṇḍala's own thirty-seven deities, praising each by a cluster of epithets rather than a single fixed name, so that the hymn functions as a condensed recitation of the entire maṇḍala already generated across this Part. Its closing promise — that whoever recites these names "with a settled and sincere mind" is themselves "like one who holds the vajra" — restates, in the register of devotional hymn rather than philosophical claim, the same non-duality between practitioner and deity this site's Mahāvairocana Sūtra translation notes already traced through the "mirror of the self" and the illusory man conjuring a further illusion of himself.

Building the Maṇḍala

Chinese Source (selected)

「次當我遍說，　勝大曼荼羅，由如金剛界，　名為金剛界。……以新線善合，　應量以端嚴，以線智應抨，　隨力曼荼羅。四方應四門，　四剎而嚴飾，四線而交絡，　繒綵鬘莊嚴。……彼中如輪形，　應入於中宮，金剛線遍抨，　八柱而莊嚴。於金剛勝柱，　應飾五輪壇，於中曼荼羅，　安立佛形像。佛一切周圍，　曼荼羅於中，四勝三昧耶，　次第而圖畫。金剛進而步，　於四曼荼羅，阿閦毘等四，　安立一切佛。應作不動壇，　劑金剛持等，金剛藏等滿，　寶生曼荼羅。金剛眼淨業，　無量壽輪壇，應畫不空成，　金剛巧等壇。安立於輪隅，　應畫金剛女，外壇於隅角，　應畫佛供養。門中一切處，　守護門四眾，安立於外壇，　應畫摩訶薩。……召集作彈指，　應請一切佛，剎那頃諸佛，　并金剛薩埵，應滿一切壇，　集會曼荼羅。……願作一切成，　如是諸壇中，金剛師事業。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"I shall now explain in full this supreme, great maṇḍala — because it is like the vajra realm itself, it is named the Vajra Realm…With new thread, well joined, measured out and finely wrought, mark it out with the thread of wisdom, according to one's own capacity, this maṇḍala. Four gates to the four directions, its four boundaries finely adorned; four threads crossing and interwoven, adorned with silken garlands. …Within it, in the shape of a wheel, enter into the central hall, marked throughout with vajra thread, adorned with eight pillars. Upon these supreme vajra pillars, adorn the altar with the five-wheel design; within it, in the maṇḍala's own center, establish the buddha images. With the Buddha surrounded on every side, within the maṇḍala itself, paint, in due sequence, the four supreme samayas. The vajra advancing step by step through the four maṇḍalas, establish every buddha — Akṣobhya and the other four. Fashion Akṣobhya's own altar, measured by Vajradhara and the rest; fill in Vajragarbha and the rest, the maṇḍala of Ratnasambhava. Vajra Eye's own pure deeds, the wheel-altar of Amitāyus; paint Amoghasiddhi's own accomplishment, the altar of Vajra Skill and the rest. At the wheel's own corners, paint the vajra goddesses; at the outer altar's own corners, paint the buddhas' own offerings. At every place within the gates, the four assemblies who guard the gates; upon the outer altar, paint these great beings. …Summoning with the finger-snap, invite every buddha; in the space of an instant, every buddha, together with Vajrasattva, will fill the whole altar, the assembled maṇḍala. …May everything be accomplished, in every altar such as this — the very work of the vajra master."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This passage gives the Vajra Realm Maṇḍala its own working blueprint: a square altar with four gates, an inner hall marked by eight pillars, a five-wheel central design, and the thirty-seven deities generated across this entire Part placed, in due order, at their own fixed positions — Mahāvairocana and the "four supreme samayas" (the four pāramitā bodhisattvas) at the center, the four buddha-families arrayed around him with their own sixteen bodhisattvas, the offering goddesses at the wheel's corners, and the gate-guardians at the outer altar's edges. Read together with the Womb Maṇḍala's own construction instructions in the Mahāvairocana Sūtra's Part 2, the contrast between the two systems is instructive: where the Womb Maṇḍala grows outward from a single central lotus through three concentric courts of increasingly worldly figures, the Vajra Realm Maṇḍala is organized instead around a fivefold symmetry — one central buddha and four surrounding families, each family itself a fourfold set — a difference in geometry that later Shingon teaching read as two complementary halves of a single, complete esoteric path, neither one sufficient without the other.

The Karma Family, Generated

The fourth and last family of four completes the sixteen. The procedure is unchanged, but the emblems turn practical: a karma-vajra of crossed prongs, a suit of armor, a fang, and a fist. Amoghasiddhi’s family is the one that acts.

“Because firmness is bodiless, it makes the vajra” and “because it accomplishes every intention, for the liberated it becomes a bond” are the two udānas of this family, and both turn on the same reversal. Armor protects because there is nothing inside to protect; a bond is put on someone already free. The family that acts is given the two emblems that would seem least compatible with freedom, and defines both by it.

Four Pāramitās Seal the Work

The sixteen are complete, and the four buddhas of the directions now answer. Each seals Mahāvairocana’s wisdom with his own and produces a pāramitā bodhisattva — Vajra, Jewel, Dharma, Karma — the four that sit closest to the center in the painted maṇḍala.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The two sets are not symmetrical in status and the text says so in its vocabulary: the inner four are “great goddesses” (大天女) issuing from the center, the outer four are “handmaids” and “maidservants” (婚使, 女使) sent back from the four buddhas. The asymmetry is directional rather than hierarchical — they are the reply, not the lesser — but the words are what they are, and softening them in translation would hide something about how this liturgy imagined its own courtesies.

Vajra-Incense’s udāna has the sequence that matters: the offering is what allows the sattva to enter everywhere, and that entering is what makes awakening swift. Offering here is not merit-making. It is the mechanism of pervasion.

集已，往詣金剛摩尼寶峯樓閣世尊毘盧遮那如來所。至已，說禮一切如來足心：……由此性成就真言隨意念誦禮一切如來已，說此嗢陀南：「奇哉大普賢，　菩薩之敬儀，是如來輪壇，　影現於如來。」時十方一切世界集會如來說已，由一切如來加持，一切菩薩集會曼荼羅，入毘盧遮那佛心。從彼一切如來心各各自菩薩眾曼荼羅出已，依世尊毘盧遮那佛金剛摩尼寶峯樓閣周圍作壇三摩地而住，說此嗢陀南：「奇哉一切佛，　廣大無始生，由一切塵數，　獲得佛一性。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Having gathered, they went to the vajra maṇi jewel-peak pavilion, to the World-Honored One Mahāvairocana Tathāgata. Having arrived, they spoke the heart of homage at the feet of all Tathāgatas: …And having by this nature-accomplishing mantra recited at will and paid homage to all the Tathāgatas, they spoke this udāna: “How marvelous, great Samantabhadra, the bodhisattva’s form of reverence! This is the Tathāgata’s wheel-altar, reflected and appearing within the Tathāgata.”

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

“This is the Tathāgata’s wheel-altar, reflected and appearing within the Tathāgata” makes the act of homage into the maṇḍala itself. The buddhas bowing to a buddha are not performing a courtesy before the diagram is drawn; the arrangement of them bowing is the diagram.
