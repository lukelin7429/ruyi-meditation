Dharma › Mahāyāna Scriptures › Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha
Amoghavajra · c. 746–771 CE · 金剛頂一切如來真實攝大乘現證大教王經

Becoming Vajradhātu.

The later of the two root scriptures of the systematic Tang esoteric tradition — the other being the Mahāvairocana Sūtra. It opens at the most famous night in the whole Buddhist tradition — Siddhārtha's own austerity beneath the bodhi tree — and retells it from the inside: every Tathāgata in existence interrupts him at once, and leads him through five confirmed stages of realization that make him, before he ever rises from that seat, into Vajradhātu. What follows is the birth of the thirty-seven deities of the Vajra Realm Maṇḍala, a hundred and eight names for Vajrapāṇi, and the blindfolded flower-casting rite that still opens Shingon initiation today. Three parts, translated by Amoghavajra, third of the Kaiyuan Three Masters who founded this tradition.

How to read this collection

This is a shorter but extraordinarily dense text — roughly 24,000 Chinese characters across three fascicles, all belonging to a single continuous chapter (金剛界大曼荼羅廣大儀軌品) that the source text itself divides into three parts (之一 / 之二 / 之三) matching its own three juan almost exactly. Unlike the Mahāvairocana Sūtra, no dedicated line-by-line Chinese commentary on this specific text survives; this site checked CBETA and its own source library directly before beginning translation, and found several ritual manuals drawing on the wider Vajraśekhara corpus but no sustained, passage-matched exegesis of this text itself. Following this site's practice on the Hongyi vinaya essays project, reading notes here are Ru-Yi Meditation Center's own original commentary, honestly labeled as such.

Each page carries a continuous run of the base text matched against Ru-Yi's English translation. Where a passage anticipates a later development in the East Asian esoteric tradition — the five-stage process behind Shingon's gosō jōjin-gan, the vajra-name ordination practice, the flower-casting rite still performed today — the reading notes trace that connection out explicitly, while remaining honest about the limits of what a text this compressed can be reconstructed to mean without a living teacher's own demonstration alongside it.

What each page includes

  • The Chinese source text beside our English translation, including the sūtra's own mantras — kept in phonetic transliteration and reconstructed Sanskrit per this site's standing dhāraṇī convention.
  • Reading notes labeled "Ru-Yi Meditation Center" throughout — original commentary, offered honestly in the absence of a surviving premodern Chinese exegesis matched to this text.
  • Cross-references to this site's Mahāvairocana Sūtra collection wherever a figure, technique, or doctrinal claim appears in both texts under a different name.
  • An honest note on the limits of this translation's own reconstruction of the sūtra's densest mudrā verses, and on the received text's own incomplete close.

The Sūtra's Own Structure

A single chapter, 金剛界大曼荼羅廣大儀軌品 ("The Chapter on the Vast Ritual of the Vajra Realm Great Maṇḍala"), divided by the source text itself into three parts that align almost exactly with its own three fascicles.

卷上 · Part 1
The Five-Stage Process of Awakening
Sarvārthasiddhi's asceticism interrupted by every Tathāgata; the five confirmed stages that make him Vajradhātu; twelve of the sixteen great bodhisattvas born from Mahāvairocana's own heart.
Part 1 · complete
卷中 · Part 2
The Vajra Realm Maṇḍala
The sixteen great bodhisattvas completed, four pāramitā bodhisattvas, eight goddesses of offering, four gate-guardians, a hundred and eight names for Vajrapāṇi, and the maṇḍala's own construction blueprint.
Part 2 · complete
卷下 · Part 3
The Disciple's Entry, and the Sūtra's Close
A maṇḍala that refuses no one, the blindfolded flower that reveals a disciple's own affinity, consecration and a new vajra name, four kinds of siddhi, and the release of every seal.

Translation Notes

Recurring terms, kept consistent across all three parts. Where a term already has an established rendering on this site's Mahāvairocana Sūtra collection, we reuse it directly. See the full translation notes for the complete glossary, and also this site's note on translation method.

婆伽梵 — BhagavānKept as the transliteration the source itself uses, matching how this site's Mahāvairocana Sūtra collection also keeps the term untranslated where the source treats it as a proper title.
一切義成就 — SarvārthasiddhiKept as the standard Sanskrit reconstruction of the historical Buddha's own pre-awakening name, rather than translated, to mark this as a deliberate identification rather than a generic epithet.
嗢陀南 — udānaKept as the Sanskrit loanword (a spontaneous utterance of realization), matching this text's own consistent use of the transliteration for every deity's paired verses.
三昧耶 — samayaKept as the Sanskrit loanword throughout, matching this site's convention on the Mahāvairocana Sūtra collection, where the same term is likewise left untranslated as a fixed technical term.
悉地 — siddhiKept as the standard Sanskrit loanword, matching this site's Mahāvairocana Sūtra glossary entry for the same term.
嚩日囉 / 金剛 — vajra金剛 translated as "vajra" throughout when naming an implement or a deity's own epithet (Vajrasattva, Vajrapāṇi), matching this site's established convention rather than the older, now less standard "diamond."
