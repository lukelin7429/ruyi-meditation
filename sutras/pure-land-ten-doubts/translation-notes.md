# Ten Doubts About the Pure Land — Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Treatise on Ten Doubts About the Pure Land* (淨土十疑論, *Jingtu Shiyi Lun*), Taishō T1961, traditionally attributed to Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597). They exist so that vocabulary stays consistent across all ten parts, and so that terminology without precedent anywhere else on this site is recorded for any later text to inherit rather than re-coin.

## The text, and its disputed authorship

Unlike the *Sūtra of Kṣitigarbha's Fundamental Vows* or the *Awakening of Faith*, whose authorship questions concern whether a text has any genuine Indic original at all, this treatise's authorship question is narrower and, on one specific piece of evidence, sharper: whether the historical Zhiyi personally wrote the text that bears his name. The Japanese scholar Mochizuki Shinkō (望月信亨, 1869–1948) argued he could not have — Doubt 7's account of Asaṅga, Vasubandhu, and Siṃhabhadra meeting again in Tuṣita matches the version recorded in Xuanzang's (玄奘) *Great Tang Records of the Western Regions*, compiled after 645 CE, forty-eight years after Zhiyi's death in 597. No Sui- or Tang-dynasty bibliography lists the treatise among Zhiyi's works, and its transmission to Japan is inconsistent: Saichō's early ninth-century catalog names no author, while a later Enryaku-ji catalog attributes it to Zhanran instead. The full account, including what this project does and does not conclude from it, is set out in [Part 1's Translation Method section](/sutras/pure-land-ten-doubts/part-01/) and is not repeated at length elsewhere, since it is a claim about the whole text rather than any one doubt.

The base text — ten question-and-answer units, each opened by the treatise's own "第X疑" marker — runs to roughly 5,300 Chinese characters, framed by Yang Jie's 1076 preface (translated in full in Part 1) and Chen Guan's 1093 postscript (translated in full in Part 10).

## Reading-note sourcing

Per this site's commentator-vetting convention, reading notes are grounded in a named commentator rather than presented as unattributed analysis. Chengyu (澄彧), a Song-dynasty monk about whom little else is recorded, wrote the *Zhu Shiyi Lun* (注十疑論, X1149), a single-fascicle line-by-line commentary prefaced in 983 CE by Zanning (贊寧, 919–1001), the eminent monk-historian who compiled the *Song Biographies of Eminent Monks*. Zanning's preface already treats the attribution to "the Great Master Zhiyi" as established fact, predating Yang Jie's own preface by nearly a century. Chengyu's commentary reads largely as a philological gloss — definitions of technical terms, citations tracing the treatise's own scriptural quotations back to their source — with occasional doctrinal elaboration and, at the close of Doubt 10, a sustained personal defense of buddha-recitation practice against critics of his own day.

This one-doubt-per-page split follows the treatise's own ten numbered markers exactly, matching this site's approach on other short, naturally-segmented treatises such as the *Hundred Dharmas* and the *Twelve Gate Treatise* rather than grouping doubts into longer, unevenly-sized pages. Doubt lengths vary more than fivefold (205 to 1,072 characters), and no attempt was made to smooth this out by merging short doubts together.

## Core terminology

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 無生忍 | the acceptance of non-arising | The bodhisattva's realization that no dharma truly arises or ceases (無生法忍, "the acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas," shortened in running prose); kept consistent with this site's other Pure Land and Madhyamaka pages rather than rendered "insight into non-arising," to preserve the sense of active acceptance the compound 忍 (kṣānti, "forbearance" or "acceptance") carries. |
| 具縛凡夫 | an ordinary being, still bound in every fetter | Literally "an ordinary person bound in every fetter" (縛 glossed by Chengyu as afflictions binding a person). Kept as a full descriptive phrase rather than shortened to "an ordinary being," since the treatise's arguments in Doubts 1, 5, 6, and 8 all turn specifically on the fact of being *bound*, not merely ordinary. |
| 易行道 / 難行道 | the easy path / the hard path | Nāgārjuna's own pair of terms from the *Daśabhūmika-vibhāṣā* (十住毗婆沙論), quoted directly in Doubts 1, 5, and 7; kept as the standard rendering used across this site's Pure Land pages rather than "the path of ease/difficulty." |
| 他力 / 自力 | other-power / self-power | Kept as a literal, hyphenated pairing rather than translated with a single English word (e.g. "grace"), since Doubt 5's entire argument depends on the reader tracking which of the two is in play at each step. |
| 機、感相應 | capacity and response answer each other | The recurring formula for how a practitioner's own readiness (機, glossed by Chengyu as a crossbow's trigger) meets a buddha's compassionate response (感應); kept as a fixed phrase across Doubts 3, 4, and 7 rather than varied for elegance. |
| 十念 | the ten thoughts | The deathbed recitation practice at the center of Doubt 8; kept literal rather than paraphrased as "ten recitations" or "ten repetitions of the name," since the treatise's own argument turns on these being moments of mind (在心, 在緣, 在決定), not merely repeated words. |
| 厭離行 / 欣願行 | the practice of renunciation / the practice of aspiration | The twofold practice that closes Doubt 10; kept as a matched pair throughout, since the treatise presents them as two inseparable halves of a single practice rather than as alternatives a practitioner might choose between. |
| 阿鞞跋致 / 阿毗跋致 | non-retrogression | Transliterated forms of avaivartika (irreversibility) that appear in Doubts 5 and 7; translated rather than transliterated, matching this site's general preference for the plain English term over the Sanskrit loanword where one is already established elsewhere on the site. |
| 麤相 / 細相 | coarse marks / subtle marks | The two levels of a buddha's or a pure land's appearance, visible according to a perceiver's own spiritual capacity (Doubts 3, 5, 6, 9); kept as a fixed pair rather than translated "gross" and "fine," to avoid "gross" reading as a value judgment in English that the Chinese does not carry. |

## A note on this treatise's authorship question and its two framing texts

Yang Jie's 1076 preface and Chen Guan's 1093 postscript both refer to the treatise's author as Zhiyi without qualification, and both were composed well after the tenth-century commentary this collection reads throughout — meaning neither framing text, nor Chengyu's own commentary, offers any independent check on the attribution; all three simply inherit and restate it. Readers should not mistake the age or reverence of these framing texts for evidence on the authorship question itself, which rests on textual-historical grounds unrelated to how long or how confidently a tradition has held to it. See Part 1 for the full discussion.

## A note for future Tiantai-line and Pure Land texts

Several renderings coined here — "the acceptance of non-arising" for 無生忍, "an ordinary being, still bound in every fetter" for 具縛凡夫, and "capacity and response answer each other" for 機感相應 — have no prior site precedent and were decided with an eye toward reuse should this site later take up further Tiantai-line material or additional Pure Land treatises beyond the three sūtras and Samantabhadra's vows already translated here. Future translators working on such texts should check this glossary before coining fresh renderings for the same terms.
