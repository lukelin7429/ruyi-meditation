The Assembly, and the Radiant Countenance

序分

At Vulture Peak, an assembly of arhats and bodhisattvas beyond counting. Then, before a word of teaching is given, Ānanda notices something strange in the Buddha's own bearing — and asks about it.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T360 · Saṃghavarman's translation, 3rd century CE Commentary guide: Jingying Huiyuan's 無量壽經義疏 (6th c.)
This part covers
The opening assembly of twelve thousand monks and countless bodhisattvas; Ānanda's question about the Buddha's unusual radiance; the Buddha's explanation of why a Tathāgata appears in the world at all.
Speaker
Ānanda opens the dialogue; Śākyamuni Buddha answers
Setting
Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa (Vulture Peak), near Rājagṛha — not Jetavana, the setting of the Amitābha and Diamond Sūtras

1. The Assembly

Chinese Source

我聞如是。一時佛住王舍城耆闍崛山中。與大比丘衆萬二千人俱。一切大聖神通已達，其名曰：尊者了本際、尊者正願、尊者正語、尊者大號、尊者仁賢、尊者離垢、尊者名聞、尊者善實、尊者具足、尊者牛王、尊者優樓頻螺迦葉、尊者伽耶迦葉、尊者那提迦葉、尊者摩訶迦葉、尊者舍利弗、尊者大目揵連、尊者劫賓那、尊者大住、尊者大淨志、尊者摩訶周那、尊者滿願子、尊者離鄣、尊者流灌、尊者堅伏、尊者面王、尊者異乘、尊者仁性、尊者嘉樂、尊者善來、尊者羅云、尊者阿難，皆如斯等上首者也。又與大乘衆菩薩俱，普賢菩薩、妙德菩薩、慈氏菩薩等，此賢劫中一切菩薩。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus have I heard: at one time the Buddha was dwelling on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa, near Rājagṛha, together with a great assembly of monks, twelve thousand in all — every one of them a great sage who had already fully mastered the spiritual powers. Their names were: the venerables Fully Comprehending the Ultimate, True Vow, True Speech, Great Renown, Benevolent and Worthy, Free of Defilement, Widely Renowned, Truly Good, Fully Endowed, Lord of Cattle, Uruvilvā Kāśyapa, Gayā Kāśyapa, Nadī Kāśyapa, Mahākāśyapa, Śāriputra, Mahāmaudgalyāyana, Kapphiṇa, Great Abiding, Great Pure Resolve, Mahācunda, the Fulfiller of Vows, Free of Obstruction, Flowing Consecration, Firmly Subdued, Lord of Countenance, the Uncommon Vehicle, Benevolent Nature, Auspicious Joy, Svāgata, Rāhula, and Ānanda — every one of them foremost within the assembly. And with them were bodhisattvas of the Great Vehicle: the bodhisattva Samantabhadra, the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, the bodhisattva Maitreya, and all the bodhisattvas of this Fortunate Aeon.

Reading note — after Huiyuan

Worth flagging before anything else: this list of monks looks almost nothing like the list that opens the Amitābha Sūtra. Kumārajīva, translating that shorter sūtra a century and a half later, rendered the disciples' names phonetically — Śāriputra, Rāhula, Ānanda, names a reader can look up. Saṃghavarman, here, mostly translates by meaning instead of sound: "Fully Comprehending the Ultimate," "True Vow," "Free of Obstruction." A handful are still recognizable — the three Kāśyapa brothers, Śāriputra, Mahāmaudgalyāyana, Kapphiṇa, Mahācunda, Rāhula, Ānanda, and almost certainly Svāgata (善來, "well come," a known disciple elsewhere transliterated) and Gavāṃpati ("Lord of Cattle," 牛王, again a meaning-rendering of the same name Kumārajīva transliterated as 憍梵波提 in the Amitābha Sūtra). The rest — "Great Abiding," "Flowing Consecration" — do not match any settled Sanskrit disciple name well enough to reconstruct with confidence, so this translation keeps them as plain English renderings of the Chinese rather than inventing a false phonetic precision.

Huiyuan reads a sūtra's opening through the traditional six markers of authenticity — what was heard, who heard it, when, by whom taught, where, before whom — and treats this sūtra's structure overall as falling into three parts running the length of the whole text: an introductory section (序), the main teaching (正宗), and a closing transmission (流通), a division this nine-part translation follows loosely in its own groupings. He also classifies the whole of Buddhist teaching into two treasuries, the Śrāvaka teaching and the Bodhisattva teaching, and places this sūtra within the latter as a teaching pitched at minds already ripe — worth noting as a scholastic framework Huiyuan brings to the text rather than a claim the sūtra makes about itself.

2. Sixteen Noble Ones, and What a Bodhisattva Can Become

Chinese Source

又賢護等十六正士：善思議菩薩、信慧菩薩、空無菩薩、神通華菩薩、光英菩薩、慧上菩薩、智幢菩薩、寂根菩薩、願慧菩薩、香象菩薩、寶英菩薩、中住菩薩、制行菩薩、解脫菩薩。皆導普賢大士之德，具諸菩薩無量行願，安住一切功德之法。遊步十方，行權方便。入佛法藏，究竟彼岸。於無量世界現成等覺。處兜率天，弘宣正法。捨彼天宮，降神母胎。從右脅生，現行七步。光明顯曜，普照十方無量佛土。六種震動，舉聲自稱：吾當於世為無上尊。釋梵奉侍，天人歸仰。示現筭計、文藝、射御。博綜道術，貫練群籍。遊於後園，講武試藝。現處宮中色味之間。見老病死悟世非常。棄國財位，入山學道。服乘、白馬、寶冠、瓔珞，遣之令還。捨珍妙衣，而著法服。剃除鬚髮。端坐樹下。勤苦六年。行如所應。現五濁剎，隨順群生。示有塵垢，沐浴金流。天案樹枝，得攀出池。靈禽翼從，往詣道場。吉祥感徵，表章功祚。哀受施草，敷佛樹下，加趺而坐。奮大光明，使魔知之。魔率官屬而來逼試。制以智力，皆令降伏。得微妙法，成最正覺。釋梵祈勸，請轉法輪。以佛遊步。佛吼而吼。扣法鼓，吹法螺，執法劍，建法幢，震法雷，曜法電，澍法雨，演法施。常以法音覺諸世間，光明普照無量佛土。一切世界六種震動，總攝魔界，動魔宮殿。衆魔慴怖，莫不歸伏。摑裂邪網，消滅諸見。散諸塵勞，壞諸欲塹。嚴護法城，開闡法門。洗濯垢污，顯明清白。光融佛法，宣流正化。入國分衛，獲諸豐饍。貯功德，示福田。欲宣法，現欣笑。以諸法藥救療三苦。顯現道意無量功德。授菩薩記，成等正覺。示現滅度，拯濟無極。消除諸漏，殖衆德本。具足功德微妙難量。遊諸佛國，普現道教。其所修行清淨無穢。譬如幻師，現衆異像，為男為女，無所不變。本學明了，在意所為。此諸菩薩亦復如是。學一切法，貫綜縷練。所住安諦，靡不感化。無數佛土，皆悉普現。未曾慢恣愍傷衆生。如是之法一切具足。菩薩經典究暢要妙。名稱普至導御十方。無量諸佛咸共護念。佛所住者皆已得住。大聖所立而皆已立。如來導化各能宣布。為諸菩薩而作大師。以甚深禪慧開導衆人。通諸法性，達衆生相。明了諸國，供養諸佛。化現其身，猶如電光。善學無畏，曉了幻法。壞裂魔網，解諸纏縛。超越聲聞、緣覺之地。得空、無相、無願三昧。善立方便。顯示三乘。於此中下而現滅度。亦無所作，亦無所有。不起不滅，得平等法。具足成就無量總持百千三昧。諸根智慧廣普寂定。深入菩薩法藏。得佛華嚴三昧。宣揚演說一切經典。住深定門。悉覩現在無量諸佛。一念之頃無不周遍。濟諸劇難諸閑不閑。分別顯示真實之際。得諸如來辯才之智。入衆言音，開化一切。超過世間諸所有法。心常諦住度世之道。於一切萬物而隨意自在。為衆生類作不請之友。荷負群生為之重任。受持如來甚深法藏。護佛種性常使不絕。興大悲，愍衆生。演慈辯，授法眼。杜三趣，開善門。以不請之法，施諸黎庶。猶如孝子愛敬父母，於諸衆生視之若己，一切善本皆度彼岸。悉獲諸佛無量功德，智慧聖明不可思議。如是菩薩大士，不可稱計，一時來會。

Ru-Yi Translation

And with them were Bhadrapāla and fifteen other noble ones, sixteen in all: the bodhisattvas Wondrous Contemplation, Faith and Wisdom, Emptiness, Flower of Spiritual Power, Radiant Splendor, Supreme Wisdom, Banner of Wisdom, Tranquil Faculties, Vow and Wisdom, Fragrant Elephant, Jeweled Splendor, Abiding in the Mean, Disciplined Conduct, and Liberation. All of them followed the virtues of the great being Samantabhadra, were endowed with the bodhisattvas' immeasurable conduct and vows, and abided in the Dharma of every kind of merit. They walked freely through the ten directions, acting with skillful and expedient means; they had entered the treasury of the Buddha-dharma and reached its farthest shore. In immeasurable worlds they appeared as fully awakened buddhas: dwelling in the Tuṣita heaven, they proclaimed the true Dharma there; then, leaving that heavenly palace, they descended in spirit into a mother's womb, were born from her right side, and at once took seven steps, their light shining out to illumine the immeasurable buddha-lands of the ten directions, while the earth shook in six ways and they raised their voice to declare: "In this world I shall be the unsurpassed one." Śakra and Brahmā waited upon them in service, and gods and humans turned to them in reverence. They showed themselves learning calculation, letters and the arts, archery and charioteering; broadly versed in every skill and discipline, they mastered every book. They wandered the rear gardens, practiced arms, and tested their skill; they appeared to dwell amid the pleasures of form and taste within the palace — and then, seeing old age, sickness, and death, awakened to the impermanence of the world. They renounced their kingdom, wealth, and rank, and went into the mountains to study the way; giving up their carriage, their white horse, their jeweled crown, and their necklace, they sent these back, put aside their fine and precious robes, and put on the robes of a renunciant. They shaved their hair and beard and sat upright beneath a tree, enduring six years of hardship, practicing exactly as was fitting. They appeared in lands of the five turbidities, following and according with the beings there, showing themselves stained with the dust of the world — and then bathed in a stream of gold; a deva bent down a branch of a tree so they could climb out of the pool, and wondrous birds flew beside them as their escort to the seat of awakening. Auspicious signs responded and made manifest their merit. They accepted, in compassion, an offering of grass, spread it beneath the bodhi tree, and sat there cross-legged. They roused a great light, so that Māra would know; Māra led his host to press them with trials, but they mastered them by the power of wisdom, and every one submitted. They attained the subtle and wondrous Dharma and became perfectly, completely awakened. Śakra and Brahmā entreated and urged them to turn the Dharma wheel. Walking with a buddha's gait, they roared with a buddha's roar; they struck the Dharma drum, blew the Dharma conch, wielded the Dharma sword, raised the Dharma banner, thundered the Dharma thunder, flashed the Dharma lightning, poured down the Dharma rain, and proclaimed the gift of the Dharma. They constantly awakened every world with the sound of the Dharma, their light shining everywhere across immeasurable buddha-lands. Every world shook in six ways at once; they wholly overcame the realm of Māra and shook Māra's own palace. Every host of demons trembled in fear, and none failed to submit and return. They tore apart the net of wrong views and dissolved every false opinion; they scattered the dust and toil of affliction and broke down every trench of desire. They guarded the city of the Dharma with vigilance, and threw open the gate of the Dharma. They washed away every stain and defilement, and made manifest what is pure and clear. Their light fused with the Buddha-dharma, and they spread abroad its true transforming influence. Entering towns to beg for alms, they received a bountiful meal; they stored up merit, and showed themselves as a field of blessing for others. Wishing to proclaim the Dharma, they showed a joyful smile; with every medicine of the Dharma they healed the three sufferings, and made manifest their immeasurable merit and their intention toward the path. They bestowed on bodhisattvas the prophecy of buddhahood, becoming themselves perfectly, completely awakened; they showed themselves entering final nirvāṇa, and yet went on rescuing beings without limit. They eliminated every outflow of affliction, and planted every root of virtue; their merit was so complete, so subtle and wondrous, that it could not be measured. They traveled through every buddha-land and everywhere made manifest the teaching of the path, and all that they practiced was pure and without stain. It is like a master of illusion, who conjures every kind of form, appearing as a man or a woman, changing into anything at all, because he has thoroughly mastered his craft and can do as he wills — these bodhisattvas were exactly the same. Having studied every teaching, and mastered it thoroughly, wherever they dwelled they brought about transformation, and there was nowhere they failed to reach; in countless buddha-lands they showed themselves everywhere, and never once, in arrogance, did they show contempt for or harm any being. All such qualities they possessed in full. The bodhisattva scriptures they had penetrated to their essential subtlety, and their renown reached everywhere, guiding the ten directions; immeasurable buddhas together kept them in mind and watched over them. Whatever state the buddhas abide in, they had already attained; whatever the great sages had established, they too had already established. Every guiding transformation of the Tathāgatas they were each able to proclaim abroad, and for all bodhisattvas they served as great teachers. With the deepest meditative wisdom they opened and guided all people; they penetrated the nature of every dharma and understood the true character of every being. They understood every land clearly, and made offerings to every buddha; the bodies they manifested were like flashes of lightning. Well trained in fearlessness, they thoroughly understood the illusory nature of all things; they tore apart the net of Māra and untied every fetter. They had gone beyond the stages of the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha, and had attained the samādhis of emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness. Skilled in establishing expedient means, they revealed the three vehicles, and in the middling and lower of these they showed themselves entering final extinction — and yet, in truth, there was nothing they did and nothing that they had, for they neither arose nor ceased, having attained the Dharma of true equality. They had fully accomplished immeasurable dhāraṇīs and hundreds of thousands of samādhis; their faculties and their wisdom were vast, universal, tranquil, and settled. They had entered deeply into the bodhisattvas' treasury of the Dharma, and attained the buddhas' Flower Adornment samādhi. They proclaimed and expounded every scripture, and abided in the gate of profound concentration, beholding all the immeasurable buddhas of the present in a single instant of thought, with nothing left unreached. They rescued beings from every extremity of hardship, whether at leisure or without it, and clearly distinguished and revealed the bounds of what is truly real. They had attained the wisdom and eloquence of every Tathāgata, and could enter into the speech of every kind of being, awakening every one. They had gone beyond every phenomenon the world possesses, and their mind constantly and truly abided in the path of saving the world. Amid all the myriad things, they were free and at ease exactly as they wished. For beings of every kind they became a friend unasked, taking up the heavy burden of every living thing on their behalf. They upheld the Tathāgatas' most profound treasury of the Dharma, protecting the buddha-lineage so that it would never be cut off. They raised up great compassion and had pity on all beings; they set forth compassionate eloquence and bestowed the Dharma-eye. They blocked off the three lower paths and opened the gate of goodness; with a Dharma that no one had asked for, they gave freely to the multitude — like a filial child who loves and reveres their parents, they looked on every being as they would look on themselves, and every root of goodness was carried by them to the farthest shore. They had gained the immeasurable merit of every buddha, and their wisdom, sagely and bright, was beyond conception. Bodhisattva-mahāsattvas such as these, beyond all counting, had all come together at once to this assembly.

Reading note — after Huiyuan

Bhadrapāla (賢護) heads a group of sixteen lay bodhisattvas well known elsewhere in the canon, most prominently in the Pratyutpanna Samādhi Sūtra; the text names only fourteen of the fifteen companions explicitly here, a minor discrepancy the tradition has not felt any need to resolve. Their inclusion matters for the same reason Huiyuan gives for the arhats in Section 1: an assembly this credentialed is itself part of how the sūtra establishes, before any doctrine is taught, that what follows deserves to be trusted.

The long passage on what these bodhisattvas can manifest is a description, compressed into a single sentence, of what tradition calls the eight aspects of a buddha's career (八相成道): descent from Tuṣita heaven, entry into the womb, birth, renunciation of the household life, the six years of austerity, the defeat of Māra, awakening beneath the bodhi tree, and turning the Dharma wheel — the same template, in outline, that later biographies of Śākyamuni himself follow in far greater detail. Huiyuan's point in reading it here is architectural rather than narrative: a bodhisattva at this level of attainment is not merely admirable in the abstract but is specifically shown to be capable of doing what a buddha does, appearing across countless worlds in the very shape of a buddha's life story — which is part of why an assembly like this one is fit to receive the teaching about to be given.

3. Ānanda Asks Why the Buddha's Face Shines So

Chinese Source

尒時世尊，諸根悅豫，姿色清淨，光顏巍巍。尊者阿難承佛聖旨，即從座起，偏袒右肩，長跪合掌，而白佛言：今日世尊，諸根悅豫，姿色清淨，光顏巍巍。如明鏡淨，影暢表裏。威容顯耀，超絕無量。未甞瞻覩殊妙如今。唯然大聖我心念言。今日世尊，住奇特之法。今日世雄，住諸佛所住。今日世眼，住導師之行。今日世英，住最勝之道。今日天尊，行如來之德。去來現在，佛佛相念。得無今佛念諸佛耶。何故威神光光乃尒？

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One's faculties were joyful and at ease, his countenance pure and clear, his radiant face towering and magnificent. The venerable Ānanda, sensing the Buddha's sacred intent, at once rose from his seat, bared his right shoulder, knelt with palms joined, and said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, today your faculties are joyful and at ease, your countenance pure and clear, your radiant face towering and magnificent — like a bright, unclouded mirror, its reflection reaching through to what lies within. Your majestic bearing shines forth, surpassing anything I have known; never before have I beheld such rare splendor as I see today. And yet, Greatly Sagely One, this thought has arisen in my mind: today the World-Honored One abides in a rare and extraordinary Dharma. Today the Hero of the World abides in that in which all buddhas abide. Today the Eye of the World abides in the conduct of a guide. Today the Finest of the World abides in the most supreme of paths. Today the Honored One of the Heavens practices the very virtue of a Tathāgata. Buddhas of the past, the future, and the present are ever mindful of one another — can it be that the Buddha, even now, is calling to mind the other buddhas? Why else would this majestic radiance shine so?"

Reading note — after Huiyuan

Notice what has not happened yet: no one has asked the Buddha to teach. Ānanda's question is about the Buddha's own face, not about doctrine — closer in shape to the Amitābha Sūtra's teaching given entirely unprompted than to the Diamond Sūtra's dialogue, which opens with Subhūti's direct request. Huiyuan reads the specificity of Ānanda's five-fold description — hero, eye, finest, honored one, each phrase circling the same observation from a different angle — as the sūtra's own way of marking this radiance as unlike anything in the Buddha's ordinary bearing, worth an entire question in its own right before a single word of the actual teaching is spoken.

4. Why the Tathāgata Appears in the World

Chinese Source

於是世尊告阿難曰：云何阿難，諸天教汝來問佛耶？自以慧見問威顏乎？阿難白佛：無有諸天來教我者，自以所見問斯義耳。佛言：善哉，阿難。所問甚快。發深智慧真妙辯才。愍念眾生，問斯慧義。如來以無盡大悲矜哀三界。所以出興於世，光闡道教，普令群萌，獲真法利。無量億劫難值難見。猶靈瑞華時時乃出。今所問者多所饒益。開化一切諸天人民。阿難，當知如來正覺，其智難量，多所導御。慧見無礙，無能遏絕。以一餐之力，能住壽命，億百千劫無數無量。復過於此。諸根悅豫不以毀損，姿色不變光顏無異。所以者何？如來定慧，究暢無極。於一切法而得自在。阿難諦聽。今為汝說。對曰：唯然。願樂欲聞。

Ru-Yi Translation

Then the World-Honored One said to Ānanda: "Why is this, Ānanda — did some deva teach you to come and question the Buddha? Or did you ask about this majestic countenance from your own wise insight?" Ānanda said to the Buddha: "No deva came to teach me this; I asked about what I myself observed, for its own meaning." The Buddha said: "Well said, Ānanda — this is a very fine question indeed. It arises from a deep wisdom and a true and wondrous eloquence, and from a mind mindful of, and moved by compassion for, all beings, that you should ask about this meaning. The Tathāgata, out of a boundless great compassion, has pity on the three realms; this is why he appears in the world, illuminating and setting forth the teaching of the path, so as to grant every living being the true benefit of the Dharma. It is hard, across immeasurable koṭis of aeons, to meet with this or to see it — like the miraculous udumbara flower, which blooms only once in a very long while. What you have asked today will bring great benefit, and will open and transform every deva and every human being. Ānanda, you should know that the wisdom of the Tathāgata's perfect awakening is hard to measure, and guides and directs a great many; his insight is unobstructed and cannot be checked or exhausted. By the strength of a single meal he is able to sustain his life for a hundred thousand koṭis of aeons, immeasurable and beyond number, and even longer still than this; his faculties remain joyful and undiminished, his countenance unchanged, his radiant face no different. And why is this? Because the Tathāgata's concentration and wisdom have reached their utmost, complete and without limit, and he has attained mastery over every dharma. Ānanda, listen carefully — I shall now explain this for you." Ānanda replied: "Yes indeed, World-Honored One — we would gladly hear it."

Reading note — after Huiyuan

The Buddha's own answer does something easy to read past: before saying one word about Amitābha or Sukhāvatī, he answers the question "why does a Tathāgata appear in the world at all" in the most general possible terms — boundless compassion for the three realms, meeting beings rarely and briefly, like the udumbara flower that blooms only once in a long while. Huiyuan takes this as the sūtra's own frame for everything that follows: the teaching about to be given is offered as one instance of that same rare, compassionate appearing, not a separate topic. The specific claim — that the Buddha's radiance today signals he has been dwelling in the buddhas' own dwelling, contemplating other buddhas — is confirmed only implicitly here; the text answers by explaining why any Buddha teaches at all, and lets the specific cause emerge only in what follows, in the story of Dharmākara.

Worth noting for readers new to this sūtra: unlike the Amitābha Sūtra, which opens directly with Śākyamuni's own description of Sukhāvatī, this sūtra takes a considerably longer run-up — Ānanda's question, the Buddha's general answer, and then, before Amitābha is named even once, the entire story of how a king named Dharmākara resolved to become that buddha in the first place.
