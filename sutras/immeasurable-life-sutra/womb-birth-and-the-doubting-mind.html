Womb-Birth and the Doubting Mind

胎生化生

Ānanda beholds Amitāyus Buddha with his own eyes. Then Maitreya asks the question this whole sūtra has been building toward: why do some who are reborn in Sukhāvatī arrive instantly, and others spend five hundred years in a palace lacking nothing — except the Three Jewels.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T360 · Saṃghavarman's translation, 3rd century CE Commentary guide: Jingying Huiyuan's 無量壽經義疏 (6th c.)
This part covers
Ānanda's direct vision of Amitāyus Buddha; Maitreya's question about womb-birth and transformation-birth; the Buddha's explanation that doubt about the scope of a buddha's wisdom, not absence of faith, is the actual cause; the parable of princes imprisoned in a golden, comfortable cage.
A caution
This is one of the sūtra's most easily misread passages. "Womb-birth" here does not describe punishment, and is not eternal — it is a temporary, materially comfortable, self-resolving state, and this reading guide flags where a casual reading could mistake it for something harsher than the text actually says.

45. Ānanda Beholds Amitāyus Buddha

Chinese Source

佛告阿難：汝起更整衣服合掌恭敬。禮無量壽佛。十方國土諸佛如來。常共稱揚讚歎彼佛無著無礙。於是阿難起整衣服。正身西面。恭敬合掌，五體投地。禮無量壽佛。白言世尊。願見彼佛安樂國土及諸菩薩聲聞大眾。說是語已。即時無量壽佛。放大光明。普照一切諸佛世界。金剛圍山、須彌山王、大小諸山，一切所有皆同一色。譬如劫水彌滿世界。其中萬物沉没不現。滉瀁浩汗，唯見大水。彼佛光明亦復如是。聲聞菩薩一切光明皆悉隱蔽。唯見佛光明耀顯赫。爾時阿難即見無量壽佛。威德巍巍，如須彌山王。髙出一切諸世界上。相好光明，靡不照耀。此會四眾一時悉見。彼見此土亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "Rise, and once more adjust your robe, and with palms joined in reverence, pay homage to Amitāyus Buddha. The buddhas and Tathāgatas of the lands of the ten directions constantly praise and proclaim together that buddha's freedom from attachment and from all obstruction." Thereupon Ānanda rose and adjusted his robe, turned his body to face directly west, and, with palms joined in reverence, threw his five limbs to the ground and paid homage to Amitāyus Buddha, saying: "World-Honored One, I wish to see that buddha's land of Peace and Bliss, and his assembled bodhisattvas and śrāvakas." No sooner had he spoken these words than Amitāyus Buddha sent forth a great light, illumining every buddha-world entirely. The ring of vajra mountains, the king of mountains Sumeru, and every mountain great and small, all became a single color, exactly as when the waters of a cosmic dissolution fill an entire world, and every one of the myriad things within it sinks beneath the surface and disappears from sight, vast and boundless, with nothing to be seen but water itself. That buddha's light was just the same: every light belonging to his śrāvakas and bodhisattvas was entirely hidden from view, and only the Buddha's own light could be seen, blazing and resplendent. At that moment Ānanda beheld Amitāyus Buddha, his majestic virtue towering, like the king of mountains Sumeru, rising high above every world there is, his marks and his light shining forth on every side. And the fourfold assembly gathered there beheld it all at once, together, and those in that land beheld this world of ours in just the same way.

Reading note — after Huiyuan

The closing detail is easy to read past: not only does this assembly see Sukhāvatī, but "those in that land beheld this world of ours in just the same way." Huiyuan reads this brief, almost throwaway line as significant — the vision is mutual, not one-directional. This matches a detail already established in Part 4 of this translation, that Sukhāvatī has no Mount Sumeru of its own; here, at the moment of greatest visual intensity in the whole sūtra, even this world's own Sumeru dissolves from view, submerged beneath Amitāyus's light exactly as "the myriad things" of a world are submerged when a great kalpa-ending flood arises — a stock image for the dissolution of a world-age, borrowed here to describe not destruction but simply the sheer intensity of one buddha's own radiance.

46. Womb-Birth Explained

Chinese Source

爾時佛告阿難及慈氏菩薩：汝見彼國。從地已上至淨居天。其中所有微妙嚴淨。自然之物為悉見不。阿難對曰。唯然已見。汝寧復聞無量壽佛大音宣布一切世界化眾生不。阿難對曰。唯然已聞。彼國人民。乘百千由旬七寶宮殿無所障礙。遍至十方供養諸佛。汝復見不。對曰已見。彼國人民有胎生者。汝復見不。對曰已見。其胎生者所處宮殿。或百由旬或五百由旬。各於其中受諸快樂。如忉利天上，亦皆自然。爾時慈氏菩薩白佛言。世尊。何因何緣。彼國人民胎生化生。佛告慈氏。若有眾生。以疑惑心修諸功德。願生彼國。不了佛智、不思議智、不可稱智、大乘廣智、無等無倫最上勝智，於此諸智疑惑不信。然猶信罪福，修習善本，願生其國。此諸眾生生彼宮殿。壽五百歲。常不見佛不聞經法。不見菩薩聲聞聖眾。是故於彼國土，謂之胎生。若有眾生。明信佛智乃至勝智。作諸功德信心迴向。此諸眾生於七寶華中自然化生。加趺而坐。須臾之頃。身相、光明、智慧、功德，如諸菩薩具足成就。復次慈氏。他方諸大菩薩。發心欲見無量壽佛。恭敬供養及諸菩薩聲聞之眾。彼菩薩等。命終得生無量壽國。於七寶華中自然化生。彌勒當知。彼化生者智慧勝故。其胎生者皆無智慧。於五百歲中。常不見佛不聞經法。不見菩薩諸聲聞眾。無由供養於佛。不知菩薩法式。不得修習功德。當知此人。宿世之時。無有智慧疑惑所致。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the Buddha said to Ānanda and to the bodhisattva Maitreya: "Have you seen that land, from the ground up to the Pure Abodes heaven, and all the subtle, wondrous, adorned, and pure things that arise there spontaneously — have you seen all of this?" Ānanda answered: "Yes indeed, I have seen it." "And have you likewise heard Amitāyus Buddha's great voice, proclaimed throughout every world, transforming beings there?" Ānanda answered: "Yes indeed, I have heard it." "The people of that land ride in palaces of the seven treasures, a hundred thousand yojanas across, unobstructed by anything, traveling to the ten directions to make offerings to the buddhas — have you seen this as well?" He answered: "I have seen it." "Among the people of that land there are those who are womb-born — have you seen this too?" He answered: "I have seen it." "Those who are womb-born dwell in palaces there, some a hundred yojanas across, some five hundred, and each of them experiences every kind of joy within them, just as in the Trāyastriṃśa heaven, all of it arising spontaneously." At that time the bodhisattva Maitreya said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, for what cause and what reason are the people of that land born some by womb and some by transformation?" The Buddha told Maitreya: "If there are beings who, with a mind of doubt and uncertainty, cultivate every kind of merit and vow to be born in that land, yet do not understand the Buddha's wisdom — his inconceivable wisdom, his indescribable wisdom, his vast wisdom of the Great Vehicle, his unequaled, unrivaled, most supreme wisdom — and toward these kinds of wisdom hold doubt and do not believe, and yet still believe in the working of wrongdoing and merit, and cultivate roots of goodness, and vow to be born in that land — such beings are born within those palaces, and there live for five hundred years, never once seeing the Buddha, never hearing the teaching of the scriptures, and never seeing the assembled bodhisattvas and śrāvaka sages. This is why, in that land, this is called womb-birth. But if there are beings who clearly believe in the Buddha's wisdom, up to his most supreme wisdom, and perform every kind of merit, dedicating it with a believing mind — such beings come spontaneously into being within a flower of the seven treasures, seated there cross-legged, and in the space of a single moment, their bodily form, their light, their wisdom, and their merit are all fully accomplished, complete just as any bodhisattva's. And furthermore, Maitreya, when the great bodhisattvas of other lands give rise to the resolve to see Amitāyus Buddha, and to pay homage and make offerings to him and to his assembled bodhisattvas and śrāvakas, these bodhisattvas, when their lives there come to an end, attain rebirth in Amitāyus's land, coming spontaneously into being within a flower of the seven treasures. Maitreya, you should know: those born by transformation do so because their wisdom is superior; those born by the womb are, all of them, without this wisdom, and throughout those five hundred years never once see the Buddha, never hear the teaching of the scriptures, never see the assembled bodhisattvas and śrāvakas, have no means to make offerings to the Buddha, do not know the proper conduct of a bodhisattva, and cannot cultivate any merit at all. You should know that this comes, for such a person, entirely from a lack of wisdom in a former life, and from the doubt that lack of wisdom produced."

Reading note — after Huiyuan

This is worth reading slowly, because the surface vocabulary — "womb-birth," a five-hundred-year confinement — invites exactly the misreading the text itself works to prevent. Huiyuan's summary distinction is precise: what separates womb-birth from instant transformation-birth is not the presence or absence of faith in Amitābha as such. The beings described here do believe in "the working of wrongdoing and merit," do cultivate roots of goodness, and do sincerely vow to be born in that land — this is not the profile of a skeptic or an unbeliever. What they lack is confidence specifically in the scope of a buddha's wisdom: an unstated, half-conscious sense that surely there must be some limit to what even a buddha can know or do, some corner of reality too vast even for that. It is this particular, narrower doubt — not disbelief in Amitābha, not moral failure — that produces the delay.

Equally worth being precise about: this state is not punishment and not permanent. There is, the text says explicitly in the next section, no penalty of any kind imposed there, and the confinement lasts a fixed five hundred years, ending automatically, or ending sooner still the moment the being recognizes the doubt for what it was. Reading this section against the "borderland" warning already given in Part 8 — being born into a jeweled palace for five hundred years as a consequence of wavering resolve — this is that same fate, now explained from the inside: a real deprivation, but a narrowly defined and temporary one, imposed by the being's own residual doubt rather than by Amitāyus withholding anything.

47. The Princes in the Golden Cage

Chinese Source

佛告彌勒：譬如轉輪聖王，別有宮室，七寶莊飾。張設床帳，懸諸繒幡。若有諸小王子。得罪於王，輒內彼宮中。繫以金鎖。供給、飲食、衣服、床蓐、華香、伎樂，如轉輪王，無所乏少。於意云何。此諸王子寧樂彼處不？對曰：不也。但種種方便。求諸大力欲自勉出。佛告彌勒：此諸眾生亦復如是。以疑惑佛智故生彼宮殿。無有刑罰，乃至一念惡事。但於五百歲中，不見三寶。不得供養，修諸善本。以此為苦。雖有餘樂，猶不樂彼處。若此眾生識其本罪。深自悔責，求離彼處。即得如意。往詣無量壽佛所。恭敬供養。亦得遍至無量無數諸如來所。修諸功德。彌勒當知。其有菩薩生疑惑者，為失大利。是故應當明信諸佛無上智慧。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha told Maitreya: "It is like a wheel-turning sage-king who has a separate palace, adorned with the seven treasures, hung with curtained beds and silk banners. If any of the lesser princes offend the king, they are placed within that palace and bound with golden chains; yet they are provided with food and drink, clothing, bedding, flowers, incense, and music, just as the wheel-turning king himself has, lacking nothing at all. What do you think — would these princes take any pleasure in that place?" He answered: "No, they would not. They would use every kind of means available to seek out someone of great power, wishing to secure their own release." The Buddha told Maitreya: "These beings are exactly the same. Because they doubted the Buddha's wisdom, they are born within those palaces — there is no punishment there, not even the least evil thought — but for those five hundred years they do not see the Three Jewels, and cannot make offerings or cultivate any root of goodness, and this alone they experience as suffering; and though every other pleasure is present, they still take no joy in that place. If such beings come to recognize their own original fault, and deeply and sincerely repent of it, seeking to leave that place, they at once obtain their wish, and go to where Amitāyus Buddha is, to pay homage and make offerings to him, and are also able to travel to immeasurable, countless Tathāgatas everywhere, cultivating every kind of merit. Maitreya, you should know: any bodhisattva who gives rise to doubt thereby loses a great benefit. For this reason, one should hold a clear and settled faith in the buddhas' unsurpassed wisdom."

Reading note — after Huiyuan

The parable makes explicit what the previous section only implied: "there is no punishment there, not even the least evil thought" — golden chains that are not really chains, a palace lacking nothing material at all. Huiyuan reads the parable's real precision in what it identifies as the actual suffering: not confinement itself, and not any lack of comfort, but exclusion specifically from the Three Jewels — from seeing a buddha, hearing the Dharma, and encountering the community of practitioners. Every other pleasure is present and genuinely fails to compensate for that one absence, which is itself the sūtra's own claim about what a practitioner is ultimately meant to want.

The resolution offered is worth underlining, since it is easy to miss in a passage this vivid: recognition and sincere regret are sufficient, on their own, to end the confinement immediately, without waiting out the full five hundred years. Nothing here describes an irreversible outcome. Doubt delays; it does not disqualify — a claim consistent with the exclusion clause attached to the Eighteenth Vow back in Part 3, which withheld rebirth altogether only from the five heinous transgressions and slander of the Dharma, a far narrower category than ordinary doubt.
