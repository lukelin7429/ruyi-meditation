Assured, and the Three Grades of Rebirth

正定聚・三輩往生

The second fascicle opens with a guarantee: anyone born in Sukhāvatī is assured of never falling back. Then three grades of rebirth — for the renunciant, the ordinary householder, and the barely capable — followed by a vision in which the Buddha smiles, and Avalokiteśvara is the one who asks why.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T360 · Saṃghavarman's translation, 3rd century CE Commentary guide: Jingying Huiyuan's 無量壽經義疏 (6th c.)
This part covers
The assurance of non-retrogression for everyone born in Sukhāvatī; the three grades of rebirth — upper, middle, and lower; a verse in which the Buddha's smile prompts Avalokiteśvara's question, and a prophecy is given to bodhisattvas of the ten directions.

28. Assured, Never to Fall Back

Chinese Source

佛告阿難：其有眾生生彼國者。皆悉住於正定之聚。所以者何。彼佛國中無諸邪聚及不定聚。十方恒沙諸佛如來。皆共讚歎無量壽佛威神功德不可思議。諸有眾生聞其名號。信心歡喜乃至一念。至心迴向願生彼國。即得往生住不退轉。唯除五逆誹謗正法。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha told Ānanda: "Any being born in that land abides, every one of them, in the assembly of the rightly settled. And why is this? Because that buddha's land contains no assembly of the wrongly settled, and no assembly of the undetermined. The buddhas and Tathāgatas of the ten directions, as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, together praise the inconceivable, majestic merit of Amitāyus Buddha. Any being who hears his name, and with a believing mind rejoices, even for a single thought, and with a sincere mind dedicates that merit, wishing to be born in that land, at once attains rebirth there and abides in non-retrogression — excepting only those who commit the five heinous transgressions or slander the true Dharma."

Reading note — after Huiyuan

Huiyuan reads the "three assemblies" named here — the rightly settled, the wrongly settled, and the undetermined — as a standard classification, used elsewhere in Buddhist doctrine, of beings by whether their karmic trajectory is fixed toward awakening, fixed away from it, or not yet decided either way. His stated point about this land is direct: it contains only the first category. Whatever level of realization a being arrives with, the land itself contains no wrongly settled or undetermined company to be drawn off course by — the assurance of non-retrogression promised in earlier vows is, on his reading, not a personal achievement each being must separately earn after arrival, but a structural feature of who else is there.

The exclusion clause closing this passage — the same one attached to the Eighteenth Vow in Part 3 — repeats verbatim here, and the tension flagged there is worth carrying forward rather than resolving twice: this section promises rebirth to "any being" who hears the name with faith, immediately followed by the same narrow exception. The next section, describing the three grades open to renunciant and layperson alike, keeps this same structure — broad promise, narrow exception — running through it as well.

29. Three Grades of Rebirth

Chinese Source · The Upper Grade

佛告阿難：十方世界諸天人民。其有至心願生彼國。凡有三輩。其上輩者。捨家棄欲而作沙門。發菩提心。一向專念無量壽佛。修諸功德願生彼國。此等眾生臨壽終時。無量壽佛與諸大眾。現其人前。即隨彼佛往生其國。便於七寶華中自然化生。住不退轉。智慧勇猛神通自在。是故阿難。其有眾生。欲於今世見無量壽佛。應發無上菩提之心。修行功德。願生彼國。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha told Ānanda: "Among the gods and people of the worlds of the ten directions, those who, with a sincere mind, wish to be born in that land fall into three grades in all. Those of the upper grade leave their households, give up worldly desire, and become renunciants; they give rise to the mind of awakening, single-mindedly hold Amitāyus Buddha in mind, cultivate every merit, and vow to be born in that land. When the lives of such beings come to their end, Amitāyus Buddha, together with a great assembly, appears before them, and they at once follow that buddha and are born in his land, coming spontaneously into being within a flower of the seven treasures, abiding in non-retrogression, their wisdom courageous and their spiritual powers free and unobstructed. This is why, Ānanda, any being who wishes to behold Amitāyus Buddha in this very life should give rise to the mind of unsurpassed awakening, cultivate merit, and vow to be born in that land."

Chinese Source · The Middle Grade

佛語阿難：其中輩者。十方世界諸天人民。其有至心願生彼國。雖不能行作沙門大修功德。當發無上菩提之心。一向專念無量壽佛。多少修善。奉持齋戒。起立塔像。飯食沙門。懸繒然燈。散華燒香。以此迴向願生彼國。其人臨終。無量壽佛。化現其身。光明相好具如真佛。與諸大眾現其人前。即隨化佛往生其國。住不退轉。功德智慧次如上輩者也。

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "Those of the middle grade — among the gods and people of the worlds of the ten directions who, with a sincere mind, wish to be born in that land, though unable to become renunciants and cultivate merit on a great scale, should still give rise to the mind of unsurpassed awakening, and single-mindedly hold Amitāyus Buddha in mind; cultivating goodness to whatever degree they are able, upholding fasts and precepts, raising up stūpas and images, feeding renunciant monks, hanging silk banners and lighting lamps, scattering flowers and burning incense, and dedicating this, vowing to be born in that land. When such a person's life comes to its end, Amitāyus Buddha manifests a transformation-body, its light and marks fully like the true buddha, and appears before them together with a great assembly; and they at once follow that transformed buddha and are born in his land, abiding in non-retrogression, their merit and wisdom next in rank after those of the upper grade."

Chinese Source · The Lower Grade

佛語阿難：其下輩者。十方世界諸天人民。其有至心欲生彼國。假使不能作諸功德。當發無上菩提之心。一向專意乃至十念。念無量壽佛願生其國。若聞深法歡喜信樂不生疑惑。乃至一念念於彼佛。以至誠心願生其國。此人臨終。夢見彼佛亦得往生。功德智慧次如中輩者也。

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "Those of the lower grade — among the gods and people of the worlds of the ten directions who, with a sincere mind, wish to be born in that land, even if they are unable to perform any great merit at all, should still give rise to the mind of unsurpassed awakening, and with single, undivided intent, call Amitāyus Buddha to mind even as few as ten times, vowing to be born in his land. If they hear this profound teaching and rejoice in it with faith, giving rise to no doubt, and call that buddha to mind even for a single thought, with an utterly sincere mind vowing to be born in his land — then, when such a person's life comes to its end, they see that buddha in a dream, and likewise attain rebirth there, their merit and wisdom next in rank after those of the middle grade."

Reading note — after Huiyuan

Huiyuan's own summary of this whole passage is worth giving close to his own words: though the three grades differ in position, all three equally require giving rise to the mind that seeks great awakening (三輩人位雖殊，至欲往生，齊須發心求大菩提). What separates the grades is not whether the mind of awakening is present — every grade requires it — but the scale of cultivation carried out afterward: full renunciation for the upper grade, ordinary lay devotion for the middle, and, for the lower grade, as little as ten sincere recollections of the name for someone who can manage nothing more elaborate. Read this way, the three grades are less a hierarchy separating who gets in from who does not, and more a description of how far into the land's more advanced attainments a given practitioner arrives — a distinction of degree, not of admission.

Worth flagging for readers who already know Pure Land art and liturgy: the far more elaborate ninefold grading of rebirth — upper, middle, and lower each further divided into upper, middle, and lower — comes from a different, later scripture, the Contemplation Sūtra (觀無量壽經), not from this text. This sūtra's own scheme is only threefold; the ninefold scheme is a later development that took this passage as its starting point and subdivided it further, not something already present in Saṃghavarman's translation.

30. The Buddha Smiles, and Avalokiteśvara Asks Why

Chinese Source

佛告阿難：無量壽佛威神無極。十方世界無量無邊不可思議諸佛如來。莫不稱歎於彼東方恒河沙佛國。無量無數諸菩薩眾。皆悉往詣無量壽佛所。恭敬供養及諸菩薩聲聞大眾。聽受經法宣布道化。南西北方四維上下亦復如是。爾時世尊而說頌曰。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha told Ānanda: "Amitāyus Buddha's majestic power is without limit. The immeasurable, boundless, inconceivable buddhas and Tathāgatas of the worlds of the ten directions are all, without exception, praising him. In those buddha-lands to the east, as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, immeasurable, countless bodhisattvas all go to where Amitāyus Buddha is, to pay homage and make offerings to him and to his assembled bodhisattvas and śrāvakas, and to listen to and receive the teaching of the Dharma and its proclamation of the way — and it is the same in the south, the west, the north, the four intermediate directions, above and below." At that time the World-Honored One spoke this verse:

Ru-Yi Translation

The buddha-lands of the east are as numerous as the sands of the Ganges; the bodhisattvas of those lands go to pay homage to the Buddha of Immeasurable Awakening. South, west, north, and the four intermediate directions, above and below, it is just the same; the bodhisattvas of those lands go to pay homage to the Buddha of Immeasurable Awakening. Every one of these bodhisattvas carries wondrous heavenly flowers, precious incense, and robes beyond price, to make offering to the Buddha of Immeasurable Awakening. Together they play heavenly music, sending out its harmonious, elegant sound, singing in praise of the most supreme, honored one, making offering to the Buddha of Immeasurable Awakening. Having fully mastered spiritual wisdom, they enter the profound gate of the Dharma; fully endowed with the treasury of merit, their wondrous wisdom has no equal. Their sun of wisdom illumines the world, dispelling the clouds of birth and death; reverently they circle him three times, and bow their heads to the unsurpassed, honored one. Beholding that adorned and pure land, subtle and wondrous, beyond conception, they thereby give rise to an immeasurable mind: "may my own land be just the same." At that moment the Immeasurable, Honored One stirs, and his countenance breaks into a smile; from his mouth come forth countless rays of light, shining fully upon the lands of the ten directions. That returning light circles his own body, three times around, and enters through the crown of his head; every god and human in the assembly leaps up, every one, in joy. The great being Avalokiteśvara adjusts his robe, bows his head, and asks: "For what reason does the Buddha smile? We ask only that you tell us its meaning." His Brahma-voice, like rolling thunder, resonating with the eight qualities of sound, speaks: "I am about to bestow a prophecy on the bodhisattvas — listen now, with care, to what I say. The true and noble ones who have come from the ten directions — I know every one of their vows; they seek, with resolve, an adorned and pure land, and will receive the prophecy: they shall become buddhas. Having awakened to the truth that every dharma is like a dream, an illusion, an echo, they shall fully satisfy every wondrous vow, and will surely accomplish such a land as this. Knowing that every dharma is like lightning, like a shadow, they shall bring the bodhisattva path to its very end; fully endowed with every root of merit, they will receive the prophecy: they shall become buddhas. Having penetrated every gate of the Dharma, knowing all things are empty and without self, single-mindedly seeking a pure buddha-land, they will surely accomplish such a land as this." The buddhas tell these bodhisattvas to go and pay homage to the Buddha of Peace and Bliss; hearing the Dharma, they joyfully receive and practice it, and swiftly attain to that pure place. On reaching that adorned and pure land, they at once and quickly gain spiritual powers, and before that Immeasurable, Honored One are certain to receive the prophecy of perfect awakening. By the power of that buddha's original vow, anyone who hears his name and wishes to be born there reaches that land, every one of them, and of themselves attains non-retrogression. Bodhisattvas who arouse this same resolve, wishing their own land to be no different, mindful everywhere of delivering all beings, their renown reaching and spreading through the ten directions, serve a hundred million Tathāgatas, flying in transformation through every land, and, having paid homage, depart in joy, returning again to the land of Peace and Bliss. If a person has no root of goodness, they cannot come to hear this sūtra; only one who is pure and holds the precepts comes to obtain the true Dharma. One who has, in a former time, beheld a World-Honored One is thereby able to believe this matter; humble and reverent, hearing it, they uphold and practice it, leaping up in the greatest of joy. The arrogant, the corrupt, and the indolent find it hard to believe this teaching; but one who has, in a past life, beheld the buddhas delights in hearing a teaching such as this. Neither the śrāvaka nor the bodhisattva can fully fathom the sage's mind — it is like someone born blind wishing to go and guide another. The ocean of the Tathāgata's wisdom is deep and vast, without any shore; the two vehicles cannot take its measure — only a buddha alone understands it fully. Suppose every person there is attained the path in full, and with a wisdom pure as its own original emptiness pondered, for a hundred million aeons, a buddha's wisdom, exhausting every strength, teaching to the utmost limit, to the very end of their lives they still could not know it. A buddha's wisdom has no boundary or limit, and it is by just such wisdom that this purity is brought about. A human life is extremely hard to obtain, and a buddha's presence in the world is likewise hard to meet; that a person should have faith and wisdom is hard too — if you hear of it, seek it with diligence. To hear the teaching and not forget it, to see it and revere it and gain great joy — such a one is my own good friend; for this reason, you should give rise to this resolve. Even were the whole world to fill with fire, you should still pass through it to hear this teaching, and will in time surely accomplish the path to buddhahood, delivering broadly all who are swept in the stream of birth and death.

Reading note — after Huiyuan

This is the first point in the sūtra where an individual bodhisattva other than Maitreya or the sixteen lay bodhisattvas of Part 1 is named and given a speaking role — Avalokiteśvara, adjusting his robe and asking, on behalf of the whole assembly, why the Buddha has smiled. A buddha's smile followed by a request to explain it, followed by a prophecy of future buddhahood for those present, is a recognizable narrative device used elsewhere in Mahāyāna literature, most famously in the Lotus Sūtra; Huiyuan reads its appearance here as this sūtra borrowing a shared convention for marking a moment of unusually high import, rather than as something unique to this text. The verse itself is careful not to prophesy buddhahood to any specific, named individual — it addresses "the true and noble ones who have come from the ten directions" collectively, holding out the promise without attaching it to particular names.

Two similes late in the verse are worth sitting with on their own: wisdom compared to someone born blind trying to guide another, and a buddha's wisdom as an ocean without any shore that even a bodhisattva or śrāvaka cannot fully fathom. Huiyuan reads both as a check against a specific misreading of everything that has come before — the extraordinary, precisely enumerated detail of this sūtra (heights in li, widths in yojanas, numbers of petals) could tempt a reader into thinking the land has now been fully specified and understood. These similes insist otherwise: even the most advanced beings in the text cannot exhaust what a buddha's wisdom actually is, and the sūtra's own precision, however extensive, does not close that gap.
