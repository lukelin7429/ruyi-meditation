# Mohe Zhiguan Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Mohe Zhiguan* (摩訶止觀, "The Great Calming and Contemplation," Taishō T46, no. 1911). They exist so that terminology stays consistent across all ten fascicles, the way the running glossary already works for [`sutras/shi-chan-boluomi/translation-notes.md`](../shi-chan-boluomi/translation-notes.md) and this site's other translation projects.

## The text and its author

Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597 CE), posthumously honored as the Great Master of Tiantai, is the founder of the Tiantai school and one of the most important systematizers in Chinese Buddhist history. The *Mohe Zhiguan* is the last, longest, and most difficult of his three major meditation treatises — delivered as oral lectures at Yuquan Monastery (玉泉寺) in Jingzhou in 594 CE and recorded directly by his disciple Guanding (灌頂), unlike Zhiyi's other treatises, which were recorded by one disciple and later revised by a second. The lecture series itself was cut short before its own announced ten-section outline could be completed, and the received text is traditionally considered unfinished for this reason. It runs to ten fascicles in the Taishō edition.

## Source and copyright

- Base text: Taishō T1911, via the CBETA digital edition. Zhiyi died in 597 CE — the text itself has been in the public domain for well over a millennium, under any jurisdiction's copyright term. CBETA's own transcription is distributed under a non-commercial-use license, consistent with how this non-profit site already uses CBETA-sourced text across every other translation project here.
- This treatise has an existing scholarly English translation of its first chapter: Neal Donner and Daniel B. Stevenson, *The Great Calming and Contemplation: A Study and Annotated Translation of the First Chapter of Chih-i's Mo-ho chih-kuan* (University of Hawaii Press, 1993). Because that translation already exists and is well known, we take particular care to translate independently from the Chinese source and not to consult, echo, or fall into the phrasing of that published translation — the same principle already followed for Bhikshu Dharmamitra's (Kalavinka Press) translations of Zhiyi's other, shorter meditation manuals, and for this site's translation of the *Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen*.

## Structure

- CBETA's edition of fascicle 1 splits it into "upper" and "lower" halves (之上 / 之下) for print-volume reasons; both halves are treated as one continuous fascicle on this site, matching the existing convention for the *Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen*.
- Zhiyi structures the entire treatise around ten framing sections (十章), named at the opening of fascicle 1: (1) the great intent, (2) explaining the name, (3) the essence and its marks, (4) the scope of the teachings it gathers in, (5) its relation to partial and complete teachings, (6) the preliminary expedients — including the famous twenty-five preliminary conditions and the ten objects of contemplation — (7) the actual practice, (8) fruits and rewards, (9) the teachings that arise from it, and (10) where it all ultimately leads.
- Confirmed from the source text: fascicle 1 (both halves) contains only topic 1, the great intent (大意), and even then completes only the first of that topic's own five brief points — giving rise to the great mind (發大心). The remaining four brief points, and topics 2 through 10 of the treatise as a whole, belong to fascicle 2 and beyond.
- The great intent's own "giving rise to the mind" section is itself organized into three parts — terminology (方言), rejecting what is wrong (簡非), and revealing what is right (顯是) — and the third of these, in turn, into four separate approaches: the Four Noble Truths, beholding the Buddha and hearing the Dharma, the four universal vows, and the six identities. Fascicle 1 covers all four in full.

| 五略：發大心、修大行、感大果、裂大網、歸大處 | the five brief points: giving rise to the great mind, cultivating great practice, evoking a great fruit, tearing apart the great net, and returning to the great abode | Topic 1's own five-part outline. Fascicle 1 completes only the first. |
| 發菩提心 / 菩提心 | to give rise to the bodhi-mind / the bodhi-mind | Bodhicitta. |
| 方言 / 簡非 / 顯是 | terminology / rejecting what is wrong / revealing what is right | The three-part structure of "giving rise to the mind" itself. |
| 十種發心（簡非） | ten deviant minds | Ten wrong motivations for practice, mapped onto the ten dharma-realms (hell, animal, ghost, asura, human, heaven, Māra, non-Buddhist paths, form-and-formless realm, the two lesser vehicles) — compare the different but overlapping list opening topic 1 of the *Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen*. |
| 十法界 | the ten dharma-realms | The destination each of the ten deviant minds is said to lead toward. |
| 感應道交 | mutual resonance between response and appeal | The treatise's answer to whether the bodhi-mind arises from oneself or from a teacher: neither alone, but the meeting of both, like a parent rushing to a drowning child. |
| 四悉檀 | the four siddhāntas | Nāgārjuna's framework for why the Buddha teaches differently depending on audience: the worldly, for the individual, the antidote, and ultimate meaning — matched in fascicle 1 to several other fourfold and fivefold pedagogical schemes (四隨, 五復次, 五因緣) as restatements of the same underlying principle. |
| 四種四諦：生滅 / 無生 / 無量 / 無作 | the four kinds of Four Noble Truths: generation-and-extinction / no generation / the immeasurable / the unconditioned | One of the treatise's most consequential passages, later systematized into Tiantai's doctrine of the Four Teachings; source is the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's chapter on noble conduct. |
| 十二因緣 | the twelve links of dependent origination | Correlated in fascicle 1 with the four kinds of Four Noble Truths, each itself opened into four kinds to match. |
| 應佛 / 勝應（身） / 報佛 / 法佛 | the response-body buddha / the superior response-body / the reward-body buddha / the Dharma-body buddha | A fourfold buddha-body scheme (finer-grained than the standard trikāya) used in fascicle 1's account of "beholding the Buddha's marks and giving rise to the mind," each read with increasing subtlety. |
| 三世止觀 | the three kinds of calming-and-contemplation | See above; recurs as the closing organizing scheme for the whole discussion of the four kinds of Four Noble Truths. |
| 化城 / 寶所 | the phantom city / the treasure-place | From the Lotus Sūtra's parable of the guide who conjures an illusory resting-place; used repeatedly in fascicle 1 to distinguish a provisional, Hīnayāna-adjacent goal from the real, ultimate one. |
| 四弘誓願 | the four universal vows | The bodhisattva's four vows, approached in fascicle 1 through three successive passes on a single arising thought: empty, provisional, and empty-provisional-and-middle at once. |
| 一念心 | a single thought of mind | The basic unit of analysis for fascicle 1's most technical passage — faculty and object meeting, a thought arising, examined as empty, then provisional, then all three truths simultaneously; anticipates what later Tiantai scholasticism would name "a single thought contains the three thousand [realms]" (一念三千), though fascicle 1 does not yet use that later formula. |
| 一大事因緣 | the one great cause and condition | The Lotus Sūtra's own phrase for why a buddha appears in the world at all; folded into fascicle 1's closing analysis of why the bodhi-mind, though described many ways, is ultimately "one." |
| 六即：理即 / 名字即 / 觀行即 / 相似即 / 分真即 / 究竟即 | the six identities: identity in principle / in name / in contemplative practice / in resemblance / in a portion of the truth / in the ultimate | The treatise's best-known teaching device, closing fascicle 1's account of "revealing what is right": every stage from ordinary being to buddhahood is already, in principle, "it," while still needing five further stages of actual realization. |
| 五菩提：發心 / 伏心 / 明心 / 出到 / 無上 | the five kinds of bodhi: of the arising resolve / the subdued mind / the illuminated mind / emergence and arrival / the unsurpassed | From the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra*; fascicle 1 re-maps this older fivefold scheme onto the six identities and the perfect teaching's own bodhisattva stages (the ten abidings, practices, dedications, and grounds). |
| 荼 | the last syllable | The final letter of the Siddhaṃ syllabary, used in Chinese Buddhist exegesis as a symbol for the utmost limit beyond which nothing further can be said; appears in fascicle 1's account of both its own opening destination and the six identities' final stage. |

Keep this table growing as new recurring terms appear in later fascicles — add a row rather than improvising a fresh translation of a term that already has an entry.

### Fascicle 2 — the four kinds of samādhi, and the close of the great intent

Confirmed from the source text: fascicle 2 (both its upper and lower halves) completes topic 1, the great intent, in full — the second through fifth of its five brief points, following on from fascicle 1's completion of the first (giving rise to the great mind). CBETA's shang/xia split falls mid-argument this time, partway through the sixfold analysis of giving via action, rather than at a clean content boundary as it did in fascicle 1; both halves are still treated as one continuous fascicle per this site's convention.

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 四種三昧 | the four kinds of samādhi | Constantly sitting (常坐), constantly walking (常行), half walking and half sitting (半行半坐), and neither walking nor sitting (非行非坐) — cultivating great practice, the second of topic one's five brief points. All four are explicitly one and the same samādhi in different postures. |
| 常坐三昧 / 一行三昧 | constantly-sitting samādhi / the one-practice samādhi | The first of the four; ninety days of unbroken seated practice, drawn from the two Mañjuśrī-related Prajñāpāramitā sūtras; its contemplation empties out the Tathāgata's own ten epithets, then sentient beings, affliction, and even the five heinous crimes, as "the seal of the dharma-realm." |
| 常行三昧 / 般舟三昧 / 佛立三昧 | constantly-walking samādhi / the pratyutpanna samādhi / "the Buddha standing present" | The second of the four; ninety days of unbroken walking meditation combined with continuous chanting of and visualization on Amitābha Buddha, drawn from the *Pratyutpanna Samādhi Sūtra* — the foundational source, in this treatise, for what would become Pure Land recitation practice. Culminates in "mind makes the Buddha; mind itself sees mind." |
| 半行半坐三昧 | half-walking-half-sitting samādhi | The third of the four, drawn from two sūtras in turn. |
| 方等三昧 | the Vaipulya samādhi | Half-walking-half-sitting's first source: a seven-day repentance rite from the Vaipulya Dhāraṇī Sūtra, with an elaborate ritual method (circumambulation, confession, a round altar, twenty-four invited images) that the treatise then reads symbolically, clause by clause, against the three paths of suffering, karma, and affliction. |
| 法華三昧 | the Lotus samādhi | Half-walking-half-sitting's second source, from the Lotus Sūtra; reconciles the Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra's six-faculties repentance ritual ("with marks," 有相) with the "Course of Ease and Bliss" chapter's markless practice ("without marks," 無相) — Nanyue Huisi's own pairing. The six-tusked white elephant of the Universal Worthy vision is read allegorically, organ by organ, as the whole of bodhisattva practice. |
| 非行非坐三昧 / 隨自意三昧 / 覺意三昧 | neither-walking-nor-sitting samādhi / following one's own intent / the samādhi of awakened intent | The fourth and richest of the four; three names for one practice, cultivated the instant any intent (意) arises, in any posture or activity. Divided into four further approaches: by way of the sūtras, wholesome states, unwholesome states, and indeterminate states. |
| 四運 | the fourfold moment | Not-yet-thought / about-to-think / thinking / having-thought (未念/欲念/念/念已) — the basic analytical tool of "following one's own intent," applied throughout to whatever arises, wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate. |
| 請觀音經 | the Sūtra of Invoking Avalokiteśvara | Source for "following one's own intent" via the sūtras; its six-syllable dhāraṇī is matched one-to-one against the six destinies and the twenty-five samādhis. |
| 貪欲即是道 | "craving is itself the path" | The treatise's most provocative line, from the *Sūtra on the Practice of Non-Action* (無行經); reached only after establishing that every level up to the bodhisattva's own six pāramitās is still "evil" relative to something deeper. Illustrated with named exemplars (Aṅgulimāla, Vasumitrā, Devadatta) and immediately hedged with an extended warning against misuse. |
| 淮河之北 | "north of the Huai River" | A historical digression naming a real antinomian sect that misused "craving is itself the path" to justify licentiousness, which Zhiyi links to actual state persecution of Buddhism (likely Northern Zhou's Emperor Wu) and, further back, to the fall of the Zhou dynasty and the license of Ruan Ji's circle — three historical collapses from one root cause. |
| 根利無遮 / 根利有遮 / 根鈍無遮 / 根鈍有遮 | sharp capacity without hindrance / sharp capacity with hindrance / dull capacity without hindrance / dull capacity with hindrance | The treatise's real answer to why evil does not always obstruct the path: only sharp capacity can break through the hindrance evil creates (as with Aṅgulimāla and King Ajātaśatru); for everyone else, ordinary wholesome practice remains what actually works. |
| 三德：法身 / 般若 / 解脫 | the three virtues: the Dharma-body / prajñā / liberation | Introduced in fascicle 2's closing account of "returning to the great abode," the fifth brief point; recurs throughout the rest of the treatise as its own account of what buddhahood consists in. Each virtue, examined closely, is shown to already contain all three within itself, and the three obstructions that block them (ignorance, grasping at marks, not-knowing) are shown to be identical with the virtues they obstruct. |
| 旨歸 | where it all leads | The section-title term for topic ten (旨歸, the treatise's own last section) previewed here at the close of topic one; "旨" (turning toward the three virtues oneself) paired with "歸" (drawing others in to the same). |

### Fascicle 3 — the Four Teachings, laid out in full

Confirmed from the source text: fascicle 3 (both halves) covers four full topics at once — topic two, explaining the name (釋名), in all four of its approaches; topic three, the essence and its marks (體相), in all four of its own sub-parts; topic four, the scope of the teachings gathered in (攝法), across all six senses; and topic five, the relation to partial and complete teachings (偏圓), across all five pairs. This is where the treatise's governing Four Teachings scheme is laid out systematically for the first time.

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 藏教 / 通教 / 別教 / 圓教 | the Tripiṭaka teaching / the shared teaching / the distinct teaching / the perfect teaching | The Four Teachings (四教), the classification scheme fascicle 3 lays out in full and which organizes the rest of the treatise. Tripiṭaka: the Hīnayāna's analytical method. Shared: entry-level Mahāyāna, shared with the two lesser vehicles up to a point. Distinct: the bodhisattva's own long, sequential path. Perfect: the treatise's own teaching, in which every stage already contains the whole. |
| 相待 / 絕待 / 會異 / 通三德 | mutual opposition / the absolute, beyond all opposition / reconciling different names / pervading the three virtues | The four approaches to explaining the treatise's own title (topic two). Each progressively dissolves the opposition the previous one still relied on; the fourth matches calming and contemplation, term by term, to the three virtues from fascicle 2. |
| 伊字三點 | the letter *i* | The Nirvāṇa Sūtra's own image for how the three virtues relate: three points arranged in a triangle, neither vertically sequential (like the "water" radical) nor horizontally separate — rejecting both the Hīnayāna's strict before-and-after sequence and residual Mahāyāna attempts to smuggle in hierarchy. |
| 教相 / 眼智 / 境界 / 得失 | the teaching-mark / eyes and wisdom / objects / gain and loss | The four sub-parts of topic three, the essence and its marks (體相). |
| 析法 / 體法 | analysis / embodying | Two routes to emptiness distinguished throughout fascicle 3: the Hīnayāna's analytical method breaks a real thing down into parts until nothing remains ("like breaking apart a pillar to make it empty space"); the Mahāyāna's embodying method recognizes the thing was never solid to begin with ("like a pillar reflected in a mirror"). |
| 體真止 / 方便隨緣止 / 息二邊分別止 | the calming that embodies the real / the calming of skillful means that follows conditions / the calming that stills the two extremes | The three kinds of calming across the teachings, matched to the three contemplations below and, ultimately, to the Tripiṭaka/shared, distinct, and perfect teachings respectively. |
| 二諦觀（從假入空）/ 平等觀（從空入假）/ 中道第一義諦觀 | the contemplation of the two truths (entering emptiness from the provisional) / the contemplation of equality (entering the provisional from emptiness) / the contemplation of the middle, the ultimate truth | The three contemplations across the teachings, from the *Yingluo Sūtra*; illustrated with the simile of a newly sighted blind person who can see form but not yet distinguish "medicinal from poisonous" among it. |
| 隨情說 / 隨情智說 / 隨智說 | spoken according with sentiment / according with sentiment-and-wisdom together / according with wisdom alone | The three ways the two or three truths can be spoken of, illustrated by the parable of blind people describing milk's color as "like a shell," "like rice-flour," "like snow," "like a crane" — each true, none complete, the blind disputants mistaking partial truth for the whole. Twelve variants result when crossed with the four siddhāntas. |
| 二十三家 | twenty-three schools | Zhiyi's own count of rival Chinese exegetical schools on the Two Truths in his day, cited as an example of the blind-and-milk parable's own moral playing out in real doctrinal dispute. |
| 境智離合 | the convergence and divergence of objects and wisdom | How the Two Truths, Three Truths, and Four Noble Truths open out or gather together differently within each of the Four Teachings; ties directly back to fascicle 1's own fourfold Four Noble Truths (生滅/無生/無量/無作), matched here to the same "Three Truths verse" from the Middle Treatise. |
| 風流二諦 | the "elegant two truths" | A historical debate between the Zhuangyan and Kaishan exegetical schools over whether the buddha-fruit lies "outside" the two truths, resolved in fascicle 3 by recognizing that the shared teaching's own third truth has a genuinely separate object and wisdom of its own. |
| 以別接通 | using the distinct teaching to graft onto the shared teaching | An advanced doctrine for practitioners of the shared teaching whose capacity is sharp enough to continue directly into the distinct teaching's contemplation of the middle without restarting within the distinct teaching's own lengthy causal path. |
| 攝一切理 / 攝一切惑 / 攝一切智 / 攝一切行 / 攝一切位 / 攝一切教 | gathering in every principle / affliction / wisdom / practice / stage / teaching | The six senses of topic four, the scope of the teachings gathered in (攝法); demonstrates that calming-and-contemplation, though a short name, gathers in the entire received vocabulary of Buddhist practice and doctrine without exception. |
| 十二因緣（界內 / 界外） | the twelve links of dependent origination (within / beyond the three realms) | Fascicle 3 doubles the classical twelve links: the familiar version operates within the three realms and ends in birth-and-death of limited span; a second, subtler twelve links operates beyond the three realms, treating the residual ignorance the Hīnayāna does not even count as an affliction. |
| 大千經卷在一微塵 | the great sūtra-scroll hidden in a mote of dust | A simile from the *Ratnagotravibhāga*, cited in fascicle 3's account of gathering in every teaching: a scroll as vast as the whole universe lies hidden within a single mote of dust, and the Buddha's own teaching simply "breaks open the dust" to release what was there all along. |
| 五味：乳 / 酪 / 生酥 / 熟酥 / 醍醐 | the Five Flavors: milk / cream / curdled butter / clarified butter / ghee | A dairy-refining sequence mapping five ways of contemplating mind onto five bodies of scripture; the seed of the later, fuller Five Periods (五時) doctrine of Tiantai historiography. Illustrated with the startling image of poison "killing" with full potency at every single flavor, not just the last. |
| 大小 / 半滿 / 偏圓 / 漸頓 / 權實 | great and small / half and full / partial and perfect / gradual and sudden / the provisional and the real | The five pairs of topic five, the relation to partial and complete teachings (偏圓) — the longest topic in this fascicle, closing with the tetralemma that even "provisional" and "real" are themselves, in the end, only forced descriptions of the inexpressible. |
| 果頭無人 | "no person at the very head of the fruit" | The claim that a practitioner of the first three teachings who reaches that teaching's own stated final fruit never actually arrives as the same person who set out, since genuine breakthrough at any point already means having graduated into the perfect teaching's own scheme instead. |
| 置毒 | placing poison [in milk] | The Nirvāṇa Sūtra's own image, used twice in fascicle 3: poison placed in milk kills just as surely as poison placed in ghee, illustrating both that the buddha-nature teaching retains full potency at every stage of the Five Flavors, and that awakening can be "triggered" unpredictably, at any stage, for any practitioner. |

### Fascicle 4 — the twenty-five preliminary conditions

Confirmed from the source text: fascicle 4 (both halves) is devoted entirely to topic six, the preliminary expedients (方便), given in full as the twenty-five preliminary conditions (二十五法). Unlike fascicles 1–3, which each covered multiple topics of the ten-section outline, fascicle 4 spends its whole length on this single topic, in exhaustive practical detail — the treatise's own bridge from doctrine to daily conduct.

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 方便 / 遠方便 / 近方便 | the preliminary expedient / the remote expedient / the near expedient | Topic six as a whole (方便), and its own internal division: the twenty-five preliminary conditions are the *remote* expedient, prior even to formal contemplation; the ten objects of contemplation (topic seven, fascicle 5 onward) are the *near* expedient. |
| 二十五法 / 二十五方便 | the twenty-five methods / the twenty-five preliminary conditions | Five groups of five: fulfilling the five conditions, rebuking the five desires, abandoning the five hindrances, regulating the five things, and practicing the five methods. Source: three groups from the *Da zhi du lun*, one from the *Chan jing*, one from the meditation masters themselves. |
| 具五緣：持戒清淨 / 衣食具足 / 閑居靜處 / 息諸緣務 / 得善知識 | fulfilling the five conditions: purity in upholding the precepts / sufficiency of robes and food / dwelling in a quiet place / cessation of entanglements / obtaining a good friend | The first and by far the longest of the five groups, itself unfolding into extended sub-treatments below. |
| 十種戒：不缺 / 不破 / 不穿 / 不雜 / 隨道 / 無著 / 智所讚 / 自在 / 隨定 / 具足 | the ten kinds of precept: not deficient / not broken / not perforated / not mixed / in accord with the path / without attachment / praised by the wise / self-mastering / in accord with samādhi / complete | From the *Da zhi du lun*; re-sorted through contemplative insight into four dharmas as object, two mapped to emptiness, two to the provisional, two to the middle — the same three-truths pattern organizing the rest of the treatise. |
| 性戒 / 受得之戒 / 無作戒 | the precept of inherent nature / the precept as formally received / the unmanifest precept | Inherent-nature precepts are violated or upheld regardless of formal ordination; received precepts generate merit specifically through the vow taken. ### Fascicle 5 — three thousand in a single thought

Confirmed from the source text: fascicle 5 (both halves) opens topic seven, the actual practice (正修止觀) — by far the longest of the treatise's ten topics. It names the ten objects of contemplation and explains their own internal logic, then works the first four of the ten modes of contemplation through the first object, the five aggregates, in exhaustive detail. This is where the treatise's single most influential passage appears — "three thousand in a single thought" — and where the fourfold Nāgārjuna-style refutation (self, other, both, neither) is run, repeatedly and patiently, against the affliction of views.

### Fascicle 6 — comprehensively breaking through every dharma, completed

Confirmed from the source text: fascicle 6 (both halves) continues directly from fascicle 5's stopping point and completes the fourth of the ten modes of contemplation, comprehensively breaking through every dharma (破法遍), for the first object of contemplation. It finishes entering emptiness from the provisional by way of habituated thought (the affliction of views was completed in fascicle 5), works through the second and third movements — entering the provisional from emptiness, and the direct contemplation of the middle way — in full, then shows the entire method run through every other teaching-gate besides "the gate of the unborn," and unified in a single mind. No new topic of the treatise's own ten-section outline opens in this fascicle; it is devoted entirely to finishing Mode Four.
