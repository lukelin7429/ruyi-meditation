Inscription on Silent Illumination

Hongzhi Zhengjue (宏智正覺, 1091–1157) was abbot of Tiantong Monastery for nearly thirty years and the leading teacher of the Caodong house of Chan in his day. This short inscription — eighteen stanzas of four four-character lines, 288 characters in all — is his most compact statement of "silent illumination," the practice of resting in a silence that is never dull and an illumination that never stirs. The poem's argument turns on keeping the two together: illumination that loses silence becomes grasping and aggressive, and silence that loses illumination becomes dead weight. It draws freely on the imagery of the Caodong tradition — the upright and the inclined, interweaving, the shuttle and the loom — and closes by handing the practice on as the "affair of our house." The inscription is preserved in fascicle 8 of Hongzhi's Extensive Record (T2001).

Chinese Source

默照銘

Ru-Yi Translation

Inscription on Silent Illumination

Chinese Source

默默忘言。　昭昭現前。　鑒時廓爾。　體處靈然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Silent, silent, words forgotten; / clear, clear, it appears before you. / When it mirrors, it is vast and open; / where it is the essence, it is numinous.

Chinese Source

靈然獨照。　照中還妙。　露月星河。　雪松雲嶠。

Ru-Yi Translation

Numinous, it illuminates alone; / within the illumination, wondrous once more: / the dewy moon, the river of stars, / the snowy pines, the cloud-wrapped peaks.

Chinese Source

晦而彌明。　隱而愈顯。　鶴夢煙寒。　水含秋遠。

Ru-Yi Translation

Dark, yet all the brighter; / hidden, yet all the more revealed. / The crane dreams in the cold mist; / the water holds the distant autumn.

Chinese Source

浩劫空空。　相與雷同。　妙存默處。　功忘照中。

Ru-Yi Translation

Through vast kalpas, empty, empty, / each with each, alike as thunder's echo. / The wondrous abides where there is silence; / effort is forgotten within illumination.

Chinese Source

妙存何存。　惺惺破昏。　默照之道。　離微之根。

Ru-Yi Translation

The wondrous abides — where does it abide? / Alert, alert, it breaks through dullness. / The way of silent illumination / is the root of the separate and the subtle.

Chinese Source

徹見離微。　金梭玉機。　正偏宛轉。　明暗因依。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seeing through to the separate and the subtle: / a golden shuttle, a jade loom. / The upright and the inclined turn and wind; / light and dark depend on each other.

Chinese Source

依無能所。　底時回互。　飲善見藥。　檛塗毒鼓。

Ru-Yi Translation

Depending, without subject or object, / at just this time they interweave. / Drink the medicine of Good Sight; / beat the poison-smeared drum.

Chinese Source

回互底時。　殺活在我。　門裡出身。　枝頭結果。

Ru-Yi Translation

Interweaving at just this time, / killing and giving life are up to me. / From inside the gate, the body comes forth; / at the branch's tip, the fruit is formed.

Chinese Source

默唯至言。　照唯普應。　應不墮功。　言不涉聽。

Ru-Yi Translation

Silence alone is the ultimate word; / illumination alone is the universal response. / The response does not fall into effort; / the word does not involve hearing.

Chinese Source

萬象森羅。　放光說法。　彼彼證明。　各各問答。

Ru-Yi Translation

The myriad forms, densely arrayed, / emit light and teach the Dharma. / This one and that one bear witness; / each and every one asks and answers.

Chinese Source

問答證明。　恰恰相應。　照中失默。　便見侵凌。

Ru-Yi Translation

Asking and answering, bearing witness, / they correspond exactly. / If within illumination silence is lost, / then encroachment appears.

Chinese Source

證明問答。　相應恰恰。　默中失照。　渾成剩法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Bearing witness, asking and answering, / they correspond, exactly. / If within silence illumination is lost, / it all becomes a superfluous dharma.

Chinese Source

默照理圓。　蓮開夢覺。　百川赴海。　千峯向岳。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the principle of silent illumination is complete, / the lotus opens, the dreamer wakes; / a hundred rivers rush to the sea, / a thousand peaks face the great mountain.

Chinese Source

如鵝擇乳。　如蜂採花。　默照至得。　輸我宗家。

Ru-Yi Translation

Like the goose that sifts out milk, / like the bee that gathers from flowers: / silent illumination, fully attained, / is rendered to our house.

Chinese Source

宗家默照。　透頂透底。　舜若多身。　母陀羅臂。

Ru-Yi Translation

The silent illumination of our house / goes through to the top and through to the bottom: / the body of Śūnyatā, / the arms of Mudrā.

Chinese Source

始終一揆。　變態萬差。　和氏獻璞。　相如指瑕。

Ru-Yi Translation

Beginning and end follow one measure; / its changing forms differ ten thousand ways. / Mr. He presented the uncut jade; / Xiangru pointed out its flaw.

Chinese Source

當機有準。　大用不勤。　寰中天子。　塞外將軍。

Ru-Yi Translation

Meeting the occasion, there is a standard; / the great function takes no toil: / within the realm, the Son of Heaven; / beyond the frontier, the general.

Chinese Source

吾家底事。　中規中矩。　傳去諸方。　不要賺舉。

Ru-Yi Translation

The affair of our house / fits the compass, fits the square. / Pass it on to every quarter — / do not take it up wrongly.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Silence and illumination (默／照). The two words are kept fixed throughout: 默 is always "silence" or "silent," 照 always "illumination" or "illuminate." Stanzas 11 and 12 state the poem's central warning in mirror image: illumination without silence turns into "encroachment" (侵凌, grasping, overbearing activity), and silence without illumination turns into a "superfluous dharma" (剩法, something dead and extra).

"Where it is the essence" (體處). Paired with "when it mirrors" (鑒時): the first line describes illumination in its functioning, the second in its essence. 靈然, "numinous," means alive and aware rather than merely still.

"Alike as thunder's echo" (相與雷同). 雷同 ordinarily means "identical," from the way all things resonate together at a thunderclap. Alternatively: "all of them alike, together."

"The separate and the subtle" (離微). A pair from the Treasure Store Treatise (寶藏論, attributed to Sengzhao): 離 is the essence, separate from all marks; 微 is its function, too subtle to grasp. Chan writers use the pair for the hidden source of mind.

Golden shuttle and jade loom (金梭玉機). Weaving imagery common in the Caodong house: the shuttle passes back and forth, and the cloth is woven of both directions at once.

The upright and the inclined (正偏). The two poles of the Caodong "five ranks" (五位): the upright is the absolute, the inclined is the relative, phenomenal world. "Interweave" (回互) is the related Caodong term, from Shitou's Can Tong Qi (參同契), for the way the two interpenetrate while each stays in its own place.

"At just this time" (底時). 底 is colloquial for "this"; the phrase is reversed in the next stanza (回互底時) as a deliberate echo.

The medicine of Good Sight and the poison-smeared drum. The Avataṃsaka Sūtra (in its chapter on entering the dharma-realm) speaks of a king of medicines called Good Sight (善見藥王) that destroys every illness, as the bodhi-mind destroys every affliction. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra tells of a great drum smeared with mixed poisons: when it is struck in a crowd, all who hear it die. In Chan they stand for teaching that gives life and teaching that kills off delusion — hence "killing and giving life are up to me."

"From inside the gate, the body comes forth" (門裡出身). To emerge into the world from within one's own house, that is, to act freely from realization.

"The goose that sifts out milk" (鵝擇乳). The proverbial goose (haṃsa) that can drink only the milk from a mixture of milk and water; a figure for discerning the true. The bee takes nectar without harming the flower.

"Rendered to our house" (輸我宗家). 輸 can mean to deliver or to yield; the sense is that silent illumination, once fully attained, is the possession of this lineage. Alternatively: "it is surpassed by our house," that is, even "attainment" must be let go of within the house's own practice.

Śūnyatā's body and Mudrā's arms (舜若多身，母陀羅臂). Śūnyatā (舜若多, emptiness) is personified in Chinese texts as a deity without a body; mudrā (母陀羅) is the sealing gesture of the hands. Together: emptiness that has no body, yet acts with countless hands.

Mr. He and Xiangru (和氏獻璞，相如指瑕). Two stories about the famous jade disc of Mr. He (和氏璧). Bian He presented an uncut stone that the kings refused to recognize as jade; centuries later, Lin Xiangru, carrying the same disc, said there was a flaw in it so that he could take it back into his hands. The same jewel is first offered whole and later "flawed" for a purpose — the "changing forms" of one realization.

"Within the realm … beyond the frontier" (寰中天子，塞外將軍). The Son of Heaven rules at the center without moving; the general acts on his own authority at the frontier. A figure for the unmoving and the fully active as one function.

"Fits the compass, fits the square" (中規中矩). Conforms exactly to the standard.

"Do not take it up wrongly" (不要賺舉). 賺 means to mislead or to get wrong; the sense is a warning not to misrepresent the practice when passing it on.
