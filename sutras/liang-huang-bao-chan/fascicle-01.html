Fascicle 1

Refuge, resolving doubt, and the opening confession《慈悲道場懺法》卷第一

The liturgy opens by explaining its own name and the twelve vows behind it, then moves through its first three traditional chapters: taking refuge in the Three Jewels, resolving the doubts that arise when karmic retribution seems to contradict what we observe in the world, and the opening confession of the ten evil deeds, the six sense-faculties, and the mind's own inequality and inverted views.

Covers

Genre note
Continuous liturgical prose meant to be recited, not narrative. The traditional chapter titles are preserved as this translation's section headings; the numbered subsections within each chapter are this translation's own, added for readability.
A recurring formula
Short Buddha-name litanies close each unit of confession in this fascicle, changing in length and content from one occurrence to the next — unlike the fixed sixteen-name litany that recurs throughout the Cibei Shuichan Fa. Each is translated in full. "We disciples" translates the ritual placeholder 某甲 ("so-and-so"), spoken by whoever performs the rite in place of their own name.
Difficulty
★★★☆☆ — Chapter 2's treatment of why the virtuous suffer and the wicked prosper is a genuine, sustained argument rather than a simple assertion, and repays close reading.

Opening: The Name of the Rite, and Its Twelve Vows

Chinese Source

立此慈悲道場四字，乃因夢感。 彌勒世尊，既慈隆即世、悲臻後劫，依事題名，弗敢移易。承此念力，欲守護三寶，令魔隱蔽，摧伏自大增上慢者，未種善根者今當令種、已種善根者今令增長，若計有所得住諸見者皆悉令發捨離之心，樂小法者令不疑大法，樂大法者令生歡喜。又此慈悲，諸善中王，一切眾生所歸依處。如日照晝、如月照夜，為人眼目、為人導師，為人父母、為人兄弟，同歸道場，為真知識。慈悲之親重於血肉，世世相隨雖死不離，故目等心，標號如上。 今日道場幽顯大眾，立此懺法并發大心，有十二大因緣。何等十二？一者願化六道心無限齊；二者為報慈恩功無限齊；三者願以此善力，令諸眾生受佛禁戒不起犯心；四者以此善力，令諸眾生於諸尊長不起慢心；五者以此善力，令諸眾生在所生處不起恚心；六者以此善力，令諸眾生於他身色不起嫉心；七者以此善力，令諸眾生於內外法不起慳心；八者以此善力，令諸眾生凡所修福不為自身，悉為一切無覆護者；九者以此善力，令諸眾生不為自身行四攝法；十者以此善力，令諸眾生見有孤獨幽繫疾病，起救濟心令得安樂；十一者以此善力，若有眾生，應折伏者而折伏之、應攝受者而攝受之；十二者以此善力，令諸眾生在所生處，恒自憶念發菩提心，令菩提心相續不斷。仰願幽顯凡聖大眾，同加覆護、同加攝受，令某甲等某甲字請各自稱名，後皆例此所悔清淨、所願成就，等諸佛心、同諸佛願。六道四生皆悉隨從滿菩提願。淨三業法淨身當洗浴，淨口讚佛功德，淨意運心懺悔。三業既淨，乃可標心歸依三寶大慈悲父。 歸依三寶第一　斷疑第二　懺悔第三　發菩提心第四　發願第五　發迴向心第六

Ru-Yi Translation

The naming of this rite as "The Hall of Compassion," these four characters, came about through a response received in a dream. Since the World-Honored Maitreya's kindness rises to its height in this very age, and his compassion will reach its fullness in the kalpa to come, this rite is named according to that very fact, and the name is not to be altered. Relying on the power of this intention, we wish to protect the Three Jewels, causing Māra to be hidden away and subduing those swollen with pride and arrogance; causing those who have not yet planted wholesome roots to plant them now, and those who have already planted them to grow further; causing all who reckon they have attained something and cling to their views to give rise to a mind of release; causing those who delight in the lesser teaching to no longer doubt the greater, and those who delight in the greater teaching to give rise to joy. This compassion, moreover, is the king among all wholesome things, the very place to which every living being turns for refuge — like the sun illuminating the day, like the moon illuminating the night, it is the eye of every person, the guide of every person, the parent of every person, the sibling of every person, and together we turn to this rite as our true and good companion. The closeness of compassion outweighs even flesh and blood, following us life after life, never parting even in death — and for this reason we set our minds equally upon it, and give it the title set out above. Today, the assembly gathered at this rite, both hidden and manifest, establishes this repentance rite and gives rise to the great mind through twelve great causes and conditions. What are these twelve? First, the vow to transform all beings of the six destinies, a mind without limit or measure. Second, to repay the kindness of compassion, a merit without limit or measure. Third, by this wholesome power, to cause all living beings to receive the Buddha's precepts without giving rise to a mind that transgresses them. Fourth, by this wholesome power, to cause all living beings to give rise to no arrogant mind toward their elders and superiors. Fifth, by this wholesome power, to cause all living beings, wherever they are born, to give rise to no mind of hatred. Sixth, by this wholesome power, to cause all living beings to give rise to no mind of jealousy toward another's body or beauty. Seventh, by this wholesome power, to cause all living beings to give rise to no mind of stinginess regarding any teaching, inward or outward. Eighth, by this wholesome power, to cause all living beings, whatever merit they cultivate, to do so not for themselves, but entirely for the sake of all who have no shelter or protection. Ninth, by this wholesome power, to cause all living beings to practice the four means of gathering others not for their own sake. Tenth, by this wholesome power, to cause all living beings, upon seeing one who is solitary, imprisoned in darkness, or ill, to give rise to a mind of rescue and relief, that they may attain peace and joy. Eleventh, by this wholesome power, that whichever beings ought to be subdued be subdued, and whichever beings ought to be gathered in and received be gathered in and received. Twelfth, by this wholesome power, to cause all living beings, wherever they are born, to constantly and continually recollect and give rise to the awakening-mind, so that this awakening-mind may continue without ever being broken off. We look up and pray that the assembly of hidden and manifest beings, ordinary and noble alike, may together extend their protection and together receive us, so that we, the disciples — here each should speak his own name, and this convention applies in every case that follows — may have what we confess made pure and what we vow fulfilled, equal to the mind of all the Buddhas, one with the vow of all the Buddhas, so that beings of the six destinies and four modes of birth may all follow along and fulfill the vow of awakening. To purify the three kinds of karma: to purify the body, one should bathe; to purify speech, one should praise the Buddha's merit; to purify the mind, one should set it in motion toward confession. Only once the three kinds of karma are purified may we then fix our minds and take refuge in the Three Jewels, the great and compassionate father. [Contents of Fascicles 1–2:] Refuge in the Three Jewels, first. Resolving Doubt, second. Confession, third. Arousing the Awakening-Mind, fourth. Making the Vow, fifth. Arousing the Mind of Dedication, sixth.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The final line above is the liturgy's own table of contents for its first two fascicles, listing the six chapters that will occupy them — the last three, on arousing the awakening-mind, the vow, and the dedication of merit, belong to Fascicle 2 and are not yet translated on this site. The twelve vows that precede it are worth reading as the liturgy's own statement of purpose: notice how many of them are framed not as personal aspirations but as vows made on behalf of "all living beings," a pattern that recurs throughout this text and distinguishes its confession from a private accounting of one's own faults.

1. Refuge in the Three Jewels歸依三寶第一

Chinese Source

今日道場同業大眾，宜各人人起覺悟意，念世無常，形不久住、少壯必衰，勿恃容姿自處污行。萬物無常皆當歸死，天上天下誰能留者？年少顏色肌膚鮮澤、氣息香潔是非身保，人生合會必歸磨滅，生老病死至來無期，誰當為我却除之者？災害卒至不可得脫，一切貴賤因而死已。身體胮脹臭不可聞，空愛惜之於事何益？自非勤行勝業，無由出離。某甲等自惟：形同朝露命速西光，生世貧乏無德可稱，智無神聖大人之明、識無聖人洞徹之照，言無忠和仁善之美、行無進退高下之節，謬立斯志，勞倦仁者、仰屈大眾，慚懼交心。既法席有期、追戀無及，從此一別願各努力，勤意朝夕親奉供養，人加精進，唯是為快。仰願大眾各秉其心，披忍辱鎧入深法門。 今日道場同業大眾！宜各慇重起勇猛心、不放逸心、安住心、大心、勝心、大慈悲心、樂善心、歡喜心、報恩心、度一切心、守護一切心、救護一切心、同菩薩心、等如來心，一心志意五體投地，奉為國王帝主、土地人民、父母師長、上中下座善惡知識、諸天諸仙護世四王、主善罰惡守護持呪五方龍王龍神八部，廣及十方無窮無盡含靈抱識水陸空界一切眾生。 歸依十方盡虛空界一切諸佛一拜。歸依十方盡虛空界一切尊法一拜。歸依十方盡虛空界一切賢聖一拜。 今日道場同業大眾！何故應須歸依三寶？諸佛菩薩有無限齊大悲度脫世間、有無限齊大慈安慰世間，念一切眾生猶如一子，大慈大悲常無懈倦，恒求善事利益一切，誓滅眾生三毒之火，教化令得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。眾生不得佛，誓不取正覺。以是義故，應須歸依。又復諸佛慈念眾生過於父母。經言「父母念兒慈止一世，佛念眾生慈心無盡。又父母見子背恩違義，心生恚恨，慈心薄少。諸佛菩薩慈心不爾，見此眾生悲心益重，乃至入於無間地獄大火輪中，代諸眾生受無量苦。」是知諸佛諸大菩薩愛念眾生過於父母。而諸眾生無明覆慧煩惱覆心，於佛菩薩不知歸向，說法教化亦不信受，乃至麁言起於誹謗，未曾發心念諸佛恩。以不信故，墮在地獄餓鬼畜生諸惡道中，遍歷三途受無量苦。罪畢得出，暫生人間，諸根不具以自莊嚴，無禪定水、無智慧力，如是等障由無信心。 今日道場同業大眾！不信之罪，眾罪之上，能令行人長不見佛。相與今日各自慷慨折意挫情，生增上心、起慚愧意，稽顙求哀懺悔往罪。業累既盡表裏俱淨，然後運想入歸信門。若不起如是心、運如是意，直恐隔絕障滯難通，一失斯向冥然無返。豈得不人人五體投地如大山崩，一心歸信無復疑想。某甲等今日以諸佛菩薩慈悲心力，始蒙覺悟，深生慚愧。若已作之罪願乞除滅，未作之罪不敢復造。從今已去至于菩提，起堅固信不復退轉。某甲等捨此身命，若生地獄道、若生餓鬼道、若生畜生道、若生人道、若生天道，於三界中若受男身、若受女身、若受非男非女等身，若大若小、若昇若降，受諸迫惱難堪難忍，誓不以苦故退失今日信心；寧於千劫萬劫受種種苦，誓不以苦故退失今日信心。仰願諸佛大地菩薩同加救護、同加攝受，令某甲等信心堅固，等諸佛心、同諸佛願，眾魔外道所不能壞，相與志心等一痛切。五體投地。 歸依十方盡虛空界一切諸佛一拜。歸依十方盡虛空界一切尊法一拜。歸依十方盡虛空界一切賢聖一拜。 今日道場同業大眾！善攝心聽。夫人天幻惑世界虛假，由其幻惑非真故無實果，虛假浮脆故則遷變無窮。無實果故，所以久滯生死之流；遷變改故，所以長泛愛苦之海。如是眾生，聖所悲念。故《悲華經》云「菩薩成佛各有本願。」釋迦不現長年，促為短壽。悲此眾生變化俄頃，長淪苦海不得捨離，故在此土救諸弊惡。教有剛強苦切之言，不捨於苦而度眾生，未嘗不以善法方便弘濟益之心。所以《三昧經》言「諸佛心者，是大慈悲。慈悲所緣，緣苦眾生。若見眾生受苦惱時，如箭入心、如破眼目。見已悲泣心無暫安，欲拔其苦令得安樂。」又諸佛等智其化是均，至於釋迦偏稱勇猛，以能忍苦度脫眾生。當知本師慈恩實重，能於苦惱眾生之中，說種種語利益一切。我等今日不蒙解脫，進不聞一音之旨、退不覩雙樹潛輝，良由業障，念與悲隔。相與今日起悲戀心，以悲戀如來故善心濃厚。既在苦中憶如來恩，嗚咽懊惱慚顏哽慟，等一痛切。五體投地，志心奉為： 國王帝主、土地人民、父母師長、信施檀越、善惡知識、諸天諸仙聰明正直、天地虛空護世四王、主善罰惡守護持呪五方龍王龍神八部，廣及十方無窮無盡一切眾生，重復 歸依十方盡虛空界一切諸佛一拜。歸依十方盡虛空界一切尊法一拜。歸依十方盡虛空界一切賢聖一拜。相與志心胡跪合掌，心念口言作如是說： 五體投地各自念言：「仰願十方一切三寶，以慈悲力、以本願力、大神通力、不思議力、無量自在力、度脫眾生力、覆護眾生力、安慰眾生力，令諸眾生皆悉覺悟知。某甲等今日為其歸依三寶，以此功德力，令諸眾生各得所願。若在諸天諸仙中者，令盡諸漏；若在阿脩羅中者，捨憍慢習；若在人道，無復眾苦；若在地獄、餓鬼、畜生道者，即得免離。又復今日若聞三寶名及與不聞，以佛神力，令諸眾生盡得解脫究竟成就無上菩提，同諸菩薩俱登正覺。」一拜。

Ru-Yi Translation

Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — each of us should arouse a mind of awakening, reflecting that the world is impermanent, that this form cannot long endure, that youth and vigor must inevitably decline. Let none of us rely on our appearance to indulge in defiled conduct. All things are impermanent and must return to death — in all the heavens above and the earth below, who can remain forever? The bloom of youth, the freshness of the skin, the fragrance of the breath — none of these can be kept safe within this body. Whatever comes together in a human life must in the end wear away and be destroyed; birth, old age, sickness, and death arrive with no fixed hour — who is there to drive them back on our behalf? Calamity arrives suddenly and cannot be escaped; the noble and the humble alike come, in the end, to this same death. The body swells and rots, its stench unbearable — what use is it, then, to cherish it in vain? Unless we diligently practice what is truly excellent, there is no way to escape. We disciples reflect on ourselves: our form is like the morning dew, our life passes as swiftly as the light of the setting sun in the west; born into this world poor and lacking, we have no virtue worth the name — no wisdom with the brilliance of a sage or a great being, no understanding with the penetrating insight of the noble ones, no words with the beauty of loyalty, harmony, benevolence, and goodness, no conduct with the proper measure of advance and retreat, high and low. And yet we have wrongly set our resolve to this task, wearying the benevolent and troubling this great assembly to bow before us — shame and dread fill our hearts together. Since this Dharma-assembly has its appointed term, and what we long for cannot now be recalled, from this parting on, may each of us strive with diligence, applying our minds morning and evening to offering our reverence in person, each person adding to their own diligence — this alone is our true joy. We look up and pray that this whole assembly, each holding fast to this resolve, may don the armor of patience and enter this profound gate of the Dharma. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — each of us should earnestly and solemnly arouse a mind of courage, a mind free of heedlessness, a mind settled and abiding, a great mind, a mind of excellence, a mind of great compassion, a mind that delights in goodness, a mind of joy, a mind of gratitude, a mind that delivers all beings, a mind that guards all beings, a mind that rescues and protects all beings, a mind equal to the bodhisattvas, a mind the same as the Tathāgatas — and with this single mind and resolve, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, on behalf of kings and rulers, the lands and their peoples, our parents and teachers, good and evil companions of high, middle, and low station, all the gods and immortals, the Four Heavenly Kings who guard the world, the dragon-kings of the five directions and the eightfold host of dragon-spirits who reward good and punish evil, and, extending it out endlessly to the ten directions without limit, every living being possessed of spirit and consciousness throughout the realms of water, land, and air. We take refuge in all the Buddhas throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space — one bow. We take refuge in all the venerable Dharma throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space — one bow. We take refuge in all the sages and worthies throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space — one bow. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — why must we take refuge in the Three Jewels? The Buddhas and bodhisattvas possess a great compassion without limit or measure, delivering the world from suffering, and a great loving-kindness without limit or measure, comforting the world; they regard every living being as an only child, their great kindness and compassion never wavering, forever seeking what is good for the benefit of all, vowing to extinguish the fire of the three poisons in every being, teaching and transforming them until they attain unsurpassed, perfect awakening. Should living beings fail to attain buddhahood, they vow never to accept true awakening themselves. For this reason, we must take refuge in them. Moreover, the Buddhas' compassionate regard for living beings surpasses even that of parents. The sūtra says: "A parent's love for a child lasts only for a single lifetime, but the Buddha's compassionate mind toward living beings is without end. Moreover, when parents see their child turn against their kindness and betray what is right, resentment arises in their hearts, and their compassion grows thin. The compassionate mind of the Buddhas and bodhisattvas is not so — seeing such a being, their heart of sorrow only grows heavier, even to the point of entering the great wheel of fire in the hell of unceasing torment, to bear immeasurable suffering in that being's place." From this we know that the Buddhas and great bodhisattvas cherish living beings even more than a parent does. And yet living beings, their wisdom covered by ignorance and their minds obstructed by affliction, do not know to turn toward the Buddhas and bodhisattvas; even when the Dharma is taught to instruct them, they do not believe or accept it, and go so far as to slander it with coarse words, never once giving rise to a thought of gratitude for the Buddhas' kindness. Because of this disbelief, they fall among the hells, the hungry ghosts, and the animals, and every kind of evil destiny, passing through the three evil paths in full, undergoing immeasurable suffering. When their fault is at last exhausted and they escape, they are born for a brief time among humans, but their faculties are incomplete and cannot adorn themselves properly; they have no water of meditative concentration, no power of wisdom — and every one of these obstructions comes from the lack of a believing mind. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — the fault of disbelief stands above every other fault, for it alone can keep a practitioner from ever seeing the Buddha for a very long time. Let us together today, each with real resolve, break down our own stubbornness and subdue our own feelings, giving rise to a mind of exalted resolve and a heart of shame, bowing our heads to the ground and begging for mercy in confessing our past faults. Only once the accumulated weight of karma is exhausted, inside and out alike made pure, can we then turn our thoughts and enter the gate of faith and refuge. If we do not arouse a mind such as this, and set such a resolve in motion, then I fear we will be cut off and obstructed, unable to pass through, and once we lose this direction, we will vanish into darkness with no way back. How, then, can each of us fail to prostrate fully to the ground like a great mountain collapsing, our whole mind turned to faith and refuge, with no trace of doubt remaining? We disciples, today, through the power of the compassionate mind of the Buddhas and bodhisattvas, have at last begun to awaken, and a deep sense of shame has arisen in us. Whatever fault we have already committed, we pray that it be entirely dissolved; whatever fault we have not yet committed, we dare not commit it now. From this day forward, until we reach awakening, we arouse a firm and unshakable faith, never to retreat from it again. We disciples vow: whatever body we take up after giving up this life — whether born in the hell-destiny, or the hungry-ghost destiny, or the animal destiny, or the human destiny, or the heavenly destiny; whether, within the three realms, we receive a male body, a female body, or a body that is neither male nor female; whether great or small, whether rising or falling — however great the oppression and torment we must undergo, however hard to bear and hard to endure, we vow never, because of such suffering, to let this faith we hold today fall away. Rather than lose this faith, we would gladly undergo every kind of suffering across a thousand, ten thousand kalpas; we vow never, because of such suffering, to let this faith we hold today fall away. We look up and pray that the Buddhas and great bodhisattvas may together extend their rescue and together receive us, so that we, the disciples, may have our faith made firm, equal to the mind of all the Buddhas, one with the vow of all the Buddhas, unable to be destroyed by any demon or heterodox teaching — and together, with one mind, in the same anguish of resolve, we prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. We take refuge in all the Buddhas throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space — one bow. We take refuge in all the venerable Dharma throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space — one bow. We take refuge in all the sages and worthies throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space — one bow. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — listen well, with a mind carefully composed. This world of gods and humans is an illusion, a deception, empty and false; because this illusion and deception are not real, it bears no true fruit, and because it is false and fragile, it changes and shifts without end. Because it bears no true fruit, beings remain long mired in the flow of birth and death; because it constantly shifts and changes, they remain long adrift on the vast sea of love and suffering. Living beings caught in this way are held in sorrowful regard by the sages. This is why the Compassionate Flower Sūtra says: "Every bodhisattva who attains buddhahood does so according to his own original vow." Śākyamuni chose not to manifest a long life, but shortened it instead — grieving that living beings change and transform in an instant, sinking for so long in the sea of suffering with no way to leave it, he therefore remained in this world to save its wicked and depraved. His teaching includes words that are stern and severe, for he does not spare beings the suffering in order to deliver them, and yet there has never been a moment when his mind of skillful, beneficent rescue through wholesome means was absent. This is why the Samādhi Sūtra says: "The mind of the Buddhas is great compassion. What great compassion takes as its object is the suffering of living beings. Whenever the Buddha sees a being undergoing suffering and affliction, it is as though an arrow pierced his heart, as though his very eyes were shattered. Seeing this, he weeps in sorrow, his mind never at ease for a moment, wishing only to draw out that suffering and bring the being to peace and joy." And though all the Buddhas share equal wisdom and their transformative work is the same, Śākyamuni alone is singled out for his courage, for his ability to endure suffering in order to deliver living beings. We should know that the kindness of our fundamental teacher is truly profound, for in the midst of suffering and afflicted beings he spoke words of every kind for the benefit of all. Today we ourselves have not yet received liberation — looking forward, we cannot hear the teaching of his very voice; looking back, we cannot see the light that once shone beneath the twin śāla trees — and this is truly because our karmic obstruction stands between us and his compassion. Let us together, today, arouse a heart of sorrowful longing, and because of this sorrowful longing for the Tathāgata, let our wholesome mind grow rich and deep. Being now in the midst of suffering, remembering the Tathāgata's kindness, we weep and grieve, our faces flushed with shame, choking with sorrow together as one — and with this same anguish, we prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, and with sincere mind, dedicate this on behalf of: kings and rulers, the lands and their peoples, our parents and teachers, the faithful donors who provide our support, good and evil companions, the gods and immortals bright and upright, the Four Heavenly Kings who guard heaven, earth, and the reaches of empty space, the dragon-kings of the five directions and the eightfold host of dragon-spirits who reward good and punish evil, and, extending it out endlessly to the ten directions without limit, every living being — and once again: We take refuge in all the Buddhas throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space — one bow. We take refuge in all the venerable Dharma throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space — one bow. We take refuge in all the sages and worthies throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space — one bow. Let us together, with sincere mind, kneel with palms joined, and speak with mind and voice together, saying: Prostrating fully to the ground, let each of us reflect and say: "We look up and pray that the Three Jewels of the ten directions, by the power of compassion, by the power of their original vows, by their power of great supernatural ability, by their inconceivable power, by their power of boundless mastery, by their power to deliver living beings, by their power to shelter and protect living beings, and by their power to comfort living beings, may cause all living beings to awaken and come to understanding. We disciples, today, take refuge in the Three Jewels on their behalf, that by the power of this merit, all living beings may each attain what they wish. If they dwell among the gods or immortals, may their every defilement be exhausted; if they dwell among the asuras, may they cast off the habit of pride; if they dwell in the human realm, may they suffer no more; if they dwell in the hell, hungry-ghost, or animal destinies, may they be freed and released at once. And moreover, whether they have heard the names of the Three Jewels today or have not heard them, may the Buddha's supernatural power cause all living beings to attain complete liberation, to accomplish unsurpassed awakening at last, and to ascend together with all the bodhisattvas to true awakening." Unlike the Cibei Shuichan Fa, which closes each unit of confession with a single fixed sixteen-name Buddha litany repeated verbatim throughout, this liturgy structures its refuge chapter around a short three-line formula — refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha, each followed by "one bow" — repeated four times in this chapter alone, each time preceded by a different meditation on why refuge matters: impermanence, the Buddhas' compassion exceeding a parent's love, the danger of disbelief itself, and finally a meditation on Śākyamuni's own historical choice to appear in a world of suffering rather than a purer one. The chapter builds cumulatively rather than repetitively — each bow is earned by a different argument for taking it.

Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — listen closely, with a single mind. Cause and effect, response and reflection, arise from and answer one another — this is a necessary principle, and there is no error in it. Yet the karmic conduct of living beings is impure, good and evil alternating within it; and because this karma is impure, its retribution comes out refined or coarse, noble or humble, good or evil, in every kind of unequal variety. Since such variation exists, and beings do not understand its underlying working, this failure to understand gives rise to confusion and doubt. Some say: one who diligently keeps the precepts ought to gain a long life, and yet we see him die young; one who slaughters and kills ought to see his years cut short, and yet, on the contrary, his life is extended. A person of pure integrity ought to be rewarded with abundance, and yet we see him poor and suffering; a person given to greed and theft ought to be brought to ruin, and yet he grows only more prosperous. Who among us has never had such a doubt arise? And yet we do not realize that this all comes from the causes planted by karma in the past. Consider, too, what the Prajñāpāramitā makes clear: if one who reads and recites this sūtra is looked down upon and despised by others, this person's karmic fault from a former life ought to have caused him to fall into an evil destiny — but because he is despised by people in this present life, that former karmic fault is thereby dissolved instead. The reason living beings cannot deeply believe the sūtra's words, and give rise to this very doubt, is entirely because ignorance and delusion give rise to false, inverted thinking. Beings likewise do not believe that what lies within the three realms is suffering, nor do they believe that what lies beyond the three realms is joy; steeped always in the world, they call it joy in every instance. But if it is truly joy, why does suffering keep arising within it? Overindulge in food and drink, and illness results — the breath grows short and labored, the belly swells and cramps in pain. Consider clothing as well, and one finds only further worry and toil: in the cold, put on garments too thin, and the deprivation is felt keenly; in the heat, put on heavy furs, and the suffering runs deep already. If this is truly joy, why does it give rise to such distress? Hence we know that food, drink, and clothing are not truly joy at all. Or one might say that one's family and kin are a joy — then surely one should be able to laugh and delight with them forever, without end. Why, then, does impermanence arrive so suddenly, taking them away in an instant — here just now, and gone; present a moment ago, and vanished now — so that one cries out to heaven and beats the earth, one's very liver and heart torn to pieces? And not knowing where birth comes from or where death leads, one carries one's grief all the way to the desolate mountain to see the dead off, holding their hand for one long parting, a single farewell across ten thousand kalpas. Sufferings of this kind are without measure, and yet living beings, deluded in their view, call them joy — while the very causes of the joy that lies beyond this world, they call suffering instead. Or one sees another who eats only simple vegetables, restrained and disciplined in his meals at the proper hour, giving up soft and fine things for the coarse robe of discarded rags — and calls this an unnatural, self-imposed hardship, not knowing that this very practice is the path of liberation. Or one sees another practicing giving, keeping the precepts, patience, diligence, walking meditation, prostration, and recitation, earnest and unflagging — and calls this suffering too, not knowing that this is the very cultivation of a mind that transcends the world. Should illness or the day of death then arrive, doubt at once arises: "To labor this body and mind all day long without a moment's rest — how can a person's strength bear such a thing? If one did not toil so hard, would one really come to this ruin? One has thrown away one's very life and body for nothing, and gained no benefit from it." Some hold to just such a view themselves, believing the reasoning to be sound. Not knowing how to trace the effect back to its cause, they wrongly construct this very confusion for themselves. If such a person meets a good companion, this confusion can be dispelled; but if he meets a bad companion, his foolishness only deepens further. Because of this very doubt, he falls into the three evil destinies — and once in an evil destiny, what good can regret possibly do? Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — every cause and condition behind this doubt is without measure, and even the habitual tendency toward doubt is not fully exhausted until one has left the three realms behind entirely — how, then, could it simply vanish all at once in this present body? If it is not cut off in this life, it will only grow further in the next. Let this whole assembly, together, as we now walk this long road and practice these austerities ourselves, rely on the Buddha's own words and cultivate exactly as he taught, and not, out of doubt, excuse ourselves from the effort as too wearisome. Now, fortunately, our four elements have not yet declined, and the five blessings are still ours in good health; we can move about and act as we please — and if we do not now make the effort, what else are we waiting for? The noble path is long and far, and cannot be accomplished in a single morning — and if we let one such morning pass and then another, when will what needs to be done ever be done? This chapter's opening argument — the "problem of unjust suffering," where the good visibly suffer and the wicked visibly prosper — is treated at more length and with more genuine argumentative texture here than in the equivalent passage of the Cibei Shuichan Fa, running through several concrete counter-examples (a devoted ascetic's simple diet called self-torture, a family's love called happiness right up until sudden loss) before resolving the doubt through the doctrine of karma ripening across different lifetimes. The hungry-ghost story that follows, from the otherwise little-known Sūtra on Guarding the Mouth, makes a narrower point specifically about the danger of criticizing another's practice — a fault this liturgy treats as unusually serious, since it damages not only the speaker but potentially discourages someone else's entire path.

We further take refuge in all the Three Jewels throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space, praying only that they come without fail to have pity on the suffering of our three poisons, and grant us peace, joy, and the great nirvāṇa. May they wash away our defilement with the water of great compassion, bringing us at last, upon reaching awakening, to complete purity; and may every being of the six destinies and four modes of birth who bears this same fault attain purity together, accomplishing unsurpassed, perfect awakening and complete liberation. Let us together, with sincere mind, in this same anguish of resolve, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, and speak with mind and voice: we disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, have been covered by ignorance, entangled by the emissary of craving, and bound by hatred, fallen into the net of folly, passing through the three realms and traversing all six destinies in full, sinking in the sea of suffering, unable to pull ourselves free. Not recognizing the causes and conditions of our past karma, whether destroying our own pure livelihood or another's, destroying our own pure conduct or another's, destroying our own pure precepts or another's — faults and evils of this kind are immeasurable and boundless. Today, in shame, we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. We disciples, once again with utmost sincerity, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, through body, speech, and mind, we have committed the ten evil deeds — in body, killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct; in speech, false speech, flowery speech, divisive speech, and harsh speech; in mind, greed, hatred, and delusion — committing the ten evils ourselves, instructing others to commit the ten evils, praising the ten evil deeds, and praising those who commit the ten evil deeds, so that within a single thought, forty kinds of evil arise at once. Faults of this kind are immeasurable and boundless; today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. We disciples, once again with utmost sincerity, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, relying on the six sense-faculties, acting through the six consciousnesses, and grasping at the six sense-objects, the eye has been attached to form, the ear to sound, the nose to smell, the tongue to taste, the body to smoothness and softness, and the mind to mental objects, giving rise to every kind of karma, extending even to the opening of the eighty-four thousand gates of worldly affliction. Faults and evils of this kind are immeasurable and boundless; today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. We disciples, once again with utmost sincerity, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, through body, speech, and mind, we have acted without impartiality — knowing only that we have a body, and not knowing that others have bodies too; knowing only that we suffer, and not knowing that others suffer too; knowing only to seek our own peace and joy, and not knowing that others seek it too; knowing only to seek our own liberation, and not knowing that others seek it too; knowing only that we have our own household and kin, and not knowing that others have kin as well; knowing only that we ourselves cannot bear even a single itch or ache, while beating another's body, we constantly fear only that the pain we inflict is not deep enough; knowing only to fear the small suffering of this present body, and not knowing to fear the evil karma that, once this body is given up, will surely cause us to fall into the hells and undergo every suffering there in full — and not even knowing of the various sufferings that exist among the hungry ghosts, the animals, the asuras, humans, and gods. Because of this lack of impartiality, the thought of "I" and "mine" arises, and the notion of enemy and friend is born, so that enmity spreads throughout all six destinies. Faults of this kind are immeasurable and boundless; today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. We disciples, once again with utmost sincerity, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, our mind has been inverted, our perception inverted, our view inverted — keeping distant from good companions and drawing close to bad companions, turning our backs on the eightfold noble path and walking the eightfold wrong path, calling what is not the Dharma the Dharma, and calling the Dharma not the Dharma, calling what is not good good, and calling what is good not good, raising the banner of arrogance, spreading the sail of foolishness, following the current of ignorance into the sea of birth and death. Faults and evils of this kind are immeasurable and boundless; today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. We disciples, once again with utmost anguish, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, through the three unwholesome roots, we have given rise to the four inverted views, committed the five heinous crimes, practiced the ten evils, blazed with the three poisons, and nourished the eight sufferings, creating the causes for the eight cold hells and eight hot hells, creating the causes for the eighty-four thousand lesser cell-hells, creating every cause for the animal realm, creating every cause for the hungry-ghost realm, and creating every kind of suffering-cause of birth, old age, sickness, and death among humans and gods, receiving in the six destinies immeasurable bitter fruit, hard to bear and unbearable to hear or see. Faults and evils of this kind are immeasurable and boundless; today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. We disciples, once again with utmost anguish, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, begging for mercy and repenting our transgressions. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, through the roots of the three poisons, we have given rise to the three realms of existence, passing through all twenty-five forms of existence within them, giving rise to every kind of evil in every place, following the winds of karma without any awareness of it, at times obstructing others from keeping the precepts, cultivating concentration, and cultivating wisdom, at times obstructing others from cultivating merit and cultivating supernormal powers. Faults and obstructions of this kind obstruct the awakening-mind, obstruct the awakening-vow, and obstruct the awakening-conduct. Today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. We disciples, once again with utmost anguish, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, through a mind of greed and craving, we have constructed the six consciousnesses, and, following the six sense-objects, given rise to many faults — whether arising toward living beings or toward what is not a living being, whether arising toward an undefiled person or toward an undefiled dharma. Faults and evils of this kind, arisen from greed and hatred, today we confess, and pray that they be dissolved. And a mind of foolishness has given rise to inverted conduct, trusting in false teachers and accepting false teachings, clinging to annihilationism, clinging to eternalism, clinging to a self, clinging to views, giving rise to immeasurable fault through following the workings of this delusion. Causes and conditions of this kind obstruct the awakening-mind, obstruct the awakening-vow, and obstruct the awakening-conduct. Today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. We disciples, once again with utmost sincerity, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, through the three evil deeds of the body, the four evil deeds of speech, and the three evil deeds of mind, arising from the beginningless afflictions of the ground of ignorance, the afflictions above the countless sands of the river, the afflictions that obstruct calming, the afflictions that obstruct insight, the afflictions of the four abiding-grounds, the three poisons, the four kinds of grasping, the five hindrances, the six cravings, the seven fluxes, the eight defilements, the nine fetters, and the ten latent tendencies — every one of these afflictions and obstructions is immeasurable and boundless, obstructing the awakening-mind, obstructing the awakening-vow, and obstructing the awakening-conduct. Today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. We disciples, once again with utmost sincerity, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, we have been unable to cultivate a mind of loving-kindness and compassion, unable to cultivate a mind of sympathetic joy and equanimity, unable to cultivate the perfection of giving, the perfection of moral discipline, the perfection of patience, the perfection of diligence, the perfection of meditative concentration, or the perfection of wisdom, and unable, moreover, to cultivate any of the practices that assist awakening. Lacking any skillful means in this way, lacking any wisdom, we have obstructed the awakening-mind, obstructed the awakening-vow, and obstructed the awakening-conduct. Today we confess this, and pray that it be dissolved. We disciples, once again with increasing anguish, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, turning through the three realms, passing through all six destinies in full and receiving bodies of every one of the four modes of birth, whether male or female, neither male nor female, we have given rise to immeasurable fault in every place — whether as a great-bodied being devouring and being devoured by others, or as a small-bodied being devouring and being devoured by others. Karma of killing of this kind is immeasurable and boundless, obstructing the awakening-mind, obstructing the awakening-vow, and obstructing the awakening-conduct. Today we confess it, and pray that it be dissolved. We disciples, once again with utmost sincerity, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. Since the very first moment we possessed consciousness until this very day, receiving bodies of all four modes of birth within the six destinies, the evils we have given rise to in between are without end and without limit. Faults of this kind are known and seen in their fullness only by all the Buddhas and great bodhisattvas of the ten directions. Whatever measure of evil the Buddhas and bodhisattvas know and see in us, today, with sincere mind, we bow our heads to the ground, beg for mercy, and confess it in shame; whatever fault we have already committed, we pray that it be entirely dissolved, and whatever fault we have not yet committed, we dare not commit it now. We look up and pray that all the Buddhas of the ten directions, with a mind of great compassion, may accept our confession today, and, with the water of great compassion, wash away every defiling fault that obstructs our awakening, bringing us at last, upon reaching the very seat of awakening, to complete purity. We further pray that all the Buddhas of the ten directions, by their inconceivable power, by the power of their original vows, by their power to deliver living beings, and by their power to shelter and protect living beings, may cause us, the disciples, to vow today to arouse the awakening-mind — and that from this day forward, until we sit at the seat of awakening, this resolve may be established once and for all, never to retreat from it again, every vow we make becoming one with the vows that the bodhisattvas themselves have carried out. We look up and pray that all the Buddhas, great bodhisattvas of the ten directions, with a compassionate mind, may together receive us, so that we, the disciples, may attain the fulfillment of the awakening-vow just as we have wished it, and that every living being may likewise, each one, fully attain and fulfill the awakening-vow.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This chapter's confession is built as a cadence: nearly every paragraph opens with "we disciples, once again," followed by a bodily gesture — prostrating fully to the ground — before naming a new category of fault, and closes with the near-identical refrain "obstructing the awakening-mind, obstructing the awakening-vow, and obstructing the awakening-conduct." The effect, in performance, would have been closer to a drumbeat than to an argument: the repeated physical act of prostration paced against an accumulating inventory of fault, ranging from the doctrinally technical (the four abiding-grounds, the twenty-five forms of existence) to the starkly plain (the mutual devouring of large and small creatures, named here as its own distinct category of killing). This fascicle ends on the very threshold of the awakening-mind rather than fully arriving at it — that chapter belongs to Fascicle 2.
