Fascicle 2

Arousing the awakening-mind, the vow, and the dedication《慈悲道場懺法》卷第二

Having confessed in Fascicle 1, the liturgy now turns outward: a visualization of compassion that widens from one's own family to fill all ten directions, a cascading sequence of vows extended to every living being without exception, and a dedication of merit that closes by naming specific bodhisattvas and exemplary figures as models for every class of person present at the rite.

Genre note
Continuous liturgical prose meant to be recited, not narrative. The traditional chapter titles are preserved as this translation's section headings.
A recurring formula
As in Fascicle 1, short Buddha-name litanies close each unit here, changing in length and content each time rather than repeating a single fixed list. Each is translated in full. "We disciples" translates the ritual placeholder 某甲.

4. Arousing the Awakening-Mind發菩提心第四

Chinese Source

今日道場同業大眾！相與已得洗浣心垢，十惡重障淨盡無餘。業累既遣內外俱潔，次應仰學菩薩修行直道，功德智慧由之而生，所以諸佛每歎發心。是道場能辦事故，唯願大眾各堅其志，莫以年命待時漏盡，勿令空去，後悔無益。相與今日值遇好時，不應日夜煩惱覆心，宜當努力發菩提心。菩提心者即是佛心，功德智慧不可格量，蓋論一念況復多念，假使歷劫修無量福，乃至今生備行餘善，不及發心萬分之一，算數譬喻亦不能盡。又有一人但作福德，不發菩提無上道心，譬如耕田不下種子，既無其芽何處求實？以是義故，須發菩提心因緣為證，上報佛恩下拔一切。所以佛讚諸天子言：「善哉善哉！如汝所說。為欲利益一切眾生發菩提心，是為第一供養如來。又發菩提心非止一過，唯應數發，令菩提心相續不斷。」是以經言「於那由他河沙佛所發大善願」，是知發心其數無量。又發菩提心，但遇善知識便得心發，未必皆須值佛出世。如文殊師利始向菩提，乃因女人以發初心。慧式不惟凡品輕標心志，實由渴仰大乘、貪求佛法，依倚諸經取譬世事，怨親無差、六道一相，願因斯善俱得解脫。若同信解，知非戲論。 今日道場同業大眾！發菩提心必須起想，先緣所親，繫念之時念己父母師長眷屬，又念地獄餓鬼畜生，又念諸天諸仙一切善神，又念人道一切人類，有受苦者當云何救？見已起想應作是念：唯有大心能拔彼苦。若一想成應作二想，二想成已應作三想，三想成已滿一室想，一室成已滿一由旬，一由旬滿已滿閻浮提，閻浮提滿已滿三天下，如是漸廣滿十方界，見東方眾生盡是其父，西方眾生盡是其母，南方眾生悉是其兄，北方眾生悉是其弟，下方眾生悉是姊妹，上方眾生悉是師長，其餘四維悉是沙門婆羅門等。見已作念：若受苦時自作我想，詣諸人所調身按摩，誓拔其苦。得解脫已為其說法，讚佛讚法讚菩薩眾。作是讚已心生歡喜，見其受樂如己無異。今日道場同業大眾！發菩提心應當如是，不捨於苦而度眾生。相與人人等一痛切，五體投地心念口言作是誓願：某甲等從今日去乃至道場，於其中間在所生處，值善知識，常發無上菩提之心。若在三途及墮八難，常使憶念發菩提心，令菩提心相續不斷一拜。 Ru-Yi Translation

Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — together we have already washed away the defilement of the mind, and the heavy obstruction of the ten evils is entirely purified, without remainder. Now that the burden of karma has been dispelled and we are pure both within and without, we should next look up and learn the bodhisattvas' own direct path of practice, from which merit and wisdom are born — and this is why the Buddhas constantly praise the arousing of this mind. Since this very rite is capable of accomplishing such a thing, we pray only that this assembly, each of us, may hold our resolve firm, and not wait until our years and our allotted span run out, letting this chance pass in vain — regret afterward will do no good. We together, today, have met with this excellent occasion, and should not let affliction cover our minds day and night, but should strive diligently to arouse the awakening-mind. The awakening-mind is precisely the Buddha's own mind, its merit and wisdom beyond all measure or comparison — and this is said of a single thought alone, how much more, then, of many thoughts! Even if one were to cultivate immeasurable merit across many kalpas, even performing every remaining good deed in this present life, it would still not equal one ten-thousandth part of arousing this mind — no number or metaphor could ever exhaust the difference. And there is another kind of person who only performs meritorious deeds, without arousing the unsurpassed mind of awakening — this is like tilling a field without sowing any seed: with no sprout, where is the fruit to be found? For this reason, one must arouse the awakening-mind as the very cause and condition that serves as its proof, repaying the Buddha's kindness above and delivering all beings below. This is why the Buddha praised the sons of the gods, saying: "Excellent, excellent! Just as you have said. To arouse the awakening-mind for the benefit of all living beings is the very first offering made to the Tathāgata. And the arousing of the awakening-mind is not a single occurrence — one should arouse it again and again, so that it continues without ever being broken off." This is why the sūtra says, "At the feet of Buddhas as numerous as the sands of nayutas of rivers, they aroused this great and excellent vow" — and from this we know that the number of times the mind is aroused is without measure. Moreover, in arousing the awakening-mind, one need only encounter a good companion for the mind to arise — it is not always necessary to meet a Buddha appearing in the world. Consider how Mañjuśrī himself first turned toward awakening: it was through a woman that he first aroused this mind. It is not that this teaching sets its sight lightly on the ordinary — it is truly because of a thirsting admiration for the Great Vehicle, a hungry seeking after the Buddha-dharma, drawing on the sūtras and taking worldly affairs as its illustration, that friend and enemy make no difference, and the six destinies are seen as one and the same, so that through this very goodness all may attain liberation together. Whoever shares in this same faith and understanding will know that this is no idle talk. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — arousing the awakening-mind requires first giving rise to a visualization, beginning with those closest to us. In the time we hold this thought in mind, we should think of our own parents, teachers, and kin; then think of those in the hells, among the hungry ghosts, and among the animals; then think of the gods, the immortals, and every good spirit; and then think of every kind of person in the human realm — and for whoever is undergoing suffering among them, how should we save them? Having seen this, we should give rise to this very thought: only the great mind can draw out their suffering. Once this single visualization is accomplished, form a second; once the second is accomplished, form a third; once the third is accomplished, let it fill a single room; once it fills a room, let it fill a single yojana; once a yojana is filled, let it fill all of Jambudvīpa; once Jambudvīpa is filled, let it fill the three continents; and expanding it gradually in this way, let it fill the ten directions entirely — seeing that the beings of the east are all one's own father, the beings of the west are all one's own mother, the beings of the south are all one's own elder brothers, the beings of the north are all one's own younger brothers, the beings below are all one's own sisters, the beings above are all one's own teachers, and the beings of the remaining four intermediate directions are all monks and brahmins. Having seen this, give rise to this thought: whenever they undergo suffering, regard it as one's own suffering, go to each of them, tend and massage their bodies, and vow to draw out their suffering. Once they are freed, teach them the Dharma, praising the Buddha, praising the Dharma, and praising the assembly of bodhisattvas. Having offered this praise, let joy arise in the mind, seeing their happiness exactly as though it were one's own. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — this is how one should arouse the awakening-mind: without avoiding suffering in order to deliver living beings. Let us together, every one of us, with this same anguish of resolve, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, and speak with mind and voice, making this vow: we disciples, from this day forward, even to the very seat of awakening, wherever we are born in the meantime, may we encounter good companions, and constantly arouse the unsurpassed awakening-mind; and should we fall among the three evil paths or into any of the eight adverse conditions, may we still constantly be made to recollect and arouse the awakening-mind, so that this awakening-mind may continue without ever being broken off. One bow. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — we should arouse a courageous mind and, with earnest and solemn vows, arouse the awakening-mind. We look up and pray that all the Buddhas, great bodhisattvas, and sages of the ten directions may now bear witness for us, so that every practice and vow of ours, the disciples, may be entirely fulfilled. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — even supposing one practiced many kinds of goodness across many kalpas, one would only attain the flowering reward of birth among humans and gods, not the true fruit that transcends the world, and when that life ends and its merit is exhausted, one falls back into an evil destiny, the body destroyed and suffering pressing in, with no escape of one's own. Unless one establishes a great vow and arouses a vast mind, there is no way to leave behind, once and for all, the decline and affliction of birth and death. Let us together, today, do nothing but hold the Buddhas in mind, arousing a firm resolve to give rise to the awakening-mind. The merit of arousing this mind cannot be measured or reckoned — even the Buddhas and bodhisattvas cannot exhaust it in speech — such a wholesome power is truly inconceivable. How, then, could we fail to apply our whole mind to this single point? The Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra says: "It is like a room darkened for a hundred years — a single lamp can dispel it." Do not think a single thought too slight a thing to be worth the effort. Let us together kneel with palms joined, our single mind reaching out to all the Three Jewels of the ten directions, and speak with mind and voice: we disciples, now before all the Buddhas of the ten directions, before all the venerable Dharma of the ten directions, before all the bodhisattvas of the ten directions, and before all the sages of the ten directions, kneel with palms joined, our minds upright and rightly mindful, arousing an earnest mind, a mind free of heedlessness, a mind settled and abiding, a mind that delights in goodness, a mind that delivers all beings, a mind that shelters and protects all beings, a mind equal to the Buddhas' own — and we arouse the awakening-mind. We disciples, from this day forward, until we sit at the seat of awakening, shall not cling to the mind of humans or gods, shall not give rise to the mind of a disciple-hearer, and shall not give rise to the mind of a pratyekabuddha, but shall give rise only to the mind of the Great Vehicle, the mind that seeks omniscience, the mind that accomplishes unsurpassed, perfect awakening. We pray only that all the Buddhas, great bodhisattvas, and sages throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space, by the power of their original vows, may now bear witness for us, and, by the power of compassion, extend their help and receive us, so that this awakening-mind we arouse today may remain firm, wherever we are born, and never retreat. Should we fall among the three evil paths or into any of the eight adverse conditions, receiving every kind of body within the three realms and undergoing every kind of suffering, hard to bear and hard to endure, we vow never, because of such suffering, to let this great mind we hold today fall away. Rather than lose it, we would gladly enter the great wheel of fire in the hell of unceasing torment and undergo every kind of suffering there; we vow never, because of such suffering, to let this great mind we hold today fall away — for this very mind, this very vow, is equal to the mind of the Buddhas, and one with the vow of the Buddhas. Once again, with utmost sincerity, we bow our heads to the Three Jewels. Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The concentric visualization at the center of this chapter — starting with one's own family, then the suffering of the six destinies, then expanding room by room, yojana by yojana, continent by continent, until the ten directions are filled with beings recast as one's own father, mother, siblings, and teachers — is a concrete meditative technique rather than a metaphor, structurally similar to the classical Buddhist practice of radiating loving-kindness outward in ever-widening circles. Its stated purpose is to make universal compassion feel literally like family feeling rather than an abstract ethical principle, which is precisely the effect the chapter tries to produce before the vows that follow ask the practitioner to bear "the weight of every living being."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Nearly every vow in this chapter follows the same grammatical shape — "we disciples, from this day forward, vow that, in life after life, in every place..." — a structural repetition that this translation preserves rather than varies, since the cumulative, almost incantatory effect of the repeated phrase is part of how the chapter functions as a chant rather than as connected prose argument. Worth noticing, too, is how many of these vows are framed around what other beings will experience upon meeting the practitioner — seeing their form, hearing their voice, hearing their name — rather than what the practitioner will personally attain, a pattern consistent with this liturgy's broader tendency to route even self-directed aspiration through its effect on others.

今日道場同業大眾！已發菩提心竟、已發大誓願竟，次應發迴向心。相與至心等一痛切，五體投地，歸依世間大慈悲父。 南無彌勒佛　南無釋迦牟尼佛　南無日光佛　南無無量寶佛　南無蓮華最尊佛　南無身尊佛　南無金光佛　南無梵自在王佛　南無金光明佛　南無金海佛　南無龍自在王佛　南無一切華香自在王佛　南無樹王佛 南無勇猛執持牢仗棄捨戰鬪佛　南無內豐珠光佛　南無無量香光明佛　南無文殊師利菩薩　南無妙音菩薩　南無無邊身菩薩　南無觀世音菩薩 又復歸依十方盡虛空界一切三寶，願以慈悲力現為我證。某甲等願過去所起一切善業、現前所起一切善業、未來當起一切善業，若多若少、若輕若重，悉以迴施四生六道一切眾生，令諸眾生皆得道心，不向二乘、不向三有，同共迴向無上菩提。又願一切眾生所起善業，若過去、若現在、若未來，各各迴施，不向二乘、不向三有，同共迴向無上菩提。今日道場同業大眾！相與發菩提心竟、發大誓願竟、發迴向心竟，廣大如法性、究竟如虛空，去來現在一切諸佛、諸大菩薩、一切賢聖皆為證明。重復至誠頂禮三寶一拜。某甲等發心發願其事已畢，喜踊無量，重復至心五體投地，奉為： 國王帝主、父母師長，歷劫親緣一切眷屬、善惡知識，諸天諸仙、護世四王，主善罰惡守護持呪五方龍王、龍神八部一切靈祇，過去現在窮未來際一切怨親及非怨親四生六道一切眾生，歸依世間大慈悲父。 南無彌勒佛　南無釋迦牟尼佛　南無師子嚮佛　南無大強精進勇力佛　南無過去堅住佛　南無鼓音王佛　南無日月英佛　南無超出眾華佛　南無世燈明佛　南無休多易寧佛　南無寶輪佛　南無常滅度佛　南無淨覺佛　南無無量寶華明佛　南無須彌步佛　南無寶蓮華佛　南無一切眾寶普集佛　南無法輪眾寶普集豐盈佛　南無樹王豐長佛　南無圍繞特尊德淨佛　南無無垢光佛　南無日光佛 又復歸依過去無數劫諸佛大師海德如來。 敬禮無量無邊盡虛空界無生法身菩薩。 敬禮無量無邊盡虛空界無漏色身菩薩。 敬禮無量無邊盡虛空界發心菩薩。 敬禮興正法馬鳴大師菩薩。 敬禮興像法龍樹大師菩薩。 敬禮十方盡虛空界無邊身菩薩。 敬禮十方盡虛空界救苦觀世音菩薩。 故號如來、應供、正遍知、明行足、善逝、世間解、無上士、調御丈夫、天人師、佛、世尊，度人無量、拔生死苦。以此發心功德因緣，仰願 當今皇帝舊云大梁皇帝、皇太子殿下、國家眷屬，從今日去至于道場，亡身為法如薩陀波崙，大悲滅罪如虛空藏，能遠聽法如瑠璃光，善解難法如無垢藏。又願某甲等所生父母、歷劫親緣，從今日去至于道場，散形空界如無邊身，具十功德如高貴德王，聞法歡喜猶如無畏，神力勇猛如大勢至。又願某甲等和尚、阿闍梨、同學眷屬、上中下座一切知識，從今日去至于道場，各得無畏如師子王，影響大化猶如寶積，聞聲濟苦如觀世音，善能諮問如大迦葉。又願某甲等出家在俗信施檀越、善惡知識各及眷屬，從今日去至于道場，解諸危厄猶如救脫，相貌端嚴猶如文殊，能捨業障如棄陰蓋，設最後供等於純陀。又願諸天諸仙、護世四王、聰明正直天地虛空、主善罰惡守護持呪五方龍王龍神八部、幽顯靈祇各及眷屬，從今日去至于道場，大慈普覆如阿逸多，精進護法如不休息，遠證讀誦猶如普賢，為法焚身猶如藥王。又願十方一切怨親及非怨親四生六道一切眾生各及眷屬，從今日去至于道場，心無愛染如離意女，微妙巧說如勝鬘夫人，能行精進如釋迦文，所有善願等無量壽，所有威神如諸天王，不可思議如維摩詰，一切功德各成就，無量佛土悉莊嚴。仰願十方盡虛空界無量無邊諸大菩薩、一切賢聖，以慈悲心同加攝受，救護拯接、所願圓滿，信心堅固德業日遠，慈育四生等如一子，令諸眾生得四無量心、得六波羅蜜，十受修禪、三願廣被，應念見佛皆如勝鬘，一切行願畢竟成就，等與如來俱登正覺拜。

Ru-Yi Translation

Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — having now finished arousing the awakening-mind, and finished making the great vow, we should next arouse the mind of dedication. Let us together, with sincere mind, in this same anguish of resolve, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, and take refuge in the great and compassionate father of the world. Homage to the Buddha Maitreya. Homage to the Buddha Śākyamuni. Homage to the Buddha of Sunlight. Homage to the Buddha of Boundless Treasure. Homage to the Buddha Most Honored as the Lotus. Homage to the Buddha Honored in Body. Homage to the Buddha of Golden Light. Homage to the Buddha King, Sovereign as Brahmā. Homage to the Buddha of Golden Brightness. Homage to the Buddha of the Golden Sea. Homage to the Buddha King, Sovereign Among Dragons. Homage to the Buddha King, Sovereign over Every Flower and Fragrance. Homage to the Buddha King of the Tree. Homage to the Buddha Who Bravely Grasped a Firm Weapon and Then Cast Away All Strife. Homage to the Buddha of the Inner Abundance of Pearl-Light. Homage to the Buddha of Boundless Fragrant Brightness. Homage to the Bodhisattva Mañjuśrī. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Wondrous Sound. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Boundless Body. Homage to the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. We further take refuge in all the Three Jewels throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space, praying that, by the power of compassion, they may now bear witness for us. We disciples vow that every wholesome deed we have done in the past, every wholesome deed we do now in the present, and every wholesome deed we shall do in the future, whether many or few, whether light or grave, be entirely dedicated and given over to every living being of the four modes of birth and six destinies, so that they may all attain the mind of the Way, turning neither toward the two lesser vehicles nor toward the three realms of existence, but together dedicating it toward unsurpassed awakening. We further vow that every wholesome deed any living being has done, whether in the past, the present, or the future, may likewise, each one, be dedicated, turning neither toward the two lesser vehicles nor toward the three realms of existence, but together dedicated toward unsurpassed awakening. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — together we have now finished arousing the awakening-mind, finished making the great vow, and finished arousing the mind of dedication, vast as the very nature of dharmas, ultimate as empty space itself, and every Buddha, great bodhisattva, and sage of the past, present, and future bears witness to it. Once again, with utmost sincerity, we bow our heads to the Three Jewels. One bow. We disciples, having now completed the arousing of the mind and the making of the vow, our joy leaps beyond measure; once again, with sincere mind, we prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, and dedicate this on behalf of: kings and rulers, our parents and teachers, every kin and family across every kalpa, good and evil companions, the gods and immortals, the Four Heavenly Kings who guard the world, the dragon-kings of the five directions and every numinous spirit among the eightfold host of dragon-spirits who reward good and punish evil, and every being, enemy or friend or neither, of the four modes of birth and six destinies, in the past, the present, and to the very limit of the future — we take refuge in the great and compassionate father of the world. Homage to the Buddha Maitreya. Homage to the Buddha Śākyamuni. Homage to the Buddha of the Lion's Echo. Homage to the Buddha of Great and Powerful, Courageous Strength. Homage to the Buddha of Past Ages, Firmly Abiding. Homage to the Buddha King of the Drum's Sound. Homage to the Buddha of the Splendor of Sun and Moon. Homage to the Buddha Who Surpasses Every Flower. Homage to the Buddha, Lamp of Illumination for the World. Homage to the Buddha of Great and Easeful Peace. Homage to the Buddha of the Jeweled Wheel. Homage to the Buddha of Constant Cessation. Homage to the Buddha of Pure Awakening. Homage to the Buddha of the Brightness of Boundless Jeweled Flowers. Homage to the Buddha Who Strides Like Sumeru. Homage to the Buddha of the Jeweled Lotus. Homage to the Buddha in Whom Every Treasure Universally Gathers. Homage to the Buddha of the Dharma-Wheel, in Whom Every Treasure Universally Gathers in Abundance. Homage to the Buddha King of the Tree, Abundant and Flourishing. Homage to the Buddha Surrounded by Especially Honored and Pure Virtue. Homage to the Buddha of Stainless Radiance. Homage to the Buddha of Sunlight. We further take refuge in the Tathāgata of Ocean Virtue, the great teacher among the Buddhas of countless past kalpas. We reverently bow to the bodhisattvas of the unborn Dharma-body, immeasurable and boundless throughout the whole reach of empty space. We reverently bow to the bodhisattvas of the undefiled form-body, immeasurable and boundless throughout the whole reach of empty space. We reverently bow to the bodhisattvas who have aroused the awakening-mind, immeasurable and boundless throughout the whole reach of empty space. We reverently bow to the great teacher and bodhisattva Aśvaghoṣa, who caused the true Dharma to flourish. We reverently bow to the great teacher and bodhisattva Nāgārjuna, who caused the Dharma to flourish in its semblance age. We reverently bow to the Bodhisattva of Boundless Body throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space. We reverently bow to the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, who rescues beings from suffering, throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space. For this reason he is called Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, Perfect in Wisdom and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed, Tamer of Men, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One — delivering beings without measure, and drawing out the suffering of birth and death. By the merit and cause and condition of arousing this mind, we look up and pray: May the present emperor — an old reading here has "the Emperor of the Great Liang" — the crown prince, and the imperial household, from this day forward, even to the very seat of awakening, forget the body for the sake of the Dharma as Sadāprarudita did; extinguish fault with great compassion as Ākāśagarbha does; be able to hear the Dharma from afar as the Buddha Bhaiṣajyaguru does; and skillfully understand the most difficult teachings as the bodhisattva Vimaladatta does. We further pray that the parents who bore us, the disciples, and our kin across every kalpa, from this day forward, even to the very seat of awakening, may scatter their form throughout empty space as the Bodhisattva of Boundless Body does; be endowed with the ten virtues as King Kauṇḍinya of Noble Virtue was; rejoice on hearing the Dharma as Abhaya does; and be courageous in spiritual power as Mahāsthāmaprāpta is. We further pray that our preceptors, instructors, fellow students and kin, and every companion of high, middle, and low station, from this day forward, even to the very seat of awakening, may each attain fearlessness as the Lion King does; extend a great transformation as Ratnākara does; hear the sound of suffering and relieve it as Avalokiteśvara does; and skillfully inquire as Mahākāśyapa does. We further pray that we, the disciples' fellow monastics and lay followers, faithful donors, and good and evil companions, each with their own kin, from this day forward, even to the very seat of awakening, may resolve every danger and crisis as the Bodhisattva of Rescue and Release does; be dignified and fine of countenance as Mañjuśrī is; be able to cast off the obstruction of karma as the Bodhisattva Who Casts Off the Hindrances does; and offer the very last offering as Cunda did. We further pray that every god and immortal, the Four Heavenly Kings who guard the world, the bright and upright spirits of heaven, earth, and empty space, the dragon-kings of the five directions and the eightfold host of dragon-spirits who reward good and punish evil, and every hidden and manifest numinous spirit, each with their own kin, from this day forward, even to the very seat of awakening, may extend great compassion universally as Ajita does; guard the Dharma with diligence unceasing as Anikṣiptadhura does; bear distant witness through reading and recitation as Samantabhadra does; and burn the body for the sake of the Dharma as the Medicine King does. We further pray that every being of the ten directions, enemy or friend or neither, of the four modes of birth and six destinies, each with their own kin, from this day forward, even to the very seat of awakening, may hold a mind free of craving and attachment as the woman Vimuktacittā does; speak with subtle and skillful eloquence as Lady Śrīmālā does; practice diligence as Śākyamuni himself did; hold every wholesome vow equal to Amitāyus; hold every awesome spiritual power equal to the heavenly kings; be inconceivable as Vimalakīrti is; each accomplish every merit; and adorn every buddha-land without limit or measure. We look up and pray that the immeasurable and boundless great bodhisattvas and every sage throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space, with a compassionate mind, may together receive us, rescue and draw us to safety, and bring every wish to complete fulfillment, our faith made firm and our virtuous work growing ever further, nurturing the four modes of birth with compassion as though each were our only child, causing every living being to attain the four immeasurable minds and the six perfections, to receive and cultivate meditation in the ten ways, and to have the three vows extended broadly, so that whenever they think of it they may behold the Buddha exactly as Lady Śrīmālā did, every practice and vow ultimately fulfilled, ascending together with the Tathāgatas to true and equal awakening. One bow.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The closing passage of this chapter names a specific bodhisattva or exemplary figure as a model for each group present at the rite in turn — the ruling household, one's own parents, one's teachers and fellow practitioners, the lay and monastic community, the protective spirits, and finally all beings without exception — a structure that makes the liturgy's court origins visible in the text itself. The phrase this translation renders as "an old reading here has 'the Emperor of the Great Liang'" reflects a textual variant already present in the Taishō apparatus: later copies of this liturgy, performed at courts long after the Liang dynasty had ended, generalized "the Emperor of the Great Liang" into "the present emperor" so the rite could be recited for whichever ruler currently reigned, while at least one surviving witness preserves the original, specific wording — a small but concrete trace of how a court-commissioned rite was adapted for repeated use across later centuries and dynasties.
