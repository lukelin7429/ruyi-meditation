Fascicle 6

Confessing body, speech, and mind — and opening the vow《慈悲道場懺法》卷第六・解怨釋結（之餘）・發願第四（初）

Having spent the whole of Fascicle 5 dissolving grudges owed to specific others — parents, teachers, fellow students — this fascicle turns the same argument inward, confessing enmity created through the confessor's own three doors of action: the body, speech, and mind. Each confession is closed with its own roll call of buddhas and bodhisattvas and a shared dedication of merit, before the fascicle ends by opening the liturgy's next chapter, Making Vows, with a vow for every being's deliverance and a set of first-person vows for the confessor's own future across each of the six destinies.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1909 Chapters: 解怨釋結第三（之餘）・發願第四（初） Status: complete
Chinese
《慈悲道場懺法》卷第六 — the sixth of ten fascicles, opening by completing the chapter Dissolving Grudges and Enmities (解怨釋結) begun in Fascicle 5, then closing with the opening lines of the fourth chapter, Making Vows (發願).
Covers
A systematic confession of enmity created through the body (killing and cruelty toward animals and people, fraud committed while posing as a physician, arrogance rooted in birth, learning, artistic skill, wealth, or eloquence), through speech (harsh, divisive, false, and idle speech, extending to judging one's own sovereign, parents, and teachers), and through the mind (an extended treatise on anger as "the brigand that plunders merit," citing the 華嚴經 Avataṃsaka Sūtra's catalog of the obstructions a single thought of hatred brings) — each confession closed with its own roll call of buddhas and bodhisattvas; then the shift into Making Vows, opening with a vow for every being's deliverance and a series of first-person vows for the confessor's own rebirth in each of the six destinies, should liberation not yet be attained.
Why it matters
This fascicle marks a structural pivot in the liturgy: Fascicle 5 dissolved enmity owed to named others, while this one turns the same practice into an audit of the confessor's own conduct, sorted by the three classical doors of karma. The digression on anger, anchored in a direct citation of the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, is the liturgy's most sustained treatment of a single affliction anywhere in the first half of the text.
Difficulty
★★★☆☆ — the confession sections read as long, itemized catalogs of specific offenses rather than continuous argument, while the closing vow passage shifts abruptly into vivid, concrete first-person imagery (birth as a mountain animal, a hungry ghost, a guardian spirit) that rewards close reading.

1. Confessing the Karma of the Body就身業懺六道怨結

Chinese Source

今日道場同業大眾！先向四生六道懺身惡業。經言：有身則苦生，無身則苦滅。而此身者眾苦之本，三途劇報皆由身得，未見他作我受、我作他受，自作其因，自受其果。若一業成罪無邊際，何況終身所起惡業。今唯知有我身、不知有他身，唯知有我苦、不知有他苦，唯知我求安樂、不知他亦求安樂，以愚癡故起吾我心、生怨親想，所以怨對遍於六道。若不解結，於六道中何時免離？從劫至劫豈不痛哉。相與今日起勇猛心、起大慚愧，作大懺悔，必使一念感十方佛、一拜斷除無量怨結。等一痛切五體投地，歸依世間大慈悲父。 南無彌勒佛　南無釋迦牟尼佛　南無月面佛　南無寶燈佛　南無寶相佛　南無上名佛　南無作名佛　南無無量音佛　南無違藍佛　南無師子身佛　南無明意佛　南無無能勝佛　南無功德品佛　南無月相佛　南無得勢佛　南無無邊行佛　南無開華佛　南無淨垢佛　南無見一切義佛　南無勇力佛　南無富足佛　南無福德佛　南無隨時佛　南無廣意佛　南無功德敬佛　南無善寂滅佛　南無財天佛 南無大勢至菩薩　南無常精進菩薩　南無無邊身菩薩　南無觀世音菩薩 又復歸依如是十方盡虛空界一切三寶。 願以佛力、法力、諸菩薩力、一切賢聖力，令四生六道一切眾怨同到道場，各各懺謝。心念口言作如是說：某甲等從無始無明住地已來至于今日，以身惡業因緣，或於天道、人道起諸怨結，或於阿脩羅道、地獄道起諸怨結，或於餓鬼道、畜生道起諸怨結。願以佛力、法力、諸菩薩力、一切賢聖力，令四生六道三世眾怨，若對非對、若輕若重，以今懺法，所懺除滅、所悔清淨，三界苦果永不復受，在所生處常值諸佛。又復今日同懺悔者，從無始生死已來至于今日，以身惡業因緣，於惡道中備起怨結，或以瞋恚、或以貪愛、或以愚癡，從三毒根造十惡行，好殺禽獸斷牛羊等，或為田業、或為舍宅、或為錢財更相殺害。又無始已來至于今日，或為利養謬刺眾生、或欺妄作醫針灸百姓。如是等罪怨對無量，今日懺悔，願乞除滅。又無始已來至于今日，或飢餓眾生、或奪人糧食、或逼眾生醎苦、或斷人水漿，如是種種惡業怨對，今日懺悔，願乞除滅。又無始已來至于今日，或殺害眾生噉食其肉、或縱三毒鞭打眾生、或以毒食飼殺眾生。如是怨對無量無邊，今日懺悔，願乞除滅。又無始已來至于今日，遠離明師親近惡友，從身三業起種種罪，肆情殺害抂夭無辜，或發撤湖池壅塞溝渠惱害水性諸餘細蟲，或焚燒山野、或設網張羅，水陸眾生備加殺害。如是怨對無量無邊，今日懺悔，願乞除滅。又無始已來至于今日，無慈悲心乖平等行，斗秤欺誑侵凌下劣，或破他城邑抄掠劫奪、或偷盜他財以自供給，無有誠信更相殺害。如是怨對無量無邊，今日懺悔，願乞除滅。又無始已來至于今日，無慈悲行，在六道中於一切眾生備加楚毒，或鞭打眷屬不以其道、或繫或縛鎖械幽閉、或考掠側立刺射傷毀、或斬截殘害剝炙燒煮。如是怨對無量無邊，今日懺悔，願乞除滅。又無始已來至于今日，身三惡業、口四惡業、意三惡業，四重五逆、諸餘不善無不備作，自恃年命不畏鬼神，唯恐我不勝人、人莫及我，或以華門望族傲物凌人作如是怨，或以識達凌人傲物作如是怨，或以篇章技藝凌人傲物作如是怨，或以誇豪奢侈凌人傲物作如是怨，或以辯口利辭凌人傲物作如是怨。如是眾怨，或於尊像福田邊起、或於和尚闍梨邊起、或於同住上中下座邊起、或於同學眷屬邊起、或於父母親戚邊起。如是怨對無量無邊，今日懺悔，願乞除滅。又無始已來至于今日，或經於天道、人道起諸怨結，或於阿脩羅道、地獄道起諸怨結，或於畜生道、餓鬼道乃至十方一切眾生邊起諸怨結。如是罪惡無量無邊，今日懺悔，願乞除滅。某甲等又復無始已來至于今日，或為嫉妬、或為諂曲自求升進、或為名譽、或為利養，隨逐邪見無有慚愧。如是怨結若輕若重，罪因苦果數量多少，唯有諸佛菩薩盡知盡見。諸佛世尊當慈念我，若我前生從無始生死已來，所作眾罪、若教他作、見作隨喜，若三寶物自取、教他取、見取隨喜，或有覆藏、或不覆藏。如諸佛菩薩所知所見罪量多少，應墮地獄、餓鬼、畜生及諸惡趣，邊地下賤受怨對者，今皆懺悔，願乞除滅。諸佛神力不可思議，願以慈悲心救護一切，受某甲等今日向四生六道、父母師長、一切眷屬，懺悔往罪解怨釋結。願令六道怨對各各歡喜，一切捨施無怨親想，一切無礙猶如虛空。從今日去至于菩提，結習煩惱畢竟斷除，三業清淨眾怨永盡，天宮寶殿隨意往生，四無量心六波羅蜜常能修行，百福嚴身萬善具足，住首楞嚴三昧得金剛身，以一念頃遍應六道，更相濟度使無遺餘，同坐道場成等正覺。

Ru-Yi Translation

Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — let us first confess, before the four modes of birth and the six destinies, the evil karma of the body. The sūtra says: where there is a body, suffering arises; where there is no body, suffering ceases. And this body is the very root of every suffering — the harsh retributions of the three evil paths are all obtained through it. We have never seen another act and ourselves bear the result, or ourselves act and another bear the result: each creates his own cause and each receives his own fruit. If a single deed already produces a fault without limit, how much more the evil karma accumulated over an entire lifetime? Now we know only that we ourselves have a body, and do not know that others too have a body; we know only that we ourselves suffer, and do not know that others too suffer; we know only that we ourselves seek ease, and do not know that others too seek ease — and because of this very folly, a sense of "I" arises, and with it the notion of enemy and friend, and this is why enmity spreads throughout the six destinies. If we do not resolve these bonds, when shall we ever be free of them within the six destinies? From kalpa to kalpa — is this not truly painful? Together, today, let us arouse a courageous mind, arouse a profound sense of shame, and undertake a great confession, so that a single thought may move the Buddhas of the ten directions, and a single bow may cut off immeasurable bound enmity. With this same anguish of resolve, let us prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, and take refuge in the great and compassionate father of the world. Homage to the Buddha Maitreya. Homage to the Buddha Śākyamuni. Homage to the Buddha of the Moon's Face. Homage to the Buddha of the Jeweled Lamp. Homage to the Buddha of the Jeweled Mark. Homage to the Buddha of Supreme Renown. Homage to the Buddha of Accomplished Renown. Homage to the Buddha of Boundless Voice. Homage to the Buddha Vilamba. Homage to the Buddha of the Lion's Body. Homage to the Buddha of Bright Intent. Homage to the Buddha Unconquerable. Homage to the Buddha of the Ranks of Merit. Homage to the Buddha of the Moon's Mark. Homage to the Buddha of Attained Power. Homage to the Buddha of Boundless Conduct. Homage to the Buddha of the Blossoming Flower. Homage to the Buddha Purified of Stain. Homage to the Buddha Who Perceives Every Truth. Homage to the Buddha of Courageous Power. Homage to the Buddha of Abundance. Homage to the Buddha of Blessed Virtue. Homage to the Buddha Who Accords with the Season. Homage to the Buddha of Vast Intent. Homage to the Buddha Revered for Merit. Homage to the Buddha of Wholesome Quiescence. Homage to the Buddha of Heavenly Wealth. Homage to the Bodhisattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta. Homage to the Bodhisattva Ever Diligent. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Boundless Body. Homage to the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. We further take refuge in all the Three Jewels throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space in this way. We pray that, by the power of the Buddha, the power of the Dharma, the power of all the bodhisattvas, and the power of all the sages, every enmity of the four modes of birth and six destinies may together arrive at this very rite, each to confess and make amends. Let us speak with mind and voice, saying: we disciples, from the beginningless ground of ignorance until this very day, through the causes and conditions of the evil karma of the body, have given rise to grudges and enmities in the heavenly destiny and the human destiny, in the asura-destiny and the hell-destiny, in the hungry-ghost destiny and the animal-destiny. We pray that, by the power of the Buddha, the power of the Dharma, the power of all the bodhisattvas, and the power of all the sages, every enmity of the three periods of time throughout the four modes of birth and six destinies, whether the debt is repaid or not, whether light or grave, may, through this rite of confession, have whatever is confessed dissolved and whatever is repented made pure, so that we never again undergo the bitter fruit of the three realms, and wherever we are born, may we constantly encounter the Buddhas. And further, we who confess together today, from beginningless birth and death until this very day, through the causes and conditions of the evil karma of the body, have given rise to grudges and enmities in full throughout the evil destinies — whether through hatred, through craving, or through folly, from the root of the three poisons we have committed the ten evil deeds, delighting in killing birds and beasts, slaughtering cattle and sheep and the like, or killing one another over farmland, over a dwelling, or over money and wealth. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, whether for the sake of profit we have wrongly lanced living beings, or, posing falsely as physicians, needled and cauterized the common people — faults and enmities of this kind are without measure; today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, whether we have starved living beings, seized another's food and grain, forced bitter and briny food upon living beings, or cut off another's water and drink — evil karma and enmity of every such kind, today we confess, and pray that it be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, whether we have killed living beings and eaten their flesh, given free rein to the three poisons and whipped living beings, or fed living beings poisoned food to kill them — enmity of this kind is immeasurable and boundless; today we confess it, and pray that it be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, having kept far from wise teachers and drawn close to evil companions, we have, through the three karmas of the body, committed every kind of fault, giving free rein to our feelings and killing, cutting short the lives of the innocent; or we have drained lakes and ponds, blocked up ditches and channels, tormenting the tiny creatures that dwell in water, or set fire to mountains and open fields, or spread nets and snares, inflicting killing upon every creature of water and land alike — enmity of this kind is immeasurable and boundless; today we confess it, and pray that it be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, without a compassionate mind, going against the practice of equanimity, we have cheated others with false measures and scales, encroaching upon and oppressing the weak; or we have breached another's city or town to raid and plunder it, or stolen another's wealth to provide for ourselves, having no honesty or trust, and killing one another because of it — enmity of this kind is immeasurable and boundless; today we confess it, and pray that it be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, without the practice of compassion, we have, throughout the six destinies, inflicted cruelty upon every living being in full — whipping our own family and dependents without cause, binding and shackling others, locking them away, torturing them, forcing them to stand aslant, stabbing, shooting, wounding, and maiming them, or cutting, mutilating, flaying, roasting, and boiling them — enmity of this kind is immeasurable and boundless; today we confess it, and pray that it be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, through the three evil deeds of the body, the four evil deeds of speech, and the three evil deeds of mind, the four grave offenses and the five heinous crimes, and every other unwholesome deed, none left uncommitted, relying on our own years and station and fearing neither ghost nor spirit, anxious only that we might not surpass another, that no one might equal us — whether through a flowering gate and an illustrious lineage we have looked down on others and trampled them with arrogance, giving rise to enmity in this way; or through learning and understanding; or through literary composition and skill; or through boasting of wealth and extravagance; or through eloquence and sharp speech — in each such way looking down on others and trampling them with arrogance, giving rise to enmity. Enmity of every such kind may have arisen toward a revered image or a field of blessing, toward a preceptor or instructor, toward a fellow resident of high, middle, or low station, toward a fellow student or family member, or toward a parent or relative. Enmity of this kind is immeasurable and boundless; today we confess it, and pray that it be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, whether we have given rise to grudges and enmities in the heavenly destiny or the human destiny, in the asura-destiny or the hell-destiny, or in the animal-destiny or the hungry-ghost destiny, extending even to every being of the ten directions — faults and evils of this kind are immeasurable and boundless; today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. We disciples, further, from beginningless time until this very day, whether out of envy, or through flattery and deceit in seeking our own advancement, or for the sake of reputation, or for the sake of profit, have followed after false views without any sense of shame. Grudges and bonds of this kind, whether light or grave, and however great in number their causes of fault and fruits of suffering, are known and seen in their entirety only by the Buddhas and bodhisattvas. May the Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, look upon us with compassion. Whatever fault we have committed in our past lives, from beginningless birth and death until now — whether we ourselves committed it, instructed another to commit it, or rejoiced upon seeing it committed; whether we took the property of the Three Jewels ourselves, instructed another to take it, or rejoiced upon seeing it taken; whether concealed or unconcealed — whatever measure of such fault the Buddhas and bodhisattvas know and see, however much it deserves that we fall into the hells, the hungry ghosts, the animals, and the other evil destinies, or be born in a border land in a lowly station and bear such enmity — all of it we confess today, and pray that it be dissolved. The Buddhas' spiritual power is inconceivable; we pray that, with a compassionate mind, they rescue and protect all beings, and receive our confession today, we disciples, on behalf of the four modes of birth and six destinies, our parents, teachers, and every family member, confessing our past faults and dissolving every grudge and enmity. We pray that every enmity throughout the six destinies may each find joy, that all may be given up entirely with no notion of enemy or friend remaining, and that all may be unhindered as empty space itself. From this day forward, until we reach awakening, may the habituated affliction of these bonds be cut off once and for all, the three karmas made pure and every enmity forever exhausted, may we be born at will into the heavenly palace's jeweled halls, constantly able to cultivate the four immeasurable minds and the six perfections, our very body adorned with a hundred blessings and every goodness fully complete, abiding in the Śūraṅgama-samādhi and attaining the vajra-body, so that within a single thought we may respond throughout the six destinies at once, delivering one another without exception, and together take our seat at this awakening-site, accomplishing true and equal awakening.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

What stands out in this catalog of bodily offense is how concrete and agrarian it is: draining ponds and blocking irrigation channels, setting fire to open fields to clear land, cheating with false weights and scales, posing as an unqualified physician for profit. This is not an abstract taxonomy of sin but a confession shaped around the specific harms of a sixth-century landholding society, extended outward from human victims to the "tiny creatures that dwell in water" caught in the drained ponds. The five-fold list of arrogance — pride of birth, of learning, of artistic skill, of wealth, and of eloquence — is similarly precise: it reads as a deliberate inventory of every socially respectable ground on which one person might look down on another.

Ru-Yi Translation

Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — together we have now confessed the fault of the body. Yet though the karma of the body is purified, the remaining fault of speech is itself the gateway to every enmity and calamity. This is why the Buddhas caution us against divisive speech, harsh speech, false speech, and flowery speech. We should know that flattering, deceitful, and ornamented words, spun to stir up discord, bring a truly grave retribution once spoken. When a person lives in the world harboring poisonous thoughts, uttering poisonous words, and committing poisonous deeds, these three things inflict harm upon living beings. Once poisoned, living beings form grudges and resentment, vowing in their hearts to take revenge — some obtaining their wish in this very life, others following through only after death. Enmity of this kind fills all six destinies, each side retaliating against the other without end, all of it truly earned through the workings of past lives, never obtained without cause. We should know that body and speech are truly the very source of every evil. For one living as a layperson, indulging in this brings a loss of filial devotion, and after death, entrance into Mount Tai brings the cruelty of boiling cauldrons and flames; for one who has left home, taking no delight in the Buddha-dharma, wherever he is born he will constantly meet with evil. Enmities of this kind all draw their strength from the three karmas, and among the three karmas, the karma of speech is truly the gravest — its retribution, once received, brings every kind of cruel torment, unrecognized and unknown through a night impossible to comprehend. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — the reason we turn through the six destinies is entirely because of the karma of speech: whether through light and reckless words, glib and clever phrases, frivolous and false embellishment, our speech and conduct at odds with one another, we bring this retribution upon ourselves, unable to escape it through many kalpas. How could each of us fail to tremble at this, and strive all the more to confess and wash away this fault? Together, since we first possessed consciousness until this very day, our karma of speech has been unwholesome — toward the four modes of birth and six destinies, our parents, teachers, and every family member, there is no evil we have not proclaimed: speaking with coarse roughness, uttering words of slander and violence, gathering with companions to speak idle and meaningless things, pointing to emptiness and calling it existence, pointing to existence and calling it empty, saying we have seen what we have not seen and have not seen what we have seen, saying we have heard what we have not heard and have not heard what we have heard, saying we have done what we have not done and have not done what we have done — inverted in this way, turning heaven upside down and exchanging earth for sky, benefiting ourselves at the expense of others and slandering one another, speaking of our own virtues we leave none unclaimed, while speaking of another's faults we leave none unmentioned, going so far as to appraise the sages and worthies, to judge our own sovereign and father, to mock our teachers and masters, and to turn our backs on good and wise friends — lawless, without principle, with no regard for the harm it does. In this world, such things bring hidden calamity, injury to the body, and loss of life; in the world to come, they bring bitter pain and retribution through endless kalpas. In the mere space of a jest or a laugh, one can already accumulate immeasurable grave fault — how much more, then, when bitter words are inflicted upon another deliberately? We, together, from beginningless time until this very day, through this evil karma of speech, whether we bear enmity in the heavenly destiny or the human destiny, in the asura-destiny or the hell-destiny, in the hungry-ghost destiny or the animal-destiny, or whether we bear enmity toward our parents, teachers, or any family member — we disciples, with a compassionate mind, in the same conduct and the same vow as the bodhisattvas, universally offer this on their behalf, taking refuge and paying reverent homage to the great and compassionate father. From the beginningless ground of ignorance until this very day, through the causes and conditions of the evil karma of speech, we have given rise to grudges and enmities in full throughout the six destinies; we pray that, by the spiritual power of the Three Jewels, every enmity of the three periods of time throughout the four modes of birth and six destinies may, through what we confess, be cut off and dissolved, and through what we repent, be extinguished forever. Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The line that gives this section its edge is the claim that "in the mere space of a jest or a laugh, one can already accumulate immeasurable grave fault." The text is not primarily worried about calculated slander — it names that too — but about the far more common case of idle, unthinking speech: gossip, exaggeration, offhand mockery, the kind of talk that feels weightless in the moment it is spoken. By placing "judging one's own sovereign and father" and "mocking one's teachers" in the same breath as frivolous jesting, the passage refuses to let the confessor treat careless speech as a lesser category of fault simply because no harm was intended.

Chinese Source

今日道場同業大眾！相與已得懺身口罪竟，次復應須清淨意業。一切眾生輪轉生死不得解脫者，皆由意業結集牢固。十惡五逆必由意造，故佛誡言：不得貪欲瞋恚、愚癡邪見，後墮地獄受苦無窮。今日相與共見心之驅役諸識，亦猶君之總策其臣，口發惡言、身行重逆，於六道中能招劇報。當知滅身事由心造，今欲改悔，先挫其心、次折其意。何以故爾？經言：制之一處，無事不辦。當知潔心是解脫之本，清淨意業則進趣有期，三途劇報不來、惡道苦受不往。然身口業麁易遣，意地微細難除。如來大聖一切智人，於身口意始得不護，況凡惑愚夫而不守慎，若不折挫未見其善。經云：防意如城，守口如瓶。相與無始世界已來及此一形，無明起愛增長生死，亦能具足十二苦事，八邪八難、三途六道，輪迴流轉無不經歷。如是諸處受無量苦，皆由意業搆起怨對，念念攀緣未曾暫捨，扇動六情、馳役五體，輕重惡業無不備造。或身口不遂心增忿毒，更相殺害無憐愍心。若微有痛癢不可抑忍，比至在他唯恐楚毒不深。見人之過意願宣說，自有愆失不喜他聞。有如是心，實可慚愧。又意地起瞋大道怨賊，所以經言：劫功德賊無過瞋恚。又《華嚴經》云「佛子若起一瞋恚心，一切惡中無過此惡。」何以故爾？起一瞋恚心則受百千障礙，所謂不見菩提障、不聞法障、生惡道障、多疾病障、被謗毀障、生闇鈍障、失正念障、少智慧障、近惡知識障、不樂賢善障、遠正見障，離佛正教入魔境界，不見善知識，諸根不具，乃至生惡業家、生於邊地，如是等障不可具說。我等無始已來至于今日，已有無量無邊瞋恚惡心，乃至起瞋不避親族，何況六道諸眾生等，及其煩惱猛毒不復自知。但事不得為，心想何所不念；若使得遂心意，則誰不被困。故天子一怒伏屍萬里，降斯已還自空紛擾，鞭楚捶縛有諸罪苦。當此之時，何處應言我違善戒，唯恐苦酷不深不重。是意地惡通於有識，智愚不免、豪賤共有，未嘗一日慚愧改悔。今日道場同業大眾。瞋恚苦惱意慮情深，雖復欲捨對境，以發動與惡俱，念念相觸，何時當得免離斯苦？大眾相與既知其罪，豈得晏然而不改悔？相與今日懇到披誠懺滅此罪，宜各人人等一痛切，五體投地，歸依世間大慈悲父。 Ru-Yi Translation

Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — together we have now finished confessing the fault of body and speech; next we should purify the karma of the mind. Every living being who turns through birth and death without attaining liberation does so entirely because the karma of the mind is bound together and firmly fixed. The ten evils and the five heinous crimes are, without exception, fashioned by the mind — this is why the Buddha cautions us: do not give way to craving, hatred, folly, and false views, or afterward you will fall into the hells and undergo suffering without end. Today let us together recognize how the mind drives and commands the various consciousnesses, just as a sovereign directs and commands his ministers — when the mouth utters evil words and the body commits grave transgression, this is what invites severe retribution throughout the six destinies. We should know that the ruin of the body is fashioned by the mind, and now, wishing to reform, we must first subdue the mind and then discipline its intent. Why is this so? The sūtra says: control it in a single place, and there is nothing that cannot be accomplished. We should know that a purified mind is the very root of liberation, and that once the karma of the mind is made pure, our advance toward the goal has a fixed term — the harsh retribution of the three evil paths will not come, and the bitter suffering of the evil destinies will not be met. And yet, while the karma of body and speech is coarse and easily set aside, the ground of the mind is subtle and difficult to remove. Even the Tathāgata, the great sage, the one who knows all things, only attained the state where body, speech, and mind needed no further guarding after long cultivation — how much more, then, should the ordinary, confused, and foolish person fail to guard and take care! Without discipline and restraint, no good result can be found. The sūtra says: guard the mind as one guards a city wall, and keep watch over the mouth as one keeps watch over a sealed jar. Together, since beginningless time and throughout this very lifetime, ignorance has given rise to craving, increasing birth and death, so that we have fully undergone the twelve kinds of suffering, the eight wrong paths, the eight adversities, the three evil paths, and the six destinies, turning and flowing through every one of them without exception. Suffering without measure, undergone in every one of these places, is entirely brought about by the karma of the mind constructing enmity — thought after thought clinging to its objects without ever once being set aside, stirring up the six faculties and driving the five limbs, so that evil karma both light and grave is committed without exception. When body and speech fail to accomplish what the mind wants, resentment and poison only increase, and we kill one another without any mind of pity. If we feel even the slightest discomfort ourselves, we cannot bear it — yet when it comes to another, we fear only that the cruelty inflicted is not severe enough. Seeing another's fault, we wish eagerly to proclaim it; having faults of our own, we do not wish others to hear of them. To harbor a mind such as this is truly a matter for shame. Moreover, hatred arising in the ground of the mind is the great brigand upon the path — this is why the sūtra says: among the thieves that plunder our merit, none surpasses hatred. And the Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "Son of the Buddha, should you give rise to a single thought of hatred, among all evils, none surpasses this evil." Why is this so? Giving rise to a single thought of hatred, one incurs a hundred thousand obstructions — namely, the obstruction of not seeing awakening, the obstruction of not hearing the Dharma, the obstruction of being born in an evil destiny, the obstruction of frequent illness, the obstruction of being slandered and defamed, the obstruction of being born dull and dim-witted, the obstruction of losing right mindfulness, the obstruction of having little wisdom, the obstruction of drawing close to evil companions, the obstruction of taking no delight in the wise and good, and the obstruction of straying far from right view — departing from the Buddha's own correct teaching and entering the realm of demons, never meeting a good and wise friend, one's own faculties incomplete, extending even to being born into a family steeped in evil karma or being born in a border land — obstructions of this kind cannot all be told. We ourselves, from beginningless time until this very day, have already harbored immeasurable and boundless hatred and evil-mindedness, going so far as to give rise to anger even against our own kin without restraint — how much more, then, against every being of the six destinies! And once caught in the fierce poison of this affliction, we no longer recognize it in ourselves; it is only that we lack the means to carry it out — is there anything the mind does not conceive? Were we able to accomplish whatever the mind desired, who would not be brought to ruin by it? This is why, when the Son of Heaven grows angry once, corpses lie strewn across a thousand miles; and short of that extreme, empty turmoil and disorder still bring whipping, beating, and binding, and every manner of crime and suffering. At such a time, who would think to say, "I have violated a wholesome precept" — fearing only that the suffering inflicted is not deep or severe enough? This evil in the ground of the mind runs through every being possessed of consciousness — the wise and the foolish alike cannot escape it, the noble and the base alike possess it — and never for even a single day do we feel shame over it and resolve to reform. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — the suffering of hatred runs deep in thought and feeling, and though we may wish to abandon the object that provokes it, the very arising of the mind comes bound together with this evil, thought after thought coming into contact with it — when, then, shall we ever be free of this suffering? This assembly, together, already knowing this fault, how could we remain at ease and fail to reform? Together, today, with earnest and open sincerity, let us confess and extinguish this fault; let each of us, with this same anguish of resolve, prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, and take refuge in the great and compassionate father of the world. Homage to the Buddha Maitreya. Homage to the Buddha Śākyamuni. Homage to the Buddha Who Owes Nothing to Anyone. Homage to the Buddha of the Moon's Mark. Homage to the Buddha of the Lightning's Mark. Homage to the Buddha of Reverence. Homage to the Buddha, Guardian of Majestic Virtue. Homage to the Buddha, Sun of Wisdom. Homage to the Buddha of the Supreme Domain. Homage to the Buddha of the Summit of Sumeru. Homage to the Buddha Who Subdues the Brigand of Enmity. Homage to the Buddha of the Lotus. Homage to the Buddha Worthy of Praise. Homage to the Buddha Who Knows the Proper Order. Homage to the Buddha Free of Pride. Homage to the Buddha Nārāyaṇa. Homage to the Buddha of Eternal Joy. Homage to the Buddha of the Great Domain. Homage to the Buddha Renowned in Heaven. Homage to the Buddha Who Sees the Boundary of Existence. Homage to the Buddha Truly Good. Homage to the Buddha of Abundant Merit. Homage to the Buddha of the Jeweled Moon. Homage to the Buddha of the Lion's Mark. Homage to the Buddha, Delight in Meditation. Homage to the Buddha Lacking Nothing. Homage to the Buddha at Ease in Play. Homage to the Bodhisattva, Lion at Play. Homage to the Bodhisattva of the Lion's Vigorous Leap. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Boundless Body. Homage to the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. We further take refuge in all the Three Jewels throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space in this way. We pray that, by the power of compassion and boundless, unhindered mastery, our confession today may be received, we disciples, on behalf of the four modes of birth and six destinies, our parents, teachers, and every family member, confessing every enmity arisen from the mind, whether the debt is repaid or not, whether light or grave — may whatever enmity has already arisen be confessed and dissolved, and may we not dare to commit what has not yet arisen. We look up and pray that, by the power of the Three Jewels, we may together be received, pitied, and sheltered, and brought to liberation. We disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, through the causes and conditions of the evil karma of the mind, have given rise to every kind of enmity, whether light or grave, toward the four modes of birth and six destinies, our parents, teachers, and every family member; today, in shame, we lay it bare in confession, and pray that every such enmity be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, relying on the root of the three poisons, we have given rise to a mind of craving, and through the compulsion of craving have committed the karma of craving, whether hidden or manifest, throughout the whole Dharma-realm of empty space — toward what belongs to another, giving rise to the evil thought, "I ought to take this for myself," extending even to the possessions of our parents and teachers, the possessions of every family member, the possessions of every living being, and the possessions of every god and immortal — thinking, in every such case, that it ought to belong to us. Faults and evils of this kind are immeasurable and boundless; today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, we have given rise to the karma of hatred, burning day and night, without a moment's respite, flying into great rage over the smallest displeasure, inflicting every kind of torment upon living beings — whether beating them with whip and rod, drowning them, or starving, suspending, binding, and imprisoning them. Faults of hatred and enmity of this kind are without measure; today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, following after ignorance, we have given rise to the karma of folly, leaving no evil uncommitted and possessing no right wisdom, believing false words and accepting false teachings. Enmity created by this karma of folly is immeasurable and boundless; today we confess it, and pray that it be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, walking the ten wrong paths, we have left no enmity unbound and no karma uncommitted, thought after thought clinging to its objects without ever once being set aside, stirring up the six faculties and giving rise to every bound karma — whether, when body and speech failed to accomplish what we wished, our mind grew all the more poisonous and fierce, or, through mere jest and laughter, we stirred up discord, never dealing with others with an honest mind, forever harboring flattery and deceit with no sense of shame. Faults of this kind are immeasurable and boundless, bringing great suffering and affliction throughout the six destinies; today we confess them, and pray that they be dissolved. We disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, through the unwholesome karma of body, the unwholesome karma of speech, and the unwholesome karma of mind — evil karma of this kind — have given rise to every obstruction of fault toward the Buddha, every obstruction of fault toward the Dharma, and every obstruction of fault toward all the bodhisattvas and sages. Obstructions of fault of this kind are immeasurable and boundless; today, with utmost sincerity, we beg for mercy in confession, and pray that they be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, through the three evil deeds of the body, the four of speech, and the three of mind, the five heinous crimes and the four grave offenses, there is no fault we have not committed. Today we confess it, and pray that it be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, through the six faculties, the six sense-objects, and the six consciousnesses, deluded and inverted in thought, clinging to every object, we have created every kind of fault. Today we confess it, and pray that it be dissolved. And further, from beginningless time until this very day, we have frequently transgressed and violated the precept of upholding dignified deportment, the precept of upholding wholesome dharmas, and the precept of upholding living beings, so that when the body perishes and life ends, we fall into the three evil destinies — in the hells, undergoing suffering immeasurable and boundless, as many as the sands of the Ganges; or falling among the hungry ghosts, without any awareness at all, forever bearing hunger and thirst and suffering heat and cold; or falling among the animals, undergoing immeasurable suffering, our food and drink impure, afflicted by hunger, cold, and hardship; or being born again among humans, falling into a family of false views, our mind forever given to flattery and deceit, believing false words and losing the right path, sinking into the sea of birth and death with no time of escape ever in sight. Every evil and enmity of the three periods of time cannot be counted or reckoned, and only the Buddhas know and see it in its entirety. Whatever measure of retribution for fault the Buddhas know and see in us, today we confess it, and pray that it be dissolved. We pray that, by the great power of compassion of all the Buddhas, their great spiritual power, and their power to tame living beings as the Dharma teaches, our confession today, we disciples, may cause every enmity to be dissolved at once. Whether the six destinies and four modes of birth have already received such requital today or have not yet received it, we pray that, by the great power of compassion of the Buddhas, the great bodhisattvas, and all the sages, this enmity may be liberated once and for all. From this day forward, until we reach awakening, may every obstruction of fault be purified once and for all; may we give up birth in the evil destinies and attain birth in the pure land; give up the life bound by enmity and attain the life of wisdom; give up the body bound by enmity and attain the vajra-body; give up the suffering of the evil destinies and attain the joy of nirvāṇa; and, mindful of the suffering of the evil destinies, arouse the awakening-mind, so that the four immeasurables and six perfections may constantly manifest before us, the four eloquences and six supernormal powers at ease and unhindered, courageous and diligent without rest, advancing in cultivation until the practice of the ten grounds is complete, and returning once more to deliver every boundless living being.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This section is unusual within the liturgy for pausing to cite a named source outside the confession's own liturgical formulas: the Avataṃsaka Sūtra's line that a single thought of hatred is worse than any other evil, followed by an eleven-item list of the specific obstructions it brings. The illustration that follows — that a sovereign's single fit of rage can leave "corpses strewn across a thousand miles," while an ordinary person's rage merely produces beating and binding — is a pointedly political image of scale: the text is not arguing that the powerful are more prone to anger, but that anger is the same fault in everyone, magnified only by how much power is behind it.

4. Opening the Chapter of Making Vows發願第四（初）

Chinese Source

今日道場同業大眾！過去現在四生六道，窮未來際一切眾生，願以今懺法同得清淨、同得解脫，具足智慧、神力自在。願諸眾生，從今日去至于菩提，常見十方盡虛空界諸佛法身，常見諸佛三十二相紫磨之身，常見諸佛八十種好分形散體遍滿十方救眾生身，常見諸佛眉間白毫大光普照濟地獄苦。又願今日道場同業大眾，以今懺悔清淨功德因緣，從今日去捨身受身，不經地獄道鑊湯鑪炭割炙之苦，不經餓鬼道烊銅灌口焦爛之苦，不經畜生道剝裂楚毒之苦，若在人道不經四百四病觸身之苦、不經大熱大寒難耐之苦、不經刀杖毒藥加害之苦、不經飢渴困乏之苦。又願大眾從今日去，奉戒清淨無點污心，常修仁義、念報恩心，供養父母如視世尊，奉敬師長如對諸佛，敬重國王如真法身，於餘一切皆如己想。又願大眾從今日去乃至菩提，達深法義智無所畏，明解大乘了見正法，即自開解不由他悟，一向堅固志求佛道，還度無邊一切眾生，等與如來俱成正覺。 今日道場幽顯大眾賜為證明，今日微願某甲等正願，願生聖人所居之處，常能建立道場興顯供養，為一切眾生作大利益，常蒙三寶慈悲攝受，常有勢力化導得行，常修精進不著世樂，知一切法空，於非怨親同以善化，乃至菩提心無退轉。從今日去，一毫之善悉資願力。某甲等又願若生人中生修善家，更立道場供養三寶，一毫之善悉施一切。願與和上闍梨不相捨離，自然蔬食、絕愛染心不須妻子，忠信清直仁恕和平，損己濟物不求名利。某甲等又願若捨此身不蒙解脫生鬼神中，願為大力護法善神、濟苦善神，不須衣食自然溫飽。某甲等又願捨此身命不蒙解脫墮畜生中，常處深山食草飲水，無諸苦事，出則為瑞不被籠縶。某甲等又願捨此身命不蒙解脫墮餓鬼中，願身心安樂無諸熱惱，化諸同苦皆令悔過、發菩提心。某甲等又願捨此身命不蒙解脫墮在地獄，自識宿命，化諸同苦皆令悔過發菩提心。某甲等亦自憶菩提心，令菩提心相續不斷。仰願十方一切諸佛、大地菩薩、一切聖人，以慈悲心現為我證。又願諸天諸仙、護世四王、主善罰惡守護持呪五方龍王、龍神八部同為證明，重復至誠歸依三寶。 故號如來、應供、正遍知、明行足、善逝、世間解、無上士、調御丈夫、天人師、佛、世尊，度人無量，拔生死苦。以今懺悔清淨讚佛功德因緣，願四生六道一切眾生，從今日去至于菩提，以佛神力隨心自在。

Ru-Yi Translation

Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — for every living being of the four modes of birth and six destinies, past and present, extending to the very limit of the future, we pray that, through this rite of confession, all may together attain purity and together attain liberation, fully endowed with wisdom and unhindered spiritual power. We pray that every living being, from this day forward until reaching awakening, may constantly behold the Dharma-body of the Buddhas throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space; may constantly behold the Buddhas' body of the thirty-two marks, radiant as purple gold; may constantly behold the Buddhas' body of the eighty excellent characteristics, its form divided and scattered to fill the ten directions in order to rescue living beings; and may constantly behold the great light of the white curl between the Buddhas' brows, universally shining to deliver beings from the suffering of the hells. We further pray that this assembly gathered together today, through the merit and causes and conditions of this pure confession, may, from this day forward, in giving up one body and taking up the next, never undergo the suffering of the hell-destiny's boiling cauldrons, burning coals, and flaying and roasting; never undergo the suffering of the hungry-ghost destiny's molten copper poured down the throat and its burning and festering; never undergo the suffering of the animal-destiny's flaying and cruel torment; and, should we be born in the human destiny, never undergo the suffering of the four hundred and four diseases that afflict the body, never undergo the unbearable suffering of extreme heat and extreme cold, never undergo the suffering of harm inflicted by blade, staff, and poison, and never undergo the suffering of hunger, thirst, and want. We further pray that this assembly, from this day forward, may uphold the precepts in purity with a mind free of every stain, constantly cultivating benevolence and righteousness and mindful of repaying kindness, honoring our parents as we would look upon the World-Honored One, revering our teachers and masters as we would face the Buddhas themselves, honoring the sovereign as the very Dharma-body itself, and regarding everyone else exactly as we regard ourselves. We further pray that this assembly, from this day forward until reaching awakening, may penetrate the profound meaning of the Dharma with a wisdom that fears nothing, clearly understand the Great Vehicle and perceive the true Dharma, awakening to it of ourselves rather than through another's instruction, single-mindedly resolved and firm in seeking the path of buddhahood, returning once more to deliver every boundless living being, and, together with the Tathāgatas, accomplishing true and equal awakening. Today, we ask the visible and unseen assembly gathered at this rite to bear witness: today, in this humble vow, we disciples make our sincere resolve. We vow to be born in the very place where the sages dwell, forever able to establish this rite and make offerings there in splendor, working great benefit for every living being, constantly receiving the compassionate embrace of the Three Jewels, constantly possessed of the strength to teach and transform others so that they may practice, constantly cultivating diligence without attachment to worldly pleasure, knowing that all dharmas are empty, and, toward those who are neither enemy nor friend, transforming them alike with goodness, our awakening-mind never once turning back. From this day forward, may even the smallest fragment of goodness be entirely dedicated to the power of this vow. We disciples further vow that, should we be born again among humans, born into a family devoted to wholesome practice, we may establish this rite once more and make offerings to the Three Jewels, giving even the smallest fragment of goodness entirely to all. We vow never to be separated from our preceptors and instructors, naturally living on a vegetarian diet, cutting off every attached and tainted thought and having no need of spouse or family, faithful, upright, benevolent, forbearing, and at peace, sacrificing ourselves to benefit others and seeking neither fame nor gain. We disciples further vow that, should we give up this body without attaining liberation and be born among the ghosts and spirits, we may become a spirit of great strength that protects the Dharma, a beneficent spirit that relieves suffering, needing no clothing or food, naturally warm and well-fed. We disciples further vow that, should we give up this body and life without attaining liberation and fall among the animals, we may forever dwell deep in the mountains, eating grass and drinking water, free of every hardship, and, when we appear, be taken as an auspicious sign rather than caught and caged. We disciples further vow that, should we give up this body and life without attaining liberation and fall among the hungry ghosts, our body and mind may be at ease and untroubled by any burning affliction, and that we may transform every being sharing our suffering, causing them all to repent their past faults and arouse the awakening-mind. We disciples further vow that, should we give up this body and life without attaining liberation and fall into the hells, we may know our own past lives, and transform every being sharing our suffering, causing them all to repent their past faults and arouse the awakening-mind. We disciples also vow to remember the awakening-mind ourselves, so that this awakening-mind may continue without a break. We look up and pray that all the Buddhas of the ten directions, the great bodhisattvas, and all the sages, with a compassionate mind, appear now to bear witness for us. We further pray that the gods and immortals, the Four Heavenly Kings who guard the world, the dragon kings of the five directions who uphold spells and reward good while punishing evil, and the eightfold division of dragon spirits, may together bear witness, as once again, with utmost sincerity, we take refuge in the Three Jewels. This is why the Buddha is titled: Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly Enlightened, Complete in Understanding and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed, Tamer of Those to Be Tamed, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One — delivering beings without measure and drawing them out of the suffering of birth and death. Through the merit and causes and conditions of this pure confession and praise of the Buddha's virtue, we pray that every living being of the four modes of birth and six destinies, from this day forward until reaching awakening, may, by the Buddha's spiritual power, be at ease and free just as they wish.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The grammar shifts here in a way worth noticing: everything up to this point in the fascicle has been confession spoken on behalf of others as much as oneself, but the vows in this closing passage are relentlessly first-person and concrete — not a general wish for good rebirth, but a specific fallback plan for each of the six destinies individually, down to living in the mountains "eating grass and drinking water" if reborn an animal, or becoming a guardian spirit that "needs no clothing or food" if reborn among ghosts. The chapter has only just opened here; the fuller argument and the closing set of vows tied to the eye, ear, and other senses belong to the fascicles still ahead.
