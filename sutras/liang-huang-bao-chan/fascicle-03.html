This fascicle opens a new movement in the liturgy, turning from confession itself to the mechanics of retribution: a dense catalog matching specific fortunes and deformities to specific past deeds, followed by a full quotation of an entire canonical sūtra in which the Buddha answers, one by one, a bodhisattva's questions about exactly which faults produce exactly which sufferings.

A quoted sūtra
Most of this fascicle is not the liturgy's own composition but an embedded quotation of an entire other sūtra, structured as a series of questions from the Bodhisattva Xinxiang and the Buddha's answers. This translation renders the quoted sūtra in full, exactly as the liturgy itself incorporates it.
Content note
This fascicle catalogs physical deformity, disease, and disfigurement as karmic retribution for specific past faults, in direct, unsparing language. This reflects the source text's own genre and rhetorical method rather than any embellishment in translation.

Chinese Source

顯果報第一　出地獄第二　解怨釋結第三　發願第四 今日道場同業大眾！前已具述罪惡過患，以過患故乖於勝業。以不善業，所以墜墮三途備歷惡趣，及生人間受諸苦報，皆由過去宿對因緣，捨身受身無暫停息。是以諸佛諸大菩薩，神通天眼見三界內一切眾生，福盡隨業墮於苦處。見無色界樂著定心，不覺命終墮於欲界，以福盡故受禽獸形。色界諸天亦復如是，從清淨處墮在欲界，既在不淨還受欲樂，六天福盡退墮地獄，於地獄中受無量苦。又見人道，就人道中以十善力資得人身，惡緣雜染復有多苦，壽盡多墮諸惡趣中。又見畜生道一切眾生受諸苦惱，鞭杖驅馳負重致遠困苦疲劇，項領穿破熱鐵燒烙。又見餓鬼常苦飢渴，恒被火燒猶如劫盡，若無微善永不解脫。有片福者劣得人身，多病短命以自莊嚴。大眾當知，善惡二輪未曾暫輟，果報連環初無休息，貧富貴賤隨行所生，非有無因而妄招果。所以經言「為人豪貴國王長者，從禮事三寶中來。為人大富，從布施中來。為人長壽，從持戒中來。為人端正，從忍辱中來。為人勤修無有懈怠，從精進中來。為人才明遠達，從智慧中來。為人音聲清徹，從歌詠三寶中來。為人潔淨無有疾病，從慈心中來。為人長大，恭敬人故。為人短小，輕蔑人故。為人醜陋，喜瞋恚故。生無所知，不學問故。為人顓愚，不教他故。為人瘖瘂，謗毀人故。為人下使，負他債故。為人醜黑，遮佛光明故。生在裸國，輕衣搪揬勝己故。生馬蹄國，著屐勝己前行故。生穿胸國，布施作福悔惜心故。生麞鹿中，驚怖人故。生墮龍中，喜調戲故。身生惡瘡，鞭撻眾生故。人見歡喜，見人歡喜故。喜遭縣官，籠繫眾生故。聞說法語於中兩舌亂人聽受，後墮耽耳狗中。聞說法語心不飡采，後生長耳驢中。慳貪獨食，墮餓鬼中，出生為人，貧窮飢餓。惡食飼人，後墮猪㹠蜣蜋之中。劫奪人物，後墮羊中，人生剝皮食噉其肉。喜偷盜人，後生牛馬，為人下使。喜作妄語傳人惡者，死入地獄烊銅灌口，拔出其舌以牛耕之，罪畢得出生鴝鵅中，人聞其聲無不驚怖，皆言變怪呪令其死。喜飲酒醉，後墮沸屎泥犁之中，罪畢得出生猩猩中，猩猩業畢後得為人，頑無所知人不齒錄。貪人力者，後生象中，夫處富貴，為人上者鞭杖捶打告訴無地。如是等人死入地獄，數千萬歲受諸苦報。從地獄出墮水牛中，貫穿鼻口挽船牽車，還復受彼大杖打扑償往宿殃。為人不淨，從猪中來。慳貪不恕己者，從狗中來。佷戾自用，從羊中來。為人輕躁不能忍事，從獼猴中來。身體鯹臭，從魚鱉中來。為人含毒，從蛇中來。人無慈心，從虎狼中來。」 今日道場同業大眾，人生世間多病短命，種種痛苦不可具說，皆由三業搆造所得，能令行人嬰三途報。所以有三途者，人有三毒，貪、恚、愚癡。又復三惡，口常言惡、心常念惡、身常行惡。以此六事，能滅人身，常苦常惱無有休息。於此命終孤魂獨逝，慈親孝子不能相救，倏忽之間到閻羅所。地獄獄卒不問尊卑，但案罪錄撿挍生時善惡多少，神識自首不敢隱匿。以是因緣，隨業至趣苦樂之地，身自當之。杳杳冥冥別離長久，道路不同會見無期。又諸天神記人善惡，乃至毛髮無片遺漏，善人行善獲倍億壽，惡人行惡命短苦長。如是輪轉又墮餓鬼，從餓鬼脫生畜生中，罪苦難忍受之無竟。 今日道場同業大眾！各自覺悟起慚愧心。經言「作善得善，作惡得惡。」而五濁惡世不可作惡，善不失善報、為惡自招殃，莫言輕脫。立此懺法，經言「莫輕小善以為無福，水滴雖微漸盈大器，小善不積無以成聖。莫輕小惡以為無罪，小惡所積足以滅身」。大眾當知，吉凶禍福皆由心作，若不作因亦不得果。殃積罪大肉眼不見，諸佛所說，誰敢不信？我等相與生世強健，復不勤學自力行善，臨窮方悔亦何所及。今已共見一切過患，如經所說，自知其罪，豈得不思捨惡從善。今生若復不能用心，判捨此形必墮地獄。何以知之？今見為罪之時，未甞不以含毒猛烈懷恨深重，若瞋一人必欲令死，若嫉一人惡見其好，若毀一人必陷苦處，若鞭一人窮天楚毒。忿恚暴害不避尊卑，惡罵醜言無復高下，聲震若雷、眼中火現。至於為福之時，善心微劣，始欲為多末遂減少，初欲速營續後且住。心既不至，日月推遷，如是進退遂就忘失。是為作罪之時心氣剛強，為福之時志意劣弱。今以弱善之因，求離強惡之報，豈可妄得？經云「懺悔，無罪不滅。」每至懺悔之時，必須五體投地如大山崩，此云不惜身命，為滅罪因緣故，殷勤督勵。某甲等相與各省：今生已來已曾幾過作此忿責？不惜身命捍勞忍苦作此懺悔，暫時禮拜，已言氣力不堪。或暫端坐，復言應須消息。或言四體不可過勞，宜應將養，不可使困。一伸脚眠，差如不死，何處復憶我應禮佛、掃塔塗地，辦所難辦？且經教所明，未見一善從懶墮懈怠中生，亦未曾見有一善法從憍慢自恣中得。某甲等今日雖有其形，心多背道。何以知然？今試撿挍：從旦至中、從中至暮、從暮至夜、從夜至曉，乃至一時一刻一念一頃，無有片心念三寶四諦、無有片心報父母恩、無有片心報師長恩、無有片心欲布施持戒忍辱精進、無有片心欲學禪定修智慧業，清白之法無一可論，煩惱重障森然滿目。若不作此撿察，亦自言我功德不少有小片善。而生恃賴，言我能作他不能作，言我能行他不能行，意氣高傲傍若無人。追此而言實可羞恥。今於大眾前披誠發露懺悔眾罪，願布施歡喜，將來無障。大眾亦宜自浣身心，果報之徵具如向說，豈得自寬不求捨離。大眾莫言：我無是罪。我既無罪，何須懺悔？若有此念，願即除滅。且幾微小失已成大咎，瞥然之恨瞋恚便起，性與習成難可改革。心不可縱、意不可逞，若能抑忍則煩惱可除，如其怠惰未見濟度。某甲等今日仰承諸佛慈悲念力、諸大菩薩本誓願力，說罪業報應教化地獄經，宜各靜慮一心諦聽。

Ru-Yi Translation

[Contents of this cycle:] Explaining the Fruits of Retribution, first. Deliverance from the Hells, second. Dissolving Grudges and Enmities, third. Making the Vow, fourth. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — we have already set out in detail the faults and calamities of evil, and it is because of these calamities that one turns away from the excellent path. Because of unwholesome karma, one falls among the three evil paths, passing through every evil destiny in full, and even when born among humans, still receives every kind of bitter retribution — all of this comes from the causes and conditions of past enmities, discarding one body and taking up another without a moment's rest. This is why the Buddhas and great bodhisattvas, with the heavenly eye of their supernormal power, see that every living being within the three realms, once their merit is exhausted, falls according to their karma into a place of suffering. They see how those in the formless realm, attached to the bliss of concentration, do not notice when their life ends and they fall into the realm of desire, receiving, once their merit is spent, the form of a bird or beast. The gods of the form realm are just the same: falling from their pure abode into the realm of desire, and, once there amid impurity, receiving again the pleasures of desire, until the merit of the six heavens is exhausted and they fall back into the hells, undergoing immeasurable suffering there. They see, too, how in the human realm, people obtain a human body through the power of the ten wholesome deeds, yet, mixed with evil conditions, still endure many sufferings, and when their life ends, most fall again among the evil destinies. They see every living being in the animal realm undergoing suffering and affliction — driven on by whip and rod, bearing heavy loads over great distances, exhausted and in misery, their necks worn through and branded with hot iron. They see the hungry ghosts, forever suffering hunger and thirst, constantly burned as though the kalpa itself were ending, and unless they have some small store of merit, they will never find release. Those with even a fragment of merit obtain a human body of the poorest kind, marked by frequent illness and a short life. This assembly should know: the two wheels of good and evil have never ceased turning even for a moment, the chain of retribution has never once rested, and poverty, wealth, nobility, and lowliness are all born according to one's own conduct — no effect is ever wrongly obtained without a cause. This is why the sūtra says: "To be born a person of noble rank, a king or an elder, comes from serving and honoring the Three Jewels. To be born a person of great wealth comes from giving. To be born a person of long life comes from keeping the precepts. To be born a person of fine appearance comes from patience. To be born a person diligent and never idle comes from perseverance. To be born a person of far-reaching wisdom comes from wisdom itself. To be born a person of clear and resonant voice comes from chanting praise of the Three Jewels. To be born a person clean and free of illness comes from a mind of loving-kindness. To be born tall comes from showing respect to others; to be born short comes from despising others. To be born ugly comes from a habit of anger; to be born ignorant comes from never studying; to be born foolish comes from never teaching others; to be born mute comes from slandering others; to be born a servant of low station comes from owing debts to others; to be born dark and unsightly comes from blocking the Buddha's light. To be born in the land of the naked comes from mocking those better dressed than oneself. To be born in the land of the horse-hoofed comes from wearing shoes to walk ahead of one's betters. To be born in the land of the pierced chest comes from giving with a heart of regret. To be born among the river-deer comes from startling people. To be born among the dragons comes from a habit of mockery. To bear an evil sore on the body comes from whipping living beings; to be one whom people are glad to see comes from being glad to see others. To be repeatedly hauled before officials comes from caging living beings. To hear the Dharma spoken and, through divisive talk, disrupt others' listening, and later be reborn as a floppy-eared dog; to hear the Dharma spoken and pay it no heed in the mind, and later be born as a long-eared donkey. To be stingy and eat alone leads to the hungry-ghost realm, and if then born human, to poverty and hunger. To feed others spoiled food leads to rebirth as a pig, a hog, or a dung beetle. To plunder others' possessions leads to rebirth as a sheep, whose skin is stripped and flesh eaten by people once reborn human. To delight in stealing from others leads to rebirth as an ox or a horse, made to serve others as a beast of burden. To delight in spreading false and malicious rumors about others leads, at death, to hell, where molten bronze is poured down the throat and the tongue is pulled out and plowed by an ox; once that fault is exhausted, one is reborn as a shrike, whose cry no one hears without being seized by terror, calling it an omen and cursing it to die. To delight in drunkenness leads, after death, to the hell of boiling excrement and mire; once that fault is exhausted, one is reborn as an orangutan, and once that karma too is exhausted, one may become human again, but dull and ignorant, counted for nothing by others. To covet the strength of others' labor leads to rebirth as an elephant; to be wealthy and of high station and yet whip and beat those beneath one, leaving them with no one to appeal to — such a person, at death, enters the hells and undergoes retribution there for thousands upon thousands of years, and upon leaving hell falls among the water buffalo, its nose and mouth pierced through, hauling boats and pulling carts, still receiving heavy blows from the rod, repaying the calamity of former days. To be unclean as a person comes from having been a pig; to be stingy and grudging toward oneself comes from having been a dog; to be stubborn and self-willed comes from having been a sheep; to be flighty and unable to bear with things comes from having been a macaque; to have a fishy-smelling body comes from having been a fish or a turtle; to harbor poison in oneself comes from having been a snake; to be without compassion comes from having been a tiger or a wolf." Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — people living in this world, beset with much illness and a short life, suffering pains of every kind beyond full description, all of this is brought about by the three kinds of karma we construct, which can cause a practitioner to be caught up in the retribution of the three evil paths. And the reason there are three evil paths is that a person has three poisons — greed, hatred, and folly — and also three evils: the mouth constantly speaking evil, the mind constantly thinking evil, and the body constantly doing evil. These six things together can destroy a person, bringing constant suffering and constant affliction without rest. When such a life comes to its end, the lone spirit departs by itself, and no loving parent or filial child can come to its rescue; in an instant it arrives before King Yama. The jailers of hell make no distinction between noble and humble, but only check the record of one's fault, examining how much good and evil was done in life, and the consciousness itself must confess, not daring to conceal anything. Because of this, one follows one's karma to a place of suffering or joy, and must bear it in one's own person. In that dim and hidden place, the separation is long, and since the roads no longer meet, there is no fixed time to see one another again. The gods and spirits, moreover, record every good and evil deed of a person, down to the smallest hair, missing nothing. The good person who does good obtains a life a hundredfold longer; the evil person who does evil has a short life and long suffering. Turning in this way, one falls further among the hungry ghosts, and, released from that state, is born among the animals, the bitter suffering of fault hard to bear and without end. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — each of us should awaken to this and arouse a mind of shame. The sūtra says: "Doing good obtains good; doing evil obtains evil." And in this world of the five turbidities, one must not do evil — good never loses its good reward, and evil brings calamity upon itself; do not say it can be shrugged off lightly. In establishing this rite of confession, the sūtra says: "Do not belittle a small good as bringing no merit — though a drop of water is tiny, it gradually fills a great vessel; without accumulating small goods, one cannot become a sage. Do not belittle a small evil as bringing no fault — the accumulation of small evils is enough to destroy the very body." This assembly should know that good fortune and misfortune, blessing and calamity, are all made by the mind; if the cause is not made, the effect cannot be obtained either. Yet accumulated calamity and great fault are invisible to the fleshly eye — how, then, could anyone dare disbelieve what the Buddhas themselves have taught? We ourselves, living in this world in health and strength, still fail to study diligently or exert our own effort toward doing good, and when the end draws near, only then do we repent — but what good can that do? Now that we have together seen every one of these faults and calamities, exactly as the sūtra describes, and know our own fault for ourselves, how could we fail to think of casting off evil and turning to good? If, in this present life, we still cannot apply our minds to this, and let go of this body without deciding otherwise, we shall surely fall into the hells. How do we know this? Consider: whenever we are in the act of committing a fault, we are never without a fierce and poisonous hatred, deep and severe resentment — if angry at one person, we wish that person dead outright; if jealous of one person, we resent seeing their good fortune; if we slander one person, we wish to see them cast into suffering; if we whip one person, we do so with a cruelty that reaches to heaven itself. In our rage and violence we spare neither the exalted nor the humble; in our cursing and foul language we make no distinction of rank — our voice roars like thunder, and fire seems to flash in our eyes. Yet when it comes time to do good, our wholesome mind is faint and feeble: we begin meaning to do much, but end up doing less and less; we begin intending to act quickly, but soon put it off again. Since the mind never fully arrives at the task, the days and months slip by, and in this back-and-forth we end up forgetting it altogether. This is why, in the moment of committing a fault, the mind's energy is fierce and strong, while in the moment of doing good, the will is feeble and weak. Now, seeking to escape a strong and evil retribution by means of such a weak and feeble goodness — how could this ever succeed? The sūtra says: "Through confession, there is no fault that cannot be dissolved." Whenever we come to the moment of confession, we must prostrate ourselves fully to the ground like a great mountain collapsing — this means not begrudging body or life, for the sake of the cause and condition that dissolves fault, urging and encouraging ourselves earnestly. Let us, the disciples, each examine ourselves together: since this present life began, how many times have we already reproached ourselves in just this way? Not begrudging body or life, enduring hardship and bearing suffering, we perform this confession — and yet, after only a brief period of bowing, we already say our strength cannot bear it; after sitting upright only a short while, we say again that we should rest; or we say the four limbs cannot be overworked, and ought to be nursed and not driven to exhaustion. The moment we stretch out to sleep, we feel as good as if we would never die — where, then, is there room to remember that we ought to bow to the Buddha, sweep the stūpa, and plaster its floor, accomplishing what is difficult to accomplish? And yet, as the sūtra's teaching makes clear, no single good has ever been seen to arise from laziness and idleness, nor has any single wholesome dharma ever been obtained through arrogance and self-indulgence. We, the disciples, though we possess this human form today, our minds turn against the Way in many ways. How do we know this is so? Let us now examine ourselves: from morning to noon, from noon to evening, from evening to night, from night to dawn, even in a single hour, a single moment, a single thought, a single instant — is there ever a fragment of mind given to the Three Jewels or the Four Noble Truths? A fragment of mind given to repaying our parents' kindness? A fragment of mind given to repaying our teachers' kindness? A fragment of mind wishing to give, to keep the precepts, to practice patience, to be diligent? A fragment of mind wishing to study meditative concentration and cultivate wisdom? Of pure and wholesome dharmas, there is not one worth mentioning, while the heavy obstruction of affliction stands thick and visible everywhere we look. And if we do not examine ourselves in this way, we are quick to say instead that our own merit is not small, that we have some small share of goodness — and, relying on this, grow complacent, saying, "I can do what others cannot," saying, "I can practice what others cannot practice," our manner proud and arrogant, as though no one else were present. Looking back on this, it is truly a thing to be ashamed of. Today, before this assembly, we bare our sincerity and lay open every fault in confession, praying that it be forgiven with joy, so that no obstruction remains for the future. This assembly should likewise wash its own body and mind clean; the signs of retribution have already been fully described as above — how could we indulge ourselves and not seek to leave it behind? Let none of us say: I have no such fault, so why should I need to confess? Should such a thought arise, we pray that it be dissolved at once. For even the smallest, most trifling fault can already become a great transgression; a fleeting resentment can already give rise to anger, and once nature and habit are formed together, they are hard to reform. The mind must not be indulged, the will must not be given free rein — if we can restrain and endure, then affliction can be removed; but if we remain idle, we shall see no deliverance. We, the disciples, today, relying upon the compassionate mindfulness-power of the Buddhas and the power of the original vows of the great bodhisattvas, shall now expound the Sūtra on the Requital of Sinful Karma, Teaching and Transforming through the Hells. Let each of us calm our thoughts and listen closely with a single mind.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The catalog of correspondences in this section — noble birth from honoring the Three Jewels, muteness from slander, a fishy-smelling body from having once been a fish — belongs to a distinct genre of karma literature that reasons backward from observed condition to inferred past cause, item by item, rather than arguing the principle abstractly. The long psychological passage that follows, on why the mind is "fierce and strong" in the moment of committing a fault but "feeble and weak" in the moment of doing good, is one of this liturgy's more introspective passages — less a doctrinal claim than an observation about motivation that a modern reader may recognize regardless of their view on karma itself.

The World-Honored One, seeing that the time had come, and knowing how earnestly the bodhisattvas had entreated him, then released a ray of light from the white curl between his brows, illuminating the immeasurable worlds of the ten directions, so that the hells were stilled and their suffering brought to peace. Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The Buddha's mother-and-infant parable, answering the condemned beings' plea that he remain in the world forever, is one of the more tender moments embedded within this otherwise unrelenting catalog of retribution — the argument being that a permanently present teacher would be taken for granted exactly as a child takes an ever-present mother for granted, and that impermanence itself, paradoxically, is what makes practice urgent rather than optional. The quoted sūtra closes, as named sūtras conventionally do, with its own title given by the Buddha and a note on the assembly's attainment upon hearing it — signaling clearly, within the text itself, where this embedded quotation ends and the liturgy's own voice resumes.

今日道場同業大眾！如佛所說，大可怖畏。相與今日起怖畏心、起慈悲心，承諸佛力行菩薩道，念地獄苦發菩提心，當為今日現受地獄道苦一切眾生、現受餓鬼道苦一切眾生、現受畜生道苦一切眾生，乃至六道現受苦者，一心一意為其禮懺，令此眾生悉得解脫。我等若不勤行方便轉禍為福者，則於一一地獄皆有罪分。相與至心，當念父母師長親戚眷屬未來應受苦報，亦念自身未來現在方嬰此苦。等一痛切五體投地，至誠懇惻至到用心，願令一念感十方佛，一拜斷除無量眾苦。若六道中已受苦者，以佛力法力賢聖力，令此眾生即蒙解脫。若六道中未受苦者，以佛力法力諸賢聖力，令此眾生永得斷除。從今日去，畢竟不復墮於惡趣，除三障業隨念往生，滅五怖畏自在解脫，勤修道業不休不息，妙行莊嚴過法雲地，入金剛心成等正覺。 今日道場同業大眾！重復用心攝耳諦聽，善思念之。《雜藏經》說：時有一鬼，白目連言：「我兩肩有眼、胸有口鼻而無有頭。何罪所致？」目連答言：「汝前世時恒作魁膾弟子，若殺人時汝常歡喜，以繩結挽。以是因緣故受此罪。此是華報，果在地獄。」復有一鬼，白目連言：「我此身形常如塊肉，無有手足眼耳鼻等，恒為虫鳥之所食噉。如是苦痛難堪難忍，何罪所致？」目連答言：「汝前世時常與他藥，令諸眾生命不全活，以是因緣故獲斯罪。此是華報，果在地獄。」 復有一鬼，白目連言：「我腹極大、咽喉如針，窮年卒歲不得飲食。何罪所致？」目連答言：「汝前世時作聚落主，自恃豪貴飲酒縱橫，輕欺他人，奪其飲食、飢困一切，以是因緣故獲斯罪。此是華報，果在地獄。」 復有一鬼，白目連言：「我一生來，有二熱鐵輪在兩腋下，舉身焦爛。何罪所致？」目連答言：「汝前世時與眾作餅，盜取二番挾兩腋下，以是因緣故獲斯罪。此是華報，果在地獄。」復有一鬼，白目連言：「我常以物自幪籠頭，畏人來殺心常怖懼。何罪所致？」目連答言：「汝前世時婬犯外色，常畏人見，或畏夫主捉縛打殺，常懷恐怖故受此罪。此是華報，果在地獄。」 今日道場同業大眾！如經所說，豈得不人人生大怖畏。相與無始已來至于今日，已應作如是無量罪惡。如是等罪皆因無明，無慈悲心，以強𣣋弱傷害眾生，作如是罪乃至盜竊他物，迷惑失道、讒謗賢善，作種種罪。如是罪報，於惡道中必受其苦。今日至心等一痛切五體投地，普為六道已受苦者未受苦者求哀禮懺，奉為父母師長一切眷屬求哀禮懺，亦為自身求哀禮懺，已作之罪願乞除滅，未作之罪不敢復作。唯願世間大慈悲父： 南無彌勒佛　南無釋迦牟尼佛　南無蓮華尊豐佛　南無淨寶興豐佛　南無電燈幡王佛　南無法空燈佛　南無一切眾德成佛　南無賢幡幢王佛　南無一切寶緻色持佛　南無斷疑拔欲除冥佛　南無意無恐懼威毛不豎佛　南無師子佛　南無名稱遠聞佛　南無法名號佛　南無奉法佛　南無法幢佛 南無大勢至菩薩　南無常精進菩薩　南無不休息菩薩　南無虛空藏菩薩　南無無邊身菩薩　南無觀世音菩薩 以大慈大悲救護六道現受苦當受苦一切眾生，令此眾生即得解脫。以神通力斷除惡道及地獄業，令諸眾生從今日去至于道場，畢竟不復墮於惡趣，捨苦報身得金剛身，四等六度常得現前，四辯六通如意自在，勇猛精進不休不息，乃至進修滿十地行，還復度脫一切眾生。

Ru-Yi Translation

Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — as the Buddha has taught, this is truly a thing to be feared. Let us together, today, arouse a mind of dread and arouse a mind of compassion, and, relying on the power of the Buddhas, walk the bodhisattva path; recollecting the suffering of the hells, let us arouse the awakening-mind, and, on behalf of every living being now suffering in the hells, every living being now suffering among the hungry ghosts, every living being now suffering among the animals, and every being now suffering throughout the six destinies, bow in confession for them with a single mind and intent, that these living beings may all attain liberation. Should we fail to diligently apply skillful means to turn calamity into blessing, then we ourselves bear a share of the fault in each and every hell. Let us together, with sincere mind, recollect that our parents, teachers, relatives, and kin must in the future undergo this same bitter retribution, and recollect also that we ourselves, in the future and even now, are on the verge of meeting this very suffering. With this same anguish of resolve, let us prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, our sincerity earnest and our minds fully applied, praying that a single thought may move the Buddhas of the ten directions, and that a single bow may cut off immeasurable suffering. Should any being of the six destinies already be suffering, may the power of the Buddha, the power of the Dharma, and the power of the sages bring them liberation at once. Should any being of the six destinies not yet be suffering, may the power of the Buddha, the power of the Dharma, and the power of every sage cause it to be cut off forever. From this day forward, may they never again fall among the evil destinies, removing the karma of the three obstructions and being reborn according to their thought, extinguishing the five fears and attaining sovereign liberation, diligently cultivating the practice of the Way without rest, their wondrous conduct adorned beyond the ground of the Dharma-cloud, entering the vajra-mind and accomplishing true and equal awakening. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — once again, apply the mind, incline your ears, and reflect well upon this. The Miscellaneous Piṭaka Sūtra relates: at one time there was a ghost who said to Maudgalyāyana: "I have eyes on both my shoulders, and a mouth and nose on my chest, but no head at all. What fault brought this about?" Maudgalyāyana answered: "In a former life, you were always the disciple of an executioner, and whenever a person was killed, you were always glad of it, binding them with a cord. Because of this cause and condition, you received this fault — but this is only the flowering retribution; the true fruit still awaits you in the hells." There was another ghost who said to Maudgalyāyana: "My body is always like a lump of flesh, with no hands, feet, eyes, ears, or nose, forever devoured by insects and birds. This pain is so hard to bear and hard to endure — what fault brought this about?" Maudgalyāyana answered: "In a former life, you constantly gave medicine to others in such a way that living beings could not survive whole. Because of this cause and condition, you incurred this fault — but this too is only the flowering retribution; the true fruit still awaits you in the hells." There was another ghost who said to Maudgalyāyana: "My belly is enormous, and my throat is as narrow as a needle, so that through the whole span of the year I can obtain no food or drink. What fault brought this about?" Maudgalyāyana answered: "In a former life, you were the head of a village, and, relying on your wealth and rank, drank wine without restraint, treating others with contempt, seizing their food and drink and leaving everyone hungry and destitute. Because of this cause and condition, you incurred this fault — but this too is only the flowering retribution; the true fruit still awaits you in the hells." There was another ghost who said to Maudgalyāyana: "Since my birth in this form, two red-hot iron wheels have burned beneath my two armpits, my whole body scorched and rotting. What fault brought this about?" Maudgalyāyana answered: "In a former life, while baking cakes for the community, you stole two of them and hid them under your two armpits. Because of this cause and condition, you incurred this fault — but this too is only the flowering retribution; the true fruit still awaits you in the hells." There was another ghost who said to Maudgalyāyana: "I constantly wrap something around my own head to cover it, always fearing that someone comes to kill me, my mind forever in dread. What fault brought this about?" Maudgalyāyana answered: "In a former life, you committed adultery with another's spouse, always fearing to be seen, always fearing that the husband would seize, bind, and beat you to death, your mind forever gripped by terror — and this is why you received this fault. But this too is only the flowering retribution; the true fruit still awaits you in the hells." Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — as the sūtra describes, how could each of us fail to feel great dread? We, together, from beginningless time until this very day, must surely have committed evil of this kind, immeasurable and boundless. Faults of this kind all come from ignorance and the lack of a compassionate mind, using strength to overpower the weak and injure living beings, committing faults such as these, even stealing others' possessions, deluded and having lost the Way, slandering the good and virtuous, and committing every kind of fault. For faults and retributions of this kind, one must surely undergo suffering among the evil destinies. Today, with sincere mind, in this same anguish of resolve, let us prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, and, on behalf of every being of the six destinies, whether already suffering or not yet suffering, beg for mercy in this rite of bowing and confession; and on behalf of our parents, teachers, and every kin, beg for mercy in this rite of bowing and confession; and for ourselves as well, beg for mercy in this rite of bowing and confession — praying that whatever fault we have already committed be dissolved, and that we dare not commit again what we have not yet committed. We pray only to the great and compassionate father of the world: Homage to the Buddha Maitreya. Homage to the Buddha Śākyamuni. Homage to the Buddha of Honored, Abundant Lotus. Homage to the Buddha of Pure, Abundant Jeweled Flourishing. Homage to the Buddha King of the Lightning-Lamp Streamer. Homage to the Buddha, Lamp of the Emptiness of Dharma. Homage to the Buddha Who Accomplishes Every Virtue. Homage to the Buddha King of the Worthy Streamer-Banner. Homage to the Buddha Who Holds Every Jewel's Fine Color. Homage to the Buddha Who Cuts Off Doubt, Uproots Desire, and Dispels Darkness. Homage to the Buddha Whose Mind Knows No Fear, Whose Majesty Never Falters. Homage to the Buddha, the Lion. Homage to the Buddha Whose Renown Reaches Far. Homage to the Buddha Named for the Dharma. Homage to the Buddha Who Upholds the Dharma. Homage to the Buddha of the Dharma-Banner. Homage to the Bodhisattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Constant Diligence. Homage to the Bodhisattva Who Never Rests. Homage to the Bodhisattva Ākāśagarbha. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Boundless Body. Homage to the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. With great compassion, may they rescue every being of the six destinies, whether now suffering or yet to suffer, so that these living beings may attain liberation at once. By their supernormal power, may they cut off the evil destinies and the karma of the hells, so that every living being, from this day forward, even to the very seat of awakening, may never again fall among the evil destinies, discarding this body of bitter retribution to attain a body like vajra, the four immeasurables and six perfections always present before them, the four eloquences and six supernormal powers theirs at will, courageous and diligent without rest, until, advancing in cultivation, they fulfill the practice of the ten grounds, and, in turn, deliver every living being.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The recurring phrase "this is only the flowering retribution; the true fruit still awaits you in the hells" (此是華報，果在地獄) names a specific concept worth pausing on: the hungry-ghost existence Maudgalyāyana describes is not itself the full penalty for the fault, but only its immediate, partial "blossom," with the hells still to come as the eventual "fruit." This two-stage structure — an intermediate retribution now, a fuller one later — is distinct from the direct fault-to-hell mappings that dominate the sūtra quoted earlier in this fascicle, and it is worth noticing as a variation in how this liturgy's sources model the timing of karmic consequence.
