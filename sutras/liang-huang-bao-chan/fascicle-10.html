Fascicle 10

Dedication, a vow for each of the six senses, and the final entrustment《慈悲道場懺法》卷第十・菩薩迴向法・發願第四（六根願）・諸行法門・囑累第五

The closing fascicle of the entire ten-fascicle liturgy. It teaches the bodhisattva's own method of dedicating merit, then makes a vow across each of the six sense-faculties in turn — eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind, followed by a seventh vow for speech itself — describing in vivid, paired detail what each faculty should never again perceive and what it should perceive instead. It closes with a catalog of the gateways of practice, and the liturgy's final act: entrusting every living being it has prayed for, across all nine preceding fascicles, to the care of the bodhisattvas, followed by a vow of literally inexhaustible scope.

Chinese
《慈悲道場懺法》卷第十 — the tenth and final fascicle. Completes the chapter Dedication (迴向第三) with the bodhisattva's own method of dedication (菩薩迴向法), then gives the chapter Making Vows (發願第四) as six sense-faculty vows plus a vow of speech, followed by the Gateway of Various Practices (諸行法門) and the closing chapter, the Final Entrustment (囑累第五).
Covers
The bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own model for dedicating merit without clinging to reward; a vow for the eye (what it should never again see, and what it should see instead), then identical paired vows for the ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind, and a seventh vow for speech; a catalog of the classical thirty-seven factors of awakening reframed as individual "gateways" of practice; and the liturgy's final act — entrusting every living being prayed for across the entire ten-fascicle rite to the bodhisattvas by name, followed by a vow explicitly declared inexhaustible until living beings, the worlds, empty space, and the Dharma-nature itself are exhausted first.
Why it matters
The six-faculty vows are architecturally the liturgy's most elegant passage: six near-identical structures, each contrasting a catalog of what a given sense should never perceive again against a catalog of the sensory richness of awakening, giving the entire ten-fascicle rite a concrete, sensory close rather than an abstract one. The final entrustment then closes the whole work by naming, one final time, nearly every bodhisattva invoked across all nine preceding fascicles.
Difficulty
★★★☆☆ — the six-faculty vows are long but highly patterned and easy to follow once the first is read closely; the closing entrustment and final vow reward being read in full, as the deliberate conclusion to the entire work.

1. The Bodhisattva's Method of Dedication菩薩迴向法

Chinese Source

Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — together we have now endured hardship and borne suffering, cultivating wholesome roots without measure such as these. Let each of us further arouse this very thought: whatever wholesome root we ourselves have cultivated, we dedicate entirely to benefiting every living being, so that every living being may attain purity in the end. Through this wholesome root of confession that we have cultivated, may every living being have the immeasurable suffering of the hells, the hungry ghosts, the animals, and King Yama's court entirely dissolved. Through this rite of confession, may we become, for every living being, a great refuge that dissolves the very aggregate of suffering; a great protector that frees them from affliction; a place of refuge that removes their fear; a resting-place that brings them to the ground of wisdom; a haven that brings them to the ultimate place of peace; a great brightness that dissolves the darkness of folly; a great lamp that brings them to abide in ultimate clarity and purity; and a great guiding teacher that brings them into the gateway of skillful means, to attain the pure body of wisdom. Today, the assembly gathered together in this common practice — dharmas such as these are what the bodhisattva-mahāsattvas practice for the sake of enemy and friend alike, dedicating every wholesome root equally to every living being without distinction, entering an equal view free of any notion of enemy or friend, and forever regarding every living being with loving eyes. Should a living being harbor resentment toward a bodhisattva and give rise to a hostile, rebellious mind, the bodhisattva remains a true and good friend to them, skillfully taming that mind and expounding the profound Dharma for their sake. Just as the great ocean cannot be spoiled by any poison poured into it, so too the bodhisattva: though foolish and unwise beings, not knowing how to repay kindness, give rise to evil without measure, they cannot disturb or shake the bodhisattva's mind set upon the Way. Just as the bright sun shines universally upon every living being and does not hide its light merely because some have no eyes to see it, so too the bodhisattva's mind set upon the Way does not retreat or fail because of the wicked, and does not abandon its wholesome roots merely because living beings are difficult to tame. The bodhisattva-mahāsattva, in every wholesome root, keeps a pure and believing mind and nurtures great compassion, dedicating every wholesome root to living beings from the depths of the heart, not merely with words on the lips. Since the bodhisattva-mahāsattva arouses a dedication of wholesome roots such as this, we ourselves, today, should look up to and learn this same dedication. We pray that we disciples may obtain just as we have vowed, our awakening-vow fulfilled, and that every being of the four modes of birth and six destinies may together obtain just as they have vowed. Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The image of the bodhisattva's mind as "the great ocean, unspoiled by any poison poured into it" and as "the bright sun, which does not hide its light merely because some have no eyes to see it" together make the same point from two directions: neither the malice of an ungrateful being nor the sheer difficulty of reaching someone changes the bodhisattva's conduct toward them. What follows is a striking reversal of the usual direction of a repentance rite — rather than the confessor asking the bodhisattvas for rescue, this section asks them to keep rescuing living beings even in the specific event that the confessor's own vow fails and they themselves fall into the evil destinies, effectively asking the bodhisattvas to keep a promise the confessor cannot guarantee to keep.

2. The Vow of the Eye發願第四・初發眼根願

Ru-Yi Translation

Having now finished the dedication, we next turn to making a vow for each of the six faculties. We further vow, today, that this assembly gathered together in this common practice, each on our own, arouse this very vow: tracing where every evil arises, we find it always conditioned by the six faculties — these are the very root of every calamity. And yet, though they are the root of calamity, they are equally able to bring about blessed karma without measure. This is why the Śrīmālā Sūtra says: guard the six faculties and purify body, speech, and mind. By this very principle, proven as the root from which goodness arises, we now arouse a great vow for each of the six faculties. We vow that this assembly gathered together today, extending broadly to every living being of the four modes of birth and six destinies throughout the ten directions, from this day forward until reaching awakening, may have our eyes forever not see the form of insatiable craving and deceptive illusion; not see the form of flattery, sycophancy, and cunning collusion; not see the bewitching forms of black, yellow, vermilion, and purple that lead people astray; not see the ugly form of hatred and contention; not see the form of beating and injury that harms another; not see the form of slaughter and mutilation inflicted upon living beings; not see the form of foolish unbelief and doubt-darkened confusion; not see the form of arrogance without humility or respect; not see the form of the ninety-six kinds of false views — may our eyes forever not see forms of evil and unwholesomeness such as these. May our eyes forever see the calm and tranquil form of the ever-abiding Dharma-body throughout the ten directions; forever see the purple-gold form of the thirty-two marks; forever see the accompanying form of the eighty excellent characteristics; forever see the form of the gods and immortals offering treasures and scattering flowers; forever see the form of the five-colored light issuing from the mouth as the Dharma is taught to deliver people; forever see the form of the body divided and scattered to fill the ten directions; forever see the form of the Buddhas emitting light from the crown of the head, drawing those with karmic affinity to come together. We further vow that our eyes may forever see the form of the bodhisattvas, pratyekabuddhas, and arhats of the ten directions, that we may constantly, together with every living being and their kin, behold the very form of the Buddha; forever see the form of every goodness needing no instruction; forever see the form of the pure flowers of the seven factors of awakening; forever see the form of the wondrous fruit of liberation; forever see the form of this very assembly today, joyfully praising and receiving the Dharma upon the crown of the head; forever see the form of the fourfold assembly gathered round, listening to the Dharma with thirsting eagerness; forever see the form of every act of giving, precept-keeping, patience, and diligence; forever see the form of everyone silently absorbed in meditation and cultivating wisdom; forever see the form of every living being attaining the acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas and receiving, before our very eyes, the joyful prediction of awakening; forever see the form of everyone ascending to vajra wisdom, cutting through the darkness of ignorance to become a bodhisattva of the final stage; forever see the form of everyone bathing in the stream of the Dharma without ever turning back. Having now finished making the vow of the eye faculty, together, with sincere mind, let us prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, and take refuge in the great and compassionate father of the world. Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This is the template the following five sense-vows will each repeat with only the specific content changed: a catalog of what the faculty should never again perceive, paired point for point against a catalog of what it should perceive instead. What makes the eye-vow distinctive on its own is the sheer visual specificity of its second half — not simply "seeing the Buddha," but the thirty-two marks in purple-gold, five-colored light issuing from the mouth, the body dividing itself to fill the ten directions. The liturgy treats awakening as something that should be, quite literally, a feast for the eyes rather than only an abstract state.

Ru-Yi Translation

We further vow that this assembly gathered together today, extending broadly to every living being of the four modes of birth and six destinies throughout the ten directions, from this day forward until reaching awakening, may have our ears forever not hear the sound of wailing, sorrow, and weeping; not hear the sound of suffering in the Avīci hell; not hear the thunderous roar of the boiling cauldron; not hear the sound of blades cutting and tearing on the mountain of swords and the forest of blades; not hear the immeasurable cries of suffering separated across the eighteen hells. We further vow that, from this day forward, our ears may forever not hear the sound of hungry ghosts, starving and parched, crying out for food they cannot obtain; not hear the sound, as hungry ghosts move, of fire crackling like five hundred carts between their very joints. We further vow that, from this day forward, our ears may forever not hear the sound of animals five hundred yojanas in length, bitten and devoured in agony by countless small creatures; not hear the sound of those who, having failed to repay a debt, are reborn as a camel, donkey, horse, or ox, forever bearing heavy loads and suffering under the whip and the rod. May our ears forever not hear the sound of the eight sufferings, including separation from what is loved and union with what is hated; not hear the sound of suffering brought by the four hundred and four diseases; not hear any sound of evil or unwholesomeness at all; not hear the beguiling sound of bells, chimes, conches, drums, zithers, harps, and jeweled ornaments. We ask only that every living being, from this day forward, may have their ears forever hear the eightfold voice of the Buddhas expounding the Dharma; forever hear the sound of impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and non-self; forever hear the sound of the eighty-four thousand perfections; forever hear the sound that every dharma is merely a provisional name without fixed nature; forever hear the sound of the Buddhas expounding the Dharma in a single voice, each understanding it according to their own capacity; forever hear the sound that every living being possesses the buddha-nature and the Dharma-body abides forever, never destroyed; forever hear the sound of the bodhisattvas of the ten grounds advancing through patient cultivation; forever hear the sound of attaining the understanding of non-arising, entering well into the Buddha's wisdom, and departing the three realms; forever hear the sound of the Dharma-body bodhisattvas entering the flowing water of the Dharma, contemplating the ultimate and the conventional together, every thought fully endowed with the ten thousand practices; forever hear the sound of the pratyekabuddhas and arhats of the four fruits throughout the ten directions; forever hear the sound of Indra expounding the Prajñā to the gods; forever hear the sound of the great beings of the ten grounds, bodhisattvas of the final stage, expounding the Dharma of the never-turning-back ground in the Tuṣita palace; forever hear the sound of every goodness returning together to buddhahood; forever hear the sound of the Buddhas praising every living being able to practice the ten wholesome deeds and rejoicing in it. May every living being forever hear the sound of the Buddhas praising them, saying: excellent — this person shall soon accomplish buddhahood. Having now finished making the vow of the ear faculty, together, with sincere mind, let us once again prostrate ourselves fully to the ground, and take refuge in the great and compassionate father of the world. Homage to the Buddha Renowned and Praised. Homage to the Buddha of Every Mark. Homage to the Buddha of Virtue Widely Spread. Homage to the Buddha of the Mastery of the World. Homage to the Buddha of the Tree of Virtue. Homage to the Buddha Who Extinguishes Folly. Homage to the Buddha Boundless. Homage to the Buddha, Excellent Moon. Homage to the Buddha of the Mark of Boundless Eloquence. Homage to the Bodhisattva of the Jeweled Moon. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Moonlight. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Boundless Body. Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The catalog of forbidden sounds here runs unusually specific about ordinary pleasures near its end — "bells, chimes, conches, drums, zithers, harps, and jeweled ornaments" grouped together with the sounds of hell and disease as things the ear should no longer hear. The point is not that music is evil in the way suffering is evil, but that both, in their own way, keep attention bound to sensation rather than free for the Dharma; the vow that follows answers this not with silence but with an entire cosmology's worth of sound to listen to instead.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The traditional list of forty-two stages of the bodhisattva path — the ten abodes, ten practices, ten dedications, and ten grounds — is compressed here into a run of adjectives attached directly to "fragrance": joyful, stainless, radiant, and so on through each of the ten grounds by their classical names. Rendering an entire technical stage-map of the path as a sequence of scents to be smelled is a small but genuine act of translation in the original text itself, turning a scholastic list into something sensory and immediate.

This is the shortest and most tightly focused of the six sense-vows — five prohibited flavors against nine desired ones, all of them literally about eating and drinking rather than opening outward into cosmology the way the nose-vow's fragrance catalog does. Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Several items here answer each other with unusual precision: the hungry ghost's "molten copper poured down the throat" from the confession sections of earlier fascicles is directly countered by "bathing body and mind in the seven-jeweled pools" of a pure land, and the ordinary discomforts of hunger, thirst, heat, and cold are matched one-for-one against their opposite. This vow is less cosmic in scope than the eye or nose vows and more concerned with simple physical comfort — a body that is merely not in pain being treated as itself a form of the sacred.

Ru-Yi Translation

We further vow that this assembly gathered together today, extending broadly to every living being of the ten directions, from this day forward until reaching awakening, may have our minds constantly know craving, hatred, and folly as an affliction; constantly know killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct of the body, and false speech, flowery speech, divisive speech, and harsh speech of the mouth, as an affliction; constantly know that killing one's father, killing one's mother, killing an arhat, drawing the blood of a buddha, and causing a schism in the harmonious community are the crimes of immediate retribution; constantly know that slandering the Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha, and disbelieving the law of cause and effect and of rebirth and requital after death, are wrong; constantly know to keep far from evil companions and draw close to good friends; constantly know that consulting and accepting the teachings of the ninety-six kinds of false teachers is wrong; constantly know that the three defilements, the five hindrances, and the ten bonds are obstructions; constantly know that the three evil paths are places to be feared, where the retribution of birth and death is cruel and severe. Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Unlike the five preceding sense-vows, the mind-vow is built entirely from "constantly know," not "not perceive" and "perceive instead" — there is no catalog of what the mind should be shielded from, only an escalating curriculum of doctrine to hold in view at all times, moving from the five precepts through the seven expedient stages, the sixteen-fold contemplation of the four truths, the twelve links of dependent origination, and the ten grounds. Naming two real historical figures — Aśvaghoṣa and Nāgārjuna — among the invoked bodhisattvas here, rather than the abstract epithets used throughout the rest of the liturgy, is also unusual, and matches their appearance again in the final entrustment that closes the whole work.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Speech receives a seventh vow beyond the six classical sense-faculties, and its content reads less like a sensory catalog than a code of conduct for how a lay Buddhist teacher should actually talk to other people day to day — not gossiping about others' looks, not mocking idle amusements, not cursing at family even in anger, not discouraging someone else's generosity. Where the eye, ear, nose, and body vows describe what a liberated being perceives, this one describes what a good person still living in the world should actually say, making it the most immediately actionable of the seven.

The second half of this section quietly reproduces the classical thirty-seven factors of awakening — the four foundations of mindfulness compressed into "the mind's own direction," followed by the four right efforts, the four bases of spiritual power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of awakening, and the eightfold path, each renamed a "gateway" and paired with a one-line gloss. It is the liturgy's most concentrated piece of doctrinal scaffolding, given here almost as a checklist rather than expounded at length, on the assumption that its audience would already recognize the underlying framework being restated.

Through the merit and causes and conditions of this confession and vow, may every living being delight in seeking the unsurpassed field of blessing, deeply believing that giving to a Buddha brings a reward without measure. May every living being turn their mind single-mindedly toward the Buddha, fully attaining a pure fruit without measure. May every living being, in the presence of the Buddhas, harbor no stinginess at all, fully endowed with great generosity and holding nothing back. We further vow that every living being, in the presence of the Buddhas, may cultivate the unsurpassed field of blessing, leaving behind the two lesser vehicles to walk the bodhisattva path, attaining the Tathāgatas' own unhindered liberation and omniscience. We further vow that every living being may plant inexhaustible wholesome roots in the presence of the Buddhas, attaining the Buddhas' own immeasurable merit and wisdom. We further vow that every living being may gather in profound wisdom, fully endowed with the pure and unsurpassed king of wisdom. We further vow that every living being may wander freely at ease, attaining the Tathāgatas' own unhindered spiritual power to reach every place. We further vow that every living being may gather in the Great Vehicle, attaining immeasurable wisdom of every kind and abiding unmoved. We further vow that every living being may be fully endowed with and accomplish the foremost field of blessing, each able to bring forth the very ground of omniscience. We further vow that every living being may harbor no resentment toward any Buddha, planting every wholesome root and delighting in seeking the Buddha's own wisdom. We further vow that every living being, through wondrous skillful means, may journey to every adorned buddha-land, entering deeply into the Dharma-realm within a single thought without ever growing weary. We further vow that every living being may attain an incomparable body, able to wander throughout the worlds of the ten directions without ever tiring. We further vow that every living being may accomplish a vast and expansive body, moving freely at will, attaining every Buddha's own spiritual power to adorn the ultimate far shore, manifesting the Tathāgatas' own unhindered spiritual power throughout the whole reach of empty space within a single thought. Having now made this great vow, vast as the Dharma-nature itself, ultimate as empty space itself, may every living being obtain just as they have vowed, their awakening-vow fulfilled. Together, with sincere mind, let us prostrate ourselves fully to the ground. Should we disciples, undergoing the retribution of our own suffering, be unable to rescue these very living beings ourselves, we entrust them, with these living beings, to: The immeasurable and boundless bodhisattvas of the unborn Dharma-body throughout the whole reach of empty space; the immeasurable and boundless bodhisattvas of the undefiled form-body throughout the whole reach of empty space; the immeasurable and boundless bodhisattvas who have aroused the awakening-mind throughout the whole reach of empty space; the great teacher Aśvaghoṣa, who revived the true Dharma; the great teacher Nāgārjuna, who revived the semblance Dharma; the Bodhisattva of Boundless Body throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space; the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, who rescues from suffering throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space; the Bodhisattva Mañjuśrī; the Bodhisattva Samantabhadra; the Bodhisattva, Lion at Play; the Bodhisattva of the Lion's Vigorous Leap; the Bodhisattva of the Lion's Banner; the Bodhisattva of the Lion's Deeds; the Bodhisattva of Firm and Courageous Diligence; the Bodhisattva of Vajra Wisdom; the Bodhisattva Who Casts Off the Hindrances; the Bodhisattva of Tranquil Faculties; the Bodhisattva of Supreme Wisdom; the Bodhisattva Never Leaving the World; the Bodhisattva Medicine King; the Bodhisattva Supreme Medicine; the Bodhisattva Ākāśagarbha; the Bodhisattva Vajragarbha; the Bodhisattva Ever Diligent; the Bodhisattva Never Resting; the Bodhisattva of Wondrous Sound; the Bodhisattva of Wondrous Virtue; the Bodhisattva of the Jeweled Moon; the Bodhisattva of Moonlight; the Bodhisattva Sadāprarudita; and the Bodhisattva Who Transcends the Three Realms. We further entrust these very living beings to every bodhisattva throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space in this way. We pray that the bodhisattva-mahāsattvas, by the power of their original vow and their vow to deliver living beings, may gather in every living being of the ten directions, without end and without limit. We pray that the bodhisattva-mahāsattvas may never abandon any living being, treating them the same as a good and wise friend, with no mark of distinction between them. We pray that every living being may know the bodhisattvas' kindness and draw close to make offerings to them; we pray that the bodhisattvas, with compassionate pity, may receive them, so that every living being attains an upright mind and follows the bodhisattvas without ever being parted from them. We pray that every living being may follow the bodhisattvas' teaching without ever rebelling against it, attaining a firm mind that never abandons a good and wise friend, leaving behind every defiled mind, indestructible. May every living being, for the sake of a good and wise friend, hold back neither body nor life, giving up everything rather than violate their teaching. May every living being cultivate great loving-kindness, keeping far from every evil, and, upon hearing the Buddha's true Dharma, be fully able to uphold it. May every living being share the same wholesome roots and karmic reward as the bodhisattvas, the bodhisattvas' own practice and vow purified once and for all, fully endowed with spiritual power and at ease as they wish, riding the Great Vehicle all the way to the ultimate omniscience, never once growing negligent along the way. Riding the vehicle of wisdom to the place of peace and ease, may they attain the unhindered vehicle and ultimate ease. From the very beginning until now — taking refuge in the Three Jewels, resolving doubt and giving rise to faith, confession and arousing the mind, revealing the fruits of karmic retribution, deliverance from the hells, dissolving grudges and rejoicing in ourselves, making vows and dedicating merit, all the way to this very entrustment — whatever merit there is in all of this, we give it entirely to every living being throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The entrustment list gathers nearly every bodhisattva name this liturgy has invoked across all ten fascicles into a single roll call — the lion-themed bodhisattvas, the paired "boundless body" and Avalokiteśvara who close every buddha-list in the entire work, and, notably, the two named historical teachers Aśvaghoṣa and Nāgārjuna, described here as having "revived the true Dharma" and "revived the semblance Dharma" respectively — treating two second-to-third-century philosopher-monks as continuations of the bodhisattva project rather than as separate historical figures. The final sentence's plain summary of the entire liturgy's own structure, chapter by chapter, functions as the work's own table of contents, restated in the last possible moment before the closing vow.

11. Praising the Buddha: The Liturgy's Close讚佛呪願

Chinese Source

彌勒世尊現為我證，十方諸佛哀愍覆護，所悔所願皆得成就。願諸眾生同慈悲父俱生此國，預在初會聞法悟道，功德智慧一切具足，與諸菩薩等無有異，入金剛心成等正覺。 多陀阿伽度、阿羅訶、三藐三佛陀，三德具足，度人無量，拔生死苦。以今懺悔禮佛功德因緣，願諸眾生各各具足，得如所願滿菩提。願某甲等今日所發誓願，悉同十方盡虛空界一切諸佛諸大菩薩所有誓願不可窮盡，我今誓願亦復如是，廣大如法性、究竟如虛空，窮未來際盡一切劫，眾生不可盡，我願不可盡；世界不可盡，我願不可盡；虛空不可盡，我願不可盡；法性不可盡，我願不可盡；涅槃不可盡，我願不可盡；佛出世不可盡，我願不可盡；諸佛智慧不可盡，我願不可盡；心緣不可盡，我願不可盡；起智不可盡，我願不可盡；世間道種法道種智慧道種不可盡，我願不可盡。若十種可盡，我願乃可盡。一切和南三乘聖眾。

Ru-Yi Translation

May the World-Honored Maitreya appear now to bear witness for us; may the Buddhas of the ten directions, with pity, extend their shelter, so that whatever we have repented and whatever we have vowed may all be accomplished. May every living being be born together in this very land alongside the compassionate father, present at that very first assembly to hear the Dharma and awaken to the Way, fully endowed with every merit and wisdom, no different at all from the bodhisattvas themselves, entering the vajra-mind and accomplishing true and equal awakening. Tathāgata, Arhat, Perfectly and Fully Awakened One — complete in the three virtues, delivering people without measure and drawing them out of the suffering of birth and death. Through the merit and causes and conditions of this confession and homage paid today, may every living being be fully endowed, each obtaining just as they have vowed, their awakening-vow fulfilled. May the vow we disciples have made today be entirely the same, inexhaustible, as every vow made by all the Buddhas and great bodhisattvas throughout the ten directions and the whole reach of empty space; our own vow, made now, is likewise vast as the Dharma-nature itself, ultimate as empty space itself, to the very limit of the future and through every kalpa to come. Should living beings ever be exhausted, only then could our vow be exhausted; should the worlds ever be exhausted, only then could our vow be exhausted; should empty space ever be exhausted, only then could our vow be exhausted; should the Dharma-nature ever be exhausted, only then could our vow be exhausted; should nirvāṇa ever be exhausted, only then could our vow be exhausted; should the Buddhas' appearance in the world ever be exhausted, only then could our vow be exhausted; should the wisdom of the Buddhas ever be exhausted, only then could our vow be exhausted; should the objects of the mind ever be exhausted, only then could our vow be exhausted; should the arising of wisdom ever be exhausted, only then could our vow be exhausted; should the roots of the worldly path, the Dharma path, and the path of wisdom ever be exhausted, only then could our vow be exhausted. Only should these ten things all be exhausted could our own vow ever be exhausted. With this, we bow in reverence to the whole assembly of sages of the three vehicles.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The ten-fold "should X ever be exhausted, only then could our vow be exhausted" that closes the entire liturgy is a known classical formula for expressing a genuinely unconditioned vow, most famously associated with Samantabhadra's vows in the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, and its appearance here as the very last substantive statement of the whole ten-fascicle work is almost certainly a deliberate echo. After nine fascicles built from confession, homage, and petition addressed outward to others, the liturgy's very last words are a vow spoken in the first person singular — "my vow" — framed so that it could only end if reality itself ran out first. It is a fitting close for a text that began, back in Fascicle 1, with the legend of a python transformed by an act of compassion: the entire work ends by declaring that the compassion it has practiced for nine fascicles has, in principle, no point at which it stops.
