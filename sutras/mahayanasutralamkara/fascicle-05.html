Fascicle 5 · 述求品第十二之二 · Complete

Seeking, Continued

The second half of the chapter on seeking carries the classificatory density of fascicle 4 into new territory: Consciousness-Only itself, argued as two lights of mind without a dual mark of defilement and purity; the marked and the marking, mapped exactly onto the three natures already established; four distinct gates of liberation; an eightfold forbearance of the non-arising of dharmas; and eight separate reasons the Buddha teaches a single vehicle even while three vehicles remain real. It closes with two of the treatise's longest enumerations — forty-four kinds of attention that fulfill the six perfections, each stated as the bodhisattva's own inner reflection, and the ten discriminations the Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra remedies one by one, in its own quoted words.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Fascicle 5 complete Source: Taishō T1604, 大乘莊嚴經論卷第五, 述求品第十二之二 Chinese attribution: 無著菩薩造 (compiled by Asaṅga)

Where This Fascicle Picks Up

Fascicle 4 opened the chapter on seeking with the three baskets, the objects of cognition, the eighteen kinds of attention, and reality argued through the magician's illusion. This fascicle continues that same chapter without a break — the source marks it only 述求品之二, "seeking, part two," with no further chapter heading of its own, picking up exactly where seeking reality left off and carrying the chapter through to its close.

1. Seeking Consciousness-Only: The Two Lights of Mind

Chinese Source

釋曰：已說求染淨，次說求唯識。

偈曰：

能取及所取，　此二唯心光，

貪光及信光，　二光無二法。

釋曰：能取及所取此二唯心光者，求唯識人應知，能取所取此之二種唯是心光。貪光及信光二光無二法者，如是貪等煩惱光及信等善法光，如是二光亦無染淨二法。何以故？不離心光別有貪等信等染淨法故，是故二光亦無二相。

偈曰：

種種心光起，　如是種種相，

光體非體故，　不得彼法實。

釋曰：種種心光起如是種種相者，種種心光即是種種事相，或異時起、或同時起。異時起者，謂貪光瞋光等。同時起者，謂信光進光等。光體非體故不得彼法實者，如是染位心數、淨位心數，唯有光相而無光體，是故世尊不說彼為真實之法。已說求唯識，次說求諸相。

Ru-Yi Translation

The exposition: seeking defilement and purification having been explained, next seeking Consciousness-Only is explained.

The grasper and the grasped —
these two are only the light of mind;
the light of craving and the light of faith —
these two lights are not two separate things.

The exposition: one who seeks Consciousness-Only should know that the grasper and the grasped are both only the light of mind. The light of craving and the light of faith, these two lights are not two separate things means: the light of the affliction of craving and the rest, and the light of the wholesome dharma of faith and the rest, are also, as two lights, not two separate dharmas of defilement and purity. Why? Because apart from the light of mind, there is no separate dharma of defilement or purity such as craving or faith — therefore these two lights are likewise without a dual mark.

Manifold lights of mind arise,
and with them these manifold marks;
because the substance of this light is not a substance,
the reality of these dharmas cannot be found.

The exposition: manifold lights of mind, arising either at different times or simultaneously, are themselves manifold marks of things. Arising at different times: the light of craving, of hatred, and so on. Arising simultaneously: the light of faith, of diligence, and so on. Because the substance of this light is not a substance, the reality of these dharmas cannot be found means: mental factors in a defiled state, and in a pure state, have only the mark of light and no substance of light — for this reason the Bhagavat does not teach them to be real dharmas. Seeking Consciousness-Only having been explained, next seeking the various marks is explained.

Reading note

不離心光別有貪等信等染淨法 is the argument's whole weight in one clause: apart from the light of mind, there is no separate dharma of defilement or purity. This is a stronger claim than saying afflictions and virtues are made of mind-stuff; it says the distinction between defiled and pure states is itself only a distinction among lights, not a distinction between two different kinds of underlying thing. Craving and faith are not two substances that happen to both be mental — they are two patterns the same light takes, which is why the treatise can say in the same breath that the two lights have "no dual mark" without thereby erasing the practical difference between a defiled mind and a pure one.

2. The Marked and the Marking: Five Marked Objects, Three Marking Natures

Chinese Source

偈曰：

所相及能相，　如是相差別，

為攝利眾生，　諸佛開示現。

釋曰：相有二種，一者所相、二者能相。此偈總舉，餘偈別釋。

偈曰：

共及心及見，　及位及不轉，

略說所相五，　廣說則無量。

釋曰：共及心及見及位及不轉者，所相有五：一者色法、二者心法、三者心數法、四者不相應法、五者無為法。彼共者，謂色法。心者，謂識法。見者，謂心數法。位者，謂不相應法。不轉者，謂虛空等無為法。略說所相五廣說則無量者，彼識常起如是五相。此五所相是世尊略說，若廣說者則有無量差別。已說所相諸相，次說能相諸相。

偈曰：

意言與習光，　名義互光起，

非真分別故，　是名分別相。

釋曰：能相略說有三種，謂分別相、依他相、真實相。此偈顯示分別相。此相復有三種：一有覺分別相、二無覺分別相、三相因分別相。意言者謂義想，義即想境，想即心數，由此想於義能如是如是起意言解，此是有覺分別相。習光者，習謂意言種子，光謂從彼種子直起義光，未能如是如是起意言解，此是無覺分別相。名義互光起者，謂依名起義光，依義起名光，境界非真，唯是分別世間，所謂若名若義，此是相因分別相。如此三種相，悉是非真分別，是名分別相。

Ru-Yi Translation

The marked, and the marking —
such are the distinctions of the marks;
in order to gather in and benefit sentient beings,
the buddhas open them up and display them.

The exposition: there are two kinds of mark — the marked, and the marking. This verse states them together; the verses that follow explain each separately.

The shared, and the mind, and the seen,
and the state, and the unchanging —
the marked, in brief, is fivefold;
stated fully, it is measureless.

The exposition: the marked is fivefold: material dharmas, mind, mental factors, formations dissociated from mind, and the unconditioned. The shared is material dharmas. Mind is consciousness. The seen is mental factors. The state is the formations dissociated from mind. And the unchanging is the unconditioned, such as space. Consciousness constantly gives rise to these five marks, stated by the Bhagavat in brief; stated fully, they have measureless distinctions. The marked's various marks having been explained, next the marking's various marks are explained.

Mental discourse, and the light of habituation;
name and meaning, each giving rise to the other's light —
since these are discrimination and not the real,
this is called the discriminated mark.

The exposition: the marking, in brief, is threefold — the discriminated mark, the dependent mark, and the true mark. This verse displays the discriminated mark, further of three kinds. Mental discourse refers to the notion of an object, through which one gives rise, in this way and that, to a mental-discourse understanding of it — this is the discriminated mark with awareness. The light of habituation is the light of the object arising directly from the seed of mental discourse, without yet giving rise to such an understanding — this is the discriminated mark without awareness. Name and meaning each giving rise to the other's light means: in dependence on a name, the light of meaning arises; in dependence on meaning, the light of a name arises, the domain not being real but only the world's discrimination — this is the discriminated mark that is the cause of a further mark. All three are discrimination and not the real, and are called the discriminated mark.

Chinese Source

偈曰：

所取及能取，　二相各三光，

不真分別故，　是說依他相。

釋曰：此偈顯示依他相。此相中自有所取相及能取相。所取相有三光，謂句光、義光、身光。能取相有三光，謂意光、受光、分別光。意謂一切時染污識，受謂五識身，分別謂意識。彼所取相三光及能取相三光，如此諸光皆是不真分別故，是依他相。

偈曰：

無體體無二，　非寂靜寂靜，

以無分別故，　是說真實相。

釋曰：此偈顯示真實相。真實，謂如也。此相有三種：一自相、二染淨相、三無分別相。無體體無二者，是真實自相。無體者，一切諸法但分別故。體者，以無體為體故。無二者，體無體無別故。非寂靜寂靜者，是真實染淨相。非寂靜者，由客塵煩惱故。寂靜者，由自性清淨故。以無分別故者，是真實無分別相。由分別不行境界，無戲論故。已說三種能相。

Ru-Yi Translation

The grasped, and the grasper —
each of these two marks has three lights;
since these are discrimination and not real,
this is called the dependent mark.

The exposition: this verse displays the dependent mark, itself having a grasped mark and a grasper mark. The grasped mark has three lights: of the phrase, of meaning, and of the body. The grasper mark has three lights: of manas, the constantly defiled consciousness; of the five sense-consciousnesses; and of the discriminating, mental consciousness. These three and three lights are all discrimination and not real — this is the dependent mark.

Without substance, yet substance is non-dual with it;
not quiescent, yet quiescent;
because it is without discrimination —
this is called the true mark.

The exposition: this verse displays the true mark — the true being suchness — of three kinds: its own mark, its mark of defilement and purity, and its non-discriminating mark. Without substance, yet substance is non-dual with it is its own mark: without substance, since all dharmas are only discrimination; yet substance, since it takes the absence of substance as its substance; non-dual, since substance and its absence are without difference. Not quiescent, yet quiescent is its mark of defilement and purity: not quiescent, owing to adventitious affliction; quiescent, owing to its own-nature's purity. Because it is without discrimination is its non-discriminating mark, discrimination not operating in its domain, it being free of discursive elaboration. The three kinds of marking having thus been explained.

Reading note

The marked and the marking map exactly onto two different classification schemes this treatise has used before, and seeing them side by side clarifies what each is for. The marked's five categories — material dharmas, mind, mental factors, formations dissociated from mind, and the unconditioned — are the standard Abhidharma sorting of everything that exists, a taxonomy of objects. The marking's three categories are the three natures from fascicle 1 onward, a taxonomy of how a mind relates to whatever it takes as object. Put together, the chapter is answering two different questions at once: what kinds of things are there to know, and in what mode is anything ever actually known. The five marked things do not change; what changes is which of the three marking-natures a mind brings to them.

The dependent mark's six lights — three for the grasped (phrase, meaning, body) and three for the grasper (manas, the five senses, discriminating consciousness) — form a precise, symmetrical inventory that is worth setting beside fascicle 3's four wisdoms arising from eight consciousnesses. Both schemes are dividing the same underlying cognitive apparatus, but for different purposes: the four wisdoms describe what a buddha's transformed mind becomes, while these six lights describe what an unenlightened, still-dependent mind is made of before any transformation. The same eighth, seventh, sixth, and five sense-consciousnesses that will later become the four wisdoms are, here, still doing the ordinary work of grasping and being grasped.

3. Five Domains of Study, and the Basis's Transformation into Five Excellences

Chinese Source

偈曰：

應知五學境，　正法及正憶，

心界有非有，　第五說轉依。

釋曰：彼能相復有五種學境，一能持、二所持、三鏡像、四明悟、五轉依。能持者，謂佛所說正法，由此法持彼能緣故。所持者，即正憶念，由正法所持故。鏡像者，謂心界，由得定故安心法界，如先所說皆見是名，定心為鏡、法界為像故。明悟者，出世間慧，彼有如實見有、非有如實見非有，有謂法無我、非有謂能取所取，於此明見故。轉依者，

偈曰：

聖性證平等，　解脫事亦一，

勝則有五義，　不減亦不增。

釋曰：聖性證平等解脫事亦一者，聖性謂無漏界。證平等者，諸聖同得故。解脫事亦一者，諸佛聖性與聲聞緣覺平等，由解脫同故。勝則有五義不減亦不增者，雖復聖性平等，然諸佛最勝自有五義：一者清淨勝，由漏習俱盡故。二者普遍勝，由剎土通淨故。三者身勝，由法身故。四者受用勝，由轉法輪受用不斷故。五者業勝，由住兜率天等現諸化事利益眾生故。不減者，謂染分減時。不增者，謂淨分增時。此是五種學地解相似，彼解脫所相法及三種能相法故。已說所相能相，次說求解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Know the five domains of study:
the true Dharma, and right recollection,
the realm of mind, and existence and non-existence,
and fifth, the transformation of the basis.

The exposition: the marking further has five domains of study: what upholds, what is upheld, the mirror-image, clear awakening, and the transformation of the basis. What upholds is the true Dharma taught by the Buddha, since it upholds what takes it as object. What is upheld is right recollection, upheld by the true Dharma. The mirror-image is the realm of mind: having attained concentration, one settles the mind in the dharma-realm — as stated before, one sees everything as name — the concentrated mind being the mirror, the dharma-realm the image. Clear awakening is transcendent wisdom, which truly sees what exists as existing — the selflessness of things — and what does not exist as not existing — the grasper and the grasped. As for the transformation of the basis:

The noble nature realizes equality;
the matter of liberation is likewise one;
yet its supremacy has five distinct meanings;
neither decreasing, nor increasing.

The exposition: the noble nature is the uncontaminated realm, and it realizes equality since all the noble ones equally attain it; the matter of liberation is likewise one, since the buddhas' noble nature is equal to that of śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, their liberation being the same. Yet its supremacy has five distinct meanings: although the noble nature is thus equal, the buddhas still have their own five meanings of supremacy — the supremacy of purity, both contaminants and their habituation being exhausted; of universality, their lands being thoroughly pure; of body, through the Dharma-body; of enjoyment, turning the Dharma-wheel without interruption; and of deed, manifesting transformative works from Tuṣita and the like to benefit beings. Not decreasing refers to when the defiled portion decreases; not increasing, to when the pure portion increases. These are the five domains of study, understood as parallel to the marked dharma of liberation and the three marking dharmas. The marked and the marking having been explained, next seeking liberation is explained.

Reading note

不減亦不增, neither decreasing nor increasing, closes this section on a formula this treatise has used before — most memorably in fascicle 3's account of the dharma-realm, where the same phrase described a purity that was never actually altered by defilement's coming and going. Here it does slightly different work: it explains how the buddhas' fivefold supremacy can be simultaneously real and consistent with the equal, shared noble nature stated just before it. The defiled portion decreasing and the pure portion increasing are two descriptions of a single process, not two separate quantities — which is exactly the kind of non-dual accounting this treatise reaches for whenever it needs to hold a genuine equality and a genuine distinction in the same sentence without contradiction.

4. Seeking Liberation: Four Gates

Chinese Source

偈曰：

如是種子轉，　句義身光轉，

是名無漏界，　三乘同所依。

釋曰：如是種子轉者，阿梨耶識轉故。句義身光轉者，謂餘識轉故。是名無漏界者，由解脫故。三乘同所依者，聲聞緣覺與佛同依止故。

偈曰：

意受分別轉，　四種自在得，

次第無分別，　剎土智業故。

釋曰：意受分別轉四種自在得者，若意、若受、若分別，如此三光若轉，即得四種自在。問：何者為四？答：次第無分別剎土智業故。一得無分別自在、二得剎土自在、三得智自在、四得業自在。

偈曰：

應知後三地，　說有四自在，

不動地有二，　餘地各餘一。

釋曰：應知後三地說有四自在者，謂不動地、善慧地、法雲地，成就彼四種自在。不動地有二餘地各餘一者，不動地有第一無分別自在、第二剎土自在，由無功用無分別故、由剎土清淨故。善慧地有第三智自在，由得四辯善巧勝故。法雲地有第四業自在，由諸通業無障礙故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus the seed is transformed,
and the light of phrase, meaning, and body is transformed;
this is called the uncontaminated realm,
the shared support of the three vehicles.

The exposition: thus the seed is transformed means the ālayavijñāna is transformed. The light of phrase, meaning, and body is transformed means the other consciousnesses are transformed. This is the uncontaminated realm, through liberation — the shared support of the three vehicles, since śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas share the same support as buddhas.

The transformation of manas, the senses, and discrimination
attains four kinds of mastery —
in order, non-discrimination,
land, wisdom, and deed.

The exposition: if manas, the five senses, and discrimination — these three lights — are transformed, four kinds of mastery are attained: mastery of non-discrimination, mastery of the buddha-land, mastery of wisdom, and mastery of deed.

Know that in the last three grounds
four kinds of mastery are taught;
the immovable ground has two,
and each of the remaining grounds has one further.

The exposition: the last three grounds — immovable, of good discernment, and of the Dharma cloud — accomplish these four kinds of mastery. The immovable ground has mastery of non-discrimination and of the buddha-land, being effortless and non-discriminating, its lands pure. The ground of good discernment has mastery of wisdom, attaining supreme skill in the four kinds of eloquence. And the ground of the Dharma cloud has mastery of deed, its activity through the supernormal powers being unobstructed.

Chinese Source

偈曰：

三有二無我，　了入真唯識，

亦無唯識光，　得離名解脫。

釋曰：復有別解脫門。三有二無我了入真唯識者，由知二無我為方便故。菩薩於三有中分別人法皆無有體，是故無我。如是知已，亦非一向都無有體，取一切諸法真實唯識故。亦無唯識光得離名解脫者，菩薩爾時安心唯識，識光亦無即得解脫。何以故？由人法不可得，離有所得故。

偈曰：

能持所持聚，　觀故唯有名，

觀名不見名，　無名得解脫。

釋曰：復有別解脫門。能持所持聚者，能持謂所聞法，所持謂正憶念，聚謂福智滿，由先聚力故而有所持。觀故唯有名者，但有言說無有義故。復次唯名者，唯識故。復次唯名者，非色四陰故。觀名不見名無名得解脫者，復次觀所觀名復不見名，由義無體故，又不見識故，又不見非色四陰故，如是名亦不可得。離有所得故，故名解脫。

偈曰：

我見熏習心，　流轉於諸趣，

安心住於內，　迴流說解脫。

釋曰：復有別解脫門。我見熏習心流轉於諸趣者，有二種我見滋灰，故言熏習。由此熏習為因，是故流轉生死。安心住於內迴流說解脫者，若知所緣不可得，置心於內攝令不散，即迴彼流說名解脫。已說求解脫，次求無自體。

Ru-Yi Translation

Understanding the twofold selflessness within the three realms of existence,
one enters into true Consciousness-Only;
and, there being no light of Consciousness-Only either,
one attains what is called liberation.

The exposition: there is a further gate of liberation. By the method of knowing the twofold selflessness, the bodhisattva discerns that, within the three realms, persons and things are both without substance. Having thus known this, it is not that there is absolutely no substance whatsoever either, since he takes hold of the true reality of all dharmas as Consciousness-Only. There being no light of Consciousness-Only either, one attains liberation means: the bodhisattva settles his mind on Consciousness-Only, and the light of consciousness too being absent, he attains liberation, since persons and things cannot be found, being free of anything to be obtained.

What upholds, what is upheld, and the accumulation —
observing these, there is only name;
observing the name, one does not see the name —
and, without name, liberation is attained.

The exposition: there is a further gate of liberation. What upholds is the Dharma one has heard; what is upheld is right recollection; the accumulation is the fulfillment of merit and wisdom, by whose power one has what is upheld. Observing these, there is only name, only speech and no meaning — only name, since it is only consciousness; only name, since it is the four formless aggregates. Observing the name, one does not see the name, and without name, liberation is attained means: observing the name that is observed, one does not see the name either, meaning having no substance, nor does one see consciousness, nor the four formless aggregates — the name itself cannot ultimately be found. Being free of anything to be obtained, it is therefore called liberation.

The habituation of the view of self perfumes the mind,
which flows on through the various destinies;
settling the mind, dwelling within,
and turning back that current, is called liberation.

The exposition: there is a further gate of liberation. There are two kinds of mordant and lime of the view of self, called habituation; by this habituation as cause, one flows on through saṃsāra. If one knows that the object taken as condition cannot be found, and places the mind within, gathering it so it does not scatter, one turns back that current — this is called liberation. Seeking liberation having been explained, next seeking the absence of self-nature is explained.

Reading note

Four gates of liberation, given one after another with no ranking stated between them, are worth reading as four separate doors onto the same room rather than four stages of one path. The first works through Consciousness-Only itself, collapsing even the light of consciousness once the twofold selflessness is understood. The second works through language, showing that "name" cannot ultimately be found once the observing of a name is itself observed — an infinite regress that terminates, rather than continuing forever, because there is finally nothing left to name. The third works through the ordinary account of saṃsāra's cause, the habituation of the view of self, and liberates simply by turning that current's direction inward. Three different starting points, each independently sufficient — a practitioner does not need to walk through Consciousness-Only to arrive at the third gate's release.

5. Seeking the Absence of Self-Nature, and the Eightfold Forbearance of Non-Arising

Chinese Source

偈曰：

自無及體無，　及以體不住，

如執無體故，　法成無自體。

釋曰：自無及體無及以體不住者，自無謂諸法自然無，由不自起故。不自起者，屬因緣故。體無謂諸法已滅者不復起故。及以體不住者，現在諸法剎那剎那不住故。此三種無自體，遍一切有為相，是義應知。如執無體故法成無自體者，如所執著實無自體，由自體無體故。如諸凡夫於自體執著常樂我淨，如是異分別相亦復無體，是故一切諸法成無自體。

偈曰：

無自體故成，　前為後依止，

無生復無滅，　本靜性涅槃。

釋曰：無自體故成前為後依止者，由前無性故，次第成立後無生等。問：此云何？答：無生復無滅本靜性涅槃。若無性則無生，若無生則無滅，若無滅則本來寂靜，若本來寂靜則自性涅槃。如是前前次第為後後依止，此義得成。已說求無自性，次說求無生忍。

偈曰：

本來及真實，　異相及自相，

自然及無異，　染污差別八。

釋曰：有八種無起法，名無生法忍。一者本來無起，由生死非有本起故。二者真實無起，由法無先後異，先起法無故。三者異相無起，由非舊種處更得起故。四者自相無起，由分別性畢竟不起故。五者自然無起，由依他性自性不起故。六者無異無起，由真實性非有異體起故。七者染污無起，由盡智時染污諸見不復起故。八者差別無起，由諸佛法身非有差別起故。此八無起法，說名無生法忍。已說求無生忍，次說求一乘。

Ru-Yi Translation

Self-absence, and substance-absence,
and substance's not abiding —
because what is grasped has no substance,
things are established as having no self-nature.

The exposition: self-absence is that things naturally lack a self, not arising of themselves, belonging instead to causes and conditions. Substance-absence is that things, once ceased, no longer arise. Substance's not abiding is that present things, moment by moment, do not abide. These three pervade every mark of conditioned things. As what is grasped and clung to really has no substance, since its own substance has no substance — just as ordinary beings cling to permanence, pleasure, self, and purity — so too this differently discriminated mark likewise has no substance, and therefore all things are established as having no self-nature.

Because of the absence of self-nature, it is established
that each stage serves as the support for the next:
no arising, and then no ceasing;
originally quiescent, and by nature nirvāṇa.

The exposition: because the earlier is without nature, the later stages are established in turn. Question: how? Answer: if there is no nature, there is no arising; if no arising, no ceasing; if no ceasing, it is originally quiescent; and if originally quiescent, it is by nature nirvāṇa. Thus each earlier stage serves as the support for each later one — this is how the point is established. Seeking the absence of self-nature having been explained, next seeking the forbearance of non-arising is explained.

Original, and true;
distinct in mark, and own-mark;
natural, and without distinction;
and defiled — these differ, eightfold.

The exposition: there are eight kinds of non-arising dharma, called the forbearance of the non-arising of dharmas. First, original non-arising, saṃsāra having no original arising to begin with. Second, true non-arising, the Dharma having no earlier dharma that arose before it. Third, non-arising distinct in mark, nothing newly arising in a place not already its own kind of seed. Fourth, non-arising as own-mark, the discriminated nature ultimately not arising at all. Fifth, natural non-arising, the dependent nature's own-nature not arising of itself. Sixth, non-arising without distinction, the true nature having no distinct substance that arises. Seventh, non-arising of defilement, at the exhaustion of wisdom defiled views no longer arising. And eighth, non-arising of distinction, the buddhas' Dharma-body having no distinction that arises. These eight are called the forbearance of the non-arising of dharmas. Seeking this forbearance having been explained, next seeking the One Vehicle is explained.

Reading note

The eight kinds of non-arising map, one for one, onto the treatise's major prior commitments, and reading them as a checklist rather than a fresh argument makes the section's purpose clear: this is a summary review disguised as a new topic. The fourth, fifth, and sixth kinds recite the three natures directly — the discriminated does not arise at all, the dependent does not arise of itself, the true has no distinct substance to arise. The seventh recalls the transformation of the basis from fascicle 3. The eighth recalls the buddhas' non-oneness argument from the same fascicle. Presenting eight familiar conclusions under one new heading, 無生法忍, is itself a teaching move: it tells the practitioner that a single forbearance, properly developed, already contains everything argued at length across the earlier fascicles.

6. Seeking the One Vehicle: Eight Reasons, and Two Kinds of Uncertain Śrāvakas

Chinese Source

偈曰：

法無我解脫，　同故性別故，

得二意變化，　究竟說一乘。

釋曰：此中八意，佛說一乘。一者法同故，謂聲聞等人無別法界，由所趣同故，故說一乘。二者無我同故，謂聲聞等人同無我體，由趣者同故，故說一乘。三者解脫同故，謂聲聞等人同滅惑障，由出離同故，故說一乘。四者性別故，謂不定三乘性人引入大乘，故說一乘。五者諸佛得同自意故，謂諸佛得如此意，如我所得一切眾生亦同我得。由此意故，故說一乘。六者聲聞得作佛意故，謂諸聲聞昔行大菩提聚時有定作佛性，彼時佛加故勝攝故，得自知作佛意。由此人前後相續無別，故說一乘。七者變化故，謂佛示現聲聞而般涅槃為教化故。如佛自說：我無量無數以聲聞乘示現涅槃。由離此方便，更無方便化小根人入大乘故。理實唯一，故說一乘。八者究竟故，謂至佛體無復去處，故說一乘。如是處處經中以此八意佛說一乘，而亦不無三乘。問：若爾，復有何義以彼彼意而說一乘？

釋曰：彼彼意有二義，一為引接諸聲聞故、二為攝住諸菩薩故。若諸聲聞於自乘性不定，佛為引接彼人令入大乘，故說一乘。若諸菩薩於自乘性不定，佛為攝住彼人令不退大乘，故說一乘。

Ru-Yi Translation

Dharma, selflessness, liberation —
because these are the same; because natures differ;
because of attaining the intent of the two; because of transformation;
and because of the ultimate — the One Vehicle is taught.

The exposition: it is with these eight intentions that the Buddha teaches the One Vehicle. First, because the Dharma is the same: śrāvakas and the rest have no separate dharma-realm, their destination being the same. Second, because selflessness is the same: they equally have the substance of selflessness. Third, because liberation is the same: they equally extinguish the afflictive obstruction, their renunciation being the same. Fourth, because natures differ: those of the three vehicles whose nature is not fixed are drawn into the Mahāyāna. Fifth, because the buddhas attain a shared intent: as I have attained, so shall all sentient beings attain the same. Sixth, because of the intent that śrāvakas will become buddhas: śrāvakas who once practiced toward great enlightenment had a fixed nature to become buddhas, and, aided by a buddha's supremacy, came to know this intent for themselves — these persons forming an unbroken continuity from before to after. Seventh, because of transformation: the Buddha displays a śrāvaka's entering final nirvāṇa for the sake of teaching beings — as the Buddha himself said, "I have, measurelessly and countlessly, displayed nirvāṇa by means of the śrāvaka vehicle" — there being no other means to transform beings of lesser faculties, and the underlying principle being truly one. And eighth, because of the ultimate: upon reaching the substance of buddhahood there is nowhere further to go. Thus, here and there in the sūtras, the Buddha teaches the One Vehicle with these eight intentions, and yet this does not mean there are not, also, three vehicles. Question: what further meaning is there in teaching the One Vehicle with these various intentions?

To draw in the śrāvakas,
and to hold and sustain the bodhisattvas —
for these two who are uncertain,
the buddhas teach the One Vehicle.

The exposition: these intentions have two purposes: to draw in the śrāvakas, and to hold and sustain the bodhisattvas. If śrāvakas are uncertain in their own vehicle's nature, the Buddha, to draw them into the Mahāyāna, teaches the One Vehicle. If bodhisattvas are uncertain in their own vehicle's nature, the Buddha, to hold and sustain them so they do not fall back, teaches the One Vehicle.

The exposition: because these two formerly had a mind that delighted in cessation, and constantly grew weary of themselves, their path is said to be the dull path, being unable to swiftly attain unsurpassed enlightenment.

The exposition: one whose task is not yet accomplished is one who has seen the truths but not yet cut off craving, not yet attained arhatship. Seeking the One Vehicle having been explained, next seeking the sciences is explained.

Reading note

The eight reasons for the One Vehicle are not eight versions of the same claim, and the treatise is careful to keep three genuinely different kinds of argument distinct within the list. Reasons one through three argue from what śrāvakas and bodhisattvas already share — the same dharma-realm, the same selflessness, the same liberation from affliction. Reasons five through eight argue from the buddhas' own intent and the shape of skillful means — what the Buddha wills for all beings, what a hidden fixed nature accomplishes over time, why an apparent nirvāṇa is sometimes only a display, and what "nowhere further to go" means at the actual endpoint. Reason four sits alone in the middle, and it is the odd one out: 性別故, because natures differ, is the one reason that does not argue for an underlying unity at all — it simply names the practical fact that beings whose vehicle is not yet fixed can still be drawn from one vehicle into another. The One Vehicle teaching, on this treatise's own accounting, rests on three different kinds of foundation, not one.

不見諦發大乘 and 見諦發大乘 — arousing the Mahāyāna mind without having seen the truths, versus after having seen them — divides uncertain śrāvakas along a line that inverts the intuition a reader might bring in: one would expect the person who has already seen the truths to be further along, and in the ordinary śrāvaka path they would be. But among these particular uncertain śrāvakas, both branches are explicitly called 軟品, of the dull grade — even having seen the truths does not exempt a person from the dull, gradual path once they are classified this way. What determines the pace of their eventual buddhahood is not how much śrāvaka attainment they already hold, but 由二樂涅槃, that both delight in nirvāṇa and grow weary of themselves — a disposition, not a stage.

7. The Five Sciences, and the Forty-Four Attentions That Fulfill the Perfections (Part 1)

偈曰：

菩薩習五明，　總為求種智，

Next: "I should praise the meaning of the perfections according to each person's capacity, to give rise to understanding in others" — praising.

Reading note

The forty-four attentions are unusual among this treatise's many lists in a specific way worth naming: they are not stated as third-person descriptions of a bodhisattva's qualities, the way the earlier chapters described the marks of arousing the mind or the marks of the lineage. Every single one is given as first-person reported speech — 我今應... "I should now..." — the bodhisattva's own inner monologue, quoted directly. The list is less a taxonomy of what a bodhisattva has than a script for what a bodhisattva says to himself, in order, as he actually cultivates the perfections. A reader working through this section is not being told about a bodhisattva's mind; the text is handing over the exact sentences to think.

為堅牢者，六六波羅蜜修 — the sixfold cultivation of each of the six perfections, generosity of generosity through generosity of wisdom, morality of generosity through morality of wisdom, and so on — produces thirty-six sub-practices nested inside a single item of this already enormous list. This is the treatise's most extreme instance of its own combinatorial habit, cross-multiplying one classification against another rather than simply enumerating each once, and it is worth noting as a limit case: even within a list this exhaustive, the text is willing to gesture at a further thirty-six-fold structure ("and so on") rather than actually spell it out, trusting the reader to complete the cross-product from the pattern given.

Reading note

The seven kinds of grasping at what is not present are worth reading as a deliberately symmetrical set of errors, each pairing a false attribution with its truth: existence attributed to the non-existent, non-fault to what is faulty, non-merit to what is meritorious, permanence to the impermanent, pleasure to the unpleasant, self to the selfless, and non-quiescence to what is in fact quiescent. Six of the seven invert a quality; only the last, grasping quiescence as non-quiescent, inverts the direction the other six run in, denying something true rather than affirming something false. Listing it seventh, after six errors of the same shape, is a quiet reminder that the practitioner's own resistance to accepting quiescence, once it is genuinely present, is itself one more instance of exactly the same fault as the others — not a separate, more sophisticated problem.

Read end to end, all forty-four attentions trace a single implicit narrative arc: knowing the cause (1) through knowing one's own supremacy (44) is the shape of a whole cultivation, from a bodhisattva's very first reflection on why the perfections can grow at all, to a settled, mature confidence that his own practice of them is unsurpassed. Nothing in the list is presented as optional or as an alternative path to any other item — each is a sentence in a sequence a practitioner is meant to actually think, in order, and the treatise's closing summary verse treats the entire forty-four as a single sustained practice rather than forty-four separate techniques to choose among.

Reading note

多慢, much conceit, assigned to the ground of faith and practice — the very earliest stage of the bodhisattva path, before the first ground — is a candid piece of psychological realism placed where a reader might least expect it. It would be easy to assume conceit belongs to advanced practitioners who have something to actually be conceited about; the treatise instead locates the most conceit at the stage with the least attainment, and steadily diminishes it as the grounds proceed — little conceit through the first seven, none at all in the last three. Read against fascicle 3's warning that a bodhisattva who reverently contemplates the Buddha's rare qualities while hoping to swiftly attain enlightenment "still has conceit," this thirteen-fold scheme gives that earlier warning a specific address: it is describing exactly the beginner's stage this list names first.

seeking both, attains the real;
according with all the world,

The chapter on seeking is complete.

Reading note

The ten discriminations and their ten remedies are the treatise's most direct citation of the Prajñāpāramitā literature anywhere in the text, and the pairing structure rewards close attention: each remedy is quoted, not paraphrased, and each addresses a discrimination that is the mirror image of its neighbor — absence of substance answered by "there is a bodhisattva," substance immediately answered by "one does not see a bodhisattva." The sūtra is not choosing a single correct statement among ten errors; it is offering ten separate correctives because ten separate, equally available mistakes exist, and curing one without the others would just open the door to whichever one was left unaddressed. The famous formula "form is emptiness, emptiness is form" turns out, in this treatise's reading, not to be a single paradoxical slogan but two of ten targeted remedies, each aimed at a specific and nameable discrimination.

The chapter on seeking closes on three kinds of greatness — method, benefiting others, and benefiting oneself — which quietly recapitulates the entire chapter's own shape. Everything from fascicle 4's three baskets through this fascicle's forty-four attentions has been, in one sense, an exercise in method: how to seek correctly. The chapter's final verse insists that this method was never separable from what it was for, self-benefit and the benefit of others together, "full as the sea" — the same image this treatise used to close its account of buddhahood itself in fascicle 3, now applied to the ordinary practitioner's own accumulated seeking.

Fascicle 5 Complete

The chapter on seeking closes here, having run through fascicles 4 and 5 as the treatise's most sustained classificatory stretch. This fascicle alone gave Consciousness-Only as two lights without a dual mark, the marked and the marking mapped onto the three natures, five domains of study, four gates of liberation, three-part proofs for the absence of self-nature and eight kinds of non-arising, eight reasons for the One Vehicle with their own subdivisions among uncertain śrāvakas, the five sciences, forty-four attentions spoken as the bodhisattva's own first-person reflection, thirteen distinctions and four causes for seeking the Dharma itself, and finally the ten discriminations the Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra answers in its own quoted words. Fascicle 6 opens two new chapters — 弘法品十三, on propagating the Dharma, and 隨修品十四, on cultivation in accordance with it — returning to a more conversational register after this fascicle's density.
