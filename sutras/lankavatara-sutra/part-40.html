The Sequence of Cessation-Attainment Across the Grounds

Mahāmati asks the Buddha to lay out, step by step, how cessation-attainment — the meditative absorption in which mental activity itself comes to rest — differs for bodhisattvas, śrāvakas, and pratyekabuddhas as they progress from the sixth ground onward. The answer traces a path where the very attainment that liberates the śrāvaka becomes, for the eighth-ground bodhisattva, a danger to be resisted: the buddhas must sustain such a bodhisattva by their own power, or he would slip into nirvāṇa and abandon the work of saving beings entirely. The passage closes with one of this sūtra's gentlest images — a man who dreams he is fording a great river, and wakes before he reaches the far shore.

The Ground-by-Ground Sequence: Six, Seven, Eight

Chinese Source

爾時，大慧菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！惟願為說一切菩薩、聲聞、緣覺滅正受次第相續。若善於滅正受次第相續相者，我及餘菩薩終不妄捨滅正受樂門，不墮一切聲聞、緣覺、外道愚癡。」佛告大慧：「諦聽，諦聽！善思念之，當為汝說。」大慧白佛言：「世尊！惟願為說。」佛告大慧：「六地起菩薩摩訶薩及聲聞、緣覺，入滅正受。第七地菩薩摩訶薩，念念正受，離一切性自性相正受，非聲聞、緣覺。諸聲聞、緣覺，墮有行，攝所攝相滅正受，是故七地非念正受。得一切法無差別相，非分得種種相性，覺一切法善不善性相正受，是故七地無善念正受。大慧！八地菩薩及聲聞、緣覺，心、意、意識妄想相滅。初地乃至七地菩薩摩訶薩，觀三界心、意、意識量，離我我所，自妄想修，墮外性種種相。愚夫二種自心，攝所攝，向無知，不覺無始過惡，虛偽習氣所薰。大慧！八地菩薩摩訶薩、聲聞、緣覺涅槃。菩薩者，三昧覺所持，是故三昧門樂，不般涅槃。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Mahāmati Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, I only wish you would explain the graduated sequence of cessation-attainment shared by every bodhisattva, śrāvaka, and pratyekabuddha. Should I become skilled in the marks of this graduated sequence, then neither I nor any other bodhisattva would ever wrongly abandon the gateway to the bliss of cessation-attainment, nor fall into the folly shared by every śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, and outsider." The Buddha told Mahāmati: "Listen carefully, listen carefully, and reflect well on this — I shall explain it for you." Mahāmati said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, I only wish you would explain it." The Buddha told Mahāmati: "Bodhisattva-mahāsattvas from the sixth ground onward, together with śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, enter cessation-attainment. The seventh-ground bodhisattva-mahāsattva attains it moment by moment, apart from the marks of every nature's own-nature — this is not shared by śrāvakas or pratyekabuddhas. Every śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha, falling into conditioned activity, attains cessation through [still] apprehending marks as apprehender and apprehended — this is why the seventh ground's attainment is not the 'moment by moment' kind. [The seventh-ground bodhisattva] attains the markless differentiation of every dharma, not a partial grasp of its manifold marks and natures, becoming aware, in attainment, of every dharma's wholesome and unwholesome natures and marks — this is why the seventh ground has no attainment [still] caught up in wholesome thought [as an object]. Mahāmati, for the eighth-ground bodhisattva, together with the śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha, the marks of false thought belonging to mind, will, and consciousness themselves come to cease. From the first ground through the seventh, the bodhisattva-mahāsattva observes the measure of the three realms' mind, will, and consciousness, apart from self and what belongs to self — yet, cultivating his own false thought, still falls into the manifold marks of external nature. The foolish and ignorant, caught between the two kinds of self-mind, apprehender and apprehended, remain entirely without knowledge, unaware of the beginningless faults and false habituation with which they have been permeated. Mahāmati, at the eighth ground, the bodhisattva-mahāsattva, together with the śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha, [reaches] nirvāṇa — but the bodhisattva is sustained by the awareness of samādhi, and it is for this reason that, though he delights in the gateway of samādhi, he does not enter final nirvāṇa.

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators frame the whole passage as addressing four distinct points of comparison, the first being the gap between the sixth and seventh grounds: "六地是三乘同入滅盡定，此位最淺，故菩薩所得之定未異二乘也。七地菩薩念念正受等者，念念則出入無間，離一切性自性相正受，則不取攝所攝一切相，故不同二乘墮有行覺" — "at the sixth ground, all three vehicles alike enter cessation-attainment; this stage is the shallowest, so the bodhisattva's attainment here is not yet different from the two lesser vehicles'. As for the seventh-ground bodhisattva's 'moment by moment' attainment: 'moment by moment' means entering and emerging without interruption, apart from the marks of every nature's own-nature, no longer grasping any mark as apprehender or apprehended — this is why it differs from the two lesser vehicles' falling into conditioned awareness." A śrāvaka must first still every mark before attaining cessation; the seventh-ground bodhisattva has already stopped grasping marks as such, so the cessation itself never needs to be manufactured moment to moment — it is simply already there.

On why the eighth ground marks such a sharp turn — the very point where śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, and bodhisattva alike "reach nirvāṇa," yet only the bodhisattva is held back — the commentators explain: "菩薩以諸佛三昧覺力所加持故，為化眾生，於三昧門不般涅槃。若不加持，則不能功行滿足，到於如來之地，是棄捨眾生而不化度，亦斷如來種性" — "the bodhisattva, sustained by the power of the buddhas' own samādhi-awareness, does not enter final nirvāṇa within the gateway of samādhi, for the sake of transforming sentient beings. Were he not so sustained, he would be unable to bring his practice to its full completion and reach the Tathāgata's own ground — this would mean abandoning sentient beings without delivering them, and would also cut off the very lineage of buddhahood." At exactly the ground where liberation becomes fully available, the bodhisattva's vow becomes the only thing standing between him and an exit the two lesser vehicles are free to take.

Held Back by the Buddhas, and the Dream of Crossing a River

Chinese Source

若不持者，如來地不滿足，棄捨一切為眾生事，佛種則斷，諸佛世尊為示如來不可思議無量功德。聲聞、緣覺三昧門，得樂所牽故，作涅槃想。大慧！我分部七地，善修心、意、意識相，善修我我所，攝受人法無我，生滅自共相，善四無礙，決定力三昧門，地次第相續，入道品法。不令菩薩摩訶薩不覺自共相，不善七地，墮外道邪徑，故立地次第。大慧！彼實無有若生若滅，除自心現量。所謂地次第相續，及三界種種行，愚夫所不覺。愚夫所不覺者，謂我及諸佛說地次第相續，及說三界種種行。「復次，大慧！聲聞、緣覺、第八菩薩地，滅三昧樂門醉所醉，不善自心現量，自共相習氣所障。墮人法無我法攝受見，妄想涅槃想，非寂滅智慧覺。大慧！菩薩者，見滅三昧門樂，本願哀愍，大悲成就，知分別十無盡句，不妄想涅槃想。彼已涅槃妄想不生故，離攝所攝妄想。覺了自心現量，一切諸法妄想不生。不墮心、意、意識，外性自性相計著妄想。非佛法因不生，隨智慧生，得如來自覺地。如人夢中方便度水，未度而覺。覺已思惟，為正為邪？非正非邪。餘無始見聞覺識因想，種種習氣，種種形處，墮有無想，心、意、意識夢現。大慧！如是菩薩摩訶薩，於第八菩薩地，見妄想生。從初地轉進至第七地，見一切法如幻等，方便度攝所攝心妄想，行已，作佛法方便，未得者令得。大慧！此是菩薩涅槃方便不懷，離心、意、意識，得無生法忍。大慧！於第一義無次第相續，說無所有妄想寂滅法。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Were he not so sustained, the Tathāgata's own ground would remain unfulfilled, he would abandon every task undertaken for the sake of sentient beings, and the seed of buddhahood would be cut off — this is why the buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, reveal to him the Tathāgata's own inconceivable, immeasurable merit. The śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha, drawn along by the bliss found within the gateway of samādhi, form the very notion of 'nirvāṇa' there. Mahāmati, I have divided the seventh ground into its own category: skillfully cultivating the marks of mind, will, and consciousness; skillfully cultivating self and what belongs to self; embracing the two kinds of selflessness, of persons and of dharmas; the shared and particular marks of birth and cessation; skill in the four unobstructed forms of eloquence; and the gateway of decisive samādhi-power — the grounds proceed in graduated succession, entering into the very factors that constitute the path. So that the bodhisattva-mahāsattva should not fail to be aware of shared and particular marks, and should not, through unfamiliarity with the seventh ground, fall into the outsiders' own mistaken path, the graduated sequence of the grounds has been established for this reason. Mahāmati, in truth, there is no birth and no cessation whatsoever apart from the very measure of one's own mind's manifestation — what is called 'the graduated succession of the grounds' and 'the manifold activities of the three realms' is simply what the foolish and ignorant fail to be aware of. What the foolish and ignorant fail to be aware of is that I, and every buddha, speak of the graduated succession of the grounds, and of the manifold activities of the three realms, [only as skillful means]. Furthermore, Mahāmati, the śrāvaka, the pratyekabuddha, and the bodhisattva at the eighth ground are, alike, intoxicated by the very intoxication of the bliss found within the gateway of cessation-samādhi, unskilled in the measure of their own mind's manifestation, obstructed by habituation to shared and particular marks — falling into a view that embraces selflessness of both person and dharma [as itself an object], forming a false notion of nirvāṇa there, which is not the awakened wisdom of true cessation. Mahāmati, the bodhisattva, though he too sees the bliss found within the gateway of cessation-samādhi, is moved by his original vow of compassion, accomplished in great compassion, understanding and discerning the ten inexhaustible vows, and does not form a false notion of 'nirvāṇa' there. Since, for him, no false thought of having already attained nirvāṇa arises, he is apart from false thought as apprehender and apprehended. Becoming aware that this is the very measure of his own mind's manifestation, false thought concerning every dharma no longer arises within him; he does not fall into attachment and false thought concerning mind, will, and consciousness, or the marks of external own-nature. It is not that the very cause of the Buddha's own Dharma fails to arise in him — rather, it arises in accordance with wisdom, and he attains the Tathāgata's own self-realized ground. It is like a man who, in a dream, is fording a river by skillful means, and wakes before he has finished crossing it; once awake, he reflects: was this true, or was it false? It is neither true nor false. All that remains is the residue of beginningless seeing, hearing, sensing, and cognizing, its causal thoughts, its manifold habituation, and its manifold forms, falling into notions of being and non-being — the dream-appearance of mind, will, and consciousness. Mahāmati, just so, the bodhisattva-mahāsattva, at the eighth bodhisattva ground, sees false thought arising [like this dream]. Having advanced from the first ground through the seventh, seeing every dharma as like an illusion and the rest of the skillful means, having already carried out the practice of freeing the mind's false thought from apprehender and apprehended, he takes up the Buddha's own skillful means, causing those who have not yet attained to attain. Mahāmati, this is the bodhisattva's skillful means toward nirvāṇa never being abandoned — apart from mind, will, and consciousness, he attains the forbearance of no-birth. Mahāmati, within ultimate meaning there is no graduated succession at all — what is spoken of is a dharma of stillness in which there is no false thought whatsoever."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators single out the phrase "十無盡句" (the ten inexhaustible vows) as the specific mechanism that keeps a bodhisattva from mistaking cessation's bliss for nirvāṇa the way the two lesser vehicles do: "本願哀愍，大悲成就，知分別十無盡句，不妄想涅槃想" is glossed simply as remembering the vow to save beings even while tasting the very bliss that, for a śrāvaka, would already count as liberation — the vow itself is what keeps the door from closing.

On the dream-crossing-a-river parable, the commentators read it as a precise map of the eighth ground's peculiar position, not a generic illustration of impermanence: "此喻菩薩自行化他之法，意謂夢時非無，覺已非有，而乃非實非虛。正喻八地菩薩始見實理，終於度生，一以如幻三昧建立故，如夢時作用。及得無生法忍，顯無功用道，如覺已無得" — "this illustrates the bodhisattva's own practice and his work of transforming others: while dreaming, it is not that nothing is there; once awake, it is not that something truly was there — it is thus neither real nor false. This precisely illustrates how the eighth-ground bodhisattva first glimpses ultimate truth, yet still carries the work of saving beings through to the end, established entirely upon an illusion-like samādhi, functioning just as one functions within a dream. And once he attains the forbearance of no-birth, this reveals the path of effortless action — like waking, with nothing further left to attain." The man in the dream is not wrong to have crossed the river, nor is the crossing simply erased by waking — the ford was real within the dream's own terms, exactly as a bodhisattva's compassionate activity remains real and necessary even once its ultimately illusion-like nature has been fully seen.

Closing Verse: The Measure of Mind Has No Fixed Sequence

Chinese Source

爾時，世尊欲重宣此義而說偈言：

「心量無所有，　此住及佛地，
去來及現在，　三世諸佛說。

心量地第七，　無所有第八，
二地名為住，　佛地名最勝。

自覺智及淨，　此則是我地，
自在最勝處，　清淨妙莊嚴。

照曜如盛火，　光明悉遍至，
熾炎不壞目，　周輪化三有。

化現在三有，　或有先時化，
於彼演說乘，　皆是如來地。

十地則為初，　初則為八地，
第九則為七，　七亦復為八；
第二為第三，　第四為第五，
第三為第六，　無所有何次。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, wishing to restate this meaning once more, the World-Honored One spoke in verse:

"The measure of mind is itself nothing at all — this is what is meant by 'abiding,' and by the Buddha's own ground; past, future, and present alike, this is what every buddha of the three time periods teaches.

The measure of mind belongs to the seventh ground; having-nothing-at-all belongs to the eighth. These two grounds alone are called 'abiding'; the Buddha's own ground alone is called supreme.

Self-realized wisdom, and its purity — this alone is my own ground: the place of mastery and supremacy, adorned with pure and wondrous splendor.

Blazing forth like a great fire, its light reaching everywhere at once, its brilliant flame never harming the eye, it circles round, transforming the three realms of existence.

Manifesting within the three realms of existence, or, at times, manifesting even before them, expounding there the [various] vehicles — all of this is the Tathāgata's own ground.

The tenth ground is itself the first; the first is itself the eighth; the ninth is itself the seventh, and the seventh is itself the eighth as well; the second is the third, the fourth is the fifth, the third is the sixth — where, then, is there any sequence at all?"

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators explain why only the seventh and eighth grounds, among all ten, are singled out and called "abiding": "為七地猶存心量，八地則無所有，故約此二地對明分齊，偏得住名。住者謂依止也。八地雖無所有，猶有定住，唯佛地為最勝也" — "because the seventh ground still retains the measure of mind, while the eighth has nothing at all, these two grounds alone are contrasted to clarify the boundary, and are singled out to receive the name 'abiding.' 'Abiding' means having something to rely upon. Though the eighth ground has nothing at all, it still abides within samādhi — only the Buddha's own ground is called supreme." Every other ground below the seventh is still visibly progressing through stages of mind; the seventh is the last to retain that measure at all, the eighth has let even that go while still resting in samādhi, and only buddhahood itself has nothing left to rest upon.

On the closing riddle — each numbered ground abruptly declared identical to some other, seemingly unrelated one — the commentators read this not as a cipher to be decoded numerically but as a direct enactment of what it teaches: "乃以不次顯其圓融，究竟而言，寂滅真如有何位次？故云『無所有何次』也" — "this uses a jumbled, out-of-sequence order to reveal the very perfect interfusion [of all the grounds]; ultimately speaking, what sequence could stillness and suchness themselves possibly have? This is why it says, 'having nothing at all, what sequence could there be?'" The graduated ten-ground path that this whole passage has just carefully laid out — sixth, then seventh, then eighth — is, in its very last line, dissolved back into the single ground beyond all sequence that every stage was only ever pointing toward.

Part 40 Complete

This closes Part 40: the graduated sequence of cessation-attainment across the sixth, seventh, and eighth bodhisattva grounds, the bodhisattva sustained by the buddhas' power and his own vow against the pull of premature nirvāṇa, the dream-crossing-a-river parable, and the closing verse dissolving the entire numbered sequence back into a single ground beyond sequence altogether.
