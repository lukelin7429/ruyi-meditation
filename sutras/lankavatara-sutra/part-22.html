Self-Realized Wisdom, and the One Vehicle

Mahāmati asks for the mark of self-realized noble wisdom itself, and for the one vehicle every other vehicle ultimately serves. The Buddha answers that self-realization is reached alone, in solitude, not handed down by another — and that the one vehicle is reached only when mind's own turning finally comes to rest. He then explains, directly, why he teaches three vehicles to most beings rather than announcing the single true vehicle outright: śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas have not yet exhausted the subtlest habituated force of affliction, and cannot yet be told the whole truth without being lost by it. The fascicle closes with the image of a drunkard who wakes only once the wine itself has finally worn off — not sooner.

Self-Realized Wisdom, and the One Vehicle

Chinese Source

大慧菩薩摩訶薩復白佛言：「世尊！唯願為說自覺聖智相及一乘，若說自覺聖智相及一乘，我及餘菩薩善自覺聖智相及一乘，不由於他，通達佛法。」

佛告大慧：「諦聽，諦聽！善思念之，當為汝說。」

大慧白佛言：「唯然受教。」

佛告大慧：「前聖所知，轉相傳授，妄想無性，菩薩摩訶薩，獨一靜處，自覺觀察，不由於他，離見妄想，上上昇進，入如來地，是名自覺聖智相。大慧！云何一乘相？謂：得一乘道覺，我說一乘。云何得一乘道覺？謂：攝所攝妄想，如實處不生妄想，是名一乘覺。大慧！一乘覺者，非餘外道、聲聞、緣覺、梵天王等之所能得，唯除如來。以是故，說名一乘。」

大慧白佛言：「世尊何故說三乘，而不說一乘？」

佛告大慧：「不自般涅槃法故，不說一切聲聞、緣覺一乘。以一切聲聞、緣覺，如來調伏，授寂靜方便，而得解脫，非自己力，是故不說一乘。「復次，大慧！煩惱障業習氣不斷，故不說一切聲聞、緣覺一乘。不覺法無我，不離分段死，故說三乘。大慧！彼諸一切起煩惱過習氣斷，及覺法無我。彼一切起煩惱過習氣斷，三昧樂味著非性，無漏界覺。覺已，復入出世間上上無漏界，滿足眾具，當得如來不思議自在法身。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Mahāmati Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva again said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, I beg you to teach the mark of self-realized noble wisdom, and the one vehicle. Should you teach the mark of self-realized noble wisdom and the one vehicle, I and the other bodhisattvas, skilled in the mark of self-realized noble wisdom and the one vehicle, will penetrate the Buddha's own teaching without relying on anyone else at all."

The Buddha told Mahāmati: "Listen well! Listen well! Consider this carefully — I shall now explain it to you."

Mahāmati said to the Buddha: "I gladly receive your teaching."

The Buddha told Mahāmati: "What the noble ones before you have known has been handed down, each to the next in turn — that false thought has no [fixed] nature at all. A bodhisattva-mahāsattva, alone in a solitary place, observes this for himself, through his own self-realization, relying on no one else at all; leaving behind the false thought bound up with [any fixed] view, he advances further and further, entering the very ground of the Tathāgata — this is called the mark of self-realized noble wisdom. Mahāmati, what is the mark of the one vehicle? It is that, once one has attained the awakening of the one-vehicle path, I speak of it as 'the one vehicle.' How does one attain the awakening of the one-vehicle path? It is that, [with respect to] the false thought bound up with the grasped and the grasper, no false thought arises within the very place where reality actually is — this is called the awakening of the one vehicle. Mahāmati, the awakening of the one vehicle is not something any of the outsiders, the śrāvakas, the pratyekabuddhas, or even Brahmā, lord of the heavens, is able to attain — only the Tathāgata excepted. It is for this reason that it is spoken of as 'the one vehicle.'"

Mahāmati said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, why do you teach three vehicles, and not simply teach the one vehicle?"

The Buddha told Mahāmati: "It is because [the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas] do not, of their own accord, attain the very dharma of ultimate nirvāṇa that I do not teach every śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha the one vehicle. Every śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha attains liberation through the Tathāgata's own taming, given the skillful means of stillness — not through his own power alone; it is for this reason that the one vehicle is not taught to them. Furthermore, Mahāmati, it is because the habituated force of the hindrance of affliction and of karma has not yet been cut off [in them] that the one vehicle is not taught to every śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha. Because they have not yet realized the no-self of dharmas, and have not yet left behind death by discrete increments, three vehicles are taught instead. Mahāmati, it is only once every arising affliction's fault and habituated force is cut off, and the no-self of dharmas is realized — once every arising affliction's fault and habituated force is cut off, and even the taste of attachment to the bliss of samādhi is seen to have no [fixed] nature — that the undefiled realm is realized. Having realized it, one enters, in turn, the supramundane realm higher still, which is entirely undefiled, becomes fully complete with every quality, and attains the Tathāgata's own inconceivable, sovereign dharma-body."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators are precise that self-realized noble wisdom being attained "alone, in a solitary place... relying on no one else" does not mean it comes from nowhere, and open their gloss by naming the unbroken lineage it nonetheless depends on: "前聖所知，即自證聖智行相及一乘行相，謂過去諸佛無不從此二種行門成等正覺，以其遞相傳授，政所謂佛佛授受、祖祖承承者也" — "'what the noble ones before you have known' refers precisely to the practice of self-realized noble wisdom and the practice of the one vehicle — every buddha of the past, without exception, attained complete enlightenment through exactly these same two practices, handed down from one to the next in succession; this is precisely what is meant by 'buddha receiving from buddha, patriarch inheriting from patriarch.'" The solitude is real — no one else can do the realizing for you — but it takes place within, not apart from, a continuous transmission stretching back through every prior buddha.

On why the one vehicle is reserved for the Buddha alone, excluding even Brahmā and the two lesser vehicles, the commentators tie the exclusion directly to the mechanics named just before it: "謂了根塵能取所取妄心，即妄顯真，如實而住，故云『如實處不生妄想』" — "it means fully understanding that the grasping and grasped mind bound up with sense-faculty and sense-object is itself false; displaying the true directly through that very falseness, one abides exactly where reality actually is — hence it is said that 'no false thought arises within the very place where reality actually is.'" This is a genuinely comprehensive realization, leaving no residual false thought of any kind — which is precisely why the three vehicles that follow, each still carrying some unresolved habituated force, are explained as the graduated, provisional path toward this one uncompromising standard, not as three separate final destinations of their own.

The Drunkard Who Wakes

Chinese Source

爾時，世尊欲重宣此義而說偈言：
「諸天及梵乘，　聲聞、緣覺乘，
諸佛如來乘，　我說此諸乘，
乃至有心轉，　諸乘非究竟。

若彼心滅盡，　無乘及乘者，
無有乘建立，　我說為一乘，
引導眾生故，　分別說諸乘。

解脫有三種，　及與法無我，
煩惱智慧等，　解脫則遠離。

譬如海浮木，　常隨波浪轉，
聲聞愚亦然，　相風所飄蕩。

彼起煩惱滅，　除習煩惱愚，
味著三昧樂，　安住無漏界。

無有究竟趣，　亦復不退還，
得諸三昧身，　乃至劫不覺。

譬如昏醉人，　酒消然後覺，
彼覺法亦然，　得佛無上身。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke in verse:
"The vehicle of the gods and of Brahmā, the vehicle of the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha,
the vehicle of every buddha, of the Tathāgata — these are the vehicles I have taught;
yet so long as mind itself still turns at all, no vehicle among them is truly ultimate.

Only once that very mind is utterly extinguished is there no vehicle, and no one who rides it either —
with no vehicle left to establish at all, it is this alone that I call the one vehicle;
it is solely to guide sentient beings along that I have distinguished and taught these several vehicles.

Liberation is of three kinds, together with the no-self of dharmas;
affliction and wisdom, held as though equal — true liberation is far removed from all of this.

It is like driftwood upon the ocean, forever turning along with the waves;
the śrāvaka's own ignorance is just the same, buffeted about by the wind of [conditioned] marks.

The afflictions that arise from this are extinguished, yet the residual habituated force of ignorance remains;
tasting attachment to the bliss of samādhi, he abides, content, within the undefiled realm.

With no further destination sought, and yet never falling back either,
he attains a body dwelling in samādhi after samādhi — remaining, for kalpa after kalpa, unaware.

It is like a person deep in drunken stupor, who awakens only once the wine itself has worn off —
his own awakening to the Dharma is just the same, and it is then that he attains the Buddha's own unsurpassed body."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators are careful that "so long as mind itself still turns at all, no vehicle among them is truly ultimate" applies without exception — even, on their reading, to the buddha vehicle as named in the list just before it: "言有心轉者，轉即起動，謂若有一念心動，雖佛乘亦非究竟，況餘乘乎" — "'so long as mind still turns' means: wherever even a single thought stirs into motion, even the buddha vehicle, [named here as one item on the list,] is not yet truly ultimate — how much less, then, the other vehicles?" The "one vehicle" that follows is not simply a fifth item added to make the list complete; it names the state reached only once every vehicle, every rider, and the very framework of "vehicles" as a category have all been left behind together — which is why the commentators gloss it as reached only "於心行處滅" ("where the very functioning of mind itself has ceased"), not by climbing to the top of a hierarchy that still includes it as one rung.

On the closing drunkard simile, the commentators are explicit that the delay it describes is not laziness or failure on the śrāvaka's part, but the simple, unavoidable time a residual habituated force takes to exhaust itself: "此三昧身墮無為坑，乃至經劫不覺。譬如世人醉酒昏亂，都無覺知，至於酒消而後乃覺" — "this body dwelling in samādhi has, in a sense, fallen into the very pit of the unconditioned, remaining, for kalpa after kalpa, without full awareness — just as a person deep in drunken confusion has no awareness at all, and awakens only once the wine itself has finally worn off." Nothing accelerates the drunkard's own waking except the wine's own residue actually running its course — and the commentators' closing gloss ties this directly back to the fascicle's central concern: "此喻二乘根轉心，迴覺法無我究竟正智" — "this illustrates how the two lesser vehicles' own faculties eventually turn, their minds coming round at last to the ultimate, correct wisdom of the no-self of dharmas" — the same realization Fascicle One's opening pages, and this entire fascicle, have been building toward all along.

Fascicle Two Complete

This closes Fascicle Two of the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra: the tathāgatagarbha shown to be identical to no-self shaped under a gentler name; the four dharmas culminating in the mind-made body; the six kinds of cause and four kinds of speech; a dozen similes against the four extremes; the Buddha's own way of teaching, apart from every fixed position yet still built up step by step; the four kinds of dhyāna and the beginning of the teaching on nirvāṇa; two kinds of spiritual power and a hard internal challenge to dependent origination; what "permanence" and confusion actually mean; names, phrases, and the questions met only with silence; the four fruits of the śrāvaka path; two kinds of awareness and the four great elements; twelve kinds of false thought; and, closing the fascicle, self-realized wisdom, the one vehicle, and the drunkard who wakes only once the wine has worn off.
