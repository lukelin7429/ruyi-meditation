Is the Buddha's "Unborn" Any Different from the Outsiders' Own?

Mahāmati now presses the Buddha with the sharpest challenge in this whole sūtra: point by point, everything the Buddha teaches under "unborn and unceasing" already has an outsider counterpart — their causes are unborn too, their nine primal substances are unborn too, their four elements never perish either. If there is truly no difference, then every outsider teacher is already a buddha, and the very idea of a world having only one buddha at a time collapses. The Buddha's answer does not deny the surface resemblance; it locates the real difference somewhere the resemblance can never reach.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 37 in progress Source: Taishō T0670, 楞伽阿跋多羅寶經 (tr. Guṇabhadra, 443 CE) Reading notes after Zongle and Ruqi's 楞伽阿跋多羅寶經註解 (T1789)

Mahāmati's Fourfold Challenge

Chinese Source

爾時，大慧菩薩，復承佛威神而白佛言：「世尊！世尊顯示不生不滅，無有奇特。所以者何？一切外道因，亦不生不滅。世尊亦說虛空非數緣滅，及涅槃界不生不滅。世尊！外道說因，生諸世間。世尊亦說無明愛業妄想為緣，生諸世間。彼因此緣，名差別耳，外物因緣亦如是。如是，世尊！與外道論無有差別。微塵勝妙自在眾生主等，如是九物不生不滅。世尊亦說一切性不生不滅，有無不可得。外道亦說四大不壞，自性不生不滅。四大常。是四大，乃至周流諸趣不捨自性。世尊所說亦復如是。是故我言無有奇特。惟願世尊，為說差別，所以奇特勝諸外道。若無差別者，一切外道皆亦是佛。以不生不滅故，而世尊說，一世界中多佛出世者，無有是處。如向所說，一世界中應有多佛，無差別故。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Mahāmati Bodhisattva, again relying on the Buddha's majestic spiritual power, said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, the World-Honored One's teaching of 'unborn and unceasing' contains nothing extraordinary at all. Why do I say this? Because every outsider's own cause is likewise unborn and unceasing. The World-Honored One also teaches that space, cessation not through deliberate effort, and the realm of nirvāṇa are unborn and unceasing — but the outsiders, too, teach that a cause gives rise to every world, just as the World-Honored One teaches that ignorance, craving, karma, and false thought, taken as conditions, give rise to every world. Their 'cause' and this 'condition' are merely different names for the same thing, and it is the same with the causes and conditions of outer things as well — in which case, World-Honored One, this is no different at all from the outsiders' own theories. Atoms, the Excellent One, Īśvara, the Lord of Beings, and the rest — these nine substances are held to be unborn and unceasing; the World-Honored One likewise teaches that every nature is unborn and unceasing, and that being and non-being can never ultimately be apprehended. The outsiders, too, teach that the four great elements are indestructible, that their own-nature is unborn and unceasing, that the four great elements are permanent, and that these four great elements, circulating through every destiny, never once relinquish their own-nature — and what the World-Honored One teaches is exactly the same. It is for this reason that I say there is nothing extraordinary here at all. I only wish the World-Honored One would explain the difference — what makes his teaching extraordinary, surpassing every outsider's. For if there is truly no difference, then every outsider is already a buddha as well, since they too teach 'unborn and unceasing' — yet the World-Honored One teaches that it is impossible for more than one buddha to appear in a single world at the same time. But as has just been shown, a single world ought to have many buddhas at once, since there is no difference between them."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators name the exact shape of Mahāmati's challenge as a set of four distinct parallels, each pairing a Buddhist teaching with its apparent outsider counterpart: "凡有四難：一、以外道說生法之因與佛所說三無為法為難...二、以彼生因與佛所說十二緣生為難。三、以微塵等生與佛所說一切性不生滅為難...四、以彼說四大與世尊所說四大為難" — "there are four challenges in all: first, contrasting the outsiders' account of what gives rise to phenomena against the Buddha's three unconditioned dharmas [space, cessation not through deliberate effort, and nirvāṇa]; second, contrasting their generative cause against the Buddha's twelvefold dependent origination; third, contrasting atoms and the rest against the Buddha's teaching that every nature is unborn and unceasing; and fourth, contrasting their four great elements against the World-Honored One's own four great elements." The commentators also gloss the nine substances by name: "九物者，一時、二方、三虛空、四微塵、五四大種、六大梵天、七勝妙天、八大自在天、九眾生主即神我也" — "the nine substances are: (1) time, (2) direction, (3) space, (4) atoms, (5) the four great elements, (6) Mahābrahmā, (7) the Excellent Heaven, (8) Maheśvara, and (9) the Lord of Beings, that is, the self." Reading the challenge this way clarifies that Mahāmati is not raising a single vague worry but methodically testing every major category of the Buddha's teaching against its nearest outsider analogue, forcing a precise answer rather than a general one.

The Buddha's Answer: Not Falling into Being and Non-Being

Chinese Source

佛告大慧：「我說不生不滅，不同外道不生不滅。所以者何？彼諸外道，有性自性，得不生不變相。我不如是墮有無品。大慧！我者離有無品，離生滅，非性非無性。如種種幻夢現，故非無性。云何無性？謂色無自性相攝受，現不現故，攝不攝故。以是故，一切性無性非無性，但覺自心現量，妄想不生，安隱快樂，世事永息。愚癡凡夫，妄想作事，非諸賢聖。不實妄想，如揵闥婆城，及幻化人。大慧！如揵闥婆城及幻化人，種種眾生商賈出入。愚夫妄想謂真出入，而實無有出者入者，但彼妄想故。如是，大慧！愚癡凡夫，起不生不滅，彼亦無有有為無為。如幻人生，其實無有若生若滅，性無性無所有故。一切法亦如是，離於生滅。愚癡凡夫，墮不如實，起生滅妄想，非諸賢聖。不如實者，不爾。如性自性妄想，亦不異。若異妄想者，計著一切性自性，不見寂靜。不見寂靜者，終不離妄想。是故，大慧！無相見勝。非相見相者，受生因，故不勝。大慧！無相者，妄想不生，不起不滅，我說涅槃。大慧！涅槃者，如真實義見，離先妄想心心數法，逮得如來自覺聖智，我說是涅槃。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha told Mahāmati: "What I teach as 'unborn and unceasing' is not the same as the outsiders' own 'unborn and unceasing.' Why is this? Because those outsiders hold that their own-nature has a substantial nature of its own, and thereby attain a mark that is unborn and unchanging — falling, in this very way, into the [fixed] categories of being and non-being. I do not do this. Mahāmati, I am apart from the categories of being and non-being, apart from birth and cessation, neither having a fixed nature nor lacking one entirely. Because things appear the way all manner of illusions and dreams appear, this is not the same as having no nature at all. What, then, is meant by 'no nature'? It means that form, apprehended under the mark of its own-nature, either appears or fails to appear, and is either grasped or not grasped [depending entirely on conditions] — and it is for this reason that every nature is without a fixed nature, yet not simply nonexistent either. One need only become aware that this is the very measure of one's own mind's manifestation, so that false thought no longer arises, and one rests secure and joyful, with all worldly affairs forever stilled. It is the foolish and ignorant, caught up in false thought, who fabricate all this activity — not the wise and noble ones. Unreal false thought is like a gandharva city, or like a magically conjured person. Mahāmati, just as with a gandharva city or a magically conjured person, all kinds of beings — merchants among them — seem to come and go within it: the fool's own false thought takes this coming and going to be real, when in truth there is no one who comes and no one who goes at all — it is nothing but that very false thought. Just so, Mahāmati, when the foolish and ignorant give rise to a mistaken notion of 'unborn and unceasing,' they too have, in truth, neither the conditioned nor the unconditioned — like a conjured person's 'birth,' which in truth involves no birth and no cessation at all, since its own-nature, whether present or absent, has no substance whatsoever. Every dharma is exactly the same, apart from birth and cessation. The foolish and ignorant, falling short of what is truly real, give rise to false thoughts of birth and cessation — this is not the way of the wise and noble. What is not truly real is simply not so; and, [rightly understood], the false thought about own-nature and own-nature itself are likewise not different from each other. Were one to suppose that they truly differed, one would become attached to the own-nature of every dharma as something fixed, and would fail to see stillness itself. Failing to see stillness, one is, in the end, never able to leave false thought behind. It is for this reason, Mahāmati, that seeing the markless is superior; seeing what bears a mark as though it were the mark itself is not superior, since it is the very cause of taking birth. Mahāmati, the markless is where false thought no longer arises, where nothing is generated and nothing ceases — this is what I call nirvāṇa. Mahāmati, nirvāṇa is seeing things exactly as they truly are, apart from all prior false-thought states of mind and mental factors, attaining the Tathāgata's own self-realized noble wisdom — this is what I call nirvāṇa."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators locate the entire reply in a single distinction, stated plainly before any of the parables are introduced: "言外道有性自性等，謂彼所說性有自性...雖曰不生不滅，實有生滅，墮於有無。佛謂我不如是，我之所說不生不滅，離於有無、生滅及非有非無" — "when it says the outsiders hold their own-nature to have a substantial nature... though they call it 'unborn and unceasing,' it is in truth subject to birth and cessation, since it falls into being and non-being. The Buddha says: I am not like this — what I teach as 'unborn and unceasing' is apart from being and non-being, apart from birth and cessation, and apart from both 'is' and 'is-not' as well." On the surface, both sides use the same four syllables; the outsiders arrive at "unborn and unceasing" by fixing some substance as permanently existent, while the Buddha arrives at the identical phrase by seeing that no fixed substance was ever there to begin with — two paths that produce the same words but point in opposite directions.

On why the gandharva-city parable is placed exactly here rather than elsewhere in the sūtra, the commentators tie it directly back to the outsiders' error just described: "凡世間有為生滅之事起於妄心，此乃凡夫所迷...乾闥婆城，喻妄境不實" — "every conditioned event of birth and cessation in the world arises from a deluded mind; this is precisely what the ordinary person is confused about... the gandharva city illustrates that a false objective realm is not real." And on the closing definition of nirvāṇa, the commentators read "無相見勝" as the turning point of the entire passage: "既云著相，不見寂靜，則欲見寂靜以無相為勝，非是相見...蓋著相見者是生死之因，故不為勝" — "since attachment to marks means failing to see stillness, seeing stillness requires taking the markless as superior, not seeing through marks themselves... for attachment to marks through seeing is precisely the cause of taking birth, and is therefore not superior." The whole fourfold challenge, on this reading, is answered not by denying any surface resemblance to outsider teaching, but by showing that the Buddha's "unborn" never once fixes a nature the way theirs does — the real difference was never in the vocabulary at all.

Part 37 Complete

This closes Part 37: Mahāmati's fourfold challenge that the Buddha's "unborn and unceasing" merely restates what the outsiders already teach under other names, and the Buddha's answer that the real difference lies not in the phrase itself but in whether one still fixes being and non-being as substantial — illustrated through the gandharva city and the conjured person, and closing with the definition of nirvāṇa as seeing the markless.
