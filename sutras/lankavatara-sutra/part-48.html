Why Does the Tathāgata Still Experience Karmic Consequences?

Mahāmati asks the boldest question of the entire sūtra, folding seven distinct puzzles into a single breath: if arhats are predicted to attain the very same enlightenment as any bodhisattva, who is even left to walk the path? If the Buddha never spoke a single word from his awakening to his final nirvāṇa, what was all his teaching? If he acts without deliberation, why does Vajrapāṇi need to guard him at all — and how could this same all-knowing Buddha still walk into false accusations, an empty almsbowl, and every other misfortune tradition records of his life? The Buddha answers each question in turn, and the pattern that emerges runs through the rest of this fascicle: what looks like contradiction dissolves once the difference between the Tathāgata's true nature and his transformation-bodies is properly understood.

Mahāmati's Seven Questions

Chinese Source

爾時，大慧菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！世尊記阿羅漢得成阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，與諸菩薩等無差別。一切眾生法不涅槃，誰至佛道？從初得佛，至般涅槃，於其中間不說一字，亦無所答。如來常定故，亦無慮，亦無察，化佛化作佛事。何故說識剎那展轉壞相。金剛力士，常隨侍衛。不施設本際。現魔魔業，惡業果報，旃遮摩納、孫陀利女、空鉢而出。惡業障現。云何如來得一切種智，而不離諸過？」佛告大慧：「諦聽，諦聽！善思念之，當為汝說。」大慧白佛：「善哉，世尊！唯然受教。」佛告大慧：「為無餘涅槃故說，誘進行菩薩行者故。此及餘世界修菩薩行者，樂聲聞乘涅槃，為令離聲聞乘，進向大乘，化佛授聲聞記，非是法佛。大慧！因是故，記諸聲聞與菩薩不異。大慧！不異者，聲聞、緣覺、諸佛如來，煩惱障斷，解脫一味，非智障斷。大慧！智障者，見法無我，殊勝清淨。煩惱障者，先習見人無我，斷七識滅，法障解脫，識藏習滅，究竟清淨。因本住法故，前後非性。無盡本願故。如來無慮無察，而演說法；正智所化故，念不忘故，無慮無察。四住地、無明住地習氣斷故，二煩惱斷，離二種死，覺人法無我，及二障斷。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Mahāmati Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, the World-Honored One predicted that arhats would attain full and complete enlightenment, no different from any bodhisattva's own attainment — yet if the dharma of every sentient being is [ultimately] not nirvāṇa, who is there left to reach buddhahood at all? From his first attainment of buddhahood to his final nirvāṇa, in the whole interval between these, [you say] the Tathāgata never spoke a single word, nor gave any answer at all. Since the Tathāgata is constantly in samādhi, he has no deliberation and no examination — his transformation-bodies alone carry out a buddha's own activity. Why, then, do you speak of consciousness's own momentary destruction and continual dissolution? Vajrapāṇi constantly attends and guards him. Why is the very origin [of birth-and-death] never explained? Māra and Māra's own deeds appear to him, the fruits of evil karma manifest — the young Brahmin woman Cañcā, the girl Sundarī, an empty almsbowl on which he departs — the very obstruction of evil karma made manifest. How, then, could the Tathāgata, who has attained omniscience itself, still fail to be apart from every fault?" The Buddha told Mahāmati: "Listen carefully, listen carefully, and reflect well upon it — I shall explain this for you." Mahāmati said to the Buddha: "Excellent, World-Honored One — yes indeed, I shall receive your teaching." The Buddha told Mahāmati: "This is taught for the sake of nirvāṇa without remainder, so as to draw forward those who practice the bodhisattva path. Those who cultivate the bodhisattva path, in this world and in every other, who [still] delight in the śrāvaka vehicle's own nirvāṇa, are, for this very reason, caused to leave the śrāvaka vehicle behind and advance toward the Great Vehicle — a transformation-body buddha bestows the prediction upon the śrāvaka, not the Dharma-body buddha itself. Mahāmati, it is for this very reason that śrāvakas are predicted to be no different from bodhisattvas. Mahāmati, as for 'no different': the śrāvaka, the pratyekabuddha, and every buddha-tathāgata alike have cut off the affliction-hindrance, sharing liberation in a single taste — but this is not to say the wisdom-hindrance has been cut off as well. Mahāmati, as for the wisdom-hindrance: it is cut off by seeing the selflessness of dharmas, [reaching] a supreme and pure state. As for the affliction-hindrance: it is cut off through the earlier practice of seeing the selflessness of persons — once the seven [active] consciousnesses cease, one is liberated from the affliction-hindrance, though the storehouse-consciousness's own habituation must still be exhausted before one reaches ultimate purity. Because [enlightenment] rests on the fundamentally abiding Dharma itself, neither 'before' nor 'after' has any fixed nature of its own [with respect to it]. Because his original vow is inexhaustible, the Tathāgata teaches the Dharma without deliberation and without examination — being transformed entirely by right wisdom itself, his mindfulness never once lapsing, he has no need for deliberation or examination at all. Because habituation belonging to the four abodes [of affliction] and the abode of fundamental ignorance has already been cut off, the two afflictions are themselves cut off, he is apart from the two kinds of death, and, awakened to the selflessness of both persons and dharmas, both hindrances are cut off as well.

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators number Mahāmati's question precisely as seven distinct puzzles folded into a single passage: "此文有七問：一、問授聲聞記；二...問佛何故言不說法；三...問何待思惟說法；四...問說剎那壞相；五、問何故金剛侍衛；六問本際；七問九惱" — "this passage contains seven questions: first, about predicting śrāvakas' enlightenment; second, why the Buddha says he teaches no Dharma at all; third, why he needs to deliberate before teaching; fourth, about the teaching of momentary destruction; fifth, why Vajrapāṇi must guard him; sixth, about the [unexplained] origin of birth-and-death; and seventh, about the nine tribulations." The commentators list the nine tribulations by name: "旃遮婆羅門女以木盂繫腹、孫陀利殺女...食馬麥、頭背俱痛、刺傷足、設火坑、毒飯" — the Brahmin woman Cañcā's false pregnancy accusation using a bound wooden bowl, the murder of Sundarī falsely blamed on the Buddha, eating horse-fodder during a famine, back pain, a thorn wound to the foot, an attempted fire-pit and poisoned meal, among others — the very traditional catalogue of misfortunes that later Buddhist literature records as having befallen the historical Buddha despite his complete enlightenment.

On the crucial distinction that resolves the first question — how a śrāvaka can be "no different" from a bodhisattva while clearly still differing in some way — the commentators locate the entire answer in which of the two hindrances has actually been cut off: "聲聞但斷見思煩惱，見人無我，未破無明、見法無我，故與菩薩異也" — "the śrāvaka has only cut off the afflictions of mistaken view and habitual clinging, seeing the selflessness of persons — he has not yet broken through fundamental ignorance to see the selflessness of dharmas as well, and this is precisely why he differs from the bodhisattva." Śrāvaka and bodhisattva share the same liberation from affliction, "in a single taste," but the wisdom-hindrance — the subtler obstruction to seeing that dharmas themselves have no fixed self-nature — remains uncut in the śrāvaka, which is exactly why the prediction of his eventual buddhahood is a promise about where he is headed, not a claim that he has already arrived.

Why the Tathāgata Needs No Guardian

Chinese Source

大慧！心、意、意識、眼識等七，剎那習氣因離，善無漏品離，不復輪轉。大慧！如來藏者，輪轉、涅槃苦樂因。空亂意。大慧！愚癡凡夫所不能覺。大慧！金剛力士所隨護者，是化佛耳，非真如來。大慧！真如來者，離一切根量。一切凡夫、聲聞、緣覺及外道根量悉滅，得現法樂住，無間法智忍故，非金剛力士所護。一切化佛不從業生。化化佛者，非佛，不離佛。因陶家輪等眾生所作相而說法，非自通處說自覺境界。「復次，大慧！愚夫依七識身滅，起斷見。不覺識藏故，起常見。自妄想故，不知本際。自妄想慧滅，故解脫。四住地、無明住地習氣斷故，一切過斷。」爾時，世尊欲重宣此義而說偈言：「三乘亦非乘，　如來不磨滅，一切佛所說，　說離諸過惡。為諸無間智，　及無餘涅槃，誘進諸下劣，　是故隱覆說。諸佛所起智，　即分別說道，諸乘非為乘，　彼則非涅槃。欲色有及見，　說是四住地，意識之所起，　識宅意所住。意及眼識等，　斷滅說無常，或作涅槃見，　而為說常住。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Mahāmati, mind, will, consciousness, and the eye-consciousness — these seven — are apart from the cause of momentary habituation once they are apart from the very grade of wholesome and undefiled activity, and no longer continue to revolve. Mahāmati, the tathāgatagarbha, however, is the cause of both revolving-existence and nirvāṇa, of pleasure and pain — it is what confuses the mind [when it is merely fixed as] emptiness. Mahāmati, this is something the foolish and ignorant are simply unable to realize. Mahāmati, what Vajrapāṇi accompanies and guards is only the transformation-body buddha, not the true Tathāgata at all. Mahāmati, the true Tathāgata is apart from every measure taken by any sense faculty — every measure taken by any ordinary person, śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, or outsider's sense faculties having entirely ceased — attaining the very bliss of the Dharma as it stands present, dwelling in the ceaseless forbearance of the Dharma's own wisdom, and is therefore not something Vajrapāṇi could ever guard. Every transformation-body buddha is not produced from karma at all. A transformation of a transformation-body buddha is not the [true] Buddha, yet is not apart from the Buddha either — teaching the Dharma by relying on marks constructed the way a potter's wheel [shapes clay] to suit sentient beings, not teaching from the very domain of his own self-realization. "Furthermore, Mahāmati, the foolish and ignorant, relying on the cessation of the seven-consciousness body, give rise to the view of annihilation; failing to be aware of the storehouse-consciousness, they give rise to the view of permanence. Through their own false thought, they fail to know the very origin [of birth-and-death]; only once their own false-thought wisdom itself ceases is there liberation. Once habituation belonging to the four abodes [of affliction] and the abode of fundamental ignorance is cut off, every fault whatsoever is cut off." At that time, wishing to restate this meaning once more, the World-Honored One spoke in verse: "The three vehicles are, in truth, not even a vehicle at all — the Tathāgata himself is never destroyed; everything every buddha has ever taught speaks of being apart from every fault and evil. For the sake of ceaseless wisdom, and for the sake of nirvāṇa without remainder, drawing forward those of lesser capacity — this is why [these teachings] are spoken with their meaning veiled. The very wisdom that arises in every buddha is what distinguishes and teaches the path — the [various] vehicles are not truly a vehicle at all, and that [teaching] is itself not [ultimate] nirvāṇa. Desire, form, existence, and views — these are spoken of as the four abodes [of affliction]; what mental consciousness itself gives rise to is the very dwelling in which will itself resides. Will and the eye-consciousness and the rest are spoken of as subject to annihilation and impermanence — or else, forming a view of nirvāṇa there, they are instead spoken of as abiding permanently."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators explain the distinction between the transformation-body Vajrapāṇi guards and the true Tathāgata as the hinge on which the fifth question turns: "問中有對論、別論。對論則佛有真、化，故曰力士所護者化佛也...又曰化佛者，非佛不離佛，乃從真起化，故非一非異，亦不待護" — "the answer involves both a comparative account and a separate account. Comparatively, the Buddha has both a true body and transformation-bodies, and so it is said that what the guardian protects is the transformation-body buddha... and it is also said that a transformation-body buddha is not the [true] Buddha, yet is not apart from the Buddha either, since it arises from the true body giving rise to transformation — being neither identical with it nor different from it, and therefore needing no guardian either." Vajrapāṇi's vigilance protects exactly what can be threatened — a manifestation appearing within the world of karma and circumstance — while the reality it manifests from was never within that world's reach in the first place.

On why annihilationism and eternalism are named as the two views that inevitably follow from misunderstanding the seven consciousnesses and the storehouse-consciousness, the commentators trace each error to its specific blind spot: "據彼所知，極於七識之外無所知故，因起斷見。而不覺識藏無盡，見其念念相續故，起常見" — "based on what they know, [the foolish] have no knowledge whatsoever beyond the seven [active] consciousnesses, and so give rise to the view of annihilation; and, failing to be aware that the storehouse-consciousness is itself inexhaustible, seeing only its own moment-to-moment continuity, they give rise to the view of permanence instead." Seeing only the surface consciousnesses ceasing, one wrongly concludes that everything ends; seeing the storehouse-consciousness's uninterrupted flow without understanding what it actually is, one wrongly concludes that something changeless persists — both errors stemming from the same failure to see the whole picture at once.

Part 48 Complete

This closes Part 48: Mahāmati's seven searching questions about the Buddha's own life and teaching answered one by one, resolving each apparent contradiction through the distinction between the Tathāgata's true, changeless nature and the transformation-bodies through which he acts within the world of karma and circumstance — a distinction that explains why Vajrapāṇi guards without the true Buddha ever needing guarding, and why the nine tribulations touch only what was always within the world's reach to begin with.
