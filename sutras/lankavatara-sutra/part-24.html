The Buddha's Knowing-Awareness

Mahāmati asks what "the Buddha's knowing-awareness" actually consists of, and the Buddha answers with a compact definition that folds together the two kinds of no-self, the two hindrances, and the two kinds of death — adding that any śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha who attains this same realization is, on that basis alone, rightly called a buddha too. Mahāmati then asks about a claim that sounds like a plain contradiction: the Buddha has said, in the same breath, that he was every buddha of the past under different names, and that he has never spoken so much as a single word across his entire teaching career. Both claims, the Buddha explains, rest on the same underlying point — a self-realized dharma identical across every buddha, and a dharma-nature that was never made by anyone speaking of it in the first place.

The Buddha's Knowing-Awareness, and Four Equalities

Chinese Source

爾時，大慧菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！唯願為說佛之知覺。世尊！何等是佛之知覺？」

佛告大慧：「覺人法無我，了知二障，離二種死，斷二煩惱，是名佛之知覺。聲聞、緣覺得此法者，亦名為佛。以是因緣故，我說一乘。」

爾時，世尊欲重宣此義而說偈言：
「善知二無我，　二障煩惱斷，
永離二種死，　是名佛知覺。」

爾時，大慧菩薩白佛言：「世尊！何故世尊於大眾中唱如是言：『我是過去一切佛，及種種受生，我爾時作漫陀轉輪聖王、六牙大象，及鸚鵡鳥、釋提桓因、善眼仙人，如是等百千生經說？』」

佛告大慧：「以四等故，如來、應供、等正覺，於大眾中唱如是言：『我爾時作拘留孫、拘那含牟尼、迦葉佛。』云何四等？謂：字等、語等、法等、身等，是名四等。以四種等故，如來、應供、等正覺，於大眾中唱如是言。云何字等？若字稱我為佛，彼字亦稱一切諸佛，彼字自性無有差別，是名字等。云何語等？謂：我六十四種梵音言語相生，彼諸如來、應供、等正覺，亦如是六十四種梵音言語相生，無增無減，無有差別，迦陵頻伽梵音聲性。云何身等？謂：我與諸佛法身及色身相好，無有差別，除為調伏彼彼諸趣差別眾生故，示現種種差別色身，是名身等。云何法等？謂：我及彼佛，得三十七菩提分法。略說佛法無障礙智，是名四等。是故如來、應供、等正覺，於大眾中唱如是言。」

爾時，世尊欲重宣此義而說偈言：
「迦葉拘留孫、　拘那含是我，
以此四種等，　我為佛子說。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then Mahāmati Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, I beg you to teach me what the Buddha's own knowing-awareness is. World-Honored One, what exactly is the Buddha's knowing-awareness?"

The Buddha told Mahāmati: "Fully realizing the no-self of persons and of dharmas, thoroughly understanding the two hindrances, being apart from the two kinds of death, and cutting through the two [kinds of] affliction — this is called the Buddha's knowing-awareness. Any śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha who attains this same dharma is, for that very reason, also called a buddha. It is on account of just this that I teach the one vehicle."

Then the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke in verse: "Rightly knowing the two kinds of no-self, the two hindrances and their affliction cut through,
forever apart from the two kinds of death — this is what is called the Buddha's knowing-awareness."

Then Mahāmati Bodhisattva said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, why does the World-Honored One proclaim, before the great assembly, words such as these: 'I am every buddha of the past, and have received birth in every kind of form — at one time I was King Māndhātṛ, the wheel-turning sage; at another, the six-tusked great elephant; and again the parrot, Śakra, lord of the gods, and the seer Sunetra' — recounting hundreds and thousands of such former births?"

The Buddha told Mahāmati: "It is on account of four equalities that the Tathāgata, the Worthy One, the Truly and Fully Enlightened One, proclaims, before the great assembly, words such as these: 'At that time I was Krakucchanda, Kanakamuni, and Kāśyapa Buddha.' What are the four equalities? The equality of name, of speech, of dharma, and of body — these are called the four equalities. It is on account of these four equalities that the Tathāgata, the Worthy One, the Truly and Fully Enlightened One, proclaims, before the great assembly, words such as these. What is the equality of name? If the name 'buddha' is used to name me, that very name equally names every buddha; the own-nature of that name admits of no distinction at all — this is called the equality of name. What is the equality of speech? It is that I give rise to speech through the sixty-four kinds of Brahmā-voice, and that every tathāgata, the Worthy One, the Truly and Fully Enlightened One, likewise gives rise to speech through these same sixty-four kinds of Brahmā-voice, with nothing added and nothing lacking, admitting of no distinction at all — the very nature of the kalaviṅka bird's own Brahmā-voice. What is the equality of body? It is that my own dharma-body and form-body, in every excellent mark, admit of no distinction at all from every other buddha's — except that, for the sake of taming the manifold, distinct sentient beings of every destiny, I manifest every kind of distinct form-body accordingly — this is called the equality of body. What is the equality of dharma? It is that I, and every one of those buddhas, have attained the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment; stated briefly, it is the unobstructed wisdom belonging to the buddha-dharma itself — these are called the four equalities. It is for this reason that the Tathāgata, the Worthy One, the Truly and Fully Enlightened One, proclaims, before the great assembly, words such as these."

Then the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke in verse: "Kāśyapa, Krakucchanda, and Kanakamuni are myself —
it is on account of these four equalities that I teach this for the sake of the Buddha's own children."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators are precise about how tightly the definition of "the Buddha's knowing-awareness" is constructed, mapping each pair explicitly onto the sūtra's own recurring vocabulary rather than leaving the terms loose: "了二無我是為覺，了二障是為知，究論人法、生佛平等，本無二執...二障者，惑障、智障也" — "fully realizing the two kinds of no-self is what is meant by 'awareness'; thoroughly understanding the two hindrances is what is meant by 'knowing'; examined to its root, persons and dharmas, sentient beings and buddhas, are equal, originally free of the twin graspings that would distinguish them... the two hindrances are the hindrance of affliction and the hindrance to knowledge." Nothing in the definition is a fresh, unexplained term — "knowing," "awareness," the "two deaths," and the "two afflictions" are each pinned to categories this sūtra has already carefully built up across two full fascicles, so that the Buddha's own knowing-awareness turns out to be the same structure the whole text has been circling from the start, now given its most compressed statement.

On the four equalities, the commentators single out why the equality of body carries an explicit exception that the other three do not: "謂我與諸佛法身及色身相好無有差別，除為調伏彼彼諸趣差別眾生故，示現種種差別色身" — "my own dharma-body and my form-body's excellent marks are, in themselves, no different from any other buddha's — except for the manifold, distinct form-bodies manifested specifically to tame the manifold, distinct beings of every different destiny." The equality is genuine at the level of essence; the visible difference between one buddha's appearance and another's is not a break in that equality but a deliberate accommodation to whichever audience needs a particular form to be reached at all — exactly the same logic, the commentators imply, that lets the Buddha use "tathāgatagarbha" for one audience and "no-self" for another without those being two different underlying teachings.

Not a Single Word

Chinese Source

大慧復白佛言：「如世尊所說：『我從某夜得最正覺，乃至某夜入般涅槃，於其中間乃至不說一字，亦不已說、當說，不說是佛說。』」

大慧白佛言：「世尊！如來、應供、等正覺，何因說言：『不說是佛說』？」

佛告大慧：「我因二法故，作如是說。云何二法？謂：緣自得法，及本住法，是名二法。因此二法故，我如是說。云何緣自得法？若彼如來所得，我亦得之，無增無減。緣自得法究竟境界，離言說妄想，離字二趣。云何本住法？謂：古先聖道。如金銀等性，法界常住，若如來出世、若不出世，法界常住。如趣彼成道，譬如士夫行曠野中，見向古城平坦正道，即隨入城，受如意樂。大慧！於意云何，彼士夫作是道及城中種種樂耶？」

答言：「不也。」

佛告大慧：「我及過去一切諸佛法界常住，亦復如是。是故說言：『我從某夜得最正覺，乃至某夜入般涅槃，於其中間，不說一字，亦不已說、當說。』」

爾時，世尊欲重宣此義而說偈言：
「我某夜成道，　至某夜涅槃，
於此二中間，　我都無所說。

緣自得法住，　故我作是說，
彼佛及與我，　悉無有差別。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Mahāmati again said to the Buddha: "As the World-Honored One has taught: 'From the night of my attaining unsurpassed, complete enlightenment, to the very night of my entering nirvāṇa, throughout that entire span, I did not speak so much as a single word, nor have I ever spoken, nor will I ever speak — for that which is not spoken is the Buddha's true speech.'"

Mahāmati said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, for what reason does the Tathāgata, the Worthy One, the Truly and Fully Enlightened One, say that 'that which is not spoken is the Buddha's true speech'?"

The Buddha told Mahāmati: "It is on account of two dharmas that I speak in this way. What are the two dharmas? The dharma realized through self-realization, and the dharma that has always already stood in place — these are called the two dharmas. It is on account of these two dharmas that I speak in this way. What is the dharma realized through self-realization? It is that whatever the Tathāgatas [of the past] have attained, I have attained it too, with nothing added and nothing lacking. The ultimate domain of the dharma realized through self-realization is apart from speech and false thought, apart from the written word and its twofold direction. What is the dharma that has always already stood in place? It is the ancient path the noble ones before me have walked — like the very nature of gold or silver, the dharma-realm forever abides. Whether a tathāgata appears in the world or does not appear in the world, the dharma-realm forever abides. It is like a road leading to that same accomplished city: it is as though a person, walking through a wilderness, came upon a level, well-made road leading toward an ancient city, and, following it, entered the city and enjoyed there whatever pleasure he wished. Mahāmati, what do you think — did that person make the road, or make the manifold pleasures found within the city?"

Mahāmati answered: "No, he did not."

The Buddha told Mahāmati: "I, and every buddha of the past, [find] the dharma-realm forever abiding in just this same way. It is for this reason that I say: 'From the night of my attaining unsurpassed, complete enlightenment, to the very night of my entering nirvāṇa, throughout that entire span, I did not speak so much as a single word, nor have I ever spoken, nor will I ever speak.'"

Then the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke in verse: "From the night I attained enlightenment to the very night of my nirvāṇa,
throughout that entire span, I have, in truth, said nothing at all.

It is because the dharma realized through self-realization always already stands in place that I speak in just this way —
between that buddha and myself, there is, in truth, no difference at all."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators are precise that the two dharmas named here are not two separate claims but two names for the same discovery, seen from different angles: "自得即自證修德也，本住即本具性德也，修性一如，皆離言說" — "'realized through self-realization' names the merit cultivated and personally verified; 'always already standing in place' names the merit already inherently possessed — cultivated merit and inherent nature are, in truth, identical, and both alike are apart from speech." What the Buddha personally verifies through his own realization and what was already true of the dharma-realm before any buddha appeared at all turn out to be the very same reality, examined once from the side of a practitioner's own effort and once from the side of what that effort was always aimed at uncovering.

On the road-and-city simile, the commentators draw out precisely what the two halves of the image represent, and why the Buddha presses Mahāmati to answer that the traveler made neither: "謂古先聖道者，即先佛所證性德之法也...所謂平坦正道者，本住法也；士夫入城受樂者，自得法也。以其本有正道，故得隨之而入" — "'the ancient path the noble ones before me have walked' refers to the dharma of inherent merit already realized by every prior buddha... the level, well-made road is precisely the dharma that always already stands in place; the traveler entering the city and enjoying its pleasures is precisely the dharma realized through self-realization. It is only because the road was already there, in its own right, that he was able to follow it in at all." Enlightenment, on this reading, is discovery rather than construction — the road to the city, and the city's own pleasures, were never brought into being by the traveler walking them, any more than a buddha's own realization brings the dharma-realm into being by realizing it.

Part 24 Complete

This closes Part 24: the Buddha's own knowing-awareness defined as the two kinds of no-self, the two hindrances, and the two kinds of death together; the four equalities of name, speech, body, and dharma explaining how the Buddha can say without contradiction that he was every buddha of the past; and the two dharmas — realized through self-realization, and always already standing in place — resolving why the Buddha can also say, in the same breath, that he has never spoken a single word.
