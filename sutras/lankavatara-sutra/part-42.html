The Tathāgatagarbha as the Storehouse-Consciousness

Mahāmati asks the Buddha to explain how the aggregates, elements, and sense-fields arise and cease at all, if there is truly no self to arise or cease. The Buddha's answer opens one of this sūtra's most influential teachings: the tathāgatagarbha itself serves as the cause of both good and evil, and, habituated from beginningless time by false habituation, becomes what is called the storehouse-consciousness — the very source from which the seven active consciousnesses rise ceaselessly, the way waves rise from the ocean and never stop. The Buddha closes by directly citing his own teaching to Queen Śrīmālā on this same truth, and comparing a tathāgata's clear vision of it to an āmalaka fruit held in the palm of the hand.

The Tathāgatagarbha as the Cause of Both Good and Evil

Chinese Source

爾時，大慧菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！惟願世尊更為我說陰界入生滅。彼無有我，誰生誰滅？愚夫者，依於生滅，不覺苦盡，不識涅槃。」佛言：「善哉！諦聽！當為汝說。」大慧白佛言：「唯然受教。」佛告大慧：「如來之藏，是善不善因，能遍興造一切趣生。譬如伎兒，變現諸趣，離我我所。不覺彼故，三緣和合方便而生。外道不覺，計著作者。為無始虛偽惡習所薰，名為識藏。生無明住地，與七識俱。如海浪身，常生不斷。離無常過、離於我論，自性無垢，畢竟清淨。其諸餘識，有生有滅。意、意識等，念念有七。因不實妄想，取諸境界，種種形處，計著名相。不覺自心所現色相，不覺苦樂，不至解脫，名相諸纏，貪生生貪。若因若攀緣，彼諸受根滅，次第不生。除自心妄想，不知苦樂。入滅受想正受，第四禪，善真諦解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Mahāmati Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, I only wish the World-Honored One would explain further the arising and ceasing of the aggregates, elements, and sense-fields. If there is truly no self there, then who arises and who ceases? The foolish and ignorant, relying on this arising and ceasing, fail to realize that suffering can be exhausted, and do not recognize nirvāṇa at all." The Buddha said: "Excellent! Listen carefully — I shall explain this for you." Mahāmati said to the Buddha: "Yes indeed, I shall receive your teaching." The Buddha told Mahāmati: "The tathāgatagarbha itself is the cause of both what is wholesome and what is unwholesome, able to bring forth and construct every destiny of birth throughout — like a skilled performer, manifesting every destiny in turn, apart from self and what belongs to self. Failing to be aware of this, [beings], through the coming-together of the three conditions [faculty, object, and consciousness] and skillful means, take birth. The outsiders, being unaware of this, become attached to positing a maker. Habituated by beginningless, false, evil habituation, [the tathāgatagarbha] comes to be called the storehouse-consciousness, giving rise to the abode of fundamental ignorance, arising together with the seven [active] consciousnesses — like the very body of the ocean's waves, ceaselessly arising without interruption. Yet it is itself apart from the fault of impermanence, apart from any theory of a fixed self, its own-nature free of defilement, ultimately and completely pure. As for the remaining consciousnesses: these do have arising and ceasing. Will and consciousness and the rest number seven, moment by moment. Through unreal false thought, they grasp at their various objective realms, taking up manifold forms and places, becoming attached to names and marks. Failing to be aware that these are simply forms manifested by one's own mind, and failing to be aware of pleasure and pain, one fails to reach liberation — bound up by names and marks, craving gives rise to further craving. Whether through cause or through what is clung to as a supporting condition, once the very faculties of sensation themselves cease, [these consciousnesses] no longer arise in succession, and, apart from one's own mind's false thought, pleasure and pain are no longer known. One then enters cessation of sensation and conception, the fourth dhyāna, well accomplished in the liberation of true reality —

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators explain the phrase "cause of both good and evil" through a careful account of how a single reality yields two entirely different outcomes depending on which condition it follows: "如來謂理性如來，現前一念所具名之為藏...根塵一念心起，隨染淨緣，染即無明，隨無明染緣則為九界生死；淨即教行，隨教行淨緣則為四種道滅...故曰『是善不善因』" — "'Tathāgata' here means the Tathāgata as principle-nature; what is present within a single thought is called the 'garbha,' the womb or store. When a single thought arises from faculty and object, it follows either a defiled or a pure condition: the defiled condition is ignorance, and following the defiled condition of ignorance produces the birth-and-death of the nine [unenlightened] realms; the pure condition is teaching and practice, and following the pure condition of teaching and practice produces the four kinds of cessation on the path... this is why it says [the tathāgatagarbha] is 'the cause of both what is wholesome and what is unwholesome.'" The tathāgatagarbha itself never changes; only the condition it happens to follow determines whether what arises from it is saṃsāra or the path to its cessation.

On why the ocean-wave image is chosen specifically for the transition from tathāgatagarbha to storehouse-consciousness, the commentators trace the sequence carefully: "彼外道以不覺故，妄計執著造作，由無始惡習所熏，名為識藏。轉生七識，無明住地。言無明，起之始也，從此根本乃生枝末無明，故喻之曰『如海浪身常生不斷』" — "the outsiders, being unaware, falsely cling to the notion of a maker; [the tathāgatagarbha,] habituated by beginningless evil habituation, comes to be called the storehouse-consciousness, which in turn gives rise to the seven consciousnesses and the abode of fundamental ignorance. 'Ignorance' here names the very beginning of arising — from this root, branch-and-leaf ignorance then arises, which is why it is likened to 'the very body of the ocean's waves, ceaselessly arising without interruption.'" And the commentators are careful to note that this ceaseless arising belongs only to the seven active consciousnesses, never to the tathāgatagarbha itself: "若能一念回光，能隨淨緣，則離無常之過、二我之執，自性清淨，所謂性德如來則究顯矣" — "should a single thought turn its own light back upon itself and follow the pure condition instead, one is thereby apart from the fault of impermanence and the grasping at a twofold self, one's own-nature being pure — and it is precisely this that is meant by the Tathāgata as inherent virtue being ultimately made manifest." The waves rise and fall without end, but the ocean beneath them is never itself agitated.

Progress Through the Grounds to the Ground of Immovability

Chinese Source

修行者，作解脫想，不離不轉，名如來藏識藏。七識流轉不滅。所以者何？彼因攀緣諸識生故，非聲聞、緣覺修行境界，不覺無我，自共相攝受，生陰界入。見如來藏、五法自性、人法無我則滅，地次第相續轉進。餘外道見，不能傾動，是名住菩薩不動地。得十三昧道門樂，三昧覺所持。觀察不思議佛法自願，不受三昧門樂及實際，向自覺聖趣，不共一切聲聞、緣覺及諸外道所修行道，得十賢聖種性道，及身智意生，離三昧行。是故，大慧！

Ru-Yi Translation

— and the meditator, [reaching this point], forms the notion of having attained liberation. Yet [the storehouse-consciousness] is neither relinquished nor transformed [at this stage] — this alone is what is called the tathāgatagarbha as storehouse-consciousness. The seven [active] consciousnesses continue their flow without ever ceasing entirely. Why is this? Because they arise through cause and through the very consciousnesses that are clung to as supporting conditions — this is not something within the śrāvaka's or pratyekabuddha's own domain of practice, since they fail to be aware of selflessness [in its fullest sense], still grasping at shared and particular marks, and thereby still giving rise to the aggregates, elements, and sense-fields. Once one truly sees the tathāgatagarbha, the five dharmas and the three own-natures, and the selflessness of both persons and dharmas, [this grasping] ceases, and one proceeds in graduated succession through the grounds. Unmoved and untroubled by any of the outsiders' remaining views, this alone is what is called abiding in the bodhisattva's ground of immovability. There, one attains the bliss of ten gateways of samādhi, sustained by the awareness of samādhi itself, observing the buddhas' own inconceivable Dharma and their own vows, not clinging to the bliss found within the gateway of samādhi nor to the very edge of ultimate reality, turning instead toward the domain of self-realized noble wisdom — a path not shared with anything practiced by any śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, or outsider. One thereby attains the path belonging to the ten stages of noble lineage, together with body, wisdom, and mind-made birth, apart from the very activity of samādhi itself. It is for this reason, Mahāmati —

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators are precise about what the meditator in cessation-attainment has and has not actually accomplished: "善真諦解脫等，即聲聞所修，於此滅定作解脫想，非究竟滅也...而實未捨未轉如來藏中藏識之名，若無藏識，七識則滅。由不轉不滅，所以七識與六識為因及攀緣而生" — "'well accomplished in the liberation of true reality' and the rest describes what the śrāvaka cultivates: within this cessation-absorption he forms the notion of having attained liberation, though this is not ultimate cessation at all... in truth, he has neither relinquished nor transformed the name 'storehouse-consciousness' within the tathāgatagarbha; were there no storehouse-consciousness, the seven [active] consciousnesses would themselves cease. Because it is neither transformed nor ceased, the seven consciousnesses continue to arise through the sixth consciousness serving as their cause and supporting condition." The śrāvaka's absorption stills the surface activity of mind, but the storehouse-consciousness that could give rise to it all again is never actually uprooted — which is exactly why this cessation, however blissful, is not the ultimate liberation a bodhisattva seeks.

On why the eighth ground specifically is named "immovability," the commentators tie it to the ten samādhis granted there by the buddhas' own sustaining power: "由前悟入得預初地，次第增進，位深德著，不為外道邪見所動，至第八不動地。於此得十種三昧樂門，為諸佛三昧力所持...獲自證智，豈與凡小所修行同" — "having first awakened and entered the first ground, [the bodhisattva] advances in graduated succession; as his stage deepens and his virtue becomes manifest, he is no longer moved by any outsider's mistaken view, arriving at the eighth, immovable ground. Here he attains the bliss of the ten gateways of samādhi, sustained by the power of every buddha's own samādhi... having attained self-realized wisdom, how could this be the same as anything practiced by the ordinary person or the lesser vehicles?" The name "immovable" describes exactly the stability this passage has been building toward since Part 40's account of the eighth ground being sustained against the pull of premature nirvāṇa — here, that same sustaining power is shown to rest on having genuinely seen the tathāgatagarbha itself, not merely stilled its surface activity.

Purifying the Name, and Queen Śrīmālā's Teaching

Chinese Source

菩薩摩訶薩欲求勝進者，當淨如來藏及藏識名。大慧！若無識藏名如來藏者，則無生滅。大慧！然諸凡聖悉有生滅，修行者自覺聖趣現法樂住，不捨方便。大慧！此如來藏識藏，一切聲聞、緣覺心想所見。雖自性淨，客塵所覆故，猶見不淨，非諸如來。大慧！如來者，現前境界，猶如掌中視阿摩勒果。大慧！我於此義，以神力建立，令勝鬘夫人及利智滿足諸菩薩等，宣揚演說如來藏及識藏名，與七識俱生。聲聞計著，見人法無我。故勝鬘夫人承佛威神，說如來境界，非聲聞、緣覺及外道境界。如來藏識藏，唯佛及餘利智依義菩薩智慧境界。是故汝及餘菩薩摩訶薩，於如來藏識藏，當勤修學，莫但聞覺作知足想。」

Ru-Yi Translation

— the bodhisattva-mahāsattva who wishes to advance further should purify the very names 'tathāgatagarbha' and 'storehouse-consciousness.' Mahāmati, were there no name 'storehouse-consciousness' given to the tathāgatagarbha, there would be no arising and ceasing at all. Mahāmati, and yet every ordinary being and every noble one does have arising and ceasing — the practitioner, [even while advancing toward] the domain of self-realized noble wisdom, dwells in the very bliss of the Dharma as it stands present before him, never relinquishing skillful means. Mahāmati, this tathāgatagarbha as storehouse-consciousness is what every śrāvaka's and pratyekabuddha's own mind perceives — though it is, in its own-nature, pure, being covered over by adventitious defilement, it still appears impure to them, unlike how it appears to any tathāgata. Mahāmati, to a tathāgata, this is a domain directly present before him, exactly like viewing an āmalaka fruit held in the very palm of one's hand. Mahāmati, it was on this very point that I, through spiritual power, established and enabled Queen Śrīmālā and other bodhisattvas of sharp wisdom and full accomplishment to proclaim and expound the names 'tathāgatagarbha' and 'storehouse-consciousness,' arising together with the seven [active] consciousnesses — for the śrāvakas, clinging as they do, perceive only the selflessness of persons and of dharmas [without seeing this further truth]. It was for this reason that Queen Śrīmālā, relying on the Buddha's own majestic spiritual power, spoke of the tathāgata's own domain, which is not the domain of any śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, or outsider. The tathāgatagarbha as storehouse-consciousness is a domain of wisdom belonging only to the buddhas and to those further bodhisattvas of sharp wisdom who rely on meaning itself. It is for this reason that you, and every other bodhisattva-mahāsattva, should diligently cultivate and study the tathāgatagarbha as storehouse-consciousness, rather than merely hearing of it and forming the notion that this alone is already enough."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators explain what "purifying the name" actually means, since the tathāgatagarbha itself needs no purification: "如來之藏本無可淨，淨其垢者耳。識藏以名言者，由迷如來藏轉成妄識，無有別體，故但有名。若無識藏之名，則轉妄識為如來藏也" — "the tathāgatagarbha itself has, in truth, nothing that could be purified; it is only its defilement that is purified. As for calling it by the name 'storehouse-consciousness': this is because, through delusion about the tathāgatagarbha, it turns into a deluded consciousness that has no separate substance of its own apart from the tathāgatagarbha — hence it is a matter of name alone. Were there no name 'storehouse-consciousness' at all, the very deluded consciousness would itself simply be turned back into the tathāgatagarbha." The tathāgatagarbha and the storehouse-consciousness are never two different things — 'storehouse-consciousness' is simply what the very same reality is called for as long as delusion still covers it, and the whole of practice is nothing but removing that covering, never manufacturing a new purity that was not already there.

On the āmalaka-fruit image, the commentators cite the alternate translation directly for its precision: "《入楞伽》云：『此如來藏識藏本性清淨，客塵所染而為不淨。一切二乘及諸外道臆度起見，不能現證。如來於此分明現見，如觀掌中菴摩勒果。』" — "the Śikṣānanda translation says: 'this tathāgatagarbha as storehouse-consciousness is, in its fundamental nature, pure — it is adventitious defilement that renders it impure. Every one of the two lesser vehicles and every outsider can only speculate and form views about it, never directly verifying it themselves. The Tathāgata, however, sees this with perfect clarity, the way one views an āmalaka fruit held in the palm of one's own hand.'" And on why Queen Śrīmālā's teaching is invoked here by name rather than left implicit, the commentators simply note: "如來之說本不假證，但於彼經已曾廣明，故茲略說得以指之" — "the Tathāgata's own teaching requires no external verification to begin with; it is simply that this same truth had already been explained at length in that other scripture [the Śrīmālādevī Siṃhanāda Sūtra], and so it is only briefly pointed to here." The Buddha is not borrowing authority from elsewhere — he is showing that the very same self-realized truth, taught to a different audience under a different teacher's name, was always one and the same teaching.

Closing Verse: The Mind as a Skilled Performer

Chinese Source

爾時，世尊欲重宣此義而說偈言：

「甚深如來藏，　而與七識俱，
二種攝受生，　智者則遠離。

如鏡像現心，　無始習所薰，
如實觀察者，　諸事悉無事。

如愚見指月，　觀指不觀月，
計著名字者，　不見我真實。

心為工伎兒，　意如和伎者，
五識為伴侶，　妄想觀伎眾。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, wishing to restate this meaning once more, the World-Honored One spoke in verse:

"The tathāgatagarbha is profound indeed, arising together with the seven consciousnesses; born through the two kinds of grasping — apprehender and apprehended — the wise remain far apart from it.

Like a reflection appearing in a mirror, mind is habituated by beginningless habituation; one who observes it exactly as it really is finds that every seeming event is, in truth, no event at all.

Like the fool who, shown the moon, stares only at the finger and never at the moon itself, one who clings to mere names and words never sees my own true reality.

Mind is like a skilled performer, will is like the one who directs the performance, the five sense-consciousnesses are their companions, and false thought is the very audience watching the show."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators tie the closing performer image directly back to the whole passage's teaching, reading it as a compact restatement rather than a fresh illustration: "頌上如來藏與七識俱，乃至由自相共相二種攝取，則有陰、界、入生，此乃隨妄緣者。若能反妄見如來藏，則遠離生死矣" — "this verse restates how the tathāgatagarbha arises together with the seven consciousnesses, and how, through the twofold grasping of particular and shared marks, the aggregates, elements, and sense-fields come to arise — this is what happens when one follows the false condition. Should one be able to turn back from falseness and see the tathāgatagarbha directly, one is thereby far removed from birth-and-death." And on the final image itself: "心謂如來藏心，隨緣變造，如伎兒之化現。意即意根，復起意識，識起善惡，如和伎者。五識取塵意識同起，是為伴侶。妄想分別如觀伎人也" — "'mind' here means the tathāgatagarbha-mind, transforming and constructing according to conditions, like a performer's own transformations. 'Will' is the mental faculty, which in turn gives rise to consciousness; consciousness gives rise to good and evil, like the one who directs the performance. The five sense-consciousnesses grasp their objects, arising together with mental consciousness, and are thus its companions. False discrimination is like the audience watching the performance." The entire elaborate teaching of this passage — tathāgatagarbha, storehouse-consciousness, the seven active consciousnesses, beginningless habituation — is compressed, in the verse's final image, into a single theater: one performer, one director, a troupe of companions, and an audience of false thought watching a show it mistakes for reality.

Part 42 Complete

This closes Part 42: the tathāgatagarbha shown as the cause of both good and evil, habituated by beginningless false habituation into the storehouse-consciousness, birthing the seven active consciousnesses the way waves rise ceaselessly from the ocean, purified not by manufacturing a new purity but by seeing through adventitious defilement to what was always there, and closing with the Buddha's own citation of his teaching to Queen Śrīmālā and the image of mind as a skilled performer directing a show that false thought mistakes for reality.
