Unreal False Thought

Mahāmati asks a tightly reasoned question: if false thought arises from attachment to the manifold, unreal marks of conventional things, and ultimate truth is equally apart from any fixed mark, why does false thought arise with respect to the one but not the other — does this not mean the Buddha teaches one thing sometimes arising and sometimes not, in flat contradiction? The Buddha's answer dissolves the premise: false thought never truly arises with respect to either, since both alike are simply one's own mind's manifestation, correctly seen. The page closes with one of the sūtra's most sweeping verses, defining "the measure of mind" through image after image — until, at the very end, it is named directly as suchness, the true limit, nirvāṇa, the dharma-realm, and every kind of mind-made body all at once.

Unreal False Thought

Chinese Source

爾時，大慧菩薩白佛言：「世尊！唯願為說不實妄想相。不實妄想云何而生？說何等法名不實妄想？於何等法中不實妄想？」

佛告大慧：「善哉，善哉！能問如來如是之義，多所饒益，多所安樂，哀愍世間一切天人。諦聽，諦聽！善思念之，當為汝說。」

大慧白佛言：「善哉，世尊！唯然受教。」

佛告大慧：「種種義，種種不實妄想計著，妄想生。大慧！攝所攝計著，不知自心現量，及墮有無見，增長外道見。妄想習氣，計著外種種義。心心數妄想，計著我我所生。」

大慧白佛言：「世尊！若種種義，種種不實妄想，計著妄想生。攝所攝計著，不知自心現量，及墮有無見，增長外道見。妄想習氣，計著外種種義。心心數妄想，我我所計著生。世尊！若如是外種種義相，墮有無相，離性非性，離見相。世尊！第一義亦如是，離量限分譬喻因相。世尊！何故一處妄想不實義，種種性計著妄想生，非計著第一義處相妄想生，將無世尊說邪因論邪？說一生一不生。」

佛告大慧：「非妄想一生一不生。所以者何？謂：有無妄想不生故。外現性非性，覺自心現量，妄想不生。大慧！我說餘愚夫自心種種妄想相故，事業在前，種種妄想性想計著生。云何愚夫得離我我所計著見？離作所作因緣過，覺自妄想心量，身心轉變，究竟明解一切地，如來自覺境界，離五法自性事見妄想。以是因緣故，我說妄想從種種不實義計著生。知如實義，得解脫息種種妄想。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then Mahāmati Bodhisattva said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, I beg you to teach the mark of unreal false thought. How does unreal false thought arise? What kind of dharma is called unreal false thought? And with respect to what kind of dharma does unreal false thought actually arise?"

The Buddha told Mahāmati: "Excellent, excellent! That you are able to ask the Tathāgata a meaning such as this brings great benefit and great ease, out of compassion for every god and every person in the world. Listen well! Listen well! Consider this carefully — I shall now explain it to you."

Mahāmati said to the Buddha: "Excellent, World-Honored One! I gladly receive your teaching."

The Buddha told Mahāmati: "It is out of attachment to every kind of unreal false thought concerning every kind of [conventional] meaning that false thought itself arises. Mahāmati, it is attachment to the grasped and the grasper, not knowing this to be the very measure of one's own mind's manifestation, and thereby falling into the view of existence or non-existence, that further increases the outsiders' own views. It is the habituated force of false thought that becomes attached to every kind of external, [conventional] meaning; it is the manifold false thought of mind and its mental factors that gives rise to attachment to 'I' and to 'mine.'"

Mahāmati said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, if false thought arises out of attachment to every kind of unreal, [conventional] meaning — attachment to the grasped and the grasper, not knowing this to be the very measure of one's own mind's manifestation, falling into the view of existence and non-existence, and thereby increasing the outsiders' own views, with the habituated force of false thought becoming attached to every kind of external meaning, and the manifold false thought of mind and its mental factors giving rise to attachment to 'I' and 'mine' — then, World-Honored One, [it would seem that] this kind of external, [conventional] meaning, falling into the mark of existence and non-existence, is [nonetheless] apart from any fixed nature or its absence, apart from the mark of any [fixed] view. World-Honored One, ultimate meaning is exactly the same in this respect — apart from any measure, boundary, division, illustrative comparison, or causal mark. World-Honored One, why is it, then, that false thought arises with respect to unreal, [conventional] meaning in every one of its manifold, [fixed] natures, and yet false thought does not arise with respect to the domain of ultimate meaning, [equally marked and un-fixed]? Does the World-Honored One not thereby teach a mistaken theory of causation — declaring that one [domain gives rise to false thought and] arises, while the other does not?"

The Buddha told Mahāmati: "It is not that false thought arises with respect to one and not the other. Why is this? Because false thought concerning existence and non-existence does not, in truth, arise at all [with respect to either]. Once what appears externally is seen to have no fixed nature, and one becomes aware of this as the very measure of one's own mind's manifestation, false thought does not arise. Mahāmati, what I mean is that it is only with respect to the other, ignorant person's own manifold mark of false thought — where some activity stands directly before him — that manifold false thought, taking [that activity's] nature and appearance as real, arises through attachment at all. How, then, does an ignorant person come to leave behind the attached view of 'I' and 'mine'? By leaving behind the fault bound up with doer, deed, cause, and condition; by becoming aware that this is the very measure of his own mind's false thought; his body and mind having thereby turned and transformed, he comes, in the end, to understand every ground clearly, [reaching] the Tathāgata's own domain of self-realization, apart from the false thought that fixes on the five dharmas, on own-nature, and on any [conventional] matter as something to be seen at all. It is for this reason that I say false thought arises out of attachment to every kind of unreal, [conventional] meaning — and that, knowing meaning exactly as it really is, one attains liberation, and every kind of false thought comes to rest."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators lay out precisely what makes Mahāmati's question so sharp: he has noticed that both conventional meaning and ultimate meaning are, by the sūtra's own account, "apart from any fixed nature" — so why does false thought supposedly attach to one and not the other, as though the Buddha were teaching one kind of arising for one domain and a flat denial of arising for the other? "意以外種種義與第一義無異，何故言種種義生分別、第一義不生分別？豈非世尊所言乖理，有生有不生耶" — "the point is that external, manifold [conventional] meaning and ultimate meaning are, in this respect, no different from each other — so why is it said that manifold meaning gives rise to discrimination while ultimate meaning does not? Does this not mean the World-Honored One's own statement runs contrary to reason, arising in one case and not arising in the other?" It is a genuinely sharp internal challenge, structurally identical to the earlier challenge about dependent origination's apparent infinite regress.

The commentators trace the Buddha's resolution to a single move: denying that false thought genuinely arises with respect to either domain at all, once both are correctly seen. "謂了有無妄想，所見外法離性，覺了唯心所現，而妄想不生，非別有第一義諦也" — "once the false thought of existence and non-existence is understood, and what is seen externally is recognized as apart from any fixed nature, and one is aware that this is nothing but mind's own manifestation, false thought simply does not arise — there is no separate 'ultimate truth' standing apart from this same recognition." The asymmetry Mahāmati worried about was never real: it is only the ignorant person's own failure to recognize conventional appearances as mind's manifestation that produces false thought there at all, and once that same recognition is applied, conventional meaning turns out to be exactly as free of false thought as ultimate meaning always was — the two were never actually two separate teachings requiring two separate accounts of why one produces confusion and the other does not.

The Verse on the Measure of Mind

Chinese Source

爾時，世尊欲重宣此義而說偈言：
「諸因及與緣，　從此生世間，
妄想著四句，　不知我所通。

世間非有生，　亦復非無生，
不從有無生，　亦非非有無。

諸因及與緣，　云何愚妄想，
非有亦非無，　亦復非有無？

如是觀世間，　心轉得無我，
一切性不生，　以從緣生故。

一切緣所作，　所作非自有，
事不自生事，　有二事過故。

無二事過故，　非有性可得。

觀諸有為法，　離攀緣所緣，
無心之心量，　我說為心量。

量者自性處，　緣性二俱離，
性究竟妙淨，　我說名為量。

施設世諦我，　彼則無實事，
諸陰陰施設，　無事亦復然。

有四種平等，　相及因性生，
第三無我等，　第四修修者，
妄想習氣轉，　有種種心生，
境界於外現，　是世俗心量。

外現而非有，　心見彼種種，
建立於身財，　我說為心量。

離一切諸見，　及離想所想，
無得亦無生，　我說為心量。

非性非非性，　性非性悉離，
謂彼心解脫，　我說為心量。

如如與空際，　涅槃及法界，
種種意生身，　我說為心量。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke in verse:
"Every cause, and every condition — it is from these that the world arises;
false thought clings to the four extremes, not knowing what I myself have penetrated.

The world is not born from existence, nor is it born from non-existence either;
it does not arise from both existence and non-existence, nor from neither of the two.

Every cause, and every condition — how, then, does the ignorant give rise to false thought,
[insisting they are] neither existent nor non-existent, and yet also neither both nor neither?

Observing the world in just this way, mind itself turns and attains no-self;
every [fixed] nature is unborn, precisely because it arises from conditions.

Everything made through conditions — what is made does not exist of itself;
a made thing cannot give rise to itself as a made thing, since this would entail the fault of two [separate] events.

And since the fault of two [separate] events cannot hold, no [fixed] nature at all can be apprehended.

Observing every conditioned dharma, apart from what clings and what is clung to,
the measure of mind that itself has no [fixed] mind — this is what I call the measure of mind.

This measure is the very place of own-nature, apart from both the clinging and the clung-to nature alike;
that nature, ultimately subtle and pure — this is what I call the measure.

Positing a conventional 'I' [as real] — there is, in truth, no real matter there at all;
positing the aggregates [as real] — there is, likewise, no real matter there either.

There are four kinds of equality: of mark, and of what gives rise to a cause;
the third, the equality of no-self; the fourth, of the practice and the one who practices —
through the turning of the habituated force of false thought, every kind of mind arises,
and an object-realm appears externally — this is the conventional measure of mind.

What appears externally has no real existence; it is mind that sees it in all its manifold forms,
establishing thereby a body and its resources — this is what I call the measure of mind.

Apart from every kind of view, and apart from both what is imagined and what does the imagining,
with nothing attained and nothing arising at all — this is what I call the measure of mind.

Neither a [fixed] nature nor the absence of one, both a nature and its absence left equally behind —
this is called the very liberation of mind — this is what I call the measure of mind.

Suchness, and the true limit; nirvāṇa, and the dharma-realm;
and every kind of mind-made body as well — this is what I call the measure of mind."

Reading note — after Zongle and Ruqi

The commentators trace the verse's opening logical move carefully: a made thing cannot be its own cause, on pain of splitting into two separate events where there should only be one. "事即果也，凡所生之法有因則必有果，如業因招生死之果，原其因既不生，果豈自生果耶！若無果生果二事之過，則任運離乎有無性執" — "'a made thing' means an effect: whatever dharma is produced, if it has a cause, must have a corresponding effect — just as karmic cause brings about the effect of birth-and-death. Tracing that cause back, since the cause itself is unborn, how could the effect give rise to itself as an effect? Once one is free of the fault of an effect producing itself as a separate effect, one is spontaneously free of the fixed grasping at either existence or non-existence." This is the same argument against self-causation that closed off the outsiders' theories back in Fascicle One, now applied directly to why the world as a whole cannot be said to simply "arise from existence" or "arise from non-existence."

On the closing run of images defining "the measure of mind," the commentators are explicit that this is not a list of different things but one thing named repeatedly from different angles, culminating in outright identity: "如如即真如，空際即實際，涅槃即究竟大涅槃，法界即佛法界，此皆一體異名" — "suchness is precisely true suchness; the true limit is precisely ultimate reality; nirvāṇa is precisely ultimate great nirvāṇa; the dharma-realm is precisely the buddha's own dharma-realm — all of these are a single essence under different names." Every image across this long verse — the measure with no fixed mind, the place of own-nature apart from both clinging and the clung-to, liberation itself, and finally suchness, the true limit, nirvāṇa, and the dharma-realm — turns out to be one and the same reality, approached through as many doors as the verse can find, precisely so that no single door gets mistaken for the whole house.

Part 26 Complete

This closes Part 26: false thought shown never to genuinely arise with respect to either conventional or ultimate meaning once both are correctly seen as mind's own manifestation, and a long closing verse defining "the measure of mind" through image after image — no-self, an effect that cannot cause itself, four kinds of equality, and finally suchness, the true limit, nirvāṇa, and the dharma-realm named as one and the same reality. Part 27 turns to word and meaning directly: what distinguishes mere language from the meaning it conveys, and why treating even the sūtra's own most exalted terms — unborn, unceasing, nirvāṇa itself — as fixed things to grasp collapses into exactly the error this fascicle has spent so long dismantling.
