The Gates of the Dependent and the Principal through Receiving and Moistening, and the Conclusion

This last part covers the remaining five gates — the dependent and the principal, self and other, the three kinds of action, the provisional and the real, and receiving and moistening — together with Zhanran's conclusion, and ends the commentary. These are the gates of "function": how a buddha's lands and bodies, responses, actions, and teachings reach beings. Zhili's recurring point is that all of this belongs, already, to the ordinary moment of mind. A buddha's pure land and an ordinary being's defiled body are not two because both are the three thousand inherent in a single thought; the buddha responds and beings appeal because both contain the same three thousand; the response-body is itself the Dharma-body, not because a separate "Vairocana" taught a superior sūtra, but because the perfect teaching opens every body to its essence. He defends again the presence of the provisional within self-practice, corrects a reading in the mirror simile, and closes by explaining why a work on the Profound Meaning, a text about teaching, explains every gate in terms of contemplating the mind: the first four gates furnish the essence of contemplation for the ten modes, and the last six the essence of contemplation for giving rise to the teaching.

6. The Gate of the Nonduality of the Dependent and the Principal

Chinese Source

六、依正不二門三：

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, the gate of the nonduality of the dependent and the principal; three:

Chinese Source

初、標。果後示現下三國土，名為依報，示現前三教主及九界身，名為正報，以寂光、圓佛本無二故，即是能開之妙法故也。此淨穢土及勝劣身，同在初心剎那，有何二邪？

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the statement. The lower three lands manifested after the fruit are called the dependent reward; manifesting as the lords of the first three teachings and in the bodies of the nine realms is called the principal reward. Because tranquil light and the buddha of the perfect [teaching] are originally not two, this is the wondrous Dharma that does the opening. These pure and impure lands and superior and inferior bodies are all within the beginner's instant of mind; what two is there?

Chinese Source

二、已證下，釋二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "That Vairocana, already realized…" onward: the explanation; two:

Chinese Source

初、明果用由因本具三：

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining that the function of the fruit comes from what the cause originally contains; three:

Chinese Source

初、示依正不二二：

Ru-Yi Translation

First, showing that the dependent and the principal are not two; two:

Chinese Source

初、明不二之由。已證者，蓋舉已證之位也。寂光、遮那依正不二，全由因德一念三千，儻因本不融，果何能一？縱修治令合，亦是無常，終歸分隔。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining the reason for nonduality. "Already realized" raises the stage of one who has already realized. That tranquil light and Vairocana, the dependent and the principal, are not two is wholly due to the three thousand in a single thought, the virtue of the cause. If the cause were not originally merged, how could the fruit be one? Even if they were made to join by cultivation and remedy, it would be impermanent and in the end return to separation.

Chinese Source

二、以三千下，示不二之相。在文可見。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Among the three thousand…" onward: showing the marks of nonduality. This can be seen in the text.

Chinese Source

二、是則下，明因理本融二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Thus already at…" onward: explaining that the principle of the cause is originally merged; two:

Chinese Source

初、明三位本妙。理等三位融相未顯，如五品人，雖以理觀遍融一切，而於事用未能自在。此位尚爾，前二可知。然迷情自異，不二天真，故云己有。自即己心、他是生佛，佛唯在果，餘二在因，果攝心生縱由修證，心能攝二全由性融，推功歸理乃言故使。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining that the three stages are originally wondrous. At the three stages, [identity in] principle and the rest, the marks of merging are not yet revealed. For example, a person of the five grades, though contemplating in accord with principle and merging everything, is not yet free in the function of events. If even this stage is so, the two before it can be known. But deluded feeling differs of itself, while nonduality is heaven-given; hence "already there are." "Self" is one's own mind; "other" is beings and buddhas. The Buddha is only in the fruit; the other two are in the cause. That the fruit gathers in mind and beings may be through cultivation and realization; that the mind can gather in the other two is wholly through the merging of the nature. Crediting this to principle, it says "this is why."

Chinese Source

二、但眾下，示一切皆融。不可任情必須順理，理智未顯見法仍差，須知本融無非妙境。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "It is only that sentient beings…" onward: showing that everything is merged. One must not follow feeling but must accord with principle. While the wisdom of principle is not yet revealed, dharmas are still seen as different; one must know that they are originally merged and none is other than the wondrous object.

Chinese Source

三、然應下，明始終無改二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "But one should further understand…" onward: explaining that beginning and end are unchanged; two:

Chinese Source

初、明情智局遍。於生局處佛能遍融，於佛遍處生自局限。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining that feeling confines and wisdom pervades. Where beings are confined, the Buddha can pervade and merge; where the Buddha pervades, beings confine themselves.

Chinese Source

二、始終下，明體用常融二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Beginning and end are unchanged…" onward: explaining that essence and function are always merged; two:

Chinese Source

初、略示。有四句，初、三約因果竪辨理同，二、四約諸法橫辨相入。意顯終既大小無妨，始亦如是，由不改故；果既依正不二，因亦復然，由理同故。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, a brief showing. There are four phrases. The first and third discuss, vertically in terms of cause and fruit, that the principle is the same; the second and fourth discuss, horizontally in terms of all dharmas, that they enter one another. The meaning shows that since at the end large and small do not obstruct each other, so it is at the beginning, because they are unchanged; and since in the fruit the dependent and the principal are not two, so it is in the cause, because the principle is the same.

Chinese Source

二、故淨下，廣示。文有八句，初、二句雙舉依正，同居等三土傳作淨穢，地獄等十界身迭分勝劣。次塵身下二句，雙示依正體性，一微塵身、一微塵國，各具三千、體遍法界，彼彼身土亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Therefore, among pure and impure lands…" onward: an extended showing. The text has eight phrases. The first two raise both the dependent and the principal: the three lands, the land of common dwelling and the rest, are in turn pure or impure; the bodies of the ten realms, hell and the rest, are in turn superior or inferior. Next, the two phrases from "a body of dust…" show both the essential nature of the dependent and of the principal: a dust-mote body and a dust-mote land each contain the three thousand, their essence pervading the dharma-realm; and so too every body and land.

Chinese Source

三是則下二句，明遍攝一切剎趣一剎、一切身趣一身，文雖剎身各攝，意必依正互收。四廣狹下二句，結妙，三千無礙出生無盡，不可心思、不可口議，如是融相今古常然，迷悟不改。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the two phrases from "Thus every dust-land…" explain pervasive gathering: all lands converge on one land, all bodies converge on one body. Though the text has lands and bodies each gathering in their own kind, the meaning must be that the dependent and the principal gather in each other. Fourth, the two phrases from "broad and narrow…" conclude the wondrous: the three thousand, unobstructed, give rise to what is without end, not to be conceived by the mind or discussed by the mouth. Such marks of merging are always so, past and present, unchanged by delusion or awakening.

Chinese Source

二、若非下，明理顯以觀為功二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "If not for the three thousand…" onward: explaining that the revealing of principle is the work of contemplation; two:

Chinese Source

初、克彰觀行之功。性具三千，若體若用本空假中，常自相攝，微塵本含法界，芥子常納須彌，無始無明強生隔礙，順性修觀即空假中，則自在體用顯現成就。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, displaying precisely the work of contemplative practice. The three thousand inherent in the nature, whether essence or function, are originally empty, provisional, and the middle, always containing one another of themselves: a dust-mote originally holds the dharma-realm, a mustard seed always takes in Mount Sumeru. Beginningless ignorance forcibly produces separation and obstruction. When one cultivates contemplation in accord with the nature — identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle — sovereign essence and function appear and are accomplished.

Chinese Source

性本空假中，性淨解脫也；修成空假中，實慧解脫也；起用空假中，方便淨解脫也。雖是修二性一，以皆空假中故則成合義。

Ru-Yi Translation

The nature, originally empty, provisional, and the middle, is liberation pure by nature; the empty, provisional, and middle accomplished by cultivation is liberation of real wisdom; the empty, provisional, and middle that gives rise to function is liberation pure in skillful means. Although these are two of cultivation and one of nature, because all are empty, provisional, and the middle, they form the meaning of combination.

Chinese Source

二、如是下，結示生佛一致。既解修成全是本具，即知迷悟體用不二，波濕無殊之譬於茲更明。我心為此，生佛為彼，緣起為事，性具為理，彼此三千理同不隔，遂令緣起互入無妨，依正不二斯之謂歟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Only in this way does one know…" onward: concluding that beings and buddhas are one. Once one understands that what cultivation accomplishes is wholly what was originally contained, one knows that delusion and awakening, essence and function, are not two; the simile of waves and wetness not differing becomes still clearer here. My mind is "this"; beings and buddhas are "that"; conditioned arising is event; inherent inclusion is principle. The three thousand of this and that are the same in principle and not separated, so that conditioned arisings enter one another without obstruction. Is this not what is meant by the nonduality of the dependent and the principal?

7. The Gate of the Nonduality of Self and Other

Chinese Source

七、自他不二門三：

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, the gate of the nonduality of self and other; three:

Chinese Source

初、標。染淨依正及以此門，都為感應神通而立，且即染之淨依正必融，即是神通及以能應，既由已辦，須名為自，唯未論感，感即他機，雖分自他同在一念，故上文云，他生他佛尚與心同，況己心生佛寧乖一念？

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the statement. Defilement and purity, the dependent and the principal, and this gate are all set up for sympathetic response and spiritual powers. The purity that is identical to defilement, and the dependent and the principal that must merge, are spiritual powers and the one who responds; since these are accomplished by oneself, they must be called "self." Only the appeal has not yet been discussed; the appeal is the capacity of others. Though self and other are distinguished, they are within the same single thought. So the text above says: "If even other beings and other buddhas are the same as the mind, how much less could the beings and buddhas of one's own mind depart from a single thought?"

Chinese Source

佛法眾生法皆名為他，而各具生佛，若己生佛顯，則與他佛生佛同俱為能化，唯他眾生生佛而為所化，既同一念自他豈殊？故名不二。依此觀察能成二妙，復名為門。

Ru-Yi Translation

The dharma of the Buddha and the dharma of beings are both called "other," and each contains beings and buddhas. When the beings and buddhas of oneself are revealed, they, together with the beings and buddhas of other buddhas, are all the one who transforms; only the beings and buddhas of other beings are those transformed. Since they are within the same single thought, how could self and other differ? Hence it is called nonduality. Contemplating on this basis can accomplish the two wondrous aspects; so it is also called a gate.

Chinese Source

二、隨機下，釋二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "In benefiting others according to their capacities…" onward: the explanation; two:

Chinese Source

初示感應之體本同二：

Ru-Yi Translation

First, showing that the essence of appeal and response is originally the same; two:

Chinese Source

初、約法示三：

Ru-Yi Translation

First, showing in terms of the dharma; three:

Chinese Source

初、約一性明自他。證果之後不動而應，眾機普益，既非謀作皆由性同，因果驗之灼然不二。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining self and other in terms of the one nature. After realizing the fruit, one responds without moving, and the many capacities are benefited everywhere. Since this is not by deliberate making, it is all because the nature is the same. Verified by cause and fruit, they are clearly not two.

Chinese Source

二、如理下，約三千明感應。先以三諦例自他本同，三千既即空假中，乃三德三諦之三千也。自行即淨穢亡泯，無不空中，利他則帝網交羅三千皆假，三諦既即三是一，自他則分而不分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Take the three virtues of the principle-nature…" onward: explaining appeal and response in terms of the three thousand. First, by analogy with the three truths, self and other are originally the same. Since the three thousand are identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle, they are the three thousand of the three virtues and three truths. In one's own practice, pure and impure vanish and dissolve, and nothing is not the empty and the middle; in benefiting others, the three thousand interlace like Indra's net, all provisional. Since the three truths are identical, three being one, self and other are divided yet not divided.

Chinese Source

然今所辨自他俱在妙假，以能化所化皆三千故。欲約三諦論不二故，且對空中辨之。妙假尚不離空中，一假豈應隔異？

Ru-Yi Translation

But self and other as discussed here are both within the wondrous provisional, because the one who transforms and those transformed are both the three thousand. Wishing to discuss nonduality in terms of the three truths, it sets them against the empty and the middle for the time being. If the wondrous provisional is not apart from the empty and the middle, how could the one provisional be separated?

Chinese Source

問：「前修外觀既當自行，但列空中與今符合，何苦責之？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "Since the outer contemplation cultivated earlier corresponds to one's own practice, and [the old copy there] listed only the empty and the middle, in agreement with this passage, why criticize it so hard?"

Chinese Source

答：「往時不解境觀之徒，據此等文妄有除削。何者？此約三千以明空中，已具不思議假，況復利他之觀，初心豈可不修？不修則何名摩訶薩？秖為假觀始行須修，方得感應同居一念，自他不二據茲而立，如何劫云自行無假？又若自行唯修空中，內觀豈非自行？何故言即空假中邪？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "In the past, people who did not understand object and contemplation cut [the text] arbitrarily on the basis of passages like this. Why [is that wrong]? Here the empty and the middle are explained in terms of the three thousand, which already contains the inconceivable provisional. Moreover, how could the beginner not cultivate the contemplation that benefits others? If one did not, how could one be called a mahāsattva? It is precisely because the contemplation of the provisional must be cultivated from the start of practice that appeal and response can dwell together in a single thought; the nonduality of self and other is established on this basis. How can one insist that one's own practice has no provisional? Again, if in one's own practice one cultivated only the empty and the middle — is the inner contemplation not one's own practice? Why then does it say 'identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle'?"

Chinese Source

物機等者，正明自他各具三千，細辨故三千，總論故十界，轉現互生即無記化，化化復作化也，依正皆爾，應必對感機豈不然！一念從事寂光約理，二必相即故互舉爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The capacities of beings" and the rest explain precisely that self and other each contain the three thousand: discussed in detail, the three thousand; discussed in general, the ten realms. "Appears in turn" and "gives rise to one another" are the morally indeterminate transformations, transformations that in turn make transformations; both the dependent and the principal are so. Since response must face appeal, how could capacity not be so? "A single thought" follows event; "tranquil light" is in terms of principle. The two must be identical, so they are raised alternately.

Chinese Source

三、眾生下，約俱具明道交。既三無差別則感應相收，眾生感心中他佛，諸佛應心中他生，不然，豈能一念皆令解脫邪？

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "Because sentient beings…" onward: explaining the meeting of the ways in terms of both containing. Since the three are without distinction, appeal and response gather each other in. Beings appeal to the other buddhas within their minds; buddhas respond to the other beings within their minds. Otherwise, how could they bring all to liberation in a single thought?

Chinese Source

二、不然下，約諭示二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Otherwise…" onward: showing by simile; two:

Chinese Source

初、順諭。諸佛三千即現像之理，眾生三千即生像之性，若不然者，不能即感即應，非任運化也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the direct simile. The three thousand of the buddhas are the principle of showing images; the three thousand of beings are the nature of producing images. If it were not so, there could be no immediate appeal and immediate response, and it would not be spontaneous transformation.

Chinese Source

二、若一下，反諭。以鑑淨形對無不現之理，而反顯之。意云若不現者可言鑑理有窮形事不通也，諸佛悟理、眾生在事，三千理滿，若一機扣之不應，則可云三千互闕，既無此理則前義善成。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "If there were a single form…" onward: the reverse simile. It shows the matter in reverse by means of the principle that a clean mirror shows every form that faces it. The meaning is: if it did not show it, one could say that the mirror's principle has a limit and the form's event does not get through. Buddhas awaken to principle; beings are in events. The principle of the three thousand is fulfilled; if a single capacity knocked and there were no response, one could say the three thousand are lacking on one side. Since there is no such principle, the foregoing meaning is well established.

Chinese Source

仍釋伏疑。何故？眾生多不見佛，故云若與鑑隔則容有是理，即障重機生名與鑑隔，機成名對，若其對者終無不現。

Ru-Yi Translation

It then resolves a hidden doubt. Why [do they not see]? Many beings do not see the Buddha; hence "if [the form] is cut off from the mirror, this may well be so." That is, when a capacity heavy with obstruction arises, it is called being cut off from the mirror; when the capacity is ripe, it is called facing; if it faces, it is never not shown.

Chinese Source

然未通字必誤，合云不通，縱移於下句，語稍不便，智者詳之。

Ru-Yi Translation

But the character 未 in 未通 must be an error; it should read 不通. Even if it were moved to the next phrase, the wording would be somewhat awkward. Let the wise consider it.

Chinese Source

二、若鑑下，明觀行之功方顯二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "If the mirror has not yet shown an image…" onward: explaining that it appears only through the work of contemplative practice; two:

Chinese Source

初、帶諭彰用匪功成者，故知心鑑本明、三千之像本具。對物未能現者，蓋三惑之塵所遮，去塵雖緣、了之功，現像乃全由性具，此中正明觀心發用。他云，由機現像。其義天隔。觀法大旨者，非唯此中，諸門皆爾，但在此說為便耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, using the simile to show that the function is not accomplished by effort. So we know that the mind-mirror is originally bright, and the images of the three thousand are originally contained. That it cannot yet show [images] when facing things is because it is covered by the dust of the three delusions. Removing the dust is the work of the conditioning and revealing [causes], but the showing of images is wholly due to inherent inclusion. This explains precisely how the contemplation of mind gives rise to function. They say: "Images are shown because of the capacity." Their meaning is as far off as heaven. "The main purport of the method of contemplation" is not only here; every gate is so, but it is convenient to say it here.

Chinese Source

二、應知下，就法明發由觀合。雖由緣、了須揀前三，稱性圓修方名一合，功成用顯設化無方。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "One should know…" onward: explaining in terms of the dharma that arising comes from contemplation uniting [with the nature]. Although it comes from the conditioning and revealing [causes], one must set aside the first three [teachings]; only perfect cultivation in accord with the nature is called being united as one. When the work is accomplished, the function appears, and one sets up transformation without fixed place.

8. The Gate of the Nonduality of the Three Kinds of Action

Chinese Source

八、三業不二門三：

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, the gate of the nonduality of the three kinds of action; three:

Chinese Source

初、標者，果後逗機示諸三業，四時三教謂有差殊。今經開之，唯圓法體，諸身尚即，三業豈分？故名不二。亦就心法示也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the statement. After the fruit, fitting the capacities, one displays the three kinds of action; in the four periods and three teachings they are said to differ. The present sūtra opens them: there is only the perfect Dharma-essence. If even the bodies are identical, how could the three kinds of action be divided? Hence it is called nonduality. This too is shown in terms of the dharma of mind.

Chinese Source

二、於化下，釋二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "In the gate of transforming others…" onward: the explanation; two:

Chinese Source

初、明所顯果用二：

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining the function of the fruit that is revealed; two:

Chinese Source

初、約對機顯逗會無差二：

Ru-Yi Translation

First, revealing in terms of facing capacities that fitting and merging are without difference; two:

Chinese Source

初、示三輪不同。三皆祕妙，非下地知，故名為密，能轉摧碾復名為輪，轉己示他摧他惑業，稱機示現毫髮不差。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, showing that the three wheels differ. All three are secret and wondrous, not known by those of lower grounds, hence called "secret." They can turn, crush, and grind, hence also called "wheels": turning one's own to show others, crushing others' delusion and karma, manifesting in accord with capacities without a hair's difference.

Chinese Source

二、在身下，明真應復殊，說三權法皆是應身，若聞圓乘必見法佛，別縱覩報猶是修成，圓見應身皆唯本具，仍約四味權實，未會真應且分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "In body…" onward: explaining that the true and the responsive also differ. Teaching the three provisional dharmas is always by the response-body; one who hears the perfect vehicle must see the Dharma-buddha. In the distinct [teaching], even if one beholds the reward [body], it is still accomplished through cultivation; in the perfect, one sees the response-body as wholly originally contained. Still, in terms of the provisional and the real of the four flavors, the true and the responsive are not yet merged and remain divided for the time being.

Chinese Source

二、約稱理明卷舒自在二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining in terms of according with principle that rolling up and unrolling are free; two:

Chinese Source

初、融身說。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, merging body and teaching.

Chinese Source

問：「此中法身說佛道邪？餘文何故不許法身有說？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "Here, does the Dharma-body teach the Buddha way? Why do other texts not allow that the Dharma-body teaches?"

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Reading Notes for Part 4

Translator's notes

The four lands and the "lower three." Tiantai distinguishes four buddha-lands: the land where ordinary and noble dwell together (同居), the land of expedient remainder (方便有餘), the land of real reward without obstruction (實報無障礙), and the land of eternal tranquil light (常寂光). The "lower three lands" are the first three; tranquil light is the fourth. "The lords of the first three teachings" are the buddhas as they appear in the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct teachings.

The five grades (五品). The five grades of disciples (五品弟子位) of the perfect teaching, at the stage of identity in contemplative practice; "the two before it" are identity in principle and identity in name.

"Already there are" (己有). Zhili glosses the base text's 已有 as 己有 ("one's own [mind] has"). The base text as translated here reads 已有 ("already there are"); the Chinese of the commentary is kept as written.

Three liberations (性淨／實慧／方便淨解脫). Liberation pure by nature, liberation of real wisdom, and liberation pure in skillful means, corresponding to the three tracks (real nature, contemplative illumination, resource-and-accomplishment).

"Two of cultivation and one of nature" (修二性一). As in Gate 3 of the base text: the two tracks accomplished by cultivation (illumination and resource) and the one track of the nature (real nature).

The old copy and the provisional in self-practice. Zhili returns to the textual dispute of Part 2: the opponents, seeing that Gate 7 says "in one's own practice they lie only in the empty and the middle," removed "the provisional" from Gate 2. Zhili replies that here "the empty and the middle" are explained in terms of the three thousand, which already includes the provisional.

"Morally indeterminate transformations" (無記化). Transformation bodies and lands produced by spiritual power, which are karmically neither good nor bad. "The dhyāna of transformations" (化禪) is the dhyāna by which such transformations are produced.

The correction of 未通. Zhili says the base text's 不通 ("would not get through") must be the correct reading, and that a variant 未通 ("does not yet get through") is an error. The base text as translated here (T1927) already reads 不通.

"They say: 'Images are shown because of the capacity'" (由機現像). An opponent's reading of the mirror simile, which Zhili rejects: the image appears because it is inherently contained in the mirror, not because of the facing form.

The Huayan view of Vairocana. Zhili rejects the Huayan claim that the Avataṃsaka alone was taught by Vairocana and the other sūtras by Śākyamuni. "The subtle, wondrous, pure Dharma-body, complete with the thirty-two marks" is from the Lotus Sūtra's Devadatta chapter (the dragon girl's verse).

"They say there are no characters 因果 after 信." A textual variant in the base text's "Only if one has faith in cause and fruit"; Zhili keeps 因果.

The four Māras (四魔). The Māras of the aggregates, of afflictions, of death, and the divine Māra.

"He teaches only bodhisattvas" and "not one who does not become a buddha." Both from the Lotus Sūtra, chapter on skillful means.

The Words and Phrases (文句). Zhiyi's Fahua Wenju, which explains the ten suchnesses in terms of the Buddha's dharma, as the Profound Meaning does in terms of beings.

"Relying solely on origin and trace to reveal the real." Zhili's abbreviated citation of the Exposition of the Principles (compare the fuller quotation in Part 1).

The first four and last six gates. Zhili's closing division: the gates of form and mind, inner and outer, cultivation and nature, and cause and fruit furnish the essence of contemplation for the ten modes; the gates from defilement and purity through receiving and moistening furnish the essence of contemplation for giving rise to the teaching (see Part 1, on the Mohe Zhiguan's section on giving rise to the teaching).
