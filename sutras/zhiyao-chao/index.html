Pointing Out the Essentials of the Ten Gates of Nonduality.

Siming Zhili (四明知禮, 960–1028) wrote this commentary in 1004 to settle a controversy that had divided the Tiantai school. Rival teachers, later called the off-mountain faction, read Zhanran's Ten Gates of Nonduality as saying that the single thought to be contemplated is the pure, true nature itself. Zhili insists instead that it is the ordinary, deluded moment of mind, afflictions and all, that inherently includes the three thousand realms — and that evil, too, is inherent in the nature. His reading became the orthodoxy of the home-mountain school, and this work is its classic statement.

How to read this collection

The commentary quotes Zhanran's text phrase by phrase; where it does, the English of this site's Ten Gates of Nonduality is followed, so the two can be read side by side. Read the Ten Gates of Nonduality first.

Part 1 holds the prefaces and Zhili's general introduction, where most of the polemic lies; Parts 2–4 go through the ten gates in order.

The Text's Structure

Two fascicles, divided here into four parts at the gates.

The Gates of Form and Mind and of Inner and Outer
This part covers the first two of the ten gates and closes the first fascicle.

Part 3
Cultivation and Nature, Cause and Fruit, Defilement and Purity
This part opens the second fascicle of Zhili's commentary and covers Zhanran's third, fourth, and fifth gates.
Translated
Part 4
The Gates of the Dependent and the Principal through Receiving and Moistening, and the Conclusion
This last part covers the remaining five gates — the dependent and the principal, self and other, the three kinds of action, the provisional and the real, and receiving and moistening — together with Zhanran's conclusion, and ends the commentary.

Translation Notes

Key terms, kept consistent across the whole text and with this site's other Tiantai translations. See the full translation notes, and also this site's note on translation method.

性惡 — inherent evilEvil as inherent in the nature itself, not only produced by cultivation; Zhili's central claim.
別理隨緣 — the distinct principle following conditionsWhether the distinct teaching's principle can follow conditions; a point of dispute with the off-mountain masters.
山家 / 山外 — home-mountain / off-mountainThe two factions of Song-dynasty Tiantai.
一念 — a single thoughtFor Zhili, the ordinary deluded moment of mind.
正因 / 緣因 / 了因 — the correct cause / the conditioning cause / the revealing causeThe three causes of buddha-nature, matched to this site's Fahua Xuanyi and Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra.
