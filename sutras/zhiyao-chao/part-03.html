Cultivation and Nature, Cause and Fruit, Defilement and Purity

This part opens the second fascicle of Zhili's commentary and covers Zhanran's third, fourth, and fifth gates. These are the gates in which Zhili's own position is most fully at stake. In the gate of cultivation and nature he argues that what practice "accomplishes" is already contained in the nature, and that cultivation is nothing but "the whole nature giving rise to cultivation." Along the way he defends the reading "relies on wisdom to give rise to cultivation" against rivals who preferred an old edition reading "relying on knowing," which they took as the "numinous knowing" of the true mind. In the gate of cause and fruit he explains why cause and fruit are kept as names for a time and then both vanish, and he states his best-known distinction: the perfect teaching's "identity" means that the whole function is the essence, because the three thousand are inherently included in the nature. A principle that merely "accords with conditions" to give rise to the nine realms, without containing them, is in his view only the distinct teaching. In the gate of defilement and purity he reads the Dharma-nature and ignorance as lending power to each other, re-reads Zhanran's simile of muddy and clear water, and shows how the contemplation of negating and illuminating is carried out in the beginner's present instant of mind.

3. The Gate of the Nonduality of Cultivation and Nature: The Heading

Chinese Source

三、修性不二門三：

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the gate of the nonduality of cultivation and nature; three:

Chinese Source

初、標。修謂修治造作，即變造三千，性謂本有不改，即理具三千，今示全性起修則諸行無作，全修在性則一念圓成，是則修外無性、性外無修，互泯互融故稱不二，而就心法妙為門。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the heading. "Cultivation" means working on and creating; that is, transforming and creating the three thousand. "Nature" means what is originally present and unchanging; that is, the three thousand contained in principle. Here it shows that when the whole nature gives rise to cultivation, all practices are without fabrication, and when the whole of cultivation lies in the nature, a single thought is perfectly accomplished. Thus there is no nature outside cultivation and no cultivation outside the nature; they mutually dissolve and mutually merge, and so it is called "nonduality"; and it takes the wondrous of the dharma of mind as the gate.

Inherent Virtue, and Cultivation and Nature Accomplishing Each Other

Chinese Source

二、性德下，釋二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Inherent virtue…" onward: the explanation; two:

Chinese Source

初、修性雙立三：

Ru-Yi Translation

First, cultivation and nature both established; three:

Chinese Source

初、修性對論二：

Ru-Yi Translation

First, discussing cultivation and nature in relation to each other; two:

Chinese Source

初、直明性德。言德者即本具三千，皆常樂我淨故。界如一念，即前內境具德剎那心也。界如既即空假中，任運成於三德三軌等，即空是般若清淨義故，即假是解脫自在義故，即中是法身究竟義故，諸三例之。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, directly explaining inherent virtue. It is called "virtue" because the originally contained three thousand are all permanence, bliss, self, and purity. "The realms and suchnesses in a single thought" is the instant of mind, containing the virtues, that was the inner object above. Since the realms and suchnesses are identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle, they spontaneously form the three virtues, the three tracks, and so on: identical to the empty — this is the sense of prajñā as pure; identical to the provisional — this is the sense of liberation as free; identical to the middle — this is the sense of the Dharma-body as ultimate. The other sets of three follow this pattern.

Chinese Source

然諸法皆可論於修性，亦為成觀唯指一念。應知前二門直明依境立觀，此門及因果不二乃委示前二，令成圓行始終也。何者？性德豈出色心不二，修德莫非一心三觀。今示修性互成成妙智行，以此智行從因至果，則位位無作，方名如夢勤加等，即自行始終皆妙也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, all dharmas can be discussed in terms of cultivation and nature, but here too, in order to accomplish contemplation, it points only to a single thought. One should know that the previous two gates directly explain setting up contemplation on the basis of the object, while this gate and [the gate of] the nonduality of cause and fruit set out the previous two in detail, to accomplish the beginning and end of perfect practice. Why? How could inherent virtue go beyond the nonduality of form and mind? Cultivated virtue is nothing but the threefold contemplation in a single mind. Here it shows that cultivation and nature accomplish each other, accomplishing wondrous wisdom and practice; and when, with this wisdom and practice, one goes from cause to fruit, each stage is without fabrication. Only this is called "like diligent effort made in a dream" and so on; that is, one's own practice is wondrous from beginning to end.

Chinese Source

二、性雖下，以修對辨二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Though the nature is originally so…" onward: setting it forth in relation to cultivation; two:

Chinese Source

初、相成者，性雖具足全體在迷，必藉妙智解了發起圓修，故云性雖本爾藉智起修，由此智行方能徹照性德。而此智行復由性德全體而發，若非性發不能照性，若非徹照性無由顯，故云由修照性、由性發修，此二句正辨相成之相。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, accomplishing each other. Although the nature is fully complete, its whole essence is in delusion; one must rely on wondrous wisdom to understand it clearly and bring forth perfect cultivation. Hence "Though the nature is originally so, one relies on wisdom to give rise to cultivation." Only through this wisdom and practice can one thoroughly illuminate inherent virtue. Yet this wisdom and practice are in turn brought forth from the whole essence of inherent virtue. If it were not brought forth by the nature, it could not illuminate the nature; if [the nature] were not thoroughly illuminated, it would have no way to become manifest. Hence "through cultivation one illuminates the nature, and from the nature cultivation is brought forth." These two phrases set forth precisely the mark of accomplishing each other.

Chinese Source

二、在性下，明互具者，相成之義雖顯，恐謂修從顯發方有、性德稍異修成，故今全指修成本來已具。如《止觀》廣辨三千之相，雖是逆順二修，全為顯於性具，則全修成性也。又一一行業因果自他，雖假修成，全是性德三千顯現，故云全性成修也。又雖全性起修，而未嘗少虧性德，以常不改故，故云性無所移。雖全修成性，而未始暫闕修德，以常變造故，故云修常宛爾。然若知修性各論三千，則諸義皆顯，故荊谿云，諸家不明修性，蓋不如此明也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "In keeping to the nature…" onward: explaining that they contain each other. Although the sense of their accomplishing each other is clear, one might fear [someone would] say that cultivation exists only from the point of its manifestation, and that inherent virtue is somewhat different from what cultivation accomplishes; so here it points wholly to what cultivation accomplishes as already originally contained. For example, the Calming and Contemplation sets forth at length the marks of the three thousand; though these are the two cultivations, contrary and compliant, they wholly serve to reveal inherent inclusion — this is "the whole of cultivation becomes the nature." Again, each practice and karma, cause and fruit, self and other, though accomplished by means of cultivation, is wholly the manifestation of the three thousand of inherent virtue; hence "the whole of the nature becomes cultivation." Again, although the whole nature gives rise to cultivation, it never lacks the least part of inherent virtue, because it is permanently unchanging; hence "the nature is not moved in the least." Although the whole of cultivation becomes the nature, it never for a moment lacks cultivated virtue, because it is permanently transforming and creating; hence "cultivation is always fully present." But if one knows that cultivation and nature are each discussed as the three thousand, then all the meanings become clear. So Jingxi says that the various schools do not understand cultivation and nature: it is that they do not understand them in this way.

A Disputed Reading: "Wisdom" or "Knowing"

Chinese Source

問：「他云，舊本作藉知曰修，而以本性靈知用釋知字。若云藉智起修者，蓋寫者書曰逼知，後人認作智字，既不成句，又見下句有起修之言，遂輒加起字爾。此復云何？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "Someone says that the old edition reads 'relying on knowing is called cultivation' (藉知曰修), and uses 'the numinous knowing of the original nature' to explain the word 'knowing.' As for 'relies on wisdom to give rise to cultivation,' [they say] a copyist wrote 曰 too close to 知, later people took [the two] as the character 智 'wisdom,' and since it no longer made a sentence, and they saw that the next phrase had the words 'give rise to cultivation,' they hastily added the character 起 'give rise to.' What about this?"

Chinese Source

答：「既許寫曰逼知，遂成智之一字，何妨往人寫日遠知，誤成知曰二字。必是因脫起字，復由二字相懸，致使有本作藉知曰修也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "If you allow that [a copyist] wrote 曰 too close to 知 and it became the single character 智, what prevents [supposing that] an earlier person wrote 日 too far from 知, and it mistakenly became the two characters 知曰? It must be that, because the character 起 dropped out, and further because the two [components] were spaced apart, a certain edition came to read 'relying on knowing is called cultivation.'

Chinese Source

故知寫字添脫，遠近難可定之，魚魯之訛豈今獨有，須將義定方見是非。何者？他既暗於三法妙義，尚將一念因心陰識，直作真知解之，況今有此訛文知字可執，豈不作靈知解邪？且靈知之名圭峯專用，既非即陰而示，又無修發之相，正是偏指清淨真如，唯於真心及緣理斷九之義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So we know that in copying, characters are added and dropped, and near and far are hard to determine; the error of 魚 for 魯 is hardly unique to today. One must settle it by meaning before right and wrong can be seen. Why? Since they are in the dark about the wondrous meaning of the three dharmas, they even take the single thought — the mind as cause, the aggregate of consciousness — and explain it outright as true knowing; how much more, when there is this erroneous text with the character 'knowing' to hold onto, would they not explain it as numinous knowing? Moreover, the term 'numinous knowing' is used particularly by Guifeng. It is neither shown as identical to the aggregates, nor does it have the mark of being brought forth by cultivation; it points one-sidedly to pure true suchness, and has only the meaning of the true mind and of taking principle as object and cutting off the nine [realms].

Chinese Source

他云，因真教緣示善惡知，即是真知，乃知諸法唯心，故云藉知曰修。今問：此之知字，為解為行？若隨闕者則不名修。若單立知字解行足者，乃玄文智行二妙，《止觀》妙解正修便為徒設，則天台但傳《禪詮都序》也。又言示善惡知即真知者，還須先用妙解即之不？次用妙行即之不？若然者，正是藉智起修。若不然者，智行二妙全無用也。今云藉智起修，直是由於智妙起於行妙耳，故後結文云，如境本來具三，依理生解故名為智。智解導行，行解契理，三法相符不異而異。然智行俱修今偏在行者，蓋智從解了發起義彊，行就進趣修治義彊，故從彊也。又此一句，全是《釋籤》行妙中文，彼云藉智起行故。他又云，智名未稱全性成修。若爾，何名智妙？應亦本是知妙，後人改為智妙乎！」

Ru-Yi Translation

"They say: 'Through the condition of the true teaching, it is shown that the knowing of good and evil is itself true knowing; then one knows that all dharmas are only mind. Hence "relying on knowing is called cultivation."' Now I ask: is this 'knowing' understanding or practice? If either is lacking, it is not called cultivation. If setting up the single word 'knowing' sufficed for understanding and practice, then the two wondrous aspects of wisdom and practice in the Profound Meaning, and the wondrous understanding and correct cultivation of the Calming and Contemplation, would have been set up in vain, and Tiantai would transmit nothing but the General Preface to the Chan Collection! Again, you say that the knowing of good and evil is shown to be identical to true knowing: must one still first use wondrous understanding to make it identical, or not? And next use wondrous practice to make it identical, or not? If so, that is precisely 'relying on wisdom to give rise to cultivation.' If not, the two wondrous aspects of wisdom and practice are of no use at all. Here, 'relies on wisdom to give rise to cultivation' is simply giving rise to the wondrous of practice from the wondrous of wisdom. So the concluding passage later says: 'For example, the object originally contains the three; understanding arises in reliance on principle, and so it is called wisdom; the understanding of wisdom guides practice, and practice and understanding accord with principle. The three tracks match one another, not different yet different.' Now, wisdom and practice are both cultivation; that here [the word 'cultivation'] falls particularly on practice is because wisdom is strong in the sense of bringing forth from understanding, while practice is strong in the sense of advancing and working on it; so [the word] follows the stronger. Again, this phrase is wholly the text of the Shiqian on the wondrous of practice, for there it says 'relying on wisdom to give rise to practice.' They also say that the name 'wisdom' does not fit 'the whole of the nature becomes cultivation.' If so, why is it called 'the wondrous of wisdom'? Should that too originally have been 'the wondrous of knowing,' which later people changed to 'the wondrous of wisdom'?"

Contrary and Compliant Cultivation

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Cultivation, further, is of two kinds…" onward: explaining that the contrary and the compliant are opposed; two:

Chinese Source

初、明對逆故二性並存。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining that, because it is set against the contrary, the two natures both remain.

Chinese Source

二、明因順故二心俱泯。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining that, because of the compliant, the two minds both dissolve.

Chinese Source

初文者，上之全性起修，一往且論順修，修名既通，有順有逆，今欲雙亡先須對辨。了性為行者，即藉智起修也。背性成迷者，始從無間至別教道，皆背性故。逆稱修者，即修惡之類也。心雖不二等者，隨緣迷了之處，心性不變，故云不二。逆順二性是全體隨緣，故即理之事常分，故曰事殊。是則以前稱圓理修，對今背性，故成二也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first passage: "the whole of the nature becomes cultivation" above discusses, at first look, only compliant cultivation; but since the name "cultivation" is general, there is compliant and contrary; now, wishing to make both vanish, it must first set them forth in relation to each other. "Understanding the nature and making it one's practice" is "relying on wisdom to give rise to cultivation." "Turning one's back on the nature and becoming deluded": from [the hell of] no interval up to the path of the distinct teaching, all turn their backs on the nature. That the contrary is called cultivation [is because] it is of the kind of cultivated evil. "Though the mind is not two" and so on: where, according with conditions, there is delusion and understanding, the mind-nature does not change; hence "not two." The two natures, contrary and compliant, are the whole essence according with conditions; so the events that are identical to principle are always divided; hence "as event they … differ." Thus the earlier cultivation that accords with the perfect principle, set against the present turning one's back on the nature, becomes two.

Chinese Source

二因順故二心俱泯者，可不可也。由，因也。不可因逆順二事同一心性，便令迷逆之事作了順邪！此乃責其不分迷悟也。故正立理云，故須一期迷了照性成修。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Second, because of the compliant, the two minds both dissolve": "It is possible" (可) [means] "is it possible?" (不可). "By way of" (由) means "because of." Could one, because the two events of contrary and compliant share one mind-nature, then make the event of delusion and contrariness into understanding and compliance? This rebukes those who do not distinguish delusion and awakening. So it sets out the principle correctly: "Therefore, over the whole span of delusion and understanding, one must illuminate the nature to accomplish cultivation."

Chinese Source

言一期者，即與一往之語同類，乃非終畢之義也。蓋言雖據寂理二修終泯，且須一期改迷為了，了心若發必照性成修，若見性修心自然二心俱泯，此義顯然如指諸掌，人何惑焉？豈非逆修如病、順修如藥，雖知藥病終須兩亡，一往且須服藥治病，藥力若効其身必康，身若安康藥病俱泯。法諭如此，智者思之。

Ru-Yi Translation

The words "over the whole span" (一期) are of the same kind as the expression "at first look" (一往); they do not have the sense of "to the very end." For what it says is: although, according to the quiescent principle, the two cultivations ultimately dissolve, for a period one must change delusion into understanding. Once the mind of understanding arises, one will surely illuminate the nature to accomplish cultivation; and if one sees the nature and cultivates the mind, the two minds naturally both dissolve. This meaning is as plain as pointing to one's palm; why are people confused about it? Is it not that contrary cultivation is like illness and compliant cultivation like medicine? Although one knows that medicine and illness must in the end both vanish, at first one must take the medicine to cure the illness. If the medicine takes effect, the body will surely be healthy; and once the body is well, medicine and illness both dissolve. Such are the dharma and the simile; let the wise reflect on it.

Chinese Source

問：「他云，舊本作此其字，釋意云，豈可由不移生死涅槃常殊之性事，便任此為了修乎？是故下句便云，故須一其迷了照性成修。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "Someone says the old edition has the character 其 here, and explains the meaning: 'How could one, by way of not moving the nature and event in which birth-and-death and nirvāṇa always differ, simply leave it so and take this as the cultivation of understanding? That is why the next phrase says: "Therefore one must unify delusion and understanding, illuminate the nature, and accomplish cultivation."'

Chinese Source

此復云何？」

Ru-Yi Translation

What about this?"

Chinese Source

答：「他雖執於舊本，而違現文。何者？文云，可由事不移心則令迷修成了。文意唯責執迷為了，何曾雙責迷了為了邪？豈非彰灼違文乎！故知迷了雙泯功由了修。何者？迷既背性，故立了修翻之，遂一期事殊也。了既順理，理無違順，故二心自泯也。是知用此期字者，既不違文兼得順理。若用此其字，相違稍多，不能廣破也。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Although they hold to the old edition, they go against the text at hand. Why? The text says: 'It is possible, by way of events and without moving the mind, to turn deluded cultivation into understanding.' The meaning of the text rebukes only taking delusion to be understanding; when did it ever rebuke both delusion and understanding [being taken] as understanding? Is this not plainly going against the text? So we know that the dissolving of both delusion and understanding is the work of the cultivation of understanding. Why? Since delusion turns its back on the nature, the cultivation of understanding is set up to overturn it; so for a period the events differ. Since understanding complies with principle, and in principle there is neither going against nor complying, the two minds dissolve of themselves. Thus we know that using the character 期 here does not go against the text and also complies with principle. If one uses the character 其 here, the contradictions are rather many; I cannot refute them all at length."

Separated and Combined: "Each Has Three" and "Two and One"

Chinese Source

三、又了下，明離合本同二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "Again, the compliant cultivation of understanding…" onward: explaining that separating and combining are originally the same; two:

Chinese Source

初、約法明離合相異者。復置逆修，但論順修法相離合。蓋此修性在諸經論不易條流，若得此離合意，則不迷修性多少。如《金光明玄義》十種三法，乃是采取經論修性法相，故具離合兩說。如三德三寶，雖是修德之極，義必該性；三身三智，文雖約悟，理必通迷；三識三道，既指事即理，必全性起修。此六豈非修性各三。三因既以一性對智行二修，三菩提、三大乘、三涅槃，並以一性對證理起用二修，此四豈非修二性一。若各三者唯屬於圓，以各相主對全性起修，故修二性一則兼於別。直以修二顯於性一，則教道所詮，若知合九為三，復是圓義。此文多用各三，如云性指三障，是故具三，修從性成，成三法爾。又云，一念心因既具三軌，此因成果名三涅槃。若後結文三法相符，雖似修二性一，乃合九為三也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining in terms of the dharma that the marks of separating and combining differ. Again setting aside contrary cultivation, it discusses only the separating and combining of the dharma-marks of compliant cultivation. For cultivation and nature are not easy to sort out in the sūtras and treatises; if one grasps this meaning of separating and combining, one will not be confused about how many belong to cultivation and how many to the nature. For example, the ten sets of three dharmas in the Profound Meaning of the Golden Light gather the dharma-marks of cultivation and nature from the sūtras and treatises, and so contain both accounts, the separated and the combined. The three virtues and the three jewels, though they are the culmination of cultivated virtue, in meaning must include the nature; the three bodies and the three wisdoms, though in wording they concern awakening, in principle must extend to delusion; the three consciousnesses and the three paths, since they point to events as identical to principle, must be the whole nature giving rise to cultivation. Are these six not "cultivation and nature each have three"? The three causes set the one nature against the two cultivations of wisdom and practice; the three bodhis, the three great vehicles, and the three nirvāṇas all set the one nature against the two cultivations of realizing principle and giving rise to function. Are these four not "cultivation is two and nature is one"? "Each has three" belongs only to the perfect [teaching], because each is matched one to one, the whole nature giving rise to cultivation; so "cultivation is two and nature is one" also includes the distinct [teaching]. Simply revealing the one nature by the two cultivations is what is expressed by the path of teaching; if one knows that the nine are combined as three, it is again the perfect meaning. This text mostly uses "each has three," as when it says: "the nature points to the three obstacles; that is why it contains three. Cultivation is accomplished from the nature, and accomplishing the three is naturally so." It also says: "Since the cause that is the mind of a single thought already contains the three tracks, when this cause becomes the fruit it is called the three nirvāṇas." As for the later concluding passage, "the three tracks match one another": though it seems to be "cultivation is two and nature is one," it is in fact combining the nine as three.

Chinese Source

修二各三等者，就合各開。如三般若等，是了因之三；如三菩提等，是緣因之三；共發三道等，正因之三。既發性三俱云修九者，雖兼性三，咸為所發，故皆屬修。又凡論修者必須兼性。九秖是三者，如三般若秖是了因，如三解脫秖是緣因，如三道等秖是正因。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The two of cultivation each have three" and so on: from the combined, each is opened out. Sets like the three prajñās are the three of the revealing cause; sets like the three bodhis are the three of the conditioning cause; "together they bring forth" the three paths and the like, the three of the correct cause. That, once the three of the nature have been brought forth, all are called the nine of cultivation is because, although they include the three of the nature, all are what is brought forth, and so all belong to cultivation. Moreover, whenever one discusses cultivation one must include the nature. "The nine are only three": for example, the three prajñās are only the revealing cause, the three liberations only the conditioning cause, the three paths and the like only the correct cause.

Chinese Source

Answer: 'not yet manifest' — 'the essence is shown to be still deluded'; it is not that principle does not contain them. This is the doubt [at the identity] in name. Since at [the identity in] contemplative practice they are also not yet manifest, they are shown by [the identity in] resemblance: the body born of father and mother develops [these powers] at [the stage of] resemblance, and the five eyes, five ears, and so on through the five minds can all illuminate everywhere. Since one's own body manifests the ten realms, it shows that others' bodies are likewise; so the person at the stage of resemblance infers that the hundred realms are together in a single mind. On reaching a portion of the truth, one universally manifests form-bodies and can manifest the ten realms, and each in turn gives rise to the three kinds of action of the ten realms; hence 'in the same way.' At the ground of the fruit one fully exhausts the true mark of all dharmas, equal to the hundred realms contained in the nature. So we know that the hundred realms of inherent inclusion are mutually merged and broadly pervasive; the defiled mind limits itself, [but] the muddy essence is originally clear."

Negating and Illuminating: The Contemplation That Reveals

Chinese Source

二、故須下，明能顯妙觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Therefore, from the beginner's mind onward, one must both negate and illuminate…" onward: explaining the wondrous contemplation that is able to reveal [it].

Chinese Source

然今十門皆為觀心而設，故色心門攝別入總，專立識心為所觀故。內外門正示觀法，雖泛論二境正在內心。第三門全性起修辨觀令妙，第四門即因成果顯證非新，故此二門皆論一念。已上四門攝自行法門，同在剎那而為觀體。從此門去純談化他，而化他法門雖即無量，豈出三千？亦攝歸剎那同為觀體，此當其首，故廣示觀門，後既倣此但略點示，不得此意徒釋十門空談一念，故今文先明淨用同在染心，理具情迷顯發由觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, all of these ten gates are set up for the contemplation of mind. So the gate of form and mind gathers the particular into the general, setting up specifically the mind of consciousness as what is contemplated. The gate of inner and outer directly shows the method of contemplation; although it discusses the two objects broadly, [its focus] is precisely on the inner mind. The third gate, [through] the whole nature giving rise to cultivation, sets forth contemplation to make it wondrous; the fourth gate, [through] the cause itself becoming the fruit, reveals that realization is nothing new; so these two gates both discuss the single thought. The four gates above gather in the dharma-gates of one's own practice, which are together in the instant and serve as the essence of contemplation. From this gate on, they speak purely of transforming others; but though the dharma-gates of transforming others are immeasurable, how could they go beyond the three thousand? They too are gathered back into the instant and likewise serve as the essence of contemplation. This [gate] stands at their head, and so it shows the gate of contemplation at length; since the later ones follow this, they only point it out briefly. Without grasping this meaning, one explains the ten gates in vain and talks emptily of the single thought. So the present text first explains that the pure function is together in the defiled mind: contained in principle, deluded in feeling, it is manifested through contemplation.

Chinese Source

遮照者，空中名遮，一相不立，假觀名照，三千宛然。復令三觀俱亡、三諦齊照，乃亡前遮照。照前遮故，故各名雙亡，照同時故云終日，此則同前即空假中無空假中也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Negate and illuminate": the empty and the middle are called negating — not a single mark is set up; the contemplation of the provisional is called illuminating — the three thousand are fully present. Further, it has the three contemplations all vanish and the three truths illuminated together; that is, it makes the preceding negating and illuminating vanish. Because it illuminates the preceding negating, each is called "both vanish"; because the illuminating is simultaneous, it says "all day long." This is the same as "identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle" and "without empty, provisional, or middle" before.

Chinese Source

他見法爾空中，欲例即空即中，而不看上句照故三千常具，彼門但舉依正之境，況不云三千及以百界，尚未結成妙境，何關假觀邪？若此中縱無上句照故三千常具，但云空中，於理亦成。何者？上已具示三千淨用在剎那，故彼秖云依正色心，據何文義云是妙假？思之思之！

Ru-Yi Translation

Someone, seeing "naturally empty and the middle," wishes to compare it with "identical to the empty … identical to the middle," without looking at the preceding phrase, "because one illuminates, the three thousand are always fully present." That gate raises only the object of the dependent and the principal; still less does it mention the three thousand or the hundred realms; it has not yet formed the wondrous object — what does it have to do with the contemplation of the provisional? Even if here there were not the preceding phrase "because one illuminates, the three thousand are always fully present," and it said only "empty and the middle," it would still hold in principle. Why? Because above it has already fully shown that the pure function of the three thousand is in the instant. So that [gate] says only "the dependent and principal, form and mind": on the basis of what passage or meaning would one say that it is the wondrous provisional? Think on it, think on it!

Chinese Source

不動此念者，明觀成相不移，即今剎那之念，而能盡未來際作三千化事，此之剎那即法界故，有何窮盡？第五記云，剎那剎那皆盡過未，施設三千皆妙假力，亡淨穢相須藉空中，故云以空以中。染中淨穢更顯明者，復是空中之力，故云轉染為淨。染淨各具三千，空中了之，三千既亡空中亦泯，方名染淨不二，此則同前因果既泯理性自亡。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Without stirring this thought" explains that when contemplation is accomplished, the marks are not moved: this very thought of the present instant can, to the end of future time, carry out the transforming activity of the three thousand. Because this instant is the dharma-realm, what end could there be? The fifth fascicle of the Commentary says: "Instant after instant fully exhausts past and future." Setting up the three thousand is all the power of the wondrous provisional; making the marks of pure and impure vanish must rely on the empty and the middle; hence "by means of the empty and the middle." That pure and impure within defilement are made still more manifest and clear is again the power of the empty and the middle; hence "defilement is turned into purity." Defilement and purity each contain the three thousand; the empty and the middle understand them; once the three thousand vanish, the empty and the middle also dissolve. Only then is it called the nonduality of defilement and purity. This is the same as "Once cause and fruit dissolve, the nature of principle itself vanishes" before.

Reading Notes for Part 3

Translator's notes

Lemmas. Wherever Zhili quotes Zhanran, the English follows the Ru-Yi translation of The Ten Gates of Nonduality word for word. Where Zhili's gloss and that draft's wording pull in different directions, this is noted below rather than changed in the quotation.

Variant lemmas. Zhili's text quotes a few phrases of the base text differently from the received text of the Ten Gates: 「在性」 for 「存性」 ("In keeping to the nature"); 「一念心因既具三軌」 for 「眾生心因既具三軌」; 「了今無明為法性」 for 「故可了今即無明為法性」. The English lemma follows the base-text translation except where Zhili's wording is quoted in full (the "mind of a single thought" line is translated as Zhili gives it).

"Someone says" (他云) and "another school" (他宗). Zhili does not name his opponents. The "someone" who prefers the "old edition" readings (藉知曰修, 一其迷了, 性成, 三身並常, and the absence of the two 與) is usually taken to be an off-mountain (山外) commentator on the Ten Gates. Tradition points to Yuanqing (源清) and his circle; this cannot be confirmed from the present text. "Another school" (他宗), which teaches that "the one principle accords with conditions" and speaks of "nature-arising" (性起), probably refers to Huayan teaching as transmitted through Zongmi. This is an inference.

"Relying on knowing is called cultivation" (藉知曰修). The rival reading takes 智 "wisdom" to be a scribal merger of 知 "knowing" and 曰 "is called". Zhili answers that the merger could just as well have gone the other way: 日 copied too far from 知 makes 知曰 out of 智. "The error of 魚 for 魯" is a proverbial name for copying errors between similar characters.

"Numinous knowing" (靈知) and Guifeng. Guifeng Zongmi (圭峯宗密, 780–841) made "numinous knowing" the central term for the true mind. The General Preface to the Chan Collection (禪詮都序) is taken to be his Chanyuan zhuquanji duxu (禪源諸詮集都序). "Taking principle as object and cutting off the nine" (緣理斷九) is Zhili's stock description of the distinct teaching: one contemplates the pure principle and eliminates the nine non-buddha realms.

"It is possible" read as a question (可不可也). Zhili glosses 可 in 「可由事不移心則令迷修成了」 as 不可, i.e., the sentence is a rhetorical question: "Could one, by way of events and without moving the mind, turn deluded cultivation into understanding?" The base-text translation renders it as a statement ("It is possible…"). Translators of the base text may want to reconsider it in light of Zhili.

"Over the whole span" (一期). Zhili says 一期 is like 一往 ("at first look," "for the time being") and does not mean "to the very end." The base-text translation's "over the whole span" is kept in the lemma as required, but it fits Zhili's gloss poorly. Something like "for a time" would match Zhili better. In Zhili's own prose 一期 is rendered "for a period." The rival "old edition" read 其 for 期 and took 一其 as "unify."

"Separated" and "combined" (離／合). "Separated" means cultivation and nature each have three (nine in all). "Combined" means cultivation has two (the revealing and conditioning causes) and the nature one (the correct cause). The Profound Meaning of the Golden Light (金光明玄義) lists ten sets of three dharmas (三德, 三寶, 三身, 三智, 三識, 三道, 三因, 三菩提, 三大乘, 三涅槃, among others). Zhili sorts six as "each has three" and four as "two and one." "The path of teaching" (教道) is the distinct teaching's expedient teaching, as opposed to what is realized.

「修性從成」. In the passage "cultivation, accomplished from the nature, is likewise not three yet set up as three," the Chinese reads 修性從成. This may be a transposition of 修從性成, the base-text wording just quoted. The English follows that sense; read literally, it would be "cultivation and nature, following [this], are accomplished…"

"Although in general they can be set up in succession" (雖通傳立). 傳 is taken in the sense of 傳傳 "in relay," i.e., each stage can be called a "fruit" relative to the one before. Alternatively: "although in general they are set up by transmission." "The ordinary ground" (博地) is the stage of ordinary beings.

The four similes. Zhili reads 夢, 空, 幻, 像 in the base text as four similes: dream, emptiness, illusion, and image. 空 is rendered "emptiness" to match the base-text translation. In the cited "ten similes of the Great Vehicle" it is "empty space" (虛空). The quotation "space has only a name and no true reality" is found in the Supporting Practice (T1912), which lists the ten similes. A variant reading 冥 for 名 after 空 is mentioned and rejected. 「為都無所辨」: 辨 is read as 辦 "accomplish."

"The principle is said to be originally present" (稱理本有). The base-text translation takes 稱 as "is said." Zhili's gloss 「稱本而證」 ("realizes in accord with the origin") suggests that he reads 稱 as "accord with": "in accord with principle, originally present." The lemma follows the draft.

Yajñadatta. From the Śūraṅgama Sūtra: Yajñadatta thought he had lost his head when he could not see it in the mirror, but it was never lost.

Identity versus joining. The Supporting Practice passage (T1912, fasc. 1) rejects the Guangya (廣雅) gloss of 即 as 合 "to join." Zhili applies this against "another school": if differentiated dharmas appear only when the one nature "joins" with ignorance and disappear when ignorance is removed, that is joining, not identity. For Zhili, the three thousand remain "fully present" even when they are not according with conditions.

"The first fascicle of the Commentary" and "Miaole." The quotation 「以別教中無性德九故自他俱斷九」 and the passage on 「從無住本立一切法……但即不即異而分教殊」 are both in Zhanran's Fahua wenju ji (T1719), fascicle 1, in its comment on Ājñāta-kauṇḍinya. The same fascicle has 「真如在迷能生九界」. Miaole (妙樂) is Zhanran.

The Vajra Scalpel quotation. Zhili quotes 「變義唯二，即是唯圓」. The Taishō text of the Jin'gangbei (T1932) reads 「變義唯二，即具唯圓」 ("the meaning of transforming belongs only to two [teachings]; identity and inclusion belong only to the perfect"). This may be a variant in Zhili's copy or a copying error in T1928. The English follows T1928.

The Laṅkāvatāra and the great pond. In the Supporting Practice (T1912) the line reads 「如來藏為善不善因」 ("the tathāgatagarbha is the cause of good and not good"). Zhili's citation drops 藏. The simile of the elephant and the pearl is cited there as Da zhidu lun fasc. 41.

The Vimalakīrti Commentary and the Record. The lines on "abiding by itself" (自住) and "abiding dependent on another" (依他住) are found in the Weimojing lüeshu (T1778). "The Record" (記) is presumably Zhanran's sub-commentary on the Vimalakīrti commentary. Zhili's quotations from it are not verified here (source not identified in the Taishō files consulted). The boundaries of those quotations in the English are therefore approximate.

"Ālaya" (梨邪). 梨邪 is a transliteration of ālaya (阿梨耶識). The two positions, that the ālaya gives rise to all dharmas and that the Dharma-nature does, are the two views that the Mohe Zhiguan attributes to the Shelun and Dilun masters respectively, and which Tiantai classifies under the distinct teaching.

"The three bodies are all permanent" (三身並常). A rival "old edition" reading for 三千並常 in the base text. Zhili rejects it because 俱體俱用 ("all essence and all function") then has no natural sense.

"Some people say: defilement is delusion; purity is response." Zhili's own objection is that the fifth gate is about the pure function after the fruit (responding to beings), which is already contained in the ordinary defiled mind. It is not simply a pairing of delusion with response.

The two 與 and the two 之. The base text reads 「法性之與無明…無明之與法性」. The rival reading drops 與 and glosses 之 as 往 "go to," following the Erya. Zhili accepts either reading with or without 與, but not the gloss "go to." He then explains 與 as "lending power."

"True cultivation" and "conditioned cultivation" (真修／緣修). True cultivation is cultivation that accords directly with the nature. Conditioned cultivation is cultivation that still works through aims, with delight in the goal and aversion to what is to be abandoned. "Inner perfuming" (內熏) is the Dharma-nature's influence from within.

"The fourth fascicle of the Commentary" and "the fifth." The passage on clear and muddy waves (「如清濁波濕性不異……染淨既分如位須辯」) and the line 「剎那剎那皆盡過未」 are both in Zhanran's Fahua wenju ji (T1719). In the Taishō text both fall in fascicle 5. Zhili's "fourth" for the first may follow a different division of the work (for example, by the fascicles of the Wenju being commented on); this is not confirmed. The Taishō text reads 辯 where Zhili has 辨 in 「如位須辨」.

「全位為相位、常相亦常」. CBETA punctuates 「全位為相位、常相亦常」. The English follows the reading 「全位為相，位常相亦常」 ("the whole position is the mark; the position is permanent, so the mark too is permanent"), which the following argument requires.

「故光以濕性諭位論等」. 光 is read as "simply." It may be an error for 先 "first," which would fit the "first … next" structure of Zhili's analysis.

"Mark and position" (相／位). These terms come from the Lotus Sūtra line 「是法住法位，世間相常住」. "Position" is the fixed "place" of a dharma in suchness; "mark" is its appearance according with conditions.

The five eyes, five ears, and so on (五眼五耳乃至五意). The stage of resemblance is the purification of the six faculties described in the Lotus Sūtra's chapter on the merits of the Dharma teacher, where "the body born of father and mother" sees and hears throughout the chiliocosm. The exact sense of the list "five eyes, five ears, … five minds" is not certain; it seems to extend the five kinds of eye to each faculty. The English keeps the list literally.

「照前遮故，故各名雙亡」. This clause is difficult. Literally: "because it illuminates the preceding negating, therefore each is called 'both vanish.'" The sense may be that the contemplation makes both the earlier negating and the earlier illuminating vanish, and that negating and illuminating are simultaneous ("all day long"). The text may be corrupt here.

"The same as … before" (同前). 「即空假中無空假中」 refers to the gate of the nonduality of inner and outer: "identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle" and "without empty, provisional, or middle." The rival here wants to read the outer object of that gate ("the form and mind of those dependent and principal [rewards]") as the "wondrous provisional." Zhili denies this.

"The three dharmas" (三法妙義) and "the principle-nature" (理性). In 「他既暗於三法妙義」, 三法 is taken as the three dharmas of mind, buddha, and beings (as in Part 1), not the three tracks; it could also be read as the three tracks. 理性 in Zhili's own prose is "the principle-nature," as in Part 1; in the base-text lemma 「理性自亡」 the base-text translation's "the nature of principle" is kept.

"Feeling" (情). 情 is rendered "feeling" throughout: the deluded, clinging attitude of the mind, as opposed to "principle" (理). Thus 「理具情迷」, "contained in principle, deluded in feeling."
