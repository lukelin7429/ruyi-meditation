Prefaces and the General Introduction

Siming Zhili (四明知禮, 960–1028) wrote this commentary in 1004 to settle a controversy that had divided the Tiantai school of his day. Rival teachers — later called the "off-mountain" (山外) faction — read Zhanran's Ten Gates of Nonduality as teaching that the "single thought" to be contemplated is the pure, true nature itself. Zhili, whose position became the "home-mountain" (山家) orthodoxy, insists instead that the single thought is the ordinary, deluded moment of mind that arises when a sense faculty meets its object, and that this very moment, afflictions and all, inherently includes the three thousand realms. His key ideas — that delusion is "identical" to awakening not by being turned into something else but because evil is itself inherent in the nature (性惡), and that the distinct teaching's principle can "accord with conditions" without being the perfect teaching — are laid out in this first part. It begins with a preface by Zhili's fellow student Ciyun Zunshi (遵式, 964–1032), continues with Zhili's own short preface and his explanation of the title, and then works through Zhanran's opening paragraphs phrase by phrase, quoting each phrase of the base text and explaining it.

Master Li, the teacher who transmits the teaching at Siming, is truly a great figure of our school! Ru-Yi Translation

When he had received full ordination and was studying under Master Tong of Baoyun, at the time he first joined the Dharma assembly, his father dreamed that he was kneeling before the master, and the master took a vase of water and poured it into his mouth — it flowed out like a spring and was taken in like a valley. And so the perfect-and-sudden purport of the great Tiantai teaching he understood on receiving it once, without waiting to hear it again. The master said to him: "There is nothing in my words that you do not penetrate; you are no help to me."

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

When the master had just passed away, Master Li dreamed again that he threaded the master's head onto his left arm and walked on. Ah! Did the first dream not signify his receiving and learning, like Ānanda pouring water from vase to vase without any difference? And the second, his transmitting and upholding, taking up the head of his master's knowledge of all modes and going forth to teach?

Chinese Source

At the beginning of the Chunhua era, Qianfu Monastery in the prefecture invited him to open a lecture seat. Students followed him gladly, like many streams meeting in the sea. Because the halls were cramped and the disciples steadily increased, before long he moved to Baoen Cloister. The Lotus, the Calming and Contemplation, the Golden Light, and other works he lectured on in a continuous round, with not a day of the year left empty.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

He once urged his disciples: "Whether I go out or stay in, whether I am silent or speak, I have never failed to take the purport of teaching and contemplation, the provisional and the real, as my clothing and food, as my staff and armrest. Do not be lax!" How great! As for his wearing the robe of tranquil patience and dwelling in the room of great compassion, leading people on step by step with skill — it is beyond praise!

Chinese Source

《釋籤》十不二門者，今昔講流以為一難文也，或多註釋各陳異端，孰不自謂握靈蛇之珠、揮彌天之筆，豈思夫一家教觀，殊不知其啟發之所。公覽之再歎，豈但釋文未允，奈何委亂大綱。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Ten Gates of Nonduality from the Shiqian has been regarded by lecturers, past and present, as a difficult text. Many have commented on it, each setting out a divergent view; and which of them does not consider himself to be holding the spirit-snake's pearl and wielding a brush that fills the heavens? Yet they never considered the teaching and contemplation of our school, and quite failed to know where it was meant to lead. Master Li read them and sighed again and again: not only were their explanations of the text inaccurate — how could they throw the main outline into disorder?

Chinese Source

山隤角崩良用悲痛，將欲正舉捨我而誰！遂而正析斯文旁援顯據，綽有餘刃兼整大途，教門權實今時同昧者於茲判矣，別理隨緣其類也。觀道所託連代共迷者於茲見矣，指要所以其立也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The mountain crumbling and the horn breaking truly caused him grief: "If it is to be set right, who but I?" So he analyzed this text correctly, drew on clear evidence from elsewhere, with room to spare for the blade, and at the same time put the great road in order. The provisional and the real of the teachings, which people today are equally in the dark about, are here decided — the distinct teaching's principle according with conditions is one example. What the path of contemplation relies on, which generation after generation has been confused about, is here made visible — that is why Pointing Out the Essentials was written.

Chinese Source

至若《法華》、《止觀》綱格之文，隱括錯綜略無不在，後之學者足以視近見遠，染指知味。《易》不云乎：「通天下之志，定天下之業，斷天下之疑。」實此一二萬言得矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the passages that set the framework of the Lotus and the Calming and Contemplation, it straightens and interweaves them, leaving almost nothing out; later students can look at what is near and see what is far, and taste the whole by dipping a finger. Does the Book of Changes not say: "It penetrates the aims of all under heaven, settles the undertakings of all under heaven, and resolves the doubts of all under heaven"? This is truly achieved in these twenty thousand words or so.

Chinese Source

式忝同學也，觀者無謂吾之亦有黨乎？取長其理、無取長其情，文理明白，誰能隱乎云也。

Ru-Yi Translation

I, Shi, have the honor of being his fellow student. Let readers not say that I, too, am taking sides! Take what is excellent in its reasoning, not what flatters its sentiment. When text and reasoning are clear, who could conceal them? So I say.

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by the monk Zhili of Siming, of the Song dynasty.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The Ten Gates of Nonduality originally comes from the Shiqian; why should it need a commentary? But among the lecturers of this school, some have presented it and some have annotated it, writing this and that, yet event and principle are still not clear, and understanding and practice have nothing to rely on. Jingxi's wondrous understanding has instead been hidden from our time, and the perfect school of Tiantai fails to benefit beings. So, in the course of lecturing, I have explained it in response to those [commentaries], and named it Pointing Out the Essentials.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

For it points to the mind of the slightest moment as the essential of event and principle, understanding and practice. I offer it merely for students to review; how would I dare expect the learned to study it closely? Prefaced on the ninth day of the first month of the first year of Jingde of the great Song, the year jiachen.

Explaining the Title

Chinese Source

鈔曰：

Ru-Yi Translation

The commentary says:

Chinese Source

此文題目多本不同，或云法華本迹十妙不二門，或無本迹二字，有唯云玄文十不二門，此或以所通之義、所釋之文而冠於首，蓋不忘其本也，而盡是別錄者私安，取捨由情無勞苦諍，

Ru-Yi Translation

The title of this text differs in many copies. Some say The Gates of Nonduality of the Ten Wondrous Aspects of Origin and Trace of the Lotus; some lack the two words "origin and trace"; some say only The Ten Gates of Nonduality of the Profound Text. These perhaps put at the head the meaning that is passed through or the text that is explained, so as not to forget their source; but all of them were privately supplied by those who copied it out separately. One may take or leave them as one likes; there is no need to argue hard.

Chinese Source

若十不二門四字，乃作者自立，故文云，為實施權則不二而二，開權顯實則二而不二，法既教部咸開成妙，故此十門不二為目，須據此文釋其題旨，

Ru-Yi Translation

The four words "ten gates of nonduality," however, were set up by the author himself. So the text says: "When the provisional is set up for the sake of the real, they are not two, yet two; when the provisional is opened to reveal the real, they are two, yet not two. Since the dharmas, the teachings, and the scriptural corpus have all been opened and made wondrous, these ten gates take 'not two' as their name." One must explain the purport of the title on the basis of this passage.

Chinese Source

豈非四時三教所談，色心乃至受潤無不隔異故皆名二？今經開會實理既彰，十異皆融互攝無外咸名不二，即以不二當體為門。然亦可云十不二為能通，十妙為所通。

Ru-Yi Translation

Is it not that what is taught in the four periods and the three teachings — form and mind through receiving and moistening — is in every case separated and different, and so all are called "two"? In the present sūtra, opening and merging, the real principle has been made manifest; the ten differences all fuse and contain one another, with nothing outside, and all are called "not two." Nonduality itself, just as it is, is the gate. But one may also say that the ten nondualities are what give passage, and the ten wondrous aspects are what is passed through.

Chinese Source

問：「妙即不二、不二即妙，俱名俱體，何分能通所通？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "The wondrous is nonduality, and nonduality is the wondrous; both are name and both are essence. Why divide them into what gives passage and what is passed through?"

Chinese Source

答：「今不以麁妙分能所，亦不以名體分之，蓋以十妙法相該博，學者難入，此文撮要徑顯彼意，乃以略顯廣、以易通難，義立能通所通，數至十者，蓋從十妙而立，雖立門對妙互有多少，而不虧本數也。此且總明，待至釋文更為點示。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Here they are not divided into agent and object by coarse and wondrous, nor by name and essence. It is because the dharma-marks of the ten wondrous aspects are vast and comprehensive and hard for students to enter, and this text gathers the essentials and shows their meaning directly — using the brief to reveal the extensive, the easy to give passage to the difficult — that, in meaning, 'what gives passage' and 'what is passed through' are set up. That the number comes to ten is because it is set up from the ten wondrous aspects; although, in matching gates to wondrous aspects, the numbers on each side vary, the original number is not lost. This is only a general explanation; I will point it out further when we come to explain the text."

Chinese Source

若欲標述作人者，即是荊谿尊者。既是後人錄出，不可正斥其諱。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one wishes to name the author, it is the Venerable Jingxi. Since it was copied out by a later person, one should not write his personal name directly.

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the meaning of the ten wondrous aspects. "Now" (然) means "it is so": it is a word that takes up what precedes, and also a word of faith and understanding; one who did not believe would say "it is not so."

Chinese Source

此迹門等者，指上玄文所談十妙，境智行位因也，三法果也，感應兼自他，神通說法，是能化者，作屬自也，眷屬利益是所化者，事屬他也。故因等四收十妙盡，一代教門所明法相豈過於此！今於十義，皆用待絕二妙而融會之令無壅礙，故云融通入妙。

Ru-Yi Translation

"This trace gate" and the rest points to the ten wondrous aspects discussed above in the Profound Meaning. Object, wisdom, practice, and stage are the cause; the three tracks are the fruit; sympathetic response includes both self and other — spiritual powers and teaching the Dharma belong to the one who transforms, whose work belongs to self, while the retinue and merit and benefit belong to those who are transformed, whose affairs belong to other. So the four — cause and the rest — gather in all ten wondrous aspects; how could the dharma-marks explained in the teachings of a lifetime go beyond this? Now, for these ten meanings, the two readings of the wondrous, the relative and the absolute, are all used to merge them so that there is no obstruction; hence "merge and pass into the wondrous."

Chinese Source

二、凡諸下，眾釋意。如初理境具有七科，一一皆用四教揀之，意開藏等俱圓，復以五味判之，欲開兼等皆妙，即使醍醐之外更無餘味，如此釋之方稱妙法。智行乃至利益各明種種法相，無不皆用四教五味，判後開之皆成極味。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "All of its explanations…" onward: the meaning of the many explanations. For example, the first, the object of principle, has seven categories, and each is sifted by the Four Teachings, intending to open the Tripiṭaka teaching and the others so that all are perfect; and again they are judged by the Five Flavors, wishing to open "combined" and the rest so that all are wondrous, with no other flavor outside the ghee. Only explaining in this way accords with "the wondrous Dharma." Wisdom, practice, and the rest through merit and benefit each explain various dharma-marks, and in every case the Four Teachings and the Five Flavors are used; after being judged, they are opened, and all become the supreme flavor.

Chinese Source

二、觀心下，敘觀略。樞即門之要也，機謂機關，有可發之義。蓋一切教行皆以觀心為要，皆自觀心而發，觀心空故一切法空，即所修諸行、所起諸教皆歸空也，假中亦然，豈不以觀心為樞機邪！

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "The contemplation of mind…" onward: recounting that the contemplation is brief. The "pivot" (樞) is the key part of a door; the "trigger" (機) means a mechanism, which has the sense of something that can be released. For all teaching and practice take the contemplation of mind as their key, and all are released from the contemplation of mind. Because the contemplated mind is empty, all dharmas are empty; that is, the practices one cultivates and the teachings one gives rise to all return to emptiness — and so too with the provisional and the middle. How could the contemplation of mind not be the pivot?

Chinese Source

然今玄文未暇廣明，寄諸文末略點示爾。又雖據義一一合有，為避繁文故有存沒。如十二因緣，境後則有、四諦則略，蓋有止觀。對此明乎教觀旁正，如常所說，

Ru-Yi Translation

But here the Profound Meaning has no leisure to explain it at length, and only lodges it at the end of the various passages, pointing it out briefly. Again, although by its meaning it should be present in every case, it is sometimes present and sometimes absent to avoid a cumbersome text. For example, [the contemplation of mind] is present after the object of the twelve links but abbreviated for the Four Noble Truths, since there is the Calming and Contemplation. In relation to this, it makes clear which of teaching and contemplation is secondary and which primary, as is commonly said.

Chinese Source

託事則借彼事義立境立觀，如王舍、耆山等，附法則攝諸法相入心成觀，如四諦、五行等，既非專行，故十乘不委。此即《義例》約行等三種觀相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Relying on events" means borrowing the meaning of those events to set up an object and set up a contemplation, as with Rājagṛha, Vulture Peak, and the like. "Attaching to dharmas" means gathering the dharma-marks into the mind to make a contemplation, as with the Four Noble Truths, the five practices, and the like. Since this is not a dedicated practice, the ten modes are not given in detail. These are the three kinds of contemplation — "in terms of practice" and the others — in the Exposition of the Principles.

Chinese Source

Second, from "What it clarifies…" onward: establishing the intent; this has two:

Chinese Source

初、重示大部意。更舉十妙方出其意，能化即應，并神通說法也，所化即感，及眷屬利益也，此十乃是一代教中能詮名字，大部明此意，在開顯諸名咸妙故也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, showing again the intent of the great work. It raises the ten wondrous aspects once more and only then brings out their intent. "The one who transforms" is the response, together with spiritual powers and teaching the Dharma; "those who are transformed" are the appeal, together with the retinue and merit and benefit. These ten are the expressing names within the teachings of a lifetime; the great work explains them, its intent being to open and reveal that all these names are wondrous.

Ru-Yi Translation

The ten must be set forth because it wishes to gather in beginning and end, self and other, completely. "Beginning" means the object and the rest, the cause of one's own practice; "end" means the three tracks, the fruit of one's own practice; self and other are as above. If one sets forth these ten as each wondrous, one fully understands the intent of the Tathāgata's appearance in the world.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Therefore one cannot fail…" onward: explaining directly the intent of the present composition; two:

First, to accomplish wondrous understanding. To know that these ten are all wondrous, one must understand the broad outline of opening and revealing: namely, that the three thousand worlds are all empty, provisional, and the middle. Chinese Source

境即此故事理俱融，智發此故無緣，行起此故無作，位歷此故相攝，三法究盡此故果滿，生具此故一念能感，佛得此故無謀而應，神通用此故化化無窮，說法據此故施開自在，眷屬全此故天性相關，利益稱此故無一不成佛。今此十門正示於此，若能知者名發妙解。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because the object is this, event and principle are both merged; because wisdom arises from this, it is without object; because practice arises from this, it is without fabrication; because the stages pass through this, they contain one another; because the three tracks fully exhaust this, the fruit is complete; because beings contain this, a single thought can make an appeal; because the Buddha has attained this, he responds without deliberation; because spiritual powers use this, transformations transform without end; because teaching the Dharma rests on this, setting up and opening are free; because the retinue is wholly this, they are bound together by heavenly nature; because merit and benefit accord with this, there is not one who does not attain buddhahood. These ten gates show exactly this; one who can know it is said to give rise to wondrous understanding.

Chinese Source

二、故撮下，為成妙行。言觀法者十乘也，應知止觀十乘是別論行相，而一一乘不離三千，即空假中，故云觀法大體。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Therefore I gather up…" onward: to accomplish wondrous practice. "The method of contemplation" means the ten modes. One should know that the ten modes of the Calming and Contemplation discuss the marks of practice separately, yet each mode is inseparable from the three thousand, which are identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle; hence "the overall framework of the method of contemplation."

Chinese Source

《義例》云：「散引諸文該乎一代，文體正意唯歸二經，一依《法華》本迹顯實，二依《涅槃》扶律顯常，以此二經同醍醐故。」是知用此十妙絕待之義為觀體者，方譬日光不與暗共。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Exposition of the Principles says: "Its scattered citations cover a whole lifetime of teaching, but the true intent of the body of the text returns to two sūtras alone: first, relying on the Lotus, it reveals the real through origin and trace; second, relying on the Nirvāṇa, it supports the Vinaya and reveals permanence — because these two sūtras are alike the ghee." From this we know that one who takes the absolute meaning of these ten wondrous aspects as the essence of contemplation is like the sunlight, which does not coexist with darkness.

Chinese Source

又此三千法門遍於諸法，若色若心、依之與正、眾生諸佛、剎剎塵塵無不具足，故《華嚴》云：「如心佛亦爾，如佛眾生然，心佛及眾生，是三無差別。」故今家釋經題法字，約此三法各具三千互具互融方名妙法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Zunshi's preface. Ciyun Zunshi (慈雲遵式, 964–1032) studied with Zhili under Baoyun Yitong (寶雲義通, 927–988), the "Master Tong of Baoyun" of the preface. "Siming" is present-day Ningbo, where Zhili taught at Yanqing Monastery (formerly Baoen Cloister). The Chunhua era began in 990.

Ānanda pouring water from vase to vase. A stock image for transmitting the teaching without loss: Ānanda received all the Buddha's words as water is poured from one vase into another.

"The spirit-snake's pearl" and "a brush that fills the heavens." Classical images for rare talent; "a brush that fills the heavens" alludes to Dao'an's self-description, 彌天釋道安.

"The mountain crumbling and the horn breaking" (山隤角崩). Expressions of grief at the decline of a tradition.

"The distinct teaching's principle according with conditions" (別理隨緣). One of the controversies Zhili settled: whether only the perfect teaching's principle can "accord with conditions" (隨緣), or whether the distinct teaching's can as well. Zhili argued that it can, and that what distinguishes the perfect teaching is inherent inclusion, not "according with conditions." Zunshi names it as one example of the questions this commentary decides.

The opponents. Zhili does not name them. Tradition identifies the "off-mountain" (山外) teachers he refutes here as Fenzhou Yuanqing (源清) and his circle, whose commentaries on this text (now partly lost) read the "single thought" as the pure true mind. "Two teachers" are those who say the single thought is the true nature, and those who refuse to make the aggregates the object of contemplation.

Works cited. The Profound Meaning (玄文) is Zhiyi's Fahua Xuanyi; Calming and Contemplation (止觀) is the Mohe Zhiguan; Supporting Practice (輔行) is Zhanran's Zhiguan fuxing chuanhong jue; Exposition of the Principles (義例) is Zhanran's Zhiguan yili; The Main Idea of Calming and Contemplation (止觀大意) is a short work by Zhanran; Four Foundations of Mindfulness (四念處) is a work attributed to Zhiyi; "the seventh fascicle of the Commentary" (第七記) is taken to be Zhanran's Fahua wenju ji, fascicle 7.

Three kinds of contemplation (託事, 附法, 約行). Contemplation "relying on events" (e.g., reading Rājagṛha or Vulture Peak as figures for the mind), "attaching to dharmas" (e.g., taking the Four Noble Truths into the mind), and "in terms of practice" (dedicated practice such as the ten modes), as set out in the Exposition of the Principles. "The five practices" (五行) here are the five practices of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (noble, pure, heavenly, infant, and illness practices).

The ten wondrous aspects divided as cause and fruit, self and other. Zhili sorts them: object, wisdom, practice, and stage are the cause; the three tracks are the fruit; spiritual powers and teaching belong to the one who transforms ("self"); retinue and benefit to those transformed ("other"); sympathetic response spans both.

Punctuation in the passage on origin and trace (離因故迹果惟明……合果故本中不云). CBETA punctuates 「離因故迹；果惟明……合果故本。中不云……」, but the parallel structure requires 「離因故；迹果惟明……合果故；本中不云……」 ("the fruit of the trace … in the origin …"). The Chinese is left as CBETA has it; the English follows the parallel reading.

"Twenty levels of the wondrous" (二十重妙). The ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate and the ten of the origin gate.

"The mind of the slightest moment" (介爾之心). Zhili's phrase for the ordinary moment of mind, however small; it is the "single thought" that is to be contemplated.

Setting aside the elements, sense-bases, and four aggregates. In the Mohe Zhiguan, the object of contemplation is narrowed from the three categories (aggregates, sense-bases, elements) to the five aggregates, then to the aggregate of consciousness, that is, the present moment of mind. Zhili calls this "leaving the ten-foot for the foot and the foot for the inch," like a physician finding the exact point for moxibustion. "The nine objects below" are the remaining nine of the ten objects of contemplation (afflictions, illness, karma, Māra, dhyāna, views, conceit, the two vehicles, and bodhisattvas).

Constant sitting and settling the mind. "The dharma-realm facing the dharma-realm" is from the Mohe Zhiguan's account of constant-sitting samādhi; "have faith only in the Dharma-nature" is from its section on skillfully settling the mind (善巧安心), one of the ten modes.

Two layers of agent and object. Zhili's central methodological point: the inconceivable object and the contemplation are object and agent in relation to each other (hammer and anvil), but together they are the "agent" in relation to what is to be broken — the ordinary aggregates and afflictions (the raw material).

"Afflictions are identical to bodhi" and the meaning of 即. Zhili rejects two readings of "identity": two things joined together, and one thing turning over into its opposite like the back and front of a hand. For the perfect teaching, a thing "just as it is, wholly" (當體全是) is the other. This is grounded in "inherent evil" (性惡): because evil is already included in the nature, afflictions need not be removed or transformed to be bodhi.

Bodhidharma's skin, flesh, and marrow. The well-known story of Bodhidharma's disciples: Zongchi, Daoyu, and Huike (and in other versions also Daofu). Zhili attributes the wording he is given to Guifeng Zongmi (圭峯宗密, 780–841), and cites what he calls the Patriarchs' Hall's account, in which Huike simply bows three times and stands in his place. "Patriarchs' Hall" may refer to a Chan record such as the Zutang ji; the reference is kept as written.

"The original edition reads" (元本云). Notes inside parentheses that preserve wording from an earlier edition of Zhili's own text, later revised. They are translated in place and marked as such.

"Penetrating what is not the path" and "the realm of Māra is identical to the Buddha." From the Vimalakīrti Sūtra ("the bodhisattva practices what is not the path and so penetrates the Buddha's path") and the Mohe Zhiguan.

"Because events differ, there are six; because principle is one, there is identity" (事異故六、理一故即). The standard summary of the six identities: six stages, because in practice they differ; "identity," because in principle they are one.

"Tearing apart the net" and "returning to the great abode." The fourth and fifth of the Mohe Zhiguan's five brief points; the latter corresponds to the never-delivered tenth section, "where it ultimately leads." Zhili argues that these passages, though they concern teaching others, are really about the beginner's own contemplation.

The three grasses and two trees. From the Lotus Sūtra's parable of the medicinal herbs: small, middle, and large herbs, and small and large trees, all watered by one rain.
