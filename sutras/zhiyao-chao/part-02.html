The Gates of Form and Mind and of Inner and Outer

This part covers the first two of the ten gates and closes the first fascicle. It is the heart of the controversy. Zhanran had written that "in general, [the objects] lie in a single thought." The opposing commentators read that single thought as the true nature — "pure, numinous knowing" — and some of them emended the text to fit. Zhili answers at length: the single thought is the ordinary moment of mind that belongs to "events," and it is precisely because such a deluded moment already includes the three thousand that the perfect teaching can be practiced by a beginner at all. Along the way he distinguishes "according with conditions" in the distinct and perfect teachings, explains why "transformation" (變) belongs only to the two Mahāyāna teachings while "creating" (造) covers all four, argues over two disputed readings in the manuscripts of Zhanran's text, and distinguishes the "contemplation of mind-only" from the "contemplation of the true mark." In the gate of inner and outer, he defends the presence of the provisional in "identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle," insisting that the contemplation must be threefold, and shows how inner and outer objects each fully contain the other.

1. The Gate of the Nonduality of Form and Mind

Chinese Source

初、色心不二門三：

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the gate of the nonduality of form and mind; three:

Chinese Source

初、標一切諸法無非妙境。本文七科亦且從要，七科尚廣妙旨難彰，今以色心二法收盡，故大論云：「一切世間中，唯有名與色，若欲如實說，但當觀名色。」此二不二諸法皆妙，故今攝別入總，特指心法明乎不二，以此為門則解行易入也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, stating that all dharmas are none other than the wondrous object. The seven categories of the base text [the Profound Meaning] already follow what is essential; but the seven categories are still broad, and the wondrous purport is hard to display. Here form and mind, two dharmas, gather them all in. So the Great Treatise says: "In all the world there is only name and form; if one wishes to speak truly, one should simply contemplate name and form." When these two are not two, all dharmas are wondrous. So here the particular is gathered into the general, and the dharma of mind is singled out to explain nonduality; taking this as the gate, understanding and practice are easy to enter.

Chinese Source

二、且十下，釋中又二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Take the objects from the ten suchnesses…" onward: the explanation, again in two:

Chinese Source

初、約諸境明總別二：

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining the general and the particular in terms of the objects; two:

Chinese Source

初、雙標。總在一念者，若論諸法互攝，隨舉一法皆得為總，即三無差別也。今為易成觀故，故指一念心法為總。然此總別不可分對理事，應知理具三千、事用三千各有總別，此兩相即方稱妙境。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, stating both. As for "in general, they lie in a single thought": if one speaks of dharmas mutually containing one another, any dharma one takes up can serve as the general — that is, "the three without distinction." Here, to make contemplation easy to accomplish, the dharma of mind, a single thought, is pointed to as the general. But this general and particular cannot be matched separately to principle and event. One should know that the three thousand inherent in principle and the three thousand of the function of events each have their general and particular; only when these two are identical to each other is it called the wondrous object.

Chinese Source

二、何者下，雙示二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "How so?" onward: showing both; two:

Chinese Source

初、別。十如中，相可別故屬色；性據內故屬心；《觀音玄義》指心為體，而諸文中雙取色心；力、作單不能運；緣或指愛或指具度，既存兩說義必雙兼，若云業為因者，則似兼色，今從習邊故因果皆心；五陰皆報則須兼心，今從受身約色義彊；

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the particular. Among the ten suchnesses: mark can be distinguished, so it belongs to form; nature is based on the inner, so it belongs to mind. The Profound Meaning of Guanyin points to mind as the substance, but the various texts take both form and mind. Power and activity cannot operate alone. Condition sometimes refers to craving and sometimes to the full set of means; since two explanations are kept, its meaning must include both. If karma is said to be the cause, it would seem to include form; here it follows the side of habit, so cause and effect are both mind. The five aggregates are all retribution, so it should include mind; here, in terms of the body one receives, the sense of form is stronger.

Chinese Source

本末究竟文雖不對，既論三等，同後三諦，因緣中現未七支皆須雙具，識、名雖獨必含中陰故亦兩兼，行、有是業不可遍屬，無明、愛、取唯心可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The ultimate consistency of root and branch," though not matched in the text, since it discusses the three as equal, is the same as the three truths that follow. Among the [twelve] links, the seven links of the present and future must all contain both; consciousness and name, though single, must include the intermediate aggregates, and so also include both; formations and existence are karma and cannot be assigned to one side only; that ignorance, craving, and grasping are mind only can be known.

Chinese Source

諸諦中，苦同七支、集既兼業、道亦含戒，皆具色心，俗論諸法兼二可知。滅及真中一實無諦，體唯是理無相可表，並心證故故不兼色。然上所對不可永殊，欲成別義故且從彊。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the truths: suffering is the same as the seven links; origination includes karma; the path also includes the precepts — all contain form and mind. That the conventional, which discusses all dharmas, includes both can be known. Cessation, and the real, the middle, the one real truth, and no truth — their substance is only principle, with no mark by which to show them, and all are realized by mind, so they do not include form. But the matching above is not permanently fixed; it follows the stronger side only to establish the meaning of the particular.

Chinese Source

二、既知下，次總。前約諸法不失自體為別，今明諸法同趣剎那為總，終日不失終日同趣，性具諸法總別相收，緣起諸法總別亦爾，非謂約事論別、以理為總。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Having understood the particular…" onward: next, the general. Before, dharmas not losing their own substance was the particular; now, dharmas all converging on the instant is the general. All day they do not lose [themselves], all day they converge. The dharmas that are inherently included contain one another as general and particular; the dharmas of conditioned arising are likewise general and particular. It is not that the particular is discussed in terms of event and principle is taken as the general.

Chinese Source

又復應知，若事若理，皆以事中一念為總，以眾生在事未悟理故，以依陰心顯妙理故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, one should know that, for both event and principle, the single thought within events is the general — because beings are in events and have not yet awakened to principle, and because the wondrous principle is revealed by relying on the mind of the aggregates.

Chinese Source

問：「他云，一念即一性也，一念靈知性體常寂，又云，性即一念，謂心性靈寂，性即法身，靈即般若，寂即解脫。又云，一念真知妙體。又云，並我一念清淨靈知。據此等文，乃直指文中一念，名真淨靈知，是約理解。今云屬事，是陰入法。與他所指，賒切如何？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "Others say: 'The single thought is the one nature; the single thought is numinous knowing, its nature and essence always still.' They also say: 'The nature is the single thought; that is, the nature of mind is numinous and still — the nature is the Dharma-body, the numinous is prajñā, the still is liberation.' They also say: 'The single thought is the wondrous essence of true knowing.' And: 'All of it is my single thought of pure, numinous knowing.' According to such passages, they point directly to the single thought in the text and call it true, pure, numinous knowing — understanding it in terms of principle. Now you say it belongs to events, that it is a dharma of the aggregates and sense-bases. Compared with what they point to, how far off or how close is it?"

Chinese Source

答：「此師秖因將此一念約理釋之，致與一家文義相違。且違文者，一、違玄文。彼判心法定在因、佛法定在果，眾生法一往通因果，二往則局因。他執心法是真性，故乃自立云，心非因果。又礙定在因句，復自立云，約能造諸法故判為因，佛定在果者，乃由研修覺了究盡為果。今問：既將因果分判法相，何得因果却不相對？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Simply because this teacher explains the single thought in terms of principle, he ends up contradicting the texts and meaning of our school. As for contradicting texts: first, he contradicts the Profound Meaning. It judges that the dharma of mind is fixed in the cause, the dharma of the Buddha is fixed in the fruit, and the dharma of beings, at first look, extends to cause and fruit, at second look is confined to the cause. He holds that the dharma of mind is the true nature, and so sets up his own claim: 'Mind is neither cause nor fruit.' Then, obstructed by the phrase 'fixed in the cause,' he sets up another claim: 'It is judged to be the cause because it can create all dharmas; the Buddha is fixed in the fruit because, through study and cultivation, awakening is fully exhausted as the fruit.' Now I ask: since cause and fruit are used to divide the dharma-marks, how can cause and fruit fail to correspond?

Chinese Source

果若從覺，因須指迷，何得自立理能造事而為因邪？既不相對，何名分判？又違《華嚴》心造之義，彼經如來林菩薩說偈云：『心如工畫師，造種種五陰，一切世間中，無法而不造。如心佛亦爾，如佛眾生然，心佛及眾生，是三無差別。』

Ru-Yi Translation

If the fruit is in terms of awakening, the cause must point to delusion. How can he set up his own claim that principle, being able to create events, is the cause? If they do not correspond, how can it be called a division? Again, he contradicts the Avataṃsaka's meaning of 'the mind creates.' In that sūtra, the bodhisattva Forest of the Tathāgata speaks in verse: 'The mind is like a skilled painter, creating the various five aggregates; in all the world there is no dharma it does not create. As the mind is, so is the Buddha; as the Buddha is, so are beings; mind, buddha, and beings — these three are without distinction.'

Chinese Source

《輔行》釋云，心造有二種，一者約理，造即是具，二者約事，即三世變。造等心法既有二造，經以心例於佛，復以佛例於生，故云『如心佛亦爾，如佛眾生然』，是則三法各具二造方無差別，故荊谿云，不解今文如何銷偈『心造一切三無差別』？何忽獨云心造諸法得名因邪？

Ru-Yi Translation

The Supporting Practice explains: the mind's creating is of two kinds — first, in terms of principle, creating is containing; second, in terms of events, it is the transformation of the three times. Since the dharma of mind, which creates, has these two kinds of creating, the sūtra compares the Buddha to the mind, and again compares beings to the Buddha; hence 'as the mind is, so is the Buddha; as the Buddha is, so are beings.' Thus only when the three dharmas each have both kinds of creating are they without distinction. So Jingxi says: if one does not understand the present text, how can one make sense of the verse's 'the mind creates everything; the three are without distinction'? Why suddenly say that the mind alone creates all dharmas and so is called the cause?

Chinese Source

據他所釋，心法是理，唯論能具能造，生佛是事，唯有所具所造，則心造之義尚虧，無差之文永失矣。又若約能造釋因，則三法皆定在因，以皆有二造故。此文應今家立義綱格，若迷此者，一家教旨皆翻倒也，焉將此解定教文之欠剩邪！

Ru-Yi Translation

According to his explanation, the dharma of mind is principle and only contains and creates, while beings and buddhas are events and are only contained and created. Then even the meaning of 'the mind creates' is lacking, and the text 'without distinction' is lost forever. Again, if the cause is explained in terms of being able to create, the three dharmas would all be fixed in the cause, since all have both kinds of creating. This passage corresponds to the framework of our school's doctrine; one who is confused about it turns our school's teaching upside down. How can such an understanding be used to decide what is missing or superfluous in the text of the teaching?

Chinese Source

「二、違《大意》及《金剛錍》。他自引云，隨緣不變名性，不變隨緣名心。引畢乃云，今言心即真如不變性也。今恐他不許荊谿立義。何者？既云不變隨緣名心，顯是即理之事，那得直作理釋？

Ru-Yi Translation

"Second, he contradicts The Main Idea and The Vajra Scalpel. He himself cites: 'According with conditions while unchanging is called nature; unchanging while according with conditions is called mind.' Having cited it, he then says: 'The mind spoken of here is the unchanging nature of true suchness.' I fear he does not accept the doctrine Jingxi established. Why? Since it says 'unchanging while according with conditions is called mind,' it is clearly an event identical to principle. How can he explain it directly as principle?

Chinese Source

若云，雖隨緣邊屬事，事即理故，故指心為不變性者。佛法生法豈不即邪？若皆即理，何獨指心名不變性？故《金錍》云：『真如是萬法，由隨緣故，萬法是真如，由不變故。』故知若約萬法即理，則生佛、依正俱理，皆不變故，何獨心是理邪？

Ru-Yi Translation

If one says, 'Although on the side of according with conditions it belongs to events, events are identical to principle, and so mind is pointed to as the unchanging nature' — are the dharma of the Buddha and the dharma of beings not identical too? If all are identical to principle, why point only to mind as the unchanging nature? So The Vajra Scalpel says: 'True suchness is the myriad dharmas, because it accords with conditions; the myriad dharmas are true suchness, because it is unchanging.' So we know that if one speaks of the myriad dharmas as identical to principle, then beings and buddhas, the dependent and the principal, are all principle, since all are unchanging. Why is mind alone principle?

Chinese Source

若據眾生在事，則內外色心俱事，皆隨緣故，何獨心非事邪？他云，生佛是因果，法心非因果。驗他直指心法名理，非指事即理，生佛二事會歸心故方云即理，亦非當處即具三千。是知他師雖引唯色之言，亦秖曲成唯真心爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one goes by beings being in events, then inner and outer form and mind are all events, since all accord with conditions. Why is mind alone not an event? He says: 'Beings and buddhas are cause and fruit; the dharma of mind is neither cause nor fruit.' This proves that he points directly to the dharma of mind as principle, not that the event is identical to principle; it is only because the two events of beings and buddhas return to mind that he speaks of identity with principle — and it is not that each, right where it is, contains the three thousand. So we know that although that teacher cites the words 'form only,' he only twists them to make 'true mind only.'

Chinese Source

況復觀心自具二種，即唯識觀及實相觀，因何纔見言心便云是理？又實相觀雖觀理具，非清淨理，乃即事之理也，以依陰等顯故。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Moreover, contemplating the mind itself has two kinds: the contemplation of mind-only and the contemplation of the true mark. Why, as soon as he sees the word 'mind,' does he say it is principle? Again, although the contemplation of the true mark contemplates what is inherent in principle, it is not a pure principle; it is the principle identical to events, because it is revealed by relying on the aggregates and the rest."

Chinese Source

問：「若爾，二觀皆依事，如何分邪？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "If so, both contemplations rely on events. How are they distinguished?"

Chinese Source

答：「實相觀者，即於識心體其本寂，三千宛然即空假中。唯識觀者，照於起心變造十界即空假中。故《義例》云，夫觀心法有理有事，從理則唯達法性更無餘途，從事則專照起心四性叵得，亦名本末相映事理不二。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "The contemplation of the true mark is, right within the mind of consciousness, to embody its original stillness, the three thousand fully present and identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle. The contemplation of mind-only is to illuminate the arising mind transforming and creating the ten realms, identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle. So the Exposition of the Principles says: the method of contemplating the mind has principle and has event; from principle, one only penetrates the Dharma-nature, with no other route; from event, one solely illuminates the arising mind, whose four natures cannot be found — this is also called root and branch reflecting each other, event and principle not two.

Chinese Source

又應知，觀於內心二觀既爾，觀於外境二觀亦然，此皆《止觀》及《輔行》文意，非從臆說。他云，真心具三千法，乃指真如名不思議境，非指陰入也。《金錍》云『旁遮偏指清淨真如』，那得持偏指邪？

Ru-Yi Translation

One should also know: as the two contemplations are with respect to the inner mind, so they are with respect to outer objects. All of this is the meaning of the texts of the Calming and Contemplation and the Supporting Practice, not my own speculation. He says the true mind contains the three thousand dharmas, pointing to true suchness as the inconceivable object, not to the aggregates and sense-bases. The Vajra Scalpel says 'it incidentally blocks one-sidedly pointing to pure true suchness' — how can one hold to one-sided pointing?

Chinese Source

又云，夫唯心之心豈唯真如心邪？須知煩惱心遍。第一記云，專緣理性而破九界，是別教義。那得句句唯於真心？又此標一念乃作一性真如釋之，後文多就剎那明具三千，亦作真如釋邪？」

Ru-Yi Translation

It also says: 'The mind of mind-only — is it only the mind of true suchness? One must know that the mind of affliction pervades.' The first fascicle of the Commentary says: to take only the principle-nature as one's object and break the nine realms is the meaning of the distinct teaching. How can he speak in every sentence only of the true mind? Again, here, where the single thought is stated, he explains it as the one nature, true suchness; later passages mostly explain containing the three thousand in terms of the instant — will he explain those as true suchness too?"

Chinese Source

問：「《永嘉集》既用今家觀法，彼奢摩他云，一念即靈知自性，他立正合於彼，何謂不然？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "Since the Yongjia Collection uses our school's method of contemplation, and its section on śamatha says 'the single thought is the self-nature of numinous knowing,' what they set up agrees exactly with it. Why say it is not so?"

Chinese Source

答：「彼文先於根塵體其本寂，作功不已，知滅對遺，靈知一念方得現前。故知彼之一念，全由妙止所顯。不爾，何故五念息已一念現前？秖如五念何由得息，那得將彼相應一念類今剎那念邪？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "That text first embodies the original stillness in faculty and object, and works at it without stopping; when knowing ceases and its counterpart is dropped, only then does the single thought of numinous knowing appear. So we know that its single thought is revealed entirely by wondrous calming. Otherwise, why would a single thought appear only after the five thoughts have ceased? How do the five thoughts come to cease? How can that single thought of accord be compared to the present instantaneous thought?

Chinese Source

況奢摩他別用妙止安心，毘鉢舍那別用妙觀安心，優畢叉方乃總用止觀，故出觀體中一念，正是今之陰識一念也。何者？彼文序中先會定慧同宗，法爾中乃云故即心為道，可謂尋流得源矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Moreover, its śamatha section uses wondrous calming alone to settle the mind, and its vipaśyanā section uses wondrous contemplation alone to settle the mind; only the upekṣā section uses calming and contemplation together. So the single thought in [the section] bringing out the essence of contemplation is exactly the present single thought of the aggregate of consciousness. Why? In that text's preface, it first unites concentration and wisdom as one cardinal doctrine; and in [the passage on] what is naturally so, it says: 'so the mind itself is the Way' — this may be called following the stream to find the source.

Chinese Source

故出觀體云，秖知一念即空不空、非空非不空。言秖知者，乃即體(止也)了(觀也)理，今剎那是三諦理，不須專亡根境顯其靈知，亦不須深推緣生求其空寂，故云秖知，此乃即心為道也。若奢摩他觀成，顯出自性一念，何用更修三觀？」

Ru-Yi Translation

So, bringing out the essence of contemplation, it says: 'Simply know that the single thought is empty and not empty, neither empty nor not empty.' 'Simply know' means to embody (calming) and understand (contemplation), right here, the principle that the present instant is the principle of the three truths. There is no need to make faculty and object vanish in order to reveal numinous knowing, nor to push deeply into conditioned arising to seek its empty stillness. Hence 'simply know': this is 'the mind itself is the Way.' If the śamatha contemplation were complete and had revealed the single thought of self-nature, why would one need to cultivate the three contemplations further?"

Chinese Source

問：「彼云，若於相應一念起五陰者，仍以二空破之，那云不更修觀？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "It says: 'If, in the single thought of accord, the five aggregates arise, one still breaks them with the two emptinesses.' How can you say one does not cultivate contemplation further?"

Chinese Source

答：「於真知起陰、以觀破之，不起陰者何用觀之？彼二空觀乃是觀陰，非觀真知。故知解一千從、迷一萬惑。若欲廣引教文驗其相違，不可令盡，書倦且止。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "When the aggregates arise in true knowing, one breaks them with contemplation; where the aggregates do not arise, why contemplate? That contemplation of the two emptinesses contemplates the aggregates, not true knowing. So we know: understand one thing and a thousand follow; be confused about one and ten thousand are confused. If I wished to cite the texts of the teaching at length to prove the contradictions, there would be no end to it. I am tired of writing, and stop here."

Chinese Source

二、違義者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, contradicting the meaning.

Chinese Source

問：「據上所引眾教，雖見相違，且如立此十門，欲通妙理亡於名相，若一念屬事，豈但通事，將不違作者意乎？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "According to the many teachings cited above, one does see contradictions. But these ten gates were set up wishing to give passage to the wondrous principle and to let names and marks vanish. If the single thought belongs to events, would it not give passage only to events, and so go against the author's intent?"

Chinese Source

答：「立門近要則妙理可通，若敻指真如，初心如何造趣？依何起觀邪？今立根塵一剎那心，本具三千即空假中，稱此觀之，即能成就十種妙法，豈但解知而已！如此方稱作者之意。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "When the gate is set up near at hand and essential, the wondrous principle can be passed through. If one points far off to true suchness, how is a beginner to approach it? On what would contemplation arise? Here we set up the one-instant mind of faculty and object, which originally contains the three thousand and is identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle. Pointing Out the Essentials of the Ten Gates of Nonduality, Fascicle One (end)

Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

"The Great Treatise" (大論). The Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra. The verse on name and form is quoted as Zhili gives it.

"The bodhisattva Forest of the Tathāgata" (如來林菩薩). The speaker of the "skilled painter" verse in the sixty-fascicle Avataṃsaka, in the chapter of verses in praise on Yāma heaven.

The opponents' formulas. "Numinous knowing" (靈知), "true knowing" (真知), and "the nature of mind is numinous and still" are characteristic of the off-mountain reading, close to the language of Guifeng Zongmi; Zhili quotes them in order to reject reading the "single thought" as the true nature. The Resolution Clarifying Delusion and Revealing the Correct (諭迷顯正決) is a work by one of these opponents, now lost; it is known only from Zhili's citations.

"Unchanging while according with conditions is called mind; according with conditions while unchanging is called nature." A formula Zhanran uses in The Main Idea of Calming and Contemplation and The Vajra Scalpel. Zhili's point: "mind" names principle as it appears in events, so it cannot simply be identified with principle.

Two kinds of contemplating the mind. The contemplation of the true mark (實相觀) embodies the stillness of the mind of consciousness and sees the three thousand present in it; the contemplation of mind-only (唯識觀) illuminates the arising mind as it transforms into the ten realms. Both rely on events, the ordinary mind. "Four natures cannot be found" refers to the fourfold analysis of arising (from self, other, both, or neither).

The Yongjia Collection (永嘉集). By Yongjia Xuanjue (永嘉玄覺, 665–713), whose sections on śamatha, vipaśyanā, and upekṣā draw on Tiantai methods. Zhili argues that its "single thought of numinous knowing" appears only after calming has done its work, and so is not the same as the ordinary instant of mind taken as the object here.

"The root of non-abiding" (無住本). From the Vimalakīrti Sūtra: "From the root of non-abiding, all dharmas are established." Zhili reads Zhanran's "the one nature is without nature" in this light.

The four levels in the seventh fascicle of the Commentary. Inherent conditioning and revealing causes (principle); the three causes of cultivated virtue (event); the three paths flowing on (delusion); the superior function in the fruit (awakening).

"Of" (之). Zhili insists that 之 in 心之色心 is merely a grammatical particle, against the opponents' reading of 之 as "go to," which would make the true mind "go out" to produce form and mind.

"The eight marks of a thing" (物之八相). The four characteristics of conditioned things (arising, abiding, change, ceasing) and their secondary characteristics; the point is that they are simultaneous, with no before or after.

Transformation (變) and creating (造). Zhili explains why The Vajra Scalpel says "transformation" belongs only to the distinct and perfect teachings, while "creating" extends to all four: "transformation" implies a real middle nature that changes into dharmas; "creating" can also mean merely putting things together from karma and delusion. "The great vehicle [says] mind only; the lesser vehicle [says] from the mind."

The textual dispute: "essence and function" or "the essence is the same." The opponents read 造謂體同 ("creating means the essence is the same") and changed some twenty characters, claiming support from an "old copy" and a copy that came from Japan. Zhili defends 造謂體用 ("creating means essence and function"), the reading in the present text. "Hangzhou's ten canons," "the Calming-and-Contemplation Preceptor of Guoqing," "Preceptor Sui" (邃和尚, probably Daosui 道邃, a Tang Tiantai teacher who taught Saichō), and "Qianshu" (乾淑) are named only as Zhili gives them. The Shizhu zhi (示珠指) is a work by Yuanqing, known from Zhili's polemics; whether it was sent to Japan is Zhili's claim.

The textual dispute: "the provisional" in Gate 2. The opponents' copy read "identical to the empty, identical to the middle" without "provisional." Zhili argues that the threefold contemplation must be complete and that the shorter reading is a later cut. The base text in this translation (T1927) has the full reading.

"How could it omit this contemplation" (那便略慈假觀邪). 慈 is read as 茲 ("this"), an apparent scribal error; kept as written in the Chinese.

"Again, also, also" (又亦亦先了). The doubled 亦 appears to be a scribal duplication; it is translated literally and kept as written.

"Three, five, seven, or nine" (三五七九). Fixed classifications such as the three vehicles, five vehicles, seven kinds of expedient practitioners, and nine realms. Alternatively, numerical groupings of beings and lands in general.

"Brilliantly clear all day long" (終日炳然). Apparently the opponents' reading of 終自炳然 ("remain, in the end, brilliantly clear of themselves"). Zhili defends 自 as the stronger reading.

"Form only and fragrance only" (唯色唯香). From the Tiantai formula "every form and every fragrance is none other than the middle way"; here, the understanding that each outer dharma is complete in itself.
