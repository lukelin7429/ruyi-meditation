The Shared, Distinct, and Perfect Teachings

This part completes Chegwan's text. It sets out the remaining three of the Four Teachings of Content, each with its stages matched against those of the other teachings. The shared teaching, with its ten grounds from "dry wisdom" to the buddha ground, is "shared" because its dull practitioners end where the Tripiṭaka teaching ends while its sharp ones are "grafted" onto the distinct or perfect teaching once they glimpse the not-empty middle. The distinct teaching, for bodhisattvas alone, is laid out in the fifty-two stages of the Yingluo Sūtra: ten faiths, abidings, practices, dedications and grounds, then equal and wondrous awakening. The perfect teaching uses the same names but places them far lower in the scale of attainment: the five grades of disciples from the Lotus, with the practice of the five repentances; the ten faiths, where the purified six faculties already equal the buddha of the Tripiṭaka teaching; and the forty-two stages from the first abiding to wondrous awakening, judged throughout by the six identities so that one neither overrates nor belittles oneself. Chegwan warns more than once against taking "identity" as permission to stop practicing. The work closes with the twenty-five preliminary expedients and the ten modes of contemplation, again matched to images from the parable of the great cart, and refers the reader to the Profound Meaning for detail. The lower fascicle on other schools' classifications, mentioned in the last line, does not survive.

The Shared Teaching

Chinese Source

次明通教者，通前藏教，通後別、圓，故名通教。又從當教得名，謂三人同以無言說道，體色入空，故名通教。依《大品經》，乾慧等十地，即是此教位次也。一乾慧地，未有理水故得其名，即外凡位，與藏教五停心、總、別等三位齊。二性地，相似得法性水，伏見思惑，即內凡位，與藏教四善根齊。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the shared teaching: it is shared with the preceding Tripiṭaka teaching and shared with the subsequent distinct and perfect [teachings]; hence it is called the shared teaching. It also gets its name from within the teaching itself: the three [kinds of] persons alike, by the path beyond words, embody form and enter emptiness; hence it is called the shared teaching. According to the Larger Sūtra, the ten grounds, dry wisdom and the rest, are the stages of this teaching. First, the ground of dry wisdom: it gets this name because it does not yet have the water of principle; it is the outer ordinary stage, equal to the three stages of the Tripiṭaka teaching, the five methods for stilling the mind and the general and specific [mindfulness]. Second, the ground of nature: one resembles having obtained the water of the dharma-nature and subdues the delusion of views and habituated thought; it is the inner ordinary stage, equal to the Tripiṭaka teaching's four wholesome roots.

Chinese Source

三八人地、四見地，此二位入無間三昧，斷三界八十八使見盡，發真無漏，見真諦理，與藏教初果齊。五薄地，斷欲界九品思前六品，與藏教二果齊。六離欲地，斷欲界九品思盡，與藏教三果齊。七已辦地，斷三界見思惑盡，但斷正使，不能侵習，如燒木成炭，與藏教四果齊。聲聞位齊此。八辟支佛地，更侵習氣，如燒炭成灰。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the ground of the eighth-stage practitioner, and fourth, the ground of vision: in these two stages one enters the uninterrupted samādhi, severs completely the eighty-eight agents of views of the three realms, brings forth the true uncontaminated [wisdom], and sees the principle of the truth of the real; equal to the Tripiṭaka teaching's first fruit. Fifth, the thinned ground: one severs the first six of the nine grades of habituated thought of the desire realm; equal to the Tripiṭaka teaching's second fruit. Sixth, the ground free of desire: one severs the nine grades of habituated thought of the desire realm completely; equal to the Tripiṭaka teaching's third fruit. Seventh, the ground of the task accomplished: one severs the delusion of views and habituated thought of the three realms completely, but severs only the main agents and cannot encroach on the habituation, like wood burned into charcoal; equal to the Tripiṭaka teaching's fourth fruit. The śrāvaka stages end here. Eighth, the pratyekabuddha ground: one further encroaches on the residual habituation, like charcoal burned into ash.

Chinese Source

九菩薩地，正使斷盡，與二乘同，扶習潤生，道觀雙流，遊戲神通，淨佛國土。十佛地，機緣若熟，以一念相應慧頓斷殘習，坐七寶菩提樹下，以天衣為座，現帶劣勝應身成佛。為三乘根性轉無生四諦法輪，緣盡入滅，正習俱除，如炭灰俱盡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, the bodhisattva ground: the main agents are severed completely, the same as the two vehicles; upholding habituation to nourish rebirth, letting path and contemplation flow together, sporting in spiritual powers, one purifies buddha-lands. Tenth, the buddha ground: when capacities and conditions are ripe, with the wisdom corresponding to a single thought one suddenly severs the remaining habituation, sits beneath the seven-jeweled bodhi tree with heavenly robes as a seat, and, manifesting a response-body both inferior and superior, attains buddhahood. For those of the faculty-natures of the three vehicles he turns the Dharma-wheel of the four truths of no generation; when conditions are exhausted he enters extinction, main [agents] and habituation both removed, like charcoal and ash both consumed.

Chinese Source

經云：「三獸度河，謂象、馬、兔也。」喻斷惑不同故。又經云：「諸法實相，三乘皆得，亦不名佛。」即此教也。此教三乘因同果異，證果雖異，同斷見思、同出分段、同證偏真。然於菩薩中有二種，謂利、鈍。鈍則但見偏空、不見不空，止成當教果頭佛，行因雖殊，果與藏教齊，故言通前；若利根菩薩，非但見空，兼見不空，不空即中道。分二種：謂但不但，若見但中，別教來接；若見不但中，圓教來接。故言通後。

Ru-Yi Translation

A sūtra says: "Three animals cross a river, namely the elephant, the horse, and the hare" — a simile for the difference in severing delusion. Again a sūtra says: "The true mark of all dharmas the three vehicles all attain, yet it is not called buddha." This is this teaching. In this teaching the three vehicles have the same cause and different fruits; though the fruits they realize differ, they alike sever views and habituated thought, alike leave segmented [birth-and-death], and alike realize the one-sided real. Yet among bodhisattvas there are two kinds, the sharp and the dull. The dull see only one-sided emptiness and not the not-empty, and become only the buddha at the head of the fruit of this teaching; though their causal practice differs, the fruit is equal to the Tripiṭaka teaching; hence it is said to be shared with what precedes. Bodhisattvas of sharp faculties see not only emptiness but also the not-empty, and the not-empty is the middle way. This is of two kinds, the mere and the not-mere. If they see the mere middle, the distinct teaching comes to graft them; if they see the not-mere middle, the perfect teaching comes to graft them. Hence it is said to be shared with what follows.

Chinese Source

問：何位受接？進入何位？答：受接人三根不同。若上根，三地、四地被接；中根之人，五地、六地；下根之人，七地、八地。所接之教，真似不同，若似位被接，別十迴向、圓十信位；若真位受接，別初地、圓初住。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: At what stage is one grafted? Into what stage does one advance? Answer: Those who are grafted differ in three [kinds of] faculties. Those of superior faculties are grafted at the third and fourth grounds; those of middling faculties at the fifth and sixth grounds; those of inferior faculties at the seventh and eighth grounds. In the teaching that grafts them, the true and the resembling differ: if grafted at a resembling stage, [one enters] the distinct teaching's ten dedications or the perfect teaching's stage of the ten faiths; if grafted at a true stage, the distinct teaching's first ground or the perfect teaching's first abiding.

Chinese Source

問：此藏通二教，同是三乘，同斷四住，止出三界，同證偏真，同行三百由旬，同入化城，何故分二？答：誠如所問，然同而不同，所證雖同，大小巧拙永異。此之二教是界內教。藏是界內小拙，不通於大故小，析色入空故拙。此教三人，雖當教內有上中下異，望通三人則一概鈍根，故須析破也。通教則界內大巧，大謂大乘初門故，巧謂體色入空故，雖當教中三人上中下異，若望藏教則一概為利。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: These two teachings, the Tripiṭaka and the shared, are alike [teachings of] the three vehicles; they alike sever the four abodes, only leave the three realms, alike realize the one-sided real, alike travel three hundred yojanas, and alike enter the phantom city. Why divide them into two? Answer: It is truly as you ask; yet they are the same and not the same. Though what they realize is the same, great and small, skillful and clumsy, are forever different. These two teachings are teachings within the realms. The Tripiṭaka is the small and clumsy [teaching] within the realms: small because it does not extend to the great, clumsy because it analyzes form to enter emptiness. Though the three persons of this teaching, within their own teaching, differ as superior, middling, and inferior, compared with the three persons of the shared teaching they are uniformly of dull faculties; hence they must analyze and break [form]. The shared teaching is the great and skillful [teaching] within the realms: great because it is the first gate of the Mahāyāna, skillful because it embodies form to enter emptiness. Though the three persons in this teaching differ as superior, middling, and inferior, compared with the Tripiṭaka teaching they are uniformly sharp.

Chinese Source

問：教既大乘。何故有二乘之人？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Since the teaching is Mahāyāna, why are there persons of the two vehicles?

Chinese Source

答：朱雀門中何妨庶民出入？故人雖有小，教定是大。大乘兼小，漸引入實，豈不巧哉？般若、方等部內共般若等，即此教也。略明通教竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Within the Vermilion Bird Gate, what prevents the common people from going in and out? So, though some of the persons are small, the teaching is certainly great. The Mahāyāna embraces the small to guide them gradually into the real — is that not skillful? The shared prajñā and the like within the Prajñā and Vaipulya divisions are this teaching. This concludes the brief clarification of the shared teaching.

The Distinct Teaching: The Seven Groups of Stages

Chinese Source

次明別教者，此教明界外獨菩薩法，教理、智斷、行位、因果別前二教，別後圓教，故名別也。《涅槃》云：「四諦因緣有無量相，非聲聞、緣覺所知。」諸大乘經廣明菩薩歷劫修行，行位次第互不相攝，此並別教之相也。《華嚴》明十住、十行、十迴向為賢，十地為聖，妙覺為佛；《纓絡》明五十二位；《金光明》但出十地佛果；《勝天王》明十地；《涅槃》明五行。如是諸經增減不同者，界外菩薩隨機利益，豈得定說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the distinct teaching: this teaching makes clear the Dharma beyond the realms that belongs to bodhisattvas alone. In teaching and principle, wisdom and severance, practice and stage, cause and fruit, it is distinct from the two preceding teachings and distinct from the subsequent perfect teaching; hence it is called distinct. The Nirvāṇa says: "The four truths and causes and conditions have immeasurable marks, not known by śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas." The Mahāyāna sūtras make clear at length the bodhisattva's practice through eons, in which the sequences of practice and stage do not include one another; these are all marks of the distinct teaching. The Avataṃsaka makes clear the ten abidings, ten practices, and ten dedications as worthies, the ten grounds as sages, and wondrous awakening as buddha; the Yingluo makes clear fifty-two stages; the Golden Light sets out only the ten grounds and the buddha-fruit; the Sheng tianwang makes clear the ten grounds; the Nirvāṇa makes clear the five practices. That these sūtras differ by adding and subtracting is because bodhisattvas beyond the realms benefit [beings] according to capacity; how could it be stated in a fixed way?

Chinese Source

然位次周足莫過《纓絡經》，故今依彼略明菩薩歷位斷證之相。以五十二位束為七科，謂信、住、行、向、地、等、妙。又合七為二：初凡、二聖。就凡又二：信為外凡；住、行、向為內凡，亦名為賢。約聖亦二：十地、等覺為因；妙覺為果。大分如此，自下細釋。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet for completeness of stages nothing surpasses the Yingluo Sūtra; so now, relying on it, I briefly make clear the marks of the bodhisattva's passage through the stages, severing and realizing. The fifty-two stages are bundled into seven groups: faith, abiding, practice, dedication, ground, equal, and subtle. Again, the seven are combined into two: first, ordinary; second, sagely. The ordinary again has two: the faiths are the outer ordinary; the abidings, practices, and dedications are the inner ordinary, also called the worthies. The sagely also has two: the ten grounds and equal enlightenment are the cause; wondrous awakening is the fruit. The broad divisions are like this; below they are explained in detail.

Chinese Source

初言十信者，一信、二念、三精進、四慧、五定、六不退、七迴向、八護法、九戒、十願。此十位伏三界見思煩惱，故名伏忍位（外凡），與藏教七賢位，通教乾慧、性地齊。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the ten faiths: first, faith; second, mindfulness; third, effort; fourth, wisdom; fifth, concentration; sixth, non-regression; seventh, dedication; eighth, protecting the Dharma; ninth, precepts; tenth, vows. These ten stages subdue the afflictions of views and habituated thought of the three realms; hence they are called the stage of the forbearance of subduing (outer ordinary), equal to the Tripiṭaka teaching's stages of the seven worthies and the shared teaching's ground of dry wisdom and ground of nature.

Chinese Source

次明十住者，一發心住（斷三界見惑盡，與藏教初果，通教八人、見地齊）、二治地、三修行、四生貴、五具足方便、六正心、七不退（已上六住斷三界思惑盡，得位不退，與藏通二佛齊）、八童真、九法王子、十灌頂（已上三住斷界內塵沙，伏界外塵沙，前二不知名目），亦名習種性，用從假入空觀，是真諦理，開慧眼，成一切智，行三百由旬。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the ten abidings: first, the abiding of arousing the resolve (one severs the delusion of views of the three realms completely; equal to the Tripiṭaka teaching's first fruit and the shared teaching's ground of the eighth-stage practitioner and ground of vision); second, cultivating the ground; third, practice; fourth, noble birth; fifth, complete with skillful means; sixth, the correct mind; seventh, non-regression (the six abidings above sever the delusion of habituated thought of the three realms completely and attain non-regression in stage; equal to the buddhas of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings); eighth, a child's purity; ninth, Dharma-prince; tenth, consecration (the three abidings above sever the dust-and-sand within the realms and subdue the dust-and-sand beyond the realms; the two preceding [teachings] do not know their names). They are also called the nature attained through habituated practice; one uses the contemplation of entering emptiness from the provisional, [which sees] the principle of the truth of the real, opens the wisdom-eye, accomplishes the wisdom that knows all things, and travels three hundred yojanas.

Chinese Source

次明十行者，一歡喜、二饒益、三無違逆、四無屈撓、五無癡亂、六善現、七無著、八難得、九善法、十真實（斷界外塵沙惑），亦云性種性，用從空入假觀見俗諦，開法眼，成道種智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the ten practices: first, joy; second, benefiting; third, no opposition; fourth, non-yielding; fifth, freedom from confused delusion; sixth, wholesome manifestation; seventh, non-attachment; eighth, hard to attain; ninth, good dharmas; tenth, the real (severing the delusion of dust-and-sand beyond the realms). They are also called the nature attained through distinguishing; one uses the contemplation of entering the provisional from emptiness, sees the conventional truth, opens the Dharma-eye, and accomplishes the wisdom of the paths.

Chinese Source

次明十迴向者，一救護眾生離眾生相、二不壞、三等一切諸佛、四至一切處、五無盡功德藏、六入一切平等善根、七等隨順一切眾生、八真如相、九無縛無著解脫、十入法界無量（伏無明、習中觀），亦名道種性，行四百由旬，居方便有餘土（已上三十位為三賢，亦名內凡。從八住至此，為行不退位）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the ten dedications: first, rescuing sentient beings while apart from the mark of sentient beings; second, indestructibility; third, equal to all buddhas; fourth, reaching every place; fifth, the inexhaustible treasury of merit; sixth, entering all the wholesome roots of equality; seventh, equally according with all sentient beings; eighth, the mark of suchness; ninth, unbound and unattached liberation; tenth, entering the immeasurable dharma-realm (subduing ignorance and practicing the contemplation of the middle). They are also called the nature attained through the path; one travels four hundred yojanas and dwells in the land of expedients with residue (the thirty stages above are the three worthies, also called the inner ordinary; from the eighth abiding to here is the stage of non-regression in practice).

The Distinct Teaching: The Grounds and Buddhahood

Chinese Source

次明十地者，一歡喜（從此用中道觀破一分無明，顯一分三德，乃至等覺，俱名聖種性），此是見道位，又無功用位，百界作佛，八相成道，利益眾生，行五百由旬，初入實報無障閡土，初入寶所；二離垢地、三發光地、四焰慧地、五難勝地、六現前地、七遠行地、八不動地、九善慧地、十法雲地（已九地，地地各斷一品無明，證一分中道）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the ten grounds: first, joy (from here on one uses the contemplation of the middle way to break one portion of ignorance and reveal one portion of the three virtues; up to equal enlightenment, all are called the noble nature). This is the stage of the path of vision, also the effortless stage: one becomes a buddha in a hundred realms, accomplishes the path through the eight aspects, benefits sentient beings, travels five hundred yojanas, first enters the land of true reward free of obstruction, and first enters the place of treasure. Second, the ground free of defilement; third, the ground of radiant light; fourth, the ground of blazing wisdom; fifth, the ground difficult to conquer; sixth, the ground of manifestation; seventh, the far-reaching ground; eighth, the immovable ground; ninth, the ground of excellent wisdom; tenth, the ground of the Dharma-cloud (in the nine grounds [above], at each ground one severs one grade of ignorance and realizes one portion of the middle way).

Chinese Source

更斷一品入等覺位，亦名金剛心，亦名一生補處，亦名有上士。更破一品無明，入妙覺位，坐蓮華藏世界七寶菩提樹下大寶華王座，現圓滿報身，為鈍根菩薩眾轉無量四諦法輪，即此佛也。有經論說：「七地已前名有功用道，八地已上名無功用道，妙覺位但破一品無明」者，總是約教道說。有處說：「初地斷見，從二地至六地斷思，與羅漢齊」者，此乃借別教位名，名通教位耳。有云：「三賢十聖住果報，唯佛一人居淨土」，此借別教名，明圓教位也。如此流類甚眾，須細知當教斷證之位，至何位斷何惑、證何理，往判諸教、諸位無不通達。略明別教竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Severing one more grade, one enters the stage of equal enlightenment, also called the vajra mind, also called "one more birth to buddhahood," also called "the one with something still above." Breaking one more grade of ignorance, one enters the stage of wondrous awakening, sits beneath the seven-jeweled bodhi tree of the Lotus-Treasury World on the throne of the great jeweled king of flowers, and manifests the perfect and complete reward-body; for the assembly of bodhisattvas of dull faculties he turns the Dharma-wheel of the four truths of the immeasurable — this is that buddha. Some sūtras and treatises say, "Before the seventh ground it is called the path with effort; from the eighth ground on it is called the effortless path; the stage of wondrous awakening breaks only one grade of ignorance" — all of this is spoken in terms of the teaching-path. Some places say, "The first ground severs views; from the second ground to the sixth ground one severs habituated thought, equal to the arhat" — this borrows the names of the distinct teaching's stages to name the shared teaching's stages. Some say, "The three worthies and ten sages dwell in the reward of the fruit; only the Buddha alone dwells in the pure land" — this borrows the names of the distinct teaching to make clear the stages of the perfect teaching. Cases of this kind are very many. One must know in detail the stages of severing and realizing of each teaching — at what stage which delusion is severed and which principle is realized — and then in judging the teachings and stages there will be nothing one does not penetrate. This concludes the brief clarification of the distinct teaching.

The Perfect Teaching

Chinese Source

次明圓教者，圓名圓妙、圓滿、圓足、圓頓，故名圓教也。所謂圓伏、圓信、圓斷、圓行、圓位、圓自在莊嚴、圓建立眾生，諸大乘經論說佛境界，不共三乘位次，總屬此教也。《法華》中開、示、悟、入四字，對圓教住、行、向、地。此四十位，《華嚴》云：「初發心時便成正覺，所有慧身不由他悟，清淨妙法身，湛然應一切。」此明圓四十二位。《維摩經》云：「薝蔔林中不嗅餘香，入此室者，唯聞諸佛功德之香。」又云：「入不二法門。」《般若》明最上乘。《涅槃》明一心五行。又經云：「有人入大海浴，已用一切諸河之水。」又「娑伽羅龍澍車軸雨，唯大海能受，餘地不堪。」又「擣萬種香為丸，若燒一塵，具足眾氣。」如是等類並屬圓教。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the perfect teaching: "perfect" means perfectly wondrous, perfectly complete, perfectly sufficient, perfectly sudden; hence it is called the perfect teaching. That is: perfect subduing, perfect faith, perfect severance, perfect practice, perfect stage, perfect self-mastery and adornment, and perfectly establishing sentient beings. Wherever the Mahāyāna sūtras and treatises speak of the Buddha's realm, not shared with the stages of the three vehicles, it all belongs to this teaching. The four words of the Lotus, opening, showing, awakening, and entering, correspond to the perfect teaching's abidings, practices, dedications, and grounds. As for these forty stages, the Avataṃsaka says: "At the time of first arousing the mind one attains correct awakening; the body of wisdom one has is not awakened through another. The pure, wondrous Dharma-body, tranquil and clear, responds to everything." This makes clear the perfect forty-two stages. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "In a campaka forest one smells no other scent; one who enters this room smells only the fragrance of the buddhas' merit." It also says: "entering the Dharma-gate of nonduality." The Prajñā makes clear the supreme vehicle. The Nirvāṇa makes clear the five practices in a single mind. Again a sūtra says: "Someone who bathes in the great ocean has already used the water of all rivers." Again: "The dragon Sāgara sends down rain like axles; only the great ocean can receive it, and other lands cannot bear it." Again: "Pound ten thousand kinds of incense into a pill; if one burns a single mote, it is complete with all the scents." Cases such as these all belong to the perfect teaching.

Next, making clear correct cultivation, the ten modes of contemplation: for the four teachings, too, the names are the same but the meanings differ. Now I make clear the perfect teaching; the other teachings can be inferred from it.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, truly and correctly arousing the bodhi-mind: relying on the wondrous object, one makes the four universal vows of the unconditioned, with pity for oneself and pity for others, seeking [bodhi] above and transforming [beings] below. Hence the sūtra says: "spread above with a canopy."

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, skillfully settling the mind in calming-and-contemplation: embodying the preceding wondrous principle, constantly and permanently still, is called concentration; still yet constantly illuminating is called wisdom. The Four Teachings of Tiantai

Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

Stage names. The shared teaching's ten grounds, the distinct teaching's ten abidings, practices, dedications, and grounds, equal and wondrous awakening, "one more birth to buddhahood," "the one with something still above," the nature attained through habituated practice / through distinguishing / through the path, the noble nature, grafting, and the teaching-path follow the site's Jiaoguan Gangzong Parts 3–5. Where Chegwan's list differs from Zhixu's (practices 3, 4, 8: 無違逆, 無屈撓, 難得; dedications 6, 7, 10), his own wording is translated ("no opposition," "non-yielding," "hard to attain," and so on).

Lands. 方便有餘土 "the land of expedients with residue," 實報無障閡土 "the land of true reward free of obstruction," and 常寂光土 "the land of eternal tranquil light" follow the project's settled renderings; the site's Jiaoguan Gangzong has "expedient land with residue" and "reward-land free of obstruction."

Yongjia's line (unit 19). Quoted from Yongjia Xuanjue's collection; the wording "Alike removing the four abodes…falls short" is adjusted from the site's Jiaoguan Gangzong Part 5, which cites the same line, and matches Part 1, unit 3, of this text.

Quotations located on the site. The Avataṃsaka line "the pure, wondrous Dharma-body, tranquil and clear, responds to everything" follows the site's Profound Meaning; the campaka passage follows the site's Vimalakīrti ("smells only the fragrance of the Buddha's own merit"), shortened as Chegwan shortens it; the Humane Kings line is also quoted in the site's Mohe Zhiguan and Profound Meaning; "a single form, a single fragrance" follows the Profound Meaning. The Lotus images for the ten modes follow the site's "Parable of the Burning House." "How much more so, then, one who is able to uphold this sūtra, and also practices giving" follows the site's Lotus (page "The Merit of Believing"); the other four five-grade quotations are translated directly.

Other sources. "Three animals cross a river" is a stock simile (cf. Upāsaka Precepts Sūtra; used in the Profound Meaning). "If you wish to repent, sit upright and be mindful of the true mark…" is from the Sūtra on the Contemplation of Samantabhadra. "Relying on these precepts…" is from the Sūtra of the Buddha's Final Bequest. The ocean-bath, Sāgara-rain, and incense-pill similes and "The true mark of all dharmas the three vehicles all attain…": sources not located.

鼠唧鳥空 (unit 15). Literally "the mouse's 'ji' and the bird's 'kong'": a mouse squeaking a sound like 即 and a bird crying a sound like 空, i.e., repeating "identity" and "emptiness" without understanding. The cases are said to be in the sūtras and treatises; source not located.

果頭佛 (unit 0). "The buddha at the head of the fruit": a buddha only in name at the culmination of a lower teaching, whose attainment is in fact that of an arhat-level fruit.

Seventh abiding (unit 8). Chegwan equates the distinct seventh abiding with "the buddhas of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings"; Jiaoguan Gangzong equates it with the shared teaching's ground of the task accomplished. Translated as written.

已九地 (unit 11). Probably for 已上九地 ("the nine grounds above"); translated so.

伊三點／天主三目. The three dots of the Siddhaṃ letter i (glossary) and the three eyes of Maheśvara, the "Lord of Heaven": images of three that are neither in a row (horizontal) nor one above another (vertical).

我家／汝家 (unit 21). "Our house" and "your house" are the perfect and the distinct teachings; the image is of a frontier where borrowed titles run high.

了因性. "The nature of the revealing cause," per the glossary (了因 the revealing cause), with 正因 and 緣因 likewise.

The final sentence (unit 44). Announces the lost lower fascicle; the Part 1 "Origin" (unit 4) quotes this sentence.
