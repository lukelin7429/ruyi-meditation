Prefaces, the Five Periods, and the Tripiṭaka Teaching

The Four Teachings of Tiantai (Tiantai sijiao yi, T1931) was compiled by the Korean monk Chegwan (Ch. Diguan, d. 971), who came from Goryeo to Wuyue around 961 bringing Tiantai texts that had been lost in China. It became the standard primer of Tiantai doctrinal classification. The Taishō edition opens with material by later hands, all translated here: a mnemonic verse matching the stages of the four teachings to one another; an account of the work's origin compiled from two Song sources (Zanning and Daoyin), with a Ming printer's colophon; and a preface by the late-Ming layman Feng Mengzhen (1548–1605) for his 1582 printing. Chegwan's own text then sets out the Five Periods of the Buddha's teaching, each matched to a time of day, one of the Five Flavors (milk to ghee), and an episode from the parable of the destitute son in the Lotus Sūtra. It describes the Four Teachings of Method (sudden, gradual, secret, indeterminate), the special place of the Lotus and the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, and then begins the Four Teachings of Content with the Tripiṭaka teaching: the four truths with the six paths and twenty-eight heavens, the eighty-eight agents of views and eighty-one grades of habituated thought, and the stages of the śrāvaka, the one awakened through conditions, and the bodhisattva who becomes the Tripiṭaka buddha. Part 2 covers the shared, distinct, and perfect teachings.

Verses on the Four Teachings

Chinese Source

七賢七位藏初機，通教位中一二齊，別信并圓五品位，見思初伏在凡居。果位須陀預聖流，與通三四地齊儔，并連別住圓初信，八十八使正方休。圓別信住二之七，藏通極果皆同級，同除四住證偏真，內外塵沙分斷伏。八之十信二惑空，假成俗備理方通，齊前別住後三位，并連行向位相同。別地全齊圓住平，無明分斷證真因，等妙二覺初二行，進聞三位不知名。

Ru-Yi Translation

The seven worthies, seven stages: the Tripiṭaka's beginners; / in the shared teaching's stages, the first and second match them; / the distinct teaching's faiths, together with the perfect's stage of five grades — / views and habituated thought first subdued, they dwell as ordinary beings. / The fruit-stage of srotāpanna enters the stream of the sages, / ranked equal with the shared teaching's third and fourth grounds, / joined with the distinct's abidings and the perfect's first faith: / the eighty-eight agents only then come to rest. / The perfect's faiths and the distinct's abidings, second to seventh, / and the highest fruits of Tripiṭaka and shared, are all of one grade: / alike they remove the four abodes and realize the one-sided real; / dust-and-sand, within and beyond, is in part severed, in part subdued. / In the eighth to tenth faiths the two delusions are emptied; / the provisional is accomplished, the conventional complete, the principle at last penetrated; / they match the last three stages of the distinct's abidings above, / joined with the practices and dedications, the stages the same. / The distinct's grounds are wholly equal, level with the perfect's abidings: / ignorance severed in part, they realize the true cause. / Equal and subtle, the two enlightenments, [match] the first two practices; / of the three stages beyond, one does not even hear the names.

The Origin of the Four Teachings

Chinese Source

宋修僧史僧統贊寧《通惠錄》云：唐末吳越錢忠懿王治國之暇，究心內典，因閱《永嘉集》有「同除四住此處為齊，若伏無明三藏則劣」之句。不曉，問于雪居韶國師，乃云天台國清寺有寂法師，善弘教法，必解此語。王召法師至詰焉，法師曰：「此天台智者大師《妙玄》中文，時遭安史兵殘，近則會昌焚毀，中國教藏殘闕殆盡。今惟海東高麗闡教方盛，全書在彼。」王聞之慨然，即為遣國書贄弊使高麗求取一家章疏。高麗國君乃勅僧曰諦觀者，報聘以天台教部還歸于我。觀既至就，稟學寂公于螺溪，終焉大教至是重昌矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Zanning, Sangha Rector and compiler of the monastic history under the Song, says in his Tonghui Record: At the end of the Tang, King Zhongyi of the Qian family of Wuyue, in his leisure from governing the state, devoted his mind to the inner scriptures. Reading the Yongjia Collection, he came upon the line, "Alike removing the four abodes, at this point they are equal; but in subduing ignorance, the Tripiṭaka falls short." Not understanding it, he asked National Teacher Shao of Xueju, who said: "At Guoqing Monastery on Tiantai there is Dharma Master Ji, who is skilled at spreading the teaching; he will surely understand these words." The king summoned the Dharma Master and questioned him. The Dharma Master said: "This is a passage from the Profound Meaning of Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai. In the time of An [Lushan] and Shi [Siming] it was damaged by war, and more recently by the burnings of the Huichang era, so that the teaching-store of the Middle Kingdom is damaged and lacking, almost entirely gone. Now only in Haedong, in Goryeo, is the teaching flourishing, and the complete texts are there." Hearing this, the king was moved, and at once sent an envoy to Goryeo with a state letter and gifts, seeking the commentaries of our school. The ruler of Goryeo then ordered a monk named Chegwan (Ch. Diguan) to return the embassy, bringing the Tiantai teaching texts back to us. When Chegwan arrived, he went to Luoxi to study under Master Ji, and stayed there until his death. The great teaching flourished again from this point.

Chinese Source

觀能探索大本，錄出《四教儀》之文成二卷。其上卷明一家判教立義，下卷明南北諸師宗途異計。後至孤山圓法師校勘刊板，但行今上卷之文者，蓋由辭句簡要，義旨易明，學者誠資之可了其一化大綱，豈曰小補之哉。下卷則破斥南北古師，文義浩漫故得以緩之。如此文末指云：「自從此下略明諸家判教儀式，抑可見後卷之大略也。」王為寂師建伽藍螺溪之上，曰定慧院，進號淨光大師，追諡九祖，并東還教藏悉付於師，教門中興實基於此。而韶公適與智者同姓，乃又毘贊宗乘，且居隣佛隴，人咸疑其為智者後身云。

Ru-Yi Translation

Chegwan was able to explore the great original [works], and recorded the text of the Four Teachings from them in two fascicles. Its upper fascicle makes clear our school's classification of the teachings and the meaning it establishes; the lower fascicle makes clear the differing views on doctrinal paths of the masters of the South and North. Later, when Dharma Master [Zhi]yuan of Gushan collated it and had it cut on blocks, only the text of the present upper fascicle was put in circulation, because its wording is concise and its meaning easy to understand. Students can truly rely on it to understand the main outline of the whole teaching career — how could one call it a small aid? The lower fascicle refutes the ancient masters of South and North; its text and meaning are vast and diffuse, so it could be left aside. As the end of this text indicates: "From here on, the forms of classifying the teachings of the various schools are briefly made clear" — from this one can also see the general outline of the later fascicle. The king built a monastery for Master Ji above Luoxi, called Dinghui Cloister, raised his title to Great Master Jingguang, posthumously named him the ninth patriarch, and entrusted to the master all the teaching-store brought back from the east. The revival of the teaching truly has its foundation here. And Master Shao happened to share the same surname as Zhiyi, further assisted the school's vehicle, and lived near Folong; so people all suspected that he was Zhiyi's later incarnation.

Chinese Source

四明草菴法師道因《教苑遺事》云：昔智者禪師剏放生池於海涯，其放之也，必為授歸戒、說大法，然後縱之海中。智者滅後，至唐末，中國天台之道浸息，而海東高麗、新羅諸國盛弘此教。扶宗繼忠法師云：「智者緣在此方，而教敷于海東者，此必放生池中諸魚聞教稟戒報生者爾然。」聞此說者頗譏以為誕殊，不知教理有憑也，流水十千天子即脫魚報，豈外此乎。

Ru-Yi Translation

Dharma Master Daoyin of Caoan in Siming says in his Remaining Matters of the Teaching Garden: Long ago Chan Master Zhiyi established a pond for releasing living beings on the seashore. Whenever he released them, he would confer on them the refuges and precepts and teach the great Dharma, and only then set them free in the sea. After Zhiyi's passing, by the end of the Tang, the way of Tiantai in the Middle Kingdom gradually died out, while in Haedong the kingdoms of Goryeo, Silla, and others greatly spread this teaching. Dharma Master Jizhong of Fuzong said: "Zhiyi's conditions lay in this land, yet his teaching spread in Haedong. This must be the fish of the release pond, who heard the teaching and received the precepts, repaying [him] with their [new] lives." Those who heard this account mocked it as quite absurd, not knowing that the principle of the teaching supports it: the ten thousand devaputras of [the elder's son] Jalavāhana escaped their reward as fish — how is this any different?

客歲拙園成，名其堂曰淨業，屬臥痾餘日。因從三四淨侶掩關結夏其中，三時禮課，求生西方。宴坐焚香，研精藏典。鐘磬間發，白雲乍留。鳥低飛而親人，草蔓生而沒徑。𮖜遺巍𨷂機息漢陰，意欣欣甚適也已。

Ru-Yi Translation

Last year my humble garden was completed, and I named its hall Pure Karma; it came when I was lying ill, with days to spare. So, with three or four pure companions, I closed the gate and held the summer retreat in it: worship three times a day, seeking rebirth in the West. Sitting at ease and burning incense, I studied the canonical scriptures in depth. Bells and chimes sounded at intervals; white clouds stopped for a while. Birds flew low and came close to people; grasses grew wild and covered the paths. Having left my traces at the palace gates behind and laid my scheming to rest like [the old man of] Hanyin, I was joyful and quite content.

Chinese Source

僧真覺者，自武林來，進余以天台之學。余首肯焉，為留旬日，剖析教觀大旨。余若有悟入者，遂合掌佛前，願世世奉揚台宗，淨佛國土。蓋淨侶同時發心，永為主伴矣。台教源流具四明磐公所譔《佛祖統紀》，而《四教儀》者，則高麗沙門諦觀稟《法華玄文》而錄出者也。書凡二卷，上卷明一家判教之義，下卷明南北諸師宗途異計。今所傳者上卷耳，言約義該，實為台教之關鑰。學者了此，則一化大綱思過半矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

A monk, Zhenjue, came from Wulin and recommended to me the learning of Tiantai. I nodded assent and kept him ten days, analyzing the main purport of teaching and contemplation. I seemed to have some awakening and entry, and so joined my palms before the Buddha and vowed, life after life, to uphold and spread the Tiantai school and purify buddha-lands. My pure companions aroused the mind at the same time, to be forever host and companions [in this]. The source and course of the Tiantai teaching are set out fully in the Record of the Lineage of the Buddhas and Patriarchs composed by Master Pan of Siming; and the Four Teachings is what the Goryeo monk Chegwan recorded on the basis of the Profound Meaning of the Lotus. The book has two fascicles in all: the upper fascicle makes clear our school's meaning of classifying the teachings; the lower makes clear the differing views on doctrinal paths of the masters of the South and North. What is transmitted now is only the upper fascicle; its words are concise and its meaning comprehensive, and it truly is the key to the Tiantai teaching. If students understand this, they will have grasped more than half of the main outline of the whole teaching career.

南天竺沙門蒙潤有《集註》三卷，亦精核可喜，近吳中有刻本焉。掩關凡兩月，既出則復濫世罔，遂不能保淨戒，啖酒肉近妻子如曩時。客有幼迷於他鄉者，一旦人告之曰：「子之家在某所，父兄宗族、墳墓田宅種種可念也。」其人即留滯不能遽返，豈作他鄉人哉？今歲春，余且以一命棄青山，行有日矣。因追前志，捨貲刻《四教儀》一卷，并科文行于世。同志者其遂執鑰洞關以窮海藏，令一家教觀如日輪當午、川流赴壑，即余留滯他鄉可藉以懺悔矣。其勉之哉！其勉之哉！

Ru-Yi Translation

The monk Mengrun of Southern Tianzhu wrote a Collected Commentary in three fascicles, also precise and pleasing; recently there has been a printed edition in Wu. I kept the gate closed for two months in all; when I came out, I sank again into the world's net and could not keep the pure precepts, eating meat, drinking wine, and going near wife and children as before. There was a man who as a child had lost his way in another land; one day someone told him, "Your home is in such-and-such a place: your father and brothers and clan, the graves, the fields and house — all of it is there to long for." If that man then lingered and could not return at once, would he therefore become a man of the other land? This spring I am about to leave the green mountains on an official appointment, and the day of my departure is near. So, recalling my earlier resolve, I have given funds to print the Four Teachings, one fascicle, together with its outline of sections, to circulate in the world. May those of the same aspiration take the key, open the gate, and so exhaust the ocean-store, making the teaching and contemplation of our school like the sun's disk at noon, like rivers rushing to the valley; then my lingering in another land may by this be repented. Strive! Strive!

The Five Periods and the Eight Teachings

Chinese Source

天台四教儀

Ru-Yi Translation

The Four Teachings of Tiantai

Chinese Source

高麗沙門諦觀錄

Ru-Yi Translation

Recorded by Chegwan, a monk of Goryeo.

Chinese Source

天台智者大師，以五時八教判釋東流一代聖教，罄無不盡。言五時者：一華嚴時；二鹿苑時（說《四阿含》）；三方等時（說《維摩》、《思益》、《楞伽》、《楞嚴三昧》、《金光明》、《勝鬘》等經）；四般若時（說《摩訶般若》、《光讚般若》、《金剛般若》、《大品般若》等諸般若經）；五法華涅槃時。是為五時，亦名五味。言八教者：頓、漸、祕密、不定，藏、通、別、圓，是名八教。頓等四教是化儀，如世藥方；藏等四教名化法，如辨藥味。如是等儀散在廣文，今依大本略錄綱要，初辨五時五味及化儀四教，然後出藏、通、別、圓。

Ru-Yi Translation

Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai, by means of the Five Periods and the Eight Teachings, classified and explained the sacred teaching of the Buddha's whole lifetime that had flowed to the East, leaving nothing out. As for the Five Periods: first, the Avataṃsaka period; second, the Deer Park period (teaching the four Āgamas); third, the Vaipulya period (teaching the Vimalakīrti, the Viśeṣacinta, the Laṅkāvatāra, the Śūraṅgama Samādhi, the Golden Light, the Śrīmālā, and other sūtras); fourth, the Prajñā period (teaching the Mahāprajñā, the Light-Praising Prajñā, the Diamond Prajñā, the Larger Prajñā, and the other prajñā sūtras); fifth, the Lotus-and-Nirvāṇa period. These are the Five Periods, also called the Five Flavors. As for the Eight Teachings: the sudden, the gradual, the secret, the indeterminate; the Tripiṭaka, the shared, the distinct, the perfect — these are called the Eight Teachings. The four teachings of the sudden and the rest are [the teachings of] method, like the prescriptions of the world; the four teachings of the Tripiṭaka and the rest are called [the teachings of] content, like distinguishing the flavors of medicines. Forms such as these are scattered through the extended texts. Now, relying on the great original, I briefly record the main outline: first I distinguish the Five Periods and Five Flavors and the Four Teachings of Method, and after that set out the Tripiṭaka, the shared, the distinct, and the perfect.

The Sudden Teaching: The Avataṃsaka

Chinese Source

第一頓教者，即《華嚴經》也。從部、時、味等，得名為頓。所謂如來初成正覺，在寂滅道場，四十一位法身大士，及宿世根熟天龍八部一時圍繞，如雲籠月。爾時如來現盧舍那身，說圓滿修多羅，故言頓教。若約機約教，未免兼權。謂初發心時便成正覺等文，為圓機說圓教。處處說行布次第，則為權機說別教。故約部為頓，約教名兼。此經中云：「譬如日出先照高山（第一時）。」《涅槃》云：「譬如從牛出乳，此從佛出十二部經（一乳味）。」《法華．信解品》云：「即遣旁人急追將還，窮子驚愕，稱怨大喚」等。此領何義？答：諸聲聞在座如聾若啞等是也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the sudden teaching is the Avataṃsaka Sūtra. In terms of division, period, flavor, and the like, it gets the name "sudden." That is, when the Tathāgata had first attained correct awakening, at the site of awakening of quiescent extinction, the great bodhisattvas of the Dharma-body of the forty-one stages, together with the gods, dragons, and the eight classes [of beings] whose roots had ripened in past lives, surrounded him all at once, like clouds enclosing the moon. At that time the Tathāgata manifested the body of Locana and taught the perfect and complete sūtra; hence it is called the sudden teaching. If [discussed] in terms of capacity and in terms of teaching, it does not escape combining the provisional. That is, passages such as "At the time of first arousing the mind one attains correct awakening" are the perfect teaching taught for perfect capacities; where it everywhere teaches the arrayed sequence of practice, this is the distinct teaching taught for provisional capacities. Hence in terms of division it is sudden; in terms of teaching it is called combined. This sūtra says: "It is like the sun rising and first illuminating the high mountains" (the first period). The Nirvāṇa says: "It is like milk coming from the cow; this is the twelve divisions of the scriptures coming from the Buddha" (the first, the flavor of milk). The "Faith and Understanding" chapter of the Lotus says: "He at once sent his attendants to pursue him quickly and bring him back. The destitute son, startled and alarmed, cried out in protest," and so on. What meaning does this convey? Answer: The śrāvakas in the seat were as if deaf and mute, and so on.

The Gradual Teaching: Deer Park, Vaipulya, Prajñā

Chinese Source

第二漸教者（此下三時三味總名為漸），次為三乘根性於頓無益，故不動寂場而游鹿苑，脫舍那珍御之服，著丈六弊垢之衣，示從兜率降下，託摩耶胎。住胎、出胎、納妃、生子，出家苦行。六年已後，木菩提樹下以草為座，成劣應身。初在鹿苑，先為五人說四諦十二因緣事六度等教。若約時，則日照幽谷（第二時）；若約味，則從乳出酪。此從十二部經出九部修多羅（二酪味）。〈信解品〉云：「而以方便密遣二人（聲聞、緣覺），形色憔悴無威德者。汝可詣彼，徐語窮子，雇汝除糞。」此領何義？答：次頓之後說三藏教，二十年中常令除糞，即破見思煩惱等義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the gradual teaching (the three periods and three flavors from here on are all called gradual): next, because those of the nature of the three vehicles gained no benefit from the sudden, without leaving the site of quiescence he went to the Deer Park. He took off the precious robes of Locana and put on the worn and soiled clothing of the sixteen-foot [body]; he showed his descent from Tuṣita and his conception in Māyā's womb, dwelling in the womb, leaving the womb, taking a consort, begetting a son, leaving home, and ascetic practice. After six years, beneath the wooden bodhi tree, with grass for a seat, he became the inferior response-body. First in the Deer Park, he taught the five men the teachings of the four truths, the twelve links of dependent origination, the six pāramitās as actual practice, and so on. In terms of period, it is the sun shining on the deep valleys (the second period); in terms of flavor, it is cream coming from milk. This is the nine divisions of sūtra coming from the twelve divisions (the second, the flavor of cream). The "Faith and Understanding" chapter says: "And by skillful means he secretly sent two men (the śrāvakas and the pratyekabuddhas), haggard in appearance and without any air of authority: 'Go to him, and say gently to the destitute son: "We hire you to clear away dung."'" What meaning does this convey? Answer: After the sudden he taught the Tripiṭaka teaching; for twenty years he had them constantly clear away dung — this is the meaning of breaking the afflictions of views and habituated thought, and so on.

Chinese Source

次明方等部，《淨名》等經，彈偏折小，歎大褒圓。四教俱說，藏為半字教，通、別、圓為滿字教。對半說滿，故言對教。若約時，則食時（第三時）；若約味，則從酪出生酥。此從九部出方等（三生酥味）。〈信解品〉云：「過是已後，心相體信，入出無難，然其所止猶在本處。」此領何義？答：三藏之後次說方等，已得道果，心相體信，聞罵不瞋。內懷慚愧，心漸淳淑。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, it makes clear the Vaipulya division: the Vimalakīrti and other sūtras, which rebuke the partial and break down the small, praise the great and extol the perfect. All four teachings are taught: the Tripiṭaka is the half-word teaching; the shared, the distinct, and the perfect are the full-word teaching. Contrasting the full with the half, it is called the contrasted teaching. In terms of period, it is mealtime (the third period); in terms of flavor, it is curdled butter coming from cream. This is the Vaipulya coming from the nine divisions (the third, the flavor of curdled butter). The "Faith and Understanding" chapter says: "After this time had passed, a mutual trust grew between them, and he came and went freely, without difficulty — though he still remained dwelling in the same place as before." What meaning does this convey? Answer: After the Tripiṭaka he next taught the Vaipulya. Having already obtained the fruit of the path, their minds trusted each other, and hearing rebuke they were not angry; inwardly harboring shame, their minds gradually became pure and gentle.

The Tripiṭaka Teaching: Accumulation, Cessation, and the Path

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the truth of accumulation, which is the delusion of views and habituated thought, also called views and cultivation, also called the four abodes, also called defiled non-knowing, also called the delusion of grasping marks, also called branch-and-leaf ignorance, also called the common delusion, also called the delusion within the realms. Though the names differ, they are only views and habituated thought. First, explaining the delusion of views: there are eighty-eight agents, namely: first, the view of the body; second, extreme views; third, attachment to views; fourth, attachment to precepts; fifth, wrong views (the above are the sharp agents); sixth, greed; seventh, anger; eighth, delusion; ninth, pride; tenth, doubt (the above are the dull agents). These ten agents, passed through the four truths of the three realms, increase and decrease differently, making eighty-eight. That is, under suffering in the desire realm all ten agents are present; under accumulation and cessation there are seven agents each, excluding the view of the body, extreme views, and attachment to precepts; under the truth of the path there are eight agents, excluding the view of the body and extreme views. Under the four truths together they make thirty-two. Under the four truths of the upper two realms, the rest is all as in the desire realm, except that under each truth the agent of anger is excluded; hence each realm has twenty-eight, the two realms together make fifty-six, and together with the previous thirty-two they make the eighty-eight agents.

In the one ground of the desire realm there are nine grades of greed, anger, delusion, and pride. As for the nine grades: upper-upper, upper-middle, upper-lower, middle-upper, middle-middle, middle-lower, lower-upper, lower-middle, lower-lower. The eight upper grounds each have nine grades, excluding the agent of anger; hence it makes eighty-one. The views and habituated thought above, though different, are altogether the Tripiṭaka teaching's truth of accumulation as really existent.

Chinese Source

三滅諦者，滅前苦集，顯偏真理，因滅會真，滅非真諦。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the truth of cessation: extinguishing the preceding suffering and accumulation reveals the principle of the one-sided real. It is through extinction that one meets the real; extinction is not the truth of the real.

Second, the four right efforts: first, causing evil not yet arisen not to arise; second, causing evil already arisen to be extinguished; third, causing good not yet arisen to arise; fourth, causing good already arisen to increase. Third, the four bases of supernormal power (aspiration, mindfulness, effort, wisdom); fourth, the five faculties (faith, effort, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom); fifth, the five powers (the names are the same as the faculties above); sixth, the seven factors of awakening (mindfulness, discernment, effort, joy, ease, concentration, equanimity); seventh, the eightfold right path (right view, right thought, right speech, right action, right effort, right concentration, right mindfulness, right livelihood). The above seven groups are the Tripiṭaka teaching's truth of the path of generation-and-extinction.

然如前所列四諦名數，通下三教，但是隨教廣狹勝劣，生滅、無生、無量、無作不同耳，故向下名數更不再列。然四諦之中分世、出世，前二諦為世間因果（苦果、集因），後二諦為出世間因果（滅果、道因）。

Yet the names and numbers of the four truths listed above extend through the three teachings below; it is only that, according to the breadth or narrowness, superiority or inferiority of each teaching, they differ as generation-and-extinction, no generation, the immeasurable, and the unconditioned. Hence below, the names and numbers are not listed again. Within the four truths there is a division into the mundane and the supramundane: the first two truths are mundane cause and effect (suffering the effect, accumulation the cause); the latter two truths are supramundane cause and effect (cessation the effect, the path the cause).

Question: Why, for the mundane and the supramundane, is the effect first and the cause after? Answer: Śrāvakas have dull faculties; they know suffering, sever accumulation, long for the effect, and cultivate the cause. That is why it is so.

The Tripiṭaka Teaching: The Stages of the Śrāvaka

Chinese Source

略明藏教修行人之與位。初明聲聞位分二：初凡、二聖。凡又二：外凡、內凡。

Ru-Yi Translation

A brief clarification of the practitioners of the Tripiṭaka teaching and their stages. First, the stages of the śrāvaka are made clear, in two divisions: first, ordinary; second, sagely. The ordinary is again twofold: outer ordinary and inner ordinary.

Chinese Source

釋外凡中自分三：初、五停心：一多貪眾生不淨觀、二多嗔眾生慈悲觀、三多散眾生數息觀、四愚癡眾生因緣觀、五多障眾生念佛觀；二、別相念處（如前四念處是）；三、總相念處，一觀身不淨，受、心、法皆不淨，乃至觀法無我，身、受、心亦無我，中間例知（已上三科名外凡，亦名資糧位）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the outer ordinary, it is itself divided into three. First, the five methods for stilling the mind: first, for beings of much greed, the contemplation of impurity; second, for beings of much anger, the contemplation of loving-kindness and compassion; third, for beings of much distraction, the contemplation of counting the breath; fourth, for deluded beings, the contemplation of dependent origination; fifth, for beings of many hindrances, the contemplation of buddha-recollection. Second, the specific-mark mindfulness (the four foundations of mindfulness above). Third, the general-mark mindfulness: one contemplates the body as impure, and feeling, mind, and dharmas as all impure, and so on up to contemplating dharmas as without self, and body, feeling, and mind as also without self; the cases in between are known by analogy. (The above three groups are called the outer ordinary, also called the stage of accumulating provisions.)

Chinese Source

二明內凡者有四：謂煗、頂、忍、世第一（此四位為內凡，亦名加行位，又名四善根位）。上來內凡、外凡總名凡位，亦名七方便位。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, making clear the inner ordinary, which has four: warmth, summit, forbearance, and the supreme worldly [dharma] (these four stages are the inner ordinary, also called the stage of intensified effort, also called the stage of the four wholesome roots). The inner ordinary and the outer ordinary above are altogether called the ordinary stages, also called the stages of the seven expedients.

Chinese Source

次明聖位，亦分三：一見道（初果）、二修道（二三果）、三無學道（四果）。一須陀洹，此翻預流，此位斷三界八十八使見惑，見真諦故名為見道，又名聖位。二斯陀含，此云一來，此位斷欲界九品思中，斷前六品盡，後三品猶在，故更一來。三阿那含，此云不來，此位斷欲殘思盡，進斷上八地思。四阿羅漢，此云無學，又云無生，又云殺賊，又云應供，此位斷見思俱盡，子縛已斷，果縛猶在，名有餘涅槃。若灰身滅智名無餘涅槃，又名孤調解脫。略明聲聞位竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the sagely stages, also divided into three: first, the path of vision (the first fruit); second, the path of cultivation (the second and third fruits); third, the path beyond training (the fourth fruit). First, srotāpanna, rendered "entering the stream": at this stage one severs the eighty-eight agents of the delusion of views of the three realms; because one sees the truth of the real, it is called the path of vision, and also called the sagely stage. Second, sakṛdāgāmin, rendered "once-returning": at this stage, of the nine grades of habituated thought in the desire realm, one has severed the first six grades completely, while the last three grades still remain; hence one returns once more. Third, anāgāmin, rendered "non-returning": at this stage one severs the remaining habituated thought of the desire [realm] completely and proceeds to sever the habituated thought of the eight upper grounds. Fourth, arhat, rendered "beyond training," also "no [further] birth," also "killer of thieves," also "worthy of offerings": at this stage views and habituated thought are both severed completely; the bondage of the arising cause is already severed, while the bondage of the resulting body still remains; this is called nirvāṇa with residue. If the body is reduced to ash and wisdom extinguished, it is called nirvāṇa without residue, also called solitary self-tamed liberation. This concludes the brief clarification of the śrāvaka stages.

The Tripiṭaka Teaching: The One Awakened through Conditions

Chinese Source

次明緣覺，亦名獨覺。值佛出世，稟十二因緣教，所謂一無明（煩惱障、煩惱道）、二行（業障、業道，此二支屬過去）、三識（託胎一分氣息）、四名色（名是心，色是質）、五六入（六根成此胎中）、六觸（出胎）、七受（領納前境好惡等事，從識至受名現在五果）、八愛（愛色男女金銀錢物等事）、九取（凡見一切境，皆生取著心，此二未來因，皆屬煩惱，如過去無明）、十有（業已成就，是未來因，屬業道，如過去行）、十一生（未來受生事）、十二老死，此是所滅之境，與前四諦開合之異耳。云何開合？謂無明、行、愛、取、有，此之五支合為集諦，餘七支為苦諦也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the one awakened through conditions, also called the solitary awakened one. Meeting a buddha who has appeared in the world, he receives the teaching of the twelve links of dependent origination, namely: first, ignorance (the obstruction of affliction, the path of affliction); second, formations (the obstruction of karma, the path of karma; these two links belong to the past); third, consciousness (the single portion of breath on entering the womb); fourth, name-and-form (name is mind, form is substance); fifth, the six entries (the six faculties are formed in the womb); sixth, contact (leaving the womb); seventh, feeling (receiving such things as the good and bad of the objects before one; from consciousness to feeling are called the five present effects); eighth, craving (craving for such things as forms, men and women, gold, silver, money, and goods); ninth, grasping (whenever one sees any object, a mind of grasping and attachment arises; these two are causes for the future, both belonging to affliction, like ignorance in the past); tenth, existence (the karma is already accomplished, a cause for the future, belonging to the path of karma, like formations in the past); eleventh, birth (the matter of receiving birth in the future); twelfth, aging-and-death. This is the object to be extinguished; it differs from the preceding four truths only in opening and combining. How do they open and combine? Ignorance, formations, craving, grasping, and existence — these five links combine to make the truth of accumulation; the other seven links make the truth of suffering.

Chinese Source

既名異義同，何故重說？為機宜不同故。緣覺之人先觀集諦，所謂無明緣行、行緣識，乃至生緣老死，此則生起。若滅觀者，無明滅則行滅，乃至生滅則老死滅。因觀十二因緣覺真諦理，故言緣覺。言獨覺者，出無佛世，獨宿孤峯，觀物變易，自覺無生，故名獨覺。兩名不同，行位無別。此人斷三界見思，與聲聞同，更侵習氣，故居聲聞上。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the names differ but the meaning is the same, why is it taught again? Because capacities and what suits them differ. The person awakened through conditions first contemplates the truth of accumulation, namely, "ignorance conditions formations, formations condition consciousness," up to "birth conditions aging-and-death": this is [the order of] arising. In the contemplation of extinction: "when ignorance is extinguished, formations are extinguished," up to "when birth is extinguished, aging-and-death is extinguished." Because by contemplating the twelve links of dependent origination he awakens to the principle of the truth of the real, he is called "awakened through conditions." As for "solitary awakened one": appearing in an age without a buddha, dwelling alone on a solitary peak, contemplating the changes of things, he awakens by himself to non-arising; hence he is called the solitary awakened one. The two names differ, but practice and stage are not distinct. This person severs the views and habituated thought of the three realms, the same as the śrāvaka, and further encroaches on the residual habituation; hence he ranks above the śrāvaka.

The Tripiṭaka Teaching: The Bodhisattva and the Buddha

Chinese Source

次明菩薩位者，從初發心，緣四諦境，發四弘願，修六度行。一未度者令度，即眾生無邊誓願度，此緣苦諦境；二未解者令解，即煩惱無盡誓願斷，此緣集諦境；三未安者令安，即法門無量誓願學，此緣道諦境；四未得涅槃者令得涅槃，即佛道無上誓願成，此緣滅諦境。既已發心，須行行填願，於三阿僧祇劫修六度行，百劫種相好。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the stages of the bodhisattva: from the first arousing of the mind, taking the object of the four truths, he makes the four universal vows and cultivates the practice of the six pāramitās. First, "to deliver those not yet delivered," which is "Sentient beings are boundless; I vow to deliver them": this takes the object of the truth of suffering. Second, "to free those not yet freed," which is "Afflictions are inexhaustible; I vow to sever them": this takes the object of the truth of accumulation. Third, "to give peace to those not yet at peace," which is "Dharma-gates are immeasurable; I vow to study them": this takes the object of the truth of the path. Fourth, "to bring those who have not attained nirvāṇa to attain nirvāṇa," which is "The buddha-path is unsurpassed; I vow to accomplish it": this takes the object of the truth of cessation. Having aroused the mind, he must carry out practice to fulfill the vows: for three asaṃkhyeya eons he cultivates the practice of the six pāramitās, and for a hundred eons he plants the marks of physical excellence.

Chinese Source

言三阿（無）僧祇（數）劫（時）者，且約釋迦修菩薩道時論分限者：從古釋迦至尸棄佛，值七萬五千佛，名初阿僧祇，從此常離女身及四惡趣，常修六度，然自不知當作佛，若望聲聞位，即五停心、總別念處（外凡）；次從尸棄佛至然燈佛，值七萬六千佛，名第二阿僧祇，此時用七莖蓮華供養，布髮掩泥，得受記莂號釋迦文，爾時自知作佛，口未能說，若望聲聞位即煗位；次從然燈佛至毘婆尸佛，七萬七千佛，名第三阿僧祇滿，此時自知，亦向人說必當作佛，自他不疑，若望聲聞位即頂位。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the three a- (without) saṃkhyeya (number) eons (time), let us discuss their measure in terms of the time when Śākyamuni practiced the bodhisattva path. From the ancient Śākyamuni to Śikhin Buddha, he met seventy-five thousand buddhas; this is called the first asaṃkhyeya. From this point on he was always free of a female body and of the four evil destinies and always cultivated the six pāramitās, yet he himself did not know that he would become a buddha. Compared with the śrāvaka stages, this is the five methods for stilling the mind and the general and specific mindfulness (the outer ordinary). Next, from Śikhin Buddha to Dīpaṃkara Buddha, he met seventy-six thousand buddhas; this is called the second asaṃkhyeya. At this time he made offerings of seven stalks of lotus flowers and spread his hair to cover the mud, and received the prediction that he would be named Śākyamuni; at that time he himself knew he would become a buddha, but could not yet say it aloud. Compared with the śrāvaka stages, this is the stage of warmth. Next, from Dīpaṃkara Buddha to Vipaśyin Buddha, seventy-seven thousand buddhas; this is called the completion of the third asaṃkhyeya. At this time he himself knew, and also told others, that he would surely become a buddha; neither he nor others doubted it. Compared with the śrāvaka stages, this is the stage of summit.

Chinese Source

經如許時修六度竟，更住百劫種相好因，修百福成一相。福義多途，難可定判。又云：大千盲人治差為一福等。修行六度各有滿時，如尸毘王代鴿檀滿；普明王捨國尸滿；羼提仙人為歌利王割截無恨忍滿；大施太子抒海，并七日翹足讚弗沙佛進滿；尚闍黎鵲巢頂上禪滿；劬嬪大臣分閻浮提七分息諍智滿。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having completed the cultivation of the six pāramitās through so long a time, he further dwells a hundred eons planting the causes of the marks of physical excellence, cultivating a hundred merits to complete one mark. The meaning of "merit" has many lines [of interpretation] and is hard to settle. It is also said: healing the blind people of a great-thousand world counts as one merit, and so on. In cultivating the six pāramitās, each has its time of fulfillment: as when King Śibi took the place of the dove, giving was fulfilled; when King Puming gave up his kingdom, precepts were fulfilled; when the seer Kṣānti was cut apart by King Kali without resentment, patience was fulfilled; when Prince Mahādāna bailed out the sea, and when for seven days he stood on one foot praising Puṣya Buddha, effort was fulfilled; when [the seer] Shangsheli [let] a magpie nest on the top of his head, dhyāna was fulfilled; when the minister Govinda divided Jambudvīpa into seven parts and stilled the quarrel, wisdom was fulfilled.

Chinese Source

望初聲聞位是下忍位。次入補處生兜率，託胎出胎，出家降魔，安坐不動，為中忍位；次一剎那入上忍位；次一剎那入世第一位，發真無漏，三十四心頓斷見思習氣，坐木菩提樹下，生草為座，成劣應丈六身佛。受梵王請三轉法輪，度三根性，住世八十年，現老比丘相，薪盡火滅入無餘涅槃者，即三藏佛果也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Compared with the earlier śrāvaka stages, this is the stage of lower forbearance. Next, entering the position of successor, he is born in Tuṣita, enters the womb, leaves the womb, leaves home, subdues Māra, and sits at peace without moving: this is the stage of middle forbearance. Next, in one instant he enters the stage of upper forbearance; next, in one instant he enters the stage of the supreme worldly [dharma], brings forth the true uncontaminated [wisdom], and with thirty-four minds suddenly severs views and habituated thought and their residual habituation. Seated beneath the wooden bodhi tree, with fresh grass for a seat, he becomes the sixteen-foot buddha of the inferior response-body. Receiving the request of the Brahmā King, he turns the Dharma-wheel three times and delivers those of three faculty-natures; he dwells in the world eighty years, displays the form of an old bhikṣu, and when the firewood is exhausted and the fire goes out, enters nirvāṇa without residue: this is the buddha-fruit of the Tripiṭaka [teaching].

Chinese Source

上來所釋三人修行證果雖則不同，然同斷見思，同出三界，同證偏真，只行三百由旬入化城耳。略明藏教竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Though the three persons explained above differ in practice and in the realization of the fruit, they alike sever views and habituated thought, alike leave the three realms, alike realize the one-sided real, and travel only three hundred yojanas to enter the phantom city. This concludes the brief clarification of the Tripiṭaka teaching.

Lotus quotations. The destitute-son passages (units 13–16, 20) and the lines "In the buddha-lands of the ten directions…," "I set aside skillful means directly…," and "the marks of the world are constantly abiding" use the site's Lotus English ("The Parable of the Destitute Son"; "Skillful Means"). "Only for bodhisattvas, not for the Lesser Vehicle" was not found on the site's Lotus pages. The line on scholars of the Tripiṭaka (unit 25) adapts the site's "The Four Peaceful Practices."

Other quotations. The Vimalakīrti verse in unit 19 uses the site's English ("The Buddha, with a single voice…"). The Nirvāṇa milk simile (unit 13) follows the site's wording loosely, since Chegwan abridges it. The Avataṃsaka line "like the sun rising…" is a paraphrase (the sūtra speaks of the sun first lighting the great mountain kings). The Great Treatise sentence on Kātyāyanīputra was not located verbatim in T1509; the same wording appears in Zhanran's Fuxing (T1912) and the Tiantai bajiao dayi (T1930). The Yongjia Collection line (unit 3) was not checked against T2013.

Persons in the "Origin." National Teacher Shao of Xueju is Tiantai Deshao (891–972); Dharma Master Ji is Luoxi Yiji (919–987), later counted as a Tiantai patriarch; Dharma Master [Zhi]yuan of Gushan is Gushan Zhiyuan (976–1022); Master Pan of Siming is Zhipan, author of the Fozu tongji; Mengrun of Southern Tianzhu (a monastery at Hangzhou) wrote the Sijiao yi jizhu. "South Indian" is not meant.

流水十千天子 (unit 5). From the Golden Light Sūtra: the ten thousand fish rescued by Jalavāhana ("Flowing Water") were reborn as devaputras after hearing the Buddha's name.

Feng Mengzhen's preface. 𮖜遺巍𨷂 is read as "traces left at the palace gates" (巍𨷂 = 魏闕), i.e., he had left office; 機息漢陰 alludes to the old gardener of Hanyin in the Zhuangzi, who refused a labor-saving device to keep his mind free of scheming. 佛歡喜日 is normally the fifteenth day of the seventh month, which does not fit "spring"; the date is translated as written. 以一命 is read as "on an official appointment."

使 "agents." The eighty-eight 使 (anuśaya, latent afflictions that "drive" beings) are rendered "agents" to keep the image of something that impels; 利使／鈍使 "sharp agents / dull agents."

無知 vs. 無明. 染污無知 is "defiled non-knowing," kept distinct from 無明 "ignorance."

The verses (unit 1). Read as twenty seven-character lines in five quatrains. 進聞三位不知名 is read as "of the three stages beyond [the second practice], one does not even hear the names"; Alternatively: "advancing, one hears of three stages whose names [the distinct teaching] does not know."

阿（無）僧祇（數）劫（時）. The interlinear glosses explain the parts of the transliteration; they are placed after the syllables they gloss.
