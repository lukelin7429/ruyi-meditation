The Secret Maṇḍala

Part 7 carries three chapters. The tenth lays out a complete phonetic wheel of the Sanskrit alphabet — every consonant carried through the same four tonal transformations already given to the single letter A. The eleventh, this sūtra's most systematic chapter on its own institutional structure, answers dozens of practical questions in one sustained exchange: how many kinds of master and disciple there are, how ground is consecrated, how initiation and siddhi are graded, and how the maṇḍala's own deepest form is built. The twelfth, its shortest chapter, closes with a single image: burning away a lifetime's accumulated karma letter by letter, the way one flame consumes another.

The Wheel of Letters

Chapter Ten presents a complete phonetic chart of the classical Sanskrit (Siddhaṃ) syllabary, carrying every consonant through the same four tonal transformations Part 4 already gave to the single letter A: short, long, nasalized, and checked — corresponding, in this tradition's own reading, to bodhicitta, conduct, attained bodhi, and nirvāṇa respectively, now generalized across the entire alphabet rather than one letter alone.

Chinese Source (closing lines)

「祕密主！如是字門道、善巧法門次第住真言道，一切如來神力之所加持，善解正遍知道菩薩行舞，過去未來現在諸佛世尊已說當說今說。祕密主！我今普觀諸佛剎土，無不見此遍一切處法門，彼諸如來無有不宣說者。是故祕密主！若欲了知真言門修菩薩行諸菩薩，於此遍一切處法門應勤修學……如是一輪，輪轉字輪。真言者了知此故，常照世間，如大日世尊而轉法輪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Lord of Secrets, this way of letter-gates, this skillful and orderly Dharma-gate, abiding within the path of mantra, is empowered by the spiritual power of every Tathāgata; bodhisattvas who rightly understand the path of perfect awakening perform their own dance through it. Every buddha, World-Honored One, of the past has taught it, of the future will teach it, and of the present now teaches it. Lord of Secrets, I now survey every buddha-field, and there is not one where this gateway, pervading every place, cannot be found; there is no Tathāgata who has not proclaimed it. Therefore, Lord of Secrets, bodhisattvas practicing bodhisattva conduct within the gateway of mantra who wish to fully understand this should diligently cultivate and study this gateway that pervades every place…Such is this one wheel, the revolving wheel of letters. A mantra practitioner who understands this constantly illuminates the world, turning the wheel of the Dharma exactly as the World-Honored One Mahāvairocana himself does."

The Dharma-Realm's Own Body, and the King of Mantras

Chinese Source

爾時薄伽梵毘盧遮那以如來眼觀察一切法界，入於法界俱舍，以如來奮迅平等莊嚴藏三昧，以現法界無盡嚴故，以是真言行門度無餘眾生界，滿足本願故。時佛在三昧中，於如是無盡眾生界，從眾聲門出隨類音聲，如其本性、業生成熟、受用果報，顯形諸色、種種語言、心所思念而為說法，令一切眾生皆得歡喜。復於一一毛孔，法界增身出現，出已等同虛空，於無量世界中以一音聲法界語表演說如來發生偈：

「能生隨類形，　諸法之法相，諸佛與聲聞，　救世因緣覺，勤勇菩薩眾，　及仁尊亦然。眾生器世界，　次第而成立，生住等諸法，　常恒如是生。由具智方便，　離於無慧疑，而觀此道故，　諸正遍知說。」

爾時法界生如來身，一切法界自身表化雲遍滿毘盧遮那世尊，纔生心頃，諸毛孔中出無量佛，展轉加持已還入法界宮中。於是大日世尊復告持金剛祕密主言：「祕密主！有造漫荼羅聖尊分位種子幖幟。汝當諦聽，善思念之，吾今演說。」時薄伽梵以偈頌曰：

「真言者圓壇，　先置於自體，自足而至臍，　成大金剛輪，從此而至心，　當思惟水輪，水輪上火輪，　火輪上風輪，次應念持地，　而圖眾形像。」

爾時金剛手昇於大日世尊身語意地法平等觀，念彼未來眾生，為斷一切疑故，說大真言王曰：

「南麼三曼多勃馱喃(一)　阿三忙鉢多達摩馱覩(二)　蘖登蘖哆喃(三)　薩婆他(四)　暗欠暗噁(五)　糝索(六)　含鶴(七)　㘕落(八)　鑁嚩(九)　莎訶[合*牛](十)　㘕落訶囉鶴(十一)　莎訶㘕落(十二)　莎訶(十三)」

持金剛祕密主說此真言王已，時一切如來住十方世界，各舒右手摩執金剛頂，以善哉聲而稱歎言：「善哉善哉佛子！汝已超昇毘盧遮那世尊身語意地，為欲照明一切方所，住平等真言道諸菩薩故說此真言王。何以故？毘盧遮那世尊、應、正等覺坐菩提座，觀十二句法界，降伏四魔，此法界生，三處流出，破壞天魔軍眾；次得世尊身語意平等，身量等同虛空，語意量亦如是；逮得無邊智生，於一切法自在而演說法，所謂此十二句真言之王。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the Bhagavān Mahāvairocana, surveying the entire dharma-realm with the Tathāgata's own eye, entered the treasury of the dharma-realm, and, through the samādhi of the Tathāgata's vigorous, equally adorned treasury, manifesting the dharma-realm's own inexhaustible adornment, liberated the boundless realm of beings through this very gateway of mantra conduct, so as to fulfill his ancient vow. While the Buddha remained in this samādhi, from every sound-gate throughout this boundless realm of beings, sounds appropriate to each being's own kind emerged, according to their own nature, their karma's ripening, and the fruits they experience, displaying every form, every language, and teaching the Dharma exactly as each being's own mind conceived it, so that every being was brought to joy. And from every single pore, a body that increases the dharma-realm itself appeared, and, once manifest, became equal to space; and, throughout measureless world-systems, with a single sound, an utterance of the dharma-realm itself, he proclaimed this verse on the Tathāgata's own arising:

"What gives rise to forms suited to every kind, and to the very marks of every dharma, is the same for every buddha and śrāvaka, every pratyekabuddha, savior of the world, every courageous bodhisattva, and every benevolent honored one alike. The receptacle-world of beings is established in due sequence; arising, abiding, and every other such dharma is ever and constantly born in just this way. Through possessing wisdom's skillful means, free of the doubt born of no wisdom, and by contemplating this very path, every one who is perfectly awakened has taught this."

At that time, the Tathāgata's own body born of the dharma-realm, its display of transformation-clouds filling the whole dharma-realm, entirely suffused the World-Honored One Mahāvairocana; and in a single instant of mind, measureless buddhas emerged from every pore, empowering one another in turn, and returned again into the dharma-realm's own palace. Then the World-Honored One Mahāvairocana said again to Vajrapāṇi, Lord of Secrets: "Lord of Secrets, there are seed-syllable emblems marking the stations of the holy ones who form the maṇḍala. Listen closely now, and reflect on this well — I shall now proclaim it." The Bhagavān then spoke this verse:

"The mantra practitioner, in constructing the round altar, should first place it upon their own body: from the feet up to the navel forms the great vajra wheel; from there up to the heart, contemplate the water wheel; above the water wheel, the fire wheel; above the fire wheel, the wind wheel; and next hold the earth in mind, and paint every image accordingly."

At that time Vajrapāṇi, ascending to the equal contemplation of Mahāvairocana's own ground of body, speech, and mind, and mindful of beings yet to come, spoke this great king of mantras so as to cut off every doubt:

(a twelve-part formula built from the seed-syllables already introduced across this collection — the four transformations of A, and the letters SA, HA, LA/RA, and VA of the five-element system — retained here in transliteration; see the note below)

Once Vajrapāṇi, Lord of Secrets, had spoken this king of mantras, every Tathāgata throughout the ten directions extended their right hand and stroked the crown of his head, praising him with the sound "excellent": "Excellent, excellent, child of the Buddha! You have now ascended to the very ground of Mahāvairocana's own body, speech, and mind — and, wishing to illuminate every direction for the bodhisattvas who abide in the equal path of mantra, you have spoken this king of mantras. Why is this so? The World-Honored One Mahāvairocana, the Worthy One, the Perfectly Awakened One, seated at the seat of bodhi, contemplated the dharma-realm through these twelve phrases, and subdued the four Māras; this very body born of the dharma-realm flowed forth from three places, and shattered the armies of the heavenly Māra. He then attained the equality of body, speech, and mind, his body's own measure equal to space, and the measure of his speech and mind likewise; he attained boundless wisdom's own arising, and, sovereign over every dharma, expounded the Dharma — namely, this very king of mantras, these twelve phrases."

Reading note

The "twelve-part king of mantras" above is left in transliteration rather than fully reconstructed, in keeping with this site's practice for the sūtra's most syllabically dense formulas — but its structure is worth noting even without a complete Sanskrit rendering: it is visibly built by stringing together several of the seed-syllables this collection has already introduced individually (the four transformations of A from Part 4, and SA, HA, and forms of RA and VA from the five-element system of Part 5), combined here into a single continuous formula rather than kept as separate one-syllable "hearts." That every Tathāgata throughout the ten directions responds by physically touching Vajrapāṇi's head marks this as one of the sūtra's highest honors given to any figure other than Mahāvairocana himself — a gesture of consecration in its own right, mirroring the initiation rite Part 3 already described a human master conferring on a human disciple, now performed by every buddha at once upon the very interlocutor whose questions have structured this entire sūtra since its first chapter.

Vajrapāṇi's Question

Vajrapāṇi now asks, in one sustained verse, nearly every practical question this sūtra has not yet directly answered — a working table of contents for its own remaining institutional structure, ranging from the number of kinds of teacher and disciple down to the timing of the fire rite.

Chinese Source (selected)

「已斷一切疑，　種智離熱惱，我為眾生故，　請問於導師。曼荼羅何先？　惟大牟尼說。阿闍梨有幾？　弟子復幾種？云何知地相？　云何而擇治？云何當作淨？　云何彼堅住，及淨諸弟子？　惟願導師說。云何已淨相？　以何而作護？云何加持地？　事業誰為初？修多羅有幾？　云何作地分？幾種修供養？　云何花香等，此華當獻誰？　香亦復如是，云何而奉獻？　應以何花香？諸食與護摩，　各以何軌儀？及諸聖天座，　願說此教法。……灌頂復幾種？　三摩耶有幾？真言者幾時，　勤修真言行，當具菩薩道？　云何見真諦？悉地有幾種？　及與成就時……幾種護摩火，　幾事而增威？諸佛差別性，　唯願導師說。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Every doubt now cut away, wisdom of every kind free of every affliction — for the sake of beings, I question the guide. What comes first in the maṇḍala? I beg only that the Great Silent Sage explain it. How many kinds of master are there? And how many kinds of disciple? How is the nature of the ground known? And how is it chosen and worked? How should it be purified? How does it become firmly settled, and how are disciples purified? I beg only that the guide explain this. What is the mark of a ground already purified? And with what is it protected? How is the ground empowered — and what task comes first? How many threads are there, and how are the ground's divisions made? How many kinds of offering are cultivated? And of flowers, incense, and the rest — to whom should these flowers be offered, and incense likewise — how are they to be presented, and with what flowers and incense? And of food, and the fire offering — with what rite is each performed? And of the seats of every holy deity — I beg you to teach this method as well…How many kinds of initiation are there? And how many kinds of samaya? For how long should a mantra practitioner diligently cultivate mantra conduct, before the bodhisattva path is complete? How does one see the true reality? How many kinds of siddhi are there, and when is each accomplished?…How many kinds of fire offering are there, and by what means is their power increased? I beg only that the guide explain the distinct nature of every buddha."

Two Kinds of Master, and Four Kinds of Disciple

Chinese Source

「阿闍梨有二，　通達印真言，彼相亦如是，　深祕顯略分。能知深廣義，　可傳者方授，正覺之長子，　遠離於世樂。第二求現法，　深著癡攀緣，世間漫荼羅，　一切為斯作。諸佛二足尊，　灌頂傳教者，說四種弟子，　時非時差別。一者時念誦，　非時俱非俱，具有一切相，　佛說親弟子。最初知地相，　即所謂心地，我已說作淨，　如前修事業。……應當為弟子，　而淨菩提心，護以不動尊，　或用降三世。……初加持是地，　依於諸佛教，第二心自在，　唯此非餘教。四種蘇多羅，　謂白黃赤黑，第五所應念，　所謂虛空色，空中而等持，　印定漫荼羅。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"There are two kinds of master. Both have penetrated seal and mantra alike; their outward manner may look the same, yet one holds the profound and secret sense, the other only its plain and general outline. One who is able to know the profound and vast meaning is the one to whom the teaching should be entrusted — the true eldest child of the Perfectly Awakened One, far removed from worldly pleasure. The second seeks only visible results in the present, deeply attached to and grasping after delusion; every worldly maṇḍala is made by such a one alone. The buddhas, most honored among the two-footed, who confer initiation and transmit the teaching, speak of four kinds of disciple, distinguished by timing and its absence: the first practices recitation at the proper time; the second, at no fixed time; the third, both at once; and the fourth, possessed of every mark at once — this the Buddha calls the true, intimate disciple. The very first thing to be known is the nature of the ground — that is, the ground of the mind itself, which I have already explained how to purify, cultivating it as described before…One should, for the disciple's sake, purify their bodhicitta, protecting them with Acala, or else with Trailokyavijaya…The first empowerment of this ground rests on the teaching of every buddha; the second, on the mind's own sovereignty — this alone, and no other teaching. There are four kinds of thread — white, yellow, red, and black — and a fifth to be held in mind, namely the color of space itself; held in equipoise within that very space, the maṇḍala is sealed and fixed in place."

Reading note

The distinction drawn here between two kinds of master — one who transmits "the profound and secret sense," the other only "its plain and general outline" — restates, in institutional terms, the two-tier reading method (淺略釋 and 深祕釋, the plain and the profound reading) this collection's own translator's notes have already flagged repeatedly as Yixing's standing interpretive practice throughout his commentary: a passage that can be taken at face value as ritual instruction, and the same passage read again for the doctrinal claim encoded within it. That the sūtra locates this same duality in the masters themselves, not only in how a text may be read, makes explicit what has been implicit throughout this collection — a teacher's own depth of realization, not merely their technical command of mudrā and mantra, determines which of the two readings a given disciple actually receives.

Offerings, Homa, and Three Grades of Initiation

Chinese Source

「供養有四種，　謂作禮合掌，并及慈悲等。　世間與華香，從手發生花，　奉諸救世者，結支分生印，　而觀菩提心。……護摩有二種，　所謂內及外。業生得解脫，　復有芽種生，以能燒業故，　說為內護摩。外用有三位，　三位三中住，成就三業道，　世間勝護摩。……灌頂有三種，　佛子至心聽。若祕印方便，　則離於作業，是名初勝法，　如來所灌頂；所謂第二者，　令起作眾事；第三以心授，　悉離於時方。令尊歡喜故，　如所說應作，現前佛灌頂，　是則最殊勝。正等覺略說，　五種三昧耶，初見漫荼羅，　具足三昧耶，未傳真實語，　不授彼密印。第二三昧耶，　入覩聖天會。第三具壇印，　隨教修妙業，復次許傳教，　說具三昧耶。雖具印壇位，　如教之所說，未逮心灌頂，　祕密慧不生。是故真言者，　祕密道場中，具第五要誓，　隨法應灌頂，當知異此者，　非名三昧耶。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"There are four kinds of offering: bowing and joining the palms; loving-kindness and compassion held in mind; worldly offerings of flowers and incense; and flowers made to arise from one's own hand, offered to every savior of the world, forming the seal of the arising of every limb, while contemplating bodhicitta…There are two kinds of fire offering, called inner and outer. Liberation from karmic birth, and the arising of the seed and sprout — because these burn away karma itself, this is called the inner fire offering. The outer offering has three stages, three positions abiding within three, accomplishing the paths of the three karmas — this is the supreme fire offering of the world…There are three kinds of initiation, children of the Buddha — listen with full attention. Should one attain it through the skillful means of the secret seal alone, free of any ritual action, this is called the first, most excellent method, the initiation of the Tathāgata himself. What is called the second causes one to rise and carry out every task. The third is conferred through the mind alone, entirely free of time and place. Causing the honored one's own joy, and acting exactly as taught, an initiation given directly, in person, before a buddha, is the most excellent of all. The Perfectly Awakened One, in brief, speaks of five kinds of samaya. The first samaya: seeing the maṇḍala for the first time, complete in itself; the true and actual word not yet transmitted, its secret seal is not yet conferred. The second samaya: entering and beholding the assembly of holy ones. The third: possessing the altar's own seal, cultivating wondrous conduct as taught; and, further, being permitted to transmit the teaching, this is spoken of as the samaya complete. Though one may possess the seal and the altar's own station, exactly as taught, if the initiation of the mind has not yet been reached, secret wisdom does not arise. Therefore, a mantra practitioner within the secret place of practice, possessing this fifth, essential vow, should be initiated in accordance with the Dharma — know that anything other than this cannot rightly be called samaya."

Reading note

The five-stage samaya sequence given here — first seeing the maṇḍala, then beholding its assembly of holy ones, then holding its seal and undertaking its conduct, then receiving permission to teach, and only at the fifth stage receiving "the initiation of the mind" itself — clarifies a distinction this collection's Part 3 initiation rite left implicit: the elaborate consecration ceremony already translated there, with its mirror, golden probe, dharma-wheel, and conch, corresponds to roughly the third and fourth of these five stages — full ritual initiation, and permission to transmit the teaching to others — while this passage insists that a further transmission, "through the mind alone, entirely free of time and place," remains distinct even from that formal consecration, and that without it, "secret wisdom does not arise" no matter how complete the outward rite. The text is explicit that this fifth stage is not merely the most advanced item on a checklist but the condition without which everything preceding it, however correctly performed, does not yet count as samaya in the fullest sense.

Seeing the Truth, and Five Kinds of Siddhi

Chinese Source

「於諸法本寂，　常無自性中，安住如須彌，　是名為見諦。此空即實際，　非虛妄言說，所見猶如佛，　先佛如是見。逮得菩提心，　悉地最無上，從此有五種，　諸悉地差別。所謂入修行，　及勝進諸地，世間五神通，　諸佛緣覺等。修業無間息，　乃至心續淨，未熟令成熟，　爾時悉地成。……悉地昇空界，　如幻無畏者，呪術網所惑，　同於帝釋網，如乾闥婆城，　所有諸人民。身祕密如是，　非身亦非識，又如於睡夢，　而遊諸天宮，不捨於此身，　亦不至於彼。……真言如意珠，　出生意語身，隨念雨眾物，　而無分別想。猶十方虛空，　離諸有為行，真言者不染，　一切分別行。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Abiding, unmoving as Mount Sumeru, within every dharma's own fundamental stillness, its ever-constant lack of any nature of its own — this is called seeing the truth. This very emptiness is reality's own limit, no mere idle claim; what is seen is exactly as a buddha sees it — the buddhas before have seen it in just this way. Once bodhicitta is attained, siddhi, most unsurpassed of all, follows from it in five distinct kinds: namely, entry into practice, advancement through the grounds, the five worldly superknowledges, and what belongs to buddhas and to pratyekabuddhas. Cultivating this practice without interruption, until the mind's own continuity is purified, what was not yet ripe is brought to ripeness — and at that moment, siddhi is accomplished…Siddhi rises into the realm of space, like an illusion, fearless, as though caught in a web of enchantment identical to Indra's own net, or like the people who dwell within a city of gandharvas. The secret of the body is exactly this — neither the body itself, nor consciousness — just as, in sleep and dream, one may roam through the palaces of the gods without ever leaving this body, and yet without ever truly arriving there either…The mantra, like a wish-fulfilling jewel, gives rise to a body of mind and speech, raining down every object according to one's own thought, and yet without any discriminating notion attached to it. Like space itself in every direction, free of every conditioned action, the mantra practitioner is undefiled by any such discrimination."

Reading note

The comparison of siddhi to "a city of gandharvas" and to being "caught in a web of enchantment identical to Indra's own net" deliberately recalls the ten similes for mantra practice given in Part 1 — illusion, mirage, dream, and the rest — applying the same guardrail there to the actual attainment of siddhi that Part 1 applied to mantra recitation itself: a genuine, functioning power that nonetheless has no fixed, independently existing substance behind it. The body described here as able to "roam through the palaces of the gods without ever leaving this body, and yet without ever truly arriving there either" gives this sūtra's clearest positive statement of what later Shingon teaching would call sokushin jōbutsu, "attaining buddhahood in this very body" — not a departure from the physical body into some other, purer vehicle, but the very same body, unmoved, becoming the site where every attainment already described across this collection actually takes place.

The Secret Maṇḍala's Own Forms

The Buddha closes this chapter with a further, compressed account of the maṇḍala's own architecture — Mahāvairocana's central eight-petal court, Avalokiteśvara's own secret maṇḍala, the vajra family's, Śākyamuni's, Acala's and Trailokyavijaya's, the Buddha-mother's, and Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin's, Kṣitigarbha's, and Ākāśagarbha's — each described through its own colors, seed-syllables, and specific implements, largely restating and further compressing the full iconography already given in Parts 2, 5, and 6.

Chinese Source (opening lines)

「最初正等覺，　敷置漫荼羅，密中之祕密，　大悲胎藏生，及無量世間，　出世漫荼羅，彼所有圖像，　次第說當聽。四方普周匝，　一門及通道，金剛印遍嚴，　中羯磨金剛。其上妙蓮華，　開敷含果實，於彼大蓮印，　大空點莊嚴。八葉悉圓正，　善好具鬚蘂，十二支生句，　普遍華臺中。其上兩足尊，　導師成正覺，以入漫荼羅，　眷屬自圍繞。當知此最初，　悲生漫荼羅，從此流諸壇，　各如其本教，事業形悉地，　安置諸佛子。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"That which the Perfectly Awakened One first laid out, this maṇḍala — the very secret within the secret, born of great compassion's own matrix — together with every worldly and transcendent maṇḍala without measure, and every image it bears: listen now, as I explain them in due order. On every side, a single gate and passageway all around, adorned throughout with the vajra seal, and, at the center, the vajra of ritual action. Above it, a wondrous lotus, open in bloom and holding its fruit within; upon that great lotus-seal, adorned with the point of great emptiness. Its eight petals, perfectly round and complete, finely formed with filament and pistil, the twelve-part phrase of arising pervading the whole lotus dais. And above it, the one most honored among the two-footed, the guide who accomplished perfect awakening, entering the maṇḍala, surrounded by his own retinue. Know that this is the very first, the maṇḍala born of compassion, from which every other altar flows, each according to its own teaching, its ritual actions, its forms, and its siddhi, where every child of the Buddha is placed."

The chapter proceeds through the same sequence in compressed form — Avalokiteśvara's own maṇḍala marked by a conch and lotus at its center; a second, wind-element altar for Trailokyavijaya and the vajra family, marked with the seed-syllable HŪṂ; Buddhalocanā's maṇḍala; Śākyamuni's own altar with the five and three buddha-crowns; Acala's beneath Mahāvairocana in the southwest; and, in the sūtra's closing turn to the third court, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin's maṇḍala in the south, Kṣitigarbha's in the north, and Ākāśagarbha's in the west — each marked, as throughout this sūtra, by its own color, seed-syllable, and implement, before the Buddha closes the chapter simply: "略說佛祕藏，諸尊密印竟" — "Thus, in brief, the Buddha's secret treasury, and the secret seals of every holy one, are now complete."

Reading note

This chapter's title — 祕密漫荼羅品, "the chapter of the secret maṇḍala" — names something more specific than a further layer of ritual detail: where Parts 2 and 6 gave the Womb Maṇḍala's full iconography and the hand-seal for each of its figures, and Part 5 gave a compressed, letter-only version of the same structure, this chapter adds a third register again, describing each figure's own maṇḍala as a nested world unto itself, generated outward from that figure's own seed-syllable the way the whole Womb Maṇḍala is generated from Mahāvairocana's. The pattern already noted in Part 2's reading note on the third court — each great bodhisattva surrounded by a smaller maṇḍala mirroring the whole — is here made explicit as the sūtra's own general principle: no boundary marks where "the" maṇḍala ends and a subordinate figure's own maṇḍala begins, since each is, in its own right, a complete instance of the same single structure at a different scale.

Entering the Secret Maṇḍala

Chinese Source

爾時世尊又復宣說入祕密漫荼羅法優陀那曰：
「真言遍學者，　通達祕密壇，如法為弟子，　燒盡一切罪。壽命悉焚滅，　令彼不復生，同於灰燼已，　彼壽命還復。謂以字燒字，　因字而更生，一切壽及生，　清淨遍無垢。以十二支句，　而作於彼器，如是三昧耶，　一切諸如來，菩薩救世者，　及佛聲聞眾，乃至諸世間，　平等不違逆。解此平等誓，　祕密漫荼羅，入一切法教，　諸壇得自在。我身等同彼，　真言者亦然，以不相異故，　說名三昧耶。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One further proclaimed this udāna verse on the method of entering the secret maṇḍala:
"A mantra practitioner who has studied fully, penetrating the secret altar, and, in accordance with the teaching, acts on the disciple's behalf, burns away every offense entirely. Their whole karmic lifespan is burned to nothing, so that it no longer arises again — and, once reduced to ash in this way, that very lifespan is restored once more. This is called burning letter with letter: by means of a letter, one is born anew — every lifespan, every birth, made pure, and stainless throughout. Through the twelve-part phrase, this vessel is fashioned; and this samaya is shared alike by every Tathāgata, every bodhisattva, savior of the world, every buddha and śrāvaka, and indeed by the whole world, in perfect equality, contradicting nothing. Whoever understands this vow of equality, this secret maṇḍala, enters every teaching of the Dharma, and attains mastery over every altar there is. My own body is equal to theirs, and so too is the mantra practitioner's — and because there is no difference between them, this is called samaya."

Reading note

"Burning letter with letter" gives this short chapter its central image: a lifetime's accumulated karma is not simply erased but burned to ash by the letters of mantra, and then, from that same ash, restored — a rebirth rather than a mere cancellation, echoing the phoenix-like structure Part 1 already gave to the practitioner who "leaves behind" the sixty ordinary minds without those minds ever having existed as something separate to discard in the first place. The chapter's closing claim — that Mahāvairocana's own body, the mantra practitioner's body, and, by implication, the body of "every buddha and śrāvaka, and indeed the whole world" are all equal under this same samaya — states as plainly as this sūtra ever does the non-duality between practitioner and Buddha this entire collection has been building toward since Part 1's opening formula of bodhicitta, compassion, and skillful means.

Part 7 Complete

This closes Part 7: a complete phonetic wheel of the Sanskrit alphabet, the twelve-part king of mantras that earns Vajrapāṇi the touch of every buddha's hand, two kinds of master and four kinds of disciple, three grades of initiation and five stages of samaya, five kinds of siddhi culminating in a body that can "roam the palaces of the gods without ever leaving this body," the maṇḍala's own nested, self-similar architecture, and a closing image of karma burned to ash and reborn. Part 8 turns to six shorter chapters — 祕密八印品第十四 through 布字品第十七 — on eight further secret seals, the vows and precepts a vidyā-holder takes, and the placement of letters upon the body.
