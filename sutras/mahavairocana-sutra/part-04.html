Obstacles, and a Treasury of Mantras

Part 4 carries three shorter chapters. The third chapter answers a practical question the sūtra has not yet addressed: what does a practitioner actually do when obstacles — a sudden storm, a demon, sheer fear — arise in the middle of practice? The fourth gathers, in one place, the mantra spoken by every buddha and bodhisattva named in Part 2's maṇḍala, followed by the single-syllable "heart" of each. The fifth opens the sūtra's turn from doctrine and iconography toward the mechanics of a working recitation practice.

Obstacles, and How to Remove Them

Chinese Source

爾時金剛手又復請問毘盧遮那世尊，而說偈言：
「云何道場時，　淨除諸障者，修真言行人，　無能為惱害？云何持真言？　云何彼成果？」
如是發問已，　大日尊歎言：「善哉摩訶薩，　快說如是語，隨汝心所問，　今當悉開示。障者自心生，　隨順昔慳悋，為除彼因故，　念此菩提心。善除妄分別，　從心思所生，憶念菩提心，　行者離諸過。常當意思惟，　不動摩訶薩，而結彼密印，　能除諸障礙。祕密主復聽，　繫除散亂風，阿字為我體，　心持阿字門，健陀以塗地，　而作大空點，依於嚩庾方，　闔以捨囉梵，思念於彼器，　大心彌盧山，時時在其上。　阿字大空點，先佛所宣說，　能縛於大風。大有情諦聽，　行者防駛雨，思惟囉字門，　大力火光色，威猛熾焰鬘，　忿怒持遏伽，隨所起方分，　治地興蔭雲，斷以慧刀印，　昏蔽尋消散。行者無畏心，　或作葪羅劍，以是金剛橛，　一切同金剛。復次今當說，　息一切諸障，念真言大猛，　不動大力者，住本漫荼羅，　行者或居中，而觀彼形像，　頂戴三昧足，彼障當淨除，　息滅而不生。或以羅邇迦，　微妙共和合，行者造形像，　而以塗其身，彼諸執著者，　由斯對治故，彼諸根熾然，　勿生疑惑心；乃至釋梵尊，　不順我教故，尚當為所焚，　況復餘眾生。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Vajrapāṇi again questioned the World-Honored One Mahāvairocana, speaking this verse:
"How, at the time of practice, is every obstacle purified and cleared away, so that a mantra practitioner suffers no harm or affliction? How should mantra be upheld? And how does its fruit come about?"
Having been thus asked, the World-Honored One Mahāvairocana praised him, saying: "Excellent, great being, well have you spoken these words; according to what your heart has asked, I shall now disclose it in full. An obstacle is born from one's own mind, following an old habit of stinginess; to remove its very cause, hold bodhicitta in mind. Skillfully removing false discrimination, which arises from the mind's own thinking, and holding bodhicitta in mind, the practitioner leaves every fault behind. One should constantly, in the mind, contemplate the great being Acala, and, forming his secret seal, thereby remove every obstacle. Lord of Secrets, listen further: to bind and remove the wind of distraction, take the letter A as your own body, hold the gateway of the letter A in mind, coat the ground with fragrant paste, and form there the point of great emptiness; relying on the direction of wind, close it with the syllable HA, and hold in mind that very vessel — the great mind's own Mount Sumeru, ever present above it. The letter A and the point of great emptiness, as the buddhas of old have taught, are able to bind even the great wind. Great being, listen closely: to guard against a driving rain, contemplate the gateway of the letter RA, the color of great and mighty flame, its blazing garland fierce and wrathful, wrathfully holding argha water; wherever the rain arises, work the ground and raise a shading cloud, and cut it off with the seal of the wisdom-sword — the obscuring gloom will at once disperse. With a fearless mind, the practitioner may also fashion a kīla peg, sharp as a sword, using it as a vajra stake, and everything becomes, thereby, one with the vajra itself. Furthermore, I shall now explain how to still every obstacle entirely: hold in mind the mantra of the greatly fierce, immovable, mighty one, and, dwelling within one's own maṇḍala, the practitioner, whether seated at its center or elsewhere, should contemplate that very image, crowning the head with the samaya-seal of his very feet — every obstacle will thereupon be purified, stilled, and will no longer arise. Or, using orpiment finely blended together, the practitioner may fashion his image, and anoint the body with it; every one of those who grasp and cling, on account of this very antidote, will have their own faculties set ablaze — give rise to no doubt about this; for even Śakra and Brahmā themselves, should they fail to follow my teaching, would still be burned by it, how much more so any other being."

Reading note

This chapter names the actual root of "obstacle" (障) as something interior rather than circumstantial — "an obstacle is born from one's own mind, following an old habit of stinginess" — before turning, in the very next breath, to a wind-storm and a driving rain as the concrete obstacles a practitioner is instructed to counter. The two registers are not in tension on this text's own logic: the letter-gate teaching of Part 3 has already established that A names what is fundamentally unborn and RA names freedom from every defiling dust, so contemplating these same syllables against a literal storm is continuous with, not a departure from, contemplating them against the subtler storm of one's own grasping. Acala — "the Immovable One," already given his full iconography in Part 2's central court, sword and lasso in hand, seated on bare rock — is invoked here in his most practical role: the deity a practitioner turns to first when practice itself is disrupted, whether by weather, by fear, or by an obstructing spirit. This is also the origin, within this sūtra, of the kīla or phurba, the ritual peg later esoteric traditions developed into an entire class of wrathful implement in its own right — introduced here in its simplest form, a stake driven into the ground to anchor a maṇḍala against disruption.

A Treasury of Mantras

Every bodhisattva named in Part 2's maṇḍala now speaks, in turn, their own mantra — the sūtra's own working reference collection, gathered in one place. The Buddha closes the chapter by naming what every one of these mantras, without exception, actually is: the letter A.

「祕密主！是等一切真言我已宣說。是中一切真言之心，汝當諦聽，所謂阿字門。念此一切諸真言心最為無上，是一切真言所住，於此真言而得決定。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Lord of Secrets, I have now proclaimed every one of these mantras. Listen closely to what is the very heart within every one of them: it is the gateway of the letter A. To hold this heart of every mantra in mind is the most supreme thing of all — it is what every mantra rests upon, and it is in this that certainty regarding mantra is found."

Reading note

The four-syllable sequence A, Ā, AṂ, AḤ, opening this chapter's single-syllable list under the headings "bodhi," "conduct," "attained bodhi," and "nirvāṇa," became one of the most widely used formulas in the later Japanese Shingon tradition, still taught today as the "four transformations" (四転) — a single vowel lengthened, then closed with an anusvāra, then closed with a visarga, tracing the entire path from the arousing of bodhicitta to nirvāṇa within four modulations of a single sound. That the sūtra ends its treasury of dozens of elaborate, multi-syllable mantras by reducing every one of them back to this single letter is not a contradiction of the elaborate deity-specific formulas above it, but this chapter's own explicit thesis, stated plainly in its closing line: however many mantras a practitioner learns, their "heart," without exception, is the same syllable this collection's Part 3 already identified as the seed from which the entire forty-two-gate alphabet unfolds. Several of these single-syllable identifications resist confident phonetic reconstruction given the terseness of the source notation; where a reading is reasonably secure this page gives it, and elsewhere renders the transliteration as conservatively as the text allows.

Beginning the Recitation Practice

Chinese Source

爾時世尊復告執金剛祕密主而說偈言：
「如真言教法，　成就於彼果，當字字相應，　句句亦如是，作心想念誦，　善住一洛叉。初字菩提心，　第二名為聲，句想為本尊，　而於自處作。第二句當知，　即諸佛勝句，行者觀住彼，　極圓淨月輪，於中諦誠想，　諸字如次第，中置字句等，　而想淨其命，命者所謂風，　念隨出入息，彼等淨除已，　作先持誦法，善住真言者，　次一月念誦，行者前方便，　一一句通達，諸佛大名稱，　說此先受持。次當隨所有，　奉塗香花等，為成正覺故，　迴向自菩提。如是於兩月，　真言當無畏。次滿此月已，　行者入持誦，山峯或牛欄，　及諸河潬等，四衢道一室，　神室大天室。彼漫茶羅處，　悉如金剛宮，是處而結護，　行者作成就。即以中夜分，　或於日出時，智者應當知，　有如是相現，[合*牛]聲或鼓音，　若復地震動，及聞虛空中，　有悅意言辭，應知如是相，　悉地總如意。諸佛兩足尊，　宣說於彼果，住是真言行，　必定當成佛，應一切種類，　常念持真言，古佛大仙說，　故應當憶念。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One said again to Vajrapāṇi in verse:
"As with the teaching of mantra, so with the accomplishment of its fruit: one should attune oneself, letter by letter, and phrase by phrase, in just the same way; making the mind's own visualization, one recites, resting well at one hundred thousand repetitions. The first letter is bodhicitta; the second is called sound; the visualized phrase is the deity contemplated (本尊), made present in its own proper place. Know that the second phrase is itself the supreme phrase of every buddha: the practitioner contemplates and dwells within it, a perfectly round and pure disc of the moon, and, sincerely visualizing within it, the letters arranged in due sequence, placing the syllable at its center, contemplates purifying the very life-force there — that life-force being the breath, held in mind as it moves in and out. Once these have been purified, one takes up the prior method of recitation; one who well abides in mantra should then recite for one full month, the practitioner's own prior skillful means penetrating each and every phrase, upheld first under the name of every buddha's own great renown. One should next, with whatever one has, make offering of scented paste and flowers, dedicating the merit toward one's own bodhi, for the sake of accomplishing true awakening. In this way, over two months, one becomes fearless in mantra. Once this second month is complete, the practitioner enters into recitation proper — upon a mountain peak, or in a cattle enclosure, or at a riverbank or sandbar, at a crossroads, in a solitary chamber, or in the hall of a spirit or a great deity. That maṇḍala site becomes, in every respect, a vajra palace; there, having bound and protected the place, the practitioner brings about accomplishment. Then, in the middle of the night, or at sunrise, the wise should know that signs such as these will appear: the sound HŪṂ, or the sound of a drum, or the earth itself may shake, and one may hear, from within the sky, words spoken in a pleasing voice — know that signs such as these mean that siddhi has been attained, exactly as wished. The buddhas, most honored among the two-footed, proclaim this very fruit: whoever abides in this mantra conduct will certainly attain buddhahood. One should, according to every kind of being, constantly hold mantra in mind and uphold it — this the buddhas and great sages of old have taught, and so it should be held always in remembrance."

Reading note

This closing chapter of Part 4 marks the sūtra's own turn from doctrine and iconography toward a working practice schedule: a full month of preparatory recitation, purifying the visualized "life-force" of the breath moving through a mantra's syllables set upon a moon-disc at the heart, followed by a second month of dedicated practice, and only then a move to an actual retreat site — a mountain, a riverbank, a solitary room — where the site itself, once ritually bound, becomes "in every respect, a vajra palace," the same status Part 2 gave the maṇḍala proper. The specific signs of accomplishment named here — the seed-syllable HŪṂ heard aloud, a drum sound with no drum present, the earth shaking, a voice from empty space — recur as a stock vocabulary across the sūtra's remaining chapters, and mark the same category of confirming sign this collection's Part 2 already discussed in connection with a disciple's favorable initiation dreams: not private reassurance alone, but evidence a practitioner is meant to actually notice and trust.

Part 4 Complete

This closes Part 4: Acala invoked against storm, fear, and the deeper obstacle of one's own stinginess; a full treasury of mantras spoken by every figure in the Womb Maṇḍala, reduced at last to a single syllable each; and the opening of a working recitation schedule — two months of preparation before a practitioner ever sets out for an actual retreat site. Part 5 continues with three further chapters: 悉地出現品第六, on how siddhi actually manifests; 轉字輪漫荼羅行品第八 by way of 成就悉地品第七, on the maṇḍala of the revolving wheel of letters.
