The Five Syllables of Earth, Water, Fire, Wind, and Space

Part 5 carries three chapters on how siddhi — spiritual accomplishment — actually manifests. At its center is one of this sūtra's most consequential teachings: a five-syllable visualization mapping A, VA, RA, HA, and KHA onto the navel, heart, throat, forehead, and crown of the practitioner's own body, each syllable identified with one of the five elements. This is the direct scriptural source of the five-tiered stūpa (gorintō) still built across Japan today, and of the "A-syllable contemplation" (阿字観) that remains central to Shingon meditation practice.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 5 of 11 complete Source: Taishō T0848, 大毘盧遮那成佛神變加持經 (tr. Śubhakarasiṃha and Yixing) Reading notes after Yixing's 大毘盧遮那成佛經疏 (T1796)

The Universal Sound of Awakening

Chinese Source

爾時世尊復觀諸大眾會，為欲滿足一切願故，復說三世無量門決定智圓滿法句：
「虛空無垢無自性，　能授種種諸巧智，由本自性常空故，　緣起甚深難可見，於長恒時殊勝進，　隨念施與無上果。譬如一切趣宮室，　雖依虛空無著行，此清淨法亦如是，　三有無餘清淨生，昔勝生嚴修此故，　得有一切如來行。非他句有難可得，　作世遍明如世尊，說極清淨修行法，　深廣無盡離分別。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One, surveying the great assembly once more, so as to fulfill every wish, spoke again this Dharma-phrase, the perfect and settled wisdom of the measureless gateways of the three times:
"Space, stainless and without any nature of its own, is able to bestow every kind of skillful wisdom — because its own nature is ever empty, dependent arising is profound and hard to see; over a long and constant span of surpassing progress, it bestows, according to mindful practice, the unsurpassed fruit. Just as every dwelling of every destiny rests upon space, and yet moves through it unattached, so too this pure Dharma: born pure, without remainder, from the three realms of existence — those of surpassing birth in ages past adorned themselves by cultivating just this, and so came to possess the conduct of every Tathāgata. This phrase, found in no other place, is hard indeed to attain — the World-Honored One alone illumines the world completely, and teaches this utterly pure practice, profound and vast, inexhaustible, free of all discrimination."

Chinese Source

爾時毘盧遮那世尊說是偈已，觀察金剛手等諸大眾會，告執金剛言：「善男子！各各當現法界神力悉地流出句。若諸眾生見如是法，歡喜踊躍得安樂住。」如是說已，諸執金剛為毘盧遮那世尊作禮：「如是。法主！依所教勅。」復請佛言：「惟願世尊哀愍我等，示現悉地流出句。何以故？於尊者薄伽梵前而自宣示所通達法，非是所宜。善哉世尊！惟願利益安樂未來眾生故。」時薄伽梵毘盧遮那告一切諸執金剛言：「善哉善哉！善男子！如來所說法毘柰耶稱讚一法，所謂有羞。若有羞善男子善女人，見如是法速生二事，謂不作所不應作、眾所稱讚。復有二事，謂所未至令至，得與佛菩薩同處。復有二事，謂住尸羅、生於人天。善哉諦聽，善思念之，我當宣說真言成就流出相應句。」

爾時世尊復住三世無礙力，依如來加持不思議力，依莊嚴清淨藏三昧，即時世尊從三摩鉢底中，出無盡界無盡語表。依法界力、無等力、正等覺信解、以一音聲四處流出、普遍一切法界、與虛空等，無所不至。真言曰：

「南麼薩婆怛他蘖帝[口*驃](一)　微濕嚩目契弊(二)　薩婆他(三)　阿阿闇噁(四)」

Ru-Yi Translation

Having spoken this verse, the World-Honored One Mahāvairocana surveyed Vajrapāṇi and the whole great assembly, and said to the vajra-holders: "Good sons, each of you should now manifest the phrase from which the spiritual power of the dharma-realm's siddhi flows forth. Should beings behold this teaching, they will leap with joy and dwell in ease." Having said this, the vajra-holders bowed to the World-Honored One Mahāvairocana: "So it is, Lord of the Dharma — we shall do as instructed." They further asked the Buddha: "We beg only that the World-Honored One, out of pity for us, disclose this phrase himself, from which siddhi flows forth. Why is this so? It would not be fitting for us to proclaim, in the presence of the Bhagavān himself, a teaching we ourselves have merely penetrated. Excellent, World-Honored One — we beg only that you do so for the benefit and ease of beings yet to come." The Bhagavān Mahāvairocana then said to all the vajra-holders: "Excellent, excellent, good sons! Among the Tathāgata's own teaching and discipline, there is one quality especially praised — namely, a sense of shame. A good son or daughter possessed of this sense of shame, on seeing a teaching such as this, will swiftly give rise to two things: they will not do what should not be done, and they will be praised by all. Two things further: what has not yet been reached will be reached, and they will come to dwell in the same place as the buddhas and bodhisattvas. And two things further still: they will abide in moral discipline, and be born among gods and humans. Listen well, and reflect on this carefully — I shall now proclaim the phrase of mantra accomplishment, from which every corresponding attainment flows."

At that time the World-Honored One abided once more in the power unobstructed across the three times, and, resting on the inconceivable power of the Tathāgata's own empowerment and the samādhi of the adorned and pure treasury, emerged from that absorption and gave voice to an inexhaustible realm's inexhaustible expression of speech. Resting on the power of the dharma-realm, on unequaled power, and on the confident understanding of perfect awakening, with a single sound it flowed forth into all four directions at once, pervading the entire dharma-realm, equal to space itself, reaching everywhere without exception. The mantra:

Namaḥ sarva-tathāgatebhyaḥ viśva-mukhebhyaḥ sarvathā a ā aṃ aḥ

Reading note

The four syllables closing this universal mantra — A, Ā, AṂ, AḤ — are the same "four transformations" already introduced in Part 4's single-syllable treasury (bodhicitta, conduct, attained bodhi, nirvāṇa); their appearance here, spoken by Mahāvairocana himself in a voice the text says reaches every corner of the dharma-realm "equal to space itself," marks them as more than one entry among many in a list — this is the sūtra's own way of naming its single most condensed formula, the whole path from first arousing bodhicitta to nirvāṇa compressed into one breath. The Buddha's own reluctance to speak the accomplishment-phrase first, deferring instead to the vajra-holders and only stepping in once they have themselves deferred back to him, echoes a courtesy this collection has already seen once, in Part 1, when Vajrapāṇi asks permission before questioning the Buddha at all — a small, recurring point of etiquette this sūtra treats as inseparable from the teaching itself.

The Mirror Within the Mind

Chinese Source

「於正等覺心，　而作成就者，於園苑僧坊，　若在巖窟中，或意所樂處，　觀彼菩提心，乃至初安住，　不生疑慮意。隨取彼一心，　以心置於心，證於極淨句，　無垢安不動，不分別如鏡，　現前甚微細。若彼常觀察，　修習而相應，乃至本所尊，　自身像皆現。第二正覺句，　於鏡漫茶羅，大蓮華王座，　深邃住三昧，總持髮髻冠，　圍繞無量光，離妄執分別，　本寂如虛空。於彼中思惟，　作攝意念誦，一月修等引，　持滿一洛叉，是為最初月，　持真言法則。次於第二月，　奉塗香華等，而以作饒益，　種種眾生類。又復於他月，　捨棄諸利養，時彼於瑜伽，　思惟而自在。願一切無障，　安樂諸群生，樂欲成如來，　所稱讚圓果。或滿足一切，　有情眾希願，應理無障蓋，　而生是攀緣。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"One who accomplishes this within the mind of perfect awakening — whether in a garden, in a monastic dwelling, in a mountain cave, or in whatever place the mind delights — contemplates that very bodhicitta, and, from the first moment it is settled in place, gives rise to no doubt or hesitation. Taking up that single mind, one places mind upon mind, and bears witness to the utterly pure phrase, stainless, at ease, unmoving, free of discrimination like a mirror, appearing before one exceedingly fine and subtle. Should one contemplate this constantly, cultivating it until it is fully attuned, even the very deity one has taken as one's own will at last appear as one's own reflected image. This is the second phrase of perfect awakening: within the maṇḍala of the mirror, upon a great lotus-king throne, dwelling deep within samādhi, crowned with bound-up hair holding dhāraṇī, encircled by measureless light, free of false grasping and discrimination, fundamentally still as space itself. Contemplating within it, gathering the mind for recitation, one cultivates equipoise for one full month, reciting to one hundred thousand repetitions — this is the very first month, the rule for holding mantra. Next, in the second month, one makes offering of scented paste and flowers, and thereby brings benefit to every kind of being. And further, in yet another month, one relinquishes every worldly gain, and, at that time, in yoga, contemplation becomes fully at ease. One vows that every being, without obstruction, may dwell in peace, and wishes to accomplish the perfect fruit every Tathāgata has praised — or else to fulfill every wish of every being, rightly and without any hindrance, giving rise, in this way, to this very aspiration."

Reading note

This "second phrase of perfect awakening" describes a maṇḍala made entirely of reflection rather than paint or sand — a practitioner who holds bodhicitta in mind steadily enough eventually sees, arising within that very stillness, an image of the deity they have taken as their own practice, "as one's own reflected image." The instruction is careful to specify that this vision arrives unbidden, the fruit of sustained contemplation rather than deliberate visualization — closer, in method, to Part 2's account of the master's own initiatory dream-signs than to the elaborate constructed iconography of the Womb Maṇḍala itself. The three-month structure here (one month of visualization proper, a second of offering and dedication, a third of relinquishing personal gain) extends the two-month schedule already given in Part 4, adding a further stage explicitly oriented toward the welfare of other beings rather than the practitioner's own attainment — a small but telling structural point: this sūtra's own account of spiritual progress does not end at personal siddhi, but requires that attainment be turned outward before the sequence is considered complete.

The Five Syllables of Earth, Water, Fire, Wind, and Space

What follows is this sūtra's single most consequential teaching for later esoteric practice and art: five Sanskrit syllables — A, VA, RA, HA, and KHA — each identified with one of the five elements, mapped in sequence onto the practitioner's own body from navel to crown. This is the direct textual source of the five-tiered stūpa (gorintō, 五輪塔) built at temples and gravesites across Japan to this day, and of the seated "A-syllable contemplation" (阿字觀) that remains a core Shingon meditation practice.

A अEarth · navel · yellow square
VA वWater · heart · white circle
RA रFire · throat · red triangle
HA हWind · forehead · black (or green) half-moon
KHA खSpace · crown · a wish-fulfilling jewel
Chinese Source

「若於真言門，　修行諸菩薩，阿字為自身，　內外悉同等，諸義利皆捨，　等礫石金寶，遠離眾罪業，　及與貪瞋等，當得俱清淨，　同諸佛牟尼，能作諸利益，　離一切諸過。復次於嚩字，　行者依瑜伽，解作業儀式，　利益眾生故，內身救世者，　一切皆如是，心水湛盈滿，　潔白猶雪乳，當生決定意，　出於一切身，悉遍諸毛孔，　流注極清淨，從此內充溢，　遍滿於大地，以是悲愍水，　觀世苦眾生。諸有飲用者，　或復身所觸，一切皆決定，　得成就菩提。思惟在等引，　一切囉字門，周輪生焰光，　寂然而普照，瑜祇光外轉，　而遍一切處。利世隨樂欲，　行者起神通，上身囉字門，　嚩字臍輪中，出火而降雨，　俱時而應現。地獄極寒苦，　囉字能消除，嚩字蠲熾然，　住真言法故。囉字為下身，　訶字為幖幟，作業速成就，　救重罪眾生，住大因陀羅，　作水龍事業，一切攝除等，　真言者勿疑。風遍一切處，　一切悉開壞，此種種雜類，　各各眾事業，色漫茶羅中，　依法而作之。觸心而念持，　逮得意根淨，輕舉習經行，　中誦獲神足。宴坐觀阿字，　想在於耳根，念持滿一月，　當得耳清淨。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Should bodhisattvas practicing conduct within the gateway of mantra take the letter A as their own body, inner and outer wholly equal, relinquishing every worldly aim — gravel and gold, jewel alike — and leaving far behind every karma of guilt, along with greed, anger, and the rest, they will attain a purity shared with every buddha, every silent sage, able to bring about every benefit, free of every fault. Further, at the letter VA, the practitioner, resting in yoga, understands the rites of ritual action, and, for the sake of benefiting beings, becomes, within, a savior of the world in every respect: the mind's own water, brimming full, pure white as snow-milk, gives rise to a resolute intention, streaming out through every pore of the body, flowing forth utterly pure — welling up from within, spreading out to fill the whole earth, this water of compassion regarding the suffering beings of the world. Whoever drinks of it, or whose body it merely touches, will without exception, and with certainty, accomplish bodhi. Contemplated in equipoise, at the letter RA a wheel of flame is born, blazing in stillness, shining everywhere; the practitioner's own light turns outward, reaching every place. To benefit the world according to its wishes, the practitioner arouses spiritual powers: the letter RA in the upper body, the letter VA at the navel's own wheel, fire and rain arising and manifesting together at once. The extreme cold-suffering of the hells is dispelled by the letter RA; the blazing fires there are quenched by the letter VA, resting on the very Dharma of mantra. The letter RA forms the lower body, the letter HA its emblem — ritual action is swiftly accomplished, rescuing beings of grave offense; abiding in the great Indra's own realm, one carries out the work of water and nāgas, gathering in and removing every affliction alike — the mantra practitioner should have no doubt of this. Wind reaches every place, opening and dissolving all things; every different kind of ritual action, each its own task, is carried out in the maṇḍala of color, in accordance with the teaching. Touching this to the mind and holding it in recitation, one attains a purified mind-faculty; moving lightly, practicing walking meditation, reciting all the while, one gains the base of miraculous power. Sitting in quiet meditation, contemplating the letter A held in mind at the ear, reciting for a full month, one attains a purified faculty of hearing."

Reading note

The five-syllable system in this passage — A at the navel for earth, VA at the heart for water, RA at the throat for fire, HA at the forehead for wind, and KHA (introduced a few verses later in this same chapter, not translated at length here) at the crown for space — became, in the centuries after this sūtra reached Japan, the direct scriptural warrant for the gorintō: the five-tiered stone stūpa, its stack of cube, sphere, pyramid, crescent, and jewel-shaped tiers still the single most common form of grave marker at Japanese Buddhist cemeteries today, each tier a solid geometric translation of exactly the shape this passage assigns each syllable — square, circle, triangle, half-moon, jewel. The same five syllables, contemplated seated in meditation rather than built in stone, are the basis of ajikan (阿字観), the "A-syllable contemplation" that remains a foundational Shingon practice: a practitioner seated before a painted moon-disc bearing the single letter A, understood, exactly as this sūtra states of the letter A elsewhere, as "one's own body" rather than an external object of devotion. What the passage above adds to that later, more austere single-syllable practice is its own, more expansive claim: each of the five elements, correctly contemplated at its own place in the body, is said to actually do something in the world — VA's water genuinely quenches the fires of hell, RA's fire genuinely warms its cold — a continuity between inner visualization and outer effect this whole sūtra has been establishing since the ten similes of Part 1, where a mantra's efficacy was shown to require no fixed, independently existing substance behind it in order to be real.

Chapter Seven — The Mirror of the Heart

Chinese Source

時吉祥金剛，　奇特開敷眼，手轉金剛印，　流散如火光，其明普遍照，　一切諸佛剎，微妙音稱歎：　「法自在牟尼，說諸真言行。　彼行不可得，真言從何來？　所去至何所？諸佛說如是，　更無過上句，一切法歸趣，　如眾流赴海。」如是說已，世尊告執金剛祕密主言：
「摩訶薩意處，　說名漫茶羅，諸真言心位，　了知得成果。諸有所分別，　悉皆從意生，分辯白黃赤，　是等從心起。決定心歡喜，　說名內心處，真言住斯位，　能授廣大果。念彼蓮華處，　八葉鬚蘂敷，華臺阿字門，　焰鬘皆妙好，光暉普周遍，　照明眾生故，如合會千電，　持佛巧色形，深居圓鏡中，　應現諸方所，猶如淨水月，　普現眾生前，知心性如是，　得住真言行。次於其首上，　頂會交際中，標以大空點，　而思惟暗字，妙好淨無垢，　如水精月電，說寂靜法身，　一切所依持，諸真言悉地，　能現殊類形，得天樂解脫，　逮見如來句。囉字為眼界，　輝燭猶明燈，俛頸小低頭，　舌近於齶間，而以觀心處，　當心現等引，無垢妙淨清，　圓鏡常現前。如是真實心，　古佛所宣說，照了心明達，　諸色皆發光。真言者當見，　正覺兩足尊，若見成悉地，　第一常恒體。從此次思惟，　轉此囉字門，邏字大空點，　置之於眼位，見一切空句，　得成不死句。若欲廣大智，　或起五神通，長壽童子身，　成就持明等，真言者未得，　由不隨順之。真言發起智，　是最勝實知，一切佛菩薩，　救世之庫藏。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the auspicious Vajrapāṇi, his eyes opened in wonder, turned the vajra-seal in his hand, radiance scattering from it like fire, its light shining everywhere across every buddha-field, and spoke in a wondrous voice, praising the Buddha: "Silent Sage, sovereign of the Dharma, who teaches this mantra conduct — that conduct itself cannot be apprehended: whence does mantra come, and whither does it go? Every buddha has taught it thus: there is no phrase that surpasses it; every dharma flows toward it, as every river flows at last to the sea." Having said this, the World-Honored One said to Vajrapāṇi:
"The place of a great being's own mind — this is what is called the maṇḍala; once the very seat of every mantra's heart is known, its fruit is accomplished. Whatever discrimination there is arises entirely from the mind; the distinguishing of white, yellow, and red all arises from the mind as well. A mind resolute and joyful — this is called the inner heart's own place; a mantra abiding at this seat is able to bestow a vast and expansive fruit. Contemplate that lotus place, its eight petals with filament and pistil open in bloom, the letter-gate A upon the lotus dais, its garland of flame wondrous and fine, its radiance shining everywhere, illuminating every being — like a thousand lightning-flashes joined as one, holding the buddha's own skillfully wrought form, dwelling deep within a round mirror, manifesting in response in every direction, just as the moon appears in every body of clear water, showing itself before every being — knowing the nature of mind to be exactly this, one comes to abide in mantra conduct. Next, at the very crown of the head, at the meeting-point of the skull, mark the point of great emptiness, and contemplate the letter AḤ, fine and pure, free of every stain, like crystal, like moonlight, like lightning — this is spoken of as the tranquil dharma-body, the very support of all things; every mantra's siddhi is able, through it, to manifest in every different form, attain the bliss of the heavens and liberation, and at last behold the Tathāgata's own phrase. The letter RA forms the field of the eye, its brightness like a lit lamp; lowering the neck, bowing the head slightly, the tongue brought near the roof of the mouth, one thereby contemplates the seat of the mind — before the mind, equipoise appears, stainless, wondrously pure and clear, a round mirror ever present before one. This true and actual mind, as the buddhas of old have taught, illumines and clarifies the mind's own understanding, and every color thereby gives forth its own light. The mantra practitioner will thereby behold the Perfectly Awakened One, most honored among the two-footed; and having beheld him, siddhi is accomplished — the very first, ever-constant body. From this, next contemplate turning this same letter-gate RA into the letter LA together with the point of great emptiness, placed at the position of the eye; seeing the phrase of universal emptiness in this way, one accomplishes the phrase of deathlessness. Should one wish for vast wisdom, or to arouse the five superknowledges, the form of an ever-youthful, long-lived body, and the accomplishment of vidyā-holding and the rest — a mantra practitioner who has not attained these has simply failed to follow this teaching accordingly. The wisdom mantra gives rise to is the most supreme and true knowing there is — the very treasury of every buddha and bodhisattva, saviors of the world."

Reading note

This short chapter returns to the mirror image of the "second phrase of perfect awakening" from earlier in this Part, but sharpens it into a specific technique: the letter RA visualized at the eye, functioning as a lamp that illuminates the mind's own seat "as a round mirror ever present," and then transformed — the text specifies the transformation directly, RA becoming LA with the addition of the point of great emptiness — into what the sūtra calls "the phrase of deathlessness." This deliberate letter-substitution technique, one Sanskrit consonant modified into a visually and phonetically related one to shift a visualization's function, recurs across this sūtra's remaining chapters and is one of the more technically demanding features of its ritual system for a reader encountering it for the first time; it depends on the same principle already established in Part 3's forty-two-gate teaching, that each letter is itself a specific gateway into emptiness, so that moving between related letters is understood as moving between specific, related aspects of the same single truth rather than switching to an unrelated formula.

The Wheel of Revolving Letters

Chapter Eight repeats the Womb Maṇḍala's construction from Parts 2–3, but in a compressed, more esoteric form: rather than full iconographic descriptions, each deity's position is now marked by a single seed-syllable alone. The chapter closes with a five-color visualization rite — white, red, yellow, blue, and black, each paired with its own contemplated letter and its own ritual purpose — and a final repetition of the initiation instructions from Part 3.

Chinese Source (abridged)

「彼阿闍梨先住阿字一切智門，持修多羅稽首一切諸佛，東方申之，旋轉而南以及西方，周於北方。次作金剛薩埵，以執金剛加持自身，或以彼印或以嚩字入於內心，置漫茶羅。……真言者應以潔白為先。」說伽陀曰：

「以此淨法界，　淨除諸眾生，自體如如來，　遠離一切過。如是而觀想，　思惟囉字門，寂然光焰鬘，　淨月商佉色。第二布赤色，　行者當憶持，思惟字明照，　本無大空點，煥炳初日輝，　最勝無能壞。第三真言者，　次運布黃色，定意迦字門，　當隨於法教。身相猶真金，　正受害諸毒，光明遍一切，　金色同牟尼。次當布青色，　超度於生死，思惟麼字門，　大寂菩提座，身色如虹霓，　除一切怖畏。最後布黑色，　其彩甚玄妙，思惟訶字門，　周遍生圓光，如劫災猛焰，　寶冠舉手印，能怖一切惡，　降伏諸魔軍。」

東方一切諸佛，以阿字門及大空點。伊舍尼方一切如來母虛空眼，應書伽字。火天方一切諸菩薩，畫真陀摩尼寶或置迦字。夜叉方觀世自在，蓮華印，并畫一生補處菩薩眷屬，或作娑字。焰摩方，越三分位置金剛慧印，持金剛祕密主并眷屬，或書嚩字。……次涅哩底方，於大日如來下，作不動尊坐於石上，手持羂索慧刀，周匝焰鬘擬作障者，或置彼印，或書字句所謂唅字。風天方，降三世尊摧大障者……或作彼印，或書字句所謂訶字。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The master first abides in the letter A, the gateway of all-wisdom, and, holding a length of thread, bows to every buddha, extending it toward the east, then turning to circle south, and on to the west, and around to the north. He then forms Vajrasattva, empowering his own body with the vajra-holder, and, either with that seal or by bringing the letter VA into his own heart, sets it within the maṇḍala…the mantra practitioner should begin with pure white." He then spoke this verse:

"With this pure dharma-realm, every being is purified; one's own body becomes as the Tathāgata's own, far removed from every fault. Contemplating in this way, hold in mind the letter-gate RA, its still garland of flame the color of pure moonlight and conch-shell white. Second, lay in red, and the practitioner should hold in mind the letter A illumined, fundamentally unborn, together with the point of great emptiness, blazing bright as the sun's first rising, most supreme, unable to be destroyed. Third, the mantra practitioner next spreads yellow, settling the mind on the letter-gate KA, in accord with the teaching — the body's own form like true gold, truly receiving and destroying every poison, its light pervading everywhere, golden as the Silent Sage himself. Next, lay in blue, transcending birth-and-death, holding in mind the letter-gate MA, the great and still seat of bodhi, the body's color like a rainbow, dispelling every fear. Last, lay in black, its hue most deep and mysterious, holding in mind the letter-gate HA, a round light arising all around, fierce as the flame of the kalpa's own destruction, jeweled crown and raised hand-seal, able to terrify every evil, subduing every army of Māra."

In the east, every buddha, marked by the letter A together with the point of great emptiness. In the northwest, Buddhalocanā, the mother of every Tathāgata, should be inscribed with the letter GA. In the southeast, every bodhisattva — paint a cintāmaṇi jewel, or mark it with the letter KA. In the northeast, Avalokiteśvara, marked with the lotus-seal, together with the retinue of one-birth-bound bodhisattvas, or mark it with the letter SA. In the southwest, setting aside three of the usual divisions, place the vajra-wisdom seal, Vajrapāṇi, Lord of Secrets, together with his retinue, or mark it with the letter VA…Next, in the southwest quarter, beneath Mahāvairocana, fashion Acala seated upon a rock, holding a lasso and a wisdom-sword in hand, encircled all about by a garland of flame, poised against every obstructing spirit — or place his seal there, or mark it with the letter HĀṂ. In the northwest quarter, Trailokyavijaya, who crushes every great obstruction…or place his seal there, or mark it with the letter HŪṂ."

Reading note

This chapter's method — reducing the same maṇḍala Part 2 painted in full iconographic detail down to a single seed-syllable per deity — is the "revolving wheel of letters" its title names: the same figures, the same positions, now represented at the level of pure sound rather than image, so that a practitioner who has internalized the full iconography can, at a more advanced stage, work with the compressed letter-form alone. The five-color rite that follows (white for RA, red for A with the point of great emptiness, yellow for KA, blue for MA, black for HA) maps onto the classical fourfold division of esoteric ritual purpose that later systematized into the "four" or "five great rites" (息災, 增益, 降伏, 敬愛, and 鉤召 — pacification, increase, subjugation, attraction, and summoning): white for pacifying obstacles and illness, yellow for increasing merit and prosperity, black for the forceful subjugation of what resists gentler means, with red and blue serving further specific purposes this chapter does not spell out at length. Together with the five-syllable body visualization earlier in this same Part, this color-and-letter system forms the working vocabulary nearly every later chapter of this sūtra continues to draw on.

Part 5 Complete

This closes Part 5: the universal mantra reaching every corner of the dharma-realm, the mirror-maṇḍala that arises from sustained contemplation rather than deliberate visualization, the five-syllable body meditation that later became both the gorintō stūpa and the ajikan practice, the letter-substitution technique that turns RA into LA, and the compressed "wheel of letters" version of the Womb Maṇḍala with its five-color rite. Part 6 turns to 密印品第九, the sūtra's largest single chapter on mudrās — the hand-seals that, together with mantra and visualization, complete the "three secrets" (三密) this sūtra has referred to since its opening chapter.
