The Hundred Syllables, and the Closing Entrustment

Part 9 carries the last thirteen chapters of this sūtra's core teaching: the "hundred-syllable" sequence built on the letter A, a parable about a magician's illusion creating a further illusion of itself, a definition of what a tathāgata actually is, a former life the Buddha recounts among the Brahmā gods, and forty-four Vedic fire-lineages absorbed and reworked into twelve fires of wisdom — before the sūtra closes its own thirty-one-chapter foundation with a final entrustment: who is fit to receive this teaching, and a last empowerment offered to protect it in the world.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 9 of 11 complete Source: Taishō T0848, 大毘盧遮那成佛神變加持經 (tr. Śubhakarasiṃha and Yixing) Reading notes after Yixing's 大毘盧遮那成佛經疏 (T1796)

The Hundred Syllables, and Four Kinds of Measurelessness

Chinese Source (abridged)

爾時毘盧遮那世尊觀察諸大會眾，說不空教……以妙音聲告大力金剛手言：「勤勇士一心諦聽諸真言、真言導師。」即時住於智生三昧，而說出生種種巧智百光遍照真言曰：「南麼三曼多勃馱喃(一)　暗」佛告金剛手：「此一切真言，真王救世者，成就大威德，即是正等覺，法自在牟尼。……」

爾時毘盧遮那世尊告執金剛祕密主言：「祕密主！若入大覺世尊大智灌頂地，自見住於三三昧耶句。」……以知心無量故，知身無量。知身無量故，知智無量。知智無量故，即知眾生無量。知眾生無量故，即知虛空界無量。祕密主！由心無量故，得四種無量。得已，成最正覺，具十智力，降伏四魔，以無所畏而師子吼。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One Mahāvairocana, surveying the whole great assembly, taught the doctrine of unfailing accomplishment…and, in a wondrous voice, said to the mighty Vajrapāṇi: "Valiant one, listen closely with a single mind to every mantra, and to the guide of mantras." He then abided in the samādhi of wisdom's own arising, and spoke the mantra that gives rise to every kind of skillful wisdom, the hundred lights universally illuminating: Namaḥ samanta-buddhānāṃ aṃ. The Buddha said to Vajrapāṇi: "This is the very king of every mantra, savior of the world, accomplishing great majestic virtue — it is itself perfect awakening, the Silent Sage sovereign over the Dharma…"

At that time the World-Honored One Mahāvairocana said to Vajrapāṇi, Lord of Secrets: "Lord of Secrets, one who enters the ground of the great awakened World-Honored One's own great wisdom-empowerment beholds themselves abiding in the phrase of the three samayas."…Because one knows mind to be measureless, one knows body to be measureless. Because one knows body to be measureless, one knows wisdom to be measureless. Because one knows wisdom to be measureless, one knows beings to be measureless. Because one knows beings to be measureless, one knows the realm of space to be measureless. Lord of Secrets, from the mind's own measurelessness, four kinds of measurelessness are attained. Having attained them, one accomplishes the most perfect awakening, complete with the ten powers of wisdom, subduing the four Māras, and roars the lion's roar, without any fear at all.

Reading note

The chain from mind's measurelessness to body's, then to wisdom's, then to that of all beings, then to space's own — each link following necessarily from the one before — restates, in a single unbroken sequence, the identity Part 1 already established between mind, space, and bodhi as "three without duality": here that identity is not simply asserted but walked through step by step, so that a practitioner who genuinely grasps the mind's own measurelessness is shown arriving, by strict implication rather than further teaching, at the measurelessness of the entire realm of space. The "hundred syllables" this chapter and the next several take their name from build outward from AṂ exactly as Part 3's forty-two gateways built outward from A — the same seed multiplied through its own transformations rather than a genuinely new alphabet.

The Mirror of the Self

Chinese Source

「復次祕密主！真言門修菩薩行諸菩薩如是自身影像生起無有殊勝過三菩提，如眼耳鼻舌身意等四大種攝持集聚，彼如是自性空，唯有名字所執，猶如虛空，無所執著，等於影像。彼如來成正覺，互相緣起無有間絕。若從緣生，彼即如影像生。是故諸本尊即我、我即本尊，互相發起身所生身尊形像生。……祕密主！云何從意生？意能生影像。祕密主！譬如若白若黃若赤，作意者作時染著意生，彼同類如是身轉。祕密主！又如內觀意中漫荼羅療治熱病，彼眾生熱病即時除愈無有疑惑。非漫荼羅異意，非意異漫荼羅。何以故？彼漫荼羅一相故。祕密主！又如幻者幻作男子，而彼男子又復作化。祕密主！於意云何？彼何者為勝？」時金剛手白佛言：「世尊！此二人者，無相異也。何以故？世尊！非實生故。是二男子本性空故，等同於幻。如是祕密主，意生眾事及意所生，如是俱空，無二無別。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Furthermore, Lord of Secrets, for bodhisattvas practicing bodhisattva conduct within the gateway of mantra, this arising of their own body's reflected image is nothing more excellent than the three-fold bodhi itself — just as the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind are held together and gathered from the four great elements, and are, in just this way, empty of any nature of their own, grasped at only through name, like space itself, with nothing genuinely to grasp, equal to a mere reflection. That Tathāgata's own accomplishment of perfect awakening arises through mutual dependent origination, without a single break. If it is born from conditions, then it is born exactly as a reflection is born. Therefore every meditational deity is myself, and I am that very deity — one body giving rise to another, arising as the deity's own reflected form…Lord of Secrets, how does this arise from mind? Mind is able to give rise to a reflected image. Lord of Secrets, it is like this: whether white, yellow, or red, when the one who forms the intention forms it, a mind attached to that color arises, and the body then turns according to that very kind. Lord of Secrets, it is also like an inwardly visualized maṇḍala within the mind that heals a fever — that being's fever is cured at once, without any doubt about it. The maṇḍala is not other than the mind, nor is the mind other than the maṇḍala. Why is this so? Because that maṇḍala is itself of a single nature with mind. Lord of Secrets, it is also like a magician who conjures an illusory man, and that illusory man in turn conjures a further illusion of his own. Lord of Secrets, what do you think — which of these two is superior?" At that time Vajrapāṇi said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, these two are no different from one another. Why is this so? World-Honored One, because neither was ever truly born in the first place. Both of these men are, in their fundamental nature, empty — equally illusory. Just so, Lord of Secrets: what mind gives rise to, and what mind itself is, are both equally empty, without duality, without any difference between them."

Reading note

The image of an illusory man conjuring a further illusion of his own, with neither more or less real than the other, gives this chapter its sharpest formulation yet of a claim this collection has traced since the ten similes of Part 1: a practitioner visualizing a deity, and the deity thereby visualized, stand in exactly the relation of the first illusory man to the second — neither is the "real" one from which the other derives, because neither was ever independently real to begin with. This is also the doctrinal foundation of a specific later practice this sūtra does not name directly but clearly anticipates: nyāsa or "deity yoga" in its broadest sense, where a practitioner does not merely petition an external deity but comes to identify their own body directly with the deity contemplated — stated here in the plainest possible terms, "every meditational deity is myself, and I am that very deity."

Every Letter as a Face of A

Chapter Twenty-Two repeats the forty-two gateways of Part 3 in a new key: rather than each letter independently negating some aspect of phenomena, every letter here is described as a specific "manifestation" (現…字形) of the letter A itself — VA appearing where ungraspability is shown, KA where freedom from fabrication is shown, and so on through the same alphabet, closing with the claim that even the thirty-two marks of a buddha's own body arise from this single sequence. Chapter Twenty-Three then turns to sound itself: a mantra's sound is empty, arising from the letter, and a practitioner who enters directly into that emptiness of sound is said thereby to enter samādhi itself.

Chinese Source (selected)

「祕密主！如是阿字，住於種種莊嚴，布列圖位。以一切法本不生故，顯示自形。或以不可得義，現嚩字形。或諸法遠離造作故，現迦字形。……祕密主！隨入此等一一三昧門，祕密主！觀是乃至三十二大人相等皆從此中出。」

「當知聲性空，　即空所造作，一切眾生類，　如言而妄執；非空亦非聲，　為修行者說，入於聲解脫，　即證三摩地。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Lord of Secrets, this letter A, dwelling amid every kind of adornment, is arrayed and set in place throughout the whole design. Because every dharma is fundamentally unborn, it displays its own true form. Through the meaning of ungraspability, it appears in the form of the letter VA. Because every dharma is far removed from fabrication, it appears in the form of the letter KA…Lord of Secrets, entering by turns into each of these samādhi-gateways, Lord of Secrets — observe that even the thirty-two marks of a great being arise entirely from within this very sequence."

"Know that the nature of sound is empty — precisely what emptiness itself fabricates; every kind of being clings falsely to it, exactly as it is spoken. It is neither emptiness nor mere sound that is taught here to the practitioner: entering into the very liberation of sound, one thereby bears witness to samādhi itself."

Reading note

Reframing the forty-two gateways as "manifestations" of a single letter A, rather than forty-two independent negations, makes explicit what Part 3's own commentary already implied about A's status as seed-syllable: every other letter is not merely built from A phonetically, but is A itself, appearing under a different name at a different angle. The teaching on sound's own emptiness that follows closes a loop this sūtra opened at its very first mantra: a practitioner is not asked to treat a mantra's sound as an arbitrary noise pointing toward a separate, silent truth beyond it, nor as itself a solid, self-existing power — "neither emptiness nor mere sound" — but to enter directly into the sound's own liberation, which the text identifies outright with samādhi.

The Nature of Bodhi, and Three Samayas

Chinese Source

「譬如十方虛空相，　常遍一切無所依，如是真言救世者，　於一切法無所依。又如空中諸色像，　雖可現見無依處，真言救世者亦然，　非彼諸法所依處。世間成立虛空量，　遠離去來現在世，若見真言救世者，　亦復出過三世法。」

「復次祕密主！有三三昧耶，最初正覺心，　第二名為法，彼心相續生，　所謂和合僧，此三三昧耶，　諸佛導師說。……」祕密主！三藐三佛陀安立教故，以一身加持，所謂初變化身。復次祕密主！次於一身示現三種，所謂佛、法、僧。……彼不著一切妄執，無能為障礙者，除不樂欲、懈怠、無利談話、不生信心、積集資財者。復應不作二事，謂飲諸酒及寢床上。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Just as the mark of space in the ten directions is ever pervasive, dependent upon nothing at all, so too the mantra practitioner, savior of the world, depends on no dharma whatsoever. And just as every form and color within space, though visible, has nowhere it truly rests, so too the mantra practitioner, savior of the world — no dharma is the ground on which they rest. What the world establishes as the measure of space is far removed from past, future, and present alike; whoever beholds the mantra practitioner, savior of the world, sees one who likewise passes beyond the dharmas of the three times."

"Furthermore, Lord of Secrets, there are three samayas: the first, the mind of perfect awakening; the second, called the Dharma; and that mind's own continuous arising, namely the harmonious saṅgha — these three samayas every buddha, every guide, has taught…" Lord of Secrets, because the Perfectly Awakened One establishes his teaching, he empowers it with a single body — namely, the first, transformation body. Furthermore, Lord of Secrets, within that one body, three are displayed: namely, the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha…Such a one clings to no false grasping, and nothing can obstruct them — except a lack of aspiration, laziness, idle and unprofitable talk, a failure to give rise to faith, and the hoarding of wealth. They should further refrain from two things: drinking any wine, and sleeping upon a raised bed."

Reading note

The two concrete prohibitions closing this chapter — no wine, no sleeping on a raised bed — stand out for their sheer ordinariness against the surrounding cosmic-scale doctrine of a bodhi "far removed from past, future, and present alike"; the sūtra does not treat this as a jarring shift in register but simply moves, in a single breath, from the outer limit of what can be said about emptiness to the plainest possible standard of a practitioner's daily conduct. The three samayas mapped onto the Three Jewels — bodhicitta as Buddha, teaching as Dharma, and the mind's own continuous arising as the harmonious saṅgha — offer this sūtra's own account of why the Three Jewels, refuge for every school of Buddhism, are not a separate layer of devotion added onto esoteric practice but are already fully present within the single body Mahāvairocana displays.

What Is a Tathāgata?

Chinese Source

爾時執金剛祕密主白世尊言：
「云何為如來？　云何人中尊？云何名菩薩？　云何為正覺？導師大牟尼，　願斷我所疑。菩薩大名稱，　棄捨疑慮心，當修摩訶衍，　行王無有上。」
爾時薄伽梵毘盧遮那觀察諸大會眾，告執金剛祕密主言：「善哉善哉！金剛手！能問吾如是義。祕密主！汝當諦聽，善思念之，吾今演說摩訶衍道。頌曰：
「『菩提虛空相，　離一切分別，樂求彼菩提，　名菩提薩埵。成就十地等，　自在善通達，諸法空如幻，　知此一切同，解諸世間趣，　故名為正覺。法如虛空相，　無二惟一相，成佛十智力，　故號三菩提。唯慧害無明，　自性離言說，自證之智慧，　故說名如來。』」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Vajrapāṇi said to the World-Honored One:
"What is a tathāgata? What is the most honored among people? What is called a bodhisattva? What is perfect awakening? Great Silent Sage, guide of the world, I beg you to resolve my doubts. Let bodhisattvas of great renown cast off every hesitant mind, and cultivate the Great Vehicle, the unsurpassed king among every path."
At that time the Bhagavān Mahāvairocana, surveying the whole great assembly, said to Vajrapāṇi, Lord of Secrets: "Excellent, excellent, Vajrapāṇi! You ask me a question of such import. Lord of Secrets, listen closely, and reflect on this well — I shall now proclaim the path of the Great Vehicle. The verse:
"'Bodhi bears the mark of space itself, free of every discrimination; one who delights in seeking that very bodhi is called a bodhisattva. Having accomplished the ten grounds and the rest, sovereign and thoroughly penetrating, knowing every dharma to be empty as an illusion, and knowing this to be true of all alike, understanding every destiny of the world — for this one is called perfectly awakened. The Dharma bears the mark of space itself, without duality, of a single mark alone; having accomplished the buddha's own ten powers of wisdom, for this one is called the fully awakened one. Only wisdom destroys ignorance; its own nature is beyond all words — the wisdom that bears witness to itself: for this, one is called a tathāgata.'"

Reading note

This chapter's four brief definitions — bodhisattva as one who delights in seeking markless bodhi, samyaksaṃbuddha as one who has accomplished the ten grounds and knows every dharma as illusion, saṃbuddha as one who has attained the Dharma's own single mark through the ten powers, and tathāgata as self-witnessing wisdom that destroys ignorance — are among the most quotably concise statements of Buddhist ontology anywhere in this sūtra, offered without a single mantra, mudrā, or maṇḍala instruction attached to any of them. Their placement here, roughly two-thirds of the way through the text's core chapters and immediately before the sūtra turns to its final ritual matters (fire offerings, deity visualization, recitation method, and the closing entrustment), reads as a deliberate pause: a moment where the sūtra restates, in the plainest possible prose-poetry, what all the preceding ritual apparatus has been in service of the whole time.

Forty-Four Vedic Fires, and Twelve Fires of Wisdom

The Buddha now recounts a story from a former life: as a bodhisattva dwelling among the Brahmā gods, he was once asked by a Brahmā how many kinds of fire there are. What follows is a genealogy of forty-four named fires drawn directly from Vedic domestic ritual — each fire proper to a specific life-cycle rite, from conception through marriage, illness, and cremation — which the Buddha, now awakened, reworks into a wholly different scheme: twelve fires of wisdom, named for qualities of mind rather than ritual occasions.

Chinese Source (abridged)

「復次祕密主！往昔一時我為菩薩，行菩薩行住於梵世，時有梵天來問我言：『大梵！我等欲知火有幾種？』時我如是答言：……〈四十四種火名，各配其禮儀場合，自受胎、澡身、生子、命名、飲食、剃髮、受戒、婚媾、造房、拜日月、乃至劫燒盡火〉……此四十四種，爾時我宣說。』復次祕密主！我於往昔時，不知諸火性，作諸護摩事，彼非護摩行，非能成業果。我復成菩提，演說十二火，智火最為初，名火因陀羅……第十一火神(梵本闕其名)；十二謨賀那，眾生所迷惑。祕密主此等，火色之所持，隨其自形色，藥物等同彼，而作外護摩，隨意成悉地。復次於內心，一性而具三，三處合為一，瑜祇內護摩。大慈大悲心，是謂息災法，彼兼具於喜，是為增益法，忿怒從胎藏，而造眾事業。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Furthermore, Lord of Secrets, long ago, at one time, I was a bodhisattva, practicing bodhisattva conduct while dwelling among the Brahmā gods, when a certain Brahmā came and asked me: 'Great Brahmā, we wish to know how many kinds of fire there are.' I answered him thus…" (forty-four named fires follow, each assigned to a specific domestic rite — conception, bathing after intercourse, the birth of a child, the naming of a child, eating, the first cutting of a child's topknot, the taking of vows, a young man's marriage, the building of a house, worship of the sun and moon, and, at the very last, the fire that consumes the world at the end of an aeon) — "'These forty-four, I proclaimed to you at that time.' Furthermore, Lord of Secrets, in that former time, not yet knowing the true nature of fire, I performed fire offerings that were, in fact, no true fire-offering practice at all, and could not bring any karmic fruit to accomplishment. Having now accomplished bodhi, I proclaim instead twelve fires. The fire of wisdom comes first of all, named Fire-Indra…the eleventh, a fire-deity whose name is missing from the Sanskrit text; the twelfth, Moha, "delusion," by which every being is confused. Lord of Secrets, these are upheld by their own colors of fire; and whatever substance and medicine shares that same color should be used accordingly, performing the outer fire offering, so as to accomplish siddhi according to one's own wish. Furthermore, within the heart, a single nature holds all three at once — three places joined as one, the yogin's own inner fire offering. The mind of great loving-kindness and great compassion is called the rite of pacification; that same mind, joined with joy, is the rite of increase; wrath, arising from the very matrix of compassion, carries out every other kind of ritual work."

Reading note

This chapter is unusually direct about its own relationship to the Vedic tradition it draws from: rather than simply borrowing fire-ritual vocabulary silently, the sūtra stages an explicit before-and-after — the Buddha himself, in a former life still bound within the Brahmanical cosmos, reciting the forty-four traditional household fires exactly as a Brahmin priest would have taught them, and only later, "having now accomplished bodhi," replacing that entire genealogy with a scheme of twelve wisdom-fires organized by psychological and ritual function rather than by life-cycle occasion. The move mirrors, in narrative form, the same absorption-rather-than-rejection this collection has already traced in the maṇḍala's own outer courts (Part 2's reading note on Indra, Brahmā, Yama, and the rest gathered into the second court rather than excluded from it): the forty-four fires are not declared false or discarded, but explicitly named as the earlier, unenlightened version of a practice the Buddha himself once performed without understanding — reworked, not erased, once real wisdom is attained. The three inner fires closing the chapter — loving-kindness and compassion for pacification, joy added for increase, and wrath arising "from the very matrix of compassion" for subjugation — restate the fourfold rite-classification already introduced with the five-color visualization of Part 5, confirming that even the most forceful, wrathful ritual action this sūtra ever describes is said to originate from compassion rather than anger in the ordinary sense.

Deity-Bodies, and the Markless Samādhi

Chinese Source

「祕密主！諸尊有三種身，所謂字、印、形像。彼字有二種，謂聲及菩提心。印有二種，所謂有形、無形。本尊之身亦有二種，所謂清淨、非清淨。彼證淨身離一切相，非淨有想之身則有顯形眾色。彼二種尊形，成就二種事：有想故成就有相悉地，無想故隨生無相悉地。」而說偈言：「佛說有想故，樂欲成有相；以住無想故，獲無相悉地。是故一切種，當住於非想。」

「祕密主！彼真言門修菩薩行諸菩薩樂欲成就無相三昧，當如是思惟：『想從何生？為自身耶？自心意耶？若從身生，身如草木瓦石，自性如是，離於造作、無所識知、因業所生，應當等觀同於外事。……愚童凡夫於自性空形像自我分生，顛倒不實起諸分別，或復供養或加毀害。』……復次祕密主！心無自性，離一切想故，當思惟性空。祕密主！心於三時求不可得，以過三世故，如是自性遠離諸相。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Lord of Secrets, every deity has three kinds of body — namely, letter, seal, and image. The letter has two kinds: sound, and bodhicitta. The seal has two kinds: with form, and without form. The deity's own body likewise has two kinds: pure, and impure. The body realized as pure is free of every mark; the body not yet pure, still bearing a mental image, still bears a displayed form and every color. These two forms of the deity accomplish two kinds of thing: because there is a mental image, siddhi with form is accomplished; because there is no mental image, siddhi without form arises accordingly." He then spoke this verse: "The Buddha teaches that, because there is a mental image, one delights in accomplishing what has form; and because one abides free of any mental image, one obtains siddhi without form. Therefore, in every respect, one should abide in what is neither thought nor its absence."

"Lord of Secrets, bodhisattvas practicing bodhisattva conduct within the gateway of mantra, wishing to accomplish the markless samādhi, should reflect thus: 'Where does this mental image arise from? From my own body? From my own mind? If it arises from the body, the body is like grass, wood, tile, or stone — its own nature just so, free of any fabrication, without any awareness of its own, born from karma alone; one should regard it, equally, the same as any outward thing…The ignorant, childish common person, out of the emptiness of their own nature, gives rise to a divided sense of self through such an image, and, through this inversion and falsehood, gives rise to every kind of discrimination — sometimes making offering to it, sometimes doing it harm.'…Furthermore, Lord of Secrets, mind has no nature of its own, being free of every mental image; one should therefore contemplate its own emptiness of nature. Lord of Secrets, mind, sought across the three times, cannot be apprehended — because it passes beyond the three times altogether, its own nature is, in just this way, far removed from every mark."

Reading note

"One should abide in what is neither thought nor its absence" gives this sūtra its most compact resolution of a tension this collection has tracked since Part 1's ten similes: a practitioner who visualizes a deity in full iconographic detail (Part 2's Womb Maṇḍala, Part 6's mudrās) is not thereby indulging in a lesser, merely provisional practice compared to one who contemplates formless emptiness directly — both are named here as legitimate, each accomplishing its own kind of siddhi, and the sūtra's actual instruction is to hold neither position exclusively. The markless samādhi chapter that follows applies the same "search through every possible location" method Part 1 used to search for mind through twelve sense-fields, now searching specifically for where a visualized image could originate — body, or mind — and finding, in both cases, only further emptiness; this collection's reading notes on Part 1 already flagged this exact search-method as the sūtra's single most frequently reused technique, and its reappearance here, in one of the very last doctrinal chapters before the sūtra's closing entrustment, marks it as a technique meant to remain live throughout a practitioner's entire path, not merely a beginner's exercise superseded by more advanced instruction.

Recitation, Worldly and Transcendent

Chinese Source

「一一諸真言，　作心意念誦，出入息為二，　常第一相應，異此而受持，　真言闕支分。內與外相應，　我說有四種，彼世間念誦，　有所緣相續，住種子字句，　或心隨本尊，故說有攀緣，　出入息為上。當知出世心，　遠離於諸字，自尊為一相，　無二無取著，不壞意色像，　勿異於法則。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"For every single mantra, recitation is made with the mind's own intention; the breath moving in and out forms a pair, and this is ever the most excellent way to be attuned to it — held and practiced in any other way, the mantra's own limbs remain incomplete. There are four kinds of attunement, inner and outer together, I say: worldly recitation has an object held continuously in mind, abiding on the seed-syllable's own phrase, or the mind following the deity contemplated — this is called recitation with an object of attention, and the breath moving in and out is its most excellent form. Know that the transcendent mind is far removed from every letter; its own honored object is a single mark alone, without duality, without any grasping, not destroying the mind's own visualized form, and yet never departing from the rule of the Dharma either."

Reading note

This short chapter's four kinds of recitation — attuned to the breath and a seed-syllable, attuned to a visualized deity, and, beyond both, a transcendent recitation "far removed from every letter" that nonetheless does not discard visualized form — closes out the sūtra's practical instruction with the same both/and structure just given for deity-bodies: worldly recitation with an object of focus is not superseded by transcendent recitation without one, but named as its own complete, legitimate stage, exactly as siddhi with form and siddhi without form were both affirmed in the preceding chapter.

The Closing Entrustment

Chinese Source

爾時世尊告一切眾會言：「汝今應當住不放逸。於此法門，若不知根性，不應授與他人，除我弟子具標相者。我今演說，汝等當一心聽。若於吉祥執宿時生、志求勝事、有微細慧、常念恩德、生渴仰心、聞法歡喜而住，其相青白或白色、廣首長頸、額廣平正、其鼻脩直面䩉圓滿、端嚴相稱，如是佛子應當殷勤而教授之。」爾時一切具威德者咸懷慶悅，聞已頂受，一心奉持。是諸眾會，以種種莊嚴廣大供養已，稽首佛足，恭敬合掌而說是言：「唯願於此法教演說救世加持句，令法眼道遍一切處久住世間。」爾時世尊於此法門說加持句真言曰：

「南麼三曼多勃馱喃(一)　薩婆他勝勝(二)　怛稜怛稜(三)　顒顒(四)　達[口*嶙]達[口*嶙](五)　娑他跛也娑他跛也(六)　勃馱薩底也嚩(七)　達摩薩底也嚩(八)　僧伽薩底也嚩(九)　[合*牛][合*牛](十)　吠娜尾吠(十一)　莎訶(十二)」

時佛說此經已，一切持金剛者及普賢等上首諸菩薩，聞佛所說，皆大歡喜，信受奉行。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One said to the whole assembly: "You should now abide free of heedlessness. Regarding this Dharma-gate, if one does not know a person's capacity, one should not confer it upon them — except upon my own disciple, one who bears the proper marks. I shall now explain this; listen with a single mind. Should one be born under an auspicious lunar mansion, seek surpassing things, possess a fine and subtle wisdom, constantly remember kindness received, feel a genuine thirst to learn, and dwell in joy on hearing the Dharma — bearing marks such as these: a complexion blue-white or white, a broad head and long neck, a wide and level forehead, a straight and well-formed nose, a full and rounded face, well-proportioned and fine of feature — such a child of the Buddha should be earnestly and diligently taught." At that time, every one of those present, possessed of majestic virtue, was filled with joy; hearing this, they received it with a bow of the head, and upheld it with a single mind. The whole assembly, having made every kind of vast and adorned offering, bowed their heads to the Buddha's feet, joined their palms in reverence, and spoke these words: "We beg only that, for this teaching, you speak the phrase that saves the world and empowers it, so that the eye of the Dharma, this path, may pervade every place and long abide in the world." At that time the World-Honored One spoke, for this Dharma-gate, this mantra of the empowering phrase:

(a closing protective mantra, retained in transliteration per this site's standing convention)

When the Buddha had finished speaking this sūtra, every vajra-holder, and Samantabhadra together with the other foremost bodhisattvas, on hearing what the Buddha had taught, were all filled with great joy, and received it with faith, and upheld it in practice.

Reading note

The closing formula — "were all filled with great joy, and received it with faith, and upheld it in practice" — is the standard closing line of a Buddhist sūtra, the same formula that ends texts across the entire canon regardless of school or period; its appearance here marks the genuine end of this sūtra's thirty-one-chapter core teaching, even though the text as preserved in Taishō continues for a further, distinct fascicle. The physiognomic description of a fit disciple — specific facial and bodily features alongside inward qualities of wisdom, gratitude, and joy — echoes the thirty-two marks of a buddha already referenced in Chapter Twenty-Two's letter-teaching, applied here at a human scale: not claiming a disciple must already resemble a buddha bodily, but describing the kind of person a teacher is instructed to actually look for, inward disposition and outward bearing read together rather than treated as separate criteria.

Part 9 Complete — and the Main Teaching Concludes

This closes Part 9, and with it the sūtra's thirty-one-chapter core teaching: the hundred syllables built on AṂ, the mirror of the self and the magician's illusion of an illusion, sound's own emptiness, the nature of bodhi beyond the three times, three samayas mapped onto the Three Jewels, four brief definitions of tathāgata and bodhisattva, forty-four Vedic fires reworked into twelve fires of wisdom, deity-bodies with and without form both affirmed, the markless samādhi, four kinds of recitation, and a final entrustment naming who is fit to receive it all. What remains in Taishō's own seventh fascicle is a distinct text in its own right: 供養次第法, the "Method of Sequential Offerings," a five-chapter ritual manual traditionally attributed to Buddhaguhya and appended to this sūtra in its printed editions — Parts 10 and 11 of this collection.
