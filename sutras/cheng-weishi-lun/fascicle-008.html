Fascicle 8 · 成唯識論卷第八

Causation, Karma, and the Three Natures

Fascicle 8 opens by completing the treatise's theory of causation: the ten causes, drawn from fifteen underlying bases, and how they map onto the two causes, the four conditions already explained, and the five kinds of fruit any cause can produce. It then turns to how birth-and-death continues without any external condition at all — through karma's habituation, the two graspings, and the twelve links of dependent origination, closing with the two kinds of birth-and-death. The fascicle's second half opens entirely new and foundational ground: the doctrine of the Three Natures — the imagined, the dependent, and the perfectly accomplished — one of this treatise's most important teachings, mapped here against nearly every other classificatory scheme the tradition uses.

The Ten Causes, from Fifteen Bases 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 釋分別生難 · 十因

Fascicle 7 closed with the four conditions. The treatise now shows these same four conditions, viewed through fifteen distinct underlying bases, yield a finer classification long used in Abhidharma scholarship: the ten causes.

The Fifteen Bases 釋「十五處」

Chinese Source

如是四緣，依十五處，義差別故，立為十因。云何此依十五處立？一、語依處：謂法名、想所起語性，即依此處立隨說因。謂依此語隨見、聞等，說諸義故。此即能說為所說因；有論說此是名、想、見，由如名字取相，執著隨起說故，若依彼說便顯此因是語依處。二、領受依處：謂所觀待能、所受性，即依此處立觀待因。謂觀待此令彼諸事或生、或住、或成、或得，此是彼觀待因。

Ru-Yi Translation

On the basis of these same four conditions, with reference to fifteen distinct bases, ten causes are established. How are these established with reference to fifteen bases?

First, the basis of verbal expression: the nature of speech arising from a dharma's name and conceptualization. It is with reference to this basis that the cause of expression-in-accordance is established — meaning that, relying on this speech, one expresses various meanings in accordance with what is seen, heard, and the like; this, then, is the capacity to express serving as the cause of what is expressed. Some treatises state that this consists in name, conceptualization, and view, since, just as a name grasps a characteristic, one comes to express things through the attachment that follows.

Second, the basis of reception: the nature of what is depended upon and what receives, in mutual regard. It is with reference to this basis that the cause of mutual dependence is established — meaning that, in dependence upon this, those various matters come to arise, abide, be accomplished, or be attained; this, then, is their cause of mutual dependence.

三、習氣依處：謂內、外種，未成熟位。即依此處，立牽引因。謂能牽引遠自果故。四、有潤種子依處：謂內、外種，已成熟位。即依此處，立生起因。謂能生起近自果故。五、無間滅依處：謂心、心所等無間緣。六、境界依處：謂心、心所所緣緣。七、根依處：謂心、心所所依六根。八、作用依處：謂於所作業、作具、作用，即除種子餘助現緣。九、士用依處：謂於所作業、作者、作用，即除種子餘作現緣。

Third, the basis of habituation: the stage at which inner and outer seeds have not yet ripened. It is with reference to this basis that the drawing-forth cause is established, since it is capable of drawing forth its own result from a distance.

Fourth, the basis of moistened seeds: the stage at which inner and outer seeds have already ripened. It is with reference to this basis that the arising cause is established, since it is capable of giving rise to its own result close at hand.

Fifth, the basis of immediately preceding cessation: mind and mental factors' own condition of immediately preceding sameness. Sixth, the basis of the sense-field: mind and mental factors' condition of the objective support. Seventh, the basis of the faculties: the six faculties on which mind and mental factors depend. Eighth, the basis of functional action: the deed done, the instrument of action, and the function itself — every manifest, assisting condition apart from seeds. Ninth, the basis of human effort: the deed done, the agent, and the function — every manifest condition of action apart from seeds.

十、真實見依處：謂無漏見，除引自種於無漏法能助引證。總依此六，立攝受因。謂攝受五辦有漏法，具攝受六辦無漏故。十一、隨順依處：謂無記、染、善現種諸行，能隨順同類勝品諸法。即依此處，立引發因。謂能引起同類勝行，及能引得無為法故。十二、差別功能依處：謂有為法，各於自果，有能起證差別勢力。即依此處，立定、異因。謂各能生自界等果，及各能得自乘果故。

Tenth, the basis of genuine insight: uncontaminated view, excepting the drawing-forth of its own seed, which is capable of assisting realization with respect to uncontaminated dharmas. It is with general reference to these six bases that the encompassing cause is established — meaning that, encompassing five of these bases, it brings about contaminated dharmas, while, encompassing all six, it brings about uncontaminated dharmas.

Eleventh, the basis of accordance: the neutral, defiled, and wholesome manifest and seed activities, capable of according with superior dharmas of their own kind. It is with reference to this basis that the eliciting cause is established, since it is capable of eliciting superior activity of its own kind, and of eliciting the attainment of unconditioned dharmas.

Twelfth, the basis of distinctive function: the distinct capacity each conditioned dharma has for giving rise to and realizing its own result. It is with reference to this basis that the fixed-and-differentiating cause is established, since each is capable of generating a result belonging to its own realm, and each is capable of attaining a result belonging to its own vehicle.

十三、和合依處：謂從領受，乃至差別功能依處，於所生、住、成、得果中，有和合力。即依此處，立同事因。謂從觀待乃至定、異，皆同生等，一事業故。十四、障礙依處：謂於生、住、成、得事中，能障礙法。即依此處，立相違因。謂彼能違生等事故。十五、不障礙依處：謂於生、住、成、得事中，不障礙法。即依此處，立不相違因。謂彼不違生等事故。

Thirteenth, the basis of concurrence: the power of concurrence, from the basis of reception up through the basis of distinctive function, with respect to the result to be generated, sustained, accomplished, or attained. It is with reference to this basis that the shared-task cause is established, since, from the cause of mutual dependence up through the fixed-and-differentiating cause, all share equally in a single task of generating and the like.

Fourteenth, the basis of obstruction: whatever dharma is capable of obstructing the matter of generating, sustaining, accomplishing, or attaining. It is with reference to this basis that the opposing cause is established, since it is capable of opposing that matter.

Fifteenth, the basis of non-obstruction: whatever dharma does not obstruct the matter of generating, sustaining, accomplishing, or attaining. It is with reference to this basis that the non-opposing cause is established, since it does not oppose that matter.

Reducing Ten Causes to Two 釋「十因二因所攝」

Chinese Source

如是十因，二因所攝：一、能生；二、方便。〈菩薩地〉說：「牽引種子、生起種子，名能生因；所餘諸因，方便因攝。」此說牽引、生起、引發、定異、同事、不相違中，諸因緣種未成熟位，名牽引種；已成熟位名生起種。彼六因中，諸因緣種，皆攝在此二位中故。雖有現起是能生因，如四因中生自種者，而多間斷，此略不說。或親辦果亦立種名，如說現行穀、麥等種。

Ru-Yi Translation

These ten causes are subsumed under two causes: the capable-of-generating cause, and the facilitating cause. The Bodhisattvabhūmi states: "the drawing-forth seed and the arising seed are called the capable-of-generating cause; every remaining cause is subsumed under the facilitating cause." This means that, among the drawing-forth, arising, eliciting, fixed-and-differentiating, shared-task, and non-opposing causes, whatever causal-condition seeds have not yet ripened are called drawing-forth seeds, and whatever have already ripened are called arising seeds. Although manifest activity, too, can be a capable-of-generating cause, this is often interrupted, and so is not discussed here in brief. Alternatively, whatever directly brings about a result is also called a "seed," as when one speaks of the manifest grain, wheat, and the like as "seeds."

所餘因謂初、二、五、九，及六因中非因緣法，皆是生、熟因緣種餘，故總說為方便因攝。非此二種，唯屬彼二因；餘四因中，有因緣種故。非唯彼八，名所餘因，彼二因亦有非因緣種故。有尋等地，說生起因，是能生因，餘方便攝。此文意說：六因中現種是因緣者皆名生起因；能親生起自類果故。此所餘因，皆方便攝。非此生起，唯屬彼因，餘五因中，有因緣故。非唯彼九，名所餘因，彼生起因中，有非因緣故。

The remaining causes — the first, second, fifth, and ninth, together with whatever within the six causes is not a causal-condition dharma — are all, being apart from the ripening and ripened causal-condition seeds, together subsumed under the facilitating cause. It is not that these two kinds of seed belong exclusively to those two causes, since the remaining four causes, too, contain causal-condition seeds; nor is it that only those eight are called the remaining causes, since those first two causes also contain seeds that are not causal-condition seeds.

The Stage with Coarse and Subtle Discursive Thought and the like states that the arising cause is the capable-of-generating cause, and the rest are subsumed under the facilitating cause. This passage means: among the six causes, whatever manifest activity or seed is a causal condition is called the arising cause, since it is capable of directly generating a result of its own kind; every remaining cause is subsumed under the facilitating cause. It is not that arising belongs exclusively to that one cause, since the remaining five causes, too, contain causal conditions; nor is it that only those nine are called the remaining causes, since the arising cause, too, contains what is not a causal condition.

或〈菩薩地〉所說「牽引、生起種子」，即彼二因；「所餘諸因」即彼餘八。雖二因內，有非能生因，而因緣種勝，顯故偏說；雖餘因內，有非方便因，而增上者多，顯故偏說。有尋等地說：「生起因是能生因；餘方便」者，「生起」即是彼生起因；「餘因」應知即彼餘九。雖生起中有非因緣種，而去果近，親顯故偏說；雖牽引中亦有因緣種，而去果遠，親隱故不說。餘方便攝，准上應知。

Alternatively, the Bodhisattvabhūmi's statement of "the drawing-forth and arising seeds" refers to those same two causes, and "every remaining cause" refers to the remaining eight. Although, within these two causes, there is also what is not the capable-of-generating cause, the causal-condition seed is the more prominent and evident, and so is spoken of preferentially; although, within the remaining causes, there is also what is not the facilitating cause, the dominant condition is present in the majority, and so is spoken of preferentially.

As for the Stage with Coarse and Subtle Discursive Thought's statement that "the arising cause is the capable-of-generating cause, and the rest are facilitating" — "arising" refers to that same arising cause, and "the remaining causes" should be understood as the remaining nine. Although, within the arising cause, there is also what is not a causal-condition seed, it is close to its result, directly and evidently so, and hence spoken of preferentially; although, within the drawing-forth cause, too, there is a causal-condition seed, it is distant from its result, and hence not spoken of this way. That the rest are subsumed under the facilitating cause should be understood by analogy with what has already been explained.

Reading note: The ten causes are best read as a finer-grained lens on the same four conditions already explained in fascicle 7, not a competing theory — the treatise is explicit that all fifteen bases and ten causes reduce back to those four conditions, and further reduce to just two: whatever directly generates its own result (the capable-of-generating cause) and everything else that merely assists or does not obstruct that generation (the facilitating cause). This scholastic layering — four conditions, refined into fifteen bases and ten causes, then collapsed back into two — is characteristic of the treatise's method throughout: multiple traditional classification schemes, drawn from different source texts, are shown to be compatible restatements of the same underlying reality rather than rival theories to choose between.

How the Four Conditions Map onto the Ten Causes 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 十因與四緣相攝

Having derived the ten causes from the four conditions, the treatise now runs the derivation the other way, showing precisely which of the four conditions corresponds to which cause and basis.

Seeds, Succession, Objects, and Everything Else 釋四緣所依十五處

Chinese Source

所說四緣，依何處立？復如何攝十因、二因？論說因緣，依種子立；依無間滅，立等無間；依境界，立所緣；依所餘，立增上。此中種子，即是三、四、十一、十二、十三、十五，六依處中因緣種攝；雖現四處，亦有因緣，而多間斷，此略不說。或彼亦能親辦自果，如外麥等，亦立種名。或種子言，唯屬第四，親、疎、隱、顯，取捨如前。

Ru-Yi Translation

With reference to which bases are the four conditions already discussed established? And how, in turn, do they subsume the ten causes and the two causes? The treatise states: the causal condition is established with reference to seeds; the condition of immediately preceding sameness is established with reference to immediately preceding cessation; the condition of the objective support is established with reference to the sense-field; the dominant condition is established with reference to everything else.

Here, "seeds" refers to the causal-condition seeds subsumed within the third, fourth, eleventh, twelfth, thirteenth, and fifteenth bases — although the fourth basis, too, involves manifest activity and thus also has a causal condition, this is often interrupted, and so is not discussed here in brief. Alternatively, this, too, is capable of directly bringing about its own result, as with external wheat and the like, and so is also called a "seed." Or else the term "seed" refers exclusively to the fourth basis, its inclusion or exclusion following the same pattern as above.

言無間滅、境界處者，應知總顯二緣依處，非唯五、六，餘依處中，亦有中間二緣義故。或唯五、六，餘處雖有，而少、隱故，略不說之。論說因緣，能生因攝；增上緣性，即方便因；中間二緣，攝受因攝。雖方便內具後三緣，而增上多，故此偏說；餘因亦有中間二緣，然攝受中，顯故偏說。初能生攝，進退如前。

As for speaking of the bases of immediately preceding cessation and the sense-field — these should be understood as broadly indicating the bases for these two conditions, and not exclusively the fifth and sixth bases, since the remaining bases, too, involve the meaning of these two intervening conditions.

The treatise states: the causal condition is subsumed under the capable-of-generating cause; the nature of the dominant condition is precisely the facilitating cause; the two intervening conditions are subsumed under the encompassing cause. Although the facilitating cause, too, possesses all three of the latter conditions, the dominant condition predominates, and so this is spoken of preferentially; although the remaining causes, too, involve the two intervening conditions, they are more evident within the encompassing cause, and so this, too, is spoken of preferentially.

Reading note: This section runs the previous one's derivation in reverse, and the two together form a single closed loop: the four conditions generate the fifteen bases and ten causes, and the fifteen bases and ten causes fold straight back into the four conditions, with nothing left over on either side. The repeated qualifier "spoken of preferentially" is doing real work throughout — the treatise is not claiming these correspondences are mutually exclusive, only that each classification highlights whichever relationship is most prominent from its own angle, the same "economy of explanation" principle already seen governing which mental factors and which perfections earn a separate name elsewhere in the treatise.

The Five Fruits 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 五果

Every cause and condition must produce some result. The treatise closes its theory of causation with a fivefold classification of results, and a detailed table showing which causes and conditions produce which kind of fruit.

Five Kinds of Fruit 釋「五果」

Chinese Source

所說因、緣，必應有果。此果有幾？依何處得？果有五種：一者異熟，謂有漏善及不善法所招自相續異熟生無記；二者等流，謂習善等所引同類，或似先業，後果隨轉；三者離繫，謂無漏道斷障所證善無為法；四者士用，謂諸作者，假諸作具，所辦事業；五者增上，謂除前四，餘所得果。

Ru-Yi Translation

The causes and conditions already discussed must certainly have results. How many kinds of result are there, and with reference to which bases are they obtained? There are five kinds of result: first, the ripened result, meaning the neutral, karmically-resultant state, belonging to one's own continuum, brought about by contaminated wholesome and unwholesome dharmas; second, the outflow result, meaning a similar kind of result drawn forth by cultivated wholesome states and the like, which either resembles the prior karma or follows after it accordingly; third, the result of liberation from bondage, meaning the wholesome, unconditioned dharma realized through the uncontaminated path's elimination of obstruction; fourth, the result of human effort, meaning the task accomplished by various agents by means of various instruments; fifth, the dominant result, meaning every result obtained apart from the previous four.

《瑜伽》等說：習氣依處，得異熟果；隨順依處，得等流果；真見依處，得離繫果；士用依處，得士用果；所餘依處，得增上果。習氣處言，顯諸依處感異熟果一切功能；隨順處言，顯諸依處引等流果一切功能；真見處言，顯諸依處證離繫果一切功能；士用處言，顯諸依處招士用果一切功能；所餘處言，顯諸依處得增上果一切功能。不爾，便應太寬、太狹。

The Yogācārabhūmi and other texts state: the basis of habituation obtains the ripened result; the basis of accordance obtains the outflow result; the basis of genuine insight obtains the result of liberation from bondage; the basis of human effort obtains the result of human effort; every remaining basis obtains the dominant result. Each of these five phrases indicates the entire capacity, across every one of the fifteen bases, for bringing about its corresponding result — were this not so, the classification would be either too broad or too narrow.

或習氣者，唯屬第三，雖異熟因餘處亦有，此處亦有非異熟因；而異熟因，去果相遠，習氣亦爾，故此偏說。隨順唯屬第十一處，雖等流果餘處亦得，此處亦得非等流果；而此因招勝行相顯，隨順亦爾，故偏說之。真見處言，唯詮第十，雖證離繫餘處亦能，此處亦能得非離繫；而此證離繫，相顯故偏說。士用處言，唯詮第九，雖士用果餘處亦招，此處亦能招增上等；而名相顯，是故偏說。所餘唯屬餘十一處，雖十一處亦得餘果，招增上果餘處亦能；而此十一多招增上，餘已顯餘，故此偏說。

Alternatively, "habituation" refers exclusively to the third basis, since the ripening cause is distant from its result, and habituation is of this same character; "accordance" refers exclusively to the eleventh basis, since this cause's eliciting of superior activity is especially evident, as is accordance; "the basis of genuine insight" refers exclusively to the tenth basis, since this basis's realization of liberation from bondage is especially evident; "the basis of human effort" refers exclusively to the ninth basis, since the name and characteristic here are especially evident; and "every remaining basis" refers exclusively to the remaining eleven bases, since these mostly bring about the dominant result, while the rest have already been shown to bring about other results.

Which Causes and Conditions Produce Which Fruit 五果與十因、四緣相攝

Chinese Source

如是即說此五果中：若異熟果，牽引、生起、定異、同事、不相違因，增上緣得；若等流果，牽引、生起、攝受、引發、定異、同事、不相違因，初、後緣得；若離繫果，攝受、引發、定異、同事、不相違因，增上緣得；若士用果，有義：觀待、攝受、同事、不相違因，增上緣得，有義：觀待、牽引、生起、攝受、引發、定異、同事、不相違因，除所緣緣，餘三緣得；若增上果，十因、四緣一切容得。

Ru-Yi Translation

This being so, it may accordingly be said: the ripened result is obtained through the drawing-forth, arising, fixed-and-differentiating, shared-task, and non-opposing causes, by way of the dominant condition. The outflow result is obtained through the drawing-forth, arising, encompassing, eliciting, fixed-and-differentiating, shared-task, and non-opposing causes, by way of the first and last conditions. The result of liberation from bondage is obtained through the encompassing, eliciting, fixed-and-differentiating, shared-task, and non-opposing causes, by way of the dominant condition.

As for the result of human effort — one view holds it is obtained through the mutual-dependence, encompassing, shared-task, and non-opposing causes, by way of the dominant condition; another view holds it is obtained through the mutual-dependence, drawing-forth, arising, encompassing, eliciting, fixed-and-differentiating, shared-task, and non-opposing causes, by way of the latter three conditions, excepting the condition of the objective support. The dominant result may be obtained through any of the ten causes and four conditions.

Reading note: This closing cross-reference table is dense scholastic bookkeeping, and it is not meant to be memorized cell by cell so much as absorbed as a general picture: causation in this system is never a single, undifferentiated relationship, but a layered structure of distinct kinds of cause producing distinct kinds of fruit through distinct kinds of condition — a ripened result unfolds on a different causal track than, say, the result of liberation from bondage, which is precisely why enlightenment cannot simply "ripen" the way ordinary karmic consequences do, but must instead be realized through the uncontaminated path's elimination of obstruction. The one result available through every cause and every condition without exception — the dominant result — functions almost as a catch-all category for the countless weaker, background influences one thing has on another, distinct from the handful of results with a clean, traceable causal line back to their source.

Applying the Four Conditions Within a Single Mind 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 釋分別生難結歸

With the full apparatus of causes, bases, and fruits now in place, the treatise returns to its actual purpose: showing precisely how the four conditions operate entirely within consciousness, seed to manifest activity, consciousness to consciousness, with no need for anything external at all.

Seeds, Manifest Activity, and the Eight Consciousnesses 釋「本識中種容作三緣」

Chinese Source

傍論已了，應辯正論。本識中種，容作三緣，生現分別，除等無間。謂各親種，是彼因緣；為所緣緣，於能緣者；若種於彼，有能助力，或不障礙，是增上緣。生淨現行，應知亦爾。現起分別，展轉相望，容作三緣，無因緣故。謂有情類自、他，展轉容作二緣，除等無間。

Ru-Yi Translation

The digression has now concluded; the main topic should be addressed. The seeds within the root consciousness are capable of serving as three of the four conditions in generating manifest discrimination, excepting the condition of immediately preceding sameness. That is: each seed, with respect to its own direct result, is that result's causal condition; it serves as the condition of the objective support for whatever consciousness takes it as an object; and, if a seed has the capacity to assist, or does not obstruct, some other dharma, it is that dharma's dominant condition. The generation of pure manifest activity should be understood the same way.

Manifest discrimination, considered in mutual relation, is likewise capable of serving as three conditions, lacking only the causal condition. That is: among different classes of sentient being, one's own and another's, considered in mutual relation, are capable of serving as two conditions, excepting the condition of immediately preceding sameness.

自八識聚，展轉相望，定有增上緣，必無等無間。所緣緣義，或無或有：八於七有；七於八無。餘七非八所仗質故。第七於六五無一有，餘六於彼一切皆無。第六於五無，餘五於彼有。五識唯託第八相故。自類前後，第六容三，餘除所緣，取現境故。許五後見緣前相者，五、七前後亦有三緣。前七於八，所緣容有，能熏成彼相、見種故。

One's own eight consciousness-clusters, considered in mutual relation, certainly have the dominant condition, and certainly lack the condition of immediately preceding sameness. As for the condition of the objective support, this may be absent or present: the eighth serves as this condition for the seventh, but the seventh does not serve as this condition for the eighth, since the remaining seven are not the underlying basis the eighth relies upon. The seventh lacks this condition with respect to the sixth and the five, having it with respect to only one; the remaining six entirely lack this condition with respect to the seventh. The sixth lacks this condition with respect to the five, while the five have it with respect to the sixth, since the five sense-consciousnesses rely solely on the eighth consciousness's perceived aspect.

Within its own kind, before and after, the sixth is capable of all three conditions; the rest, excepting the condition of the objective support, take only present objects. For those who grant that a later moment of the five sense-consciousnesses can take an earlier moment's aspect as its object, the five and the seventh, too, before and after, likewise have all three conditions. The first seven consciousnesses, with respect to the eighth, can also possess the condition of the objective support, since they are capable of perfuming and forming its perceived and perceiving-aspect seeds.

同聚異體，展轉相望，唯有增上。諸相應法，所仗質同，不相緣故。或依見分，說不相緣；依相分說，有相緣義。謂諸相分，互為質起，如識中種，為觸等相質。不爾，無色，彼應無境故；設許變色，亦定緣種，勿見分境不同質故。同體相分，為見二緣；見分於彼，但有增上。見與自證，相望亦爾。餘二展轉，俱作二緣。此中不依種相分說，但說現起互為緣故。

Within the same cluster, distinct essences, considered in mutual relation, have only the dominant condition, since the various associated dharmas, relying on the same underlying basis, do not take one another as their object. Or else, with reference to the perceiving aspect, it is said that they do not take one another as object; with reference to the perceived aspect, there is a sense in which they do — meaning that the various perceived aspects arise serving as one another's underlying basis, just as a seed within consciousness serves as the underlying basis for the perceived aspect of contact and the rest. Were this not so, in the formless realm these would have no object at all; and, even granting that they transform into color-form, they would still necessarily take a seed as their object, lest the perceiving aspect's object fail to share the same underlying basis.

The perceived aspect, belonging to the same essence, serves the perceiving aspect as two conditions; the perceiving aspect, with respect to it, has only the dominant condition. The perceiving aspect and the self-witnessing aspect, considered in mutual relation, are the same way. The remaining two, considered in mutual relation, each serve as two conditions for the other. This discussion does not concern the perceived aspect as seed, but speaks only of manifest activity mutually serving as a condition.

淨八識聚，自、他展轉，皆有所緣，能遍緣故，唯除見分，非相所緣，相分理無能緣用故。既現分別緣種現生，種亦理應緣現種起，現種於種能作幾緣？種必不由中二緣起，待心、心所立彼二故。現於親種具作二緣，與非親種但為增上。種望親種亦具二緣，於非親種亦但增上。

For the pure eight consciousness-clusters, one's own and another's, considered in mutual relation, all possess the condition of the objective support, since these are capable of universally taking one another as object — excepting only the perceiving aspect, which is not itself an object taken by another perceived aspect, since a perceived aspect, by its very nature, has no capacity to take an object at all.

Since manifest discrimination arises taking both seeds and manifest activity as its object, seeds, too, should by the same reasoning arise taking manifest activity and seeds as their object — how many conditions, then, can manifest activity and seeds serve as for seeds? Seeds certainly do not arise through either of the two intervening conditions, since these two are established only with reference to mind and mental factors. Manifest activity serves its own direct seed as two conditions, and serves a seed that is not its own only as the dominant condition. A seed, with respect to its own direct seed, likewise serves as two conditions, and, with respect to a seed that is not its own, likewise serves only as the dominant condition.

依斯內識互為緣起，分別因、果，理、教皆成。所執外緣，設有無用，況違理、教，何固執為？雖「分別」言，總顯三界心及心所，而隨勝者，諸聖教中多門顯示，或說為二、三、四、五等。如餘論中，具廣分別。

On this basis, the inner consciousnesses' mutual functioning as conditions, and the discrimination of cause and result, are established by both reason and scriptural teaching alike. Even granting that the externally-grasped-at condition existed, it would serve no purpose — how much less so, then, when it stands in conflict with both reason and scriptural teaching? Why cling to it so stubbornly?

Although the term "discrimination" broadly indicates the minds and mental factors of the three realms, the sacred teachings, following whatever is most prominent in each context, display this through many different approaches, at times speaking of it as two, three, four, five, and so on — as is fully and extensively distinguished in other treatises.

Reading note: This section is the payoff for the entire ten-causes-and-five-fruits detour: it maps every one of the four conditions onto the actual, moment-to-moment relationships between seeds, manifest consciousness, and the eight consciousnesses themselves, showing exhaustively that all of it can be accounted for without positing anything external. The asymmetric map of which consciousness can take which other consciousness as its object — the eighth serving as the seventh's object but never the reverse, the five senses depending on the eighth's perceived aspect but not on each other — reads almost like a wiring diagram of the mind, and is worth holding onto for later fascicles, since this same dependency structure keeps resurfacing whenever the treatise discusses how one consciousness perfumes or is perfumed by another.

The closing line is a fitting close to the entire "external condition" objection that has run since the start of the four-conditions discussion: even granting, for argument's sake, that some external condition existed, it would add nothing the inner-consciousness account cannot already explain — so clinging to it is not just contrary to reason and scripture, but simply pointless. This is characteristic of how the treatise closes its more technical detours: not just refuting the rival position, but showing that even a maximally charitable version of it would be causally idle.

Karma's Habituation, and the Two Graspings 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 釋生死相續難

The treatise turns to a new objection: if there is inner consciousness but no external condition, what actually keeps sentient beings cycling through birth-and-death? A twelfth root verse answers with two kinds of habituation working together.

The Verse: Two Habituations Together 頌釋「業、二取習氣」

Chinese Source

雖有內識，而無外緣，由何有情生死相續？頌曰：由諸業習氣，二取習氣俱；前異熟既盡，復生餘異熟。論曰：諸業謂福、非福、不動，即有漏善、不善思業。業之眷屬亦立業名，同招引、滿異熟果故。此雖纔起，無間即滅，無義能招當異熟果，而熏本識，起自功能，即此功能，說為習氣，是業氣分，熏習所成，簡曾現業，故名習氣。如是習氣，展轉相續，至成熟時，招異熟果，此顯當果勝增上緣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Although inner consciousness exists, with no external condition at all, how do sentient beings continue through birth-and-death? The verse states: "It is through the habituation of karma, together with the habituation of the two graspings, that, once the former ripening is exhausted, a further ripening is again produced."

The treatise states: the various karmic acts are meritorious, demeritorious, and unmoving — that is, contaminated wholesome and unwholesome volitional actions. What accompanies these acts is likewise called "karma," since it shares in drawing forth and completing the ripened result. Although this karmic act, once it arises, ceases immediately without interruption, it would make no sense for it to be capable of directly bringing about a future ripened result on its own — rather, it perfumes the root consciousness, giving rise to its own functional capacity, and it is precisely this functional capacity that is called "habituation": karma's own residual trace, brought about through habituation, distinguished from the karmic act itself — hence it is called "habituation." Such habituation, continuing in unbroken succession, brings about the ripened result once it reaches maturity — this shows the future result's dominant condition.

相見、名色、心及心所、本末，彼取皆二取攝。彼所熏發，親能生彼本識上功能，名二取習氣。此顯來世異熟果心，及彼相應諸因緣種。「俱」謂業種、二取種俱，是疎、親緣，互相助義。業招生顯，故頌先說。「前異熟」者，謂前前生業異熟果；「餘異熟」者，謂後後生業異熟果。

The perceiving and perceived aspects, name and form, mind and mental factors, the fundamental and the derivative — every instance of grasping among these is included within the two graspings. The functional capacity within the root consciousness that this grasping perfumes and brings forth, and that is directly capable of generating this same grasping again, is called the habituation of the two graspings. This shows the mind of the ripened result in a future life, together with its associated causal-condition seeds. "Together" means that the karmic seed and the seed of the two graspings occur together, standing in an indirect and direct conditional relationship, mutually assisting one another.

Since it is karma that draws forth and makes manifest rebirth, the verse mentions it first. "The former ripening" refers to the ripened result of karma from an earlier life; "a further ripening" refers to the ripened result of karma from a subsequent life.

雖二取種受果無窮，而業習氣受果有盡，由異熟果性別難招；等流、增上性同易感。由感餘生業等種熟，前異熟果受用盡時，復別能生餘異熟果。由斯生死輪轉無窮，何假外緣方得相續？此頌意說：由業、二取，生死輪迴皆不離識，心、心所法為彼性故。

Although the seed of the two graspings receives its result without end, the habituation of karma's receiving of its result is finite, since a ripened result, differing in kind from its cause, is difficult to bring about, while an outflow or dominant result, being of the same kind as its cause, is readily brought about. On account of the ripening of the seeds belonging to karma that brings forth a further life, once the former ripened result's experience is exhausted, a further, distinct ripened result is again generated. It is through this that the wheel of birth-and-death turns without end — what need is there to posit an external condition before this continuity can occur? This verse means to say: through karma and the two graspings, the entire cycle of birth-and-death is never apart from consciousness, since mind and mental factors are its very nature.

Reading note: The distinction drawn here between karma's habituation and the two graspings' habituation is worth holding onto, since it explains something that might otherwise look like a contradiction: karma's own habituation is explicitly finite — a given act's power to ripen is eventually used up — yet birth-and-death itself never runs dry. The resolution is that karma's habituation and the habituation of the two graspings (the deep-seated grasping at a perceiving subject and a perceived object) work on different tracks and different timescales: individual karmic debts are paid off and exhausted one at a time, while the underlying grasping that sets the whole machinery in motion keeps generating fresh karma to replace what is spent. Birth-and-death, on this account, is not one continuous thing running down like a battery, but a self-renewing cycle, and this is precisely why liberation requires uprooting the graspings themselves, not merely working through one's existing karmic backlog.

Three Kinds of Habituation, and the Root of Suffering 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 三種習氣 · 惑業苦

The treatise now offers two complementary ways of analyzing the same continuity of birth-and-death: first, a threefold breakdown of habituation itself, and then the classical framework of affliction, karma, and suffering — which it identifies as nothing other than the twelve links of dependent origination.

Naming, Self-Attachment, and Existence 釋「三種習氣」

Chinese Source

復次，生死相續，由諸習氣。然諸習氣總有三種：一、名言習氣，謂有為法各別親種。名言有二：一表義名言，即能詮義，音聲差別；二顯境名言，即能了境心、心所法。隨二名言所熏成種，作有為法各別因緣。二、我執習氣，謂虛妄執我、我所種。我執有二：一、俱生我執，即修所斷我、我所執；二、分別我執，即見所斷我、我所執。隨二我執所熏成種，令有情等自、他差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, birth-and-death's continuity is brought about by the various habituations. These habituations, broadly speaking, are of three kinds. First, the habituation of verbal designation, meaning each conditioned dharma's own distinct, direct seed. Verbal designation is of two kinds: expressive verbal designation, meaning the distinct sound capable of conveying meaning; and object-revealing verbal designation, meaning the mind and mental factors capable of cognizing an object. The seeds formed through the habituation of these two kinds of verbal designation serve as each conditioned dharma's own distinct causal condition.

Second, the habituation of self-attachment, meaning the seed of the false attachment to a self and to what belongs to a self. Self-attachment is of two kinds: the innate attachment to self, eliminated on the path of cultivation; and the discriminated attachment to self, eliminated on the path of seeing. The seeds formed through the habituation of these two kinds of self-attachment bring about the distinction between self and other among sentient beings.

三、有支習氣，謂招三界異熟業種。有支有二：一、有漏善，即是能招可愛果業；二、諸不善，即是能招非愛果業。隨二有支所熏成種，令異熟果善、惡趣別。應知我執、有支習氣，於差別果是增上緣。此頌所言「業習氣」者，應知即是有支習氣；「二取習氣」，應知即是我執、名言二種習氣。取我、我所及取名言而熏成故，皆說名「取」。「俱」等餘文，義如前釋。

Third, the habituation of the link of existence, meaning the seed of karma that brings about a ripened result within the three realms. This link is of two kinds: contaminated wholesome karma, capable of bringing about a desirable result; and unwholesome karma, capable of bringing about an undesirable result. The seeds formed through the habituation of these two kinds bring about the distinction between wholesome and unwholesome destinies in the ripened result. It should be understood that the habituation of self-attachment and of the link of existence serve as the dominant condition for this distinction in results.

"The habituation of karma," as spoken of in the earlier verse, should be understood as precisely the habituation of the link of existence; "the habituation of the two graspings" should be understood as precisely the two habituations of self-attachment and of verbal designation — since both are formed through habituation by grasping at a self and what belongs to self, and by grasping at verbal designation, both are called "grasping."

Affliction, Karma, and Suffering 釋「惑、業、苦」

Chinese Source

復次，生死相續，由惑、業、苦。發業、潤生煩惱，名惑；能感後有諸業，名業；業所引生眾苦，名苦。惑、業、苦種，皆名習氣。前二習氣，與生死苦為增上緣，助生苦故。第三習氣，望生死苦能作因緣，親生苦故。此惑、業、苦，應知總攝十二有支。謂從無明乃至老死，如論廣釋。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, birth-and-death's continuity is brought about by affliction, karma, and suffering. The affliction that initiates karma and moistens rebirth is called "affliction"; the karma capable of bringing about a subsequent existence is called "karma"; the manifold suffering that karma draws forth and gives rise to is called "suffering." The seeds of all three are called "habituation." The first two serve as the dominant condition for the suffering of birth-and-death, assisting in generating it; the third serves as its causal condition, directly generating it.

It should be understood that affliction, karma, and suffering together encompass the twelve links of existence, from ignorance up through old age and death, as is extensively explained in the treatise.

Reading note: These two frameworks — three habituations, and affliction-karma-suffering — are not competing theories but two different cuts through the same material, and the treatise is explicit that they line up: the habituation of the link of existence is karma; the habituations of self-attachment and verbal designation together form the "two graspings." What is worth noticing is where the deepest root is located in each account: not in karma itself (which is more like the currency the system trades in) but in the two kinds of grasping — the primal, near-instinctive sense that there is a "self" here and "objects" out there — that keeps generating fresh karma in the first place. This is why the treatise identifies affliction, not karma, as the first and most fundamental of the three: karma is what affliction produces, and suffering is what karma yields.

The Twelve Links, Grouped into Four 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 十二有支

The treatise now works through the classical twelve links of dependent origination one group at a time — organizing them into four functional groups, defining each link precisely, and closing with an extended cross-tabulation against reality-status, moral quality, realm, feeling, the truths, and the elimination path.

Drawing Forth, Drawn Forth, Generating, Generated 釋「十二支略攝為四」

Chinese Source

然十二支，略攝為四：一、能引支：謂無明、行，能引識等五果種故。此中無明，唯取能發正感後世善、惡業者，即彼所發，乃名為行。由此一切順現受業，別助當業，皆非行支。二、所引支：謂本識內親生當來異熟果，攝識等五種，是前二支所引發故。此中識種，謂本識因，除後三因，餘因皆是名色種攝。後之三因，如名次第，即後三種。或名色種，總攝五因，於中隨勝，立餘四種，六處與識，總別亦然。

Ru-Yi Translation

The twelve links, however, may be summarized under four groups. First, the drawing-forth links: ignorance and volitional formations, since these are capable of drawing forth the seeds of the five subsequent results, consciousness and the rest. Here, "ignorance" refers exclusively to whatever is capable of initiating wholesome or unwholesome karma that genuinely brings about a future life; what this initiates is, precisely, called "volitional formations." On this account, every karmic act experienced within the very life it is performed, or that merely assists some other karma still to come, is not included within the link of volitional formations.

Second, the drawn-forth links: the seeds of consciousness and the other four results, directly generated within the root consciousness, that will produce the future ripened result — drawn forth by the previous two links. Here, the seed of consciousness refers to the cause that is the root consciousness itself; apart from the latter three causes, every other cause is included within the seed of name-and-form. These latter three causes, in the order named, are precisely the latter three seeds.

《集論》說識亦是能引，識中業種，名識支故；異熟識種，名色攝故。經說識支通能、所引，業種、識種，俱名識故；識是名色依，非名色攝故。識等五種由業熏發，雖實同時，而依主伴、總別、勝劣、因果相異，故諸聖教假說前後，或依當來、現起分位有次第故，說有前後。由斯識等，亦說現行；因時定無現行義故。復由此說生、引同時；潤、未潤時必不俱故。

The Abhidharmasamuccaya states that consciousness, too, can be a drawing-forth link, since the karmic seed within consciousness is called the link of consciousness, while the seed of the ripening consciousness is included within name-and-form. The sūtras state that the link of consciousness extends to both the drawing-forth and the drawn-forth aspects, since both the karmic seed and the consciousness-seed are alike called "consciousness" — consciousness being the basis on which name-and-form depend, rather than being included within it.

Although the seeds of consciousness and the other four results, perfumed and brought forth by karma, genuinely arise at the same time, yet, because they differ in their relationship as principal and accompanying, general and specific, superior and inferior, cause and result, the sacred teachings provisionally speak of them as sequential; or else, with reference to the positional sequence of their future manifest arising, they are spoken of as sequential. On this account, consciousness and the rest are also spoken of as manifest activity — though, at the causal stage, they certainly have no meaning as manifest activity. It is also on this basis that drawing-forth and being drawn forth are said to occur simultaneously, since the moistened and not-yet-moistened states can certainly never coincide.

三、能生支：謂愛、取、有，近生當來生、老死故。謂緣迷內異熟果愚，發正能招後有諸業為緣，引發親生當來生、老死位五果種已，復依迷外增上果愚，緣境界受，發起貪愛，緣愛復生欲等四取，愛、取合潤，能引業種及所引因，轉名為「有」，俱能近有後有果故。有處唯說業種名「有」，此能正感異熟果故。復有唯說五種名有，親生當來識等種故。

Third, the generating links: craving, grasping, and existence, since these give rise, in the near term, to birth and old-age-and-death still to come. That is: conditioned by delusion regarding the inner ripened result, one initiates karma genuinely capable of bringing about a subsequent existence, drawing forth and directly generating the seeds of the five results at the stage of future birth and old-age-and-death; then, further conditioned by delusion regarding the outer dominant result, one takes feeling regarding the sense-field as a condition and gives rise to craving; craving in turn generates the four kinds of grasping, desire and the rest; and craving and grasping, jointly moistening the drawn-forth karmic seed and cause, thereby transform into what is called "existence," since both are capable of nearly bringing about the result of a subsequent existence.

四、所生支：謂生、老死，是愛、取、有近所生故。謂從「中有」至「本有」中，未衰變來，皆「生支」攝，諸衰變位，總名為老；身壞命終，乃名為「死」。老非定有，附死立支。病何非支？不遍、定故。老雖不定，遍故立支，諸界、趣、生除中夭者，將終皆有衰朽行故。名色不遍，何故立支？定故立支，胎、卵、濕生者，六處未滿，定有名色故。又名色支，亦是遍有。有色化生，初受生位，雖具五根，而未有用，爾時未名六處支故。初生無色，雖定有意根而不明了，未名意處故；由斯論說，十二有支，一切一分，上二界有。

Fourth, the generated links: birth and old-age-and-death, since these are what craving, grasping, and existence give rise to in the near term. That is: from the intermediate-state existence up through the actual, embodied existence, everything prior to decline and change is included within the link of birth; every stage of decline and change is collectively called "old age"; the body's destruction and the ending of the life-faculty are, precisely, called "death." Old age is not always present, and is established as a link only in connection with death.

Why is illness not established as a link? Because it is neither universal nor fixed. Although old age, too, is not fixed, it is universal, and so is established as a link, since, in every realm, destiny, and form of birth, excepting those who die prematurely, one invariably undergoes the activity of decline and decay as death approaches.

Name-and-form is not universal — why, then, is it established as a link? Because it is fixed: for those born from a womb, an egg, or moisture, the six sense-fields are not yet complete, and name-and-form is certainly present. It is, moreover, also universally present: for a being born through spontaneous generation, at the very first moment of rebirth, though the five faculties are already complete, they do not yet have function, and so this is not yet called the link of the six sense-fields; at the first moment of rebirth in the formless realm, the mental faculty, though certainly present, is not yet clear and distinct — hence the treatise states that, of the twelve links, a portion of each is present in the two upper realms.

愛非遍有，寧別立支？生惡趣者，不愛彼故。定故別立，不求無有生善趣者，定有愛故。不還潤生，愛雖不起，然如彼取，定有種故。又愛亦遍，生惡趣者，於現我境，亦有愛故。依無希求，惡趣身愛，經說非有，非彼全無。何緣所生立生、老死？所引別立識等五支？「因位」難知差別相故，依當果位，別立五支。謂續生時，因識相顯，次根未滿，名色相增；次根滿時，六處明盛；依斯發觸，因觸起受；爾時乃名受果究竟。依此果位，立因為五。果位易了，差別相故，總立二支，以顯三苦。然所生果若在未來，為生厭故，說生、老死；若至現在，為令了知分位相生，說識等五。

Craving is not universally present — why, then, is it separately established as a link? Because those born into an unfortunate destiny do not crave that destiny. It is separately established because it is fixed: those born into a fortunate destiny who do not seek non-existence certainly have craving. For a non-returner moistening rebirth, though craving does not manifestly arise, still, just as with grasping, its seed is certainly present. Craving, moreover, is also universal: even one born into an unfortunate destiny has craving with respect to the self that presently appears to them — it is with reference to the absence of any wish for it that the sūtras state there is no craving for an unfortunate destiny's body, not that it is entirely absent.

Why are the generated links established as birth and old-age-and-death, while the drawn-forth links are separately established as consciousness and the other four? Because the distinct characteristics of the causal stage are difficult to discern, five links are separately established with reference to the corresponding future result: at the moment of continued rebirth, the characteristic of consciousness as cause is prominent; next, once the faculties are not yet complete, the characteristic of name-and-form increases; next, once the faculties are complete, the six sense-fields become clear and vigorous; on this basis, contact arises, and, through contact, feeling arises — at that point, the result of feeling is complete. It is with reference to this stage of result that the cause is established as five links. The stage of result, being comparatively easy to discern, is established in general as only two links, in order to display the three kinds of suffering — spoken of as birth and old-age-and-death when the result still lies in the future, in order to induce aversion toward it, and as consciousness and the other five once it has arrived in the present, in order to make its positional sequence known.

Ignorance, Craving, and the Links' Moral Character 釋「發業潤生」· 十二支性、界

Chinese Source

何緣發業總立無明，潤業位中別立愛、取？雖諸煩惱皆能發、潤，而發業位無明力增，以具十一殊勝事故。謂所緣等，廣如經說。於潤業位，愛力偏增，說愛如水，能沃潤故。要數溉灌方生「有」芽。且依初、後，分愛、取二，無重發義，立一無明。雖取支中，攝諸煩惱，而愛潤勝，說是愛增。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is it that, at the stage of initiating karma, ignorance alone is generally established as the link, while, at the stage of moistening karma, craving and grasping are separately established? Although every affliction is capable of both initiating and moistening karma, at the stage of initiating karma, the power of ignorance is especially pronounced, since it alone possesses eleven distinguishing features. At the stage of moistening karma, the power of craving is especially pronounced, and craving is compared to water, capable of soaking and moistening — for it is only through repeated irrigation that the sprout of "existence" can grow. With reference to the earlier and later stages, craving and grasping are distinguished as two, without any sense of repeated initiation, and so only a single ignorance is established. Although the link of grasping encompasses every affliction, craving's moistening predominates, and so it is spoken of as craving's own intensification.

諸緣起支皆依自地？有所發行依他無明，如下無明發上地行。不爾，初伏下地染者，所起上定，應非行支，彼地無明猶未起故。從上、下地生下、上者，彼緣何受而起愛支？彼愛亦緣當生地受，若現、若種，於理無違。此十二支，十因、二果，定不同世。因中前七與愛、取、有，或異或同。若二、三、七各定同世。如是十二，一重因果，足顯輪轉，及離斷、常，施設兩重，實為無用，或應過此，便致無窮。

Do all the links of dependent origination each belong to their own stage exclusively? There are cases where volitional formations initiated depend on ignorance of another stage — as when ignorance of a lower stage initiates volitional formations of an upper stage. Were this not so, one who has only just suppressed the lower stage's defilement, and who thereby brings forth an upper-stage concentration, should not have this counted as the link of volitional formations at all, since that upper stage's own ignorance has not yet arisen.

When one born in an upper or lower stage is to be reborn in a lower or upper stage, respectively, with reference to which feeling does the link of craving arise? That craving, too, takes as its object the feeling of the stage one is to be born into, whether manifest or as a seed — this is in no way contrary to reason.

Among these twelve links, the ten causal links and the two resultant links are certainly never of the same lifetime as each other. Among the causal links, the first seven, in relation to craving, grasping, and existence, may be of either a different or the same lifetime — but the second, third, and seventh are each certainly of the same lifetime as these three. Thus, these twelve links, forming a single round of cause and result, already suffice to display the turning of the wheel of rebirth and to steer clear of both the extremes of annihilation and permanence — positing two full rounds would truly serve no purpose, and going further still would lead to an infinite regress.

此十二支義門別者：九實、三假。已潤六支，合為有故；即識等五，三相位別，名生等故。五是一事，謂無明、識、觸、受、愛五；餘非一事。三唯是染，煩惱性故；七唯不染，異熟果性故；七分位中容起染故，假說通二，餘通二種。無明、愛、取說名獨相，不與餘支相交雜故；餘是雜相。六唯非色，謂無明、識、觸、受、愛、取；餘通二種。皆是有漏，唯有為攝。無漏無為，非有支故。

As for the further distinctions among these twelve links: nine are genuinely real, and three are merely provisional — the already-moistened six links, combined, are called "existence," while consciousness and the other four, distinguished by their three positional characteristics, are called birth and the rest. Five are single matters — ignorance, consciousness, contact, feeling, and craving; the rest are not single matters. Three are exclusively defiled, being of the nature of affliction; seven are exclusively undefiled, being of the nature of a ripened result, though, since defilement can arise within their seven positional stages, they are provisionally said to extend to both; the rest extend to both kinds as well.

Ignorance, craving, and grasping are called "exclusive characteristics," since they are not intermixed with the other links; the rest are "mixed characteristics." Six are exclusively non-physical — ignorance, consciousness, contact, feeling, craving, and grasping; the rest extend to both physical and non-physical. All are contaminated, and are included exclusively among the conditioned — the uncontaminated and the unconditioned are not links of existence at all.

「無明」、「愛」、「取」，唯通不善、有覆無記；「行」唯善、惡；「有」通善、惡、無覆無記；餘七唯是無覆無記。七分位中，亦起善、染。雖皆通三界，而有分、有全。上地行支，能伏下地，即麁、苦等六種行相。有求上生，而起彼故。一切皆唯非學、無學聖者所起有漏善業，明為緣故，違有支故，非有支攝。由此應知聖必不造感後有業，於後苦果不迷求故。雜修靜慮，資下故業，生淨居等，於理無違。

Ignorance, craving, and grasping extend only to the unwholesome and to the obscured-neutral; volitional formations are exclusively wholesome or unwholesome; existence extends to the wholesome, the unwholesome, and the unobscured-neutral; the remaining seven are exclusively unobscured-neutral, though, within their seven positional stages, wholesome and defiled states can also arise. Although all twelve extend to the three realms, some are only partially present and some fully so. The link of volitional formations at an upper stage is capable of suppressing a lower stage, through the six modes of activity such as coarseness and suffering, since there are those who seek rebirth in an upper realm and, accordingly, give rise to this.

All twelve links are exclusively brought forth by contaminated wholesome karma — never karma initiated by a noble one, whether trainee or beyond training, since such karma has clarity as its condition, contrary to the link of existence, and so is not included within it. From this it should be understood that a noble one certainly never creates karma that brings about a subsequent existence, since such a being is never deluded into seeking a future painful result. Cultivating meditative absorption in mixed fashion, so as to support and complete a prior act of karma, and thereby being reborn in the Pure Abodes and the like, is in no way contrary to reason.

Which Path Eliminates Each Link, and the Twelve Mapped onto Suffering 釋十二支「迷諦所斷」· 受、諦相攝

Chinese Source

有義：無明唯見所斷，要迷諦理能發行故，聖必不造後有業故；愛、取二支唯修所斷，貪求當有而潤生故，九種命終心俱生愛俱故；餘九皆通見、修所斷。有義：一切皆通二斷。論說：預流果已斷一切一分有支，無全斷者故。若無明支唯見所斷，寧說預流無全斷者？若愛、取支唯修所斷，寧說彼已斷一切支一分？又說全界一切煩惱，皆能結生，往惡趣行，唯分別起煩惱能發，不言潤生唯修所斷，諸感後有行，皆見所斷發。

Ru-Yi Translation

One view holds: ignorance is eliminated only on the path of seeing, since it is only through delusion regarding the principle of the truths that it is capable of initiating volitional formations, and a noble one certainly never creates karma for a subsequent existence; the links of craving and grasping are eliminated only on the path of cultivation, since these crave and seek a future existence and moisten rebirth; the remaining nine links extend to both paths.

Another view holds: all twelve links extend to both. The treatise states: a stream-enterer has already eliminated a portion of every link of existence, with none entirely eliminated. If ignorance were eliminated only on the path of seeing, how could it be said that a stream-enterer has none entirely eliminated? If craving and grasping were eliminated only on the path of cultivation, how could it be said that this person has already eliminated a portion of every link? It is also stated that every affliction throughout an entire realm is capable of binding one to rebirth, while volitional formations leading to an unfortunate destiny are initiated only by discriminated affliction — it is not said that moistening rebirth is accomplished only by what is eliminated on the path of cultivation, nor that every volitional formation bringing about a subsequent existence is initiated only by what is eliminated on the path of seeing.

由此故知無明、愛、取三支，亦通見、修所斷。然無明支正發行者，唯見所斷，助者不定；愛、取二支正潤生者，唯修所斷，助者不定。又染污法自性應斷，對治起時彼永斷故。一切有漏不染污法，非性應斷，不違道故。然有二義說之為斷：一、離縛故，謂斷緣彼、雜彼煩惱；二、不生故，謂斷彼依令永不起。依離縛斷，說有漏善、無覆無記，唯修所斷；依不生斷說諸惡趣、無想定等，唯見所斷。說十二支通二斷者，於前諸斷，如應當知。

From this, it is known that ignorance, craving, and grasping likewise extend to both paths. Yet, with respect to ignorance, whatever genuinely initiates volitional formations is eliminated only on the path of seeing, while whatever merely assists is not fixed either way; with respect to craving and grasping, whatever genuinely moistens rebirth is eliminated only on the path of cultivation, while whatever merely assists is not fixed either way.

Furthermore, a defiled dharma, by its own nature, must be eliminated, since, once its counteragent arises, it is permanently eliminated; a contaminated, undefiled dharma is not, by its own nature, something to be eliminated, since it does not conflict with the path. Yet there are two senses in which something is spoken of as "eliminated": elimination through freedom from bondage, meaning the elimination of the affliction that takes it as an object; and elimination through non-arising, meaning the elimination of its basis, causing it to permanently cease arising. With reference to freedom from bondage, contaminated wholesome states and the unobscured-neutral are eliminated only on the path of cultivation; with reference to non-arising, an unfortunate destiny, the concentration of no-conception, and the like are eliminated only on the path of seeing.

十樂、捨俱，受不與受共相應故；老死位中，多分無樂及容捨故。十一苦俱，非受俱故。十一少分壞苦所攝，老死位中，多無樂受，依樂立壞，故不說之。十二少分苦苦所攝，一切支中，有苦受故；十二全分，行苦所攝，諸有漏法皆行苦故，依捨受說十一少分，除老死支，如壞苦說。實義如是，諸聖教中，隨彼相增，所說不定。皆苦諦攝，取蘊性故。五亦集諦攝，業煩惱性故。

Ten links occur together with pleasure and equanimity, since, in the stage of old-age-and-death, there is for the most part no pleasant feeling, though equanimity can still be present. Eleven links occur together with suffering. Eleven links, in part, are included within the suffering of change, since old-age-and-death for the most part lacks pleasant feeling, and the suffering of change is established with reference to pleasure. All twelve links, in part, are included within the suffering of suffering, since painful feeling is present within every link; and all twelve, in full, are included within the suffering of formations, since every contaminated dharma is subject to it. This is the genuine meaning; within the various sacred teachings, what is said varies according to whichever characteristic is emphasized in each case.

All twelve links are included within the truth of suffering, being of the nature of the aggregates of grasping. Five are also included within the truth of origination, being of the nature of karma and affliction.

諸支相望，增上定有，餘之三緣，有無不定。契經依定，唯說有一。愛望於取，有望於生，有因緣義。若說識支是業種者，行望於識，亦作因緣。餘支相望，無因緣義。而《集論》說，無明望行，有因緣者，依無明時，業習氣說，無明俱故，假說無明，實是行種。《瑜伽論》說，諸支相望無因緣者，依現愛、取，唯業有說。無明望行，愛望於取，生望老死有餘二緣。有望於生，受望於愛，無等無間，有所緣緣。餘支相望，二俱非有。此中且依隣近、順次、不相雜亂，實緣起說。異此相望，為緣不定。諸聰慧者，如理應思。

Considered in mutual relation, the twelve links certainly possess the dominant condition; the remaining three conditions may or may not be present between them. Craving in relation to grasping, and existence in relation to birth, involve a causal condition; if the link of consciousness is a karmic seed, volitional formations, in relation to consciousness, likewise serve as a causal condition. Between the remaining links, there is no such relation — though the Abhidharmasamuccaya's statement that ignorance, in relation to volitional formations, does involve a causal condition is spoken with reference to the karmic habituation present at the time of ignorance, "ignorance" here being a provisional way of speaking for what is genuinely the seed of volitional formations.

Ignorance in relation to volitional formations, craving in relation to grasping, and birth in relation to old-age-and-death also possess the remaining two conditions; existence in relation to birth, and feeling in relation to craving, possess the condition of the objective support but not the condition of immediately preceding sameness. This discussion is offered only with reference to adjacent, sequential links, in accordance with the genuine account of dependent origination — considered in other ways, the conditions involved are not fixed. Every discerning reader should reflect on this according to reason.

惑、業、苦三攝十二者：無明、愛、取，是惑所攝；行、有一分，是業所攝；七、有一分，是苦所攝。有處說業全攝有者，應知彼依「業有」說故。有處說識業所攝者，彼說業種為識支故。惑、業所招獨名苦者，唯苦諦攝，為生厭故。由惑、業、苦即十二支故，此能令生死相續。

As for how affliction, karma, and suffering encompass these twelve links: ignorance, craving, and grasping are included within affliction; volitional formations, and a portion of existence, are included within karma; the seven remaining links, and the rest of existence, are included within suffering. What is brought about by affliction and karma is exclusively called "suffering" because it is included exclusively within the truth of suffering, in order to induce aversion toward it. Since affliction, karma, and suffering are precisely these twelve links, it is these that are capable of making birth-and-death continue without end.

Reading note: The fourfold grouping — drawing-forth, drawn-forth, generating, generated — turns the traditional twelve links from a flat list into a genuine causal mechanism spanning two full lifetimes: ignorance and volitional formations in a past life draw forth the seeds that will become a new consciousness, name-and-form, the six sense-fields, contact, and feeling; craving, grasping, and existence in the present life water those seeds until they generate the future birth and old-age-and-death. The water imagery for craving is worth sitting with, since it explains why this tradition treats craving, not ignorance, as the more urgent target for a practitioner already on the path — ignorance plants the seed once, far in the past, but craving is the daily watering that a person can, in principle, simply stop doing today.

The debate over which links are eliminated on the path of seeing versus the path of cultivation is really a debate about the architecture of practice itself: is delusion about the truths (an intellectual, all-at-once event) doing the work, or is habitual craving and grasping (worn in slowly, released slowly) doing the work? The treatise's own resolution — most links have both a "genuinely initiating" component tied to insight and an "assisting" component that lingers regardless — mirrors an experience many practitioners report directly: a single flash of clear seeing can permanently end certain kinds of delusion, while old habitual cravings keep reasserting themselves long after the intellectual conviction has changed, requiring sustained cultivation rather than a single insight to fully release.

Two Kinds of Birth-and-Death 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 二種生死

The treatise closes this topic by distinguishing the ordinary cycle of birth-and-death from a second, far subtler kind — one available only to advanced practitioners who have already broken free of the first, and who take on a further, chosen form of continued existence in order to complete the path.

Fragmentary and Transformative Birth-and-Death 釋「分段」、「變易」二種生死

Chinese Source

復次，生死相續，由內因緣，不待外緣，故唯有識。因謂有漏、無漏二業正感生死，故說為因；緣謂煩惱、所知二障助感生死，故說為緣。所以者何？生死有二：一、分段生死，謂諸有漏善、不善業，由煩惱障緣助勢力，所感三界麁異熟果，身命短長，隨因緣力，有定齊限，故名分段。二、不思議變易生死，謂諸無漏有分別業，由所知障緣助勢力，所感殊勝細異熟果。由悲願力，改轉身命，無定齊限，故名變易；無漏定願正所資、感，妙用難測，名不思議。或名「意成身」，隨意願成故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, birth-and-death's continuity is brought about by inner causes and conditions, not dependent upon anything external — hence there is only consciousness. "Cause" refers to the two kinds of karma, contaminated and uncontaminated, which genuinely bring about birth-and-death; "condition" refers to the two obstructions, afflictive and cognitive, which assist in bringing it about.

Birth-and-death is of two kinds. First, fragmentary birth-and-death: the coarse ripened result within the three realms, brought about by contaminated wholesome and unwholesome karma through the assisting power of the afflictive obstruction — the body and life-span, long or short, having a fixed limit according to the power of cause and condition; hence it is called "fragmentary."

Second, inconceivable transformative birth-and-death: the superior, subtle ripened result brought about by uncontaminated karma still involving discrimination, through the assisting power of the cognitive obstruction. Through the power of compassion and vow, one's body and life-span are altered and transformed, without any fixed limit; hence it is called "transformative." Since uncontaminated concentration and vow genuinely support and bring this about, and its subtle function is difficult to fathom, it is called "inconceivable." It is also called "the mind-made body," since it is accomplished according to one's own intention and vow.

如契經說：如取為緣，有漏業因，續後有者，而生三有。如是無明習地為緣，無漏業因，有阿羅漢、獨覺、已得自在菩薩，生三種意成身。亦名變化身，無漏定力，轉令異本，如變化故。如有論說：聲聞無學永盡後有，云何能證無上菩提？依變化身，證無上覺，非業報身，故不違理。若所知障助無漏業，能感生死，二乘定性，應不永入無餘涅槃。如諸異生拘煩惱故。

As the sūtras state: just as grasping serves as the condition, with contaminated karma as cause, for continuing into a subsequent existence — so too, with the stage of ignorance's habituation as condition, and uncontaminated karma as cause, arhats, solitary buddhas, and bodhisattvas who have attained mastery give rise to three kinds of mind-made body. This is also called the "transformation body," since the power of uncontaminated concentration transforms it, causing it to differ from its original form.

As a certain treatise states: how could a śrāvaka beyond training, who has permanently exhausted any subsequent existence, still be capable of realizing unsurpassed bodhi? It is by relying on the transformation body, not the karma-retribution body, that unsurpassed awakening is realized — hence this is not contrary to reason. Were the cognitive obstruction, assisting uncontaminated karma, capable of bringing about birth-and-death, then those of the two vehicles with fixed spiritual lineage should never permanently enter remainderless nirvāṇa at all, just as ordinary beings, bound by affliction, cannot.

如何道諦實能感苦？誰言實感？不爾，如何？無漏定、願，資有漏業，令所得果，相續長時，展轉增勝，假說名感。如是感時，由所知障為緣助力，非獨能感。然所知障，不障解脫，無能發業潤生用故。何用資感生死苦為？自證菩提，利樂他故。謂不定性獨覺、聲聞，及得自在大願菩薩，已永斷伏煩惱障故，無容復受當分段身。恐廢長時修菩薩行，遂以無漏勝定願力，如延壽法資現身因，令彼長時與果不絕，數數如是定願資助，乃至證得無上菩提。

How, then, could the truth of the path genuinely bring about suffering? Who has said that it genuinely brings it about? Uncontaminated concentration and vow support contaminated karma, causing the result obtained to continue for a long duration, growing progressively more pronounced — it is this that is provisionally called "bringing about." When this occurs, it is through the cognitive obstruction serving as an assisting condition, not something brought about by uncontaminated karma alone; yet the cognitive obstruction does not obstruct liberation, since it has no capacity to initiate karma or moisten rebirth.

What purpose is served by supporting and bringing about the suffering of birth-and-death this way? It is so that one may personally realize bodhi and bring benefit and happiness to others. Solitary buddhas and śrāvakas of indeterminate spiritual lineage, and bodhisattvas of great vow who have attained mastery, having already eliminated or suppressed the afflictive obstruction, are no longer capable of receiving a future fragmentary body. Fearing that this would abandon the long-term cultivation of bodhisattva practice, they instead rely on the power of uncontaminated supreme concentration and vow, like a life-extending method, to support the cause of their present body, causing its result to continue unbroken over a long duration — through such repeated support, again and again, until unsurpassed bodhi is finally realized.

彼復何須所知障助？既未圓證無相大悲，不執菩提、有情實有，無由發起猛利悲願；又所知障，障大菩提，為永斷除，留身久住；又所知障為有漏依，此障若無，彼定非有，故於身住，有大助力。若所留身，有漏定願所資助者，分段身攝，二乘、異生所知境故。無漏定願所資助者，變易身攝，非彼境故。由此應知，變易生死，性是有漏，異熟果攝，於無漏業，是增上果。有聖教中說為無漏，出三界者，隨助因說。

Why, then, would such a being still need the cognitive obstruction's assistance? Since they have not yet perfectly realized signless great compassion, without grasping at bodhi and sentient beings as genuinely real, there would be no basis for arousing fierce, compassionate vow at all. Moreover, since the cognitive obstruction obstructs great bodhi, in order to permanently eliminate it, one retains the body and abides for a long duration; and since the cognitive obstruction serves as the basis for contaminated states, were it absent, those states would be absent too — hence it provides substantial assistance toward the body's continued abiding.

If the body one retains is supported by contaminated concentration and vow, it is included within the fragmentary body; if supported by uncontaminated concentration and vow, it is included within the transformative body. From this, it should be understood that transformative birth-and-death, in its own nature, is contaminated and included within the ripened result — with respect to uncontaminated karma, it is the dominant result. Where the sacred teachings speak of it as uncontaminated and as transcending the three realms, this is spoken with reference to its assisting cause.

頌中所言諸業習氣，即前所說二業種子；二取習氣即前所說二障種子，俱執著故。「俱」等餘文，義如前釋。變易生死，雖無分段前後異熟別盡別生，而數資助，前後改轉，亦有前盡餘復生義。雖亦由現生死相續，而種定有，頌偏說之。或為顯示真異熟，因果皆不離本識，故不說現。現異熟因，不即與果。轉識間斷，非異熟故。前、中、後際，生死輪迴，不待外緣，既由內識，淨法相續，應知亦然。謂無始來，依附本識，有、無漏種，由轉識等，數數熏發，漸漸增勝，乃至究竟，得成佛時，轉捨本來雜染識種，轉得始起清淨種識，任持一切功德種子。由本願力，盡未來際，起諸妙用，相續無窮。由此應知，唯有內識。

"The habituation of karma," as spoken of in the verse, refers to the seeds of the two kinds of karma already discussed; "the habituation of the two graspings" refers to the seeds of the two obstructions, since both involve attachment.

Although transformative birth-and-death lacks fragmentary birth-and-death's distinct, sequential exhaustion and generation of separate ripened results, it too, through repeated support and progressive transformation, involves a sense in which the former is exhausted and a further one is generated. Although this, too, occurs through the continuity of manifest birth-and-death, the seed is certainly always present, and it is this that the verse speaks of preferentially.

In the beginning, middle, and final periods alike, the wheel of birth-and-death turns without depending on any external condition — and, since this is entirely through inner consciousness, the continuity of pure dharmas, too, should be understood the same way. Since beginningless time, contaminated and uncontaminated seeds have depended upon the root consciousness, and, through the transforming consciousnesses' repeated perfuming, they gradually grow more pronounced, until, at last, upon attaining buddhahood, one permanently relinquishes the originally defiled consciousness-seeds and instead attains the newly-arisen pure seed-consciousness, sustaining every seed of merit and virtue. Through the power of one's original vow, one gives rise to manifold sublime functions throughout all future time, continuing without end. From this, it should be understood that there is only inner consciousness.

Reading note: The distinction between fragmentary and transformative birth-and-death answers a question the tradition has to face honestly: what keeps a bodhisattva, or an arhat who chooses to remain active in the world, embodied and functioning, once the ordinary machinery of karma and affliction that drives everyone else's rebirth has already been dismantled? The treatise's answer is that the cognitive obstruction — subtler and less obviously harmful than the afflictive obstruction, and compatible with genuine liberation from suffering — still has one remaining, almost paradoxically useful function: it can be deliberately left in place a while longer, like a support structure, to sustain a chosen body through vow and concentration rather than through compulsive craving, precisely so that the deeper work of uprooting it can continue.

The fascicle's closing lines are worth reading as a genuine turning point: everything explained about birth-and-death's self-perpetuating machinery — karma, the two graspings, the twelve links — has a mirror image in how purification unfolds, seed by seed, within the very same root consciousness, until, at buddhahood, the defiled seeds are entirely replaced by pure ones. The path to awakening, on this account, is not an escape from the causal system just described, but a redirection of it — the same consciousness that once perpetuated suffering without end is precisely what, transformed, sustains awakened activity without end as well.

The Three Natures: What Is Universally Discriminated 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 釋三自性不成難

The fascicle's second half turns to genuinely new ground: the doctrine of the Three Natures, one of this treatise's foundational teachings. A thirteenth root verse, quoted here in full, introduces all three at once — the imagined, the dependent, and the perfectly accomplished — before the commentary works through each in turn, starting with what it means to "universally discriminate."

The Verse: All Three Natures at Once 頌釋三性總說

Chinese Source

若唯有識，何故世尊處處經中說有三性？應知三性亦不離識。所以者何？頌曰：由彼彼遍計，遍計種種物，此遍計所執，自性無所有。依他起自性，分別緣所生。圓成實於彼，常遠離前性。故此與依他，非異非不異，如無常等性，非不見此彼。

Ru-Yi Translation

If there is only consciousness, why does the World-Honored One, throughout the sūtras, speak of the three natures existing? It should be understood that the three natures, too, are never apart from consciousness. Why is this so? The verse states: "Through this and that universal discrimination, one universally discriminates all manner of things; this discriminated, attached-to nature has no essence of its own whatsoever. The dependent nature is that which discrimination, arising from conditions, produces. The perfectly accomplished nature is what, within that dependent nature, is forever free of the former, discriminated-and-attached-to nature. Hence this and the dependent nature are neither different nor not different — like impermanence and similar characteristics, nor is it that seeing one precludes seeing the other."

The Imagined Nature 釋「遍計所執性」

Chinese Source

論曰：周遍計度，故名遍計；品類眾多，說為「彼彼」。謂能遍計虛妄分別。即由彼彼虛妄分別，遍計種種所遍計物：謂所妄執蘊、處、界等，若法、若我自性差別。此所妄執自性差別，總名遍計所執自性。如是自性，都無所有，理、教推徵，不可得故。或初句顯能遍計識，第二句示所遍計境，後半方申遍計所執若我、若法自性非有，已廣顯彼不可得故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The treatise states: pervasively discriminating and measuring is why it is called "universal discrimination"; its kinds being manifold, it is spoken of as "this and that." This refers to the false discrimination capable of universally discriminating. It is precisely through this and that false discrimination that one universally discriminates all manner of universally-discriminated things — namely, the falsely grasped-at aggregates, sense-fields, elements, and the rest, whether their distinct nature as dharmas or as a self. This falsely grasped-at distinct nature is, in general, called the discriminated, attached-to nature. Such a nature has no existence whatsoever, since, whether sought through reason or through scriptural teaching, it can nowhere be found.

Alternatively: the first line shows the consciousness that universally discriminates; the second line indicates the object that is universally discriminated; the latter half then explains that the discriminated, attached-to self or dharma-nature does not exist, having already extensively shown that it cannot be found.

初能遍計，自性云何？有義：八識及諸心所，有漏攝者，皆能遍計，虛妄分別為自性故；皆似所取、能取現故；說阿賴耶以遍計所執自性，妄執種為所緣故。有義：第六、第七心品執我、法者，是能遍計。唯說意識能遍計故，意及意識名意識故；計度分別能遍計故；執我、法者必是慧故；二執必與無明俱故；不說無明有善性故；癡、無癡等不相應故；不見有執導空智故；執有、達無不俱起故；曾無有執非能熏故。

What, first, is the nature of that which universally discriminates? One view holds: the eight consciousnesses and their mental factors, insofar as they are contaminated, are all capable of universal discrimination, since false discrimination is their very nature, since all of them appear resembling a grasped object and a grasping subject, and since the treatise states that the ālayavijñāna takes, as its object, the seed of the false attachment to the discriminated, attached-to nature.

Another view holds: only the sixth and seventh consciousnesses, insofar as they grasp at self and dharmas, are what universally discriminate. This is because it is said that only the mental consciousness is capable of universal discrimination, since both manas and the mental consciousness are called by the name "mental consciousness"; because calculating discrimination alone is capable of universal discrimination; because whatever grasps at self and dharmas must be a form of discernment; because these two forms of attachment must occur together with ignorance, which is never said to have a wholesome nature; because delusion and non-delusion are never mutually associated; because attachment is never seen to lead into the wisdom of emptiness; because grasping at existence and realizing non-existence never arise together; and because whatever has never involved attachment has no capacity to perfume.

有漏心等不證實故，一切皆名虛妄分別。雖似所取、能取相現，而非一切能遍計攝，勿無漏心亦有執故，如來後得應有執故。經說佛智現身、土等種種影像，如鏡等故，若無緣用應非智等。雖說藏識緣遍計種，而不說唯，故非誠證。由斯理趣，唯於第六、第七心品，有能遍計。識品雖二，而有二、三、四、五、六、七、八、九、十等遍計不同，故言「彼彼」。

Since contaminated mind and the rest never realize reality directly, all such states are called false discrimination. Although they appear resembling a grasped object and a grasping subject, this does not mean all of them are included within what universally discriminates — otherwise, even uncontaminated mind would have to involve attachment, and a tathāgata's subsequently-attained wisdom would have to involve attachment as well. The sūtras state that a buddha's wisdom manifests the body, the land, and various other reflected images, just like a mirror — were it to lack the function of taking an object, it could not be called wisdom at all. Although it is stated that the storehouse consciousness takes the seeds of universal discrimination as its object, it is not stated that this is exclusively so — hence this is not genuine proof to the contrary.

By this line of reasoning, it is only within the sixth and seventh consciousnesses that what universally discriminates is found. Although these are two consciousnesses, the ways in which universal discrimination occurs differ — two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, and so on — hence the verse speaks of "this and that."

Reading note: The Three Natures — the imagined (遍計所執, parikalpita), the dependent (依他起, paratantra), and the perfectly accomplished (圓成實, pariniṣpanna) — are one of the treatise's most important and lasting contributions, and this section starts with the trickiest of the three to pin down: not the falsely grasped-at object itself, but the very act of universal discrimination that does the grasping. The debate over whether all eight consciousnesses discriminate this way, or only the sixth and seventh, has real stakes: if even a buddha's wisdom counted as "universal discrimination," awakening would be incoherent, since the whole point of that wisdom is to see through exactly this kind of false grasping. Locating the imagined nature's source specifically in the sixth and seventh consciousnesses — ordinary conceptual thought and manas's innate self-grasping — while leaving the five sense-consciousnesses and the eighth consciousness free of it, sets up a precise diagnosis of where delusion actually originates, rather than treating "consciousness" as uniformly deluded from top to bottom.

The Dependent Nature 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 釋「依他起自性」

Having explained what falsely grasps, the treatise turns to what is actually there to be grasped at in the first place — the genuinely existent, conditioned flow of mind and mental factors that the imagined nature gets superimposed onto.

What Is Universally Discriminated 釋「所遍計」· 依他起與遍計所執之別

Chinese Source

次所遍計自性云何？《攝大乘》說是依他起，遍計心等所緣緣故。圓成實性寧非彼境？真非妄執所緣境故，依展轉說亦所遍計。遍計所執雖是彼境，而非所緣緣，故非所遍計。遍計所執，其相云何？與依他起，復有何別？有義：三界心及心所，由無始來虛妄熏習，雖各體一，而似二生：謂見、相分，即能、所取。如是二分，情有理無，此相說為遍計所執。二所依體，實託緣生，此性非無，名依他起，虛妄分別緣所生故。云何知然？諸聖教說：虛妄分別是依他起，二取名為遍計所執。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, what is the nature of that which is universally discriminated? The Mahāyānasaṃgraha states that this is the dependent nature, since it is the condition of the objective support for the mind and the rest that universally discriminate. Should not the perfectly accomplished nature, too, be its object? Since genuine reality is never the object taken by false attachment, it is only spoken of, in a further, derivative sense, as also being universally discriminated. The discriminated, attached-to nature, though it too is an object of this discrimination, is not itself a condition of objective support, and hence is not what is universally discriminated.

What is the character of the discriminated, attached-to nature, and how does it differ from the dependent nature? One view holds: the minds and mental factors of the three realms, through beginningless false habituation, though each possesses a single essence, appear to arise as two — namely, the perceiving and perceived aspects, the grasping subject and grasped object. These two aspects exist as far as ordinary feeling is concerned but not in genuine reality, and it is this appearance that is called the discriminated, attached-to nature. The essence on which these two aspects depend genuinely arises through conditions, and this nature is not nonexistent — it is called the dependent nature, since it is what false discrimination, arising from conditions, produces. How is this known? The sacred teachings state: false discrimination is the dependent nature; the two graspings are called the discriminated, attached-to nature.

有義：一切心及心所，由熏習力所變二分，從緣生故，亦依他起。遍計依斯妄執定實有無、一異、俱不俱等，此二方名遍計所執。諸聖教說：唯量、唯二、唯種種，皆名依他起故。又相等四法、十一識等，論皆說為依他起攝故。不爾，無漏後得智品二分，應名遍計所執。許應聖智不緣彼生，緣彼智品應非道諦；不許應知有漏亦爾。又若二分是遍計所執，應如兔角等非所緣緣，遍計所執體非有故。又應二分不熏成種，後識等生，應無二分。又諸習氣是相分攝，豈非有法能作因緣？若緣所生內相、見分，非依他起，二所依體，例亦應然，無異因故。

Another view holds: every mind and mental factor's two transformed aspects, brought about through the power of habituation, likewise arise from conditions, and so are also the dependent nature. It is only when universal discrimination, relying on these, falsely grasps at their being fixedly existent or nonexistent, one or different, co-occurring or not co-occurring, and so forth, that these two aspects come to be called the discriminated, attached-to nature. The sacred teachings state that mere cognition, mere duality, and mere manifoldness are all called the dependent nature; and the treatise, moreover, states that the four dharmas beginning with characteristic, and the eleven kinds of consciousness, are all included within it.

Were this not so, the two aspects belonging to uncontaminated, subsequently-attained wisdom would have to be called the discriminated, attached-to nature — meaning either that a noble one's wisdom would have to arise without taking any object at all, or that the wisdom-state taking such an object would then not be the truth of the path. Furthermore, were these two aspects the discriminated, attached-to nature, they should, like a rabbit's horn, not be a condition of objective support at all. Furthermore, they should then not be perfumed into forming seeds, and subsequent consciousness and the rest should then arise without them entirely. Furthermore, the various habituations are themselves included within the perceived aspect — how could this not itself be a real dharma capable of serving as a causal condition? If the inner perceived and perceiving aspects, arising from conditions, were not the dependent nature, then the very essence these two depend upon should, by the same reasoning, likewise not be so, there being no relevant difference in cause between them.

由斯理趣，眾緣所生心、心所體及相、見分，有漏、無漏皆依他起。依他眾緣而得起故。頌言：「分別緣所生」者，應知且說染分依他，淨分依他亦圓成故。或諸染淨心、心所法，皆名分別，能緣慮故。是則一切染、淨依他，皆是此中依他起攝。

By this line of reasoning, the essence of mind and mental factors, together with their perceived and perceiving aspects, arising from manifold conditions, whether contaminated or uncontaminated, are all the dependent nature, since they arise in dependence upon manifold conditions. The verse's phrase "discrimination, arising from conditions, produces" should be understood as speaking, for now, only of the defiled portion of the dependent nature — the pure portion of the dependent nature is also the perfectly accomplished nature. Or else, every defiled and pure mind and mental factor is called "discrimination," since all are capable of taking an object and reflecting upon it — in which case, every defiled and pure instance of the dependent nature is included within the dependent nature spoken of here.

Reading note: The two views on offer here mark out genuinely different scope for "the dependent nature," and the difference matters for how sweeping the doctrine of emptiness becomes: the narrower view treats only the raw appearing of a perceiving and perceived aspect as dependent and real, with the false attachment layered separately on top; the broader view treats the entire apparatus of consciousness — its own two transformed aspects included — as dependent, with the imagined nature consisting only in the further, superimposed conviction that these aspects are fixed, singular, independently real things. The rabbit's-horn argument is a useful diagnostic worth remembering elsewhere in the text: whatever is purely imaginary, like a rabbit's horn, can never actually be perceived, perfumed into a seed, or serve as a genuine cause of anything — so anything that demonstrably does function this way cannot be purely imaginary, whatever else may be true of it.

The Perfectly Accomplished Nature, and How the Three Relate 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 釋「圓成實性」

The third nature — suchness itself, revealed once the imagined nature is seen through — closes the doctrine with a careful statement of exactly how it relates to the dependent nature it is found within: neither identical with it, nor separate from it.

Suchness, Neither the Same Nor Different 釋「圓成實」與依他起「非異非不異」

Chinese Source

二空所顯圓滿成就，諸法實性，名圓成實。顯此遍、常，體非虛謬，簡自、共相、虛空、我等。無漏有為，離倒、究竟、勝用周遍，亦得此名。然今頌中，說初非後。此即於彼依他起上，常遠離前遍計所執，二空所顯真如為性。說「於彼」言：顯圓成實與依他起，不即不離；「常遠離」言：顯妄所執能、所取性，理恒非有。「前」言義顯不空依他；「性」顯二空非圓成實，真如離有離無性故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The genuine nature of all dharmas, perfectly accomplished through the two emptinesses, is called the perfectly accomplished nature. This shows it to be pervasive and constant, its essence never false or erroneous, distinguishing it from a thing's own particular characteristic, its shared characteristic, empty space, a self, and the like. Uncontaminated conditioned dharmas, too, being free of distortion, ultimate, and of superior, pervasive function, may also receive this name — yet the present verse speaks only of the former, unconditioned sense.

This is precisely suchness, revealed by the two emptinesses, taking for its nature the permanent freedom, upon that same dependent nature, from the former, discriminated, attached-to nature. The phrase "within that" shows that the perfectly accomplished nature and the dependent nature are neither identical nor separate. The phrase "forever free of" shows that the falsely grasped-at nature of a grasping subject and grasped object, in principle, is eternally nonexistent. The word "former" conveys that the dependent nature is not itself empty; the word "nature" shows that the two emptinesses themselves are not the perfectly accomplished nature, since suchness has a nature free of both existence and nonexistence.

由前理故，此圓成實與彼依他起，非異非不異。異應真如非彼實性；不異此性應是無常。彼此俱應淨、非淨境，則本後智，用應無別。云何二性，非異非一？如彼無常、無我等性。無常等性與行等法異，應彼法非無常等；不異此應非彼共相。由斯喻顯，此圓成實與彼依他，非一非異，法與法性，理必應然，勝義、世俗，相待有故。

For this same reason, this perfectly accomplished nature and that dependent nature are neither different nor not different. Were they different, suchness would not be the dependent nature's own genuine nature; were they not different, this nature would then have to be impermanent. Were they both, the pure and impure would become each other's object, and the fundamental and subsequently-attained wisdoms would have no distinct function.

How, then, can these two natures be neither different nor one? Just as with impermanence, non-self, and similar characteristics: were impermanence and the like different from conditioned dharmas and the rest, those dharmas would then not be impermanent; were they not different, impermanence should then not be their shared characteristic either. By this analogy, it is shown that this perfectly accomplished nature and that dependent nature are neither one nor different — a dharma and its nature must, by principle, necessarily be related this way, since the ultimate and the conventional exist only in mutual dependence upon each other.

Seeing Through Illusion to Suchness 釋「如幻事等」· 結歸三性不離心

Chinese Source

非不證見此圓成實，而能見彼依他起性。未達遍計所執性空，不如實知依他有故。無分別智證真如已，後得智中，方能了達依他起性如幻事等。雖無始來心、心所法，已能緣自相、見分等，而我、法執恒俱行故，不如實知。眾緣所引自心、心所，虛妄變現，猶如幻事、陽焰、夢境、鏡像、光影、谷響、水月、變化所成，非有似有。依如是義，故有頌言：非不見真如，而能了諸行，皆如幻事等，雖有而非真。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is not that one could see the dependent nature without ever realizing this perfectly accomplished nature — without having penetrated the emptiness of the discriminated, attached-to nature, one cannot genuinely know the dependent nature's existence either. Only once non-discriminating wisdom has realized suchness can one, within subsequently-attained wisdom, come to understand that the dependent nature is like a magical illusion and the like. Although, since beginningless time, mind and mental factors have already been capable of taking their own perceived and perceiving aspects as their object, since the attachment to self and dharmas has always accompanied this activity, one does not genuinely know it as such.

One's own mind and mental factors, drawn forth by manifold conditions, falsely transform and manifest, just like a magical illusion, a mirage, a dream, a reflection in a mirror, a shadow, an echo in a valley, the moon reflected in water, or a conjured apparition — not truly existing, yet appearing to exist. With reference to this meaning, a verse states: "It is not that, without seeing suchness, one could still come to understand that all conditioned things are like magical illusions and the like — existing, yet not truly real."

此中意說：三種自性，皆不遠離心、心所法。謂心、心所及所變現，眾緣生故，如幻事等，非有、似有，誑惑愚夫，一切皆名依他起性。愚夫於此橫執我法、有無、一異、俱不俱等，如空花等，性、相都無，一切皆名遍計所執。依他起上，彼所妄執我、法俱空，此空所顯識等真性，名圓成實。是故此三，不離心等。

This verse means to say: the three natures are all never removed from mind and mental factors. Mind, mental factors, and what they transform and manifest, arising from manifold conditions, are like a magical illusion and the like — not truly existing yet appearing to exist, deceiving and confusing the ignorant — and all of this is called the dependent nature. The ignorant, with respect to this, wrongly grasp at self and dharmas, at existence and nonexistence, at oneness and difference, at co-occurrence and non-co-occurrence, and so forth — like flowers in the sky, having no nature or characteristic whatsoever — and all of this is called the discriminated, attached-to nature. Upon this same dependent nature, the falsely grasped-at self and dharmas are both empty, and this emptiness, revealing the genuine nature of consciousness and the rest, is called the perfectly accomplished nature. Hence these three are never apart from mind and the rest.

Reading note: The "neither different nor not different" formula, already used earlier in this fascicle for mind and its mental factors, and for the eight consciousnesses' relationship to one another, here reaches its most important application: a dharma and its own true nature can be neither strictly identical (or suchness would be impermanent, arising and ceasing along with the dependent phenomena it underlies) nor strictly separate (or suchness would not actually be that phenomenon's own nature at all, and awakened wisdom would have nothing left to realize about ordinary experience). The impermanence analogy is worth sitting with: nobody thinks impermanence is a separate thing floating apart from impermanent objects, nor that it simply is any one of them — it is how they are, without being identical to any one of them. Suchness relates to the dependent nature the very same way.

The catalogue of illusion-similes — a magical illusion, a mirage, a dream, a mirror's reflection, an echo, the moon in water — is not decoration; each image isolates a different way something can appear vividly real while having no independent substance behind the appearance, and together they do real philosophical work: they show that "not ultimately real" does not mean "nothing at all." A reflection in water is genuinely there to be seen, genuinely produced by real conditions (water, light, an object), and genuinely absent as an independently existing thing at the same time — which is exactly the treatise's claim about the dependent nature itself. The closing summary is the doctrine's cleanest statement: the dependent nature is what genuinely appears, the imagined nature is what ignorance wrongly takes that appearance to be, and the perfectly accomplished nature is simply the appearance's own emptiness of that wrongly-imagined status — three names for three different ways of relating to the very same mind and mental factors, not three separate realities.

The Three Natures, Mapped Against Every Other Framework 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 三性與諸門相攝

Fascicle 8 closes with an extended reference section, cross-tabulating the Three Natures against nearly every other classificatory scheme this tradition uses — the seven kinds of suchness, the six categories of dharma, several competing accounts of the five categories, the four kinds of genuine reality, the four noble truths in triplicate, the three gates of liberation, and the two truths — before asking, one final time, which of the three natures are ultimately real.

Suchness, the Six Categories, and the Five Categories 三性攝七真如、六法、五事

Chinese Source

虛空、擇滅、非擇滅等，何性攝耶？三皆容攝。心等變似虛空等相，隨心生故，依他起攝；愚夫於中，妄執實有，此即遍計所執性攝；若於真如，假施設有虛空等義，圓成實攝。有漏心等，定屬依他；無漏心等，容二性攝。眾緣生故，攝屬依他；無顛倒故，圓成實攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Which of the three natures do empty space, analytical cessation, non-analytical cessation, and the like belong to? All three can, in fact, be involved. Mind and the rest transform so as to resemble the characteristics of empty space and the like, arising according to mind — this is included within the dependent nature. The ignorant, with respect to this, falsely grasp at its genuine existence — this is included within the discriminated, attached-to nature. Where empty space and the like are provisionally posited with reference to suchness itself, this is included within the perfectly accomplished nature. Contaminated mind and the rest are fixedly included within the dependent nature; uncontaminated mind and the rest can be included within either of two natures.

如是三性與七真如，云何相攝？七真如者：一、流轉真如，謂有為法，流轉實性；二、實相真如，謂二無我，所顯實性；三、唯識真如，謂染淨法，唯識實性；四、安立真如，謂苦實性；五、邪行真如，謂集實性；六、清淨真如，謂滅實性；七、正行真如，謂道實性。此七實性，圓成實攝，根本、後得二智境故；隨相攝者，流轉、苦、集三，前二性攝，妄執雜染故；餘四皆是圓成實攝。

How, then, do these three natures correspond to the seven kinds of suchness? They are: the suchness of flux, conditioned dharmas' own genuine nature of flux; the suchness of true characteristic, the genuine nature revealed by the two forms of selflessness; the suchness of Consciousness-Only, the genuine nature of defiled and pure dharmas as Consciousness-Only; the suchness of establishment, the genuine nature of suffering; the suchness of wrong practice, the genuine nature of origination; the suchness of purity, the genuine nature of cessation; and the suchness of correct practice, the genuine nature of the path.

These seven genuine natures are included within the perfectly accomplished nature, since they are the object of both fundamental and subsequently-attained wisdom. Classified according to their corresponding characteristic instead: the suchness of flux, and the truths of suffering and origination, are included within the first two natures, since they involve falsely grasped-at defilement; the remaining four are all included within the perfectly accomplished nature.

三性六法，相攝云何？彼六法中，皆具三性。色、受、想、行、識及無為，皆有妄執緣生理故。三性五事，相攝云何？諸聖教說，相攝不定：謂或有處說：依他起攝彼相、名、分別、正智；圓成實性攝彼真如；遍計所執不攝五事。彼說有漏心、心所法，變似所詮，說名為相；似能詮現，施設為名；能變心等，立為分別；無漏心等，離戲論故，但總名正智，不說能、所詮。四從緣生，皆依他攝。

How do the three natures correspond to the six categories of dharma? Within these six categories, all three natures are present — form, feeling, conceptualization, volitional formations, consciousness, and the unconditioned all involve the principle of false grasping and of arising from conditions alike.

How do the three natures correspond to the five categories? The sacred teachings speak of this in more than one way. In some places, it is said: the dependent nature encompasses characteristic, name, discrimination, and correct wisdom; the perfectly accomplished nature encompasses suchness; the discriminated, attached-to nature encompasses none of the five. This account holds that contaminated mind and mental factors, transforming to resemble what is expressed, are called "characteristic"; transforming to resemble what expresses, are called "name"; and the transforming mind itself is called "discrimination"; uncontaminated mind, free of conceptual proliferation, is simply called "correct wisdom." All four, arising from conditions, are included within the dependent nature.

或復有處說：依他起攝相、分別；遍計所執，唯攝彼名；正智、真如，圓成實攝。彼說有漏心及心所相分名相，餘名分別；遍計所執，都無體故，為顯非有，假說為名；二無倒故，圓成實攝。或有處說：依他起性唯攝分別；遍計所執，攝彼相、名；正智、真如，圓成實攝。彼說有漏心及心所相、見分等，總名分別，虛妄分別為自性故，遍計所執，能詮、所詮，隨情立為名、相二事。復有處說：名屬依他起性，義屬遍計所執。彼說有漏心、心所法相、見分等，由名勢力成所遍計，故說為名；遍計所執，隨名橫計，體實非有，假立義名。諸聖教中所說五事，文雖有異，而義無違。然初所說不相雜亂，如《瑜伽論》廣說應知。

In other places, it is said: the dependent nature encompasses characteristic and discrimination; the discriminated, attached-to nature encompasses only name; correct wisdom and suchness are encompassed by the perfectly accomplished nature — this account holds that contaminated mind and mental factors' perceived aspect is called "characteristic," and the rest is called "discrimination," while the discriminated, attached-to nature, having no essence whatsoever, is provisionally called "name" precisely to show its nonexistence.

In yet other places, it is said: the dependent nature encompasses only discrimination; the discriminated, attached-to nature encompasses characteristic and name; correct wisdom and suchness are encompassed by the perfectly accomplished nature — this account holds that contaminated mind and mental factors' perceived and perceiving aspects are, in general, called "discrimination," since false discrimination is their very nature, while the discriminated, attached-to nature's expressing and expressed aspects are established, according to ordinary feeling, as name and characteristic.

In still other places, it is said: name belongs to the dependent nature, and meaning belongs to the discriminated, attached-to nature — this account holds that contaminated mind and mental factors' perceived and perceiving aspects, through the power of name, become what is universally discriminated, and are hence called "name," while the discriminated, attached-to nature, wrongly conceived according to name, having no genuine essence, is provisionally called "meaning."

Although the sacred teachings' statements regarding the five categories differ in wording, their meaning is not in conflict; the first account given, however, keeps them from being intermixed, as is extensively explained in the Yogācārabhūmi.

Genuine Reality, the Four Truths Threefold, and the Two Truths 三性攝五相、四真實、四諦、三解脫門、二諦

Chinese Source

又聖教中說有五相，此與三性相攝云何？所詮、能詮各具三性：謂妄所計，屬初性攝；相、名、分別，隨其所應，所詮、能詮，屬依他起；真如、正智，隨其所應，所詮、能詮，屬圓成實，後得變似能詮相故。二相屬相，唯初性攝，妄執義、名，定相屬故。彼執著相，唯依他起，虛妄分別為自性故。不執著相，唯圓成實，無漏智等為自性故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The sacred teachings also speak of five aspects — how do these correspond to the three natures? Both what is expressed and what expresses each involve all three natures: what is falsely conceived is included within the first nature; characteristic, name, and discrimination, as what is expressed and what expresses, are included within the dependent nature; suchness and correct wisdom, as what is expressed and what expresses, are included within the perfectly accomplished nature.

The aspect of mutual belonging between the two is included exclusively within the first nature, since the false grasping at meaning and name are fixedly interrelated; the aspect of attachment is included exclusively within the dependent nature, since false discrimination is its very nature; the aspect of non-attachment is included exclusively within the perfectly accomplished nature, since uncontaminated wisdom is its very nature.

又聖教中說四真實，與此三性相攝云何？世間、道理所成真實，依他起攝；三事攝故；二障淨智所行真實，圓成實攝；二事攝故。《辯中邊論》說：初真實唯初性攝，共所執故；第二真實通屬三性，理通執無執，雜染、清淨故；後二真實唯屬第三。

The sacred teachings also speak of four kinds of genuine reality — how do these correspond to the three natures? Worldly genuine reality, and genuine reality established through reasoning, are included within the dependent nature; the genuine reality that is the object of wisdom purified of the two obstructions is included within the perfectly accomplished nature. The Madhyāntavibhāga states: the first genuine reality is included exclusively within the first nature, since it is commonly grasped at; the second extends to all three natures, since it extends to both attachment and non-attachment, defilement and purity; the latter two belong exclusively to the third nature.

三性四諦，相攝云何？四中一一皆具三性。且苦諦中無常等四各有三性。無常三者：一、無性無常，性常無故；二、起盡無常，有生滅故；三、垢淨無常，位轉變故。苦有三者：一、所取苦，我、法二執所依取故；二、事相苦，三苦相故；三、和合苦，苦相合故。空有三者：一、無性空，性非有故；二、異性空，與妄所執自性異故；三、自性空，二空所顯為自性故。無我三者：一、無相無我，我相無故；二、異相無我，與妄所執我相異故；三、自相無我，無我所顯為自相故。

How do the three natures correspond to the four noble truths? Each of the four truths, in turn, involves all three natures. Take the truth of suffering, whose four aspects — impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and non-self — each have three forms.

Impermanence: through the absence of any fixed nature; through arising and ceasing; through the alternation of defilement and purity. Suffering: the suffering of what is grasped at, since it is what the two attachments depend upon; the suffering of actual characteristics, the three kinds of suffering; the suffering of combination. Emptiness: through the absence of any nature; through a nature different from the falsely grasped-at one; through its own nature, revealed by the two emptinesses. Non-self: through the absence of any characteristic of self; through a characteristic different from the falsely grasped-at self; through its own characteristic, revealed by the absence of self.

集諦三者：一、習氣集，謂遍計所執自性執習氣，執彼習氣假立彼名；二、等起集，謂業、煩惱；三、未離繫集，謂未離障真如。滅諦三者：一、自性滅，自性不生故；二、二取滅，謂擇滅二取不生故；三、本性滅，謂真如故。道諦三者：一、遍知道，能知遍計所執故；二、永斷道，能斷依他起故；三、作證道，能證圓成實故。然遍知道，亦通後二。七三三性，如次配釋。今於此中，所配三性，或假或實，如理應知。

Origination: origination through habituation, the habituation of attachment to the discriminated, attached-to nature; origination through equal arising, karma and affliction; origination through not-yet-being-freed-from-bondage, suchness not yet freed from obstruction. Cessation: cessation through own-nature; cessation of the two graspings, the analytical cessation in which they no longer arise; cessation as fundamental nature, suchness itself. The path: the path of complete knowledge, knowing the discriminated, attached-to nature; the path of permanent elimination, eliminating the dependent nature; the path of realization, realizing the perfectly accomplished nature — though the path of complete knowledge also extends to the latter two.

These seven groups of three each correspond, in the order given, to the three natures; whether each is provisional or real should be understood according to reason.

三解脫門所行境界，與此三性相攝云何？理實皆通，隨相各一。空、無願、相，如次應知。緣此復生三無生忍：一、本性無生忍；二、自然無生忍；三、惑苦無生忍。如次此三，是彼境故。此三云何攝彼二諦？應知世俗具此三種；勝義唯是圓成實性。世俗有三：一、假世俗；二、行世俗；三、顯了世俗。如次應知即此三性。勝義有三：一、義勝義，謂真如，勝之義故；二、得勝義，謂涅槃，勝即義故；三、行勝義，謂聖道，勝為義故。無變、無倒，隨其所應，故皆攝在圓成實性。

How do the objects of the three gates of liberation correspond to these three natures? In principle, all three gates extend to all three natures; classified according to their most fitting correspondence, emptiness, wishlessness, and signlessness, in the order given, correspond to the three. Taking these as their object, three further forms of the patient acceptance of non-arising are generated: of essential nature, of spontaneous nature, and of affliction and suffering — these three, in the same order, take the three natures as their object.

How do these three natures correspond to the two truths? Conventional truth encompasses all three; ultimate truth is exclusively the perfectly accomplished nature. Conventional truth is of three kinds — provisional, functional, and manifest — corresponding, in the same order, to the three natures. Ultimate truth is of three kinds: the ultimate as meaning, namely suchness; the ultimate as attainment, namely nirvāṇa; the ultimate as practice, namely the noble path — all three, being unchanging and free of distortion, are included within the perfectly accomplished nature.

Real or Provisional: The Three Natures Themselves 釋「三性假、實」· 結歸

Chinese Source

如是三性，何智所行？遍計所執，都非智所行，以無自體，非所緣緣故。愚夫執有，聖者達無，亦得說為凡、聖智境。依他起性，二智所行。圓成實性，唯聖智境。此三性中，幾假？幾實？遍計所執，妄安立故，可說為假；無體相故，非假非實。依他起性，有實有假：聚集、相續、分位性故，說為假有；心、心所、色，從緣生故，說為實有。若無實法，假法亦無，假依實因而施設故。圓成實性，唯是實有，不依他緣而施設故。此三為異？為不異耶？應說俱非，無別體故；妄執、緣起、真義別故。如是三性，義類無邊，恐厭繁文，略示綱要。

Ru-Yi Translation

Which wisdom, then, takes each of these three natures as its object? The discriminated, attached-to nature is not, in fact, the object of any wisdom at all, since it has no essence of its own and is not a condition of objective support — yet, since the ignorant grasp at its existence while the noble realize its nonexistence, it may also be spoken of as the object of both ordinary and noble wisdom. The dependent nature is the object of both fundamental and subsequently-attained wisdom. The perfectly accomplished nature is the object of noble wisdom alone.

Among these three natures, how many are provisional, and how many real? The discriminated, attached-to nature, being falsely posited, may be called provisional; yet, having no essence or characteristic at all, it is, strictly speaking, neither provisional nor real. The dependent nature has both real and provisional aspects: what is of the nature of an aggregation, a continuity, or a positional state is called provisionally existent; mind, mental factors, and form, arising from conditions, are called genuinely existent — for, without a real dharma, no provisional dharma could exist either, since a provisional designation is established only in dependence upon a real cause. The perfectly accomplished nature is exclusively, genuinely existent, since it is established without depending on any other condition.

Are these three natures different from one another, or not different? Neither applies: they have no separate essence apart from one another, and yet false attachment, dependent arising, and genuine reality are each distinct in meaning.

The full range of meaning involved in these three natures is boundless; for fear of wearying the reader with excessive text, only its essential outline has been shown here.

Reading note: This closing cross-reference sweep is not meant to be studied cell by cell on a first pass — its real purpose is to demonstrate that the Three Natures is not one doctrine among many, but the master framework this entire tradition's other classificatory schemes all ultimately cash out in terms of. Suchness, the categories of dharma, the five aspects of language and cognition, the four truths, the gates of liberation, the two truths — every one of them, however differently organized on its own terms, sorts cleanly into imagined, dependent, and perfectly accomplished once examined closely enough. That convergence is itself a kind of argument for the framework's importance.

The final verdict on real versus provisional existence is worth isolating from the surrounding detail: the imagined nature is strictly neither (having no essence at all, it fails to qualify as either real or fake); the dependent nature is a genuine mixture, with real momentary events like mind and mental factors underneath and provisional groupings like "a person" or "a lifetime" layered on top of them; and the perfectly accomplished nature is unconditionally real, needing nothing else to establish it. This gives the entire system a clear ontological floor — mind, mental factors, and form, arising through causes and conditions — everything else, from persons to concepts to the very structure of language, is a provisional overlay on that floor, while suchness is what that floor's own nature turns out to be once every false overlay is finally seen through.

With this, fascicle 8 closes the treatise's foundational metaphysics: causation fully mapped (the ten causes and five fruits), the mechanics of birth-and-death fully explained (karma, the twelve links, and the two kinds of birth-and-death), and now the Three Natures — arguably the single framework the remaining fascicles will lean on most heavily as the treatise turns, from here, toward the structure of the path itself.
