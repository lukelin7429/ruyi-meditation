Fascicle 5 · 成唯識論卷第五

When Manas Finally Falls Silent

Fascicle 5 opens by finishing the root verses on manas: which feeling accompanies it, its "covered-yet-neutral" moral quality, how it is bound to whatever realm a being is born into, and the three states where it is absent — arhatship, cessation-attainment, and the supramundane path. A long debate then asks whether an undefiled manas survives even there, before the treatise gives its threefold classification of manas across the entire path to buddhahood.

Manas's Feeling, Moral Quality, and Realm 明唯識相 · 釋第二能變 · 何受相應 · 有覆無記 · 何地繫

The treatise continues working through the root verses on manas, now asking three questions in turn: what feeling accompanies it, what moral category it belongs to, and which realm it is bound to.

Only Equanimous Feeling 釋「何受相應」

Chinese Source

此染污意，何受相應？有義：此俱唯有喜受，恒內執我，生喜愛故。有義：不然。應許喜受，乃至有頂，違聖言故。應說：此意四受相應。謂生惡趣憂受相應，緣不善業所引果故。生人、欲天、初、二靜慮，喜受相應，緣有喜地善業果故。第三靜慮，樂受相應，緣有樂地善業果故。第四靜慮乃至有頂，捨受相應，緣唯捨地善業果故。

Ru-Yi Translation

With what feeling is this defiled manas associated? One view holds: it is accompanied only by joyful feeling, since it constantly, internally clings to a self, giving rise to joy and love. Another view holds: this is not so — for accepting joyful feeling all the way up to the peak of existence would contradict the scriptures' own statement.

One should say, instead: this manas is associated with four kinds of feeling. Born into the lower destinies, it is associated with sorrowful feeling, since it takes as its object the result drawn forth by unwholesome karma. Born as a human, in the desire-realm heavens, or in the first and second meditative absorptions, it is associated with joyful feeling, since it takes as its object the result of wholesome karma belonging to a realm possessing joy. In the third meditative absorption, it is associated with pleasurable feeling, since it takes as its object the result of wholesome karma belonging to a realm possessing pleasure. From the fourth meditative absorption up to the peak of existence, it is associated with equanimous feeling, since it takes as its object only the result of wholesome karma belonging to a realm of equanimity.

有義：彼說亦不應理。此無始來，任運一類，緣內執我，恒無轉易，與變異受，不相應故。又此末那與前藏識義有異者，皆別說之。若四受俱，亦應別說。既不別說，定與彼同。故此相應，唯有捨受。

Another view holds: that account, too, is unreasonable. This manas, from beginningless time, spontaneously operates as a single, unchanging kind, taking hold of an internally clung-to self, forever without alteration — and so it cannot be associated with a feeling that itself varies and changes. Moreover, wherever this manas differs in meaning from the storehouse consciousness discussed earlier, the treatise states this difference explicitly. If manas were associated with all four feelings, this too would have been stated explicitly. Since it is not stated explicitly, manas must certainly be the same as the ālaya in this respect. Hence manas is associated only with equanimous feeling.

未轉依位，與前所說心所相應；已轉依位，唯二十一心所俱起，謂遍行、別境各五，善十一。如第八識，已轉依位，唯捨受俱，任運轉故。恒於所緣平等轉故。

Before the transformation of basis, manas is associated with the mental factors already described. Once the transformation has occurred, only twenty-one mental factors arise together with it — the five universal factors, the five particular factors, and the eleven wholesome factors — just as with the eighth consciousness. Once the transformation has occurred, manas is accompanied only by equanimous feeling, since it operates spontaneously, constantly turning toward its object with perfect evenness.

Covered-Yet-Neutral 釋「有覆無記攝」

Chinese Source

末那心所，何性所攝？有覆無記所攝，非餘。此意相應四煩惱等，是染法故；障礙聖道，隱蔽自心，說名有覆；非善、不善，故名無記。如上二界諸煩惱等，定力攝藏，是無記攝。此俱染法，所依細故，任運轉故，亦無記攝。若已轉依，唯是善性。

Ru-Yi Translation

To what moral category does manas, together with its mental factors, belong? It belongs to the covered-yet-neutral category, and nothing else. The four afflictions and the rest associated with this manas are defiled dharmas — obstructing the noble path and concealing one's own mind, they are called "covered"; being neither wholesome nor unwholesome, they are called "neutral." Just as the afflictions of the upper two realms, held in check by the power of meditative concentration, are classified as neutral — this manas, too, together with its defiled dharmas, is classified as neutral, since its support is subtle and it operates spontaneously. Once the transformation of basis has occurred, it is exclusively wholesome in nature.

Bound to Whichever Realm 釋「隨所生所繫」

Chinese Source

末那心所，何地繫耶？隨彼所生，彼地所繫。謂生欲界，現行末那相應心所，即欲界繫。乃至有頂應知亦然；任運恒緣自地藏識，執為內我，非他地故。若起彼地異熟藏識現在前者，名生彼地；染污末那，緣彼執我，即繫屬彼，名彼所繫。或為彼地諸煩惱等之所繫縛，名彼所繫。若已轉依，即非所繫。

Ru-Yi Translation

To which realm is manas, together with its mental factors, bound? It is bound to whatever realm a being is born into. That is: when born in the desire realm, the manifest manas and its associated mental factors are bound to the desire realm; and the same should be understood all the way up to the peak of existence — since manas spontaneously, constantly takes as its object the storehouse consciousness of its own realm specifically, clinging to it as an inner self, never that of another realm.

Whenever the ripening storehouse consciousness of a given realm is currently manifest, the being is said to be "born in that realm"; the defiled manas, taking that consciousness as its object and clinging to it as self, is thereby bound to that realm, and so is said to be "bound to that realm." Or else, it is bound by the afflictions of that particular realm, and is thus said to be "bound to that realm" in this sense. Once the transformation of basis has occurred, it is no longer bound in this way at all.

Reading note: All three questions here get the same kind of answer, and it's worth noticing the pattern: manas's feeling, moral coloring, and realm are all inherited from, or matched to, the ālaya it clings to, rather than generated independently. It feels only equanimity because the ālaya it fixates on is itself changeless from moment to moment — a variable feeling would require a variable object, which self-grasping, by its very nature as an unbroken, undifferentiated clinging, cannot have. It counts as "neutral" for the same reason the afflictions of deep meditative absorption count as neutral: not because it is harmless, but because its operation is too subtle and automatic to register as an active moral choice the way a deliberate unwholesome act does. And it is bound to whatever realm a being is currently in for the simplest possible reason — it only ever clings to the ālaya "right here," never reaching across to another realm's.

Three States Where It Falls Silent 明唯識相 · 釋第二能變 · 阿羅漢、滅定、出世道無有

The verse's closing line names three states where manas is absent: arhatship, cessation-attainment, and the supramundane path. The treatise distinguishes permanent severance from mere temporary suppression, then asks a harder question — does an undefiled manas survive even in these three states?

Permanently Severed, or Only Suppressed 釋「阿羅漢、滅定、出世道無有」

Chinese Source

此染污意，無始相續，何位永斷？或暫斷耶？「阿羅漢、滅定、出世道無有。」阿羅漢者，總顯三乘無學果位。此位染意種及現行，俱永斷滅，故說無有。學位滅定、出世道中，俱暫伏滅，故說無有。

Ru-Yi Translation

This defiled manas has continued since beginningless time — at what stage is it permanently severed, or merely temporarily suppressed? The verse says: "It is absent in arhats, in cessation-attainment, and on the supramundane path." "Arhat" here refers broadly to the fruit of having-finished-training in any of the three vehicles — at this stage, both the seed and the manifest activity of this defiled manas are permanently severed and extinguished, and so it is said to be absent. In the stages of cessation-attainment and the supramundane path, still within training, it is only temporarily suppressed and extinguished, and so it, too, is said to be absent there.

謂染污意，無始時來，微細一類，任運而轉，諸有漏道，不能伏滅；三乘聖道，有伏滅義。真無我解，違我執故。後得無漏現在前時，是彼等流，亦違此意。真無我解，及後所得，俱無漏故，名出世道。滅定既是聖道等流，極寂靜故，此亦非有。由未永斷此種子故，從滅盡定、聖道起已，此復現行，乃至未滅。

This defiled manas, from beginningless time, operates subtly, as a single unbroken kind, spontaneously — no contaminated path can suppress or extinguish it; only the noble paths of the three vehicles have the capacity to suppress and extinguish it, since genuine understanding of no-self directly opposes self-grasping. When the subsequently-attained uncontaminated wisdom that flows from this understanding manifests, it too opposes this manas, since both the genuine understanding of no-self and what is subsequently attained from it are equally uncontaminated — hence together they are called "the supramundane path."

Cessation-attainment, being itself an outflow of the noble path, is extremely tranquil, and so this manas is absent there as well. Yet since its seed has not been permanently severed, once cessation-attainment or the noble path is exited, this manas manifests again, right up until it is finally, permanently extinguished.

然此染意相應煩惱，是俱生故，非見所斷。是染污故，非非所斷。極微細故，所有種子，與有頂地，下下煩惱，一時頓斷，勢力等故；金剛喻定現在前時，頓斷此種，成阿羅漢，故無學位，永不復起。二乘無學，迴趣大乘，從初發心，至未成佛，雖實是菩薩，亦名阿羅漢。應義等故，不別說之。

The afflictions associated with this defiled mind, being entirely innate, are not eliminated on the path of seeing; being genuinely defiled, they are certainly not beyond elimination altogether. Being extremely subtle, their seeds are severed all at once, together with the very subtlest afflictions of the peak of existence, their power being equal — when the vajra-like concentration manifests, it severs these seeds all at once, and one becomes an arhat; hence, at the stage beyond further training, they never arise again.

Śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas beyond further training who turn toward the Mahāyāna — from their initial arousal of intention up until they have not yet attained buddhahood — though genuinely bodhisattvas, are also called "arhats," since the meaning applies equally to them; hence this is not separately discussed here.

Does an Undefiled Manas Survive? 有義：末那唯有煩惱障俱 · 有義：教理相違

Chinese Source

此中有義：末那唯有煩惱障俱。聖教皆言：三位無故。又說：四惑恒相應故。又說：為識雜染依故。有義：彼說教、理相違，出世末那，經說有故。無染意識，如有染時，定有俱生不共依故。

Ru-Yi Translation

On this point, one view holds: manas is associated only with the afflictive obstruction. The scriptures everywhere say that it is absent in the three stages just discussed; they also say that it is constantly associated with the four afflictions; and they say that it serves as the basis for the defilement of the other consciousnesses.

Another view holds: that account conflicts with both scripture and reason, since the scriptures state that manas exists even on the supramundane path. An undefiled mental consciousness, just as when it is defiled, must certainly have some innate, exclusive support of its own.

論說：藏識決定恒與一識俱轉，所謂末那；意識起時，則二俱轉，所謂意識，及與末那；若五識中隨起一識，則三俱轉，乃至或時頓起五識，則七俱轉。若住滅定，無第七識，爾時，藏識應無識俱，便非恒定一識俱轉。住聖道時，若無第七。爾時，藏識應一識俱，如何可言，若起意識，爾時，藏識定二俱轉？

The treatise states: the storehouse consciousness always, certainly turns together with at least one other consciousness, namely manas; when the mental consciousness arises, then two turn together, namely the mental consciousness and manas; if any one of the five sense-consciousnesses arises, then three turn together; and if, at some time, all five sense-consciousnesses arise at once, then seven turn together.

But if, while abiding in cessation-attainment, the seventh consciousness were absent, then, at that time, the storehouse consciousness would have to turn together with no consciousness at all, and it would then no longer be certain that it constantly turns together with at least one. And while abiding on the noble path, if the seventh were absent, then, at that time, the storehouse consciousness would turn together with only itself alone — so how, then, could it be said that, whenever the mental consciousness arises, the storehouse consciousness certainly turns together with two?

《顯揚論》說：末那恒與四煩惱相應，或翻彼相應，恃舉為行，或平等行。故知此意通染、不染。若由論說：阿羅漢位無染意故，便無第七。應由論說：阿羅漢位捨賴耶故，便無第八。彼既不爾，此云何然？

The Prakaraṇāryavācā-śāstra says: manas is constantly associated either with the four afflictions, or else with their opposite, its activity being either one of self-important pride, or one of perfect equanimity. From this it can be known that this manas extends to both a defiled and an undefiled state.

If, on the strength of the treatise's saying that an arhat has no defiled manas, one concludes that the seventh consciousness is thereby entirely absent — then, by the same reasoning, since the treatise also says that an arhat abandons the ālaya, one would have to conclude the eighth consciousness is thereby entirely absent too. Since that conclusion is not accepted, why should this one be?

又諸論言：轉第七識，得平等智。彼如餘智，定有所依相應淨識。此識無者，彼智應無。非離所依有能依故。不可說彼依六轉識，許佛恒行，如鏡智故。又無學位，若無第七識，彼第八識，應無俱有依。然必有此依，如餘識性故。

Furthermore, various treatises say: by transforming the seventh consciousness, one attains the wisdom of equality. Like every other wisdom, this wisdom certainly has a support, a purified consciousness with which it is associated. If this consciousness did not exist, that wisdom could not exist either, since a supported thing cannot exist apart from its support. Nor can one say that this wisdom instead depends on the sixth, evolving consciousness, since it is accepted that this wisdom operates constantly in a buddha, exactly like the mirror wisdom.

Furthermore, if the seventh consciousness were absent at the stage beyond further training, the eighth consciousness would then have no co-arising support at all. Yet it must certainly have such a support, being of the same essential nature as consciousness as the others.

又如未證補特伽羅無我者，彼我執恒行；亦應未證法無我者，法我執恒行，此識若無，彼依何識？非依第八，彼無慧故。由此應信，二乘聖道、滅定、無學此識恒行，彼未證得法無我故。又諸論中，以五同法證有第七，為第六依。聖道起時，及無學位，若無第七為第六依，所立宗、因，便俱有失。或應五識亦有無依。五恒有依，六亦應爾。

Furthermore, just as one who has not yet realized the selflessness of the person has self-grasping constantly operating, so too one who has not yet realized the selflessness of dharmas should have dharma-grasping constantly operating — if this consciousness did not exist, on what consciousness would that grasping depend? It cannot depend on the eighth, since the eighth lacks the discriminating wisdom needed for such grasping. By this, one should believe that, on the noble path of the two vehicles, in cessation-attainment, and at the stage beyond further training, this consciousness continues to operate constantly, since these practitioners have not yet realized the selflessness of dharmas.

Furthermore, various treatises use the parallel case of the five sense-consciousnesses to prove that the seventh exists, serving as the sixth's support. If, when the noble path arises, or at the stage beyond further training, there were no seventh consciousness to serve as the sixth's support, then both the thesis and the reason offered in that proof would fail together — or else one would have to say the five sense-consciousnesses, too, sometimes lack a support. Since the five always have a support, the sixth, too, should always have one.

是故定有無染污意，於上三位，恒起現前。言彼無者，依染意說；如說四位無阿賴耶，非無第八，此亦應爾。

Hence there certainly exists an undefiled manas, constantly manifesting in these three stages. When it is said to be "absent" there, this refers only to the defiled manas; just as it is said that the ālaya is absent in four states without meaning the eighth consciousness itself is absent, so too should this be understood here.

Reading note: This debate rhymes deliberately with the earlier argument, back in fascicle 3, that the ālaya is only ever said to be "absent" in its defiled aspect, never in its bare existence — the treatise applies the very same move to manas here, and flags the parallel explicitly at the end. The core evidence is structural rather than scriptural proof-texting: manas is the mental consciousness's support, and the mental consciousness plainly still operates in an arhat, in cessation-attainment, and on the supramundane path — so whatever "absent" means in the verse, it cannot mean manas has vanished as a support altogether, only that its self-grasping, defiled aspect has fallen silent.

The argument from the wisdom of equality (平等性智) is worth flagging for what comes later in the treatise: a buddha's wisdom needs something to operate through, the same way ordinary cognition needs a consciousness to run on, and this wisdom is specifically the transformation of manas, not the sixth consciousness wearing a different hat. An undefiled manas, then, isn't a strange leftover — it's the very same seventh consciousness, freed of self-grasping, that becomes the vehicle for one of a buddha's four wisdoms.
