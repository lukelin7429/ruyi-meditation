Fascicle 5 · 成唯識論卷第五

When Manas Finally Falls Silent

Fascicle 5 opens by finishing the root verses on manas: which feeling accompanies it, its "covered-yet-neutral" moral quality, how it is bound to whatever realm a being is born into, and the three states where it is absent — arhatship, cessation-attainment, and the supramundane path. A long debate then asks whether an undefiled manas survives even there, before the treatise gives its threefold classification of manas across the entire path to buddhahood.

Manas's Feeling, Moral Quality, and Realm 明唯識相 · 釋第二能變 · 何受相應 · 有覆無記 · 何地繫

The treatise continues working through the root verses on manas, now asking three questions in turn: what feeling accompanies it, what moral category it belongs to, and which realm it is bound to.

Only Equanimous Feeling 釋「何受相應」

Chinese Source

此染污意，何受相應？有義：此俱唯有喜受，恒內執我，生喜愛故。有義：不然。應許喜受，乃至有頂，違聖言故。應說：此意四受相應。謂生惡趣憂受相應，緣不善業所引果故。生人、欲天、初、二靜慮，喜受相應，緣有喜地善業果故。第三靜慮，樂受相應，緣有樂地善業果故。第四靜慮乃至有頂，捨受相應，緣唯捨地善業果故。

Ru-Yi Translation

With what feeling is this defiled manas associated? One view holds: it is accompanied only by joyful feeling, since it constantly, internally clings to a self, giving rise to joy and love. Another view holds: this is not so — for accepting joyful feeling all the way up to the peak of existence would contradict the scriptures' own statement.

One should say, instead: this manas is associated with four kinds of feeling. Born into the lower destinies, it is associated with sorrowful feeling, since it takes as its object the result drawn forth by unwholesome karma. Born as a human, in the desire-realm heavens, or in the first and second meditative absorptions, it is associated with joyful feeling, since it takes as its object the result of wholesome karma belonging to a realm possessing joy. In the third meditative absorption, it is associated with pleasurable feeling, since it takes as its object the result of wholesome karma belonging to a realm possessing pleasure. From the fourth meditative absorption up to the peak of existence, it is associated with equanimous feeling, since it takes as its object only the result of wholesome karma belonging to a realm of equanimity.

有義：彼說亦不應理。此無始來，任運一類，緣內執我，恒無轉易，與變異受，不相應故。又此末那與前藏識義有異者，皆別說之。若四受俱，亦應別說。既不別說，定與彼同。故此相應，唯有捨受。

Another view holds: that account, too, is unreasonable. This manas, from beginningless time, spontaneously operates as a single, unchanging kind, taking hold of an internally clung-to self, forever without alteration — and so it cannot be associated with a feeling that itself varies and changes. Moreover, wherever this manas differs in meaning from the storehouse consciousness discussed earlier, the treatise states this difference explicitly. If manas were associated with all four feelings, this too would have been stated explicitly. Since it is not stated explicitly, manas must certainly be the same as the ālaya in this respect. Hence manas is associated only with equanimous feeling.

未轉依位，與前所說心所相應；已轉依位，唯二十一心所俱起，謂遍行、別境各五，善十一。如第八識，已轉依位，唯捨受俱，任運轉故。恒於所緣平等轉故。

Before the transformation of basis, manas is associated with the mental factors already described. Once the transformation has occurred, only twenty-one mental factors arise together with it — the five universal factors, the five particular factors, and the eleven wholesome factors — just as with the eighth consciousness. Once the transformation has occurred, manas is accompanied only by equanimous feeling, since it operates spontaneously, constantly turning toward its object with perfect evenness.

Covered-Yet-Neutral 釋「有覆無記攝」

Chinese Source

末那心所，何性所攝？有覆無記所攝，非餘。此意相應四煩惱等，是染法故；障礙聖道，隱蔽自心，說名有覆；非善、不善，故名無記。如上二界諸煩惱等，定力攝藏，是無記攝。此俱染法，所依細故，任運轉故，亦無記攝。若已轉依，唯是善性。

Ru-Yi Translation

To what moral category does manas, together with its mental factors, belong? It belongs to the covered-yet-neutral category, and nothing else. The four afflictions and the rest associated with this manas are defiled dharmas — obstructing the noble path and concealing one's own mind, they are called "covered"; being neither wholesome nor unwholesome, they are called "neutral." Just as the afflictions of the upper two realms, held in check by the power of meditative concentration, are classified as neutral — this manas, too, together with its defiled dharmas, is classified as neutral, since its support is subtle and it operates spontaneously. Once the transformation of basis has occurred, it is exclusively wholesome in nature.

Bound to Whichever Realm 釋「隨所生所繫」

Chinese Source

末那心所，何地繫耶？隨彼所生，彼地所繫。謂生欲界，現行末那相應心所，即欲界繫。乃至有頂應知亦然；任運恒緣自地藏識，執為內我，非他地故。若起彼地異熟藏識現在前者，名生彼地；染污末那，緣彼執我，即繫屬彼，名彼所繫。或為彼地諸煩惱等之所繫縛，名彼所繫。若已轉依，即非所繫。

Ru-Yi Translation

To which realm is manas, together with its mental factors, bound? It is bound to whatever realm a being is born into. That is: when born in the desire realm, the manifest manas and its associated mental factors are bound to the desire realm; and the same should be understood all the way up to the peak of existence — since manas spontaneously, constantly takes as its object the storehouse consciousness of its own realm specifically, clinging to it as an inner self, never that of another realm.

Whenever the ripening storehouse consciousness of a given realm is currently manifest, the being is said to be "born in that realm"; the defiled manas, taking that consciousness as its object and clinging to it as self, is thereby bound to that realm, and so is said to be "bound to that realm." Or else, it is bound by the afflictions of that particular realm, and is thus said to be "bound to that realm" in this sense. Once the transformation of basis has occurred, it is no longer bound in this way at all.

Reading note: All three questions here get the same kind of answer, and it's worth noticing the pattern: manas's feeling, moral coloring, and realm are all inherited from, or matched to, the ālaya it clings to, rather than generated independently. It feels only equanimity because the ālaya it fixates on is itself changeless from moment to moment — a variable feeling would require a variable object, which self-grasping, by its very nature as an unbroken, undifferentiated clinging, cannot have. It counts as "neutral" for the same reason the afflictions of deep meditative absorption count as neutral: not because it is harmless, but because its operation is too subtle and automatic to register as an active moral choice the way a deliberate unwholesome act does. And it is bound to whatever realm a being is currently in for the simplest possible reason — it only ever clings to the ālaya "right here," never reaching across to another realm's.

Three States Where It Falls Silent 明唯識相 · 釋第二能變 · 阿羅漢、滅定、出世道無有

The verse's closing line names three states where manas is absent: arhatship, cessation-attainment, and the supramundane path. The treatise distinguishes permanent severance from mere temporary suppression, then asks a harder question — does an undefiled manas survive even in these three states?

Permanently Severed, or Only Suppressed 釋「阿羅漢、滅定、出世道無有」

Chinese Source

此染污意，無始相續，何位永斷？或暫斷耶？「阿羅漢、滅定、出世道無有。」阿羅漢者，總顯三乘無學果位。此位染意種及現行，俱永斷滅，故說無有。學位滅定、出世道中，俱暫伏滅，故說無有。

Ru-Yi Translation

This defiled manas has continued since beginningless time — at what stage is it permanently severed, or merely temporarily suppressed? The verse says: "It is absent in arhats, in cessation-attainment, and on the supramundane path." "Arhat" here refers broadly to the fruit of having-finished-training in any of the three vehicles — at this stage, both the seed and the manifest activity of this defiled manas are permanently severed and extinguished, and so it is said to be absent. In the stages of cessation-attainment and the supramundane path, still within training, it is only temporarily suppressed and extinguished, and so it, too, is said to be absent there.

謂染污意，無始時來，微細一類，任運而轉，諸有漏道，不能伏滅；三乘聖道，有伏滅義。真無我解，違我執故。後得無漏現在前時，是彼等流，亦違此意。真無我解，及後所得，俱無漏故，名出世道。滅定既是聖道等流，極寂靜故，此亦非有。由未永斷此種子故，從滅盡定、聖道起已，此復現行，乃至未滅。

This defiled manas, from beginningless time, operates subtly, as a single unbroken kind, spontaneously — no contaminated path can suppress or extinguish it; only the noble paths of the three vehicles have the capacity to suppress and extinguish it, since genuine understanding of no-self directly opposes self-grasping. When the subsequently-attained uncontaminated wisdom that flows from this understanding manifests, it too opposes this manas, since both the genuine understanding of no-self and what is subsequently attained from it are equally uncontaminated — hence together they are called "the supramundane path."

Cessation-attainment, being itself an outflow of the noble path, is extremely tranquil, and so this manas is absent there as well. Yet since its seed has not been permanently severed, once cessation-attainment or the noble path is exited, this manas manifests again, right up until it is finally, permanently extinguished.

然此染意相應煩惱，是俱生故，非見所斷。是染污故，非非所斷。極微細故，所有種子，與有頂地，下下煩惱，一時頓斷，勢力等故；金剛喻定現在前時，頓斷此種，成阿羅漢，故無學位，永不復起。二乘無學，迴趣大乘，從初發心，至未成佛，雖實是菩薩，亦名阿羅漢。應義等故，不別說之。

The afflictions associated with this defiled mind, being entirely innate, are not eliminated on the path of seeing; being genuinely defiled, they are certainly not beyond elimination altogether. Being extremely subtle, their seeds are severed all at once, together with the very subtlest afflictions of the peak of existence, their power being equal — when the vajra-like concentration manifests, it severs these seeds all at once, and one becomes an arhat; hence, at the stage beyond further training, they never arise again.

Śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas beyond further training who turn toward the Mahāyāna — from their initial arousal of intention up until they have not yet attained buddhahood — though genuinely bodhisattvas, are also called "arhats," since the meaning applies equally to them; hence this is not separately discussed here.

Does an Undefiled Manas Survive? 有義：末那唯有煩惱障俱 · 有義：教理相違

Chinese Source

此中有義：末那唯有煩惱障俱。聖教皆言：三位無故。又說：四惑恒相應故。又說：為識雜染依故。有義：彼說教、理相違，出世末那，經說有故。無染意識，如有染時，定有俱生不共依故。

Ru-Yi Translation

On this point, one view holds: manas is associated only with the afflictive obstruction. The scriptures everywhere say that it is absent in the three stages just discussed; they also say that it is constantly associated with the four afflictions; and they say that it serves as the basis for the defilement of the other consciousnesses.

Another view holds: that account conflicts with both scripture and reason, since the scriptures state that manas exists even on the supramundane path. An undefiled mental consciousness, just as when it is defiled, must certainly have some innate, exclusive support of its own.

論說：藏識決定恒與一識俱轉，所謂末那；意識起時，則二俱轉，所謂意識，及與末那；若五識中隨起一識，則三俱轉，乃至或時頓起五識，則七俱轉。若住滅定，無第七識，爾時，藏識應無識俱，便非恒定一識俱轉。住聖道時，若無第七。爾時，藏識應一識俱，如何可言，若起意識，爾時，藏識定二俱轉？

The treatise states: the storehouse consciousness always, certainly turns together with at least one other consciousness, namely manas; when the mental consciousness arises, then two turn together, namely the mental consciousness and manas; if any one of the five sense-consciousnesses arises, then three turn together; and if, at some time, all five sense-consciousnesses arise at once, then seven turn together.

But if, while abiding in cessation-attainment, the seventh consciousness were absent, then, at that time, the storehouse consciousness would have to turn together with no consciousness at all, and it would then no longer be certain that it constantly turns together with at least one. And while abiding on the noble path, if the seventh were absent, then, at that time, the storehouse consciousness would turn together with only itself alone — so how, then, could it be said that, whenever the mental consciousness arises, the storehouse consciousness certainly turns together with two?

《顯揚論》說：末那恒與四煩惱相應，或翻彼相應，恃舉為行，或平等行。故知此意通染、不染。若由論說：阿羅漢位無染意故，便無第七。應由論說：阿羅漢位捨賴耶故，便無第八。彼既不爾，此云何然？

The Prakaraṇāryavācā-śāstra says: manas is constantly associated either with the four afflictions, or else with their opposite, its activity being either one of self-important pride, or one of perfect equanimity. From this it can be known that this manas extends to both a defiled and an undefiled state.

If, on the strength of the treatise's saying that an arhat has no defiled manas, one concludes that the seventh consciousness is thereby entirely absent — then, by the same reasoning, since the treatise also says that an arhat abandons the ālaya, one would have to conclude the eighth consciousness is thereby entirely absent too. Since that conclusion is not accepted, why should this one be?

又諸論言：轉第七識，得平等智。彼如餘智，定有所依相應淨識。此識無者，彼智應無。非離所依有能依故。不可說彼依六轉識，許佛恒行，如鏡智故。又無學位，若無第七識，彼第八識，應無俱有依。然必有此依，如餘識性故。

Furthermore, various treatises say: by transforming the seventh consciousness, one attains the wisdom of equality. Like every other wisdom, this wisdom certainly has a support, a purified consciousness with which it is associated. If this consciousness did not exist, that wisdom could not exist either, since a supported thing cannot exist apart from its support. Nor can one say that this wisdom instead depends on the sixth, evolving consciousness, since it is accepted that this wisdom operates constantly in a buddha, exactly like the mirror wisdom.

Furthermore, if the seventh consciousness were absent at the stage beyond further training, the eighth consciousness would then have no co-arising support at all. Yet it must certainly have such a support, being of the same essential nature as consciousness as the others.

又如未證補特伽羅無我者，彼我執恒行；亦應未證法無我者，法我執恒行，此識若無，彼依何識？非依第八，彼無慧故。由此應信，二乘聖道、滅定、無學此識恒行，彼未證得法無我故。又諸論中，以五同法證有第七，為第六依。聖道起時，及無學位，若無第七為第六依，所立宗、因，便俱有失。或應五識亦有無依。五恒有依，六亦應爾。

Furthermore, just as one who has not yet realized the selflessness of the person has self-grasping constantly operating, so too one who has not yet realized the selflessness of dharmas should have dharma-grasping constantly operating — if this consciousness did not exist, on what consciousness would that grasping depend? It cannot depend on the eighth, since the eighth lacks the discriminating wisdom needed for such grasping. By this, one should believe that, on the noble path of the two vehicles, in cessation-attainment, and at the stage beyond further training, this consciousness continues to operate constantly, since these practitioners have not yet realized the selflessness of dharmas.

Furthermore, various treatises use the parallel case of the five sense-consciousnesses to prove that the seventh exists, serving as the sixth's support. If, when the noble path arises, or at the stage beyond further training, there were no seventh consciousness to serve as the sixth's support, then both the thesis and the reason offered in that proof would fail together — or else one would have to say the five sense-consciousnesses, too, sometimes lack a support. Since the five always have a support, the sixth, too, should always have one.

是故定有無染污意，於上三位，恒起現前。言彼無者，依染意說；如說四位無阿賴耶，非無第八，此亦應爾。

Hence there certainly exists an undefiled manas, constantly manifesting in these three stages. When it is said to be "absent" there, this refers only to the defiled manas; just as it is said that the ālaya is absent in four states without meaning the eighth consciousness itself is absent, so too should this be understood here.

Reading note: This debate rhymes deliberately with the earlier argument, back in fascicle 3, that the ālaya is only ever said to be "absent" in its defiled aspect, never in its bare existence — the treatise applies the very same move to manas here, and flags the parallel explicitly at the end. The core evidence is structural rather than scriptural proof-texting: manas is the mental consciousness's support, and the mental consciousness plainly still operates in an arhat, in cessation-attainment, and on the supramundane path — so whatever "absent" means in the verse, it cannot mean manas has vanished as a support altogether, only that its self-grasping, defiled aspect has fallen silent.

The argument from the wisdom of equality (平等性智) is worth flagging for what comes later in the treatise: a buddha's wisdom needs something to operate through, the same way ordinary cognition needs a consciousness to run on, and this wisdom is specifically the transformation of manas, not the sixth consciousness wearing a different hat. An undefiled manas, then, isn't a strange leftover — it's the very same seventh consciousness, freed of self-grasping, that becomes the vehicle for one of a buddha's four wisdoms.

Manas's Three Stages Across the Path 明唯識相 · 釋第二能變 · 此意差別

With the existence of an undefiled manas established, the treatise now maps its entire career, from ordinary beings all the way to buddhahood, onto three distinct stages — distinguishing self-grasping from a subtler, longer-lasting grasping at dharmas as real.

Grasping the Person, Grasping Dharmas, Equality 補特伽羅我見 · 法我見 · 平等性智

Chinese Source

此意差別，略有三種：一、補特伽羅我見相應；二、法我見相應；三、平等性智相應。初通一切異生相續、二乘有學、七地以前一類菩薩有漏心位，彼緣阿賴耶識，起補特伽羅我見。次通一切異生、聲聞、獨覺相續、一切菩薩法空智果不現前位，彼緣異熟識，起法我見。後通一切如來相續、菩薩見道及修道中法空智果現在前位，彼緣無垢異熟識等，起平等性智。

Ru-Yi Translation

This manas, broadly speaking, has three distinct kinds: first, associated with the view of a personal self; second, associated with the view of dharmas as self; third, associated with the wisdom of equality.

The first extends through the entire continuity of ordinary beings, through śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas still in training, and through the contaminated mental states of a certain class of bodhisattvas before the eighth ground — taking the ālayavijñāna as its object, it gives rise to the view of a personal self.

The second extends through the entire continuity of ordinary beings, śrāvakas, and pratyekabuddhas, and through every bodhisattva's states in which the fruit of the wisdom realizing the emptiness of dharmas is not currently manifest — taking the ripening consciousness as its object, it gives rise to the view of dharmas as self.

The third extends through the entire continuity of every tathāgata, and through the states, on the bodhisattva's path of seeing and path of cultivation, in which the fruit of the wisdom realizing the emptiness of dharmas is currently manifest — taking the undefiled ripening consciousness as its object, it gives rise to the wisdom of equality.

補特伽羅我見起位，彼法我見亦必現前。我執必依法執而起。如夜迷杌等，方謂人等故。我、法二見，用雖有別，而不相違，同依一慧。如眼識等，體雖是一，而有了別青等多用，不相違故，此亦應然。

Whenever the view of a personal self arises, the view of dharmas as self must also be manifest at that very time, since self-grasping necessarily arises in dependence on dharma-grasping — just as, mistaking a post for something in the dark at night, one then takes it to be a person. Although the view of self and the view of dharmas as self differ in their function, they are not mutually opposed, and both rely on a single act of discriminating wisdom — just as the eye-consciousness and the rest, though a single essence, can perform multiple functions, such as discerning blue and other colors, without contradiction; this, too, should be understood in the same way.

二乘有學聖道，滅定現在前時；頓悟菩薩，於修道位；有學漸悟，生空智果現在前時，皆唯起法執，我執已伏故。二乘無學及此漸悟法空智果不現前時，亦唯起法執，我執已斷故。

When the noble path or cessation-attainment is currently manifest for a śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha still in training; for a bodhisattva of sudden awakening, on the path of cultivation; or for a gradually-awakening trainee, when the fruit of the wisdom realizing the emptiness of the person is currently manifest — in all these cases, only dharma-grasping arises, since self-grasping has already been suppressed.

For a śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha beyond further training, and for this gradually-awakening bodhisattva when the fruit of the wisdom realizing the emptiness of dharmas is not currently manifest — here, too, only dharma-grasping arises, since self-grasping has already been permanently severed.

八地以上，一切菩薩所有我執皆永不行，或已永斷；或永伏故。法空智果不現前時，猶起法執，不相違故。如契經說：八地以上，一切煩惱，不復現行；唯有所依所知障在。此所知障，是現非種。不爾，煩惱亦應在故。

From the eighth ground onward, every bodhisattva's self-grasping never operates at all — either already permanently severed, or permanently suppressed. When the fruit of the wisdom realizing the emptiness of dharmas is not currently manifest, dharma-grasping still arises, and there is no contradiction in this. As the sūtra says: from the eighth ground onward, no afflictions manifest at all any longer — only the cognitive obstruction, together with what it depends on, remains. This cognitive obstruction here refers to its manifest activity, not its seed — were this not so, the afflictions, too, would have to remain present.

法執俱意，於二乘等，雖名不染；於諸菩薩，亦名為染，障彼智故。由此亦名有覆無記。於二乘等，說名無覆，不障彼智故。是異熟生攝，從異熟識恒時生故，名異熟生，非異熟果，此名通故，如增上緣，餘不攝者，皆入此攝。

Manas together with dharma-grasping, although not called "defiled" with respect to śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, is nevertheless called "defiled" with respect to bodhisattvas, since it obstructs their wisdom — and for this reason it, too, is called "covered-yet-neutral." With respect to śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, it is called "uncovered," since it does not obstruct their wisdom in this way. It is included among what is "born of ripening," since it is constantly generated from the ripening consciousness — called "born of ripening" rather than "the fruit of ripening," since this name applies broadly, in the way that whatever is not otherwise included is classified under "dominant condition."

Reading note: This threefold map is one of the treatise's most useful diagnostic tools, because it separates two things ordinary language tends to blur together: believing in a fixed personal self, and the subtler, longer-lasting habit of treating any dharma whatsoever — including "emptiness" itself, including the teachings, including one's own realization — as some kind of fixed, independently real thing. The relationship between them is asymmetric and worth sitting with: self-grasping cannot occur without dharma-grasping underneath it (you can't mistake a post for a person unless you first take the post itself to be some real, definite thing), but dharma-grasping plainly can and does continue long after self-grasping is gone, all the way from the śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha arhat up through bodhisattvas below the eighth ground.

The eighth-ground marker matters because it's where the treatise says self-grasping stops being merely suppressed and becomes permanently, structurally impossible — a genuinely different achievement from an arhat's suppression, and the reason bodhisattvas at this stage are sometimes described in language that sounds almost identical to full buddhahood while dharma-grasping, in its cognitive-obstruction form, still lingers. The closing point about "covered-yet-neutral" being relative to the observer is a small but important piece of precision: the very same mental state can count as an obstruction from a bodhisattva's vantage (blocking the omniscience their path demands) while counting as harmless, "uncovered" neutrality from a śrāvaka's (who was never aiming at that in the first place) — moral and cognitive classification, here, is keyed to what a given path actually requires, not to some single universal yardstick.

Proving Manas Exists: The Scriptural Case 明唯識相 · 釋第二能變 · 諸門分別 · 教證

With manas fully described, the treatise now does for the seventh consciousness what it earlier did for the eighth: prove, independently, that it exists as something genuinely distinct from the other consciousnesses. The case opens, as before, with scripture.

Mind, Manas, Consciousness — Three Distinct Meanings 釋「心、意、識」三義

Chinese Source

云何應知此第七識，離眼等識，有別自體？聖教正理，為定量故。謂薄伽梵處處經中說：心、意、識三種別義。集起名心，思量名意，了別名識，是三別義。如是三義，雖通八識，而隨勝顯。第八名心，集諸法種，起諸法故；第七名意，緣藏識等，恒、審思量，為我等故；餘六名識，於六別境，麁動間斷，了別轉故。如《入楞伽》伽他中說：「藏識說名心，思量性名意，能了諸境相，是說名為識。」

Ru-Yi Translation

How should it be known that this seventh consciousness has a distinct essence of its own, apart from the eye-consciousness and the rest? Scripture and reason together serve as the standard of proof.

The Bhagavān states, again and again throughout the scriptures, that mind, manas, and consciousness carry three distinct meanings: what accumulates and gives rise is called "mind"; what deliberates is called "manas"; what discerns is called "consciousness." These are the three distinct meanings. Although all three meanings apply, in some measure, to all eight consciousnesses, each is displayed most prominently in a particular one: the eighth is called "mind," since it accumulates the seeds of every dharma and gives rise to every dharma; the seventh is called "manas," since it takes the storehouse consciousness as its object, constantly and closely deliberating, taking it to be a self; and the remaining six are called "consciousness," since, operating on their own six distinct objects, coarsely, in motion, and with interruption, they discern and cognize.

As the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra's verse says: "The storehouse consciousness is called mind; that which has deliberation as its nature is called manas; what is capable of discerning the characteristics of all objects is called consciousness."

又大乘經處處別說有第七識，故此別有。諸大乘經是至教量，前已廣說，故不重成。

Furthermore, the Mahāyāna sūtras, again and again, specifically speak of a seventh consciousness — hence it exists as something distinct. That the Mahāyāna sūtras are a valid standard of authoritative scripture has already been argued at length above, and so is not established again here.

《解脫經》中，亦別說有此第七識。如彼頌言：「染污意恒時，諸惑俱生滅，若解脫諸惑，非曾非當有。」彼經自釋此頌義言：有染污意，從無始來，與四煩惱，恒俱生滅。謂我見、我愛，及我慢、我癡。對治道生，斷煩惱已，此意從彼便得解脫。爾時，此意相應煩惱，非唯現無，亦無過、未，過去、未來，無自性故。如是等教，諸部皆有，恐厭廣文，故不繁述。

The Vimokṣa Sūtra, too, specifically speaks of this seventh consciousness. As its verse says: "Defiled manas constantly arises and ceases together with its afflictions; once liberated from these afflictions, it neither existed before nor will exist afterward."

That sūtra itself explains the meaning of this verse: there is a defiled manas which, from beginningless time, constantly arises and ceases together with four afflictions — self-view, self-love, together with self-conceit and self-delusion. Once the path that counteracts these arises and the afflictions are severed, this manas is thereby liberated from them. At that point, the afflictions associated with this manas are not only presently nonexistent, but have no past or future existence either, since past and future have no real nature of their own.

Teachings of this kind are found across every school's canon; fearing to weary the reader with excessive citation, they are not further elaborated here.

Reading note: This opens a new proof, structured exactly like the one that established the ālaya's existence back in fascicles 2 through 4 — scripture first, reason to follow. The three-way naming distinction (mind / manas / consciousness) is the same one introduced when the three transformations were first named in fascicle 2, now put to a sharper use: since Buddhist scripture consistently reserves the specific term "manas" for a constant, deliberating function distinct from the six ordinary consciousnesses' coarser, intermittent discernment, that vocabulary choice is itself evidence that a distinct seventh consciousness is what the tradition has been referring to all along, not merely a poetic name for something the sixth consciousness already does.

The Vimokṣa Sūtra's verse adds something the naming argument alone doesn't give: a description of what liberation from this manas actually looks like, and a metaphysical point worth remembering from earlier fascicles — that once the four afflictions bound to manas are severed, they don't merely stop in the present, they are said never to have had any true past or future existence at all, since (as established when refuting the Sarvāstivāda's "everything exists across all three times" view) only the present genuinely exists in the first place.

Proving Manas Exists: The Rational Case 明唯識相 · 釋第二能變 · 諸門分別 · 正理

The scriptural case complete, the treatise turns to reason — arguments for manas's existence that need no sūtra citation at all, beginning with the puzzle of a distinctive, subtle ignorance running beneath every ordinary being's mind.

The First Rational Argument: Exclusive Ignorance 正理（一）· 不共無明

Chinese Source

已引聖教；當顯正理。謂契經說：不共無明，微細恒行，覆蔽真實。若無此識，彼應非有。謂諸異生，於一切分，恒起迷理不共無明，覆真實義，障聖慧眼。如伽他說：「真義心當生，常能為障礙，俱行一切分，謂不共無明。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The scriptural case has now been made; the argument turns next to reason. The sūtras speak of an exclusive ignorance, subtle and constantly operating, concealing true reality — without this consciousness, that ignorance could not exist. Ordinary beings, at every stage without exception, constantly give rise to this ignorance, deluded about the true principle, concealing the meaning of true reality, and obstructing the eye of noble wisdom. As the verse says: "When the mind of true meaning is about to arise, it is constantly capable of obstructing it; occurring together across every stage, this is called exclusive ignorance."

是故契經說：異生類恒處長夜，無明所盲，惛醉纏心，曾無醒覺。若異生位，有暫不起此無明時，便違經義。俱異生位，迷理無明，有行、不行，不應理故。此依六識，皆不得成。應此間斷，彼恒染故。許有末那，便無此失。

Hence the sūtra says: ordinary beings dwell constantly in a long night, blinded by ignorance, their minds bound in a stupor, never once awakening. If there were some moment, at the stage of an ordinary being, when this ignorance failed to arise, this would contradict the sūtra's meaning — for it makes no sense that, throughout the entire stage of being an ordinary being, this deluding ignorance should sometimes operate and sometimes not. If this ignorance depended on the six consciousnesses alone, none of this could be established, since it would then have to be intermittent, whereas it is constantly defiling. Once one accepts that manas exists, this difficulty disappears.

染意恒與四惑相應，此俱無明，何名不共？有義：此俱我見、慢、愛，非根本煩惱，名不共何失？有義：彼說理、教相違。純隨煩惱中，不說此三故，此三，六、十煩惱攝故。處處皆說染污末那與四煩惱恒相應故。應說四中，無明是主，雖三俱起，亦名不共。從無始際，恒內惽迷，曾不省察，癡增上故。此俱見等，應名相應，若為主時，應名不共？如無明故，許亦無失。

Defiled manas is constantly associated with four afflictions — so why is the ignorance among them specifically called "exclusive," rather than the others?

One view holds: since the self-view, self-conceit, and self-love accompanying it are not themselves fundamental afflictions in the way ignorance uniquely is, what fault is there in calling only ignorance "exclusive"?

Another view holds: that account conflicts with both reason and scripture, since these three are never listed among the purely secondary afflictions, and are instead classified among the six, or the ten, primary afflictions. Passage after passage states that defiled manas is constantly associated with all four afflictions together. It should be said, instead: among these four, ignorance is the governing one — although the other three arise together with it, ignorance alone is called "exclusive," since, from beginningless time, it constantly, internally befuddles and deludes, never once examined or reflected upon, its delusive power being especially pronounced. Self-view and the rest, accompanying it, are properly called merely "associated" — but could they not, when acting as the governing factor themselves, equally be called "exclusive"? There would be no fault, either, in granting this, in the same way as with ignorance.

有義：此癡名不共者，如不共佛法，唯此識有故。若爾，餘識相應煩惱，此識中無，應名不共。依殊勝義，立不共名，非互所無，皆名不共。謂第七識相應無明，無始恒行，障真義智，如是勝用，餘識所無，唯此識有，故名不共。既爾，此俱三亦應名不共。無明是主，獨得此名。或許餘三，亦名不共；對餘「癡」故，且說無明。

Another view holds: this ignorance is called "exclusive" in the sense of "the exclusive qualities of a buddha" — meaning it belongs to this consciousness alone, and no other.

If that were so, however, then the afflictions associated with other consciousnesses, being absent from this consciousness, should equally be called "exclusive" to those other consciousnesses. Rather, the name "exclusive" is established with reference to a distinctive, pre-eminent quality, not simply because something happens to be absent elsewhere — it is not the case that anything absent from one thing but present in another automatically deserves the name "exclusive." That is: the ignorance associated with the seventh consciousness, operating constantly from beginningless time, obstructing the wisdom of true meaning — this distinctive function is found nowhere else, present only in this consciousness, and so it alone is called "exclusive."

Given this, the other three afflictions accompanying it should equally be called "exclusive" — yet ignorance, being the governing factor, alone receives this name. Or else, one may grant that the other three, too, are called "exclusive"; it is simply that, in contrast to other, ordinary forms of "delusion," the text speaks specifically of ignorance.

不共無明，總有二種：一、恒行不共，餘識所無；二、獨行不共，此識非有。故《瑜伽》說無明有二：若貪等俱者，名相應無明；非貪等俱者，名獨行無明。是主獨行，唯見所斷。如契經說：「諸聖有學，不共無明，已永斷故，不造新業。」非主獨行，亦修所斷。忿等皆通見、修所斷故。恒行不共，餘部所無；獨行不共，此、彼俱有。

"Exclusive ignorance," broadly speaking, is of two kinds: first, "constantly-operating exclusive ignorance," absent from every other consciousness; second, "independently-operating exclusive ignorance," which this consciousness does not itself have. Hence the Yogācārabhūmi speaks of two kinds of ignorance: ignorance accompanied by craving and the like is called "associated ignorance"; ignorance not accompanied by craving and the like is called "independently-operating ignorance." The governing, independently-operating kind is eliminated only on the path of seeing, as the sūtra says: "Noble ones still in training, once their exclusive ignorance has been permanently severed, create no new karma." The non-governing, independently-operating kind is eliminated on the path of cultivation as well, since wrath and the other such afflictions are eliminated on both the path of seeing and the path of cultivation. The constantly-operating exclusive ignorance is absent from every other school's account; the independently-operating exclusive ignorance is accepted both by this school and by others.

Reading note: "Exclusive" (不共) is doing careful technical work, and the treatise takes real care distinguishing two things that sound similar: an ignorance that operates constantly, underneath every moment of ordinary experience, found only in manas and nowhere else in the mental economy — versus an ignorance that simply operates alone, unaccompanied by craving or other afflictions, which any of the six ordinary consciousnesses can also have on occasion. The scriptural image of ordinary beings living in "a long night," never once waking, is the intuition the whole argument is built to explain: an ignorance that came and went with the six consciousnesses' own comings and goings (interrupted in sleep, absent in certain wholesome states) couldn't produce that description at all — only something running underneath those interruptions, exactly the way manas has been shown to run beneath them throughout this fascicle, fits what the scripture is actually describing.

The debate over whether self-view, conceit, and love deserve the name "exclusive" too is a good example of the treatise's insistence on precision even in what might look like a minor labeling dispute: the final position doesn't deny that all four afflictions are, in some sense, exclusive to manas — it simply reserves the name for whichever one is functioning as the governing factor being discussed, which happens to be ignorance in this context, without that being an exhaustive verdict on the other three.

The Second Argument: A Support of Its Own 正理（二）· 意識別有不共所依

Chinese Source

又契經說：眼、色為緣，生於眼識。廣說乃至意、法為緣，生於意識。若無此識，彼意非有。謂如五識，必有眼等增上不共俱有所依。意識既是六識中攝，理應許有如是所依。此識若無，彼依寧有？不可說色為彼所依，意非色故。意識應無隨念、計度二分別故。亦不可說五識無有俱有所依，彼與五根俱時而轉，如牙、影故。又識與根，既必同境，如心、心所，決定俱時。由此理趣，極成意識如眼等識必有不共，顯自名、處。等無間不攝，增上生所依，極成六識隨一攝故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, the sūtras say: the eye and form serve as conditions, giving rise to the eye-consciousness; and so on, up to: mind and dharma serve as conditions, giving rise to the mental consciousness. Without this consciousness, that "mind" mentioned as a condition could not exist.

Just as the five sense-consciousnesses each certainly have their own dominant, exclusive co-arising support — namely, the eye and the other faculties — so too the mental consciousness, being itself included among the six consciousnesses, should by the same reasoning be granted just such a support. If this consciousness did not exist, what could that support possibly be?

One cannot say that form itself is this support, since mind is not form. Nor could the mental consciousness, without such a support, ever have the two kinds of discrimination — recollective and inferential. Nor can one say that the five sense-consciousnesses, too, lack a genuine co-arising support of their own, merely happening to occur alongside their faculties by coincidence, the way a sprout happens to occur alongside its shadow — for consciousness and its faculty, being necessarily bound to the same object, must certainly occur together at the very same time, just like mind and its mental factors.

By this line of reasoning, it is well established that the mental consciousness, exactly like the eye-consciousness and the rest, must certainly have its own exclusive support — one which gives this consciousness its very name, "mental," and its own sense-field. This support cannot be the equal-immediate-antecedent condition, since it must instead be a dominant-condition support belonging clearly to one of the six established consciousnesses.

The Third Argument: What "Manas" Actually Means 正理（三）· 釋「思量名意」

Chinese Source

又契經說：思量名意。若無此識，彼應非有。謂若意識現在前時，等無間意已滅非有。過去、未來，理非有故。彼思量用，定不得成。既爾，如何說名為意？若謂假說，理亦不然。無正思量，假依何立？若謂現在，曾有思量，爾時名識，寧說為意？故知別有第七末那，恒、審思量，正名為意，已滅依此，假立「意」名。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, the sūtras say: what deliberates is called "manas." Without this consciousness, that manas could not exist.

Consider: whenever the mental consciousness is currently present, the equal-immediate-antecedent mind — its own preceding moment — has already ceased, and no longer exists; and since past and future have, by reason, no real existence at all, that deliberating function could certainly not be established this way. Given this, how could this be called "manas" at all?

Should one say this is merely a figure of speech — this, too, does not hold up: without any genuine deliberation actually occurring, on what basis could even a figurative usage be established? Should one say that, when it was present, deliberation did occur, and it is on that basis that it is now called "manas" — but at that earlier moment it was properly called "consciousness," not "manas" — so how could it be called "manas" now?

Hence it is known that there exists a distinct seventh consciousness, manas, which constantly and closely deliberates, and is properly named "manas" in its own right — it is only in a merely figurative sense that the already-ceased mental consciousness could ever be provisionally called "manas."

The Fourth Argument: Telling Two Attainments Apart 正理（四）· 無想、滅定染意

Chinese Source

又契經說：無想、滅定染意若無，彼應無別。謂彼二定，俱滅六識及彼心所，體數無異。若無染意，於二定中，一有一無，彼二何別？若謂加行、界、地、依等有差別者，理亦不然。彼差別因，由此有故。此若無者，彼因亦無。是故定應別有此意。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, the sūtras say: without defiled manas, there would be no distinguishing the attainment of no-thought from cessation-attainment. Consider: both of these attainments equally extinguish the six consciousnesses and their mental factors — in essence and in number, there is no difference between them. If there were no defiled manas, present in one of these attainments and absent in the other, how could these two be distinguished at all?

Should one say the difference lies instead in their preliminary practice, the realm and stage they belong to, their basis, and so on — this, too, does not hold up, since the very cause of these differences depends on the presence or absence of manas: if manas did not exist, none of these differentiating causes could exist either.

Hence there certainly must exist this distinct manas.

The Fifth Argument: A Whole Lifetime Without Six Consciousnesses 正理（五）· 無想有情

Chinese Source

又契經說：無想有情，一期生中，心、心所滅。若無此識，彼應無染。謂彼長時，無六轉識；若無此意，我執便無。非於餘處有具縛者，一期生中，都無我執。彼無我執，應如涅槃，便非聖賢同所訶厭。初、後有故，無如是失。中間長時無故，有過。去、來有故，無如是失。彼非現、常，無故，有過。所得無故，能得亦無。不相應法，前已遮破。藏識無故，熏習亦無。餘法受熏，已辯非理。故應別有染污末那，於無想天恒起我執，由斯賢聖同訶厭彼。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, the sūtras say: sentient beings in the heaven of no-thought have both mind and mental factors entirely extinguished for the entire span of that life. Without this consciousness, that being should have no defilement at all.

Consider: throughout that entire long span, the six evolving consciousnesses are absent — and if this manas did not exist either, self-grasping would then be entirely absent as well. Yet nowhere else does a being still fully bound by the fetters go an entire lifetime with absolutely no self-grasping whatsoever. If that being genuinely had no self-grasping, it would be no different from nirvāṇa itself — and the sages and worthies would then have no reason to unanimously condemn and disparage rebirth there.

Should one object that self-grasping is present at the very beginning and the very end of that life — this presents no such fault. But if it were genuinely absent throughout that long middle span, this fault would indeed arise. Should one instead say that self-grasping existed in the past and will exist again in the future — this presents no such fault only if manas exists continuously; but since the past and future are not themselves presently, constantly existent, this fault would arise on that account as well. Without anything actually "obtained," there could be no "obtaining" of it either — and the disjoined dharma that some propose to fill this gap has already been refuted above. Without the storehouse consciousness available to receive it, there could be no perfuming for this either — and it has already been argued at length that no other dharma can be genuinely perfumed.

Hence there certainly must exist a distinct, defiled manas, constantly giving rise to self-grasping even in the heaven of no-thought — and it is precisely because of this that the sages and worthies unanimously condemn and disparage rebirth there.

The Sixth Argument: Why Wholesome Acts Still Count as Contaminated 正理（六）· 異生善、染、無記心時恒帶我執

Chinese Source

又契經說：異生善、染、無記心時，恒帶我執，若無此識，彼不應有。謂異生類，三性心時，雖外起諸業，而內恒執我。由執我故，令六識中所起施等，不能亡相。故《瑜伽》說：染污末那，為識依止，彼未滅時，相了別縛，不得解脫。末那滅已，相縛解脫。言相縛者，謂於境相不能了達，如幻事等。由斯見分相分所拘，不得自在，故名相縛。依如是義，有伽他言：「如是染污意，是識之所依，此意未滅時，識縛終不脫。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, the sūtras say: ordinary beings, even in a wholesome or morally neutral state of mind, constantly carry self-grasping with them. Without this consciousness, that self-grasping could not exist.

Consider: an ordinary being, whatever the moral quality of the mind — wholesome, defiled, or neutral — although outwardly performing various actions, constantly, internally clings to a self. Because of this self-grasping, whatever acts of giving and the like arise within the six consciousnesses can never be free of the mark of self.

Hence the Yogācārabhūmi says: defiled manas serves as the other consciousness's support; as long as it has not been extinguished, that consciousness remains bound by characteristics, and cannot be liberated. Once manas is extinguished, this bondage by characteristics is released. "Bondage by characteristics" means being unable to fully penetrate the character of one's object, taking it as substantially real, like a magician's illusion. It is because the perceiving aspect is thus constrained by the perceived aspect that one is not free — hence it is called "bondage by characteristics." With this meaning in view, there is a verse that says: "This defiled manas is what the other consciousness depends on; as long as this manas has not been extinguished, the consciousness's bondage can never be released."

又善、無覆無記心時，若無我執，應非有漏。自相續中，六識煩惱與彼善等不俱起故。去、來緣縛，理非有故。非由他惑，成有漏故；勿由他解，成無漏故。又不可說：別有隨眠，是不相應，現相續起，由斯善等成有漏法。彼非實有，已極成故。亦不可說：從有漏種，生彼善等，故成有漏。彼種先無因可成有漏故。非由漏種，彼成有漏；勿學無漏心，亦成有漏故。

Furthermore: in wholesome or uncovered-neutral states of mind, if self-grasping were absent, these states should not be contaminated at all — since, within one's own mental continuity, the afflictions belonging to the six consciousnesses do not arise together at the same time as these wholesome states. Nor is it reasonable that some past-or-future bondage could be the cause, since past and future have no real existence. Nor is it the case that these states become contaminated through some other being's affliction, lest, by the same token, one should become uncontaminated through some other being's liberation.

Nor can one say that there is some separate latent tendency, itself a disjoined dharma, arising alongside the present mental continuity, which is what makes these wholesome states contaminated — such a thing has already been well established not to really exist. Nor can one say that these wholesome states arise from contaminated seeds, and are thereby rendered contaminated — for such seeds would have no cause to have become contaminated in the first place. Nor is it the case that these states become contaminated merely by having contaminated seeds — otherwise, even a trainee's uncontaminated mind would have to count as contaminated too.

雖由煩惱，引施等業，而不俱起，故非有漏正因；以有漏言，表漏俱故；又無記業，非煩惱引，彼復如何得成有漏？然諸有漏，由與自身現行煩惱俱生、俱滅，互相增益，方成有漏。由此熏成有漏法種，後時現起，有漏義成；異生既然，有學亦爾。無學有漏，雖非漏俱；而從先時有漏種起，故成有漏，於理無違。由有末那恒起我執，令善等法有漏義成。此意若無，彼定非有，故知別有此第七識。

Although the afflictions do draw forth acts of giving and the like, they do not arise at the very same time as these acts, and so cannot be the direct cause of their contamination, since the very term "contaminated" implies actually occurring together with contamination. And morally neutral karma, not itself drawn forth by any affliction at all — how, then, could that be rendered contaminated either?

Rather: whatever is contaminated becomes so because it arises and ceases together with, and mutually reinforces, the afflictions currently manifest within that very same being — it is this that establishes contamination. Through this, contaminated seeds are formed by perfuming, and when these later manifest, the sense of "contaminated" is thereby established. This is how it works for ordinary beings, and likewise for those still in training. Even an adept beyond further training's contaminated states, although not occurring together with any affliction, nevertheless arise from contaminated seeds formed earlier — and so are properly called contaminated, without any contradiction in reason.

It is because manas constantly gives rise to self-grasping that wholesome states and the rest come to count as contaminated at all. If this manas did not exist, that could certainly not be the case — hence it is known that this seventh consciousness exists as something distinct.

證有此識，理趣甚多，隨《攝大乘》，略述六種，諸有智者，應隨信學。然有經中說六識者，應知彼是隨轉理門，或隨所依六根說六，而識類別，實有八種。

The lines of reasoning proving this consciousness's existence are very many; following the Mahāyānasaṃgraha, I have given only a brief account of six kinds here — those with wisdom should accept and study them accordingly. As for the sūtras that speak of only six consciousnesses, it should be understood that these follow a provisional, conventional mode of exposition, or else speak of six with reference to the six sense-faculties they depend on — in reality, and by kind, there are eight distinct consciousnesses.

Reading note: These six reasons, taken together, are a compact tour of manas doing its characteristic work in ever more specific corners of ordinary experience. The support argument (二) simply extends a pattern already established for the five senses to the sixth. The naming argument (三) is a small but genuinely elegant piece of logic: "manas" means "that which deliberates," present tense, but the sixth consciousness's own preceding moment has, by the time it is named, already ceased to exist — so whatever is doing the deliberating that earns the name "manas" has to still be there, not merely have been there a moment ago.

The fourth and fifth arguments both use the "how do we tell two things apart" move: no-thought and cessation-attainment look identical from the outside (both extinguish the six consciousnesses completely) unless something else — manas, present in one, absent in the other — is doing the differentiating; and an entire lifetime with no six consciousnesses at all still has to count as bound and defiled, which nothing but a continuously operating self-grasping underneath can explain, given how uniformly the tradition condemns rebirth there as a wasted, deluded existence rather than praising it as accidental liberation.

The sixth argument reaches the furthest into ordinary life: even an act of genuine generosity, performed by an unenlightened being with a wholesome state of mind, still counts as "contaminated" in the technical sense — not because the six consciousnesses driving the act are themselves afflicted in that moment, but because manas is quietly clinging to a self underneath the whole performance, the way a shadow follows a body regardless of what the body is currently doing. This is a genuinely important point for practice: it explains why ordinary virtue, however sincere, is still described as bound up with self-view, and why the path requires more than simply cultivating more wholesome states — it requires uprooting the self-grasping running beneath all of them.

With the sixth argument, the treatise closes the entire proof of manas's existence — and, with it, the entire second-transformation discussion that has run from the middle of fascicle 4 through this point in fascicle 5. As the closing lines note, any scripture that speaks of only six consciousnesses is simply using a conventional shorthand; there are, in truth, eight. What follows next is the third and final transformation: the six consciousnesses that discern objects.
