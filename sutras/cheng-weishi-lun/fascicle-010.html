Fascicle 10 · 成唯識論卷第十 · Final Fascicle

Buddhahood, and the Treatise's Close

The tenth and final fascicle completes the account of the transformation of the basis begun in fascicle 9: the ten kinds of suchness realized ground by ground, the six kinds of transformation, and the four-part analysis of what is transformed, relinquished, and attained — including the four kinds of nirvana and the four wisdoms transformed from the eight consciousnesses. It then reaches the treatise's thirtieth and final verse, describing the ultimate stage itself, before closing with the classical account of a buddha's three bodies and lands, a final return to the Consciousness-Only thesis that opened the entire work, and the treatise's own colophon explaining its title and its base text, the Thirty Verses.

When Each Obstruction Is Finally Eliminated 明位次 · (四) 修習位 · 十一障斷位

Before moving forward, the treatise closes out the ten-obstacles discussion from fascicle 9 with a precise technical accounting of exactly when — gradually or all at once, before the grounds or upon reaching them — each obstruction's seeds are actually eliminated, distinguishing eleven obstructions in total (the ten ground-level obstructions, plus an eleventh eliminated only at buddhahood itself) within the two great obstructions already familiar from fascicle 9.

Gradual Suppression, Sudden Elimination 釋十一障斷位、漸頓

Chinese Source

此十一障，二障所攝。煩惱障中見所斷種，於極喜地見道初斷。彼障現起，地前已伏。修所斷種，金剛喻定現在前時，一切頓斷。彼障現起，地前漸伏，初地以上，能頓伏盡，令永不行，如阿羅漢。由故意力，前七地中雖暫現起，而不為失。八地以上，畢竟不行。所知障中見所斷種，於極喜地見道初斷。彼障現起，地前已伏。修所斷種，於十地中，漸次斷滅；金剛喻定現在前時，方永斷盡。彼障現起，地前漸伏；乃至十地，方永伏盡。

Ru-Yi Translation

These eleven obstructions are included within the two obstructions. Among the seeds of the afflictive obstruction, those eliminated on the path of seeing are first eliminated at the ground of utmost joy's path of seeing; their manifest activity has already been suppressed prior to reaching the grounds. Those eliminated on the path of cultivation are all eliminated at once when the vajra-like concentration manifests; their manifest activity is gradually suppressed prior to reaching the grounds, and, from the first ground onward, can be suppressed all at once and entirely, causing it to permanently cease operating, just as with an arhat. Through the power of deliberate intention, though it can still briefly manifest throughout the first seven grounds, this is not counted as a fault; from the eighth ground onward, it never operates at all.

Among the seeds of the cognitive obstruction, those eliminated on the path of seeing are likewise first eliminated at the ground of utmost joy's path of seeing. Those eliminated on the path of cultivation are gradually extinguished throughout the ten grounds, and are only permanently and entirely eliminated when the vajra-like concentration manifests; their manifest activity is gradually suppressed prior to reaching the grounds, and is only permanently and entirely suppressed by the tenth ground.

八地以上，六識俱者，不復現行，無漏觀心，及果相續，能違彼故；第七俱者，猶可現行，法空智果起位方伏。前五轉識，設未轉依，無漏伏故，障不現起。雖於修道十地位中，皆不斷滅煩惱障種，而彼麁重，亦漸斷滅，由斯故說二障麁重，一一皆有三位斷義。雖諸位中，皆斷麁重，而三位顯，是故偏說。

From the eighth ground onward, whatever is associated with the six consciousnesses no longer manifestly operates, since uncontaminated contemplative mind, and its resulting continuity, are capable of opposing it; whatever is associated with the seventh consciousness can still manifestly operate, and is only suppressed once the result of the wisdom of the emptiness of dharmas arises. As for the first five transforming consciousnesses, even if their basis has not yet been transformed, since they are suppressed by uncontaminated activity, this obstruction does not manifestly arise in them.

Although, throughout the path of cultivation's ten grounds, the afflictive obstruction's seeds are never eliminated, its coarseness and heaviness is nevertheless gradually extinguished as well — hence it is said that each of the two obstructions' coarseness and heaviness has a threefold sense of elimination. Although coarseness and heaviness is eliminated at every stage, it is at these three particular stages that this is most evident, and hence these are spoken of preferentially.

斷二障種，漸、頓云何？第七識俱煩惱障種，三乘將得，無學果時，一剎那中，三界頓斷。所知障種，將成佛時，一剎那中，一切頓斷，任運內起，無麁細故。餘六識俱煩惱障種，見所斷者，三乘見位真見道中，一切頓斷；修所斷者，隨其所應：一類二乘，三界九地，一一漸次，九品別斷；一類二乘，三界九地，合為一聚，九品別斷；菩薩要起金剛喻定，一剎那中，三界頓斷。

As for eliminating the two obstructions' seeds — is this gradual, or sudden? The afflictive obstruction's seeds associated with the seventh consciousness, when any of the three vehicles is about to attain the fruit of no further training, are eliminated throughout the three realms all at once, within a single instant. The cognitive obstruction's seeds, when one is about to attain buddhahood, are all eliminated at once, within a single instant, since they arise spontaneously and inwardly, without distinction of coarse or subtle.

As for the remaining afflictive obstruction's seeds associated with the six consciousnesses — those eliminated on the path of seeing are all eliminated at once, within the genuine path of seeing common to the three vehicles; those eliminated on the path of cultivation, as appropriate: for one type of practitioner among the two vehicles, elimination proceeds gradually, in nine distinct grades, throughout each of the three realms' nine stages individually; for another type, the three realms' nine stages are taken together as a single whole, with elimination likewise proceeding through nine grades; a bodhisattva, however, must give rise to the vajra-like concentration, eliminating them throughout the three realms all at once, within a single instant.

所知障種，初地初心，頓斷一切見所斷者。修所斷者，後於十地修道位中，漸次而斷，乃至正起金剛喻定，一剎那中方皆斷盡。通緣內外麁細境生，品類差別有眾多故。二乘根鈍，漸斷障時，必各別起無間、解脫；加行、勝進，或別或總。菩薩利根，漸斷障位，非要別起無間、解脫，剎那剎那能斷證故。加行等四，剎那剎那前後相望，皆容具有。

As for the cognitive obstruction's seeds — at the first ground's initial mind-moment, every one eliminated on the path of seeing is eliminated all at once. Those eliminated on the path of cultivation are gradually eliminated afterward, throughout the ten grounds, and are only entirely eliminated when the vajra-like concentration is actually given rise to, within a single instant — since they generally arise taking both inner and outer, coarse and subtle objects, and their distinct kinds are exceedingly numerous.

Since those of the two vehicles have dull faculties, when gradually eliminating an obstruction, they must each separately give rise to the path of uninterrupted progress and the path of liberation, with applied practice and superior advancement either separate or combined. Since a bodhisattva has sharp faculties, there is no need to separately give rise to these, since elimination and realization can occur moment by moment — the four paths, considered moment by moment, before and after, can all, in fact, be simultaneously present.

Reading note: This closing accounting is dense, but the underlying pattern is worth extracting: seeds tied to the path of seeing are always eliminated suddenly, in a single decisive moment, since seeing through a discriminated error is inherently all-or-nothing; seeds tied to the path of cultivation are, for the most part, eliminated gradually, since dismantling an innate, deeply worn habit takes sustained effort over time — except for the very last, most stubborn residue of each obstruction, which even gradual cultivation only finally clears in one last, sudden stroke (the vajra-like concentration for the afflictive obstruction's subtlest seeds, and again for the cognitive obstruction at buddhahood). The comparison between a bodhisattva's sharp faculties and the two vehicles' dull ones is a practical rather than a moral distinction: it is specifically about whether elimination and realization can happen in the very same moment (a bodhisattva) or require separately staged phases of effort and release (the two vehicles) — a difference in cognitive capacity for this particular kind of task, not a claim about virtue.

The Ten Kinds of Suchness 明位次 · (四) 修習位 · 十真如

Fascicle 9 named the ten grounds and the ten perfections each ground cultivates. The treatise now completes that triad with the ten kinds of suchness realized at each ground — ten provisional names for a single, undivided reality, distinguished only by which of its virtues becomes most prominent as realization deepens.

Ten Names for One Undivided Suchness 釋「十真如」

Chinese Source

十真如者：一、遍行真如：謂此真如二空所顯，無有一法而不在故。二、最勝真如：謂此真如具無邊德，於一切法最為勝故。三、勝流真如：謂此真如所流教法，於餘教法極為勝故。四、無攝受真如：謂此真如無所繫屬，非我執等所依取故。五、類無別真如：謂此真如類無差別，非如眼等類有異故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ten kinds of suchness are: first, universally-pervading suchness — this suchness, revealed by the two emptinesses, is present in every single dharma without exception. Second, supreme suchness — this suchness possesses boundless virtue, being supreme among all dharmas. Third, suchness flowing forth what is supreme — the teaching that flows forth from this suchness is exceedingly supreme among every other teaching. Fourth, suchness free of any object of attachment — this suchness has nothing to which it is bound, not being the basis grasped at by self-attachment and the like. Fifth, suchness without distinction of kind — this suchness has no distinction of kind, unlike the eye and the other faculties, which do differ in kind.

六、無染淨真如：謂此真如本性無染，亦不可說後方淨故。七、法無別真如：謂此真如雖多教法，種種安立而無異故。八、不增減真如：謂此真如離增減執，不隨淨染有增減故。即此亦名相土自在所依真如，謂若證得此真如已，現相、現土俱自在故。九、智自在所依真如：謂若證得此真如已，於無礙解得自在故。十、業自在等所依真如：謂若證得此真如已，普於一切神通作業、總持定門皆自在故。

Sixth, suchness free of defilement and purity — this suchness is, by its own nature, free of defilement, and it cannot be said to become pure only afterward. Seventh, suchness undifferentiated by dharma — this suchness, though set forth through many different teachings and various formulations, has no real difference among them. Eighth, suchness free of increase and decrease — this suchness is free of the attachment to increase and decrease, neither increasing nor decreasing according to purity or defilement; this same suchness is also called the suchness that is the basis for mastery over manifestation and lands, since, once realized, one attains mastery over manifesting both characteristics and lands. Ninth, the suchness that is the basis for mastery of wisdom — once realized, one attains mastery over the unimpeded understandings. Tenth, the suchness that is the basis for mastery of activity and the rest — once realized, one attains universal mastery over every supernormal activity, retention, and gate of concentration.

雖真如性實無差別，而隨勝德假立十種。雖初地中已達一切，而能證行，猶未圓滿，為令圓滿，後後建立。如是菩薩於十地中，勇猛修行十種勝行，斷十重障，證十真如，於二轉依便能證得。

Although suchness's own nature is, in genuine reality, without any real distinction, ten kinds are provisionally established with reference to its most excellent virtues. Although, already at the first ground, one has penetrated all of them, the practice capable of realizing them is not yet complete — in order to bring this to completion, each subsequent kind is established in turn. Thus, a bodhisattva, throughout the ten grounds, courageously cultivates the ten superior practices, eliminates the ten serious obstructions, and realizes the ten kinds of suchness, thereby becoming capable of realizing the two fruits of the transformation of the basis.

Reading note: The explicit disclaimer here — suchness "has, in genuine reality, no real distinction" even though ten kinds are named — is worth holding onto as the interpretive key to this entire list, and indeed to the whole ten-grounds/ten-perfections/ten-obstructions/ten-suchness framework spanning fascicles 9 and 10. None of these tenfold divisions describes ten separate things a bodhisattva acquires one by one; each describes the very same undivided reality, and the very same continuous cultivation, viewed through whichever facet becomes most vivid as one particular obstruction clears at one particular ground. The framework's real elegance is structural: exactly one ground, one perfection cultivated preferentially, one obstruction eliminated, and one facet of suchness revealed, ten times over, tracing a single continuous arc from the first breakthrough to the very edge of buddhahood.

Six Kinds of Transformation 明位次 · 六種轉依

Having traced the transformation of the basis across the five stages, the treatise now names six distinct kinds of transformation, sorting them by depth of realization and by whether the practitioner seeks liberation alone or the vast scope of a bodhisattva's path.

From Weakening the Old to the Vast Transformation 釋「六種轉依」

Chinese Source

轉依位別，略有六種。一、損力益能轉：謂初、二位，由習勝解及慚、愧故，損本識中染種勢力，益本識內淨種功能，雖未斷障種，實證轉依，而漸伏現行，亦名為轉。二、通達轉：謂通達位，由見道力，通達真如，斷分別生二障麁重，證得一分真實轉依。

Ru-Yi Translation

The stages of the transformation of the basis, broadly speaking, are of six kinds. First, the transformation of diminishing power and enhancing capacity: at the first two stages, through cultivating resolve, shame, and embarrassment, one diminishes the power of the defiled seeds within the root consciousness and enhances the function of the pure seeds within it — although the seeds of the obstructions are not yet eliminated, and this is not yet a genuine realization of the transformation of the basis, since manifest activity is gradually suppressed, this too is called a "transformation."

Second, the transformation of realization: at the stage of realization, through the power of the path of seeing, one penetrates suchness, eliminating the coarseness and heaviness of the discriminated forms of the two obstructions, and realizing one portion of the genuine transformation of the basis.

三、修習轉：謂修習位，由數修習十地行故，漸斷俱生二障麁重，漸次證得真實轉依。《攝大乘》中說通達轉在前六地。有無相觀，通達真、俗間雜現前，令真、非真現、不現故。說修習轉在後四地。純無相觀長時現前，勇猛修習，斷餘麁重，多令非真不顯現故。四、果圓滿轉：謂究竟位，由三大劫阿僧企耶，修集無邊難行勝行，金剛喻定現在前時，永斷本來一切麁重，頓證佛果圓滿轉依，窮未來際利樂無盡。

Third, the transformation of cultivation: at the stage of cultivation, through repeatedly cultivating the practices of the ten grounds, one gradually eliminates the coarseness and heaviness of the innate forms of the two obstructions, gradually realizing the genuine transformation of the basis. The Mahāyānasaṃgraha states that the transformation of realization occurs within the first six grounds, since signless contemplation there still manifests intermixed with ultimate and conventional truth alike; and that the transformation of cultivation occurs within the latter four grounds, since purely signless contemplation manifests over a long duration, eliminating the remaining coarseness and heaviness.

Fourth, the transformation of the fruit's complete fulfillment: at the ultimate stage, through three great incalculable eons of cultivating boundless, difficult, superior practices, when the vajra-like concentration manifests, one permanently eliminates every original coarseness and heaviness, realizing all at once the buddha-fruit's complete transformation of the basis, benefiting and bringing happiness to all beings without end throughout all future time.

五、下劣轉：謂二乘位，專求自利，厭苦、欣寂，唯能通達生空真如，斷煩惱種，證真擇滅，無勝堪能，名下劣轉。六、廣大轉：謂大乘位，為利他故，趣大菩提。生死涅槃俱無欣、厭，具能通達二空真如，雙斷所知、煩惱障種，頓證無上菩提、涅槃。有勝堪能，名廣大轉。此中意說廣大轉依，捨二麁重而證得故。

Fifth, the inferior transformation: at the stage of the two vehicles, exclusively seeking one's own benefit, averse to suffering and delighting in cessation, one is capable only of penetrating the suchness of the emptiness of self, eliminating the seeds of affliction and realizing genuine analytical cessation, without any superior capacity — hence this is called the inferior transformation.

Sixth, the vast transformation: at the stage of the Mahāyāna, for the sake of benefiting others, one advances toward great bodhi. Neither delighting in nor averse to either birth-and-death or nirvāṇa, one is fully capable of penetrating the suchness of both emptinesses, eliminating both the cognitive and afflictive obstructions' seeds together, realizing unsurpassed bodhi and nirvāṇa all at once — possessing superior capacity, hence this is called the vast transformation. What is meant here is specifically this vast transformation of the basis, realized through the relinquishment of both kinds of coarseness and heaviness.

Reading note: These six kinds of transformation are best read as two overlapping ways of sorting the same material: the first four (weakening-and-enhancing, realization, cultivation, and complete fulfillment) sort by depth, tracking the very same bodhisattva's progress through the five stages already explained; the last two (inferior and vast) sort by scope, contrasting the two vehicles' narrower goal against the Mahāyāna's. The fifth kind is a useful reminder that this treatise does not consider the two vehicles' liberation illusory or worthless — genuine analytical cessation is genuinely realized — only narrower in scope, since it stops at eliminating the afflictive obstruction and never touches the cognitive obstruction that a bodhisattva's "vast transformation" eliminates as well.

The Path That Transforms 明位次 · 轉依四義 · (一) 能轉道

The treatise now analyzes the transformation of the basis into four precise components, beginning with the path itself — distinguishing mere suppression, which any contaminated or uncontaminated practice can achieve, from genuine, permanent elimination, which only a very specific kind of wisdom can accomplish.

Suppression Versus Genuine Elimination 釋「能伏道」、「能斷道」

Chinese Source

轉依義別，略有四種：一、能轉道，此復有二：一、能伏道，謂伏二障隨眠勢力，令不引起二障現行，此通有漏、無漏二道，加行、根本、後得三智，隨其所應，漸頓伏彼。二、能斷道，謂能永斷二障隨眠。此道定非有漏加行，有漏曾習、相執所引，未泯相故；加行趣求所證、所引，未成辦故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The meaning of the transformation of the basis, distinguished in detail, is broadly of four kinds. First, the transforming path, which is itself of two kinds. First, the path of suppression: suppressing the power of the two obstructions' underlying tendency, preventing it from drawing forth their manifest activity — this extends to both the contaminated and uncontaminated paths, and to the three kinds of wisdom of applied practice, fundamental wisdom, and subsequently-attained wisdom, suppressing it gradually or suddenly, as appropriate.

Second, the path of elimination: capable of permanently eliminating the two obstructions' underlying tendency. This path is certainly never the contaminated form of applied practice, since a contaminated practice, having been previously cultivated and drawn forth by attachment to characteristics, has not yet dissolved that characteristic; and applied practice, still striving toward what is to be realized, has not yet accomplished it.

有義：根本無分別智，親證二空所顯真理，無境相故，能斷隨眠；後得不然，故非斷道。有義：後得無分別智，雖不親證二空真理，無力能斷迷理隨眠，而於安立、非安立相，明了現前，無倒證故，亦能永斷迷事隨眠。故《瑜伽》說：修道位中，有出世斷道；世、出世斷道。無純世間道，能永害隨眠。是曾習故，相執引故。由斯理趣，諸見所斷，及修所斷，迷理隨眠，唯有根本無分別智，親證理故，能正斷彼；餘修所斷迷事隨眠，根本、後得俱能正斷。

One view holds: fundamental non-discriminating wisdom, directly realizing the true principle revealed by the two emptinesses, being without any objective aspect, is capable of eliminating the underlying tendency; subsequently-attained wisdom is not this way, and so is not the path of elimination.

Another view holds: subsequently-attained non-discriminating wisdom, though it does not directly realize the two emptinesses' true principle, and hence has no power to eliminate the underlying tendency of delusion regarding principle, is nevertheless capable, through its clear and undistorted realization of the manifest aspects of the established and non-established truths, of also permanently eliminating the underlying tendency of delusion regarding particular matters. Hence the Yogācārabhūmi states: within the path of cultivation, there is a supramundane path of elimination, and a mundane-and-supramundane path of elimination — there is no purely mundane path capable of permanently destroying the underlying tendency.

By this line of reasoning, every underlying tendency of delusion regarding principle, whether eliminated on the path of seeing or the path of cultivation, is genuinely eliminated only by fundamental non-discriminating wisdom, since it alone directly realizes the principle; the remaining underlying tendency of delusion regarding particular matters, eliminated on the path of cultivation, is genuinely eliminated by both fundamental and subsequently-attained wisdom.

Reading note: The suppression/elimination distinction is one of the treatise's most practically important technical points, since it explains why even significant meditative attainment can fall short of genuine liberation: any contaminated practice, however refined, can only ever suppress an underlying tendency, temporarily preventing it from manifesting, because that very practice was itself cultivated through — and remains entangled with — the same attachment to characteristics it is trying to overcome. Only a wisdom that directly realizes emptiness itself, free of that entanglement, can permanently sever the tendency at its root, rather than merely holding it in check. This is the technical grounding for why this tradition insists that ethical cultivation and meditative concentration alone, however sustained, cannot substitute for direct realization — they can quiet affliction, but they cannot, by themselves, eliminate it for good.

What Is Depended Upon, and What Is Relinquished 明位次 · 轉依四義 · (二) 所轉依 · (三) 所轉捨

The second and third components of the transformation of the basis identify exactly what is being transformed, and exactly what falls away in the process — the root consciousness and suchness on one side, and the seeds of the two obstructions together with every remaining contaminated seed on the other.

Two Bases: The Consciousness That Holds Seeds, and Suchness Itself 釋「所轉依」

Chinese Source

二、所轉依，此復有二：一、持種依，謂本識。由此能持染、淨法種，與染、淨法俱為所依，聖道轉令捨染得淨；餘依他起性，雖亦是依，而不能持種，故此不說。二、迷悟依，謂真如。由此能作迷、悟根本，諸染、淨法依之得生，聖道轉令捨染得淨。餘雖亦作迷悟法依，而非根本，故此不說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, what is transformed and depended upon, which is itself of two kinds. First, the basis that holds seeds: the root consciousness. Since this is capable of holding both defiled and pure dharma-seeds, serving as the basis for both alike, the noble path transforms it, causing defilement to be relinquished and purity to be attained. Although the rest of the dependent nature, too, serves as a basis, it is not capable of holding seeds, and hence is not discussed here.

Second, the basis of delusion and awakening: suchness. Since this is capable of serving as the root of both delusion and awakening, every defiled and pure dharma arises in dependence upon it, and the noble path transforms it, causing defilement to be relinquished and purity to be attained. Although other things, too, serve as a basis for deluded and awakened dharmas, they are not the root basis, and hence are not discussed here.

Elimination and Discarding 釋「所轉捨」

Chinese Source

三、所轉捨，此復有二：一、所斷捨，謂二障種。真無間道現在前時，障、治相違，彼便斷滅，永不成就，說之為捨。彼種斷故，不復現行妄執我、法。所執我、法，不對妄情，亦說為捨。由此名捨遍計所執。二、所棄捨，謂餘有漏、劣無漏種。金剛喻定現在前時，引極圓、明純淨本識，非彼依故，皆永棄捨。彼種捨已，現有漏法及劣無漏，畢竟不生；既永不生，亦說為捨。由此名捨生死劣法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, what is transformed and relinquished, which is itself of two kinds. First, what is relinquished through elimination: the seeds of the two obstructions. When the genuine, uninterrupted path manifests, the obstruction and its counteragent being mutually opposed, the seeds are thereby eliminated, permanently ceasing to exist — this is what is meant by "relinquished." Since these seeds are eliminated, the false attachment to self and dharmas no longer manifestly operates; and the self and dharmas thus grasped at, no longer confronting deluded feeling, are likewise said to be "relinquished" — hence this is called relinquishing the discriminated, attached-to nature.

Second, what is discarded: the remaining contaminated seeds and inferior uncontaminated seeds. When the vajra-like concentration manifests, drawing forth the perfectly complete, luminous, and purely undefiled root consciousness, since these seeds are no longer its basis, they are all permanently discarded. Once relinquished, manifest contaminated dharmas and inferior uncontaminated dharmas will, in the end, never again arise — hence this is called relinquishing the inferior dharmas of birth-and-death.

有義：所餘有漏法種及劣無漏，金剛喻定現在前時，皆已棄捨，與二障種俱時捨故。有義：爾時猶未捨彼，與無間道不相違故。菩薩應無生死法故；此位應無所熏識故，住無間道應名佛故；後解脫道應無用故。由此應知，餘有漏等，解脫道起，方棄捨之。第八淨識，非彼依故。

One view holds: the remaining contaminated seeds and the inferior uncontaminated ones are all already discarded when the vajra-like concentration manifests, since they are relinquished at the same time as the two obstructions' seeds. Another view holds: at that moment, these are not yet relinquished, since they do not conflict with the uninterrupted path — otherwise, a bodhisattva would have to be without any dharma of birth-and-death at that stage, this stage would have to be without any consciousness capable of being perfumed, one abiding in the uninterrupted path would have to already be called a buddha, and the subsequent path of liberation would then serve no purpose. From this, it should be understood that the remaining contaminated dharmas are discarded only once the path of liberation arises, since the eighth, purified consciousness is no longer their basis.

Reading note: The two bases named here — the root consciousness as the holder of seeds, and suchness as the root of both delusion and awakening — are worth reading as two levels of the same claim already made throughout this treatise: nothing about liberation involves acquiring some entirely new, external possession, only the transformation of what was already, structurally, the very basis of ordinary experience. The debate over exactly when the last remaining contaminated seeds are discarded is a genuinely careful piece of reasoning about the mechanics of the very final moments before buddhahood — it insists that even at the vajra-like concentration, the single most advanced moment of the entire bodhisattva path, there must still be a coherent, functioning consciousness capable of being perfumed, or the sequence "uninterrupted path, then path of liberation" would collapse into incoherence.

What Is Attained: The Four Kinds of Nirvana 明位次 · 轉依四義 · (四) 所轉得 · 四種涅槃

The fourth and final component of the transformation of the basis is what is actually attained — and the treatise opens this with one of its most important teachings: nirvana is not a single thing, but four distinct kinds, of which only a buddha possesses all four.

Four Kinds of Nirvana, and Who Possesses Each 釋「四種涅槃」

Chinese Source

四、所轉得，此復有二：一、所顯得，謂大涅槃。此雖本來自性清淨，而由客障覆令不顯。真聖道生，斷彼障故，令其相顯名得涅槃。此依真如離障施設，故體即是清淨法界。涅槃義別，略有四種：一、本來自性清淨涅槃，謂一切法相真如理，雖有客染而本性淨，具無數量微妙功德，無生無滅，湛若虛空，一切有情平等共有，與一切法不一不異。離一切相、一切分別，尋思路絕，名言道斷，唯真聖者自內所證，其性本寂，故名涅槃。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, what is transformed and attained, which is itself of two kinds. First, what is revealed through attainment: great nirvāṇa. Although this is, by its own nature, pure from the very beginning, it is obscured by adventitious obstruction, preventing it from being revealed. When the genuine, noble path arises, eliminating that obstruction, its own character is revealed — this is what is called "attaining nirvāṇa." This is established with reference to suchness's freedom from obstruction, and so its very essence is precisely the pure dharma-realm.

The meaning of nirvāṇa, distinguished in detail, is broadly of four kinds. First, the nirvāṇa of original, intrinsic purity: the suchness-principle underlying the characteristics of all dharmas — although adventitious defilement is present, its own nature is originally pure, possessing innumerable, sublime virtues, without arising or ceasing, tranquil as empty space, equally shared by every sentient being, neither identical with nor different from every dharma. Free of every characteristic and every discrimination, the path of investigative reflection cut off and the way of verbal designation severed, this is realized only inwardly by genuine noble ones themselves; its nature is originally still, and hence it is called "nirvāṇa."

二、有餘依涅槃，謂即真如出煩惱障，雖有微苦所依未滅，而障永寂，故名涅槃。三、無餘依涅槃，謂即真如出生死苦，煩惱既盡，餘依亦滅，眾苦永寂，故名涅槃。四、無住處涅槃，謂即真如出所知障，大悲般若常所輔翼。由斯不住生死涅槃，利樂有情，窮未來際，用而常寂，故名涅槃。一切有情，皆有初一，二乘無學，容有前三，唯我世尊可言具四。

Second, nirvāṇa with residual basis: this is precisely suchness, freed from the afflictive obstruction — although the basis of slight suffering has not yet been extinguished, the obstruction is permanently stilled, and hence this, too, is called "nirvāṇa." Third, nirvāṇa without residual basis: this is precisely suchness, freed from the suffering of birth-and-death — affliction being exhausted, the residual basis, too, is extinguished, and every form of suffering is permanently stilled. Fourth, nirvāṇa of no fixed abode: this is precisely suchness, freed from the cognitive obstruction, constantly supported by great compassion and wisdom — on account of this, one abides in neither birth-and-death nor nirvāṇa, benefiting sentient beings throughout all future time, active and yet forever still.

Every sentient being possesses the first kind; those of the two vehicles who are beyond training may possess the first three; only our World-Honored One can be said to possess all four.

如何善逝有有餘依？雖無實依，而現似有；或苦依盡，說無餘依，非苦依在，說有餘依。是故世尊可言具四。若聲聞等有無餘依，如何有處說彼非有？有處說彼都無涅槃，豈有餘依彼亦非有？然聲聞等身、智在時，有所知障，苦依未盡，圓寂義隱，說無涅槃；非彼實無煩惱障盡所顯真理有餘涅槃，爾時未證無餘圓寂，故亦說彼無無餘依；非彼後時滅身、智已，無苦依盡無餘涅槃。

How can the Well-Gone One possess nirvāṇa with residual basis? Although there is no genuinely real basis remaining, one still manifests as though it were present; or else, once the basis of suffering is exhausted, this is called "without residual basis" — hence the World-Honored One can be said to possess all four.

If śrāvakas and the like possess nirvāṇa without residual basis, why do some passages say they do not? When a śrāvaka's body and wisdom are still present, since the cognitive obstruction remains and the basis of suffering is not yet exhausted, the meaning of complete cessation remains hidden, and hence it is said that they have no nirvāṇa — it is not that they genuinely lack the nirvāṇa with residual basis revealed once the afflictive obstruction is exhausted. Since they have not yet realized complete cessation of the basisless kind, it is also said that they lack nirvāṇa without residual basis — it is not that, once their body and wisdom are later extinguished, they still lack it.

或說二乘無涅槃者，依無住處，不依前三。又說彼無無餘依者，依不定性二乘而說，彼纔證得有餘涅槃，決定迴心求無上覺，由定願力留身久住，非如一類入無餘依。謂有二乘深樂圓寂，得生空觀親證真如，永滅感生煩惱障盡，顯依真理有餘涅槃，彼能感生煩惱盡故，後有異熟無由更生，現苦所依任運滅位。餘有為法既無所依，與彼苦依同時頓捨，顯依真理無餘涅槃，爾時雖無二乘身智，而由彼證可說彼有。此位唯有清淨真如，離相湛然寂滅安樂，依斯說彼與佛無差，但無菩提利樂他業，故復說彼與佛有異。

Alternatively, when it is said that the two vehicles lack nirvāṇa, this is spoken with reference to nirvāṇa of no fixed abode, not the first three kinds. And when it is said that they lack nirvāṇa without residual basis, this is spoken with reference to those of indeterminate spiritual lineage, who, having just realized nirvāṇa with residual basis, are certain to turn their mind toward seeking unsurpassed awakening, retaining their body through the power of settled vow, unlike the type who enters nirvāṇa without residual basis directly: such a practitioner, deeply delighting in complete cessation, attains the contemplation of the emptiness of self, permanently extinguishes the affliction capable of bringing about rebirth, and thereby reveals nirvāṇa with residual basis; since that affliction is exhausted, a subsequent ripened result cannot arise again, and the present basis of suffering spontaneously reaches extinction, together with every other conditioned dharma, revealing nirvāṇa without residual basis. At that moment, although the body and wisdom are absent, on account of this realization, it may still be said that they possess it.

At this stage, only pure suchness remains, free of characteristics, tranquil, still, and blissful — with reference to this, it is said that they are no different from a buddha; yet, lacking bodhi and the activity of benefiting others, it is also said that they differ from a buddha.

Why the Cognitive Obstruction's Elimination Still Counts as Nirvana 釋「無住處涅槃」與「擇滅」

Chinese Source

諸所知障，既不感生，如何斷彼，得無住處？彼能隱覆法空真如，令不發生大悲般若，窮未來際，利樂有情。故斷彼時顯法空理，此理即是無住涅槃，令於二邊，俱不住故。若所知障，亦障涅槃，如何斷彼，不得擇滅？擇滅離縛，彼非縛故。既爾，斷彼寧得涅槃？非諸涅槃皆擇滅攝。不爾，性淨應非涅槃。能縛有情住生死者，斷此說得擇滅無為。諸所知障不感生死，非如煩惱能縛有情，故斷彼時不得擇滅；然斷彼故，法空理顯，此理相寂，說為涅槃，非此涅槃擇滅為性。故四圓寂諸無為中，初、後即真如，中二擇滅攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the cognitive obstruction does not bring about rebirth, how can its elimination result in nirvāṇa of no fixed abode? Because it is capable of concealing the suchness of the emptiness of dharmas, preventing the arising of great compassion and wisdom that would benefit sentient beings throughout all future time. Hence, when it is eliminated, the principle of the emptiness of dharmas is revealed — and this very principle is precisely the nirvāṇa of no fixed abode, causing one to abide in neither of the two extremes.

If the cognitive obstruction, too, obstructs nirvāṇa, why does its elimination not result in analytical cessation? Because analytical cessation is freedom from bondage, and the cognitive obstruction is not itself a form of bondage. Not every nirvāṇa is included within analytical cessation — otherwise, intrinsic purity itself should not count as nirvāṇa at all. What binds sentient beings, keeping them in birth-and-death, is what, upon elimination, is said to yield analytical cessation; the cognitive obstruction does not bind sentient beings this way, and so its elimination does not result in analytical cessation — yet, because of its elimination, the principle of the emptiness of dharmas is revealed, and this principle's own stillness is what is called "nirvāṇa," though not with analytical cessation as its nature. Hence, among the unconditioned dharmas belonging to the four kinds of complete cessation, the first and last are precisely suchness, while the middle two are included within analytical cessation.

若唯斷縛得擇滅者，不動等二，四中誰攝？非擇滅攝，說暫離故；擇滅無為，唯究竟滅。有非擇滅，非永滅故。或無住處亦擇滅攝，由真擇力，滅障得故。擇滅有二：一、滅縛得；二、滅障得。故四圓寂諸無為中，初一即真如，後三皆擇滅，不動等二，暫伏滅者，非擇滅攝；究竟滅者，擇滅所攝。既所知障亦障涅槃，如何但說是菩提障？說煩惱障但障涅槃，豈彼不能為菩提障？應知聖教依勝用說，理實俱能通障二果。如是所說四涅槃中，唯後三種，名所顯得。

If only the elimination of bondage results in analytical cessation, then, among the four kinds, which does immovability and the like belong to? It is included within non-analytical cessation, since it is only a temporary departure — analytical cessation is exclusively ultimate cessation, whereas non-analytical cessation is not permanent. Alternatively, nirvāṇa of no fixed abode, too, may be included within analytical cessation, since it is attained through the power of genuine discernment eliminating an obstruction. Analytical cessation is of two kinds: attainment through the elimination of bondage, and attainment through the elimination of any other obstruction.

Since the cognitive obstruction, too, obstructs nirvāṇa, why is it said to be only an obstruction to bodhi? By the same token, the afflictive obstruction is said to obstruct only nirvāṇa — does this mean it cannot also obstruct bodhi? It should be understood that the sacred teachings speak with reference to what is most prominent — in genuine reality, both obstructions are capable of obstructing both fruits. Among the four kinds of nirvāṇa thus explained, only the latter three are called "what is revealed through attainment."

Reading note: The four kinds of nirvana are one of this treatise's most quietly radical teachings, since the first kind — original, intrinsic purity — is said to belong equally to every sentient being, right now, whether or not they have done anything at all to earn it. What differs between an ordinary being, an arhat, and a buddha is not whether this pure ground is present, but how much of it has been uncovered: an ordinary being possesses only the first kind, still buried under both obstructions; an arhat has cleared away the afflictive obstruction and so possesses the first three; and only a buddha has cleared away both obstructions completely, adding the fourth kind, "no fixed abode," which explains why a buddha is neither drawn back into ordinary birth-and-death nor content to simply rest in personal cessation.

Nirvana of no fixed abode is worth sitting with as the treatise's answer to a real tension in the tradition: if nirvana means release from suffering, how can an awakened being remain active in the world at all, indefinitely, without that itself becoming a kind of bondage? The answer given here is structural rather than effortful — compassion and wisdom together mean such a being is simply never pulled toward either extreme in the first place, neither toward the self-focused escape of ordinary nirvana nor back into the compulsive cycle of ordinary birth-and-death, "active and yet forever still" describing a state rather than a discipline being continuously maintained.

What Is Attained: The Four Wisdoms 明位次 · 轉依四義 · (四) 所轉得 · 所生得 · 四智

The second kind of attainment is bodhi itself — and the treatise identifies it precisely with the four wisdoms that arise once each of the eight consciousnesses is transformed. This is the treatise's own answer to what enlightenment actually consists of: not the disappearance of mind, but mind transformed into four distinct, functioning forms of wisdom.

The Four Wisdoms Defined 釋「四智相應心品」

Chinese Source

二、所生得，謂大菩提。此雖本來有能生種，而所知障礙故不生；由聖道力，斷彼障故，令從種起，名得菩提。起已相續，窮未來際。此即四智相應心品。云何四智相應心品？一、大圓鏡智相應心品：謂此心品，離諸分別，所緣、行相，微細難知，不忘不愚，一切境相，性相清淨，離諸雜染，純淨圓德，現種依持，能現、能生身、土智影，無間無斷，窮未來際，如大圓鏡，現眾色像。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, what is generated through attainment: great bodhi. Although this has, from the very beginning, possessed the seed capable of generating it, it fails to arise because it is obstructed by the cognitive obstruction; through the power of the noble path, that obstruction is eliminated, causing it to arise from its seed — this is what is called "attaining bodhi." Once arisen, it continues without interruption throughout all future time. This is precisely the mind associated with the four wisdoms.

What are the minds associated with the four wisdoms? First, the mind associated with the great, perfect mirror wisdom: this mind is free of every discrimination; its object and mode of activity are subtle and difficult to know; it neither forgets nor is deluded; every objective aspect is, in nature and characteristic, pure, free of every defilement, possessing purely pure and perfect virtue; it serves as the basis sustaining both manifest activity and seeds, capable of manifesting and generating the reflected images of body, land, and wisdom, without interruption, throughout all future time — like a great, perfect mirror, manifesting every form of image.

二、平等性智相應心品：謂此心品，觀一切法，自、他有情，悉皆平等。大慈悲等，恒共相應，隨諸有情所樂，示現受用身、土，影像差別。妙觀察智不共所依，無住涅槃之所建立，一味相續，窮未來際。三、妙觀察智相應心品：謂此心品，善觀諸法自相、共相，無礙而轉。攝觀無量總持定門，及所發生功德珍寶。於大眾會，能現無邊作用差別，皆得自在，雨大法雨，斷一切疑，令諸有情皆獲利樂。

Second, the mind associated with the wisdom of equality: this mind contemplates all dharmas, oneself, and other sentient beings, as entirely equal alike. It is constantly and jointly associated with great compassion and loving-kindness, and, according to what each sentient being delights in, manifests the distinct reflected images of the body and land of enjoyment. It is the unshared basis of the wisdom of wondrous contemplation, and is established by nirvāṇa of no fixed abode, continuing in a single unbroken flavor throughout all future time.

Third, the mind associated with the wisdom of wondrous contemplation: this mind skillfully contemplates the particular and shared characteristics of all dharmas, operating without obstruction. It encompasses the contemplation of boundless gateways of retention and concentration, and the treasured virtues thereby brought forth. Within the great assembly, it is capable of manifesting boundless distinct functions, all with complete mastery, raining down the great rain of the dharma, severing every doubt, and causing all sentient beings to obtain benefit and happiness.

四、成所作智相應心品：謂此心品，為欲利樂諸有情故，普於十方，示現種種變化三業，成本願力所應作事。如是四智相應心品，雖各定有二十二法，能變、所變種現俱生，而智用增，以智名顯。故此四品，總攝佛地一切有為功德皆盡。此轉有漏八、七、六、五識相應品，如次而得。智雖非識，而依識轉，識為主故，說轉識得。又有漏位智劣識強，無漏位中，智強識劣。為勸有情依智捨識，故說轉八識而得此四智。

Fourth, the mind associated with the wisdom of accomplishing what is to be done: this mind, in order to benefit and bring happiness to sentient beings, universally manifests, throughout the ten directions, the various transformed three karmic actions, accomplishing whatever task the power of one's original vow calls for.

Although each of these four minds certainly possesses its own twenty-two dharmas, since wisdom's function is here predominant, they are named and made evident through wisdom. These four minds, taken together, entirely encompass every conditioned virtue belonging to the stage of buddhahood, and are obtained by transforming the minds associated with the contaminated eighth, seventh, sixth, and five sense-consciousnesses, respectively. Although wisdom is not itself consciousness, it operates in dependence on consciousness, and, since consciousness is the more prominent factor, it is said that consciousness is transformed into wisdom. In order to encourage sentient beings to rely on wisdom and relinquish consciousness, it is said that the eight consciousnesses are transformed to obtain these four wisdoms.

When Each Wisdom First Arises 釋四智初起之位

Chinese Source

大圓鏡智相應心品，有義：菩薩金剛喻定現在前時，即初現起，異熟識種與極微細所知障種，俱時捨故；若圓鏡智爾時未起，便無能持淨種識故。有義：此品解脫道時，初成佛故，乃得初起；異熟識種，金剛喻定現在前時，猶未頓捨，與無間道不相違故。由斯此品，從初成佛，盡未來際，相續不斷，持無漏種令不失故。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the mind associated with the great, perfect mirror wisdom — one view holds: this first manifests precisely when a bodhisattva's vajra-like concentration manifests, since the ripening consciousness's seeds and the extremely subtle cognitive obstruction's seeds are relinquished at that very same moment; were the perfect mirror wisdom not yet to have arisen then, there would be no consciousness capable of holding the pure seeds at all. Another view holds: this first arises only at the moment of the path of liberation, since it is only then that buddhahood is first attained; the ripening consciousness's seeds are not yet entirely relinquished at the moment the vajra-like concentration manifests, since this does not conflict with the uninterrupted path. Hence this wisdom, from the very moment of first attaining buddhahood, continues without interruption throughout all future time, sustaining the uncontaminated seeds so that they are never lost.

平等性智相應心品，菩薩見道初現前位，違二執故，方得初起。後十地中，執未斷故，有漏等位，或有間斷；法雲地後，與淨第八，相依相續，盡未來際。妙觀察智相應心品，生空觀品，二乘見位，亦得初起。此後展轉至無學位，或至菩薩解行地終，或至上位，若非有漏，或無心時，皆容現起。法空觀品，菩薩見位，方得初起，此後展轉乃至上位，若非有漏，生空智果，或無心時，皆容現起。

The mind associated with the wisdom of equality first arises at the moment the bodhisattva's path of seeing first manifests, since it is opposed to the two attachments. Throughout the subsequent ten grounds, since attachment is not yet entirely eliminated, it may be interrupted during contaminated states; after the ground of the dharma cloud, it continues in mutual dependence with the purified eighth consciousness, throughout all future time.

The mind associated with the wisdom of wondrous contemplation, in its aspect of contemplating the emptiness of self, first arises already at the two vehicles' stage of seeing, and continues on, as appropriate, through to the stage of no further training, or to higher stages; its aspect of contemplating the emptiness of dharmas first arises only at a bodhisattva's stage of seeing, and continues on likewise to higher stages.

成所作智相應心品，有義：菩薩修道位中，後得引故，亦得初起。有義：成佛方得初起。以十地中，依異熟識所變眼等非無漏故，有漏不共，必俱同境，根發無漏識，理不相應故。此二於境，明昧異故。由斯此品，要得成佛，依無漏根，方容現起，而數間斷，作意起故。此四種性雖皆本有，而要熏發方得現行，因位漸增，佛果圓滿，不增不減，盡未來際，但從種生；不熏成種，勿前佛德勝後佛故。

As for the mind associated with the wisdom of accomplishing what is to be done — one view holds: this, too, can first arise already within a bodhisattva's stage of cultivation, drawn forth by subsequently-attained wisdom. Another view holds: this first arises only upon attaining buddhahood, since, throughout the ten grounds, the eye and other faculties, transformed and dependent on the ripening consciousness, are not themselves uncontaminated, and a contaminated faculty necessarily co-occurring with an uncontaminated consciousness would not accord with reason. Hence this wisdom, only upon attaining buddhahood, relying on uncontaminated faculties, is capable of manifestly arising.

Although these four capacities are all originally, innately present, they only manifest through habituation and elicitation — at the causal stage, they gradually increase, while, at the buddha-fruit, they are perfectly complete, neither increasing nor decreasing, arising, throughout all future time, solely from their seed; they are not perfumed into forming new seeds, lest an earlier buddha's virtue should thereby surpass a later buddha's.

What Each Wisdom Takes as Its Object 釋四智所緣境

Chinese Source

大圓鏡智相應心品，有義：但緣真如為境，是無分別，非後得智，行相、所緣不可知故。有義：此品緣一切法，《莊嚴論》說：大圓鏡智，於一切境，不愚迷故。《佛地經》說：如來智鏡，諸處、境、識，眾像現故。又此決定緣無漏種，及身、土等諸影像故，行、緣微細，說不可知，如阿賴耶，亦緣俗故。緣真如故，是無分別；緣餘境故，後得智攝。其體是一，隨用分二。了俗由證真，故說為後得。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the mind associated with the great, perfect mirror wisdom — one view holds: it takes only suchness as its object, and is purely non-discriminating, since its mode of activity and object cannot be known. Another view holds: this wisdom takes all dharmas as its object — since it certainly also takes the uncontaminated seeds, and the various reflected images of body and land, as its object, its mode of activity and object being subtle, it is said to be "unknowable," just as the ālayavijñāna, too, takes conventional objects as its object. Since it takes suchness as its object, it is non-discriminating; since it also takes other objects, it is included within subsequently-attained wisdom — its essence is a single one, distinguished into two according to its function.

平等性智相應心品，有義：但緣第八淨識，如染第七緣藏識故。有義：但緣真如為境，緣一切法平等性故。有義：遍緣真、俗為境，《佛地經》說：平等性智，證得十種平等性故。《莊嚴論》說：緣諸有情自他平等，隨他勝解，示現無邊佛影像故。由斯此品，通緣真、俗，二智所攝，於理無違。妙觀察智相應心品，緣一切法自相、共相，皆無障礙，二智所攝。

As for the mind associated with the wisdom of equality — one view holds: it takes only the eighth, purified consciousness as its object; another view holds: it takes only suchness as its object; another view holds: it universally takes both the ultimate and the conventional as its object — the Buddhabhūmi Sūtra states that the wisdom of equality realizes ten kinds of equality, and the Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra states that it takes the equality of oneself and other sentient beings as its object, manifesting boundless buddha-reflections accordingly. On this basis, this wisdom, taking both the ultimate and the conventional as its object, is included within both kinds of wisdom, in no way conflicting with reason.

The mind associated with the wisdom of wondrous contemplation takes the particular and shared characteristics of all dharmas as its object, entirely without obstruction, and is included within both kinds of wisdom.

成所作智相應心品，有義：但緣五種現境，《莊嚴論》說：如來五根，一一皆於五境轉故。有義：此品亦能遍緣三世諸法，不違正理，《佛地經》說：成所作智，起作三業諸變化事，決擇有情心行差別，領受去來現在等義。若不遍緣，無此能故。然此心品，隨意樂力，或緣一法、或二、或多。且說五根於五境轉，不言唯爾，故不相違。隨作意生，緣事相境，起化業故，後得智攝。

As for the mind associated with the wisdom of accomplishing what is to be done — one view holds: it takes only the five kinds of present sense-objects; another view holds: this wisdom, too, is capable of universally taking the dharmas of the three times as its object — the Buddhabhūmi Sūtra states that it initiates the various transformative deeds of the three karmic actions, discerns sentient beings' differing mental conduct, and comprehends past, future, and present alike; were it not universal in its object, it would lack this capacity. Yet this wisdom, according to the power of its own intention, may take one dharma, or two, or many, as its object — arising according to attention, taking the aspects of particular matters as its object, giving rise to transformative activity, it is included within subsequently-attained wisdom.

此四心品，雖皆遍能緣一切法，而用有異：謂鏡智品，現自受用身、淨土相，持無漏種；平等智品，現他受用身、淨土相；成事智品，能現變化身及土相；觀察智品，觀察自、他功能過失，雨大法雨，破諸疑網，利樂有情。如是等門，差別多種。此四心品，名所生得。此所生得，總名菩提；及前涅槃，名所轉得。雖轉依義總有四種，而今但取二所轉得。頌說「證得轉依」言故。此修習位說能證得，非已證得，因位攝故。

Although all four of these minds are, in fact, universally capable of taking every dharma as their object, their functions differ: the mirror-wisdom's mind manifests the aspect of the self-enjoyment body and pure land, sustaining the uncontaminated seeds; the equality-wisdom's mind manifests the aspect of the other-enjoyment body and pure land; the accomplishing-wisdom's mind manifests the transformation body and its land's aspect; the contemplation-wisdom's mind contemplates the capacities and faults of oneself and others, raining down the great rain of the dharma, breaking through every net of doubt, and benefiting sentient beings.

These four minds are called "what is generated through attainment." This attainment through generation is, in general, called "bodhi"; together with the nirvāṇa already discussed, this is called "what is transformed and attained." Although the meaning of the transformation of the basis has, in general, four aspects, only these two — what is transformed and attained — are taken up here, since the verse speaks of "realizing the transformation of the basis." This is spoken of, at the stage of cultivation, as being capable of realization, not as having already been realized, since it is still included within the causal stage.

Reading note: The four wisdoms are this treatise's precise, structural answer to a question every account of enlightenment eventually has to face: what does an awakened mind actually consist of? The answer is not "no mind" but "mind transformed, one consciousness at a time": the eighth consciousness, once purified of every defiled seed, becomes the great, perfect mirror wisdom — reflecting everything without distortion, forgetting nothing, adding nothing of its own; the seventh consciousness, once freed from its innate self-grasping, becomes the wisdom of equality — the same self-referential structure that once generated ego now generates universal compassion instead; the sixth consciousness, freed from the delusion woven into ordinary conceptual thought, becomes the wisdom of wondrous contemplation, capable of the very same discriminating, comparing, teaching activity ordinary thought performs, but now without distortion; and the five sense-consciousnesses become the wisdom of accomplishing what is to be done — direct, effective engagement with the world, no longer clouded.

This mapping is worth holding onto as one of Yogācāra's most distinctive moves: enlightenment is described not as escaping or extinguishing the ordinary cognitive apparatus, but as every part of that same apparatus being transformed into its own corresponding form of wisdom — the very consciousness responsible for self-grasping becomes the very basis of universal compassion, not by being replaced with something foreign, but by being purified into what it was always capable of becoming.

The extended debates over exactly when each wisdom first arises, and what precisely each one takes as its object, reflect a treatise unwilling to leave its central teaching vague even at the moment of its greatest claim — the same rigorous, multiple-competing-views method used throughout all ten fascicles is applied here to buddhahood itself, refusing to let reverence for the subject matter excuse imprecision about it.
