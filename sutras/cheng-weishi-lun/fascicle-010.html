Fascicle 10 · 成唯識論卷第十 · Final Fascicle

Buddhahood, and the Treatise's Close

The tenth and final fascicle completes the account of the transformation of the basis begun in fascicle 9: the ten kinds of suchness realized ground by ground, the six kinds of transformation, and the four-part analysis of what is transformed, relinquished, and attained — including the four kinds of nirvana and the four wisdoms transformed from the eight consciousnesses. It then reaches the treatise's thirtieth and final verse, describing the ultimate stage itself, before closing with the classical account of a buddha's three bodies and lands, a final return to the Consciousness-Only thesis that opened the entire work, and the treatise's own colophon explaining its title and its base text, the Thirty Verses.

When Each Obstruction Is Finally Eliminated 明位次 · (四) 修習位 · 十一障斷位

Before moving forward, the treatise closes out the ten-obstacles discussion from fascicle 9 with a precise technical accounting of exactly when — gradually or all at once, before the grounds or upon reaching them — each obstruction's seeds are actually eliminated, distinguishing eleven obstructions in total (the ten ground-level obstructions, plus an eleventh eliminated only at buddhahood itself) within the two great obstructions already familiar from fascicle 9.

Gradual Suppression, Sudden Elimination 釋十一障斷位、漸頓

Chinese Source

此十一障，二障所攝。煩惱障中見所斷種，於極喜地見道初斷。彼障現起，地前已伏。修所斷種，金剛喻定現在前時，一切頓斷。彼障現起，地前漸伏，初地以上，能頓伏盡，令永不行，如阿羅漢。由故意力，前七地中雖暫現起，而不為失。八地以上，畢竟不行。所知障中見所斷種，於極喜地見道初斷。彼障現起，地前已伏。修所斷種，於十地中，漸次斷滅；金剛喻定現在前時，方永斷盡。彼障現起，地前漸伏；乃至十地，方永伏盡。

Ru-Yi Translation

These eleven obstructions are included within the two obstructions. Among the seeds of the afflictive obstruction, those eliminated on the path of seeing are first eliminated at the ground of utmost joy's path of seeing; their manifest activity has already been suppressed prior to reaching the grounds. Those eliminated on the path of cultivation are all eliminated at once when the vajra-like concentration manifests; their manifest activity is gradually suppressed prior to reaching the grounds, and, from the first ground onward, can be suppressed all at once and entirely, causing it to permanently cease operating, just as with an arhat. Through the power of deliberate intention, though it can still briefly manifest throughout the first seven grounds, this is not counted as a fault; from the eighth ground onward, it never operates at all.

Among the seeds of the cognitive obstruction, those eliminated on the path of seeing are likewise first eliminated at the ground of utmost joy's path of seeing. Those eliminated on the path of cultivation are gradually extinguished throughout the ten grounds, and are only permanently and entirely eliminated when the vajra-like concentration manifests; their manifest activity is gradually suppressed prior to reaching the grounds, and is only permanently and entirely suppressed by the tenth ground.

八地以上，六識俱者，不復現行，無漏觀心，及果相續，能違彼故；第七俱者，猶可現行，法空智果起位方伏。前五轉識，設未轉依，無漏伏故，障不現起。雖於修道十地位中，皆不斷滅煩惱障種，而彼麁重，亦漸斷滅，由斯故說二障麁重，一一皆有三位斷義。雖諸位中，皆斷麁重，而三位顯，是故偏說。

From the eighth ground onward, whatever is associated with the six consciousnesses no longer manifestly operates, since uncontaminated contemplative mind, and its resulting continuity, are capable of opposing it; whatever is associated with the seventh consciousness can still manifestly operate, and is only suppressed once the result of the wisdom of the emptiness of dharmas arises. As for the first five transforming consciousnesses, even if their basis has not yet been transformed, since they are suppressed by uncontaminated activity, this obstruction does not manifestly arise in them.

Although, throughout the path of cultivation's ten grounds, the afflictive obstruction's seeds are never eliminated, its coarseness and heaviness is nevertheless gradually extinguished as well — hence it is said that each of the two obstructions' coarseness and heaviness has a threefold sense of elimination. Although coarseness and heaviness is eliminated at every stage, it is at these three particular stages that this is most evident, and hence these are spoken of preferentially.

斷二障種，漸、頓云何？第七識俱煩惱障種，三乘將得，無學果時，一剎那中，三界頓斷。所知障種，將成佛時，一剎那中，一切頓斷，任運內起，無麁細故。餘六識俱煩惱障種，見所斷者，三乘見位真見道中，一切頓斷；修所斷者，隨其所應：一類二乘，三界九地，一一漸次，九品別斷；一類二乘，三界九地，合為一聚，九品別斷；菩薩要起金剛喻定，一剎那中，三界頓斷。

As for eliminating the two obstructions' seeds — is this gradual, or sudden? The afflictive obstruction's seeds associated with the seventh consciousness, when any of the three vehicles is about to attain the fruit of no further training, are eliminated throughout the three realms all at once, within a single instant. The cognitive obstruction's seeds, when one is about to attain buddhahood, are all eliminated at once, within a single instant, since they arise spontaneously and inwardly, without distinction of coarse or subtle.

As for the remaining afflictive obstruction's seeds associated with the six consciousnesses — those eliminated on the path of seeing are all eliminated at once, within the genuine path of seeing common to the three vehicles; those eliminated on the path of cultivation, as appropriate: for one type of practitioner among the two vehicles, elimination proceeds gradually, in nine distinct grades, throughout each of the three realms' nine stages individually; for another type, the three realms' nine stages are taken together as a single whole, with elimination likewise proceeding through nine grades; a bodhisattva, however, must give rise to the vajra-like concentration, eliminating them throughout the three realms all at once, within a single instant.

所知障種，初地初心，頓斷一切見所斷者。修所斷者，後於十地修道位中，漸次而斷，乃至正起金剛喻定，一剎那中方皆斷盡。通緣內外麁細境生，品類差別有眾多故。二乘根鈍，漸斷障時，必各別起無間、解脫；加行、勝進，或別或總。菩薩利根，漸斷障位，非要別起無間、解脫，剎那剎那能斷證故。加行等四，剎那剎那前後相望，皆容具有。

As for the cognitive obstruction's seeds — at the first ground's initial mind-moment, every one eliminated on the path of seeing is eliminated all at once. Those eliminated on the path of cultivation are gradually eliminated afterward, throughout the ten grounds, and are only entirely eliminated when the vajra-like concentration is actually given rise to, within a single instant — since they generally arise taking both inner and outer, coarse and subtle objects, and their distinct kinds are exceedingly numerous.

Since those of the two vehicles have dull faculties, when gradually eliminating an obstruction, they must each separately give rise to the path of uninterrupted progress and the path of liberation, with applied practice and superior advancement either separate or combined. Since a bodhisattva has sharp faculties, there is no need to separately give rise to these, since elimination and realization can occur moment by moment — the four paths, considered moment by moment, before and after, can all, in fact, be simultaneously present.

Reading note: This closing accounting is dense, but the underlying pattern is worth extracting: seeds tied to the path of seeing are always eliminated suddenly, in a single decisive moment, since seeing through a discriminated error is inherently all-or-nothing; seeds tied to the path of cultivation are, for the most part, eliminated gradually, since dismantling an innate, deeply worn habit takes sustained effort over time — except for the very last, most stubborn residue of each obstruction, which even gradual cultivation only finally clears in one last, sudden stroke (the vajra-like concentration for the afflictive obstruction's subtlest seeds, and again for the cognitive obstruction at buddhahood). The comparison between a bodhisattva's sharp faculties and the two vehicles' dull ones is a practical rather than a moral distinction: it is specifically about whether elimination and realization can happen in the very same moment (a bodhisattva) or require separately staged phases of effort and release (the two vehicles) — a difference in cognitive capacity for this particular kind of task, not a claim about virtue.

The Ten Kinds of Suchness 明位次 · (四) 修習位 · 十真如

Fascicle 9 named the ten grounds and the ten perfections each ground cultivates. The treatise now completes that triad with the ten kinds of suchness realized at each ground — ten provisional names for a single, undivided reality, distinguished only by which of its virtues becomes most prominent as realization deepens.

Ten Names for One Undivided Suchness 釋「十真如」

Chinese Source

十真如者：一、遍行真如：謂此真如二空所顯，無有一法而不在故。二、最勝真如：謂此真如具無邊德，於一切法最為勝故。三、勝流真如：謂此真如所流教法，於餘教法極為勝故。四、無攝受真如：謂此真如無所繫屬，非我執等所依取故。五、類無別真如：謂此真如類無差別，非如眼等類有異故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ten kinds of suchness are: first, universally-pervading suchness — this suchness, revealed by the two emptinesses, is present in every single dharma without exception. Second, supreme suchness — this suchness possesses boundless virtue, being supreme among all dharmas. Third, suchness flowing forth what is supreme — the teaching that flows forth from this suchness is exceedingly supreme among every other teaching. Fourth, suchness free of any object of attachment — this suchness has nothing to which it is bound, not being the basis grasped at by self-attachment and the like. Fifth, suchness without distinction of kind — this suchness has no distinction of kind, unlike the eye and the other faculties, which do differ in kind.

六、無染淨真如：謂此真如本性無染，亦不可說後方淨故。七、法無別真如：謂此真如雖多教法，種種安立而無異故。八、不增減真如：謂此真如離增減執，不隨淨染有增減故。即此亦名相土自在所依真如，謂若證得此真如已，現相、現土俱自在故。九、智自在所依真如：謂若證得此真如已，於無礙解得自在故。十、業自在等所依真如：謂若證得此真如已，普於一切神通作業、總持定門皆自在故。

Sixth, suchness free of defilement and purity — this suchness is, by its own nature, free of defilement, and it cannot be said to become pure only afterward. Seventh, suchness undifferentiated by dharma — this suchness, though set forth through many different teachings and various formulations, has no real difference among them. Eighth, suchness free of increase and decrease — this suchness is free of the attachment to increase and decrease, neither increasing nor decreasing according to purity or defilement; this same suchness is also called the suchness that is the basis for mastery over manifestation and lands, since, once realized, one attains mastery over manifesting both characteristics and lands. Ninth, the suchness that is the basis for mastery of wisdom — once realized, one attains mastery over the unimpeded understandings. Tenth, the suchness that is the basis for mastery of activity and the rest — once realized, one attains universal mastery over every supernormal activity, retention, and gate of concentration.

雖真如性實無差別，而隨勝德假立十種。雖初地中已達一切，而能證行，猶未圓滿，為令圓滿，後後建立。如是菩薩於十地中，勇猛修行十種勝行，斷十重障，證十真如，於二轉依便能證得。

Although suchness's own nature is, in genuine reality, without any real distinction, ten kinds are provisionally established with reference to its most excellent virtues. Although, already at the first ground, one has penetrated all of them, the practice capable of realizing them is not yet complete — in order to bring this to completion, each subsequent kind is established in turn. Thus, a bodhisattva, throughout the ten grounds, courageously cultivates the ten superior practices, eliminates the ten serious obstructions, and realizes the ten kinds of suchness, thereby becoming capable of realizing the two fruits of the transformation of the basis.

Reading note: The explicit disclaimer here — suchness "has, in genuine reality, no real distinction" even though ten kinds are named — is worth holding onto as the interpretive key to this entire list, and indeed to the whole ten-grounds/ten-perfections/ten-obstructions/ten-suchness framework spanning fascicles 9 and 10. None of these tenfold divisions describes ten separate things a bodhisattva acquires one by one; each describes the very same undivided reality, and the very same continuous cultivation, viewed through whichever facet becomes most vivid as one particular obstruction clears at one particular ground. The framework's real elegance is structural: exactly one ground, one perfection cultivated preferentially, one obstruction eliminated, and one facet of suchness revealed, ten times over, tracing a single continuous arc from the first breakthrough to the very edge of buddhahood.

Six Kinds of Transformation 明位次 · 六種轉依

Having traced the transformation of the basis across the five stages, the treatise now names six distinct kinds of transformation, sorting them by depth of realization and by whether the practitioner seeks liberation alone or the vast scope of a bodhisattva's path.

From Weakening the Old to the Vast Transformation 釋「六種轉依」

Chinese Source

轉依位別，略有六種。一、損力益能轉：謂初、二位，由習勝解及慚、愧故，損本識中染種勢力，益本識內淨種功能，雖未斷障種，實證轉依，而漸伏現行，亦名為轉。二、通達轉：謂通達位，由見道力，通達真如，斷分別生二障麁重，證得一分真實轉依。

Ru-Yi Translation

The stages of the transformation of the basis, broadly speaking, are of six kinds. First, the transformation of diminishing power and enhancing capacity: at the first two stages, through cultivating resolve, shame, and embarrassment, one diminishes the power of the defiled seeds within the root consciousness and enhances the function of the pure seeds within it — although the seeds of the obstructions are not yet eliminated, and this is not yet a genuine realization of the transformation of the basis, since manifest activity is gradually suppressed, this too is called a "transformation."

Second, the transformation of realization: at the stage of realization, through the power of the path of seeing, one penetrates suchness, eliminating the coarseness and heaviness of the discriminated forms of the two obstructions, and realizing one portion of the genuine transformation of the basis.

三、修習轉：謂修習位，由數修習十地行故，漸斷俱生二障麁重，漸次證得真實轉依。《攝大乘》中說通達轉在前六地。有無相觀，通達真、俗間雜現前，令真、非真現、不現故。說修習轉在後四地。純無相觀長時現前，勇猛修習，斷餘麁重，多令非真不顯現故。四、果圓滿轉：謂究竟位，由三大劫阿僧企耶，修集無邊難行勝行，金剛喻定現在前時，永斷本來一切麁重，頓證佛果圓滿轉依，窮未來際利樂無盡。

Third, the transformation of cultivation: at the stage of cultivation, through repeatedly cultivating the practices of the ten grounds, one gradually eliminates the coarseness and heaviness of the innate forms of the two obstructions, gradually realizing the genuine transformation of the basis. The Mahāyānasaṃgraha states that the transformation of realization occurs within the first six grounds, since signless contemplation there still manifests intermixed with ultimate and conventional truth alike; and that the transformation of cultivation occurs within the latter four grounds, since purely signless contemplation manifests over a long duration, eliminating the remaining coarseness and heaviness.

Fourth, the transformation of the fruit's complete fulfillment: at the ultimate stage, through three great incalculable eons of cultivating boundless, difficult, superior practices, when the vajra-like concentration manifests, one permanently eliminates every original coarseness and heaviness, realizing all at once the buddha-fruit's complete transformation of the basis, benefiting and bringing happiness to all beings without end throughout all future time.

五、下劣轉：謂二乘位，專求自利，厭苦、欣寂，唯能通達生空真如，斷煩惱種，證真擇滅，無勝堪能，名下劣轉。六、廣大轉：謂大乘位，為利他故，趣大菩提。生死涅槃俱無欣、厭，具能通達二空真如，雙斷所知、煩惱障種，頓證無上菩提、涅槃。有勝堪能，名廣大轉。此中意說廣大轉依，捨二麁重而證得故。

Fifth, the inferior transformation: at the stage of the two vehicles, exclusively seeking one's own benefit, averse to suffering and delighting in cessation, one is capable only of penetrating the suchness of the emptiness of self, eliminating the seeds of affliction and realizing genuine analytical cessation, without any superior capacity — hence this is called the inferior transformation.

Sixth, the vast transformation: at the stage of the Mahāyāna, for the sake of benefiting others, one advances toward great bodhi. Neither delighting in nor averse to either birth-and-death or nirvāṇa, one is fully capable of penetrating the suchness of both emptinesses, eliminating both the cognitive and afflictive obstructions' seeds together, realizing unsurpassed bodhi and nirvāṇa all at once — possessing superior capacity, hence this is called the vast transformation. What is meant here is specifically this vast transformation of the basis, realized through the relinquishment of both kinds of coarseness and heaviness.

Reading note: These six kinds of transformation are best read as two overlapping ways of sorting the same material: the first four (weakening-and-enhancing, realization, cultivation, and complete fulfillment) sort by depth, tracking the very same bodhisattva's progress through the five stages already explained; the last two (inferior and vast) sort by scope, contrasting the two vehicles' narrower goal against the Mahāyāna's. The fifth kind is a useful reminder that this treatise does not consider the two vehicles' liberation illusory or worthless — genuine analytical cessation is genuinely realized — only narrower in scope, since it stops at eliminating the afflictive obstruction and never touches the cognitive obstruction that a bodhisattva's "vast transformation" eliminates as well.

The Path That Transforms 明位次 · 轉依四義 · (一) 能轉道

The treatise now analyzes the transformation of the basis into four precise components, beginning with the path itself — distinguishing mere suppression, which any contaminated or uncontaminated practice can achieve, from genuine, permanent elimination, which only a very specific kind of wisdom can accomplish.

Suppression Versus Genuine Elimination 釋「能伏道」、「能斷道」

Chinese Source

轉依義別，略有四種：一、能轉道，此復有二：一、能伏道，謂伏二障隨眠勢力，令不引起二障現行，此通有漏、無漏二道，加行、根本、後得三智，隨其所應，漸頓伏彼。二、能斷道，謂能永斷二障隨眠。此道定非有漏加行，有漏曾習、相執所引，未泯相故；加行趣求所證、所引，未成辦故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The meaning of the transformation of the basis, distinguished in detail, is broadly of four kinds. First, the transforming path, which is itself of two kinds. First, the path of suppression: suppressing the power of the two obstructions' underlying tendency, preventing it from drawing forth their manifest activity — this extends to both the contaminated and uncontaminated paths, and to the three kinds of wisdom of applied practice, fundamental wisdom, and subsequently-attained wisdom, suppressing it gradually or suddenly, as appropriate.

Second, the path of elimination: capable of permanently eliminating the two obstructions' underlying tendency. This path is certainly never the contaminated form of applied practice, since a contaminated practice, having been previously cultivated and drawn forth by attachment to characteristics, has not yet dissolved that characteristic; and applied practice, still striving toward what is to be realized, has not yet accomplished it.

有義：根本無分別智，親證二空所顯真理，無境相故，能斷隨眠；後得不然，故非斷道。有義：後得無分別智，雖不親證二空真理，無力能斷迷理隨眠，而於安立、非安立相，明了現前，無倒證故，亦能永斷迷事隨眠。故《瑜伽》說：修道位中，有出世斷道；世、出世斷道。無純世間道，能永害隨眠。是曾習故，相執引故。由斯理趣，諸見所斷，及修所斷，迷理隨眠，唯有根本無分別智，親證理故，能正斷彼；餘修所斷迷事隨眠，根本、後得俱能正斷。

One view holds: fundamental non-discriminating wisdom, directly realizing the true principle revealed by the two emptinesses, being without any objective aspect, is capable of eliminating the underlying tendency; subsequently-attained wisdom is not this way, and so is not the path of elimination.

Another view holds: subsequently-attained non-discriminating wisdom, though it does not directly realize the two emptinesses' true principle, and hence has no power to eliminate the underlying tendency of delusion regarding principle, is nevertheless capable, through its clear and undistorted realization of the manifest aspects of the established and non-established truths, of also permanently eliminating the underlying tendency of delusion regarding particular matters. Hence the Yogācārabhūmi states: within the path of cultivation, there is a supramundane path of elimination, and a mundane-and-supramundane path of elimination — there is no purely mundane path capable of permanently destroying the underlying tendency.

By this line of reasoning, every underlying tendency of delusion regarding principle, whether eliminated on the path of seeing or the path of cultivation, is genuinely eliminated only by fundamental non-discriminating wisdom, since it alone directly realizes the principle; the remaining underlying tendency of delusion regarding particular matters, eliminated on the path of cultivation, is genuinely eliminated by both fundamental and subsequently-attained wisdom.

Reading note: The suppression/elimination distinction is one of the treatise's most practically important technical points, since it explains why even significant meditative attainment can fall short of genuine liberation: any contaminated practice, however refined, can only ever suppress an underlying tendency, temporarily preventing it from manifesting, because that very practice was itself cultivated through — and remains entangled with — the same attachment to characteristics it is trying to overcome. Only a wisdom that directly realizes emptiness itself, free of that entanglement, can permanently sever the tendency at its root, rather than merely holding it in check. This is the technical grounding for why this tradition insists that ethical cultivation and meditative concentration alone, however sustained, cannot substitute for direct realization — they can quiet affliction, but they cannot, by themselves, eliminate it for good.
