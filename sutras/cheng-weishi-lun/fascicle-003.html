Fascicle 3 · 成唯識論卷第三

Contact, Attention, and What the Ālaya Feels

Fascicle 3 picks up exactly where the opening verse left off: the ālayavijñāna's constant association with the five universal mental factors, its feeling (always neutral), its moral quality, and the long argument — promised at the end of fascicle 2 — that this consciousness never truly ceases, even in the deepest states of meditation.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: translation in progress Source: Taishō T31, no. 1585 Covers: the five universal mental factors, and why the ālaya feels only neutral

The Five Universal Mental Factors 依教廣成分 · 常與觸作意受想思相應

The opening verse promised that the ālaya is "constantly joined with contact, attention, feeling, perception, and volition" — the five mental factors present in every single act of consciousness, of any kind, without exception. Fascicle 3 works through each one in turn, starting with contact and attention.

Contact 觸

Chinese Source

此識與幾心所相應？常與觸、作意、受、想、思相應。阿賴耶識無始時來乃至未轉，於一切位恒與此五心所相應，以是遍行心所攝故。

Ru-Yi Translation

With how many mental factors does this consciousness associate? It constantly associates with contact, attention, feeling, perception, and volition. From beginningless time, until it is finally transformed, the ālayavijñāna constantly associates with these five mental factors in every state, since they are the universally-operating mental factors.

「觸」謂三和分別變異，令心、心所觸境為性，受、想、思等所依為業。謂根、境、識更相隨順，故名三和。觸依彼生，令彼和合，故說為彼；三和合位皆有順生心所功能，說名變異；觸似彼起，故名分別。根變異力，引觸起時，勝彼識、境，故《集論》等但說分別根之變異。和合一切心及心所，令同觸境，是觸自性。既似順起心所功能，故以受等所依為業。

"Contact" means the distinctive change arising from the coming-together of three things — faculty, object, and consciousness — mutually according with one another; its nature is to bring mind and mental factors into contact with an object, and its function is to serve as the basis for feeling, perception, volition, and the rest. Contact arises in dependence on this coming-together, and in turn unites them — hence it is said to belong to them. At the very moment the three come together, a capacity favorable to generating further mental factors is already present — this is its "transformation." Contact arises resembling this coming-together — hence it is called "distinctive."

Since it is specifically the faculty's own transformation that draws contact into being, and this outweighs the consciousness and object in explanatory importance here, some treatises speak only of the faculty's transformation. Uniting all of mind and mental factors, bringing them together onto their object, is contact's essential nature — and since it already favors the arising of further mental factors, serving as their basis is its function.

《起盡經》說：受、想、行蘊一切皆以觸為緣故，由斯故說識、觸、受等因二、三、四和合而生。《瑜伽》但說與受、想、思為所依者，思於行蘊為主勝故，舉此攝餘。《集論》等說為受依者，以觸生受近而勝故，謂觸所取可意等相與受所取順益等相，極相隣近，引發勝故。然觸自性，是實非假，六六法中，心所性故，是食攝故，能為緣故，如受等性，非即三和。

The Sūtra on Arising and Ceasing says the aggregates of feeling, perception, and formations are all conditioned by contact — which is why consciousness, contact, feeling, and the rest are said to arise from the coming-together of two, three, or four factors, depending on the case. The Yogācārabhūmi names contact as the basis specifically for feeling, perception, and volition because volition is the foremost member of the formations-aggregate, and naming it stands in for the rest. Other texts name it specifically as the basis for feeling because contact's production of feeling is especially immediate and forceful: the pleasant-or-otherwise character contact grasps and the favorable-or-harmful character feeling grasps are extremely close to one another.

Contact's own nature, however, is real, not merely provisional: it counts as a genuine mental factor among the standard enumerations, it is included among the nutriments, and it can serve as a condition — just like feeling and the other genuine mental factors. It is not simply identical with the bare coming-together of the three that gives rise to it.

Attention 作意

Chinese Source

作意謂能警心為性，於所緣境，引心為業；謂此警覺，應起心種，引令趣境，故名作意。雖此亦能引起心所；心是主故，但說引心。有說：令心迴趣異境，或於一境，持心令住，故名作意。彼俱非理，應非遍行，不異定故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Attention" has, as its nature, the capacity to rouse the mind; its function is to draw the mind toward its object. This rousing draws out the very seed that ought to give rise to mind, directing it toward its object — hence the name. Though it also rouses the mental factors, since mind is the principal, it is simply said to "draw the mind."

Some say attention means turning the mind toward a different object, or holding the mind steady on a single object — but neither account is correct: were that its meaning, it could not operate universally across every state of mind, and it would be indistinguishable from concentration, which specifically means holding the mind steady on one object.

Reading note: "Universal" (遍行) mental factors are the five present in absolutely every moment of consciousness, of any kind, in any being — as opposed to factors like faith or anger that only arise sometimes. Contact is the most foundational: the bare coming-together of a sense faculty, an object, and consciousness, which makes any further mental activity (feeling something, perceiving it, willing something about it) possible at all — nothing can be felt or perceived or willed without contact having already occurred. Attention is what "aims" consciousness at an object in the first place, rousing it out of dormancy — carefully distinguished here from concentration, which holds attention fixed rather than merely directing it. Both factors, remember, are present even in the ālaya's most basic activity — the barest possible flicker of awareness already involves this much structure.

Feeling, Perception, Volition — and Why the Ālaya Only Ever Feels Neutral 依教廣成分 · 受、想、思 · 唯捨受

The remaining three universal factors complete the picture, and answer why the verse specifically says the ālaya is joined "only with neutral feeling" — never pleasure, never pain.

Feeling 受

Chinese Source

「受」謂領納順、違、俱非境相為性，起愛為業，能起合、離、非二欲故。有作是說：受有二種：一、境界受，謂領所緣；二、自性受，謂領俱觸。唯自性受是受自相。以境界受共餘相故。彼說非理，受定不緣俱生觸故。若似觸生，名領觸者，似因之果，應皆受性。又既受因，應名因受，何名自性？

Ru-Yi Translation

"Feeling" has, as its nature, the receiving of an object's character as favorable, unfavorable, or neither; its function is to give rise to craving, since it can produce the desire for union, for separation, or for neither.

Some say feeling is of two kinds: "object-feeling," which receives the object itself, and "own-nature feeling," which receives the accompanying contact — and that only the latter is feeling's true character, since object-feeling shares its character with other mental factors. This is unreasonable: feeling certainly does not take the contact arising alongside it as its object. If feeling is said to "receive contact" merely because it arises resembling and following from contact, then every effect resembling its cause would have to count as feeling. And if this "own-nature feeling" is really just contact's effect, it should be called "feeling caused by contact," not "feeling's own nature" at all.

若謂如王食諸國邑，受能領觸所生受體，名自性受。理亦不然，違自所執不自證故。若不捨自性，名自性受，應一切法皆是受自性。故彼所說，但誘嬰兒。然境界受，非共餘相，領順等相，定屬己者，名境界受，不共餘故。

If one answers that feeling "receives" contact-produced feeling the way a king "consumes" the cities of his realm — meaning governs, not literally eats — this too fails, since it contradicts their own claim that feeling does not cognize itself. And if "own-nature feeling" simply means "not departing from one's own nature," then every dharma whatsoever would qualify, since everything equally doesn't depart from its own nature. What is said here is fit only to placate small children. Object-feeling, in truth, shares its character with nothing else: receiving an object's favorable-or-otherwise character, definitely as one's own, is uniquely feeling's own affair.

Perception and Volition 想 · 思

「想」謂於境取像為性，施設種種名言為業，謂要安立境分齊相，方能隨起種種名言。「思」謂令心造作為性，於善品等役心為業，謂能取境正因等相，驅役自心令造善等。此五既是遍行所攝，故與藏識決定相應，其遍行相後當廣釋。此觸等五與異熟識行相雖異，而時、依同，所緣、事等，故名相應。

"Perception" has, as its nature, the grasping of an image with respect to an object; its function is to establish various verbal designations — only once an object's boundary-marks are established can such designations follow. "Volition" has, as its nature, causing the mind to act; its function is to drive the mind toward wholesome activity and the like — grasping an object's correct causal character and driving one's own mind to act accordingly.

Since these five are among the universally-operating factors, they certainly associate with the storehouse consciousness; their universal character will be explained more fully later. Though contact and the rest differ from the ripening consciousness in specific perceiving-activity, they share its time and basis, and match it in object and in number — hence they are said to "associate" with it.

Why Only Neutral Feeling 唯捨受

Chinese Source

此識行相極不明了，不能分別違、順境相，微細、一類、相續而轉，是故唯與捨受相應。又，此相應受唯是異熟，隨先引業轉；不待現緣，住善、惡業勢力轉故。唯是捨受；苦、樂二受是異熟生，非真異熟，待現緣故，非此相應。

Ru-Yi Translation

This consciousness's perceiving-activity is extremely unclear and indistinct; it cannot discriminate an object as favorable or unfavorable. Being subtle, uniform, and continuous, it therefore associates only with neutral feeling. Furthermore, the feeling associated with it is only ever true ripening, following the power of karma that projected it long ago — it does not depend on present conditions, operating purely through the force of past wholesome or unwholesome karma, and so is only ever neutral. Painful and pleasant feelings are "ripened arisings" rather than true ripening in this strict sense, since they depend on present conditions — and so are not associated with this consciousness.

又，由此識常無轉變，有情恒執為自內我；若與苦、樂二受相應，便有轉變，寧執為我，故此但與捨受相應。若爾，如何此識亦是惡業異熟？既許善業能招捨受，此亦應然。捨受不違苦、樂品故，如無記法，善、惡俱招。

Furthermore, it is precisely because this consciousness is constant and never felt to change that sentient beings constantly cling to it as an inner self; were it associated with painful or pleasant feeling, it would then be felt to change — and how, then, could it still be clung to as self? Hence it associates only with neutral feeling.

If so, how can this consciousness also be the ripened result of unwholesome karma? — Since wholesome karma is already granted to bring about neutral feeling, the same should hold for unwholesome karma too: neutral feeling conflicts with neither the painful nor the pleasant class, just as a morally neutral dharma in general can be brought about by either wholesome or unwholesome karma.

Reading note: The argument for "only neutral feeling" is doing double duty. Descriptively, the ālaya's activity is too subtle and uniform to register anything as pleasant or painful — that discrimination only happens at the level of the six active consciousnesses built on top of it. But there's a second, sharper point: if the ālaya itself felt pleasure or pain, it would be felt to change from moment to moment, and it is precisely its felt changelessness that makes it so convincing as "the self" to a mind clinging to it — the seventh consciousness's grip on the ālaya as "I" depends on the ālaya presenting as still, constant, unmoved. A restless, fluctuating ālaya would be a much less persuasive candidate for a fixed self, which is exactly the illusion this whole treatise has been dismantling since fascicle 1.
