Fascicle 9 · 成唯識論卷第九

The Five Stages of the Path

Fascicle 9 opens by completing the Three Natures doctrine with its mirror teaching, the three naturelessnesses, and by identifying Consciousness-Only's true nature outright with suchness itself — the treatise's closest approach yet to naming the ultimate directly. It then turns to entirely new terrain: the complete five-stage path by which a bodhisattva actually awakens into Consciousness-Only, from the initial stage of provisioning, through applied practice and the path of seeing, into the long stage of cultivation — the ten grounds, with their ten perfections and the ten obstacles eliminated one by one, ground by ground, all the way to the threshold of buddhahood itself.

The Three Naturelessnesses 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 釋「三無性」

Fascicle 8 closed with the Three Natures. The treatise now completes that teaching with its necessary mirror image: the three naturelessnesses, which explain how the sūtras can say that all dharmas lack their own nature without contradicting the reality of the three natures already established.

Hidden Intent: Why the Buddha Spoke of "No Nature" 頌釋「三無性」

Chinese Source

若有三性，如何世尊說一切法皆無自性？頌曰：即依此三性，立彼三無性，故佛密意說，一切法無性。論曰：即依此前所說三性，立彼後說三種無性，謂即相、生、勝義無性，故佛密意說一切法皆無自性，非性全無。

Ru-Yi Translation

If there are three natures, how can the World-Honored One say that all dharmas are entirely without their own nature? The verse states: "It is precisely with reference to these three natures that those three naturelessnesses, spoken of afterward, are established; hence the Buddha, with hidden intent, said that all dharmas are without their own nature."

The treatise states: it is precisely with reference to the three natures already discussed that the three naturelessnesses spoken of afterward are established — naturelessness of characteristic, naturelessness of arising, and naturelessness in the ultimate sense. Hence the Buddha, with hidden intent, said that all dharmas are entirely without their own nature — not that nature is altogether absent.

說「密意」言顯非了義，謂後二性雖體非無，而有愚夫於彼增益，妄執實有我、法自性，此即名為遍計所執。為除此執，故佛世尊於有及無，總說無性。

Speaking of "hidden intent" shows that this is not a statement of definitive meaning: although the latter two natures are not, in their essence, nonexistent, the ignorant nonetheless superimpose upon them, falsely grasping at the real existence of a self and dharma-nature — this is precisely what is called the discriminated, attached-to nature. It is in order to remove this attachment that the World-Honored One, with respect to both what exists and what does not, spoke generally of "naturelessness."

云何依此而立彼三？謂依此初遍計所執立相無性，由此體相畢竟非有，如空華故。依次依他立生無性，此如幻事託眾緣生，無如妄執自然性故，假說無性，非性全無。依後圓成實立勝義無性，謂即勝義由遠離前遍計所執我、法性，故假說無性，非性全無。如太虛空雖遍眾色，而是眾色無性所顯。雖依他起非勝義故，亦得說為勝義無性，而濫第二，故此不說。

How, with reference to these three natures, are those three naturelessnesses established? With reference to the first, the discriminated, attached-to nature, naturelessness of characteristic is established, since its own essence and characteristic are utterly nonexistent, like a flower in the sky. With reference to the next, the dependent nature, naturelessness of arising is established — this is like a magical illusion, arising in dependence upon manifold conditions, lacking any spontaneous, independent nature such as false attachment imagines; hence naturelessness is provisionally spoken of, not that nature is altogether absent. With reference to the last, the perfectly accomplished nature, naturelessness in the ultimate sense is established — meaning that the ultimate, precisely through being permanently free of the former, discriminated, attached-to nature of self and dharmas, is provisionally called "naturelessness" — just as vast empty space, though it pervades every form, is itself what these forms' own naturelessness reveals.

Although the dependent nature, too, not being ultimate, could also be spoken of as naturelessness in the ultimate sense, this would be too easily confused with the second kind, and so is not stated this way here.

Reading note: The three naturelessnesses are not a retraction of the Three Natures but a careful gloss on scriptural language, and the "hidden intent" framing does real work: when the sūtras flatly deny that dharmas have any nature, this treatise insists that statement was never meant as the final word, but as a corrective aimed specifically at the false attachment ordinary beings layer onto the dependent and perfectly accomplished natures. Each naturelessness targets its corresponding nature in a logically distinct way: the imagined nature simply has no characteristic to speak of at all (like a flower blooming in empty sky); the dependent nature lacks the kind of independent, self-caused arising naive attachment imagines, though it certainly arises through real conditions; and the perfectly accomplished nature is called "natureless" only in the sense of being free from the imagined nature's false overlay, not in the sense of being nothing. The empty-space analogy captures this precisely — space itself is not a thing among the objects it contains, yet it is definitely not nothing, and it is exactly what those objects' own lack of independent existence reveals when properly seen.

Consciousness-Only's True Nature Is Suchness Itself 明唯識性 · 釋「唯識實性」

The treatise now states, as directly as it ever will, what Consciousness-Only ultimately is: not a doctrine about consciousness set apart from ultimate reality, but suchness itself, viewed through the lens this treatise has built up across eight fascicles.

Ultimate Truth, Fourfold, and Suchness by Many Names 頌釋「唯識實性」· 四種勝義

Chinese Source

頌曰：此諸法勝義，亦即是真如，常如其性故，即唯識實性。論曰：此性即是諸法勝義，是一切法勝義諦故。然勝義諦略有四種：一、世間勝義，謂蘊、處、界等；二、道理勝義，謂苦等四諦；三、證得勝義，謂二空真如；四、勝義勝義，謂一真法界。此中勝義依最後說，是最勝道所行義故，為簡前三，故作是說。

Ru-Yi Translation

The verse states: "This, the dharmas' ultimate, is likewise suchness itself, since it is permanently as its own nature is — this is Consciousness-Only's genuine nature."

The treatise states: this nature is precisely the ultimate reality of all dharmas, since it is the ultimate truth of every dharma. Ultimate truth, broadly speaking, is of four kinds: worldly ultimate truth, meaning the aggregates, sense-fields, elements, and the rest; ultimate truth as principle, meaning suffering and the other three noble truths; ultimate truth as realization, meaning the suchness of the two emptinesses; and ultimate truth as the ultimate itself, meaning the single, genuine dharma-realm. Here, "ultimate" is spoken of with reference to this last sense, since it is the object of the supreme path — this is stated in order to distinguish it from the first three.

此諸法勝義亦即是真如，真謂真實，顯非虛妄，如謂如常，表無變易，謂此真實於一切位常如其性，故曰真如，即是湛然不虛妄義。「亦」言顯此復有多名，謂名法界及實際等，如餘論中隨義廣釋。

This dharmas' ultimate is likewise suchness itself: "true" means genuinely real, showing it is not false; "suchness" means constant sameness, indicating it undergoes no change — meaning that this genuine reality, in every state, is permanently as its own nature is, and hence is called "suchness," precisely the meaning of being tranquil and never false. The word "likewise" shows that this, moreover, has many further names — called the dharma-realm, the true limit, and the rest — as is extensively explained, according to their meaning, in other treatises.

此性即是唯識實性，謂唯識性略有二種：一者虛妄，謂遍計所執；二者真實，謂圓成實性，為簡虛妄說「實性」言。復有二性：一者世俗，謂依他起；二者勝義，謂圓成實，為簡世俗故說「實性」。

This nature is precisely Consciousness-Only's genuine nature: Consciousness-Only's own nature, broadly speaking, is of two kinds — one false, namely the discriminated, attached-to nature; the other genuine, namely the perfectly accomplished nature — and it is in order to distinguish it from the false that the phrase "genuine nature" is used. There are, further, two other natures: one conventional, namely the dependent nature; the other ultimate, namely the perfectly accomplished nature — and it is in order to distinguish it from the conventional that the phrase "genuine nature" is used.

三頌總顯諸契經中說無性言非極了義，諸有智者不應依之總撥諸法都無自性。

These three verses, together, show that the sūtras' statements of "naturelessness" are not statements of ultimate, definitive meaning — no wise person should, on this basis, categorically deny that all dharmas have any nature whatsoever.

Reading note: This is one of the most important identifications the treatise makes explicit: Consciousness-Only, at its deepest, ultimate level, simply is suchness — the same reality named, in other traditions and other sūtras, the dharma-realm, the true limit, or emptiness itself. The fourfold breakdown of "ultimate truth" is worth remembering, since later fascicles will use "ultimate" loosely at times and precisely at others — worldly, principled, and realized ultimates are all still relative to some more limited perspective, and only the fourth, the single genuine dharma-realm itself, is truly unconditioned.

The closing line functions as the treatise's own reading instructions for the entire Three Natures and three-naturelessnesses teaching just concluded: scriptural statements of universal "no-nature" are correctives against a specific error, not literal metaphysical nihilism, and readers who take them as flat denials that anything exists have misunderstood the hidden intent behind them — precisely the same caution the treatise gave, in fascicle 7, against misreading "Consciousness-Only" as claiming only a single consciousness exists.

The Path in Five Stages 明位次 · 總標五位

With Consciousness-Only's theoretical structure now complete, the treatise turns to genuinely new ground for the remainder of the treatise: the actual path by which a bodhisattva comes to realize everything explained so far, structured into five sequential stages.

Two Lineages, Five Stages 釋「二種姓」、「五位」

Chinese Source

如是所成唯識相、性，誰於幾位？如何悟入？謂具大乘二種姓者，略於五位漸次悟入。云何大乘二種種姓？一、本性住種姓，謂無始來依附本識，法爾所得無漏法因。二、習所成種姓，謂聞法界等流法已，聞所成等熏習所成。要具大乘此二種姓，方能漸次悟入唯識。

Ru-Yi Translation

Given that Consciousness-Only's characteristics and nature have thus been established — who, and at how many stages, comes to awaken into this? And how does this awakening occur? Those who possess the Mahāyāna's two kinds of spiritual lineage, broadly speaking, awaken into it gradually, through five stages.

What are the Mahāyāna's two kinds of spiritual lineage? First, the innately abiding lineage, meaning the uncontaminated dharma-cause, naturally possessed since beginningless time, depending upon and adhering to the root consciousness. Second, the lineage accomplished through cultivation, meaning what is brought about through habituation from hearing the dharma-realm's outflow teaching and the wisdom born from such hearing, and the like. Only one who possesses both of these Mahāyāna lineages is capable of gradually awakening into Consciousness-Only.

何謂悟入唯識五位？一、資糧位，謂修大乘順解脫分；二、加行位，謂修大乘順決擇分；三、通達位，謂諸菩薩所住見道；四、修習位，謂諸菩薩所住修道；五、究竟位，謂住無上正等菩提。

What are the five stages of awakening into Consciousness-Only? First, the stage of provisioning, meaning the cultivation of the Mahāyāna's portion conducive to liberation. Second, the stage of applied practice, meaning the cultivation of the Mahāyāna's portion conducive to decisive discernment. Third, the stage of realization, meaning the path of seeing upon which bodhisattvas abide. Fourth, the stage of cultivation, meaning the path of cultivation upon which bodhisattvas abide. Fifth, the ultimate stage, meaning abiding in unsurpassed, perfect awakening.

云何漸次悟入唯識？謂諸菩薩於識相、性，資糧位中能深信解；在加行位能漸伏除所取、能取，引發真見；在通達位如實通達；修習位中如所見理，數數修習伏斷餘障；至究竟位出障圓明，能盡未來化有情類，復令悟入唯識相、性。

How does one gradually awaken into Consciousness-Only? At the stage of provisioning, bodhisattvas are able to deeply believe in and understand the characteristics and nature of consciousness; at the stage of applied practice, they are able to gradually suppress and remove the grasped object and grasping subject, drawing forth genuine insight; at the stage of realization, they genuinely penetrate it; at the stage of cultivation, in accordance with the principle already seen, they cultivate again and again, suppressing and eliminating the remaining obstructions; upon reaching the ultimate stage, free of obstruction and perfectly luminous, they are able, throughout all future time, to transform sentient beings, causing them, too, to awaken into Consciousness-Only's characteristics and nature.

Reading note: This overview is the treatise's own table of contents for everything remaining in the work: fascicle 9 will work through the first four of these five stages in detail — provisioning, applied practice, the path of seeing, and the long path of cultivation across the ten grounds — with only the fifth and final stage, buddhahood itself, left for fascicle 10. The two spiritual lineages are worth noting as a genuinely dual requirement: an innate capacity present since beginningless time is not, on its own, sufficient — it must be actively cultivated through hearing and reflecting on the teaching, and a bodhisattva needs both working together to progress. The five-stage structure itself maps cleanly onto ordinary language for spiritual development: gathering the raw resources (provisioning), disciplined preparatory practice (applied practice), a first genuine breakthrough (the path of seeing), sustained integration of that breakthrough (cultivation), and finally its complete fulfillment (the ultimate stage) — a shape recognizable across many contemplative traditions, not unique to this one.

The Stage of Provisioning 明位次 · (一) 資糧位

The first stage covers the long period from the initial arousal of the resolve for bodhi up until one is ready to begin the applied practice that precedes genuine insight. A bodhisattva at this stage cultivates deep confidence in Consciousness-Only, but has not yet gained any real power over the underlying tendencies that drive ordinary delusion — which the treatise here defines with unusual precision.

What This Stage Covers 頌釋「資糧位」

Chinese Source

初資糧位，其相云何？頌曰：乃至未起識，求住唯識性，於二取隨眠，猶未能伏滅。論曰：從發深固大菩提心，乃至未起順決擇識，求住唯識真勝義性，齊此皆是資糧位攝，為趣無上正等菩提，修集種種勝資糧故。為有情故，勤求解脫，由此亦名順解脫分。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the character of the first stage, the stage of provisioning? The verse states: "Until one has not yet given rise to the consciousness that seeks to abide in Consciousness-Only's nature, one is still not yet able to suppress and eliminate the underlying tendency of the two graspings."

The treatise states: from the arousal of the profound and firm resolve for great bodhi, up until the consciousness conducive to decisive discernment has not yet arisen, seeking to abide in Consciousness-Only's true, ultimate nature — everything up to this point is included within the stage of provisioning, since one cultivates and accumulates every kind of superior provision in order to advance toward unsurpassed, perfect awakening. For the sake of sentient beings, one diligently seeks liberation, and it is for this reason that this stage is also called the portion conducive to liberation.

此位菩薩依因、善友、作意、資糧四勝力故，於唯識義雖深信解，而未能了能、所取空，多住外門修菩薩行，故於二取所引隨眠，猶未有能伏滅功力，令彼不起二取現行。此「二取」言顯二取取，執取「能取」、「所取」性故。二取習氣名彼隨眠，隨逐有情，眠伏藏識，或隨增過，故名隨眠。即是所知、煩惱障種。

A bodhisattva at this stage, relying on the four superior powers of cause, good friends, attention, and provisions, though deeply believing in and understanding the meaning of Consciousness-Only, is not yet able to realize the emptiness of the grasping subject and grasped object, and, for the most part, still practices the bodhisattva path through outward-facing methods — hence, with respect to the underlying tendency drawn forth by the two graspings, this bodhisattva does not yet possess the capacity to suppress and eliminate it, so as to prevent the two graspings' manifest activity from arising.

This phrase "the two graspings" indicates the grasping-at-two-graspings, since it grasps at and clings to the nature of "the grasping subject" and "the grasped object." The habituation of the two graspings is called their "underlying tendency," since it constantly follows sentient beings, lying dormant within the storehouse consciousness, or since it follows along and increases their fault. This is precisely the seed of the cognitive obstruction and the afflictive obstruction.

Two Obstructions Defined 釋「煩惱障」、「所知障」

Chinese Source

煩惱障者，謂執遍計所執實我，薩迦耶見而為上首，百二十八根本煩惱，及彼等流諸隨煩惱。此皆擾惱有情身、心，能障涅槃，名煩惱障。所知障者，謂執遍計所執實法，薩迦耶見而為上首，見、疑、無明、愛、恚、慢等。覆所知境無顛倒性，能障菩提，名所知障。

Ru-Yi Translation

The afflictive obstruction: the attachment to a real self, discriminated and attached to, with the view of a perishable, composite self foremost among the 128 fundamental afflictions, together with the various secondary afflictions that flow from them. All of these disturb and afflict sentient beings' body and mind, and are capable of obstructing nirvāṇa — hence they are called the afflictive obstruction.

The cognitive obstruction: the attachment to real dharmas, discriminated and attached to, with the view of a perishable, composite self foremost among them, together with view, doubt, ignorance, craving, hatred, conceit, and the rest. These obscure the object of knowledge's undistorted nature, and are capable of obstructing bodhi — hence they are called the cognitive obstruction.

此所知障，決定不與異熟識俱，彼微劣故，不與無明、慧相應故，法空智品與俱起故。七轉識內，隨其所應或少或多，如煩惱說。眼等五識無分別故，法見、疑等定不相應，餘由意力皆容引起。此障但與不善、無記二心相應，論說無明唯通不善、無記性故，癡、無癡等不相應故。煩惱障中此障必有，彼定用此為所依故，體雖無異而用有別。故二隨眠，隨聖道用有勝、有劣，斷惑前後。

This cognitive obstruction certainly never occurs together with the ripening consciousness, since that consciousness is too feeble, since it is never associated with ignorance or discernment, and since the wisdom of the emptiness of dharmas can arise together with it. Among the seven transforming consciousnesses, it is present to a greater or lesser extent, as appropriate, as already explained for the afflictive obstruction. Since the eye-consciousness and the other four sense-consciousnesses lack discrimination, they are certainly never associated with the view regarding dharmas, doubt, and the rest; the remaining consciousnesses, through the power of the mental faculty, can all give rise to it.

This obstruction is associated only with unwholesome and neutral minds, since the treatise states that ignorance extends only to the unwholesome and the neutral, and since delusion and non-delusion are never mutually associated. Within the afflictive obstruction, this obstruction is certainly present, since that obstruction certainly relies upon it as its basis — though their essence is not different, their function differs. Hence these two underlying tendencies, in relation to the noble path's function, are respectively superior or inferior, and are eliminated at different points.

此於無覆無記性中，是異熟生，非餘三種。彼威儀等勢用薄弱，非覆所知，障菩提故。此名無覆，望二乘說，若望菩薩亦是有覆。若所知障有見、疑等，如何此種，契經說為無明住地？無明增故，總名無明，非無見等。如煩惱種立見一處、欲、色、有愛四住地名，豈彼更無慢、無明等。

Among the unobscured-neutral states, this obstruction belongs to the karmically-resultant kind, not to the other three, since deportment and the like are too weak in function to obscure the object of knowledge or obstruct bodhi. It is called "unobscured" with reference to the two vehicles; with reference to bodhisattvas, however, it is, in fact, obscuring.

If the cognitive obstruction includes view, doubt, and the rest, how can the sūtras call its seed "the ground of ignorance"? Because ignorance predominates, it is called, in general, "ignorance" — not that view and the rest are absent. Just as the afflictive obstruction's seed is named the four grounds of the single ground of view, and of desire, form, and craving-for-existence — does this mean these lack conceit, ignorance, and the rest?

如是二障分別起者，見所斷攝；任運起者，修所斷攝。二乘但能斷煩惱障，菩薩俱斷。永斷二種，唯聖道能；伏二現行，通有漏道。菩薩住此資糧位中，二麁現行雖有伏者，而於細者及二隨眠，止、觀力微，未能伏滅。

Of these two obstructions, whatever is discriminated is eliminated on the path of seeing; whatever is spontaneously arisen is eliminated on the path of cultivation. Those of the two vehicles are capable of eliminating only the afflictive obstruction; bodhisattvas eliminate both. Permanently eliminating both kinds is possible only through the noble path; suppressing both kinds' manifest activity is possible through a mundane path as well. A bodhisattva abiding at this stage of provisioning, though capable of suppressing the coarse manifest activity of both obstructions, still has, with respect to their subtler forms and their two underlying tendencies, only feeble calming and insight, and is not yet capable of suppressing or eliminating them.

Reading note: These definitions of the afflictive obstruction (煩惱障) and cognitive obstruction (所知障) are among the most important in the entire treatise, since nearly everything in the remaining fascicles' account of the path is organized around eliminating them. The distinction is worth holding precisely: the afflictive obstruction is what blocks liberation from suffering — the 128 fundamental afflictions built on self-attachment — and is what the two vehicles (śrāvakas and solitary buddhas) are capable of eliminating on their own. The cognitive obstruction is subtler still: it is what blocks bodhi, full awakening, specifically because it obscures the object of knowledge itself, and only a bodhisattva's path addresses it. This is why the treatise insists a bodhisattva must eliminate both, while an arhat's liberation, genuine as it is, leaves the cognitive obstruction untouched — the difference between being free of suffering and being fully, perfectly awake to reality.

The tempering point about "ground of ignorance" is a useful piece of exegetical method that recurs throughout the treatise: a scriptural name that highlights one element (ignorance, here) does not mean the other elements grouped under it are absent — it simply names whichever is most prominent, the same reasoning already used elsewhere for why some mental factors get named individually while structurally similar ones do not.

Merit and Wisdom; Three Moments of Discouragement 釋「福智」二種勝行 · 三退屈

Chinese Source

此位未證唯識真如，依勝解力修諸勝行，應知亦是解行地攝。所修勝行，其相云何？略有二種：謂福及智，諸勝行中慧為性者皆名為智，餘名為福。且依六種波羅蜜多，通相皆二，別相前五說為福德，第六智慧；或復前三唯福德攝，後一唯智；餘通二種。復有二種：謂利自、他所修勝行，隨意樂力一切皆通自他利行，依別相說，六到彼岸、菩提分等，自利行攝；四種攝事、四無量等，一切皆是利他行攝。如是等行差別無邊，皆是此中所修勝行。

Ru-Yi Translation

At this stage, one has not yet realized Consciousness-Only's suchness, but relies on the power of resolve to cultivate various superior practices — it should be understood that this, too, is included within the stage of resolve-and-practice. What is the character of the superior practices cultivated at this stage? Broadly speaking, there are two: merit and wisdom. Among the various superior practices, whatever has discernment as its nature is called wisdom; the rest are called merit. With reference to the six perfections, their general character involves both, but, in their specific character, the first five are said to be merit, and the sixth, wisdom; or else the first three are included exclusively within merit, and the last exclusively within wisdom, with the remaining two extending to both.

There are, further, two other kinds: superior practices cultivated for benefiting oneself and benefiting others. According to the power of one's own intention, every such practice can, in fact, extend to benefiting both; but, according to their specific character, the six perfections and the factors of awakening and the like are included within benefiting oneself, while the four means of attraction and the four immeasurables and the like are all included within benefiting others. The distinctions among such practices are boundless, and all are included among the superior practices cultivated at this stage.

此位二障雖未伏除，修勝行時有三退屈，而能三事練磨其心，於所證、修勇猛不退：一、聞無上正等菩提廣大深遠，心便退屈，引他已證大菩提者，練磨自心勇猛不退。二、聞施等波羅蜜多甚難可修，心便退屈，省己意樂能修施等，練磨自心勇猛不退。三、聞諸佛圓滿轉依極難可證，心便退屈，引他麁善況己妙因，練磨自心勇猛不退。由斯三事練磨其心，堅固熾然，修諸勝行。

Although the two obstructions are not yet suppressed or removed at this stage, three kinds of discouragement can arise while cultivating these superior practices — yet three corresponding methods of tempering the mind allow one to remain courageous and unwavering in what is to be realized and cultivated.

First, on hearing that unsurpassed, perfect awakening is vast, profound, and far-reaching, the mind may grow discouraged — one tempers one's own mind into courageous steadfastness by calling to mind others who have already realized great bodhi.

Second, on hearing that generosity and the other perfections are extremely difficult to cultivate, the mind may grow discouraged — one tempers one's own mind into courageous steadfastness by reflecting on one's own capacity and willingness to cultivate generosity and the rest.

Third, on hearing that the buddhas' perfect transformation of their basis is extremely difficult to realize, the mind may grow discouraged — one tempers one's own mind into courageous steadfastness by comparing the coarse virtues achievable by others against one's own more subtle causes.

Through these three methods of tempering the mind, one becomes firm and ardent, cultivating the various superior practices.

Reading note: The three moments of discouragement are a strikingly practical, almost pastoral passage for such a technical treatise — a direct acknowledgment that the bodhisattva path is genuinely daunting, and a set of concrete countermeasures rather than mere exhortation to "try harder." Each remedy works the same way: it does not deny the difficulty, but relativizes it, either by pointing to others who have already succeeded, by turning attention inward to one's own actual willingness rather than an abstract standard, or by reframing one's present, ordinary efforts as already a kind of "subtle cause" worth taking seriously next to more visible but coarser achievements. This is recognizable as basic psychological wisdom about sustaining long-term effort toward a goal that cannot be reached quickly, wrapped in the treatise's own technical vocabulary.

The Stage of Applied Practice 明位次 · (二) 加行位

The second stage is a short but intense final approach to the path of seeing: four increasingly refined levels of practice, each dissolving a further layer of the subject-object duality, right up to the very threshold of genuine insight — a threshold this stage, by definition, can never quite cross.

Warmth, Summit, Forbearance, Supreme Worldly Dharma 釋「煖、頂、忍、世第一法」

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, what is the character of the stage of applied practice? The verse states: "Positing something as presently manifest, calling it Consciousness-Only's own nature — since this still involves something attained, one is not yet genuinely abiding in Consciousness-Only." A further citation states: "A bodhisattva, in the state of concentration, contemplates a reflection as being mind alone; once meaning and appearance have both been dissolved away, one examines closely, seeing only one's own conception. The treatise states: a bodhisattva, having first, across the initial incalculable eon, thoroughly prepared the provisions of merit and wisdom, and having already fully completed the portion conducive to liberation, in order to enter the path of seeing and abide in Consciousness-Only's nature, further cultivates applied practice, suppressing and removing the two graspings — namely, warmth, summit, forbearance, and supreme worldly dharma. These four are collectively called the portion conducive to decisive discernment, since they accord with and advance toward the genuine portion of decisive discernment. Since they are close to the path of seeing, the name "applied practice" is given — not that the preceding stage of provisioning involves no sense of applied effort at all.

Warmth and the other three dharmas are established with reference to the initial and final stages of the four kinds of investigative reflection and the four kinds of genuine, realized wisdom. The four kinds of investigative reflection are: investigating name, meaning, own-nature, and distinction — each provisionally existing, but genuinely nonexistent. Genuinely and completely knowing that these four, apart from consciousness, and even consciousness itself, are nonexistent, is called genuine, realized wisdom.

Relying on the concentration of attaining brightness, giving rise to the lower investigative reflection, contemplating the absence of any grasped object, the stage of warmth is established: at this stage, one first contemplates that the grasped object — name and the other three dharmas — is entirely a transformation of one's own mind, provisionally posited as existing, but genuinely unattainable. Since one has, for the first time, obtained the mode of activity that precedes the sun of wisdom, this is called "attaining brightness"; and, since this is precisely the precursory sign of the fire of the path thus obtained, it is also called "warmth."

Relying on the concentration of increasing brightness, giving rise to the higher investigative reflection, the stage of summit is established: at this stage, one contemplates once more that the grasped object is entirely a transformation of one's own mind. Since the sign of brightness grows increasingly pronounced, this is called "increasing brightness"; and, since this is the culmination of the stage of investigative reflection, it is also called "summit."

依印順定，發下如實智，於無所取決定印持，無能取中亦順樂忍。既無實境離能取識，寧有實識離所取境？所取、能取相待立故。印順忍時總立為忍，印前順後立印順名，忍境、識空故亦名忍。依無間定，發上如實智，印二取空，立世第一法。謂前上忍唯印能取空，今世第一法二空雙印，從此無間必入見道故，立無間名，異生法中此最勝故，名世第一法。

Relying on the concentration of confirming assent, giving rise to the lower genuine, realized wisdom, one decisively confirms the absence of any grasped object, and, with respect to the absence of any grasping subject, likewise assents with delight and forbearance. Since there is, indeed, no real object apart from the grasping consciousness, how could there be a real consciousness apart from the grasped object? The grasped and the grasping are established only in mutual dependence on each other. When confirming and assenting, this stage is generally established as "forbearance" — confirming the former and assenting to the latter gives it the name "confirming assent"; and, since it forbears the emptiness of both object and consciousness, it is also called "forbearance."

Relying on the concentration of immediate succession, giving rise to the higher genuine, realized wisdom, confirming the emptiness of both graspings, the stage of supreme worldly dharma is established: the preceding, higher forbearance confirms only the emptiness of the grasping subject; now, at supreme worldly dharma, both emptinesses are jointly confirmed, and, immediately following this without interruption, one certainly enters the path of seeing — hence the name "immediate succession" is given; and, since this is supreme among all dharmas belonging to ordinary beings, it is called "supreme worldly dharma."

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus, warmth and summit, relying on the grasping consciousness, contemplate the emptiness of the grasped object; when the lower forbearance arises, it confirms the object's empty aspect; as the middle forbearance turns, with respect to the grasping consciousness, it assents with delight and forbearance that it, too, like the object, is empty; when the higher forbearance arises, it confirms the emptiness of the grasping subject; supreme worldly dharma jointly confirms the aspect of both emptinesses — yet all of these still bear an aspect, and so are not yet capable of realizing reality.

Hence it is said that a bodhisattva, throughout these four stages, still posits something as presently manifest, calling it Consciousness-Only's true, ultimate nature — since the two aspects of emptiness and existence have not yet been removed, and the mind, contemplating while still bearing an aspect, still involves something attained, this is not yet genuinely abiding in the true principle of Consciousness-Only. Only once that aspect has been extinguished does one genuinely abide within it.

This stage of applied practice has not yet removed the bondage of appearance, nor is it yet capable of eliminating the bondage of coarseness and heaviness — it is capable only of suppressing and removing the discriminated forms of the two graspings, since these are what conflict with the path of seeing. With respect to the innate forms of the two graspings and their underlying tendency, since one's contaminated contemplating mind still involves something attained, and still involves discrimination, these are not yet entirely suppressed and removed, and certainly not yet capable of being eliminated.

此位菩薩於安立諦、非安立諦俱學觀察，為引當來二種見故，及伏分別二種障故，非安立諦是正所觀，非如二乘唯觀安立。菩薩起此煖等善根，雖方便時通諸靜慮，而依第四方得成滿，託最勝依入見道故。唯依欲界善趣身起，餘慧、厭心非殊勝故。此位亦是解行地攝，未證唯識真勝義故。

A bodhisattva at this stage trains in contemplating both the established truths and the non-established truth together, in order to draw forth the two kinds of insight still to come, and in order to suppress the two kinds of discriminated obstruction; the non-established truth is the genuine object contemplated, unlike the two vehicles, who contemplate only the established truths.

Although a bodhisattva, in giving rise to these wholesome roots of warmth and the rest, may, during the preparatory stage, rely on any of the meditative absorptions, it is only in dependence on the fourth that these are brought to full completion, since one enters the path of seeing relying on the most excellent basis. These arise only in dependence on a fortunate-destiny body within the desire realm, since the wisdom and aversion possible elsewhere are not sufficiently excellent. This stage, too, is included within the stage of resolve-and-practice, since one has not yet realized Consciousness-Only's true, ultimate nature.

Reading note: The four stages of applied practice trace a precise, symmetrical dismantling of subject-object duality: warmth and summit dissolve the grasped object while still relying on the grasping consciousness to do so; the lower and middle forbearance extend that same emptiness back onto the grasping consciousness itself; the higher forbearance confirms the grasping subject's emptiness outright; and supreme worldly dharma finally holds both emptinesses at once. What makes this stage distinct from the path of seeing that follows it is a single, precise technical point: every one of these four levels of insight still "bears an aspect" — meaning the mind achieving this insight still operates through the very subject-object structure it is trying to see through, so the insight, however refined, remains something attained by a subject, rather than the direct, aspect-free realization the path of seeing alone provides. This is a subtle but consequential distinction: extremely advanced contemplative achievement is not automatically the same thing as genuine breakthrough, if the achievement still implicitly retains the structure it claims to be dissolving.

The requirement that this culminate specifically in the fourth meditative absorption, in a human body in the desire realm, is a concrete, almost logistical detail worth noting alongside the philosophy: this tradition treats genuine breakthrough insight as requiring a particular kind of embodied, cultivated stability, not as something that can occur in just any state of mind or any realm of existence, however subtle.

The Path of Seeing 明位次 · (三) 通達位

The third stage is the treatise's account of a bodhisattva's first genuine breakthrough — the moment non-discriminating wisdom finally realizes suchness directly, free of the "bearing an aspect" that limited every level of applied practice. The treatise distinguishes a single, genuine moment of this realization from its more extended, representational unfolding across many further mind-moments.

Non-Discriminating Wisdom, and Whether It Has Aspects 頌釋「通達位」· 無分別智相、見有無

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, what is the character of the stage of realization? The verse states: "When, with respect to the object taken, wisdom is entirely without discrimination and without anything attained, since it does not grasp at any of the various forms of conceptual proliferation — at that time, one abides in Consciousness-Only, free of the characteristics of both graspings."

The treatise states: at the moment when a bodhisattva, with respect to the object taken, has, through non-discriminating wisdom, attained absolutely nothing, since it no longer grasps at any characteristic of conceptual proliferation — it is only then that one is genuinely said to abide in Consciousness-Only's true, ultimate nature. This is precisely because the wisdom that realizes suchness and suchness itself are perfectly equal, both being free of the characteristics of a grasping subject and grasped object — for the characteristics of grasping subject and grasped object are both themselves discrimination, and a mind that still involves something attained is one in which conceptual proliferation still manifests.

有義：此智二分俱無，說無所取、能取相故。有義：此智相、見俱有，帶彼相起名緣彼故，若無彼相名緣彼者，應色智等，名聲等智，若無見分應不能緣，寧可說為緣真如智？勿真如性亦名能緣，故應許此定有見分。有義：此智見有相無，說無相取，不取相故，雖有見分而無分別，說非能取，非取全無。雖無相分，而可說此帶如相起，不離如故。如自證分緣見分時不變而緣，此亦應爾；變而緣者便非親證，如後得智應有分別，故應許此有見無相。

One view holds: this wisdom entirely lacks both aspects, since it is said to be free of the characteristics of both grasped object and grasping subject. Another view holds: this wisdom possesses both the perceived and perceiving aspects, since it is called "taking that as its object" precisely because it arises bearing that aspect — for, were there no such aspect and it were still called this way, then a wisdom cognizing color-form should equally be called a wisdom cognizing sound; and, were there no perceiving aspect, it should be incapable of taking any object at all — how, then, could it be called the wisdom that takes suchness as its object? One must grant that this wisdom certainly does possess a perceiving aspect.

Another view holds: this wisdom possesses the perceiving aspect but lacks the perceived aspect — it is said to be "without the grasping of any aspect" since it does not grasp at an aspect at all; though it possesses a perceiving aspect, it is without discrimination, and so is said to be "not a grasping subject," not that grasping is altogether absent. Although it lacks a perceived aspect, it may still be said to bear the aspect of suchness in its arising, since it is never apart from suchness — just as the self-witnessing aspect, when taking the perceiving aspect as its object, cognizes it without transforming into it; for if it cognized its object by transforming into it, this would no longer be direct realization, and, like subsequently-attained wisdom, would then have to involve discrimination — hence one must grant that this wisdom possesses a perceiving aspect but lacks a perceived aspect.

One view holds: these three are the genuine path of seeing, since the representational path of seeing takes the four noble truths as its object. Another view holds: these three are themselves the representational path of seeing, since the genuine path of seeing does not take its object in this separated way.

Furthermore, if this wisdom did not transform so as to resemble an object, then any dharma apart from its own essence should not be its object at all. On this basis, subsequently-attained wisdom possesses both aspects.

此二見道與六現觀相攝云何？六現觀者：一、思現觀，謂最上品喜受相應，思所成慧，此能觀察諸法共相，引生煖等。二、信現觀，謂緣三寶世、出世間決定淨信，此助現觀令不退轉，立現觀名。三、戒現觀，謂無漏戒除破戒垢令觀增明，亦名現觀。四、現觀智諦現觀，謂一切種緣非安立根本、後得無分別智。五、現觀邊智諦現觀，謂現觀智諦現觀後，諸緣安立世、出世智。六、究竟現觀，謂盡智等究竟位智。此真見道攝彼第四現觀少分，此相見道攝彼第四、第五少分，彼第二、三雖此俱起而非自性，故不相攝。

How do these two paths of seeing correspond to the six kinds of direct realization? They are: first, direct realization through reflection — the wisdom formed from reflection, capable of contemplating the shared characteristics of the various dharmas and drawing forth warmth and the rest. Second, direct realization through faith — the settled, pure faith taking the Three Jewels as its object, which assists direct realization and causes it not to regress. Third, direct realization through moral discipline — uncontaminated moral discipline, which removes the defilement of broken precepts. Fourth, direct realization of the wisdom-truth — the fundamental and subsequently-attained non-discriminating wisdom taking every non-established object as its object. Fifth, direct realization of the truth adjacent to that wisdom — the mundane and supramundane wisdom taking established objects as its object. Sixth, ultimate direct realization — the wisdom of the ultimate stage.

This genuine path of seeing is included within a portion of the fourth kind of direct realization; this representational path of seeing is included within a portion of the fourth and fifth. The second and third, though arising together with these, are not themselves the path of seeing's essence, and so are not included within it.

菩薩得此二見道時，生如來家，住極喜地，善達法界，得諸平等，常生諸佛大集會中，於多百門已得自在，自知不久證大菩提，能盡未來利樂一切。

When a bodhisattva attains these two paths of seeing, they are born into the tathāgata's family, abiding on the ground of utmost joy; skillfully penetrating the dharma-realm, attaining every form of equality; constantly born within the great assemblies of the buddhas; having already attained mastery over many hundreds of approaches; knowing for themselves that great bodhi will soon be realized; and capable, throughout all future time, of benefiting and bringing happiness to all beings.

Reading note: The genuine/representational distinction (真見道/相見道) is this treatise's way of separating a single, decisive breakthrough from everything that unfolds because of it: the genuine path of seeing is the actual moment of realizing the two emptinesses directly, spoken of as "one mind" even though it technically spans several moments of activity; the representational path of seeing is the far more elaborate unpacking of that same realization across specific objects — sentient beings, dharmas, and each of the four noble truths in turn — which is why it can be counted as sixteen mind-moments, or nine, depending on how calming and insight are divided up. The relationship between the two is asymmetrical and important: the representational path of seeing is only ever a provisional restatement of what the genuine path of seeing already accomplished, not a separate achievement that adds anything new.

The debate over whether wisdom itself — both the fundamental, non-discriminating kind and the subsequently-attained kind — possesses a perceived aspect is a genuinely difficult technical puzzle worth sitting with: if wisdom has no aspect of its own object at all, in what sense can it be said to "take suchness as its object"? But if it does transform into an aspect resembling its object, has it not thereby fallen back into the very subject-object structure enlightenment is supposed to transcend? The treatise's own preferred resolution — a perceiving aspect without a corresponding perceived aspect, cognizing suchness the way the self-witnessing aspect cognizes the perceiving aspect, without transforming into it — is a genuinely subtle attempt to have direct, unmediated contact with reality without collapsing into either "no cognition at all" or "cognition just like any ordinary act of perception."

The closing description of what a bodhisattva gains upon reaching this stage — birth into the tathāgata's family, the ground of utmost joy — marks entry onto the first of the ten grounds this fascicle takes up next, and the phrase is worth noting for what it implies: this is described as a genuine, irreversible change of spiritual "family," not merely an accomplishment layered onto an unchanged person.

The Stage of Cultivation Begins: The Transformation of the Basis 明位次 · (四) 修習位 · 釋「轉依」

The fourth stage is where a bodhisattva, having already broken through on the path of seeing, spends the great bulk of the path — the ten grounds — cultivating non-discriminating wisdom again and again until the very basis of mind itself is transformed.

What "Transformation of the Basis" Means 頌釋「修習位」· 轉依

Chinese Source

次修習位，其相云何？頌曰：無得不思議，是出世間智，捨二麁重故，便證得轉依。論曰：菩薩從前見道起已，為斷餘障，證得轉依，復數修習無分別智。此智遠離所取、能取，故說「無得」及「不思議」；或離戲論說為「無得」，妙用難測名「不思議」。是出世間無分別智，斷世間故，名「出世間」。二取隨眠是世間本，唯此能斷獨得「出」名。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, what is the character of the stage of cultivation? The verse states: "Without attainment and inconceivable, this is supramundane wisdom; through relinquishing the two kinds of coarseness and heaviness, one thereby realizes the transformation of the basis."

The treatise states: a bodhisattva, having arisen from the preceding path of seeing, in order to eliminate the remaining obstructions and realize the transformation of the basis, further cultivates non-discriminating wisdom again and again. This wisdom is permanently free of the grasped object and grasping subject, and hence is called "without attainment" and "inconceivable." It is supramundane, non-discriminating wisdom, since it eliminates what is mundane. The underlying tendency of the two graspings is the root of what is mundane, and it is this wisdom alone that is capable of eliminating it, hence uniquely receiving the name "supramundane" — this is precisely the non-discriminating wisdom present throughout the ten grounds.

數修此故，「捨二麁重」。二障種子立「麁重」名，性無堪任，違細、輕故，令彼永滅，故說為「捨」。此能捨彼二麁重故，便能證得廣大「轉依」。「依」謂所依，即依他起，與染、淨法為所依故；染謂虛妄遍計所執；淨謂真實圓成實性。轉謂二分，轉捨、轉得。由數修習無分別智，斷本識中二障麁重，故能轉捨依他起上遍計所執，及能轉得依他起中圓成實性。由轉煩惱得大涅槃，轉所知障證無上覺。成立唯識，意為有情證得如斯二轉依果。

Through repeated cultivation of this wisdom, "the two kinds of coarseness and heaviness are relinquished." The seeds of the two obstructions are given the name "coarseness and heaviness," since their nature is incapable of proper function; causing them to be permanently extinguished is what is meant by "relinquished." It is precisely this capacity to relinquish these two kinds of coarseness and heaviness that makes it possible to realize the vast "transformation of the basis."

"Basis" means what is depended upon — namely, the dependent nature, since it serves as the basis for both defiled and pure dharmas: "defiled" means the false, discriminated, attached-to nature; "pure" means the genuine, perfectly accomplished nature. "Transformation" refers to two aspects: transformative relinquishment, and transformative attainment. Through repeated cultivation of non-discriminating wisdom, eliminating the two obstructions' coarseness and heaviness within the root consciousness, one is thereby able to transformatively relinquish the discriminated, attached-to nature upon the dependent nature, and to transformatively attain the perfectly accomplished nature within that same dependent nature. Through transforming affliction, one attains great nirvāṇa; through transforming the cognitive obstruction, one realizes unsurpassed awakening. The establishing of Consciousness-Only is intended precisely so that sentient beings may realize these two fruits of the transformation of the basis.

或「依」即是唯識真如，生死、涅槃之所依故。愚夫顛倒，迷此真如，故無始來受生死苦，聖者離倒，悟此真如，便得涅槃畢竟安樂。由數修習無分別智，斷本識中二障麁重，故能轉滅依如生死，及能轉證依如涅槃，此即真如離雜染性。如雖性淨而相雜染，故離染時假說新淨，即此新淨說為轉依，修習位中斷障證得。雖於此位亦得菩提，而非此中頌意所顯，頌意但顯轉唯識性，二乘滿位名解脫身，在大牟尼名法身故。

Alternatively, "basis" refers to Consciousness-Only's own suchness, since it is the very basis on which both birth-and-death and nirvāṇa depend. The ignorant, distorted in view, are deluded regarding this suchness, and hence, since beginningless time, undergo the suffering of birth-and-death; the noble, free of distortion, awaken to this suchness, and thereby attain nirvāṇa's ultimate ease. Through repeated cultivation of non-discriminating wisdom, one is thereby able to transformatively extinguish the birth-and-death that depends on suchness, and to transformatively realize the nirvāṇa that depends on suchness — this is precisely suchness's own freedom from defilement. Although suchness's nature is intrinsically pure, its appearance is defiled — hence, once free of defilement, it is provisionally called "newly pure," and it is this very newness-of-purity that is called the transformation of the basis, eliminated and realized within the stage of cultivation.

Although bodhi, too, is attained at this stage, this is not what the verse's own meaning is meant to display here — the verse's meaning shows only the transformation of Consciousness-Only's own nature; the two vehicles' completed stage is called "the body of liberation," while, in the great sage, it is called "the dharma body."

Reading note: "Transformation of the basis" (轉依) is one of this tradition's most important technical terms, and the treatise is careful to explain it two complementary ways. Read narrowly, the "basis" being transformed is the dependent nature itself — the same flow of mind and mental factors that, uncorrected, gets misread through the imagined nature, but which, once purified, simply is the perfectly accomplished nature. Read more broadly, the "basis" is suchness itself, the single ground on which both ordinary suffering and nirvāṇa equally depend — meaning liberation is never a matter of escaping to some other, separate reality, but of the very same underlying ground being newly free of the defilement that obscured it. Either way, the picture is the same: nothing is discarded and replaced with something foreign; what already existed is simply purified into what it was always capable of being.

The Ten Grounds 明位次 · (四) 修習位 · 十地

The stage of cultivation unfolds across ten grounds, each named for its own defining achievement, each cultivating one of the ten perfections preferentially, and each eliminating one of ten specific obstructions in turn.

Ten Grounds, Named for Their Defining Quality 釋「十地」

Chinese Source

云何證得二種轉依？謂十地中修十勝行、斷十重障、證十真如，二種轉依由斯證得。言十地者，一、極喜地，初獲聖性，具證二空，能益自、他，生大喜故。二、離垢地，具淨尸羅，遠離能起微細毀犯煩惱垢故。三、發光地，成就勝定，大法總持，能發無邊妙慧光故。四、焰慧地，安住最勝菩提分法，燒煩惱薪，慧焰增故。五、極難勝地，真、俗兩智行相互違，合令相應極難勝故。

Ru-Yi Translation

How does one realize these two fruits of the transformation of the basis? Throughout the ten grounds, one cultivates ten superior practices, eliminates ten serious obstructions, and realizes ten kinds of suchness — it is through this that the two fruits of the transformation of the basis are realized.

The ten grounds are named as follows. First, the ground of utmost joy: one first attains the noble nature, fully realizing the two emptinesses, capable of benefiting both oneself and others, and hence giving rise to great joy. Second, the ground of freedom from defilement: one possesses pure moral discipline, permanently free of the subtle afflictive defilement capable of causing transgression. Third, the ground of radiance: one accomplishes supreme concentration and the great retention of the dharma, capable of emitting the boundless light of sublime wisdom. Fourth, the ground of blazing wisdom: one abides in the supreme factors of awakening, burning the firewood of affliction as wisdom's flame intensifies. Fifth, the ground of extreme difficulty in conquering: the modes of activity of ultimate and conventional wisdom, mutually opposed, are here made to harmonize and correspond, this being extremely difficult to accomplish.

六、現前地，住緣起智，引無分別最勝般若令現前故。七、遠行地，至無相住功用後邊，出過世間二乘道故。八、不動地，無分別智任運相續，相、用煩惱不能動故。九、善慧地，成就微妙四無閡解，能遍十方善說法故。十、法雲地，大法智雲含眾德水，蔽如空麁重，充滿法身故。如是十地，總攝有為、無為功德以為自性。與所修行為勝依持，令得生長，故名為地。

Sixth, the ground of manifestation: abiding in the wisdom of dependent origination, one draws forth the supreme, non-discriminating wisdom, causing it to manifest. Seventh, the ground of far-reaching practice: one reaches the very furthest limit of effortful abiding in the signless, transcending the paths of the mundane and the two vehicles alike. Eighth, the ground of immovability: non-discriminating wisdom continues spontaneously, its characteristic and function no longer disturbed by affliction. Ninth, the ground of excellent wisdom: one accomplishes the subtle, unimpeded understanding in its four forms, capable of skillfully teaching the dharma throughout the ten directions. Tenth, the ground of the dharma cloud: the great cloud of dharma-wisdom holds the water of manifold virtues, obscuring coarseness and heaviness as vast as space, and filling the dharma body to completion.

These ten grounds, in general, encompass conditioned and unconditioned virtues as their own nature. They serve as the superior basis and support for the practice cultivated upon them, causing it to grow and flourish — hence they are called "grounds."

Reading note: The ten grounds' names trace a recognizable arc: the early grounds establish the basics (joy at the initial breakthrough, then moral purity, then concentration and wisdom's first light), the middle grounds handle the genuinely hard integration work (harmonizing ultimate and conventional wisdom at the fifth ground is explicitly named for its difficulty), and the final grounds describe an almost effortless, unshakeable maturity — non-discriminating wisdom simply continuing on its own at the eighth ground, no longer requiring the deliberate effort every earlier stage needed. The tenth ground's cloud image is worth pausing on: a cloud, in this tradition's imagery, doesn't hoard the rain it holds, but exists specifically to release it everywhere at once — the dharma cloud's "water of manifold virtues" is explicitly there to fall on everyone, not to remain the bodhisattva's private accomplishment.

The Ten Perfections 明位次 · (四) 修習位 · 十勝行 · 十波羅蜜多

The ten grounds are, in practice, the ten perfections in action. The treatise devotes an unusually detailed section to these — their subdivisions, their essential nature, the seven qualifications a practice must meet before it counts as a genuine perfection at all, and how all ten fit together as a single, coordinated path.

Ten Perfections, Their Subdivisions and Essential Nature 釋「十波羅蜜多」種類、自性

Chinese Source

十勝行者，即是十種波羅蜜多。施有三種：謂財施、無畏施、法施。戒有三種：謂律儀戒、攝善法戒、饒益有情戒。忍有三種：謂耐怨害忍、安受苦忍、諦察法忍。精進有三種：謂被甲精進、攝善精進、利樂精進。靜慮有三種：謂安住靜慮、引發靜慮、辦事靜慮。般若有三種：謂生空無分別慧、法空無分別慧、俱空無分別慧。方便善巧有二種：謂迴向方便善巧、拔濟方便善巧。願有二種：謂求菩提願、利樂他願。力有二種：謂思擇力、修習力。智有二種：謂受用法樂智、成熟有情智。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ten superior practices are precisely the ten perfections. Generosity is of three kinds: the giving of wealth, the giving of fearlessness, and the giving of the dharma. Moral discipline is of three kinds: the discipline of moral restraint, the discipline of gathering wholesome dharmas, and the discipline of benefiting sentient beings. Patience is of three kinds: patience enduring harm from enemies, patience calmly bearing suffering, and patience examining dharmas closely. Diligence is of three kinds: the diligence of donning armor, the diligence of gathering what is wholesome, and the diligence of benefiting and bringing happiness to others. Meditative concentration is of three kinds: concentration abiding in stillness, concentration that draws forth capacities, and concentration that accomplishes tasks. Wisdom is of three kinds: non-discriminating wisdom of the emptiness of self, of the emptiness of dharmas, and of both emptinesses together. Skillful means is of two kinds: the skillful means of dedication, and of rescuing beings. Vow is of two kinds: the vow to seek bodhi, and to benefit others. Power is of two kinds: the power of reflective discernment, and of cultivation. Wisdom is of two kinds: the wisdom of enjoying the dharma's happiness, and of bringing sentient beings to maturity.

此十性者，施以無貪及彼所起三業為性；戒以受學菩薩戒時三業為性；忍以無瞋、精進、審慧及彼所起三業為性；精進以勤及彼所起三業為性；靜慮但以等持為性；後五皆以擇法為性，說是根本、後得智故。有義：第八以欲、勝解及信為性，願以此三為自性故。此說自性，若并眷屬，一一皆以一切俱行功德為性。

The essential nature of these ten: generosity has non-greed, together with the three karmic actions it gives rise to, as its nature; moral discipline has the three karmic actions at the time of undertaking the bodhisattva precepts as its nature; patience has non-hatred, diligence, careful discernment, together with the three karmic actions these give rise to, as its nature; diligence has vigor, together with its three karmic actions, as its nature; meditative concentration has simply concentration itself as its nature; the latter five all have the discernment of dharmas as their nature, since they are said to be fundamental and subsequently-attained wisdom. This states only the essential nature of each — including their accompanying factors, each, in turn, has as its nature every virtue that arises together with it.

Seven Excellences, and Why Exactly Ten 釋「七最勝」· 十數不增減

Chinese Source

此十相者，要七最勝之所攝受，方可建立波羅蜜多。一、安住最勝，謂要安住菩薩種性；二、依止最勝，謂要依止大菩提心；三、意樂最勝，謂要悲愍一切有情；四、事業最勝，謂要具行一切事業；五、巧便最勝，謂要無相智所攝受；六、迴向最勝，謂要迴向無上菩提；七、清淨最勝，謂要不為二障間雜。若非此七所攝受者，所行施等非到彼岸。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the character of these ten — only when embraced by seven excellences can they properly be established as perfections. First, the excellence of abiding, meaning one must abide in the bodhisattva's spiritual lineage. Second, the excellence of reliance, meaning one must rely on the resolve for great bodhi. Third, the excellence of intention, meaning one must feel compassion for every sentient being. Fourth, the excellence of undertaking, meaning one must fully carry out every task. Fifth, the excellence of skillful means, meaning one must be embraced by signless wisdom. Sixth, the excellence of dedication, meaning one must dedicate the merit toward unsurpassed bodhi. Seventh, the excellence of purity, meaning one must remain unmixed with either of the two obstructions. Should generosity and the rest not be embraced by these seven, they would not qualify as reaching the far shore.

此但有十不增減者，謂十地中對治十障、證十真如，無增減故。復次，前六不增減者，為除六種相違障故，漸次修行諸佛法故，漸次成熟諸有情故，此如餘論廣說應知。又施等三增上生道，感大財體及眷屬故；精進等三決定勝道，能伏煩惱、成熟有情及佛法故，諸菩薩道唯有此二。又前三種饒益有情，施彼資財、不損惱彼、堪忍彼惱而饒益故。精進等三對治煩惱，雖未伏滅，而能精勤修對治彼諸善加行，永伏永滅諸煩惱故。又由施等不住涅槃，及由後三不住生死，為無住處涅槃資糧，由此前六不增不減。

There are precisely ten, neither more nor fewer, since, throughout the ten grounds, exactly ten obstructions are counteracted and exactly ten kinds of suchness are realized. Furthermore, the first six are neither increased nor decreased because they remove six kinds of opposing obstruction, gradually cultivate every buddha-dharma, and gradually bring sentient beings to maturity.

Furthermore, generosity and the first two others form the path of superior rebirth, since they bring about great wealth and a following of companions; diligence and the last two form the path of decisive excellence, since they are capable of suppressing affliction and bringing sentient beings and the buddha-dharma to maturity — every bodhisattva path consists only of these two aspects. Furthermore, the first three benefit sentient beings directly, by giving them resources, not injuring them, and patiently bearing their afflictions; diligence and the last two counteract affliction, by diligently cultivating the counteracting practices that will permanently eliminate it. Furthermore, through generosity and the rest one does not abide in nirvāṇa, and through the last three one does not abide in birth-and-death — together forming the provisions for the nirvāṇa of no fixed abode. It is for these reasons that the first six are neither increased nor decreased.

後唯四者，為助前六令修滿足不增減故，方便善巧助施等三，願助精進，力助靜慮，智助般若，令修滿故，如《解深密》廣說應知。

That the latter number exactly four is because they assist the first six, causing their cultivation to reach fulfillment, neither more nor fewer needed: skillful means assists generosity and the first two others; vow assists diligence; power assists meditative concentration; wisdom assists wisdom itself — causing their cultivation to reach fulfillment, as extensively explained in the Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra.

Order, Cultivation, and Three Names Across the Path 釋「十次第」、「五種修」、「三學」相攝、遠近大波羅蜜多

Chinese Source

As for their sequence: each prior perfection draws forth the one following it, and each subsequent perfection sustains and purifies the ones before it. Moreover, since each prior one is coarser and each subsequent one subtler, the sequence of easier to more difficult cultivation follows this same order.

As for cultivating these ten, there are five kinds of cultivation: cultivation relying on sustaining support, on attention, on intention, on skillful means, and on mastery. Relying on these five, the cultivation of the ten perfections reaches complete fulfillment.

As for how these ten correspond to the three trainings: the training in moral discipline is of three kinds — the discipline of moral restraint, of gathering wholesome dharmas, and of benefiting sentient beings. The training in concentration is of four kinds: the Mahāyāna's concentration of luminosity; the concentration of the king who gathers merit; the concentration of the worthy guardian; and the concentration of vigorous practice. The training in wisdom is of three kinds: the non-discriminating wisdom of applied practice, the fundamental non-discriminating wisdom, and the subsequently-attained non-discriminating wisdom.

此十因位有三種名：一名遠波羅蜜多，謂初無數劫，爾時施等勢力尚微，被煩惱伏，未能伏彼，由斯煩惱不覺現行。二名近波羅蜜多，謂第二無數劫，爾時施等勢力漸增，非煩惱伏，而能伏彼，由斯煩惱故意方行。三名大波羅蜜多，謂第三無數劫，爾時施等勢力轉增，能畢竟伏一切煩惱，由斯煩惱永不現行，猶有所知微細現種及煩惱種，故未究竟。此十義類差別無邊，恐厭繁文，略示綱要。十於十地雖實皆修，而隨相增，地地修一。雖十地行有無量門，而皆攝在十到彼岸。

These ten, at the causal stage, have three names: "distant perfection," referring to the first incalculable eon, when their power is still feeble, suppressed by affliction rather than suppressing it, so that afflictions arise without one even being aware of them; "near perfection," referring to the second incalculable eon, when their power gradually increases, no longer suppressed by affliction but capable of suppressing it, so that afflictions now arise only through deliberate intention; and "great perfection," referring to the third incalculable eon, when their power grows still further, capable of ultimately suppressing every affliction, so that afflictions never again manifestly arise — though subtle, manifest seeds of the cognitive obstruction, and seeds of affliction, still remain, and so this is not yet ultimate.

The distinctions in meaning and kind among these ten are boundless; for fear of wearying the reader with excessive text, only their essential outline has been shown here. Although, in genuine practice, all ten are cultivated throughout all ten grounds, each ground cultivates one of them preferentially, according to which is most prominent there. Although the practices of the ten grounds involve countless approaches, all are included within these ten perfections.

Reading note: The seven excellences function as a kind of quality-control test the treatise applies to every act of generosity, discipline, or patience a bodhisattva performs — without all seven present, the act is still a good deed, but it does not count as a "perfection" in this technical sense. This is a genuinely demanding standard: an act of pure generosity that lacks, say, the dedication of merit toward unsurpassed bodhi, or that is entangled with either obstruction, falls short of qualifying, however admirable it looks from the outside. The distinction between the six original perfections and the four added ones is worth remembering as a structural principle that recurs throughout this section: the last four (skillful means, vow, power, wisdom) are not separate achievements in their own right, but exist specifically to make the first six succeed — a pattern of "support perfections" serving "primary perfections" that mirrors how heedfulness and equanimity, back in fascicle 6's account of the eleven wholesome factors, turned out to have no separate essence apart from the factors they helped complete.

The three-name progression across the three incalculable eons — distant, near, and great perfection — offers an unusually honest picture of just how long and gradual this path is understood to be: even at the "great" stage, after two full incalculable eons of cultivation, subtle seeds of both obstructions still remain, and the path is explicitly said to be "not yet ultimate." This treatise never suggests the bodhisattva path is a quick undertaking, and the willingness to name three vast, sequential eons of steadily increasing but still-incomplete mastery is part of what gives its account of spiritual development its distinctive, unhurried realism.

The Ten Obstacles, Ground by Ground 明位次 · (四) 修習位 · 十重障

Fascicle 9 closes with the treatise's ground-by-ground account of exactly what is eliminated at each stage of the path — ten specific obstructions, each paired with two forms of subtle delusion, tracing a bodhisattva's progress from the first breakthrough all the way to the threshold of buddhahood itself.

Ground One: The Obstruction of an Ordinary Being's Nature 釋「異生性障」

Chinese Source

十重障者：一、異生性障，謂二障中分別起者，依彼種立異生性故。二乘見道現在前時，唯斷一種，名得聖性；菩薩見道現在前時，具斷二種，名得聖性。二真見道現在前時，彼二障種必不成就，猶明與闇定不俱生，如秤兩頭低昂時等，諸相違法理必應然，是故二性無俱成失。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ten serious obstructions: first, the obstruction of the nature of an ordinary being — the discriminated forms among the two obstructions, since the nature of an ordinary being is established with reference to their seeds. When the path of seeing of the two vehicles manifests, only one kind is eliminated, and this is called attaining noble nature; when a bodhisattva's path of seeing manifests, both kinds are eliminated together, and this, too, is called attaining noble nature.

At the moment the genuine path of seeing manifests, the seeds of these two obstructions certainly cannot both continue to exist — just as light and darkness certainly never coexist, and just as the two ends of a scale rise and fall together in the same instant. Every pair of mutually opposed dharmas must, in principle, behave this way — hence there is no fault in these two natures never coexisting.

無間道時已無惑種，何用復起解脫道為？斷惑證滅，期心別故。為捨彼品麁重性故，無間道時雖無惑種，而未捨彼無堪任性，為捨此故，起解脫道，及證此品擇滅無為。雖見道生亦斷惡趣諸業果等，而今且說能起煩惱是根本故，由斯初地說斷二愚及彼麁重。一、執著我法愚，即是此中異生性障；二、惡趣雜染愚，即是惡趣諸業果等。

At the moment of the path of uninterrupted progress, the afflictions' seeds are already absent — what further use, then, is there in giving rise to the path of liberation? Because eliminating affliction and realizing cessation are aimed at with distinct intentions: it is in order to relinquish that category's coarseness and heaviness that, although the afflictions' seeds are already absent at that moment, their incapacity for proper function has not yet been relinquished — and it is in order to relinquish this that the path of liberation arises, realizing this category's analytical cessation as an unconditioned dharma.

Although the arising of the path of seeing also eliminates the karmic results of unfortunate destinies, the present discussion speaks only of what is capable of giving rise to affliction, since this is the root cause — hence, at the first ground, two forms of delusion, and their coarseness and heaviness, are eliminated: the delusion of attachment to self and dharmas, precisely the obstruction of an ordinary being's nature discussed here; and the delusion of the defilement of unfortunate destinies, precisely their karmic results.

雖初地所斷實通二障，而異生性障意取所知，說十無明非染污故；無明即是十障品愚。二乘亦能斷煩惱障，彼是共故，非此所說。住、滿地中時既淹久，理應進斷所應斷障，不爾三時道應無別，故說菩薩得現觀已，復於十地修道位中，唯修永滅所知障道，留煩惱障助願受生，非如二乘速趣圓寂，故修道位不斷煩惱，將成佛時方頓斷故。

Although what is eliminated at the first ground genuinely extends to both obstructions, the obstruction of an ordinary being's nature is intended, in meaning, to refer to the cognitive obstruction, since the ten forms of ignorance across the ten obstructions are said not to be defiled. The two vehicles, too, are capable of eliminating the afflictive obstruction, but, since this is shared with them, it is not what is discussed here.

Since the time spent abiding on and completing each ground is genuinely long, it stands to reason that one should progressively eliminate whatever obstruction is to be eliminated there — otherwise, the three periods of the path would have no distinction at all. Hence it is said that a bodhisattva, having already attained direct realization, further cultivates, throughout the path of cultivation across the ten grounds, only the path that permanently extinguishes the cognitive obstruction, deliberately retaining the afflictive obstruction in order to assist their vow to take rebirth — unlike the two vehicles, who swiftly hasten toward complete cessation. Hence, on the path of cultivation, affliction is not eliminated, and is only eliminated all at once when buddhahood is about to be attained.

Grounds Two Through Five: Wrong Practice, Dullness, Subtle Affliction, and the Lesser Vehicles' Pull 釋二地至五地所斷障

Chinese Source

二、邪行障，謂所知障中俱生一分及彼所起悞犯三業。彼障二地極淨尸羅，入二地時便能永斷，由斯二地說斷二愚及彼麁重：一、微細悞犯愚；二、種種業趣愚，即彼所起悞犯三業。三、闇鈍障，謂所知障中俱生一分，令所聞、思、修法忘失。彼障三地勝定、總持及彼所發殊勝三慧，入三地時便能永斷。由斯三地說斷二愚及彼麁重：一、欲貪愚，即是此中能障勝定及修慧者；二、圓滿聞持陀羅尼愚，即是此中能障總持、聞、思慧者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the obstruction of wrong practice: a portion of the innate cognitive obstruction, together with the inadvertent transgressions of the three karmic actions it gives rise to. This obstructs the second ground's utmost pure moral discipline, and is permanently eliminated upon entering the second ground — hence, at the second ground, two forms of delusion, and their coarseness and heaviness, are eliminated: the delusion of subtle, inadvertent transgression; and the delusion of manifold karmic destinies, precisely the inadvertent transgressions it gives rise to.

Third, the obstruction of dullness: a portion of the innate cognitive obstruction, causing what has been heard, reflected upon, and cultivated to be forgotten. This obstructs the third ground's supreme concentration, retention, and the three sublime forms of wisdom it brings forth, and is permanently eliminated upon entering the third ground. Hence, at the third ground, two forms of delusion are eliminated: the delusion of desire and craving, which obstructs supreme concentration and the wisdom of cultivation; and the delusion obstructing the complete retention of what is heard, which obstructs retention and the wisdom of hearing and reflection.

四、微細煩惱現行障，謂所知障中俱生一分，第六識俱身見等攝，最下品故，不作意緣故，遠隨現行故，說名微細。彼障四地菩提分法，入四地時便能永斷。初、二、三地行施、戒、修，相同世間，四地修得菩提分法方名出世，故能永害二身見等。「身見等」言亦攝無始所知障攝定愛、法愛。彼定、法愛三地尚增；入四地時方能永斷，菩提分法特違彼故。由斯四地說斷二愚及彼麁重。一、等至愛愚；二、法愛愚。

Fourth, the obstruction of subtle affliction's manifest activity: a portion of the innate cognitive obstruction, included within the view of a perishable self associated with the sixth consciousness — since it is of the very lowest grade and follows manifest activity only at a distance, it is called "subtle." This obstructs the fourth ground's factors of awakening, and is permanently eliminated upon entering the fourth ground. The first three grounds' practice of generosity, discipline, and cultivation resembles the mundane; only at the fourth ground, once the factors of awakening are attained, is it properly called supramundane. The phrase "view of self and the like" also encompasses the beginningless cognitive obstruction's craving for concentration and craving for the dharma, permanently eliminated only upon entering the fourth ground. Hence, at the fourth ground, two forms of delusion are eliminated: the delusion of craving for attainment, and the delusion of craving for the dharma.

五、於下乘般涅槃障，謂所知障中俱生一分，令厭生死樂趣涅槃，同下二乘厭苦欣滅。彼障五地無差別道，入五地時便能永斷。由斯五地說斷二愚及彼麁重：一、純作意背生死愚；二、純作意向涅槃愚。

Fifth, the obstruction of tending toward the lesser vehicles' complete cessation: a portion of the innate cognitive obstruction, causing one to become averse to birth-and-death and delight in nirvāṇa, resembling the lower two vehicles' aversion to suffering and delight in cessation. This obstructs the fifth ground's undifferentiated path, and is permanently eliminated upon entering the fifth ground. Hence, at the fifth ground, two forms of delusion are eliminated: the delusion of exclusively intending to turn away from birth-and-death; and the delusion of exclusively intending toward nirvāṇa.

Grounds Six Through Eight: Coarse and Subtle Characteristics, and Effortlessness 釋六地至八地所斷障

Chinese Source

六、麁相現行障，謂所知障中俱生一分，執有染、淨麁相現行，彼障六地無染、淨道，入六地時便能永斷。由斯六地說斷二愚及彼麁重。一、現觀察行流轉愚；二、相多現行愚。七、細相現行障，謂所知障中俱生一分，執有生滅細相現行，彼障七地妙無相道，入七地時便能永斷。由斯七地說斷二愚及彼麁重。一、細相現行愚；二、純作意求無相愚。純於無相作意勤求，未能空中起有勝行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, the obstruction of coarse characteristics' manifest activity: a portion of the innate cognitive obstruction, grasping at the manifest activity of coarse characteristics of defilement and purity. This obstructs the sixth ground's path free of defilement and purity, and is permanently eliminated upon entering the sixth ground. Hence, at the sixth ground, two forms of delusion are eliminated: the delusion of contemplating conditioned activity's flow, precisely the grasping at defilement; and the delusion of the manifest activity of many characteristics, precisely the grasping at purity.

Seventh, the obstruction of subtle characteristics' manifest activity: a portion of the innate cognitive obstruction, grasping at the manifest activity of subtle characteristics of arising and ceasing. This obstructs the seventh ground's sublime, signless path, and is permanently eliminated upon entering the seventh ground. Hence, at the seventh ground, two forms of delusion are eliminated: the delusion of subtle characteristics' manifest activity, still grasping at the subtle characteristic of arising; and the delusion of exclusively intending to seek the signless, still grasping at the subtle characteristic of return-to-cessation. Exclusively striving toward signless intention, one is not yet capable of giving rise to superior activity within emptiness.

八、無相中作加行障，謂所知障中俱生一分，令無相觀不任運起。前之五地，有相觀多，無相觀少；於第六地，有相觀少，無相觀多；第七地中純無相觀，雖恒相續而有加行，由無相中有加行故，未能任運現相及土，如是加行障八地中無功用道；故若得入第八地時便能永斷。彼永斷故，得二自在。由斯八地說斷二愚及彼麁重。一、於無相作功用愚；二、於相自在愚。八地以上純無漏道任運起故，三界煩惱永不現行，第七識中細所知障猶可現起，生空智果不違彼故。

Eighth, the obstruction of applied effort within the signless: a portion of the innate cognitive obstruction, causing signless contemplation to fail to arise spontaneously. Within the first five grounds, contemplation with characteristics predominates; at the sixth ground, signless contemplation increases; within the seventh ground, contemplation is purely signless, yet still requires applied effort, and so cannot yet spontaneously manifest characteristics and lands. This applied effort obstructs the eighth ground's effortless path, and is permanently eliminated upon attaining the eighth ground — through this, one attains mastery over two things. Hence, at the eighth ground, two forms of delusion are eliminated: the delusion of applied effort within the signless; and the delusion of lacking mastery over characteristics.

From the eighth ground onward, since the purely uncontaminated path arises spontaneously, the afflictions of the three realms permanently cease to manifest; the subtle cognitive obstruction within the seventh consciousness, however, can still manifestly arise, since the result of the wisdom realizing the emptiness of self does not conflict with it.

Grounds Nine and Ten, and the Obstructions Eliminated at Buddhahood 釋九地、十地及佛地所斷障

Chinese Source

九、利他中不欲行障，謂所知障中俱生一分，令於利樂有情事中，不欲勤行，樂修己利。彼障九地四無閡解，入九地時便能永斷。由斯九地說斷二愚及彼麁重。一、於無量所說法、無量名句字、後後慧辯陀羅尼自在愚；二、辯才自在愚。愚能障此四種自在，皆是此中第九障攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, the obstruction of unwillingness in benefiting others: a portion of the innate cognitive obstruction, causing one to be unwilling to diligently engage in benefiting sentient beings, preferring to cultivate one's own benefit instead. This obstructs the ninth ground's four unimpeded understandings, and is permanently eliminated upon entering the ninth ground. Hence, at the ninth ground, two forms of delusion are eliminated: the delusion regarding mastery over retaining boundless taught dharmas, names, phrases, and syllables, and progressively refined wisdom and eloquence — mastery, respectively, over what is expressed, what expresses, and the successive interpretation of spoken sound; and the delusion regarding mastery of eloquence itself, skillfully discerning what is appropriate to each occasion.

十、於諸法中未得自在障，謂所知障中俱生一分，令於諸法不得自在。彼障十地大法智雲及所含藏、所起事業。入十地時便能永斷。由斯十地說斷二愚及彼麁重。一、大神通愚；二、悟入微細祕密愚。此地於法雖得自在，而有餘障，未名最極。

Tenth, the obstruction of not yet attaining mastery over dharmas: a portion of the innate cognitive obstruction, causing one to lack mastery over dharmas. This obstructs the tenth ground's great cloud of dharma-wisdom, together with what it holds and the tasks it undertakes, and is permanently eliminated upon entering the tenth ground. Hence, at the tenth ground, two forms of delusion are eliminated: the delusion regarding great supernormal powers; and the delusion of awakening into subtle secrets. At this ground, although one attains mastery over dharmas, further obstruction still remains, and this is not yet called the utmost extreme.

謂有俱生微所知障，及有任運煩惱障種，金剛喻定現在前時，彼皆頓斷，入如來地。由斯佛地說斷二愚及彼麁重：一、於一切所知境極微細著愚，即是此中微所知障；二、極微細礙愚，即是此中一切任運煩惱障種。故《集論》說：得菩提時頓斷煩惱及所知障，成阿羅漢及成如來，證大涅槃、大菩提故。

That is: there remains a subtle portion of the innate cognitive obstruction, together with the spontaneously-arising seeds of the afflictive obstruction — when the vajra-like concentration manifests, all of these are eliminated at once, and one enters the stage of the tathāgata. Hence, at the stage of buddhahood, two forms of delusion are eliminated: the delusion of extremely subtle attachment with respect to every object of knowledge, precisely the subtle cognitive obstruction; and the delusion of extremely subtle obstruction, precisely every spontaneously-arising seed of the afflictive obstruction. Hence the Abhidharmasamuccaya states: at the moment of attaining bodhi, affliction and the cognitive obstruction are eliminated all at once, becoming an arhat and becoming a tathāgata, realizing great nirvāṇa and great bodhi.

Reading note: Reading straight through all ten obstructions in sequence reveals a coherent psychological arc, not just a scholastic list. The early obstructions are still relatively coarse and rule-shaped (subtle transgressions, forgetting what one has learned, unconscious flickers of self-view); the middle obstructions concern subtle attractions to spiritual goods themselves (craving for concentration, craving for the dharma, even craving for nirvāṇa and aversion to continued birth-and-death, which is explicitly named as a fault at the fifth ground); the later obstructions concern residual effort and self-orientation even within very refined realization (needing applied effort within signless contemplation at the seventh and eighth grounds, preferring one's own cultivation to active service at the ninth); and what remains at the very threshold of buddhahood is almost unimaginably subtle — not craving or self-view in any ordinary sense, but the last, faintest trace of attachment to objects of knowledge as such, and afflictive seeds so quiet they only ever arise spontaneously, never through deliberate intention.

The deliberate retention of the afflictive obstruction throughout the entire stage of cultivation is one of this treatise's most distinctive and consequential teachings: a bodhisattva who has already attained the wisdom to eliminate ordinary affliction chooses not to, specifically in order to keep taking birth and serving beings, only allowing its final elimination at the very moment of buddhahood itself, together with the cognitive obstruction, in a single decisive stroke. This is the technical grounding for why a bodhisattva's path looks so different from an arhat's "swift hastening toward complete cessation" — not a lesser attainment, but a specific, chosen strategy that trades an available shortcut to personal liberation for continued presence in the world.

With this, fascicle 9 has carried the treatise from the completion of the Three Natures doctrine, through the entire structure of the path — provisioning, applied practice, the path of seeing, and the ten grounds of cultivation — right up to the vajra-like concentration that ends in buddhahood itself. Only the fifth and final stage, the ultimate stage, remains, along with it the treatise's own concluding word on what buddhahood actually consists of — the subject fascicle 10 takes up to close the entire work.
