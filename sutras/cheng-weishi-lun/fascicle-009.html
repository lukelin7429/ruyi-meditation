Fascicle 9 · 成唯識論卷第九

The Five Stages of the Path

Fascicle 9 opens by completing the Three Natures doctrine with its mirror teaching, the three naturelessnesses, and by identifying Consciousness-Only's true nature outright with suchness itself — the treatise's closest approach yet to naming the ultimate directly. It then turns to entirely new terrain: the complete five-stage path by which a bodhisattva actually awakens into Consciousness-Only, from the initial stage of provisioning, through applied practice and the path of seeing, into the long stage of cultivation — the ten grounds, with their ten perfections and the ten obstacles eliminated one by one, ground by ground, all the way to the threshold of buddhahood itself.

The Three Naturelessnesses 明唯識性 · 釋外難 · 釋「三無性」

Fascicle 8 closed with the Three Natures. The treatise now completes that teaching with its necessary mirror image: the three naturelessnesses, which explain how the sūtras can say that all dharmas lack their own nature without contradicting the reality of the three natures already established.

Hidden Intent: Why the Buddha Spoke of "No Nature" 頌釋「三無性」

Chinese Source

若有三性，如何世尊說一切法皆無自性？頌曰：即依此三性，立彼三無性，故佛密意說，一切法無性。論曰：即依此前所說三性，立彼後說三種無性，謂即相、生、勝義無性，故佛密意說一切法皆無自性，非性全無。

Ru-Yi Translation

If there are three natures, how can the World-Honored One say that all dharmas are entirely without their own nature? The verse states: "It is precisely with reference to these three natures that those three naturelessnesses, spoken of afterward, are established; hence the Buddha, with hidden intent, said that all dharmas are without their own nature."

The treatise states: it is precisely with reference to the three natures already discussed that the three naturelessnesses spoken of afterward are established — naturelessness of characteristic, naturelessness of arising, and naturelessness in the ultimate sense. Hence the Buddha, with hidden intent, said that all dharmas are entirely without their own nature — not that nature is altogether absent.

說「密意」言顯非了義，謂後二性雖體非無，而有愚夫於彼增益，妄執實有我、法自性，此即名為遍計所執。為除此執，故佛世尊於有及無，總說無性。

Speaking of "hidden intent" shows that this is not a statement of definitive meaning: although the latter two natures are not, in their essence, nonexistent, the ignorant nonetheless superimpose upon them, falsely grasping at the real existence of a self and dharma-nature — this is precisely what is called the discriminated, attached-to nature. It is in order to remove this attachment that the World-Honored One, with respect to both what exists and what does not, spoke generally of "naturelessness."

云何依此而立彼三？謂依此初遍計所執立相無性，由此體相畢竟非有，如空華故。依次依他立生無性，此如幻事託眾緣生，無如妄執自然性故，假說無性，非性全無。依後圓成實立勝義無性，謂即勝義由遠離前遍計所執我、法性，故假說無性，非性全無。如太虛空雖遍眾色，而是眾色無性所顯。雖依他起非勝義故，亦得說為勝義無性，而濫第二，故此不說。

How, with reference to these three natures, are those three naturelessnesses established? With reference to the first, the discriminated, attached-to nature, naturelessness of characteristic is established, since its own essence and characteristic are utterly nonexistent, like a flower in the sky. With reference to the next, the dependent nature, naturelessness of arising is established — this is like a magical illusion, arising in dependence upon manifold conditions, lacking any spontaneous, independent nature such as false attachment imagines; hence naturelessness is provisionally spoken of, not that nature is altogether absent. With reference to the last, the perfectly accomplished nature, naturelessness in the ultimate sense is established — meaning that the ultimate, precisely through being permanently free of the former, discriminated, attached-to nature of self and dharmas, is provisionally called "naturelessness" — just as vast empty space, though it pervades every form, is itself what these forms' own naturelessness reveals.

Although the dependent nature, too, not being ultimate, could also be spoken of as naturelessness in the ultimate sense, this would be too easily confused with the second kind, and so is not stated this way here.

Reading note: The three naturelessnesses are not a retraction of the Three Natures but a careful gloss on scriptural language, and the "hidden intent" framing does real work: when the sūtras flatly deny that dharmas have any nature, this treatise insists that statement was never meant as the final word, but as a corrective aimed specifically at the false attachment ordinary beings layer onto the dependent and perfectly accomplished natures. Each naturelessness targets its corresponding nature in a logically distinct way: the imagined nature simply has no characteristic to speak of at all (like a flower blooming in empty sky); the dependent nature lacks the kind of independent, self-caused arising naive attachment imagines, though it certainly arises through real conditions; and the perfectly accomplished nature is called "natureless" only in the sense of being free from the imagined nature's false overlay, not in the sense of being nothing. The empty-space analogy captures this precisely — space itself is not a thing among the objects it contains, yet it is definitely not nothing, and it is exactly what those objects' own lack of independent existence reveals when properly seen.

Consciousness-Only's True Nature Is Suchness Itself 明唯識性 · 釋「唯識實性」

The treatise now states, as directly as it ever will, what Consciousness-Only ultimately is: not a doctrine about consciousness set apart from ultimate reality, but suchness itself, viewed through the lens this treatise has built up across eight fascicles.

Ultimate Truth, Fourfold, and Suchness by Many Names 頌釋「唯識實性」· 四種勝義

Chinese Source

頌曰：此諸法勝義，亦即是真如，常如其性故，即唯識實性。論曰：此性即是諸法勝義，是一切法勝義諦故。然勝義諦略有四種：一、世間勝義，謂蘊、處、界等；二、道理勝義，謂苦等四諦；三、證得勝義，謂二空真如；四、勝義勝義，謂一真法界。此中勝義依最後說，是最勝道所行義故，為簡前三，故作是說。

Ru-Yi Translation

The verse states: "This, the dharmas' ultimate, is likewise suchness itself, since it is permanently as its own nature is — this is Consciousness-Only's genuine nature."

The treatise states: this nature is precisely the ultimate reality of all dharmas, since it is the ultimate truth of every dharma. Ultimate truth, broadly speaking, is of four kinds: worldly ultimate truth, meaning the aggregates, sense-fields, elements, and the rest; ultimate truth as principle, meaning suffering and the other three noble truths; ultimate truth as realization, meaning the suchness of the two emptinesses; and ultimate truth as the ultimate itself, meaning the single, genuine dharma-realm. Here, "ultimate" is spoken of with reference to this last sense, since it is the object of the supreme path — this is stated in order to distinguish it from the first three.

此諸法勝義亦即是真如，真謂真實，顯非虛妄，如謂如常，表無變易，謂此真實於一切位常如其性，故曰真如，即是湛然不虛妄義。「亦」言顯此復有多名，謂名法界及實際等，如餘論中隨義廣釋。

This dharmas' ultimate is likewise suchness itself: "true" means genuinely real, showing it is not false; "suchness" means constant sameness, indicating it undergoes no change — meaning that this genuine reality, in every state, is permanently as its own nature is, and hence is called "suchness," precisely the meaning of being tranquil and never false. The word "likewise" shows that this, moreover, has many further names — called the dharma-realm, the true limit, and the rest — as is extensively explained, according to their meaning, in other treatises.

此性即是唯識實性，謂唯識性略有二種：一者虛妄，謂遍計所執；二者真實，謂圓成實性，為簡虛妄說「實性」言。復有二性：一者世俗，謂依他起；二者勝義，謂圓成實，為簡世俗故說「實性」。

This nature is precisely Consciousness-Only's genuine nature: Consciousness-Only's own nature, broadly speaking, is of two kinds — one false, namely the discriminated, attached-to nature; the other genuine, namely the perfectly accomplished nature — and it is in order to distinguish it from the false that the phrase "genuine nature" is used. There are, further, two other natures: one conventional, namely the dependent nature; the other ultimate, namely the perfectly accomplished nature — and it is in order to distinguish it from the conventional that the phrase "genuine nature" is used.

三頌總顯諸契經中說無性言非極了義，諸有智者不應依之總撥諸法都無自性。

These three verses, together, show that the sūtras' statements of "naturelessness" are not statements of ultimate, definitive meaning — no wise person should, on this basis, categorically deny that all dharmas have any nature whatsoever.

Reading note: This is one of the most important identifications the treatise makes explicit: Consciousness-Only, at its deepest, ultimate level, simply is suchness — the same reality named, in other traditions and other sūtras, the dharma-realm, the true limit, or emptiness itself. The fourfold breakdown of "ultimate truth" is worth remembering, since later fascicles will use "ultimate" loosely at times and precisely at others — worldly, principled, and realized ultimates are all still relative to some more limited perspective, and only the fourth, the single genuine dharma-realm itself, is truly unconditioned.

The closing line functions as the treatise's own reading instructions for the entire Three Natures and three-naturelessnesses teaching just concluded: scriptural statements of universal "no-nature" are correctives against a specific error, not literal metaphysical nihilism, and readers who take them as flat denials that anything exists have misunderstood the hidden intent behind them — precisely the same caution the treatise gave, in fascicle 7, against misreading "Consciousness-Only" as claiming only a single consciousness exists.

The Path in Five Stages 明位次 · 總標五位

With Consciousness-Only's theoretical structure now complete, the treatise turns to genuinely new ground for the remainder of the treatise: the actual path by which a bodhisattva comes to realize everything explained so far, structured into five sequential stages.

Two Lineages, Five Stages 釋「二種姓」、「五位」

Chinese Source

如是所成唯識相、性，誰於幾位？如何悟入？謂具大乘二種姓者，略於五位漸次悟入。云何大乘二種種姓？一、本性住種姓，謂無始來依附本識，法爾所得無漏法因。二、習所成種姓，謂聞法界等流法已，聞所成等熏習所成。要具大乘此二種姓，方能漸次悟入唯識。

Ru-Yi Translation

Given that Consciousness-Only's characteristics and nature have thus been established — who, and at how many stages, comes to awaken into this? And how does this awakening occur? Those who possess the Mahāyāna's two kinds of spiritual lineage, broadly speaking, awaken into it gradually, through five stages.

What are the Mahāyāna's two kinds of spiritual lineage? First, the innately abiding lineage, meaning the uncontaminated dharma-cause, naturally possessed since beginningless time, depending upon and adhering to the root consciousness. Second, the lineage accomplished through cultivation, meaning what is brought about through habituation from hearing the dharma-realm's outflow teaching and the wisdom born from such hearing, and the like. Only one who possesses both of these Mahāyāna lineages is capable of gradually awakening into Consciousness-Only.

何謂悟入唯識五位？一、資糧位，謂修大乘順解脫分；二、加行位，謂修大乘順決擇分；三、通達位，謂諸菩薩所住見道；四、修習位，謂諸菩薩所住修道；五、究竟位，謂住無上正等菩提。

What are the five stages of awakening into Consciousness-Only? First, the stage of provisioning, meaning the cultivation of the Mahāyāna's portion conducive to liberation. Second, the stage of applied practice, meaning the cultivation of the Mahāyāna's portion conducive to decisive discernment. Third, the stage of realization, meaning the path of seeing upon which bodhisattvas abide. Fourth, the stage of cultivation, meaning the path of cultivation upon which bodhisattvas abide. Fifth, the ultimate stage, meaning abiding in unsurpassed, perfect awakening.

云何漸次悟入唯識？謂諸菩薩於識相、性，資糧位中能深信解；在加行位能漸伏除所取、能取，引發真見；在通達位如實通達；修習位中如所見理，數數修習伏斷餘障；至究竟位出障圓明，能盡未來化有情類，復令悟入唯識相、性。

How does one gradually awaken into Consciousness-Only? At the stage of provisioning, bodhisattvas are able to deeply believe in and understand the characteristics and nature of consciousness; at the stage of applied practice, they are able to gradually suppress and remove the grasped object and grasping subject, drawing forth genuine insight; at the stage of realization, they genuinely penetrate it; at the stage of cultivation, in accordance with the principle already seen, they cultivate again and again, suppressing and eliminating the remaining obstructions; upon reaching the ultimate stage, free of obstruction and perfectly luminous, they are able, throughout all future time, to transform sentient beings, causing them, too, to awaken into Consciousness-Only's characteristics and nature.

Reading note: This overview is the treatise's own table of contents for everything remaining in the work: fascicle 9 will work through the first four of these five stages in detail — provisioning, applied practice, the path of seeing, and the long path of cultivation across the ten grounds — with only the fifth and final stage, buddhahood itself, left for fascicle 10. The two spiritual lineages are worth noting as a genuinely dual requirement: an innate capacity present since beginningless time is not, on its own, sufficient — it must be actively cultivated through hearing and reflecting on the teaching, and a bodhisattva needs both working together to progress. The five-stage structure itself maps cleanly onto ordinary language for spiritual development: gathering the raw resources (provisioning), disciplined preparatory practice (applied practice), a first genuine breakthrough (the path of seeing), sustained integration of that breakthrough (cultivation), and finally its complete fulfillment (the ultimate stage) — a shape recognizable across many contemplative traditions, not unique to this one.

The Stage of Provisioning 明位次 · (一) 資糧位

The first stage covers the long period from the initial arousal of the resolve for bodhi up until one is ready to begin the applied practice that precedes genuine insight. A bodhisattva at this stage cultivates deep confidence in Consciousness-Only, but has not yet gained any real power over the underlying tendencies that drive ordinary delusion — which the treatise here defines with unusual precision.

What This Stage Covers 頌釋「資糧位」

Chinese Source

初資糧位，其相云何？頌曰：乃至未起識，求住唯識性，於二取隨眠，猶未能伏滅。論曰：從發深固大菩提心，乃至未起順決擇識，求住唯識真勝義性，齊此皆是資糧位攝，為趣無上正等菩提，修集種種勝資糧故。為有情故，勤求解脫，由此亦名順解脫分。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the character of the first stage, the stage of provisioning? The verse states: "Until one has not yet given rise to the consciousness that seeks to abide in Consciousness-Only's nature, one is still not yet able to suppress and eliminate the underlying tendency of the two graspings."

The treatise states: from the arousal of the profound and firm resolve for great bodhi, up until the consciousness conducive to decisive discernment has not yet arisen, seeking to abide in Consciousness-Only's true, ultimate nature — everything up to this point is included within the stage of provisioning, since one cultivates and accumulates every kind of superior provision in order to advance toward unsurpassed, perfect awakening. For the sake of sentient beings, one diligently seeks liberation, and it is for this reason that this stage is also called the portion conducive to liberation.

此位菩薩依因、善友、作意、資糧四勝力故，於唯識義雖深信解，而未能了能、所取空，多住外門修菩薩行，故於二取所引隨眠，猶未有能伏滅功力，令彼不起二取現行。此「二取」言顯二取取，執取「能取」、「所取」性故。二取習氣名彼隨眠，隨逐有情，眠伏藏識，或隨增過，故名隨眠。即是所知、煩惱障種。

A bodhisattva at this stage, relying on the four superior powers of cause, good friends, attention, and provisions, though deeply believing in and understanding the meaning of Consciousness-Only, is not yet able to realize the emptiness of the grasping subject and grasped object, and, for the most part, still practices the bodhisattva path through outward-facing methods — hence, with respect to the underlying tendency drawn forth by the two graspings, this bodhisattva does not yet possess the capacity to suppress and eliminate it, so as to prevent the two graspings' manifest activity from arising.

This phrase "the two graspings" indicates the grasping-at-two-graspings, since it grasps at and clings to the nature of "the grasping subject" and "the grasped object." The habituation of the two graspings is called their "underlying tendency," since it constantly follows sentient beings, lying dormant within the storehouse consciousness, or since it follows along and increases their fault. This is precisely the seed of the cognitive obstruction and the afflictive obstruction.

Two Obstructions Defined 釋「煩惱障」、「所知障」

Chinese Source

煩惱障者，謂執遍計所執實我，薩迦耶見而為上首，百二十八根本煩惱，及彼等流諸隨煩惱。此皆擾惱有情身、心，能障涅槃，名煩惱障。所知障者，謂執遍計所執實法，薩迦耶見而為上首，見、疑、無明、愛、恚、慢等。覆所知境無顛倒性，能障菩提，名所知障。

Ru-Yi Translation

The afflictive obstruction: the attachment to a real self, discriminated and attached to, with the view of a perishable, composite self foremost among the 128 fundamental afflictions, together with the various secondary afflictions that flow from them. All of these disturb and afflict sentient beings' body and mind, and are capable of obstructing nirvāṇa — hence they are called the afflictive obstruction.

The cognitive obstruction: the attachment to real dharmas, discriminated and attached to, with the view of a perishable, composite self foremost among them, together with view, doubt, ignorance, craving, hatred, conceit, and the rest. These obscure the object of knowledge's undistorted nature, and are capable of obstructing bodhi — hence they are called the cognitive obstruction.

此所知障，決定不與異熟識俱，彼微劣故，不與無明、慧相應故，法空智品與俱起故。七轉識內，隨其所應或少或多，如煩惱說。眼等五識無分別故，法見、疑等定不相應，餘由意力皆容引起。此障但與不善、無記二心相應，論說無明唯通不善、無記性故，癡、無癡等不相應故。煩惱障中此障必有，彼定用此為所依故，體雖無異而用有別。故二隨眠，隨聖道用有勝、有劣，斷惑前後。

This cognitive obstruction certainly never occurs together with the ripening consciousness, since that consciousness is too feeble, since it is never associated with ignorance or discernment, and since the wisdom of the emptiness of dharmas can arise together with it. Among the seven transforming consciousnesses, it is present to a greater or lesser extent, as appropriate, as already explained for the afflictive obstruction. Since the eye-consciousness and the other four sense-consciousnesses lack discrimination, they are certainly never associated with the view regarding dharmas, doubt, and the rest; the remaining consciousnesses, through the power of the mental faculty, can all give rise to it.

This obstruction is associated only with unwholesome and neutral minds, since the treatise states that ignorance extends only to the unwholesome and the neutral, and since delusion and non-delusion are never mutually associated. Within the afflictive obstruction, this obstruction is certainly present, since that obstruction certainly relies upon it as its basis — though their essence is not different, their function differs. Hence these two underlying tendencies, in relation to the noble path's function, are respectively superior or inferior, and are eliminated at different points.

此於無覆無記性中，是異熟生，非餘三種。彼威儀等勢用薄弱，非覆所知，障菩提故。此名無覆，望二乘說，若望菩薩亦是有覆。若所知障有見、疑等，如何此種，契經說為無明住地？無明增故，總名無明，非無見等。如煩惱種立見一處、欲、色、有愛四住地名，豈彼更無慢、無明等。

Among the unobscured-neutral states, this obstruction belongs to the karmically-resultant kind, not to the other three, since deportment and the like are too weak in function to obscure the object of knowledge or obstruct bodhi. It is called "unobscured" with reference to the two vehicles; with reference to bodhisattvas, however, it is, in fact, obscuring.

If the cognitive obstruction includes view, doubt, and the rest, how can the sūtras call its seed "the ground of ignorance"? Because ignorance predominates, it is called, in general, "ignorance" — not that view and the rest are absent. Just as the afflictive obstruction's seed is named the four grounds of the single ground of view, and of desire, form, and craving-for-existence — does this mean these lack conceit, ignorance, and the rest?

如是二障分別起者，見所斷攝；任運起者，修所斷攝。二乘但能斷煩惱障，菩薩俱斷。永斷二種，唯聖道能；伏二現行，通有漏道。菩薩住此資糧位中，二麁現行雖有伏者，而於細者及二隨眠，止、觀力微，未能伏滅。

Of these two obstructions, whatever is discriminated is eliminated on the path of seeing; whatever is spontaneously arisen is eliminated on the path of cultivation. Those of the two vehicles are capable of eliminating only the afflictive obstruction; bodhisattvas eliminate both. Permanently eliminating both kinds is possible only through the noble path; suppressing both kinds' manifest activity is possible through a mundane path as well. A bodhisattva abiding at this stage of provisioning, though capable of suppressing the coarse manifest activity of both obstructions, still has, with respect to their subtler forms and their two underlying tendencies, only feeble calming and insight, and is not yet capable of suppressing or eliminating them.

Reading note: These definitions of the afflictive obstruction (煩惱障) and cognitive obstruction (所知障) are among the most important in the entire treatise, since nearly everything in the remaining fascicles' account of the path is organized around eliminating them. The distinction is worth holding precisely: the afflictive obstruction is what blocks liberation from suffering — the 128 fundamental afflictions built on self-attachment — and is what the two vehicles (śrāvakas and solitary buddhas) are capable of eliminating on their own. The cognitive obstruction is subtler still: it is what blocks bodhi, full awakening, specifically because it obscures the object of knowledge itself, and only a bodhisattva's path addresses it. This is why the treatise insists a bodhisattva must eliminate both, while an arhat's liberation, genuine as it is, leaves the cognitive obstruction untouched — the difference between being free of suffering and being fully, perfectly awake to reality.

The tempering point about "ground of ignorance" is a useful piece of exegetical method that recurs throughout the treatise: a scriptural name that highlights one element (ignorance, here) does not mean the other elements grouped under it are absent — it simply names whichever is most prominent, the same reasoning already used elsewhere for why some mental factors get named individually while structurally similar ones do not.

Merit and Wisdom; Three Moments of Discouragement 釋「福智」二種勝行 · 三退屈

Chinese Source

此位未證唯識真如，依勝解力修諸勝行，應知亦是解行地攝。所修勝行，其相云何？略有二種：謂福及智，諸勝行中慧為性者皆名為智，餘名為福。且依六種波羅蜜多，通相皆二，別相前五說為福德，第六智慧；或復前三唯福德攝，後一唯智；餘通二種。復有二種：謂利自、他所修勝行，隨意樂力一切皆通自他利行，依別相說，六到彼岸、菩提分等，自利行攝；四種攝事、四無量等，一切皆是利他行攝。如是等行差別無邊，皆是此中所修勝行。

Ru-Yi Translation

At this stage, one has not yet realized Consciousness-Only's suchness, but relies on the power of resolve to cultivate various superior practices — it should be understood that this, too, is included within the stage of resolve-and-practice. What is the character of the superior practices cultivated at this stage? Broadly speaking, there are two: merit and wisdom. Among the various superior practices, whatever has discernment as its nature is called wisdom; the rest are called merit. With reference to the six perfections, their general character involves both, but, in their specific character, the first five are said to be merit, and the sixth, wisdom; or else the first three are included exclusively within merit, and the last exclusively within wisdom, with the remaining two extending to both.

There are, further, two other kinds: superior practices cultivated for benefiting oneself and benefiting others. According to the power of one's own intention, every such practice can, in fact, extend to benefiting both; but, according to their specific character, the six perfections and the factors of awakening and the like are included within benefiting oneself, while the four means of attraction and the four immeasurables and the like are all included within benefiting others. The distinctions among such practices are boundless, and all are included among the superior practices cultivated at this stage.

此位二障雖未伏除，修勝行時有三退屈，而能三事練磨其心，於所證、修勇猛不退：一、聞無上正等菩提廣大深遠，心便退屈，引他已證大菩提者，練磨自心勇猛不退。二、聞施等波羅蜜多甚難可修，心便退屈，省己意樂能修施等，練磨自心勇猛不退。三、聞諸佛圓滿轉依極難可證，心便退屈，引他麁善況己妙因，練磨自心勇猛不退。由斯三事練磨其心，堅固熾然，修諸勝行。

Although the two obstructions are not yet suppressed or removed at this stage, three kinds of discouragement can arise while cultivating these superior practices — yet three corresponding methods of tempering the mind allow one to remain courageous and unwavering in what is to be realized and cultivated.

First, on hearing that unsurpassed, perfect awakening is vast, profound, and far-reaching, the mind may grow discouraged — one tempers one's own mind into courageous steadfastness by calling to mind others who have already realized great bodhi.

Second, on hearing that generosity and the other perfections are extremely difficult to cultivate, the mind may grow discouraged — one tempers one's own mind into courageous steadfastness by reflecting on one's own capacity and willingness to cultivate generosity and the rest.

Third, on hearing that the buddhas' perfect transformation of their basis is extremely difficult to realize, the mind may grow discouraged — one tempers one's own mind into courageous steadfastness by comparing the coarse virtues achievable by others against one's own more subtle causes.

Through these three methods of tempering the mind, one becomes firm and ardent, cultivating the various superior practices.

Reading note: The three moments of discouragement are a strikingly practical, almost pastoral passage for such a technical treatise — a direct acknowledgment that the bodhisattva path is genuinely daunting, and a set of concrete countermeasures rather than mere exhortation to "try harder." Each remedy works the same way: it does not deny the difficulty, but relativizes it, either by pointing to others who have already succeeded, by turning attention inward to one's own actual willingness rather than an abstract standard, or by reframing one's present, ordinary efforts as already a kind of "subtle cause" worth taking seriously next to more visible but coarser achievements. This is recognizable as basic psychological wisdom about sustaining long-term effort toward a goal that cannot be reached quickly, wrapped in the treatise's own technical vocabulary.
