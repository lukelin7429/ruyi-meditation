Fascicle 4 · 成唯識論卷第四

The Four Foods, and the Deepest Absorption

Fascicle 4 opens by finishing what fascicle 3 began: the last three of the ten purely rational arguments for the ālayavijñāna's existence — from the four kinds of food that sustain a living being, through the treatise's most sustained and technical argument (that this consciousness alone explains why "cessation-attainment" is not literal unconsciousness), to the final argument from moral cause-and-effect. The treatise then closes this entire line of proof with its own summary judgment.

The Argument From the Four Foods 依教廣成分 · 證有此識 · 正理（八）· 四食

Fascicle 3 closed with the seventh of ten purely rational arguments for the ālayavijñāna's existence. The eighth turns to a basic sūtra teaching: that every sentient being depends on food to stay alive.

Four Kinds of Food 段食 · 觸食 · 意思食 · 識食

Chinese Source

又契經說：一切有情皆依食住，若無此識，彼識食體不應有故。謂契經說食有四種：一者、段食，變壞為相。謂欲界繫香、味、觸三，於變壞時能為食事。由此色處非段食攝，以變壞時，色無用故。二者、觸食，觸境為相，謂有漏觸纔取境時，攝受喜等，能為食事。此觸雖與諸識相應，屬六識者食義偏勝，觸麁顯境，攝受喜、樂及順益捨，資養勝故。三、意思食，希望為相。謂有漏思與欲俱轉，希可愛境，能為食事。此思雖與諸識相應，屬意識者食義偏勝，意識於境，希望勝故。四者、識食，執持為相。謂有漏識由段、觸、思勢力增長，能為食事。此識雖通諸識自體，而第八識食義偏勝，一類相續執持勝故。由是《集論》說此四食，三蘊、五處、十一界攝。此四能持有情身命，令不壞斷，故名為食。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, the sūtras speak of all sentient beings as sustained by food. Without this consciousness, the very substance of that "consciousness food" could not exist. The sūtras speak of four kinds of food:

First, edible food, characterized by decomposing. This refers to the desire realm's scent, taste, and touch, which serve as food precisely as they decompose; visible form is not included among edible food, since form serves no purpose in the process of decomposing.

Second, contact food, characterized by contact with an object. This refers to contaminated contact, which, the very moment it takes hold of an object, gathers in delight and the like, and so serves as food. Although this contact is associated with all the consciousnesses, its function as food is especially pronounced when it belongs to the six sense-consciousnesses, since their contact grasps its object coarsely and vividly, gathering in joy, pleasure, and beneficial equanimity, and so nourishes especially well.

Third, mental-volition food, characterized by anticipation. This refers to contaminated volition, which arises together with desire, anticipating some agreeable object, and so serves as food. Although this volition is associated with all the consciousnesses, its function as food is especially pronounced when it belongs to the mental consciousness, since the mental consciousness anticipates its object especially strongly.

Fourth, consciousness food, characterized by grasping and holding. This refers to contaminated consciousness which, through the accumulated power of edible, contact, and volitional food, serves as food. Although this consciousness is common to the essential nature of all the consciousnesses, its function as food is especially pronounced in the eighth consciousness, since it alone grasps and holds in a single, continuous kind especially well.

Hence the Abhidharmasamuccaya says these four foods are included within three aggregates, five sense-fields, and eleven elements. These four sustain a sentient being's body and life, keeping them from decaying and breaking off — hence they are called "food."

段食唯於欲界有用，觸、意思食雖遍三界而依識轉，隨識有無。眼等轉識有間、有轉，非遍、恒時能持身命，謂無心定、睡眠、悶絕、無想天中有間斷故，設有心位，隨所依、緣、性、界、地等有轉易故，於持身命，非遍、非恒。

Edible food is useful only in the desire realm. Contact food and mental-volition food, though pervading all three realms, operate in dependence on consciousness, following whatever consciousness is present or absent. The eye-consciousness and the other evolving consciousnesses are interrupted and keep changing, and so cannot sustain body and life universally and constantly: they are interrupted in mindless meditative absorption, in sleep, in unconsciousness, and in the heaven of no-thought; and even in states where mind is present, they keep changing according to their supporting basis, condition, moral nature, realm, and stage — so, in sustaining body and life, they are neither universal nor constant.

諸有執無第八識者，依何等食？《經》作是言：「一切有情皆依食住。」非無心位過去、未來識等為食；彼非現、常；如空花等，無體用故。設有體用非現在攝，如虛空等，非食性故，亦不可說入定心等與無心位有情為食，住無心時彼已滅故，過去非食已極成故。又不可說無想定等不相應行即為彼食，段等四食所不攝故，不相應法非實有故。有執滅定等猶有第六識，於彼有情能為食事。彼執非理，後當廣破。

For those who hold there is no eighth consciousness — what, then, serves as food during a mindless state? The sūtra says: "All sentient beings are sustained by food." It cannot be past or future consciousness that serves as food during a mindless state, since these are neither present nor constant, being, like a flower blooming in empty space, without real substance or function. And even granting them some substance or function, if that is not included in the present moment, they would be like empty space, not the kind of thing food can be. Nor can one say that the mind which entered absorption serves as food for the being now in a mindless state, since, once abiding in that mindless state, that prior mind has already ceased — and it is already well established that something past cannot serve as food. Nor can one say that the forces disjoined from mind, such as the attainment of no-thought, themselves serve as that food, since they are not included among the four foods already listed, and disjoined forces have no real substance to begin with. Some hold that even in cessation-attainment and the like, a sixth consciousness remains present, capable of serving as food for that being — this view is unreasonable, and will be refuted at length below.

又彼應說生上二界無漏心時，以何為食？無漏識等破壞有故，於彼身、命不可為食。亦不可執無漏識中有有漏種能為彼食，無漏識等猶如涅槃，不能執持有漏種故。復不可說上界有情身、命相持即互為食，四食不攝彼身、命故，又無色無身，命無能持故，眾同分等無實體故。由此定知異諸轉識，有異熟識一類恒、遍執持身、命，令不壞斷。世尊依此，故作是言：一切有情皆依食住。

Furthermore, they ought to explain: when a being born in the upper two realms gives rise to an uncontaminated mind, what then serves as food? An uncontaminated consciousness works to break down contaminated existence, and so cannot serve as food for that being's body and life. Nor can one hold that an uncontaminated consciousness contains contaminated seeds capable of serving as its food, since an uncontaminated consciousness, being like nirvāṇa itself, cannot hold contaminated seeds at all. Nor, again, can one say that in the upper realms body and life simply sustain each other mutually as food, since body and life are not themselves included among the four foods; and in the formless realm there is no body at all, so life would have nothing there to sustain it — and a "shared class of being," as an abstract designation, has no real substance either. By this it can be known with certainty that, distinct from the evolving consciousnesses, there is a ripening consciousness — single in kind, constant, and all-pervading — which grasps and holds body and life, keeping them from decaying and breaking off. It is on this basis that the World-Honored One made the statement: all sentient beings are sustained by food.

唯依「取蘊」建立有情，佛無有漏，非有情攝，說為有情依食住者，當知皆依示現而說。既異熟識是勝食性，彼識即是此第八識。

"Sentient being" is established only with reference to the grasped aggregates — a Buddha, having nothing contaminated, is not included among sentient beings; wherever it is said that a Buddha, too, is sustained by food, this should be understood as spoken only for the sake of a provisional appearance. Since it is the ripening consciousness, then, that has the pre-eminent nature of food, that consciousness is precisely this eighth consciousness.

Reading note: "Food" here means something much broader than what we eat — the treatise defines it as whatever actually keeps a body and a life from falling apart, moment to moment. Edible food is the obvious case; but contact, anticipation, and the grasping-and-holding of consciousness itself are all, on this analysis, equally food, because they equally nourish continued existence. The argument's real target, though, is narrower and sharper: whatever "consciousness food" is, it has to be present at literally every moment a being is alive, including the moments — dreamless sleep, deep absorption, the heaven of no-thought — where the six ordinary consciousnesses have gone dark. Something has to still be quietly "eating" in those gaps, or a being would starve out of existence every time it fell asleep.

The formless-realm case, again, is where the argument bites hardest: a being there has an uncontaminated mind and no body at all in the ordinary sense, and the treatise walks through every candidate — past consciousness, disjoined forces, mutual body-life support — showing none of them can coherently serve as food. Only a single, constant, all-pervading ripening consciousness threads every one of these needles at once.
