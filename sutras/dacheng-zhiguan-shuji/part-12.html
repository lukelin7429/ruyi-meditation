Cutting Off and Attaining, and the Activity of Calming-and-Contemplation

Part 12 finishes the fourth round, what calming-and-contemplation cuts off and attains, and gives the whole of the fifth, its activity, except for the closing verses. Within the dependent nature, the samādhis "like illusions and transformations" are only partly attained by contemplation and completed by calming. In the real nature, contemplation establishes the mind, and calming lets the movement of thought cease at its first arising, so that the abiding ground of ignorance is ended and great awakening appears. A general discussion follows. Obstructions are not removed by direct opposition, since understanding and delusion never meet; understanding perfumes the root consciousness, as incense perfumes a foul garment, and Dixian applies this to reciting the Buddha's name with wandering thoughts. Understanding perfumes the mind with no further means, as a wave moves the water. The stages of the three-nature practice are set out vertically and horizontally in terms of the distinct and perfect teachings. In the fifth round the long ornate passage on the activity of contemplation is read phrase by phrase as offerings, spiritual powers, and the eight phases of a buddha's life, with the seven places and nine assemblies of the Avataṃsaka. The part closes with double negation and illumination, the dependent nature as the basis of activity, and the steps a beginner takes before acting.

The Discriminated Nature: Cutting Off and Attaining in the Practice of Contemplation (continued)

Chinese Source

此明分別性觀行除障得益也。不知境虛，境謂一切諸法。執實之心，心謂一念。即此不知境虛執實之一念，便是無明妄想。無明者，果時無明惑也。妄想者，發業無明。即是業也。且不知境虛，執以為實，便是為境所繫。苦也。為境所繫而不能解脫，非苦而何？噫！即此一念，惑業苦三完全具足矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

This explains the removal of obstruction and attainment of benefit of the practice of contemplation of the discriminated nature. "Does not know that objects are illusory": "objects" means all dharmas. "The mind that grasps them as real": "mind" means a single thought. This very single thought that does not know that objects are illusory and grasps them as real is ignorance and deluded thinking. "Ignorance" is the delusion of ignorance in the fruit phase. "Deluded thinking" is the ignorance that initiates karma; that is, it is karma. Moreover, not knowing that objects are illusory and grasping them as real is being bound by objects; this is suffering. Being bound by objects and unable to be liberated — what is that if not suffering? Ah! In this single thought, delusion, karma, and suffering, all three, are completely present.

Chinese Source

能解執虛為實，是無明妄想，即是觀行成者。謂自心中，實能覺悟。非僅聞知名義。且非僅一時覺，餘時復不覺。然後謂之能解也。故能解即是觀行成。而觀成即是除障。所謂以觀成故，能除無明妄想上迷妄是也。何謂迷妄之上迷妄？謂不知下，正釋其義。蓋執實之心，已是迷妄。此為前一重。而不知彼是迷妄，即復是迷，且又執為非迷，即復是妄。是因不能解知之故，又增一重迷妄矣。非迷妄上之迷妄乎？

Ru-Yi Translation

"One understands that grasping the illusory as real is ignorance and deluded thinking; this is the accomplishment of the practice of contemplation": this means that within one's own mind one truly awakens to it, not merely hearing and knowing the names and meanings; and not merely awakening at one moment and at other moments again not awakening; only then is it called being able to understand. Therefore being able to understand is the accomplishment of the practice of contemplation, and the accomplishment of contemplation is the removal of obstruction. This is what is meant by "Because contemplation is accomplished, one can remove the confused delusion upon ignorance and deluded thinking." "What is 'confused delusion upon confused delusion'?" From "Not knowing" on, it directly explains the meaning. For the mind that grasps reality is already confused delusion; this is the earlier layer. Not knowing that it is confused delusion is again confusion, and further grasping it as not confusion is again delusion. Because one cannot understand and know it, a further layer of confused delusion is added. Is this not confused delusion upon confused delusion?

Chinese Source

今既解知前一重是迷妄。雖前一重病根，未能便除，而能覺知其非。是第二重迷妄已除矣。故曰此是除障。夫不知執實為非，則無明妄想，日益增長，能障修止。今能解知其非，便是發覺初心。堪能進修分別性止行，以漸除無明妄想。其為益也多矣。故曰即是得益也。此書凡說修功處，極為細密。須澄心體會之，受用無盡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, since one understands and knows that the earlier layer is confused delusion, although the root of the illness of the earlier layer cannot yet be removed at once, one can awaken to and know its wrongness; then the second layer of confused delusion has already been removed. Therefore it says, "This is the removal of obstruction." If one does not know that grasping reality is wrong, ignorance and deluded thinking grow daily and can obstruct the cultivation of calming. Now that one can understand and know its wrongness, this is the initial mind of awakening; one is able to advance and cultivate the practice of calming of the discriminated nature, so as gradually to remove ignorance and deluded thinking. Its benefit is great. Therefore it says, "this is the attainment of benefit." Wherever this book speaks of the work of cultivation, it is extremely detailed and precise. One must clarify the mind and personally comprehend it; its benefit is inexhaustible.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Further, this confused delusion upon confused delusion is shown again by a simile. It is like a person confused about east, who takes it for west; this is deluded grasping. This is one layer of confused delusion. Another person tells him: "You are now confused and deluded, taking east for west." This person still thinks: "What I see is not confused delusion." Because he does not know, grasping it as not confusion is again deluded thinking. This is confused delusion arising again upon confused delusion. What fault does this person have? He has the fault of turning his back on home and wandering wildly. If this person, though not yet awakened, simply accepts the other's words and believes and knows that his own mind is confused and deluded, then he has no confused delusion upon confused delusion. What benefit does this person gain? Although his confused delusion is not yet awakened, he gains the benefit of facing home.

Chinese Source

大師血心片片。恐人不解迷妄上迷妄之義，復舉迷方喻以顯之。迷東為西，喻執虛為實。此是第一重迷妄也。我所見者，非是迷妄，執為非迷。喻不解執實之非。此第二重迷妄上迷妄也。過失喻障。背家浪走，喻背覺合塵，流浪生死。未醒悟及迷妄未醒，喻未能除無明妄想。信知自心是迷妄，喻能解執實之非。向家之益者。家喻自性不生不滅。謂既知執實之非，便是一念迴光。從此進修止行，可以漸趣涅槃也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Great Master's heart's blood, piece by piece! Fearing that people would not understand the meaning of confused delusion upon confused delusion, he again raises the simile of confusion about direction to reveal it. Being confused about east and taking it for west stands for grasping the illusory as real; this is the first layer of confused delusion. "What I see is not confused delusion," grasping it as not confusion, stands for not understanding the wrongness of grasping reality; this is the second layer, confused delusion upon confused delusion. "Fault" stands for obstruction. "Turning his back on home and wandering wildly" stands for turning one's back on awakening and uniting with the dust, wandering in birth-and-death. "Not yet awakened" and "his confused delusion is not yet awakened" stand for not yet being able to remove ignorance and deluded thinking. "Believes and knows that his own mind is confused and deluded" stands for being able to understand the wrongness of grasping reality. "The benefit of facing home": "home" stands for the self-nature that neither arises nor ceases. That is, once one knows the wrongness of grasping reality, this is a single thought of turning the light around; advancing from here to cultivate the practice of calming, one can gradually approach nirvāṇa.

Chinese Source

雖未證知諸法是虛。但能知境虛是無明，執實是妄想者，即常不信己之所執。堪能進修止行，漸趣涅槃。若都不知此者。即當隨流苦海，增長三毒，背失涅槃寂靜之舍也。此明分別性中，觀行斷得之義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Although one may realize and know that dharmas are illusory, if one simply knows that [regarding] the illusoriness of objects there is ignorance, and that grasping them as real is deluded thinking, one never trusts what one grasps, and is able to advance and cultivate the practice of calming and gradually approach nirvāṇa. If one does not know this at all, one follows the flow in the sea of suffering, increases the three poisons, and turns one's back on and loses the dwelling of the tranquility of nirvāṇa. This explains the meaning of the cutting off and attaining of the practice of contemplation within the discriminated nature.

Chinese Source

分別性止行成時，方為了了證知諸法是虛。故能遣其實執。今觀行中，但是發覺初心，解知執實之非耳。故曰未證知。能知境虛，當云能知不了境虛。文中脫「不了」二字。不了境虛，是惑。故曰是無明。而執之為實，便是發業。故曰是妄想也。能知合上信知。漸趣涅槃，合上向家之益。涅槃者，不生不滅之義。都不知者。既不知是迷妄，復執為非迷。具有兩重迷妄。故曰都不知，隨流苦海等，合上背家浪走之過。寂靜者，自性本寂也。末句，結成。

Ru-Yi Translation

Only when the practice of calming of the discriminated nature is accomplished does one realize and know clearly that dharmas are illusory, and so can dispel the grasping of reality. Now, in the practice of contemplation, there is only the initial mind of awakening, understanding and knowing the wrongness of grasping reality; therefore it says, "not yet realized and known." "Knows . the illusoriness of objects" should read "knows the not-understanding of the illusoriness of objects"; the two words "not understanding" have dropped out of the text. Not understanding that objects are illusory is delusion; therefore it says, "is ignorance." Grasping them as real is initiating karma; therefore it says, "is deluded thinking." "Knows" matches "believes and knows" above; "gradually approach nirvāṇa" matches "the benefit of facing home" above. "Nirvāṇa" means neither arising nor ceasing. "Does not know this at all": not knowing that it is confused delusion, and further grasping it as not confusion, one has both layers of confused delusion; therefore it says, "does not know . at all." "Follows the flow in the sea of suffering" and so on matches "the fault of turning his back on home and wandering wildly" above. "Tranquility" is the self-nature's being originally tranquil. The final sentence concludes it.

Ru-Yi Translation

(H) Second, explaining the cutting off and attaining of the practice of calming.

Chinese Source

所言分別性中止行除障得益者。謂依彼觀行作方便故，能知諸法本來無實。實執止故。即是能除果時迷事無明，及以妄想也。復於貪瞋，漸已微薄。雖有罪垢，不為業繫。設受苦痛，解苦無苦。即是除障。復依此止，即能成就依他性中觀行故。無塵智用，隨心行故。即是得益。此明分別性中止行除障得益。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: As for the removal of obstruction and attainment of benefit of the practice of calming within the discriminated nature: by using that practice of contemplation as an expedient, one can know that dharmas are originally without reality. Because the grasping of reality is calmed, one can remove ignorance in the fruit phase deluded about phenomena, together with deluded thinking. Further, greed and anger have gradually become slight; although there are stains of offense, one is not bound by karma. Even if one undergoes pain, understanding suffering, there is no suffering; this is the removal of obstruction. Further, in dependence on this calming one can accomplish the practice of contemplation within the dependent nature, so that the function of the wisdom of no sense-objects follows the mind's activity; this is the attainment of benefit. This explains the removal of obstruction and attainment of benefit of the practice of calming within the discriminated nature.

Ru-Yi Translation

Here too it first explains removing obstruction, then explains attaining benefit. "By using that practice of contemplation as an expedient": relying on the power of understanding in the earlier practice of contemplation, one removes the confused delusion upon confused delusion, and only then can one advance to cultivate the practice of calming; therefore it says, "as an expedient." The words "the grasping of reality is calmed" involve great effort. As was said, "why do I still want to trust this foolish, deluded mind? Therefore, going against it ." Only when one takes it up in this way thought after thought, ashamed of oneself and reproaching oneself, is it called going against it. "Going against" is like saying turning one's back on the dust. Then one forcibly contemplates that dharmas are only marks of the mind, to break the grasping of objects; and further one contemplates that this contemplating mind too has no real thought, to break the grasping of the mind. "Going against" and "forcibly contemplating" are precisely where the work is done. For while turning one's back on the dust on one side, one must contemplate on the other. If contemplative illumination is not forceful, the deluded mind is not broken, and then one cannot go against it either. The crucial point lies entirely in the word "forcibly." One must be resolute and decisive. Whether meeting objects and conditions, or while walking, standing, sitting, or lying down, one takes up the critical phrase "all is made by the mind, only illusory and without reality," contemplating and illuminating thought after thought, never slackening. Doing so for a long, long time, the familiar becomes unfamiliar and the unfamiliar becomes familiar; only then can one suddenly awaken and know that dharmas originally have not a single real one. Being able to know "originally" is turning one's initial awakening toward original awakening; only then is the understanding-nature accomplished. When understanding is accomplished, deluded feelings are broken; when they are broken, it is called the grasping of reality being calmed. If one does not put in painful effort, how can one know "originally"? If one does not know "originally," how can the grasping of reality be calmed? Think on it, think on it!

Ru-Yi Translation

"One can remove ignorance in the fruit phase" and so on: for being able to know that objects are illusory is not being deluded by objects; the grasping mind's ceasing is having no deluded thinking. Therefore it says, "one can remove . ignorance deluded about phenomena, together with deluded thinking." (Ignorance in the fruit phase is ignorance deluded about phenomena, also called ignorance deluded about objects.) "Further, greed and anger have gradually become slight" and so on: since ignorance deluded about phenomena has been removed, one cuts off delusion about conditions; therefore greed and anger are also thin. Why? Formerly, because one did not understand that objects were illusory and grasped them as real, one gave rise to greed and anger because of agreeable and disagreeable objects. Now one does not grasp them as real; therefore [greed and anger] are slight. It says "slight" because although one is not deluded by objects and does not grasp them as real, one is not without slight likes and dislikes; therefore it says only "slight." This makes clear that one has removed only the discriminative self-grasping, while the innate self-grasping is subdued but not yet cut off. Because the innate self-grasping is not yet cut off, one is not without stains of offense; because the discriminative self-grasping has been removed, one is not bound by karma. Pain is a disagreeable object; now that one understands that objects are illusory, one is right then free and unconstrained and does not suffer from it. Therefore it says, "this is the removal of obstruction."

"Attainment of benefit": the practice of calming here, because it clearly knows that objects are illusory, can dispel the grasping of reality; and the illusory marks of dharmas are the dependent nature. Therefore "in dependence on this calming one can accomplish the practice of contemplation within the dependent nature." Further, once ignorance in the fruit phase and deluded thinking have been removed, mind-consciousness changes its name to the wisdom of no sense-objects. "Follows the mind's activity" means that the wisdom of no sense-objects gives rise to function following the mind. This is the attainment of benefit. The final sentence concludes it.

The Dependent Nature: Cutting Off and Attaining in the Practice of Contemplation

Chinese Source

（庚）二約依他性。又二。（辛）初明觀行斷得。二明止行斷得。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(G) Second, in terms of the dependent nature, again in two: (H) first, explaining the cutting off and attaining of the practice of contemplation; second, explaining the cutting off and attaining of the practice of calming. Now the first.

Chinese Source

次明依他性中止觀斷得者。初明觀門。此觀門者，與分別性中止門不異。而少有別義。此云何也？謂彼中止門者，必緣一切法是虛，故能遣無明。無明滅故，執實妄心即止。然此緣虛之遣，即此依他性中觀門。更無異法。是故彼止若成，此觀亦就。但彼由緣虛故，能滅實執。故名為止。此即由知無實故，便解諸法是虛，因緣集起不無心相。故名為觀。彼以滅實破執為宗。此以立虛緣起為旨。故有別也。以是義故。除障義同。得益稍別。別者是何？謂依此觀方便進修，堪入依他性止門。又復分成如幻化等三昧。故言得益。此是依他性中觀行斷得也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, explaining the cutting off and attaining of calming-and-contemplation within the dependent nature. First, the gate of contemplation is explained. This gate of contemplation is not different from the gate of calming within the discriminated nature, yet it has a slightly distinct meaning. What is this? There, the gate of calming necessarily takes all dharmas as illusory, and so can dispel ignorance; because ignorance ceases, the deluded mind that grasps reality is calmed. Yet this dispelling by taking [dharmas] as illusory is precisely the gate of contemplation within this dependent nature; there is no other dharma. Therefore when that calming is accomplished, this contemplation is also achieved. But that one, because it takes [dharmas] as illusory, can extinguish the grasping of reality, and so is called calming; this one, because it knows there is no reality, understands that dharmas are illusory, gathered and arising from causes and conditions, the marks of the mind not absent, and so is called contemplation. That one takes extinguishing reality and breaking grasping as its purpose; this one takes establishing the illusory and conditioned arising as its aim; therefore they differ. For this reason the meaning of removing obstruction is the same, but the attainment of benefit differs slightly. What is the difference? Relying on this contemplation and advancing in cultivation by expedient means, one is able to enter the gate of calming of the dependent nature. Further, one partially accomplishes the illusion-like, transformation-like, and other samādhis; therefore it is called attaining benefit. This is the cutting off and attaining of the practice of contemplation within the dependent nature.

Chinese Source

首句，總標。初明觀門下，言與上止門，同中有別。謂彼至亦就，明其所以不異也。緣者緣念。緣虛之遣，猶言緣虛遣實。謂前止門中，必須緣念諸法是虛，方能遣其執實。而唯虛無實，便是依他性。所以緣虛遣實，止行成時，依他性觀門亦就。故曰不異也。但彼至別也，明其少有別義。因緣集起不無心相者，即如能觀之智便是因。所觀之法便是緣。此因緣和合之虛狀，乃唯心所作也。故曰心相。謂前由緣念諸法是虛故，滅其無明執實。是彼以破除執實之妄心為宗旨，故名為止。今由了解諸法是虛故，知其不無心相。是此以建立緣起之虛相為宗旨，故名為觀。故曰有別也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first sentence announces it in general. "First, the gate of contemplation is explained" and what follows says that it is the same as the gate of calming above yet has a difference. From "There" to "is also achieved" makes clear why they are not different. "Takes" means taking as the object of thought. "Dispelling by taking [dharmas] as illusory" is like saying taking them as illusory and dispelling reality. That is, in the earlier gate of calming, one must take dharmas as illusory in thought, and only then can one dispel the grasping of reality. And "only illusory and without reality" is precisely the dependent nature. Therefore, taking them as illusory and dispelling reality, when the practice of calming is accomplished, the gate of contemplation of the dependent nature is also achieved. Therefore it says, "not different." From "But" to "they differ" makes clear that there is a slightly distinct meaning. "Gathered and arising from causes and conditions, the marks of the mind not absent": for example, the contemplating wisdom is the cause and the dharma contemplated is the condition; the illusory forms of this combination of cause and condition are only made by the mind; therefore it says "marks of the mind." Root text: Question: When obstructions are removed, are they removed by direct opposition, or removed by the perfuming of wisdom and understanding? Answer: They cannot be removed by direct opposition. Why? While the deluded mind is present, there is not yet understanding; when understanding arises, the delusion has already ceased; earlier and later do not see each other; therefore they cannot be removed by direct opposition. Thus, although because a single thought of the understanding mind arises the function of delusion does not arise, yet because the seeds of delusion and defilement in the root consciousness remain and have not ceased, when the single thought of the understanding mind ceases, the function of delusion arises again. When understanding and delusion arise alternately in this way, thought after thought, the function of understanding gradually perfumes the mind, increasing the power of the nature of understanding and forming seeds of the function of understanding. When that function of understanding has perfumed and formed seeds, it can perfume those seeds of delusion and defilement so that they diminish portion by portion. It is like perfuming foul-smelling clothes with incense: as the fragrance clings to the clothes portion by portion, the foul smell ceases portion by portion. The seeds of delusion are also like this: as the seeds of understanding are formed portion by portion, delusion ceases portion by portion. Because the seeds of delusion cease portion by portion, the function of delusion gradually weakens; because the seeds of understanding increase portion by portion, the function of understanding grows ever stronger; this is how they are removed. It is not as the Lesser Vehicle says, removal by direct opposition, which has words but no meaning. Yet the Lesser Vehicle too in fact removes by perfuming; it simply does not know this principle.

The text says, "It is like perfuming foul-smelling clothes with incense"; one can see that the foul smell itself becomes fragrance, and it is still the original clothes, not another set of clothes. One must know that as soon as one speaks of conditioned arising, it is a dharma of the dependent nature; therefore the answer says "relying on the principle of the dependent nature." Yet the three natures have never been apart; therefore it further says "uses the other natures as well to help accomplish." "What does this mean?" and what follows directly explain its meaning. For when dharmas, existing, are not existent, then, not existing, they exist; therefore it says "does not prevent." "Does not prevent" is "form is emptiness." Since, existing, they are not existent, and yet seeming dharmas are not absent in their manifestation, one can know that dharmas of conditioned arising are the mind-nature being naturally so. For this reason the bodhisattva can give rise to function right in tranquility. The phrase "always in samādhi" refers to "right in tranquility"; the phrase "give rise to the mind and have compassionate thoughts for sentient beings" refers to "gives rise to function." This explains and completes "relying on the principle of the dependent nature." Here, "although he knows … he also knows" and so on is the help of the discriminated nature; giving rise to the mind within samādhi is the help of the real nature.

Ru-Yi Translation

"Further" and what follows explain it in more detail. That relying on the dependent nature one manifests through the universal gate and gives birth to the function of gathering in and transforming is because, relying on the discriminated nature, one knows the suffering and vexation of all sentient beings, and relying on the real nature, one knows that oneself and sentient beings are of the same substance. This shows the help in terms of the gates of contemplation. Further, that relying on the dependent nature, the three wheels being empty in substance, one does not see the marks of one who delivers and those who are delivered, is because, relying on the discriminated nature, one knows that sentient beings can all become buddhas, and relying on the real nature, one knows that self and others originally have no arising or ceasing. This shows the help in terms of the gates of calming. In sum, as for dharmas of dependent conditioned arising: if there were no function of discrimination to discern capacities, conditioned arising would have no way to come about; if there were no substance of the real nature as the support, from what would anything arise by conditions? Therefore, whether on the causal ground or at the stage of the fruit, the three natures have never been apart. The fault of ordinary beings lies only in giving rise to grasping through pervasive imagining. If one does not grasp and calculate, then one is unchanging yet following conditions, following conditions yet unchanging, and the discriminated and dependent [natures] all become wondrous function.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Further, if a bodhisattva beginning practice wishes to do something, he must first make a vow, next enter the gate of calming, then from calming give rise to contemplation; after that, whatever the mind makes is accomplished. Why must he first make a vow? To specify what he seeks and to request the addition of surpassing power. Why must he further enter calming? Because he wishes to know that all dharmas are not existent; therefore all obstructive dharmas are penetrated as he thinks. Why then give rise to contemplation from calming? Because he wishes to know that all dharmas are made from the mind; therefore whatever he would establish among all dharmas is accomplished as he thinks. A bodhisattva of long practice is not like this: he simply resolves to do something, and as he thinks it is accomplished. The buddha-tathāgatas again are not like this: they illuminate without taking objects and know without deliberating; according to what those with capacities, in resonance, should see and hear, without resolving, the matter accomplishes itself. It is like the maṇi jewel, which has no mind wishing to benefit the world, yet according to what calls upon it rains down various treasures. The Tathāgata is also like this: in whatever he does he makes no mental intention, yet he accords with what is to be benefited. This is because of perfuming through three great asaṃkhyeya eons until it is pure and ripe; therefore he can be so; there is no other dharma.

Translator's notes

Root text. Variants in Dixian's root text. Unit 24 has 謂知彼所起之相 (T1924 所起之體相, "the substance and marks that have arisen"); unit 33 has 即是熏心 (T1924 即自熏心, "they perfume the mind by themselves"); unit 58 has 純熟 (T1924 淳熟). The root English is unchanged.

Root quotations inside the commentary. Unit 25 quotes the dependent-nature calming ("one can know that all illusory marks take only the one mind as substance"); unit 28 quotes "it moves of itself in nonawakening" and the habit-energy sentence from the root's Part 1 discussion, with Dixian's 即亦隨滅 rendered "also accordingly ceases" (root English: "breaks down"); unit 37 quotes "even a beginner is not prevented from learning all three rounds together in every thought"; unit 43 quotes "all sentient beings and all buddhas share only one pure mind, the equal Dharma-body of the tathāgatagarbha." Removal by perfuming (units 30–34). 敵對除 removal by direct opposition and 智解熏除 removal by the perfuming of wisdom and understanding follow the root English. 全三障成三德 the three obstructions in their entirety forming the three virtues; 以幻除幻 removing illusion with illusion (cf. 以幻修幻 "cultivating illusion with illusion," Parts 3–4); 一心不亂 follows the site's Amitābha Sūtra, "a single, undistracted mind."

Stages (unit 37). The five abiding grounds (見一切處住地, 欲愛住地, 色愛住地, 有愛住地, 無明住地) are listed as in The Four Teachings of Tiantai (T1931); 五品 "the five grades" and the ten faiths / abidings / practices / dedications follow the glossary. Dixian glosses the root's 一相 as 一向 "straight through."

Three truths (unit 28). The fifth round (unit 43). Vimalakīrti (unit 43). "The Buddha, with a single voice, expounds the Dharma, and beings, each according to their kind, each gain understanding" follows the site's Vimalakīrti (Ratnākara's verse in "The Assembly and the Jeweled Canopy"). Dixian has 各領解 for the sūtra's 各得解.

Avataṃsaka (unit 43). Dixian's account of the seven places and nine assemblies differs from the usual eighty-fascicle scheme (which places the second, seventh, and eighth assemblies in the Hall of Universal Light on earth and the ninth in the Jeta Grove); he puts the Hall of Universal Light in the heaven of the Four Heavenly Kings and counts six heavens. Translated as he gives it. "Without rising from beneath the bodhi tree, his body pervades the ten directions": source not located in this wording.

Buddha's life (unit 43). The birth in Lumbinī under the aśoka tree, the nine dragons, the seven steps, leaving home at nineteen, and the eight śāla trees (four in bloom, four turned white) follow the popular Chinese accounts; Dixian's dates are kept.
