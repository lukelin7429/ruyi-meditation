The Substance and Form in the Pure Three Natures

Part 11 completes the third round, the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation, and opens the fourth. It begins with the end of the handkerchief-rabbit simile, where the three non-natures are read as calming, and then explains why the two gates must be practiced together: calming abandons the worldly truth and enters the three non-natures, while contemplation keeps conditioned arising and establishes the worldly truth. The other similes, and the dream analyzed in five parts, show the same pattern. The root text then turns to the pure three natures, where the object is no longer one's own five aggregates but the Three Jewels: the buddhas' bodies, voices, relics, images, and scriptures are seen as made by the mind, then as illusory and provisional marks, and finally as the one mind shared by buddhas and sentient beings. A general sorting in six questions follows: tranquility and function together, even for a beginner ("form is itself emptiness"); why there are names "buddha" and "sentient being"; why buddhas are distinct though the same; why rejoicing in others' merit benefits oneself, yet does not make practice unnecessary; whether the buddhas' virtues are real or illusory; and why the Buddha's virtues abide while sentient beings cease. The part ends as the fourth round begins with the discriminated nature.

The Simile of Illusion: The Handkerchief and the Rabbit (continued)

Chinese Source

此喻三無性以明止也。初，能知兔依巾有，唯虛無實。喻能知諸法心現，無實相性。是為無相性智。無實相性者。謂分別性所執，唯是虛狀而無實也。次，能知唯是一巾，兔本不生，有即非有。喻能知唯是淨心，虛相非有，自性無生。是為無生性智。自性無生者，自性中本無生滅也。三，能知巾是本有，不將無兔以為巾。喻能知淨心本有，不執二性之無以為心。是為無性性智。

Ru-Yi Translation

This uses a simile for the three non-natures to explain calming. First, being able to know that the rabbit exists in dependence on the handkerchief, only illusory and without reality, stands for being able to know that dharmas are manifested by the mind, without the nature of real marks. This is the wisdom of the nature of no marks. "Without the nature of real marks" means that what the discriminated nature grasps is only illusory forms, without reality. Next, being able to know that it is only one handkerchief, that the rabbit originally does not arise and, existing, is not existent, stands for being able to know that it is only the pure mind, that illusory marks are not existent and are without arising in their own nature. This is the wisdom of the nature of no arising. "Without arising in their own nature" means that within the self-nature there is originally no arising and ceasing. Third, being able to know that the handkerchief originally exists, and not taking the absence of the rabbit to be the handkerchief, stands for being able to know that the pure mind originally exists, and not grasping the absence of the two natures to be the mind. This is the wisdom of the nature of no nature.

Chinese Source

須知修觀時，了達三自性是一心之緣起，便是三無性智。即以此三智，遣除三自性之病。病除即入三無性而為止矣。故曰，此即喻三無性止門也。蓋三智為能照，三自性為所照。三智為能顯，三諦為所顯。三諦者，即三無性是。以無相性智，觀照徧計所執，當體即空，而入無相性。即顯真諦理也。以無生性智，觀照依他性虛，有即非有，而入無生性。即顯俗諦理也。以無性性智，觀照性體本有。不因無相無生，方有真實。如即兔是巾，何必無兔方為有巾也。則入無性性，而顯中諦理矣。所謂一中一切中。即二邊便是中道，無假無空而非中也。南嶽此喻，通身吐露。真善巧哉！

Ru-Yi Translation

One must know that when cultivating contemplation, comprehending that the three self-natures are the conditioned arising of the one mind is precisely the wisdom of the three non-natures. With these three wisdoms one then dispels the illness of the three self-natures; when the illness is removed, one enters the three non-natures, and this is calming. Therefore it says, "This is a simile for the gate of calming of the three non-natures." For the three wisdoms are what illuminates, and the three self-natures are what is illuminated; the three wisdoms are what reveals, and the three truths are what is revealed. The three truths are precisely the three non-natures. With the wisdom of the nature of no marks one contemplates and illuminates what is grasped by pervasive imagining as empty in its very substance, and enters the nature of no marks; this reveals the principle of the truth of the real. With the wisdom of the nature of no arising one contemplates and illuminates the illusoriness of the dependent nature, that existing, it is not existent, and enters the nature of no arising; this reveals the principle of the conventional truth. With the wisdom of the nature of no nature one contemplates and illuminates that the substance of the nature originally exists, and that it is not because of no marks and no arising that there is then the real — just as, the rabbit being itself the handkerchief, why must there be no rabbit before there is a handkerchief? — and so one enters the nature of no nature and reveals the principle of the truth of the middle. This is what is called "one middle, all middle": the two extremes are themselves the middle way, and there is no provisional and no empty that is not the middle. In this simile Nanyue lays everything bare with his whole body. How truly skillful!

Distinguishing Calming and Contemplation Together

Chinese Source

（子）止觀合辨。

Ru-Yi Translation

(K) Distinguishing calming and contemplation together.

Chinese Source

是故若欲捨離世諦。當修止門，入三無性。若欲不壞緣起，建立世諦。當修觀門，解知三性。若不修觀門，即不知世諦所以緣起。若不修止門，即不知真諦所以常寂。若不修觀門，便不知真即是俗。若不修止門，即不知俗即是真。以是義故。須依幻喻，通達三性三無性。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Therefore, if one wishes to abandon the worldly truth, one should cultivate the gate of calming and enter the three non-natures. If one wishes not to destroy conditioned arising and to establish the worldly truth, one should cultivate the gate of contemplation and understand the three natures. If one does not cultivate the gate of contemplation, one does not know why the worldly truth arises by conditions; if one does not cultivate the gate of calming, one does not know why the truth of the real is always tranquil. If one does not cultivate the gate of contemplation, one does not know that the true is the conventional; if one does not cultivate the gate of calming, one does not know that the conventional is the true. For this reason one must rely on the simile of illusion to penetrate the three natures and the three non-natures.

Chinese Source

修觀為緣起有門。修止為解脫空門。空有二門，偏即成病。必須並修。以是之故，今復止觀合辨也。合辨義有三重。重重深入。

Ru-Yi Translation

Cultivating contemplation is the gate of existence, of conditioned arising; cultivating calming is the gate of emptiness, of liberation. Of the two gates of emptiness and existence, being one-sided becomes an illness; they must be cultivated together. For this reason calming and contemplation are now distinguished together again. The meaning of distinguishing them together has three layers, going deeper layer upon layer.

Chinese Source

初義中，若不修止，即不能證入三無性。故欲捨離世諦，了脫生死者，當修止門。若不修觀，即不能解知三性。故欲建立世諦，廣度眾生者，當修觀門。此中先止後觀者。明由定生慧，自度乃能度他也。世諦即是俗諦。次義中，謂由一心建立三自性，便是世諦緣起之所以。若不知之，如何度眾生？故當修觀。泯三性入三無性，便是真諦常寂之所以。若不知之，如何了生死？故當修止。三義中，謂修觀不但知俗諦，且知真即是俗。修止不但知真諦，且知俗即是真。即者，不二之義。真俗相即，便是不住生死，不入涅槃，中道第一義諦。所謂終日不變，而終日隨緣。終日隨緣，復終日不變。是之謂妙法蓮花。以是義故下結成。謂如上來所舉之義，必須止觀並運，以解知三性。證入三無性也。若於法說未明者，便須依幻喻以通達之。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the first meaning: if one does not cultivate calming, one cannot realize and enter the three non-natures; therefore one who wishes to abandon the worldly truth and be finished with and liberated from birth-and-death should cultivate the gate of calming. If one does not cultivate contemplation, one cannot understand the three natures; therefore one who wishes to establish the worldly truth and deliver sentient beings widely should cultivate the gate of contemplation. Here calming comes first and contemplation after, to make clear that wisdom arises from concentration, and that only one who has delivered oneself can deliver others. The worldly truth is the conventional truth. In the second meaning: that the one mind establishes the three self-natures is the reason for the conditioned arising of the worldly truth; if one does not know it, how can one deliver sentient beings? Therefore one should cultivate contemplation. Dissolving the three natures and entering the three non-natures is the reason the truth of the real is always tranquil; if one does not know it, how can one be finished with birth-and-death? Therefore one should cultivate calming. In the third meaning: cultivating contemplation, one not only knows the conventional truth but also knows that the true is the conventional; cultivating calming, one not only knows the truth of the real but also knows that the conventional is the true. "Is" means nondual. The true and the conventional being identical is precisely not abiding in birth-and-death and not entering nirvāṇa: the truth of the middle way of the first meaning. This is what is called "All day unchanging, yet all day following conditions; all day following conditions, yet all day unchanging." This is called the Wondrous Dharma Lotus Flower. "For this reason" and what follows concludes it: according to the meanings raised above, one must operate calming and contemplation together so as to understand the three natures and realize and enter the three non-natures. If it is not yet clear from the explanation by dharma, one must rely on the simile of illusion to penetrate it.

Concluding by Example: The Other Similes

Chinese Source

（癸）三例結。

Ru-Yi Translation

(J) Third, concluding by example.

Chinese Source

如幻喻能通達三性三無性。其餘夢，化，影，像，水月，陽𦦨，乾城，餓鬼等喻。但是依實起虛，執虛為實者，悉喻三性。類以可知。若直以此等諸喻依實起虛故，偏喻依他性亦得也。但虛體是實，即可喻真實性。虛隨執轉，即可喻分別性。是故此等諸喻，通譬三性。解此喻法次第無相，即可喻三無性也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: As the simile of illusion can penetrate the three natures and the three non-natures, so the other similes — dreams, transformations, shadows, images, the moon in water, mirages, gandharva cities, hungry ghosts, and so on — in which the illusory arises in dependence on the real and the illusory is grasped as real, are all similes for the three natures; by analogy this can be known. If, simply because in these similes the illusory arises in dependence on the real, one takes them exclusively as similes for the dependent nature, that too is acceptable. But the substance of the illusory being real can be a simile for the real nature, and the illusory turning according to grasping can be a simile for the discriminated nature. Therefore these similes illustrate all three natures. Understanding these simile-dharmas, in order, as without marks can be a simile for the three non-natures.

Chinese Source

初謂，凡依實起虛，執虛為實等法，悉可譬喻三性。可由幻喻，類推而知。如心之現夢，山之化雲，燈下見影，鏡中現像，以及水中之月，曠野之陽𦦨，海上之乾闥婆城，謂樹橛為餓鬼等，皆可為喻。其所依之實，可喻真實性。所起之虛，可喻依他性。執虛為實，則喻分別性也。次謂，若以依實起虛之影像等，偏喻依他性者。則即就依他而辨三性，亦得。蓋虛相當體，原是實所變現，如本識中淨分。即可喻真實性。虛相隨執，轉而成為妄境，如本識中染分。即可喻分別性。末復謂，此等諸喻，既通喻三性，即可喻三無性。次第無相者。如先除分別執實，入無相性，即是無相相。次除依他執虛，入無生性，即是無生相。更除真性上橫執之真，入無性性，即是無性相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, it says that all dharmas in which the illusory arises in dependence on the real and the illusory is grasped as real can serve as similes for the three natures; this can be known by inference from the simile of illusion. For example, the mind manifesting a dream, a mountain transforming into clouds, a shadow seen under a lamp, an image appearing in a mirror, as well as the moon in water, a mirage in the open wilderness, a gandharva city over the sea, taking a tree stump for a hungry ghost, and so on — all can serve as similes. The real on which they depend can stand for the real nature; the illusory that arises can stand for the dependent nature; grasping the illusory as real stands for the discriminated nature. Next, it says that if the shadows, images, and so on, in which the illusory arises in dependence on the real, are taken exclusively as similes for the dependent nature, then distinguishing the three natures solely within the dependent is also acceptable. For the very substance of the illusory marks was originally manifested by transformation of the real, like the pure portion in the root consciousness; this can stand for the real nature. The illusory marks, following grasping, turn and become deluded objects, like the defiled portion in the root consciousness; this can stand for the discriminated nature. Finally it says that since these similes illustrate all three natures, they can also stand for the three non-natures. "In order, as without marks": for example, first removing the discriminated grasping of reality and entering the nature of no marks is the mark of no marks; next removing the dependent grasping of the illusory and entering the nature of no arising is the mark of no arising; further removing the true wrongly grasped upon the true nature and entering the nature of no nature is the mark of no nature.

The Simile of the Dream

Chinese Source

（壬）三約夢喻。

Ru-Yi Translation

(I) Third, in terms of the simile of the dream.

Chinese Source

又更分別夢喻，以顯三性三無性，譬如凡夫慣習諸法故，即於夢中心現諸法。依他性法，亦復如是。由無始已來果時無明，及以妄想，熏習真實性故。真心依熏，現於虛相果報也。彼夢裏人，為睡蓋所覆故。不能自知己身他身，皆是夢心所作，即便執為實事。是故夢裏自他種種受用得成。分別性法，亦復如是。意識為果時無明所迷故。不知自他，咸是真心依熏所作，便即妄執為實。是故自他種種受用得成也。是以經言。是身如夢，為虛妄見。虛者，即是依他性。妄者，即是分別性。此即緣起三性為觀門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Further, the simile of the dream is distinguished again to reveal the three natures and the three non-natures. It is like an ordinary person who, because of habitual familiarity with dharmas, has dharmas appear in his mind in a dream. The dharma of the dependent nature is also like this: because ignorance in the fruit phase and deluded thinking have perfumed the real nature since beginningless time, the true mind, in dependence on perfuming, manifests illusory marks and fruits of retribution. The person in the dream, being covered by the hindrance of sleep, cannot know by himself that his own body and others' bodies are all made by the dreaming mind, and grasps them as real phenomena; therefore within the dream the various enjoyments of self and others are accomplished. The dharma of the discriminated nature is also like this: mind-consciousness, deluded by ignorance in the fruit phase, does not know that self and others are all made by the true mind in dependence on perfuming, and deludedly grasps them as real; therefore the various enjoyments of self and others are accomplished. Hence a sūtra says: "This body is like a dream — it is an illusory and deluded seeing." "Illusory" is the dependent nature; "deluded" is the discriminated nature. This is the conditioned arising of the three natures, as the gate of contemplation.

Chinese Source

然此夢中所執為實者，但是夢心之相。本無有實。分別性法，亦復如是。但是虛想從心所起，本來無實。即是無相性也。又彼夢中虛相，有即非有。唯是夢心，更無餘法。依他性法，亦復如是。自他虛相，有即非有。唯是本識，更無餘法。即是無生性也。又彼夢心，即是本時覺心。但由睡眠因緣故，名為夢心。夢心之外，無別覺心可得。真實性法，亦復如是。平等無二。但以無明染法熏習因緣故，與染和合，名為本識。然實本識之外，無別真心可得。即是無性性法。此即除滅三性為止門也。以是喻故。三性三無性，即可顯了。此明止觀體狀中，約染濁三性以明止觀體狀竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Yet what is grasped as real within this dream is only a mark of the dreaming mind, originally without reality. The dharma of the discriminated nature is also like this: it is only illusory imagining arising from the mind, originally without reality; this is the nature of no marks. Further, the illusory marks in that dream, existing, are not existent; they are only the dreaming mind, and there is no other dharma. The dharma of the dependent nature is also like this: the illusory marks of self and others, existing, are not existent; they are only the root consciousness, and there is no other dharma; this is the nature of no arising. Further, that dreaming mind is the mind that was awake before; only because of the causes and conditions of sleep is it called the dreaming mind; apart from the dreaming mind no separate waking mind can be found. The dharma of the real nature is also like this: it is equal and nondual; only because of the causes and conditions of the perfuming of the defiled dharmas of ignorance does it combine with defilement and is called the root consciousness. Yet in reality, apart from the root consciousness no separate true mind can be found; this is the dharma of the nature of no nature. This is removing and extinguishing the three natures, as the gate of calming. By this simile the three natures and the three non-natures can be made clear. This completes the explanation, within the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation, of the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation in terms of the defiled and turbid three natures.

Chinese Source

此中可分為五。初兩句，標。次，譬如至觀門也，喻三性。三然此至止門也，喻三無性。四以是喻故兩句，結成。五末兩句，總結。初標者。就上八喻中，更將夢喻分晰說之。俾三性三無性，益得顯明也。次喻三性中，由慣習而現夢，喻由妄熏而現相也。睡蓋所覆者，謂睡眠能蓋覆心性。此是五蓋之一。五蓋者。貪欲蓋，瞋恚蓋，睡眠蓋，掉悔蓋，疑蓋也。果時無明等，解已見前。是身如夢，猶言人生若夢。見之似有，全為虛妄。故曰為虛妄見。此兩句見維摩詰經。此中但舉分別依他，未及真實者。蓋以能夢之人醒時心，喻真實性，可不煩言而知也。

Ru-Yi Translation

This can be divided into five. The first two sentences announce it. Second, from "It is like" through "as the gate of contemplation" is the simile for the three natures. Third, from "Yet" through "as the gate of calming" is the simile for the three non-natures. Fourth, the two sentences "By this simile" conclude it. Fifth, the final two sentences conclude in general. First, announcing it: from among the eight similes above, the simile of the dream is taken again and analyzed, so that the three natures and the three non-natures become all the clearer. Next, in the simile for the three natures, dreams appearing through habitual familiarity stand for marks appearing through deluded perfuming. "Covered by the hindrance of sleep" means that sleep can cover the mind-nature. This is one of the five hindrances. The five hindrances are the hindrance of covetous desire, the hindrance of anger, the hindrance of sleep, the hindrance of agitation and remorse, and the hindrance of doubt. Ignorance in the fruit phase and the rest have been explained above. "This body is like a dream" is like saying "Human life is like a dream": what is seen seems to exist but is entirely illusory and deluded; therefore it says, "it is an illusory and deluded seeing." These two lines are found in the Vimalakīrti Sūtra. That here only the discriminated and the dependent are raised, without reaching the real, is because the waking mind of the person who dreams stands for the real nature, which can be known without need of words.

Chinese Source

三喻三無性中，夢心即是本時覺心者。謂夢中心，即是本來之醒時心。並非二心。故夢心之外，無別醒心可得。喻分別依他，即是真實。不可執二無以為心也。故曰平等無二。其餘文相易知。四結中謂以此夢喻止觀體狀，更可顯了矣。永嘉大師曰：夢裏明明有六趣。覺後空空無大千。三性三無性，一迷一悟，正如一夢一醒。南嶽以夢喻止觀體狀，巧妙已極。上來法說喻說，真可謂有義皆彰。無微弗顯。明得此大乘止觀法門，尚有何法不明哉！五，總結染濁三性之止觀體狀已竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, in the simile for the three non-natures, "that dreaming mind is the mind that was awake before" means that the mind in the dream is the originally waking mind; they are not two minds. Therefore apart from the dreaming mind no separate waking mind can be found. This stands for the discriminated and the dependent being themselves the real; one may not grasp the absence of the two as the mind. Therefore it says, "equal and nondual." The rest of the text is easy to understand. Fourth, the conclusion says that by this simile of the dream the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation can be made all the clearer. Great Master Yongjia says: "In the dream, clearly, there are the six destinies; after awakening, empty and empty, there is no great chiliocosm." The three natures and the three non-natures, one deluded and one awakened, are just like dreaming and waking. Nanyue's use of the dream as a simile for the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation is skillful in the extreme. The explanation by dharma and the explanation by simile above can truly be said to make every meaning manifest and leave nothing subtle unrevealed. If one understands this dharma-gate of Mahāyāna calming-and-contemplation, what dharma could remain unclear? Fifth, it concludes in general that the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation of the defiled and turbid three natures is complete.

The Pure Three Natures

Chinese Source

（庚）二約清淨三性。分三。（辛）初分科。二各釋。三通簡。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(G) Second, in terms of the pure three natures, in three: (H) first, dividing the sections; second, explaining each; third, sorting out in general. Now the first.

Chinese Source

次明清淨三性中止觀體狀。就中亦有三番。一明分別性中止觀體狀。二明依他性中止觀體狀。三明真實性中止觀體狀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation within the pure three natures. Within this there are also three rounds: first, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation within the discriminated nature; second, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation within the dependent nature; third, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation within the real nature.

Chinese Source

淨分三性與染分三性，名同相異者。蓋名從性。其性既同，故名亦同。相從體。（體謂三性當體。非體相用之體也。）體有清濁，故相有清濁也。是故欲明其相之所以不同，不可不知其體。其義上來雖已散見。今復為撮要對舉以明之，則條理秩然，更易明了矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

That the three natures of the pure portion and the three natures of the defiled portion are the same in name but different in mark is because names follow the nature: since their natures are the same, the names too are the same. Marks follow the substance. (Substance here means the very substance of the three natures, not the substance of substance, mark, and function.) Ru-Yi Translation

This sorts out and shows that the Buddha's virtues and sentient beings differ as accordant and contrary, and therefore their abiding and ceasing are not the same. Because the worldly form-dharma was used as an example above, the questioner raises a difficulty back about form-dharmas. He means: the pure virtues of the buddhas and the form-dharmas of sentient beings are alike, existing, not existent; why is one permanently abiding and the other arising and ceasing? One must know that the buddhas have emerged from obstruction; the accomplishment of their pure virtues is revealing phenomena by being identical with principle; this is the accordant function; therefore they abide permanently. Sentient beings are within obstruction; the accomplishment of their form-dharmas is hiding principle through delusion about phenomena; this is the contrary function; therefore they arise and cease. Therefore, although both alike, existing, are not existent and, not existing, are existent, this does not prevent what abides permanently from abiding permanently and what arises and ceases from arising and ceasing. "Does not prevent" means that one abiding permanently and the other arising and ceasing is no hindrance to the meaning that both alike, existing, are not existent and, not existing, are existent. Why press the difficulty? The final sentence is a concluding phrase, as can be known.

Chinese Source

既修止觀，必有利益。利益之大小。因功行淺深，次第不同。故繼止觀體狀，而明止觀斷得。令學者明其功不唐捐，事必漸除也。斷者，斷惑。即是除障。得即得益。因修止觀，所以除障。因其障除，所以得益也。蓋從分別性觀行，先除少分障，得少分益。由是步步精進，次第以至真實性止行，則一轍入修滿足。既除滿分障，便得滿分益矣。須知所得之益，自性本具。但向為無明業識妄想所障故，義等於失。今以修習止觀故。障分分除，本性即分分顯。假名為得耳。非由外得。所謂但復本來性，更無一法新是也。又上明修中，開為染淨兩重。今明斷得，合為一重者。因染淨止觀，修法雖別，而除障得益則無異。故不別開耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since one cultivates calming-and-contemplation, there must be benefit. The size of the benefit differs in order according to the depth of one's effort and practice. Therefore, following the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation, he explains the cutting off and attaining of calming-and-contemplation, so that learners will understand that their effort is not spent in vain and that [the obstructions] will certainly be removed gradually. "Cutting off" is cutting off delusion, which is removing obstructions; "attaining" is attaining benefit. Because one cultivates calming-and-contemplation, obstructions are removed; because the obstructions are removed, benefit is attained. For from the practice of contemplation of the discriminated nature, one first removes a small portion of obstruction and attains a small portion of benefit; from this, advancing vigorously step by step, in order up to the practice of calming of the real nature, one enters cultivation along a single track, fulfilled. Once the full measure of obstruction is removed, one attains the full measure of benefit. One must know that the benefit attained is originally possessed by the self-nature; but because formerly it was obstructed by ignorance, karmic consciousness, and deluded thinking, it was as good as lost. Now, because one cultivates calming-and-contemplation, as the obstructions are removed portion by portion, the original nature is revealed portion by portion, and this is provisionally called attaining. It is not attained from outside. As it is said, "Only restoring the original nature; not a single dharma is new." Further, in the explanation of cultivation above, it was opened into two layers, defiled and pure; now, in explaining cutting off and attaining, they are combined into one layer, because although the methods of cultivation of defiled and pure calming-and-contemplation differ, removing obstructions and attaining benefits are no different; therefore they are not opened separately.

Chinese Source

初明分別性中所除障者。謂能解不知境虛執實之心，是無明妄想故。即是觀行成。以觀成故，能除無明妄想上迷妄。何謂迷妄之上迷妄？謂不知迷妄是迷妄，即是迷也。以此迷故，即執為非迷，復是妄想。此一重迷妄，因前一重上起。故名迷妄之上迷妄也。是故行者雖未能除不了境虛執實之心。但能識知此心是癡妄者，即是能除癡妄之上迷妄也。此是除障。以除障故，堪能進修止行。即是得益。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: First, explaining the obstructions removed within the discriminated nature: one understands that the mind that does not know that objects are illusory and grasps them as real is ignorance and deluded thinking; this is the accomplishment of the practice of contemplation. Because contemplation is accomplished, one can remove the confused delusion upon ignorance and deluded thinking. What is "confused delusion upon confused delusion"? Not knowing that confused delusion is confused delusion is confusion; because of this confusion, one grasps it as not confusion, and this again is deluded thinking. This layer of confused delusion arises upon the previous layer; therefore it is called confused delusion upon confused delusion. Therefore, although the practitioner cannot yet remove the mind that does not understand that objects are illusory and grasps them as real, if he can recognize and know that this mind is foolish delusion, he can remove the confused delusion upon foolish delusion. This is the removal of obstruction; because the obstruction is removed, he is able to advance and cultivate the practice of calming; this is the attainment of benefit.

Translator's notes

Root text. Vimalakīrti (unit 9).
