The Defiled Nature, Defiled Phenomena, and the One Tathāgatagarbha Shared by All

Part 5 continues Dixian's commentary on the nonempty tathāgatagarbha. It first completes the pure side: the pure karma that perfumes the mind (teaching, understanding, practice, and fruition) is itself made by the one mind, illustrated by the root text's simile of gold that already holds the nature of a finished vessel. It then turns to the defiled side. The mind is said to possess a defiled nature able to "give birth to" and "make" birth-and-death, and the root text answers seven doubts: how the ordinary can become holy, how two opposed natures can coexist, how beginningless defilement can end, why pure karma removes defiled karma and not the reverse, and how ignorance, which goes against the mind, can still perfume it. Dixian reads these through the distinction between the unchanging substance of the nature and its function that follows conditions, and through the parable of the destitute son. A section on defiled phenomena, with the mirror of the Awakening of Faith, closes the first topic. The second topic, the oneness and difference of the storehouse's substance, opens with six sections; the first establishes that all beings and buddhas share one tathāgatagarbha, and Dixian adds a note on the hundred realms, thousand suchnesses, and three worlds.

The Pure Virtues That Emerge from Obstruction (Commentary)

Chinese Source

障者，煩惱業報三障也。眾生未有修德。為三障所障，而不能出，故淨德不顯。然雖未顯，而實本具。故曰具出障淨德。即此淨心三句。言因其體具淨德之性，故能攝持淨熏之力。若非性具，即不能攝。淨業者，一切出世功德皆是。如聽大乘止觀，亦淨業也。由熏力故兩句。言此體具之淨德，非藉淨業熏習之力。雖曰本具，用亦不顯。此義云何下，正明熏顯之義。因地者，凡未證究竟覺果以前諸位次，皆名因地。因者修因也。加行者精進之義。猶言加功修行。梵語般若，華言智慧。梵漢雙舉，故曰般若智業。如聞法如看經，皆智業也。三種智，即一切智、道種智、一切種智。眾生尚未熏顯，故但曰智性。必以智業熏之，令其生起智用，是謂起用顯現。若智用顯現，即是般若波羅蜜也。此即如來果德上三種大智慧，成為自受用報身者是也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Obstruction" means the three obstructions of affliction, karma, and retribution. Sentient beings do not yet have cultivated virtue; obstructed by the three obstructions, they cannot emerge, and so the pure virtues are not revealed. Yet although not revealed, they are really originally possessed. Hence it says "possessing the pure virtues that emerge from obstruction." The three phrases from "this pure mind" mean: because its substance possesses the nature of the pure virtues, it can hold the force of pure perfuming. If they were not inherently possessed, it could not hold it. "Pure karma" is all world-transcending merit; listening to [the teaching of] Mahāyāna Calming-and-Contemplation, for example, is also pure karma. The two phrases from "through the force of perfuming" mean: if the pure virtues possessed by this substance did not rely on the force of the perfuming of pure karma, then although they are said to be originally possessed, their function would not be revealed. "What does this mean?" and what follows directly explains the meaning of being revealed by perfuming. "Causal ground": all the stages before realizing the fruit of ultimate awakening are called the causal ground. "Cause" means the cause of cultivation. "Preparatory practice" (加行) has the meaning of diligence, as if to say applying added effort in cultivation and practice. The Sanskrit prajñā is in Chinese 智慧, "wisdom"; Sanskrit and Chinese are both given, hence "the karma of prajñā-wisdom." Hearing the Dharma and reading the sūtras are both wisdom-karma. "The three kinds of wisdom" are the wisdom that knows all things, the wisdom of the paths, and the wisdom knowing all modes. Since in sentient beings they have not yet been perfumed into manifestation, it says only "wisdom-nature." It must be perfumed by wisdom-karma so that it gives rise to the function of wisdom; this is called "causing them to arise as function and manifest." When the function of wisdom manifests, that is prajñā-pāramitā. These are the three great wisdoms of the Tathāgata's fruition-virtue, which form the reward-body of self-enjoyment.

五波羅蜜者。即是布施，持戒，忍辱，精進，禪定。因般若上已言之，茲故但舉其五。在因地為五度，到果位方名波羅蜜。波羅蜜之義，翻為所作成辦。亦翻彼岸到。今係就因地之加行而言，故曰因地五波羅蜜。言五則不止一種，故曰等也。此之五度，為成就一切種智之功行。故曰一切種行。而對上智業言之乃是福業。能熏眾生性具之三十二相，八十隨形好。故曰熏於相好之性。迨至熏令用顯，即是如來相好之報，成為他受用，及勝劣應身是也。夫智福二性，因熏顯用而成果德，名為出障。然此果德，雖有智福二相之別。而智福之性體，則唯是一心。以心體具此出障果德故，名為不空。勿因體是一心之言，誤認為係就心體明其不空也。以心體平等，無所謂空不空故。又由熏力故，德用顯現，是增上緣。體具性淨功德為因，成如來智慧相好為果，是親因緣。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The five pāramitās" are giving, keeping the precepts, forbearance, diligence, and dhyāna. Because prajñā has already been mentioned above, here only the five are named. In the causal ground they are the five perfections (五度); only at the stage of fruition are they called pāramitās. The meaning of pāramitā is rendered "what is to be done is accomplished," and is also rendered "the other shore reached." Since it now speaks of the preparatory practice of the causal ground, it says "the five pāramitās … in the causal ground." Saying "five" means there is not just one kind; hence it says "and all other." These five perfections are the meritorious practice for accomplishing the wisdom knowing all modes; hence it says "kinds of practice" (一切種行). Compared with the wisdom-karma above, they are blessing-karma. They can perfume the thirty-two marks and the eighty subsidiary excellent features inherently possessed by sentient beings; hence it says "perfume the nature of the marks and excellent features." When the perfuming causes the function to manifest, that is the Tathāgata's reward of the marks and excellent features, which forms the [body of] enjoyment for others and the superior and inferior response-bodies. The two natures of wisdom and blessing, revealing their function through perfuming and forming fruition-virtue, are called "emerged from obstruction." Yet although this fruition-virtue has the distinction of the two marks of wisdom and blessing, the nature-substance of wisdom and blessing is only the one mind. Because the mind's substance possesses this fruition-virtue that emerges from obstruction, it is called not empty. Do not, because of the words "their substance is the one mind," mistakenly think that it is explaining nonemptiness in terms of the mind's substance; for the mind's substance is equal, and there is no such thing as empty or not empty. Further, "through the force of perfuming, the function of these virtues manifests": this is the dominant condition. The inherently pure merits possessed by the substance serving as cause, and the Tathāgata's wisdom and marks and excellent features being formed as fruit: this is the direct causal condition.

What Perfumes Is Also Possessed Only by the Mind

Chinese Source

（午）二明能熏亦唯心所具。

Ru-Yi Translation

(Q) Second, explaining that what perfumes is also possessed only by the mind.

Chinese Source

問曰：能熏淨業。為從心起。為心外別有淨法，以為能熏耶？答曰：能熏之法，悉是一心所作。此義云何？謂所聞教法，悉是諸佛菩薩心作。諸佛心，菩薩心，眾生心是一。故教法即不在心外也。復以此教熏心解性，性依教熏以起解用，故解復是心作也。以解熏心行性，性依解熏以起行用，故行復是心作也。以行熏心果性，性依行熏起於果德，故果復是一心作也。以此言之。一心為教，乃至一心為果，更無異法也。以是義故。心體在凡之時，本具解行果德之性。但未為諸佛真如用法所熏，故解等未顯用也。若本無解等之性者，設復熏之，德用終不顯現也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Question: Does the pure karma that perfumes arise from the mind? Or is there a separate pure dharma outside the mind that perfumes? Answer: The perfuming dharmas are all made by the one mind. What does this mean? The teaching-dharmas one hears are all made by the minds of buddhas and bodhisattvas; since the minds of buddhas, the minds of bodhisattvas, and the minds of sentient beings are one, the teaching-dharmas are not outside the mind. Further, this teaching perfumes the mind's nature of understanding; the nature, in dependence on the perfuming of the teaching, gives rise to the function of understanding; therefore understanding too is made by the mind. Understanding perfumes the mind's nature of practice; the nature, in dependence on the perfuming of understanding, gives rise to the function of practice; therefore practice too is made by the mind. Practice perfumes the mind's nature of fruition; the nature, in dependence on the perfuming of practice, gives rise to the fruition-virtues; therefore the fruition too is made by the one mind. Speaking in this way, the one mind is the teaching, up to the one mind is the fruition; there is no other dharma. For this reason, when the mind's substance is in the ordinary state, it originally possesses the natures of understanding, practice, and fruition-virtue; it is only that it has not yet been perfumed by the dharmas of the function of suchness of the buddhas, so understanding and the rest have not yet manifested their function. If it originally lacked the nature of understanding and the rest, then even if perfumed, the function of these virtues would never manifest.

Chinese Source

如世真金。本有器樸之性，乃至具有成器精妙之性。但未得椎鍛而加，故器樸等用不現。後加以鉗椎，樸器成器次第現也。若金本無樸器成器之性者。設使加以功力，樸用成用終難顯現。如似壓沙求油，鑽水覓火，鍛冰為器，鑄木為瓶，永不可成者。以本無性故也。是故論言。若眾生無佛性者，設使修道，亦不成佛。以是義故。淨心之體，本具因行果德性也。依此性故，起因果之德。是故此德，唯以一心為體。一心具此淨德，故以此心為不空如來藏也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: It is like genuine gold, which originally has the nature of a vessel blank, and even the nature of a finely finished vessel; it is only that it has not yet been hammered and forged, so the functions of the blank and the rest do not appear. Later, when tongs and hammer are applied, the blank and the finished vessel appear in order. If gold originally lacked the natures of blank and finished vessel, then even if effort were applied, the functions of blank and finished vessel would in the end hardly appear — like pressing sand to seek oil, drilling ice to look for fire, forging ice into a vessel, or casting wood into a bottle: these can never be accomplished, because originally there is no such nature. Therefore a treatise says: "If sentient beings had no buddha-nature, then even if they cultivated the path they would not attain buddhahood." For this reason, the substance of the pure mind originally possesses the natures of the virtues of cause, practice, and fruition. In dependence on these natures, the virtues of cause and fruit arise; therefore these virtues take only the one mind as substance. Because the one mind possesses these pure virtues, this mind is taken as the nonempty tathāgatagarbha.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The previous section, the pure virtues that emerge from obstruction, speaks in terms of the fruition-virtue inherently possessed. This section, the pure karma that perfumes, speaks in terms of the causal practice inherently possessed. Since we know that it is because the substance possesses inherent purity that it can hold pure perfuming and so form fruition-virtue, we should know that it is also because the substance possesses inherent purity that it can bring forth what perfumes and so form causal practice; it is not that there is a separate pure dharma outside the mind that perfumes. Hence it says "the perfuming dharmas are all made by the one mind." "What does this mean?" and what follows directly explains the meaning that what perfumes comes from the mind. Knowing this, the principle that the mind is the nonempty tathāgatagarbha becomes still clearer. The explanation can be roughly divided into four. First, from "The teaching-dharmas one hears" to "there is no other dharma," it explains inherent possession in terms of the four dharmas of teaching, understanding, practice, and fruition. Second, from "For this reason" to "would never manifest," it reveals it generally in terms of the dharma. Third, from "It is like genuine gold" to "because originally there is no such nature," it explains it skillfully by simile. Fourth, from "Therefore a treatise says" to the end, it cites proof and concludes as a whole. Now the first. The Buddhadharma taught in all the sūtras and teachings was originally made by the one mind of the buddhas and bodhisattvas. And mind, Buddha, and sentient beings — these three are without distinction. Therefore the minds of the buddhas are the minds of sentient beings. Since the mind is one, how could the teaching-dharmas made by the minds of the buddhas be outside the minds of sentient beings? You must know that all the Buddhadharma is originally a treasure stored in sentient beings' own house, originally complete within the one mind. Having understood this principle, whenever we read the sūtras or hear the teachings, we should turn the light back on our own minds. If one does not turn back to contemplate and illuminate within, but clings to the written words and runs outside in search, one is like a fool who opens his eyes to look for his eyes, and on the contrary takes another's as his own family's eyes. Is that not pitiable?

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the teaching-dharmas are not outside the mind, then gaining understanding by the teaching, giving rise to practice by understanding, and realizing the fruit when practice is perfected are all made by the mind; this is easy to know. The character 解 ("understanding") should be read in the departing tone, meaning "comprehending"; it comes from within. If read in the rising tone, it means analyzing from outside, which is not the meaning here. "The nature of understanding" refers to the original nature that can comprehend; "the function of understanding" refers to the effective function that can comprehend. "The nature of practice" and the rest follow this pattern. When the ultimate fruit is realized, the effective function is accomplished and the pure virtues manifest; therefore it does not say "the function of fruition" but "the fruition-virtues," namely the three virtues of the Dharma-body, prajñā, and liberation. "Speaking in this way" and what follows concludes by revealing the idea that causal practice and fruition-virtue are only the one mind; hence it says "there is no other dharma." In the second, revealing it generally in terms of the dharma, it first reveals it directly by original possession and then reveals it inversely by "if it originally lacked"; this can be seen in the text. In sum, the active and the acted-upon are originally not two; principle and phenomena are originally one suchness. What is inherently possessed in principle is possessed in what is brought about in actual phenomena; what is brought about in actual phenomena is brought about from what is inherently possessed in principle. If one understands this, then all day long the whole nature giving rise to cultivation is all day long the whole of cultivation lying in the nature; all day long the whole of cultivation lying in the nature is all day long the whole nature giving rise to cultivation. This principle is what practitioners should urgently realize; otherwise it is grasping dharmas outside the mind. "The dharmas of the function of suchness" refers to the teaching-dharmas, because all teaching-dharmas are the virtuous function of the buddhas' suchness. The next "function of these virtues" refers to the virtuous function of suchness originally possessed by the person himself. What is originally possessed is the seed, serving as cause; being perfumed into arising by the dharmas of the function of the buddhas' suchness is the dominant condition; practice perfected and virtue revealed is the fruit.

Chinese Source

三以喻巧明中，如世真金下，喻上本具一段文。真金喻淨心。器樸喻解性。成器喻果性。未及行性，故用乃至二字括之。樸，坯形也。未成精器，故曰器樸。椎鍛而加，及加以鉗椎，喻淨業熏力。鉗椎者，鉗而椎之也。樸器成器次第現者，喻解用行用果德次第現也。若金本無樸器下，喻上反顯一段文。壓沙成油等，更引他喻以明，若非性具不能修顯之義。四引證總結中，初引論為證。修道指因言。成佛指果言。謂若非本具佛性，即無因行果德也。以是義故下結成。先承引證之義，結顯心體本具因果淨性，故起因果德用。次以唯以一心為體，一心具此淨德兩語。總結上來兩科所言顯果修因，種種淨事，皆藏體之本具。以明如來藏不空也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the third, explaining skillfully by simile, "It is like genuine gold" and what follows is a simile for the passage above on original possession. Genuine gold is a simile for the pure mind; the vessel blank, for the nature of understanding; the finished vessel, for the nature of fruition. It does not get as far as the nature of practice, so it covers it with the words "and even." 樸 is a rough shape; not yet a fine vessel, hence "vessel blank." "Hammered and forged" and "when tongs and hammer are applied" are similes for the perfuming force of pure karma. "Tongs and hammer" means gripping with tongs and striking with a hammer. "The blank and the finished vessel appear in order" is a simile for the function of understanding, the function of practice, and the fruition-virtues appearing in order. "If gold originally lacked the natures of blank and finished vessel" and what follows is a simile for the passage above that reveals it inversely. "Pressing sand to make oil" and the rest cite further similes to make clear the meaning that, without inherent possession, nothing can be revealed by cultivation. In the fourth, citing proof and concluding as a whole, it first cites a treatise as proof. "Cultivated the path" refers to the cause; "attain buddhahood" refers to the fruit. It means that if buddha-nature were not originally possessed, there would be no causal practice and no fruition-virtue. "For this reason" and what follows concludes. First, continuing the meaning of the cited proof, it concludes by revealing that the mind's substance originally possesses the pure natures of cause and fruit and therefore gives rise to the virtuous functions of cause and fruit. Next, with the two statements "these virtues take only the one mind as substance" and "the one mind possesses these pure virtues," it concludes as a whole that the various pure matters spoken of in the two sections above — revealing the fruit and cultivating the cause — are all originally possessed by the storehouse's substance, so as to make clear that the tathāgatagarbha is not empty.

Possessing the Defiled Nature

Ru-Yi Translation

Chinese Source

初明具足染性者。此心雖復平等離相，而復具足一切染法之性。能生生死。能作生死。是故經云：「心性是一，云何能生種種果報？」即是能生生死。又復經言。即是法身流轉五道，說名眾生。即是能作生死也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: First, explaining that it fully possesses the defiled nature: although this mind is equal and free of marks, it also fully possesses the nature of all defiled dharmas, able to give birth to birth-and-death and able to make birth-and-death. Therefore a sūtra says: "The mind's nature is one; how can it give birth to various fruits of retribution?" This is being able to give birth to birth-and-death. This is called unobstructed perfect interfusion.

此義云何下，舉喻反顯其理。何謂反顯？泥團具眾微塵，有似藏體具無量法。然而藏體，非如泥團之比也。何以故下，正釋其非。蓋泥團由眾塵和合而幻有。藏體則自起諸法之幻用。故如來藏是真實法。非如泥團為幻有法也。當知眾塵本各有別質。故泥團之具有眾塵，即各成差別。諸法乃並無實體。故如來藏雖具足諸法，而圓融無二也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"What does this mean?" and what follows gives a simile to reveal the principle inversely. What is revealing it inversely? A lump of clay containing many motes of dust seems like the storehouse's substance possessing measureless dharmas; yet the storehouse's substance is not comparable to a lump of clay. "Why?" and what follows directly explains why it is not. A lump of clay exists illusorily through the combination of many motes, whereas the storehouse's substance itself gives rise to the illusory functions of all dharmas. Therefore the tathāgatagarbha is a true and real dharma, not an illusorily existing dharma like a lump of clay. You should know that the many motes originally each have their own separate stuff; therefore the lump of clay, containing many motes, is differentiated in each of them. All dharmas have no real substance at all; therefore, although the tathāgatagarbha fully possesses all dharmas, it is perfectly interfused and nondual.

The Whole of Principle Forming Phenomena

Chinese Source

（申）二明全理成事。

Ru-Yi Translation

(S) Second, explaining the whole of principle forming phenomena.

Chinese Source

是故如來之藏，全體是一眾生一毛孔性。全體是一眾生一切毛孔性。如毛孔性。其餘一切所有世間，一一法性，亦復如是。如一眾生世間法性。一切眾生所有世間一一法性，一切諸佛所有出世間一一法性，亦復如是。是如來藏全體也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Therefore the whole substance of the tathāgatagarbha is the nature of a single pore of a single sentient being, and its whole substance is the nature of all the pores of a single sentient being. As with the nature of pores, so too with the nature of each and every other worldly dharma whatsoever. As with the worldly dharma-natures of a single sentient being, so too with each worldly dharma-nature of all sentient beings and each world-transcending dharma-nature of all buddhas: each is the whole substance of the tathāgatagarbha.

Chinese Source

此約不變隨緣，舉如來藏全體而成眾法，以明其無礙圓融也。由一眾生之一毛孔，推至一切諸佛出世法，皆是如來藏全體起用。非少分藏性所成。故舉一眾生一毛孔性，全體即是一切毛孔性。乃至一眾生世間法性，全體即是一切世間出世間一一法之性也。何以故？理外無事故。

Ru-Yi Translation

This is in terms of unchanging yet following conditions: taking up the whole substance of the tathāgatagarbha forming all dharmas, to explain its unobstructed perfect interfusion. From one pore of one sentient being up to the world-transcending dharmas of all buddhas, all are the whole substance of the tathāgatagarbha giving rise to function, not formed by a small part of the storehouse-nature. Therefore, taking up the nature of one pore of one sentient being, its whole substance is the nature of all pores; and so on up to the worldly dharma-nature of one sentient being, whose whole substance is the nature of each and every worldly and world-transcending dharma. Why? Because there are no phenomena outside principle.

The Whole of Phenomena Including Principle

Chinese Source

（申）三明全事攝理。

Ru-Yi Translation

(S) Third, explaining the whole of phenomena including principle.

Chinese Source

是故舉一眾生一毛孔性。即攝一切眾生所有世間法性，及攝一切諸佛所有出世間法性。如舉一毛孔性，即攝一切法性。舉其餘一切世間一一法性，亦復如是。即攝一切法性。如舉世間一一法性，即攝一切法性。舉一切出世間所有一一法性，亦復如是。即攝一切法性。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Therefore, taking up the nature of a single pore of a single sentient being includes the natures of all the worldly dharmas of all sentient beings, and includes the natures of all the world-transcending dharmas of all buddhas. As taking up the nature of one pore includes all dharma-natures, so taking up each of the other worldly dharma-natures likewise includes all dharma-natures. As taking up each worldly dharma-nature includes all dharma-natures, so taking up each world-transcending dharma-nature likewise includes all dharma-natures.

Chinese Source

此約隨緣不變，舉一一法即攝如來藏全體，以明其無礙圓融也。由一眾生之一毛孔性，推至一切諸佛出世法，一一皆攝如來藏全體。非攝少分。故舉一眾生一毛孔性，即攝一切世出世間法之全性也。乃至舉一切出世間一一法性，亦復如是即攝一切法性。何以故？事外無理故。

Ru-Yi Translation

This is in terms of following conditions yet unchanging: taking up each and every dharma includes the whole substance of the tathāgatagarbha, to explain its unobstructed perfect interfusion. From one pore of one sentient being up to the world-transcending dharmas of all buddhas, each one includes the whole substance of the tathāgatagarbha, not a small part of it. Therefore taking up the nature of one pore of one sentient being includes the whole nature of all worldly and world-transcending dharmas; and so on up to taking up each world-transcending dharma-nature, which likewise includes all dharma-natures. Why? Because there is no principle outside phenomena.

The Whole of Phenomena Including Phenomena

Chinese Source

（申）四明全事攝事。

Ru-Yi Translation

(S) Fourth, explaining the whole of phenomena including phenomena.

Chinese Source

又復如舉一毛孔事。即攝一切世出世事。如舉一毛孔事，即攝一切事。舉其餘世間出世間中一切所有，隨一一事，亦復如是即攝一切世出世事。何以故？謂以一切世間出世間事，即以彼世間出世間性為體故。是故世間出世間性，體融相攝故。世間出世間事，亦即圓融相攝無礙也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Further, taking up the phenomenon of a single pore includes all worldly and world-transcending phenomena. As taking up the phenomenon of one pore includes all phenomena, so taking up any phenomenon whatever among all the other worldly and world-transcending [dharmas] likewise includes all worldly and world-transcending phenomena. Why? Because all worldly and world-transcending phenomena take those worldly and world-transcending natures as substance. Therefore, because worldly and world-transcending natures are merged in substance and include each other, worldly and world-transcending phenomena too are perfectly interfused, including each other without obstruction.

Chinese Source

上兩科，明全理而成一一事，一一事即攝全理。所謂理事無礙也。今明事事皆全理之事，故一事可攝一一事。所謂事事無礙也。夫事相雖差，性體則一。性體既圓攝，事相亦即圓攝。故無礙也。須知凡夫處處執著，所以事事成礙。如證人空成羅漢。便耳能見，眼能聞，納須彌於芥子等。可知事相虛假，原無質礙耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

The two sections above explained the whole of principle forming each and every phenomenon, and each and every phenomenon including the whole of principle; this is what is called principle and phenomena being unobstructed. Now it explains that every phenomenon is a phenomenon of the whole principle, so one phenomenon can include each and every phenomenon; this is what is called phenomena and phenomena being unobstructed. Phenomenal marks differ, but the nature-substance is one; since the nature-substance includes perfectly, phenomenal marks too include perfectly; hence they are unobstructed. You must know that ordinary beings cling everywhere, so that every phenomenon becomes an obstruction. One who realizes the emptiness of persons and becomes an arhat, by contrast, can then see with the ears and hear with the eyes, put Sumeru into a mustard seed, and so on. We can know that phenomenal marks are false and provisional and originally have no material obstruction.

Concluding That Difference Is Itself No Difference

Chinese Source

（申）五結成差即無差。

Ru-Yi Translation

(S) Fifth, concluding that difference is itself no difference.

Chinese Source

是故經言。心佛及眾生，是三無差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Therefore a sūtra says: "Mind, Buddha, and sentient beings — these three are without distinction."

Chinese Source

全藏性而成事用。全事用即是藏性。雖性為能成，事為所成，似有能所之差。實則藏體平等，本無差別。既是無差成差，當知差即無差。因引《華嚴經》心佛眾生，三無差別為證。夫由一心而成佛成眾生，無差之差也。然眾生是佛心內之眾生。佛是眾生心內之佛。差即無差矣。明得理事無差之差，差即無差。則藏體一異無礙圓融之義，罄無餘蘊矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

The whole storehouse-nature forms phenomenal functions; the whole of phenomenal function is the storehouse-nature. Although the nature is what forms and phenomena are what is formed, so that there seems to be a difference of active and acted-upon, in reality the storehouse's substance is equal and originally without difference. Since it is no difference forming difference, you should know that difference is itself no difference. Therefore it cites the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, "mind, Buddha, and sentient beings — these three are without distinction," as proof. Becoming a buddha and becoming sentient beings from the one mind is the difference that arises from no difference. Yet sentient beings are sentient beings within the Buddha's mind, and the Buddha is the buddha within sentient beings' minds: difference is itself no difference. Having understood that principle and phenomena are no difference yet differentiated, and that difference is itself no difference, the meaning of the oneness and difference of the storehouse's substance, unobstructed and perfectly interfused, is exhausted without remainder.
