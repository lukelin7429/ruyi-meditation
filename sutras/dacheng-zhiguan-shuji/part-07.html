Times and Eons, Counteracting Delusion, Shared and Unshared Marks, and Why the Mind Is Relied Upon

Part 7 finishes the long account of the nonempty tathāgatagarbha and opens the second question of the root text's support of calming-and-contemplation. It begins with Dixian's comment on the pore-and-city dialogue and the companion dream dialogue, in which a meal's time holds many years, showing that long and short, like large and small, are made by the whole one mind. The fifth topic then asks why counteracting wisdom extinguishes delusion as phenomena while both remain as natures, and why one being cannot receive six bodies at once although many beings and sages exist together. Dixian's key phrase is "always the same and always distinct": sameness is inherent virtue, distinctness cultivated virtue. The visitor's proposal that each being has a separate pure mind is refuted by the Dharma-body of shared marks and by examples from the Lotus, Sudhana, dreams, and bodhisattvas' vows, with practical advice on stray thoughts in reciting the Buddha's name. The sixth topic, the consciousness of shared and unshared marks, explains how beings see the same or different worlds, and Dixian defends rebirth in Sukhāvatī against "mind-only" objections. The part closes with the reason the mind is relied upon: it is the root of all dharmas and holds the two meanings of tranquility and function.

The Pore and the Great City

Chinese Source

心能作一小毛孔。復能作一大城。此二皆是心性全體所作。不增不減。故毛孔之性，即是大城之性。既相即矣，寧不相攝。所以芥子納須彌，而芥子不增。須彌入芥子，而須彌不減。此約緣起之相言，故說大說小。實則心體上，並無大小之相。相生心亦不生。相滅心亦不滅。唯一湛寂常恆之不變真體而已。須知心性作一毛孔，復作大城。譬如心性作一眾生，復能作佛。心性原無大小。若著眾生相，心即非小似小矣。此修行人，所以須離相也。了了見者，心中了了而見。非用眼見。兩閉目字著眼。此明行人當返觀內照，不得向外馳求也。其餘文相易知。

Ru-Yi Translation

The mind can make one small pore and can also make a great city. Both of these are made by the whole substance of the mind-nature, neither increased nor reduced. Therefore the nature of the pore is the nature of the great city. Since they are mutually identical, how could they not include each other? That is why the mustard seed contains Sumeru and the mustard seed is not increased, and Sumeru enters the mustard seed and Sumeru is not reduced. This is said in terms of the marks of conditioned arising, so it speaks of large and of small. In reality, on the mind's substance there are no marks of large and small at all. When marks arise, the mind does not arise; when marks cease, the mind does not cease. There is only the deep, tranquil, constant, and eternal unchanging true substance. You should know that the mind-nature making a pore and also making a great city is like the mind-nature making a sentient being and also being able to make a buddha. The mind-nature originally has no large and small; but if one is attached to the mark of a sentient being, the mind, though not small, seems small. This is why practitioners must be apart from marks. "Clearly see" means seeing clearly within the mind, not seeing with the eyes. Look closely at the two words "close your eyes." This makes clear that practitioners should turn their contemplation back and illuminate within, and must not run outward seeking. The rest of the wording of the text is easy to understand.

Times and Eons Include Each Other

Chinese Source

（申）二示時劫相攝相即。

Ru-Yi Translation

(S) Second, showing the mutual inclusion and mutual identity of times and eons.

Chinese Source

我今又問汝。汝嘗夢不？外人曰：我嘗有夢。沙門曰：汝曾夢見經歷十年五歲時節事不？外人曰：我實曾見歷涉多年，或經旬月時節，亦有晝夜，與覺無異。沙門曰：汝若覺已。自知睡經幾時。外人曰：我既覺已。借問他人。言我睡始經食頃。沙門曰：奇哉！於一食之頃，而見多年之事。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: "Now I ask you further: have you ever dreamed?" The visitor said: "I have dreamed." The śramaṇa said: "Have you ever dreamed of passing through periods of ten years or five years?" The visitor said: "I have indeed seen myself pass through many years, or through periods of ten days or a month, with days and nights as well, no different from waking." The śramaṇa said: "Once you awoke, did you yourself know how long you had slept?" The visitor said: "Once I awoke, I asked others, and they said I had slept only the time of a meal." The śramaṇa said: "Remarkable! In the time of a single meal, you saw the events of many years.

Chinese Source

以是義故。據覺論夢。夢裏長時，便則不實。據夢論覺。覺時食頃，亦則為虛。若覺夢據情論，即長短各論各謂為實。一向不融。若覺夢據理論。即長短相攝，長時是短。短時是長。而不妨長短相別。若以一心望彼，則長短俱無。本來平等一心也。正以心體平等，非長非短故。心性所起長短之相，即無長短之實，故得相攝。若此長時自有長體，短時自有短體，非是一心起作者，即不得長短相攝。又雖同一心為體。若長時則全用一心而作，短時即減少許心作者，亦不得長短相攝。正以一心全體復作短時，全體復作長時，故得相攝也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: "For this reason, if one discusses the dream from the standpoint of waking, the long time in the dream is unreal; if one discusses waking from the standpoint of the dream, the meal's time while awake is also false. If waking and dreaming are discussed according to feelings, long and short are each discussed separately and each taken to be real, wholly not merged. If waking and dreaming are discussed according to principle, long and short include each other — the long time is short and the short time is long — without hindering the distinction of long and short. If one looks at them from the one mind, both long and short are absent; there is originally only the equal one mind. Precisely because the mind's substance is equal, neither long nor short, the marks of long and short that arise from the mind's nature have no reality of long and short; therefore they can include each other. If this long time had its own long substance and the short time its own short substance, not arising as made by the one mind, then long and short could not include each other. Further, even though they share the one mind as substance, if the long time were made using the whole one mind and the short time made with a reduced bit of mind, then too long and short could not include each other. Precisely because the whole substance of the one mind makes the short time, and the whole substance makes the long time, they can include each other.

Chinese Source

是故聖人依平等義故，即不見三世時節長短之相。依緣起義故，即知短時長時體融相攝。又復聖人善知緣起之法，唯虛無實，悉是心作。是心作故，用心想彼七日以為一劫。但以一切法，本來皆從心作。故一劫之相，隨心即成。七日之相，隨心即謝。演短既爾，促長亦然。若凡夫之輩，於此緣起法上妄執為實。是故不知長短相攝，亦不能演短促長也。此明第四事用相攝之相竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: "Therefore sages, relying on the meaning of equality, do not see the marks of long and short in the periods of the three times; relying on the meaning of conditioned arising, they know that short times and long times are merged in substance and include each other. Further, sages know well that conditioned dharmas are only illusory and without reality, all made by the mind. Because they are made by the mind, they use the mind to imagine those seven days as an eon. Only because all dharmas have from the beginning been made from the mind, the mark of an eon is accomplished according to the mind, and the mark of seven days recedes according to the mind. As extending the short is so, so too is contracting the long. As for ordinary beings, they deludedly grasp these conditioned dharmas as real; therefore they do not know that long and short include each other, nor can they extend the short or contract the long." This completes the fourth, explaining the marks of the mutual inclusion of phenomenal functions.

Chinese Source

境界大小，唯心所作。時劫長短，亦唯心所作。所謂一切唯心造也。然諸凡惑，夢時固是迷心。不夢時亦是妄心。由其迷妄，故夢時則執長時為實。不夢時又執短時為實。據理而言。所起長短之相，本無長短之實，皆由心作。且皆由心性全體而作，不增不減。是故夢時之長，即是覺時之短。覺時之短，即是夢時之長。可知本來平等，何有時節長短之異？即依緣起義，不妨說長說短。實則體融相攝。惟聖人善知緣起無實，悉是心作。故想彼七日以為一劫，則一劫之相即成。七日之相即謝。演短如此，促長亦然。成謝隨心，融攝無礙。凡夫則不然。妄執緣起，以為實事。是故理為相隱，自生障隔。而反謂本不相融，誠可悲矣。上來皆大師巧施方便，於現前事相上，善為點醒。令凡夫當下了得一切唯心之旨。其發揮圓融無礙法界法門，可謂罄無不盡。末句，總結第四科。

Ru-Yi Translation

Spheres, large and small, are made only by the mind; times and eons, long and short, are also made only by the mind. This is what is called "all is made only by the mind." But for all ordinary, deluded [beings], the time of dreaming is certainly a deluded mind, and the time of not dreaming is also a false mind. Because of their delusion and falsity, when dreaming they grasp the long time as real, and when not dreaming they again grasp the short time as real. Speaking in terms of principle, the marks of long and short that arise originally have no reality of long and short; all are made by the mind. Moreover, all are made by the whole substance of the mind-nature, neither increased nor reduced. Therefore the long of the dreaming time is the short of the waking time, and the short of the waking time is the long of the dreaming time. We can know that they are originally equal; what difference of long and short time could there be? In terms of the meaning of conditioned arising, there is no harm in speaking of long and of short; in reality they are merged in substance and include each other. Only sages know well that conditioned arising has no reality and is all made by the mind. Therefore, when they imagine those seven days as an eon, the mark of an eon is accomplished and the mark of seven days recedes. As extending the short is like this, so too is contracting the long. Accomplishing and receding follow the mind; merging and including are unobstructed. Ordinary beings are not so: they deludedly grasp conditioned arising, taking it to be real phenomena. Therefore principle is hidden by marks, they themselves create obstructions and barriers, and on the contrary they say that [things] originally do not merge. This is truly lamentable. All of the above is the Great Master skillfully applying expedients, pointing things out well on the phenomenal marks right before us, so that ordinary beings may at once understand the purport that all is only mind. His exposition of the dharma-gate of the perfectly interfused, unobstructed dharma-realm can be said to be exhaustive, leaving nothing out. The last sentence concludes the fourth section as a whole.

The Fifth Topic: Counteracting Delusion and Receiving Retribution

Chinese Source

（巳）五明治惑受報不同之義。分三。（午）初正明。二釋疑。三破執。初中二。（未）初明治惑不同。二明受報不同。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(P) Fifth, explaining the meaning of the differences in counteracting delusion and receiving retribution, in three: (Q) first, explaining it directly; second, dispelling doubts; third, refuting attachment. The first is in two: (R) first, explaining the differences in counteracting delusion; second, explaining the differences in receiving retribution. Now the first.

Chinese Source

次明第五治惑受報同異所由。問曰：如來之藏，既具一切世法出世法種子之性，及果報性。若眾生修對治道，熏彼對治種子性，分分成對治種子事用時。何故彼先所有惑染種子事，即分分滅也？即能治所治種子，皆依性起？即應不可一成一壞？答曰：法界法爾，所治之法為能治之所滅也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, explaining the fifth, the reasons for sameness and difference in counteracting delusion and receiving retribution. Question: Since the tathāgatagarbha possesses all worldly and world-transcending dharmas — the nature of seeds and the nature of fruits of retribution — when sentient beings cultivate the path of counteraction and perfume the nature of counteracting seeds, so that portion by portion it becomes the phenomenal function of counteracting seeds, why do the phenomena of the seeds of delusion and defilement that they had before cease portion by portion? The seeds that counteract and the seeds that are counteracted both arise in dependence on the nature; so it should not be that one is accomplished and the other destroyed. Answer: The dharma-realm is naturally so: the dharma that is counteracted is extinguished by what counteracts.

Chinese Source

問曰：所治之事，既為能治之事所滅者，所治之性，亦應為能治之性所滅。答曰：不然。如上已說，事法有成有敗，故此生彼滅。性義無始並具。又復體融無二，故不可一滅一存也。是故眾生未修治道之前，雙有能治所治之性。但所治染法之性，依熏起用。能治淨法之性，未有熏力，故無用也。若修治道之後，亦並具能治所治之性。但能治之性，依熏力故，分分起於淨用。所治之性，無所熏力，被對治故，染用分分損減。是故經言。但治其病，而不除法。法者，法界法爾。即是能治所治之性。病即是所治之事。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Question: Since the phenomenon that is counteracted is extinguished by the phenomenon that counteracts, the nature that is counteracted should also be extinguished by the nature that counteracts. Answer: Not so. As already said above, because phenomenal dharmas have accomplishment and decay, this arises and that ceases; but the natures, in meaning, are possessed together from beginningless time; and further, because they are merged in substance and nondual, it cannot be that one is extinguished and the other remains. Therefore, before sentient beings cultivate the path of counteraction, they have both the nature that counteracts and the nature that is counteracted. But the nature of the defiled dharmas that are counteracted gives rise to function in dependence on perfuming, while the nature of the pure dharmas that counteract does not yet have the force of perfuming, and so has no function. After they cultivate the path of counteraction, they likewise possess both the nature that counteracts and the nature that is counteracted; but the nature that counteracts, by the force of perfuming, gives rise portion by portion to pure functions, while the nature that is counteracted has no force of perfuming and, being counteracted, its defiled functions diminish portion by portion. Therefore a sūtra says: "Only cure the illness, and do not remove the dharmas." The "dharmas" are the dharma-realm being naturally so, namely the nature that counteracts and the nature that is counteracted; the "illness" is the phenomenon that is counteracted.

Chinese Source

上數科文，發明理事無礙，事事無礙之旨。是約性德，以顯藏體圓融。今第五科，則明修因得果種種不同，而不礙共以一心為體。是約修德，以顯藏體圓融也。治惑者，修因也。受報者，得果也。惑分理事。事惑即是十惡。理惑即見思塵沙無明。謂之惑者，惑亂人心之意。治者，如修十善以對治十惡。事治事也。修空假中三觀，對治見思塵沙無明。理治理也。受報者。十善破十惡，得人天果。空觀破見思，得二乘果。假觀破塵沙，得菩薩果。中觀破無明。證佛果也。同異者。眾生無量。所治之惑，有理事不同。所受之報，有凡聖差別。此之謂異。若同修十善，同修三觀，即同受人天二乘菩薩佛果。此之謂同。所由者明其所以也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The several sections above set forth the purport of principle and phenomena being unobstructed and of phenomena and phenomena being unobstructed; that was in terms of inherent virtue, to reveal the perfect interfusion of the storehouse's substance. Now the fifth section explains the various differences of cultivating causes and attaining fruits, which do not hinder their all sharing the one mind as substance; this is in terms of cultivated virtue, to reveal the perfect interfusion of the storehouse's substance. "Counteracting delusion" is cultivating the cause; "receiving retribution" is attaining the fruit. Delusions are divided into [delusions about] principle and [delusions about] phenomena. Phenomenal delusions are the ten evils; delusions about principle are views and habituated thought, dust-and-sand, and ignorance. They are called "delusions" (惑) in the sense that they confuse and disturb people's minds. "Counteracting" is, for example, cultivating the ten goods to counteract the ten evils: phenomena counteracting phenomena. Cultivating the three contemplations of the empty, the provisional, and the middle counteracts views and habituated thought, dust-and-sand, and ignorance: principle counteracting principle. As for "receiving retribution": when the ten goods break the ten evils, one attains the fruit of humans and gods; when the contemplation of emptiness breaks views and habituated thought, one attains the fruit of the two vehicles; when the contemplation of the provisional breaks dust-and-sand, one attains the fruit of the bodhisattva; when the contemplation of the middle breaks ignorance, one realizes the fruit of buddhahood. As for "sameness and difference": sentient beings are measureless; the delusions counteracted differ as [those of] principle and [those of] phenomena, and the retributions received are distinguished as ordinary and holy. This is called difference. If they alike cultivate the ten goods, or alike cultivate the three contemplations, they alike receive the fruits of humans and gods, of the two vehicles, of bodhisattvas, and of buddhas. This is called sameness. "The reasons" means clarifying why it is so.

Chinese Source

對治道即十善三觀等。問意謂心體本具之十善等性，因修道之熏力，分分成就現行事用，而為能治。彼先有之惑染種子事用，即分分被滅，而為所治。夫一切世間染法出世淨法之種子，以染淨熏為緣，而得凡聖果報。此之因緣及果，皆不空如來藏之所性具也。既是能治之淨法，所治之染法，皆依性起。即應存則俱存。壞則俱壞。今乃淨法成而染法壞，其故何耶？須知性是心體之本具。事乃緣起之幻相。虛幻之相，當然能治成時，所治壞滅。所謂法界法爾，勿須致疑也。復問曰：法界法爾，染法應為淨法所滅者。亦應法界法爾，染性亦為淨性所滅。何以事法則有成有敗，性獨不然耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

"The path of counteraction" is the ten goods, the three contemplations, and so on. The intent of the question is: the natures of the ten goods and so on, originally possessed by the mind's substance, are through the perfuming force of cultivating the path accomplished portion by portion as manifest phenomenal functions, and become what counteracts; the phenomenal functions of the seeds of delusion and defilement that existed before are extinguished portion by portion, and become what is counteracted. Now, the seeds of all worldly defiled dharmas and world-transcending pure dharmas, with defiled and pure perfuming as conditions, attain the fruits of retribution of ordinary beings and sages. These causes and conditions, and the fruits, are all inherently possessed by the nature of the nonempty tathāgatagarbha. Since the pure dharmas that counteract and the defiled dharmas that are counteracted both arise in dependence on the nature, they should remain together if they remain and be destroyed together if they are destroyed. Now the pure dharmas are accomplished while the defiled dharmas are destroyed; what is the reason? You should know that the nature is what the mind's substance originally possesses, while phenomena are illusory marks of conditioned arising. With illusory marks, naturally, when what counteracts is accomplished, what is counteracted is destroyed and extinguished. This is what is meant by "the dharma-realm is naturally so"; there is no need to raise doubts. He asks again: if the dharma-realm being naturally so means that defiled dharmas should be extinguished by pure dharmas, then, the dharma-realm likewise being naturally so, the defiled nature should also be extinguished by the pure nature. Why is it that phenomenal dharmas have accomplishment and decay, but the nature alone is not so?

Chinese Source

此中問意，前已廣釋。如上初明具足染淨二法中所明是也。須知性是心體本具。豈能與虛幻事相，此生彼滅者，相提並論。蓋性義可開為三。即是正因，緣因，了因。正因，為非染非淨不變之體。緣，了，具可染可淨隨緣之能。是故就緣了言。染淨二性，無始並具。不可一滅一存也。就正因言。名雖有三，體融無二。不可一滅一存也。故無論未修道前，已修道後，能治所治，二性雙有。但前則所治之性，依熏起用。能治之性，未有熏力，是故無用。後則能治之性，依熏起用。所治之性，無所熏力，是故分減。總之原是一性。但依熏起用不同耳。故約事言，可論存壞。約性言不可論存壞。因引《維摩經》，但除其病而不除法為證。經中法字，係法界法爾之義。即指能治所治之性言。與當文所治之法法字義別。（所治之法，乃指事言也。）病字即指所治之事。須知染事病根，由於情執。但除情執，染事自無。則隨染之性，原是淨性。不必除也。譬如眼中生翳。但除其翳，空花即滅。則見花之眼，原是淨眼。何可除乎？

Ru-Yi Translation

The intent of this question has already been explained broadly before; it is what was explained above in the first [topic], explaining the full possession of the two dharmas of defilement and purity. You should know that the nature is what the mind's substance originally possesses; how could it be discussed on a par with illusory phenomenal marks, of which this arises and that ceases? For the meaning of the nature can be opened into three, namely the correct cause, the conditioning cause, and the revealing cause. The correct cause is the unchanging substance that is neither defiled nor pure; the conditioning and revealing [causes] both possess the capacity, following conditions, to be defiled or pure. Therefore, in terms of the conditioning and revealing [causes], the two natures of defilement and purity are possessed together from beginningless time; it cannot be that one is extinguished and the other remains. In terms of the correct cause, though the names are three, the substance is merged and nondual; it cannot be that one is extinguished and the other remains. Therefore, whether before cultivating the path or after cultivating it, the two natures, that which counteracts and that which is counteracted, are both present. Only, before, the nature that is counteracted gives rise to function in dependence on perfuming, while the nature that counteracts does not yet have the force of perfuming and so has no function; after, the nature that counteracts gives rise to function in dependence on perfuming, while the nature that is counteracted has no force of perfuming and so diminishes portion by portion. In sum, it is originally one nature; it is only that giving rise to function in dependence on perfuming differs. Therefore in terms of phenomena one can speak of remaining and being destroyed; in terms of the nature one cannot speak of remaining and being destroyed. Hence he cites the Vimalakīrti Sūtra, "remove only the illness, and not the dharmas," as proof. The word "dharmas" in the sūtra has the meaning of the dharma-realm being naturally so; it refers to the natures that counteract and are counteracted. Its meaning differs from the word "dharma" in "the dharma that is counteracted" in the present text. ("The dharma that is counteracted" refers to phenomena.) The word "illness" refers to the phenomena that are counteracted. You should know that the root of the illness of defiled phenomena lies in emotional grasping. Only remove emotional grasping, and defiled phenomena are naturally absent; then the nature that follows defilement is originally the pure nature and need not be removed. It is like a cataract arising in the eye: only remove the cataract, and the sky-flowers cease; then the eye that saw the flowers is originally a pure eye. How could it be removed?

Why One Being Does Not Receive Six Bodies at Once

Chinese Source

（未）二明受報不同。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ru-Yi Translation

First he concludes why all ordinary beings and sages see one another or not and share in enjoyment or not: it is because within the one mind there are this consciousness of shared marks and this consciousness of unshared marks. "Therefore" and what follows points to phenomena as a warning and spur. Our World-Honored One teaches the Dharma on Vulture Peak, and his sixteen-foot golden body always shines with radiance. Yet those who see him as having entered quiescence between the twin trees, his radiance hidden and his spiritual bones already transformed ("the various colors of earth and ash" refers to his passing), do so because they created separate karma, which perfumed into the consciousness of unshared marks; so they cannot see. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra records that the World-Honored One pressed his toe upon the ground, and the Sahā world at once became the wondrous lotus-flower realm adorned with the seven jewels; when he withdrew his foot, it was again seen as sand and gravel, hills and ruins. For the Buddha's mind is pure, so the land he manifests is pure; sentient beings' minds are defiled, so the land they see is defiled. Since sentient beings do not cultivate the Buddha's pure karma to perfume the buddha-nature they possess in common [with him], it comes about that the wondrous realm and jeweled ground, when the Buddha withdraws his foot, are perversely seen as hills and ruins, sand and gravel. Long ago our Great Master Zhizhe, worshipping and reciting the Lotus, reached "this is true diligence; this is called a true offering of the Dharma," and suddenly entered concentration and personally saw that the one assembly on Vulture Peak was solemnly there and had not yet dispersed. This was because he had created the same karma, which perfumed into the consciousness of shared marks; so he could see it. It means that all that has been said above — sometimes seeing and hearing together and enjoying together, sometimes not seeing and hearing together and not enjoying together — all such events are brought about by the consciousness of shared marks and the consciousness of unshared marks.

What has been explained above is the name, meaning, substance, and form of the mind pure in its own nature. Now it explains the reason one relies upon this mind to cultivate calming-and-contemplation. All worldly and world-transcending dharmas are manifested by the mind alone, just as all grasses and trees grow in dependence on the great earth. Therefore from the beginning there has never been a single dharma that could be established outside the mind. Since this mind is the root that gives rise to all dharmas, now in cultivating calming-and-contemplation, if one does not rely on this mind, it will surely be destroyed and not accomplished. Variants in Dixian's root text. Unit 2 時節事不 (T1924 時節以不); unit 12 既以一心 (T1924 即) and 何名法界法爾具一切法 (T1924 法界法門; the root English "the dharma-gate of the dharma-realm" is kept, while Dixian's gloss in unit 13 reads "the dharma-realm being naturally so"); unit 28 定知 (T1924 定如); unit 31 具有 (T1924 俱有); unit 54 時頃 (T1924 時顧) and 所依 (T1924 聽依). Dixian's punctuation (unit 45/46). Dixian reads 餘處託生不廢，此土常存不缺 and glosses 不廢 as "without cease"; the root English, following the T1924 punctuation (託生，不廢此土), has "this land is not abandoned." The root English is unchanged; the commentary follows Dixian.

Misplaced 不 (unit 26). Dixian moves 不 in 不反流盡源即是佛法身 to after 源. The root English ("were not itself the Buddha's Dharma-body when it reverses the flow") already gives the sense he wants.

Lotus chapter (unit 26). Dixian names the chapter on Emerging from the Earth (湧現品), but the opening of Prabhūtaratna's stūpa and the gathering of Śākyamuni's emanation-bodies are in the chapter on the Jeweled Stūpa (見寶塔品). Translated as he says it.

Quotations in unit 23. The Lotus line follows the site's Universal Gate chapter ("brought across by the form of a buddha"). Ānanda's vow is the Śūraṅgama verse (T945, 如一眾生未成佛); Samantabhadra's words are from the forty-fascicle Avataṃsaka (T293). Eight-consciousness verse (unit 18). "Just before the immovable ground one relinquishes the storehouse; after the vajra path the maturation is empty" is from the Verses on the Structure of the Eight Consciousnesses (see T1865).

Śūraṅgama (units 41, 64). Both quotations in unit 41 are in T945. Unit 64, "taking the mind that neither arises nor ceases as the root cause of cultivation," paraphrases T945 (以不生滅為本修因); exact wording not located.

Pure land (unit 46). "When the mind contemplates the Buddha …" reuses the wording of Part 2 (site Contemplation Sūtra). Avalokiteśvara succeeding Amitābha is from the Sūtra of the Prediction of Avalokiteśvara (T371, cited in Daochuo's Anle ji, T1958), but the statement that the land will be "twice" what it was was not located there; the claim that Pūrṇa's land will surpass Sukhāvatī is Dixian's, not the Lotus chapter on Pūrṇa.

Vulture Peak (unit 58). The Vimalakīrti episode follows the site ("pressed his toe upon the ground"); Zhiyi's experience quotes the site's Lotus ("this is true diligence; this is called a true offering of the Dharma"), as in Part 1. Dixian corrects the root's 之致 to 所致.
