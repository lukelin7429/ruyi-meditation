Realizing the Other, Cultivating Without Thought, and the Three Self-Natures as Spheres

Part 9 finishes the long refutation of practitioners who would realize the pure mind without using mind-consciousness, and then opens the second of the root text's five rounds, the spheres of calming-and-contemplation. Dixian first completes the case against realization by another and takes the root text through "realizing the other": the habit-energy of deluded thought keeps it arising, the pure mind cannot do the work by itself, thoughts cannot stop one another, and a thought that dislikes thought only adds movement. He then turns to cultivation: the practice of not thinking merely ties mind-consciousness to the object "not thinking." It is a lesser calming, useful as a lock on the monkey mind but far from the samādhi of suchness; reciting Amitābha's name is set apart from it. The spheres are the three self-natures: the real, the dependent, and the discriminated. Each is explained in general, then divided into a pure and a defiled side, with question-and-answer passages on why the buddhas can show a "contrary" function while sentient beings' pure function stays hidden. Dixian adds Yogācāra tables of the eight consciousnesses and the fifty-one mental factors. The part closes with the root text's two further ways of distinguishing the three natures, through the one mind and through the dependent nature.

Refuting Realization by Another (concluded)

Chinese Source

轉計曰：大師先答我云，心體實自寂靜照明，但無始以來，為無明妄想熏故，不覺自動，顯現諸法。由此觀之。因有妄念習氣，所以心體不能證寂。然則我今不云自證，謂由他證，何大師又不許耶？破之曰：我先曾言，若不方便尋名知義，依義修行，觀知境界有即非有者。何由可得寂照之用？今汝不云方便，但云他證。所謂他者，正是妄念。何能除心中妄念習氣，令心證寂？然則汝將用何等方便乎？言下含有，用意識修止觀，正是除習氣之方便。所謂但治其病，而不除法也。汝不許用意識修。是因其病，並除其法。今反用意識證。是不善用其法，更增其病矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

The shifted speculation says: "The Great Master first answered me that the mind's substance is really tranquil and illuminating of itself, but that from beginningless time, because it has been perfumed by ignorance and deluded thinking, it moves of itself in nonawakening and manifests all dharmas. Viewed from this, because there is the habit-energy of deluded thoughts, the mind's substance cannot realize tranquility. Then if I now do not speak of self-realization but say it is realization by another, why does the Great Master again not allow it?" Refuting it, he says: I said earlier, "If one did not, by expedient means, follow names to know meanings, practice according to the meanings, and contemplate and know that spheres, existing, are not existent, how could one obtain the function of tranquility and illumination?" Now you do not speak of expedients but speak only of realization by another. What is called "another" is precisely deluded thought. How could it remove the habit-energy of deluded thought in the mind and make the mind realize tranquility? Then what kind of expedient will you use? Implied in these words is that using mind-consciousness to cultivate calming-and-contemplation is precisely the expedient for removing habit-energy. This is what is called "Only cure the illness, and do not remove the dharmas." You do not allow cultivation with mind-consciousness; this is removing the dharma together with the illness on account of the illness. Now, on the contrary, you use mind-consciousness to realize; this is not using the dharma well and increasing the illness further.

Chinese Source

又轉計曰：言他證者，非謂他能除心中習氣也。乃是他自除滅，令心證寂耳。是故我有方便。方便云何？即更不起新念是已。以不起故。便不熏彼習氣，令之增長。不增長故，彼即自滅。破之曰：彼諸習氣未滅之時。若無別法以為對治，法應起念。念起則更益彼力，新念愈起。汝今但云不起，不云對治。然則有何因由，而能不起乎？須知除轉意識為無塵智，更無對治方便。更無不起所以也。故結破云，以是義故，由他所證亦無道理。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again the shifted speculation says: "Speaking of realization by another does not mean that the other can remove the habit-energy in the mind. It is that [the habit-energy] removes and extinguishes itself and lets the mind realize tranquility. Therefore I do have an expedient. What is the expedient? It is simply no longer giving rise to new thoughts. Because they do not arise, they do not perfume that habit-energy and make it increase. Because it does not increase, it ceases of itself." Refuting it, he says: While those habit-energies have not yet ceased, if there were no separate dharma to counteract them, they would as a matter of course give rise to thoughts. When thoughts arise, they further increase that power, and new thoughts arise all the more. Now you speak only of not arising and do not speak of counteracting. Then by what reason can they not arise? One must know that apart from turning mind-consciousness into the wisdom of no sense-objects, there is no other expedient of counteraction, and no other reason for their not arising. Therefore he concludes the refutation, saying, "For this reason realization by another is also without principle."

Refuting Realizing the Other

Chinese Source

（卯）三破證他。分四。（辰）初明他不易證。二明心不證他。三明他不相止。四明嫌非方便。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(N) Third, refuting realizing the other, in four: (O) first, explaining that the other is not easily realized; second, explaining that the mind does not realize the other; third, explaining that the other [thoughts] do not stop one another; fourth, explaining that disliking is not an expedient. Now the first.

Chinese Source

若言不須用他證心，但證於他。以證他故，習氣自滅者。是亦不然。他既有習氣為根本故，念念常起。若不先除彼習氣種子者，妄念何由可證也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: If you say: "There is no need to use another to realize the mind; one only realizes the other, and because the other is realized, the habit-energy ceases of itself" — that too is not so. Since the other has habit-energy as its root, it arises thought after thought, constantly. If one does not first remove those seeds of habit-energy, how can deluded thoughts be realized?

Chinese Source

上破他證文中有云，乃可證他，何須他證？彼不得意者，將又轉計能證於他。因破之曰：若言不須他證，但證於他。以證知他是妄念故，習氣自滅者。是亦不然。何以故？他有習氣種子故。既有種子，所以念念常起，妄想不息。然則若不先除彼習氣種子，則即此證他之說，亦妄念也。既全是妄念，何由可證乎？須知必用意識修觀行，乃可了知境虛。由是分分除妄，分分證真，然後習氣方得除盡。否則全真成妄，云何妄能證妄？故曰妄念，何由可證也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the text above refuting realization by another, it said, "Therefore one may realize the other." One who does not grasp the meaning will again shift his speculation to being able to realize the other. Therefore he refutes it, saying: If you say there is no need for realization by another, but one only realizes the other, and because one realizes and knows that the other is deluded thought, the habit-energy ceases of itself, that too is not so. Why? Because the other has seeds of habit-energy. Since it has seeds, it arises thought after thought, constantly, and deluded thinking does not cease. Then if one does not first remove those seeds of habit-energy, this very talk of realizing the other is also deluded thought. Since it is entirely deluded thought, how can it be realized? One must know that only by using mind-consciousness to cultivate the practice of contemplation can one clearly know that objects are illusory. From this, removing the false portion by portion and realizing the true portion by portion, only then can the habit-energy be entirely removed. Otherwise the whole of the true forms the false; how could the false realize the false? Therefore it says, "How can deluded thoughts be realized?"

Chinese Source

（辰）二明心不證他。

Ru-Yi Translation

(O) Second, explaining that the mind does not realize the other.

Chinese Source

又復淨心，無有道理能證於他。若能證他者。一切眾生皆有淨心，應悉自然除於妄念也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Further, there is no principle by which the pure mind could realize the other; if it could realize the other, then since all sentient beings have the pure mind, they should all naturally be rid of deluded thoughts.

Chinese Source

又防轉計妄念雖起，而性淨不改。何以不能證他。破之曰：若以性淨不改，便謂淨心能證他者。則眾生皆有不改之淨心，應悉自然除妄念矣。故淨心證他之說，無有道理。

Ru-Yi Translation

He further guards against a shifted speculation: "Although deluded thoughts arise, the purity of the nature does not change. Why can it not realize the other?" Refuting it, he says: If, because the purity of the nature does not change, you say the pure mind can realize the other, then since sentient beings all have the unchanging pure mind, they should all naturally be rid of deluded thoughts. Therefore the claim that the pure mind realizes the other is without principle.

Chinese Source

（辰）三明他不相止。

Ru-Yi Translation

(O) Third, explaining that the other [thoughts] do not stop one another.

Chinese Source

若言妄念前後自相抑止，久久即息，故名為證他者。為前止後。為後止前。若言前念止後念者。前在之時，後識未生。後若起時，前念已謝。不相逐及，云何能止？若言後念止前念者。亦復如是。不相逐及，云何能止？

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: If you say: "Deluded thoughts, earlier and later, restrain one another, and after a long time they cease; therefore it is called realizing the other" — does the earlier stop the later, or does the later stop the earlier? If you say the earlier thought stops the later thought: while the earlier is present, the later consciousness has not yet arisen; when the later arises, the earlier thought has already passed away; they do not catch up with each other; how can it stop it? If you say the later thought stops the earlier thought, it is likewise: they do not catch up with each other; how can it stop it?

Chinese Source

轉計曰：所言證他者。非不知他有種子，念念不息。亦非謂淨心自然除妄。乃是以彼妄念，前後自相抑止。抑止既久，妄念即息。故名為證他耳。破之曰：自相抑止者，前念止後耶？後念止前耶？無論前後，皆不能相止也。何以故？當汝前念在時，後念猶未生起。後念起時，前念便已謝滅。前後尚且不相逐及，云何能相抑止？由此可知，非用意識依止本識而修止觀，更無證入之方便矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

The shifted speculation says: "What I call realizing the other does not mean not knowing that the other has seeds and does not cease thought after thought. Nor does it mean that the pure mind naturally removes the false. It is that those deluded thoughts, earlier and later, restrain one another. When they have restrained one another for a long time, deluded thoughts cease. Therefore it is called realizing the other." Refuting it, he says: As for restraining one another, does the earlier thought stop the later, or does the later thought stop the earlier? Whether earlier or later, they cannot stop one another. Why? While your earlier thought is present, the later thought has not yet arisen; when the later thought arises, the earlier thought has already passed away and ceased. Since earlier and later do not even catch up with each other, how can they restrain each other? From this one can know that apart from using mind-consciousness, relying on the root consciousness, to cultivate calming-and-contemplation, there is no other expedient for realizing and entering.

Chinese Source

（辰）四明嫌非方便。

Ru-Yi Translation

(O) Fourth, explaining that disliking is not an expedient.

Chinese Source

若前念起時，即自嫌起。嫌起之心，熏於本識，令不起後念者。心不自見，云何自嫌？若後念嫌前故，能嫌之心熏於本識，令不更起後念者。能嫌之心嫌前心時，為知前心是空故嫌。為不知是空故嫌。若知是空，即是無塵智也。汝云何言，不須此智？又若知是空，則應不嫌。若不知前念空者，此心即是無明。何以故？以其前念實空，而不能知故。又復不知前念空故，執有實念而生嫌心。即是妄想。何以故？以其於空，妄起實有想故。此能嫌之心，既是無明妄想故，即是動法。復言熏心。此乃亦增不覺。重更益動。生起之識，於是雲興。而言能令後念不起者，蓋是夢中之夢，未醒覺也。故作斯說。彷彿不睡者，必應不言如此。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: If, when the earlier thought arises, it dislikes its own arising, and this disliking mind perfumes the root consciousness so that it does not give rise to later thoughts — the mind does not see itself; how can it dislike itself? If the later thought dislikes the earlier, and so the disliking mind perfumes the root consciousness so that it does not give rise to later thoughts again — when the disliking mind dislikes the earlier mind, does it dislike it knowing that the earlier mind is empty, or dislike it not knowing that it is empty? If it knows it is empty, that is the wisdom of no sense-objects; how can you say this wisdom is not needed? Further, if it knows it is empty, it should not dislike it. If it does not know that the earlier thought is empty, this mind is ignorance. Why? Because the earlier thought is really empty and it cannot know this. Further, because it does not know the earlier thought is empty, it grasps that there is a real thought and gives rise to the disliking mind; this is deluded thinking. Why? Because it deludedly gives rise to the thought of real existence regarding what is empty. Since this disliking mind is ignorance and deluded thinking, it is a dharma of movement. And to say further that it perfumes the mind — this only increases nonawakening and adds yet more movement, and arising consciousnesses spring up like clouds; and to say that it can make later thoughts not arise is a dream within a dream, not yet awake; that is why one says such things. Anyone even half awake would surely not speak like this.

Chinese Source

轉計曰：言相止者，非謂逐及而後止之。謂當前念起時，自嫌其起。即此嫌起之心，熏於本識，令不起後念耳。破之曰：汝謂前念能自嫌耶？前念之心，既已生起，何能自見彼是妄念？既不自見，何能自嫌？然則汝所謂嫌，乃轉念耳。念既云轉，便成後念。汝乃認為仍是前念誤矣。又轉計曰：非謂能嫌之心，不是後念也。但以此能嫌之一念，熏於本識，令不更起後念耳。破之曰：此能嫌之念，嫌前念時。知前念是空故嫌。不知前念是空故嫌耶？若知前念空者，即是無塵之智。須知用意識修止觀，正欲轉之成無塵智。然則汝前云，何須意識為？云何可言不須耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

The shifted speculation says: "What I call stopping one another does not mean catching up and then stopping it. It means that when the earlier thought arises, it dislikes its own arising, and this very disliking mind perfumes the root consciousness so that it does not give rise to later thoughts." Refuting it, he says: Do you say the earlier thought can dislike itself? The mind of the earlier thought, having already arisen, how could it see by itself that it is a deluded thought? Since it does not see itself, how could it dislike itself? Then what you call disliking is a turning of thought. Since the thought is said to turn, it becomes a later thought; you take it to be still the earlier thought, which is a mistake. Again the shifted speculation says: "I do not mean that the disliking mind is not a later thought. It is only that this single disliking thought perfumes the root consciousness so that it does not give rise to later thoughts again." Refuting it, he says: When this disliking thought dislikes the earlier thought, does it dislike it knowing that the earlier thought is empty, or dislike it not knowing that the earlier thought is empty? If it knows the earlier thought is empty, that is the wisdom of no sense-objects. One must know that using mind-consciousness to cultivate calming-and-contemplation is precisely intended to turn it into the wisdom of no sense-objects. Then you said before, "What need is there for mind-consciousness?" How can you say it is not needed?

Chinese Source

又復若知是空，便應不嫌。若不知是空而嫌者，則前念實空而不知。且執虛為實而生嫌。此一念心，即是無明即是妄想。既是無明妄想紛動之法，乃云以此熏心。於是更增無明紛動，妄識雲興。而反言能令後念不起，直夢囈耳。彷彿似醒之人，即應不如此言也。上來約證破斥文中。先明淨心為無明妄想所覆，故不能自證。次明意識未轉成智，正是妄想，故不能證心。更明妄想未除，則全真成妄，故不能證他。如剝芭蕉。層層批剝，剝至蕉心，當體是空。故最後特標知空不知空兩義。知空便是無塵智。若不知空，則以妄熏妄，妄想益多。至此則必用意識依止淨心修行之理，了了洞明。若不轉意識為無塵智者。便不知前念是空，而生嫌心。則以念止念，轉更增動。無明窠臼，終不能出矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, if it knows it is empty, it should not dislike it. If it dislikes it without knowing it is empty, then the earlier thought is really empty and it does not know this; moreover, it grasps the illusory as real and gives rise to dislike. This single thought is ignorance; it is deluded thinking. Since it is a dharma of the agitation of ignorance and deluded thinking, and yet one says it is used to perfume the mind, then ignorance and agitation increase further, and deluded consciousnesses spring up like clouds. And on the contrary to say that it can make later thoughts not arise is simply talking in one's sleep. Anyone even half awake would not speak like this. In the above text of refutation in terms of realization: first it explained that the pure mind is covered by ignorance and deluded thinking and so cannot realize itself. Next it explained that mind-consciousness, not yet turned into wisdom, is precisely deluded thinking and so cannot realize the mind. Further it explained that while deluded thinking is not yet removed, the whole of the true forms the false, and so it cannot realize the other. It is like peeling a plantain: peeling layer upon layer, one peels down to the heart of the plantain, which in its very substance is empty. Therefore at the end he specially marks the two meanings of knowing emptiness and not knowing emptiness. Knowing emptiness is the wisdom of no sense-objects. If one does not know emptiness, then the false perfumes the false and deluded thinking increases. At this point the principle that one must use mind-consciousness, relying on the pure mind, to practice is entirely clear. If one does not turn mind-consciousness into the wisdom of no sense-objects, one does not know that the earlier thought is empty and gives rise to a disliking mind; then using thought to stop thought only increases movement, and one can never get out of the old nest of ignorance.

Refutation in Terms of Cultivation

Chinese Source

（丑）次約修破。又二。（寅）初正破。二判簡。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(L) Next, refuting in terms of cultivation, again in two: (M) first, refuting directly; second, assessing and sorting. Now the first.

Chinese Source

又復若言不作心念諸法，故念不起者。為淨心不作心念。為是意識不作心念。若是淨心不作心念者，本來何因作心念法？今忽何因不念法也？若是意識不念法者，意識即是其念。若言意識不作心念法者，為對見法塵而不念。為不對見法塵而不念。為對而不見而不念。為全不對塵名為不念。若不對塵，云何說為意識？何以故？以識者必識所識故。若對而不見，即是頑瞽之法。若見而不念，為何所因而得不念？為知空故，所以不念。謂為有故，所以不念。若知是空，是無塵之智。對而不見，見而不念，二俱無妨。何故汝言不須此智？若謂為有，即不能不念。又復謂有之時，即已是念。又復謂為有故，即是無明妄想。而復不念。譬如怯人閉目入闇道理。開眼而入，唯有外闇。倒生怕怖。閉目而入，內外俱黑。反謂安隱。此亦如是。念前法時，唯有迷境無明。而生嫌心。不念之時，心境俱闇。反謂為善。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Further, if you say that because one does not make the effort of mind to think of dharmas, thoughts do not arise: is it the pure mind that does not make the effort of mind to think, or is it mind-consciousness that does not make the effort of mind to think? If it is the pure mind that does not make the effort of mind to think, for what reason did it originally make the effort of mind to think of dharmas, and for what reason does it now suddenly not think of dharmas? If it is mind-consciousness that does not think of dharmas, mind-consciousness is precisely that thinking. If you say that mind-consciousness does not make the effort of mind to think of dharmas: does it face and see dharma-objects and not think of them? Does it not face and see dharma-objects and not think of them? Does it face them but not see them, and not think? Or is it called not thinking because it does not face objects at all? If it does not face objects, how can it be called mind-consciousness? Why? Because consciousness necessarily is conscious of what it is conscious of. If it faces but does not see, it is a dharma of dullness and blindness. If it sees but does not think, for what reason can it not think? Is it that it does not think because it knows they are empty? Or that it does not think because it takes them to exist? If it knows they are empty, that is the wisdom of no sense-objects, and facing without seeing and seeing without thinking are both no problem; why do you say this wisdom is not needed? If it takes them to exist, it cannot not think. Further, the moment it takes them to exist, that is already thought. Further, taking them to exist is ignorance and deluded thinking; and yet it does not think? It is like a timid person who closes his eyes to enter a dark place: entering with eyes open, there is only the darkness outside, and yet he is perversely frightened; entering with eyes closed, inside and outside are both black, and on the contrary he calls it safe. This is the same: when one thinks of former dharmas there is only ignorance deluded about objects, and one gives rise to a disliking mind; when one does not think, mind and object are both dark, and on the contrary one calls it good.

Chinese Source

又復若不作意念法，心則馳散。若作意不念諸法，作意即是亂動。非寂靜法。云何得名證心也？

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Further, if one does not make an intention to think of dharmas, the mind scatters and runs off; if one makes an intention not to think of dharmas, making an intention is disordered movement, not a dharma of tranquility; how can it be called realizing the mind?

Chinese Source

（己）二釋。即為二。（庚）初總明三性。二別明三性。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(F) Second, explaining, which is in two: (G) first, explaining the three natures in general; second, explaining the three natures individually. Now the first.

Chinese Source

所言總明三性者。謂出障真如，及佛淨德，悉名真實性。在障之真，與染和合，名阿梨耶識，此即是依他性。六識七識妄想分別，悉名分別性。此是大位之說也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: As for explaining the three natures in general: suchness emerged from obstruction and the pure virtues of the buddhas are all called the real nature. The true within obstruction, combined with defilement, is called the ālaya consciousness; this is the dependent nature. The deluded thinking and discrimination of the six consciousnesses and the seventh consciousness are all called the discriminated nature. This is an account by broad division.

Chinese Source

障即是垢。出障者，盡除垢染之謂。佛淨德者，佛之圓滿清淨妙德也。蓋一明出障之淨用。一明圓顯之淨德。故分列出障真如，佛淨德二名。然即此真如出障，便成諸佛淨德。亦即此淨德圓顯，便是出障真如。即二即一，故悉名真實性。真實者，真實不虛也。《唯識論》名之為圓成實性。在障之真，即在障真如，為不生滅法。染即無明，為生滅法。生滅與不生滅和合，名阿梨耶識。亦名業識。今復名為依他性者，即明其非同真實故。《唯識論》云：「依他眾緣而得起故。」他字即通指染淨緣熏。謂依淨熏起淨用。依染熏起染用。故《唯識論》名為依他起性。六識依七識為根。故此二悉名分別性。妄想分別，即指六七識言。《唯識論》名之為徧計執性。謂周徧計度，而起執著也。然周徧執計，義局凡夫。今此書取分別性之名者。乃徹上徹下，義通十界。因分別性，佛亦有之。如對機說法，即清淨分別性是也。以三性立義不一，今且約大位為說。又委細別明，尚在下文，今乃略判。故曰此是大位之說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Obstruction is stains. "Emerged from obstruction" means that defiling stains are entirely removed. "The pure virtues of the buddhas" are the perfect, pure, wondrous virtues of a buddha. For one makes clear the pure function emerged from obstruction, and the other makes clear the pure virtues perfectly manifest; therefore the two names, suchness emerged from obstruction and the pure virtues of the buddhas, are listed separately. Yet this very suchness, emerged from obstruction, becomes the pure virtues of all buddhas; and this very pure virtue, perfectly manifest, is suchness emerged from obstruction. They are both two and one; therefore both are called the real nature. "Real" means true and real, not empty. The Consciousness-Only Treatise calls it the perfectly accomplished real nature. "The true within obstruction" is suchness within obstruction, a dharma that neither arises nor ceases. Defilement is ignorance, a dharma that arises and ceases. What arises and ceases combined with what neither arises nor ceases is called the ālaya consciousness; it is also called the karmic consciousness. That here it is further called the dependent nature makes clear that it is not the same as the real. The Consciousness-Only Treatise says, "Because it arises in dependence on the many conditions of another." The word "another" refers generally to the perfuming of defiled and pure conditions. That is, in dependence on pure perfuming it gives rise to pure functions; in dependence on defiled perfuming it gives rise to defiled functions. Therefore the Consciousness-Only Treatise calls it the nature of dependent arising. The six consciousnesses take the seventh consciousness as their faculty; therefore these two are both called the discriminated nature. "Deluded thinking and discrimination" refers to the sixth and seventh consciousnesses. The Consciousness-Only Treatise calls it the nature of pervasive imagining and grasping, meaning that it pervasively imagines and calculates and gives rise to grasping. But pervasive grasping and calculating is in meaning limited to ordinary beings. That this book takes the name "discriminated nature" is because it runs through from top to bottom and in meaning extends to the ten realms. For the discriminated nature belongs also to a buddha: for example, teaching the Dharma in response to capacities is the pure discriminated nature. Because the meanings set up for the three natures are not uniform, he now speaks for the time being by broad division. Further, the detailed individual explanation is still in the text below; here it is only a brief assessment. Therefore it says, "This is an account by broad division."

Chinese Source

問：下文約凡聖差別，將三自性各開為二。而此總明中，真實性，則偏約聖邊無垢義說。分別性，則偏約凡邊染濁義說。何耶？答：此正大師教化眾生之要旨也。誠以凡夫全真成妄。非從染濁分別性進修，何由返妄歸真。又復染淨體融，本來平等。故真實偏約淨，分別偏約染，以明凡夫修止觀，唯有從染濁分別性著手。令其性依淨熏，而起淨用。於是轉識成智，染除淨顯。便能真如出障，淨德圓明也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The text below, in terms of the differences between ordinary beings and sages, opens each of the three self-natures into two. Yet in this general explanation, the real nature is spoken of only from the side of the sages, in the meaning of being without stains, and the discriminated nature is spoken of only from the side of ordinary beings, in the meaning of defilement and turbidity. Why? Answer: This is precisely the essential purport of the Great Master's teaching and converting of sentient beings. Truly, ordinary beings have turned the whole of the true into the false. If they do not advance in cultivation from the defiled and turbid discriminated nature, how could they turn back from the false and return to the true? Further, defilement and purity are merged in substance and originally equal. Therefore the real is spoken of only in terms of purity and the discriminated only in terms of defilement, to make clear that when ordinary beings cultivate calming-and-contemplation, they can set their hand only to the defiled and turbid discriminated nature, causing their nature, in dependence on pure perfuming, to give rise to pure functions. Thereupon they turn consciousness into wisdom, defilement is removed and purity revealed, and then suchness emerges from obstruction and the pure virtues are perfectly bright.

The Real Nature: The Pure Mind with Stains and Without Stains

（庚）二別明三性。又二。（辛）初別明。二合辨。初中三。（壬）初別辨真實性。二別辨依他性。三別辨分別性。初又三。（癸）初標章。二各釋。三合結。今初。

所言別明三性者。初辨真實性。就中復有兩種。一者，有垢淨心以為真實性。二者，無垢淨心以為真實性。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: As for explaining the three natures individually, first the real nature is distinguished. Within it there are again two kinds: first, the pure mind with stains as the real nature; second, the stainless pure mind as the real nature.

Chinese Source

何謂別明三性？即將此三自性，各開為二。分別凡聖，明其義相也。真實性中，開為兩種者。約凡言即是有垢淨心。約聖言即是無垢淨心。

Ru-Yi Translation

What does "explaining the three natures individually" mean? It is opening each of these three self-natures into two, distinguishing ordinary beings and sages, and explaining their meanings and marks. As for opening the real nature into two kinds: in terms of ordinary beings it is the pure mind with stains; in terms of sages it is the stainless pure mind.

Chinese Source

（癸）二各釋。即為二。（子）初釋有垢淨心。二釋無垢淨心。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(J) Second, explaining each, which is in two: (K) first, explaining the pure mind with stains; second, explaining the stainless pure mind. Now the first.

Chinese Source

所言有垢淨心者，即是眾生之體實。事染之本性。具足違用，依熏變現，故言有垢。而復體包淨用，自性無染。能熏之垢本空，所現之相常寂，復稱為淨。故言有垢淨心也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: As for the pure mind with stains: it is the substantial reality of sentient beings, the original nature of phenomenal defilement. It fully possesses the contrary function and, in dependence on perfuming, transforms and manifests; therefore it is called "with stains." Yet its substance contains the pure function, and its own nature is undefiled; the stains that perfume are originally empty, and the marks manifested are always tranquil; so it is further called "pure." Therefore it is called the pure mind with stains.

Chinese Source

既云有垢，何又云淨心？即是下正釋其義。流轉五道，名為眾生。既稱眾生，便是染也。而眾生體具之真如實性，則是淨也。四大五陰等生死事相染也。即此事相之本有妙性則淨也。須知染淨性用，原是一體。故雖具足染用，（文中違用即是染用。）依染熏而變現染事，名為有垢。而復體包淨用，自性本來無染。則以此淨性，收彼能熏之垢，所現之相。亦復垢空相寂。故復稱為淨心也。末句結成。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since it is called "with stains," why is it also called "pure mind"? "It is" and what follows explains its meaning directly. Flowing on through the five paths is called being a sentient being. Since one is called a sentient being, that is defilement; but the real nature of suchness possessed in the substance of sentient beings is pure. The phenomenal marks of birth-and-death, the four elements, the five aggregates, and so on, are defiled; the originally existing wondrous nature of these very phenomenal marks is pure. One must know that the defiled and pure natures and functions are originally one substance. Therefore, although it fully possesses the defiled function (the contrary function in the text is the defiled function) and, in dependence on defiled perfuming, transforms and manifests defiled phenomena, and is called "with stains," yet its substance contains the pure function, and its own nature is originally undefiled. Then, with this pure nature taking in the stains that perfume and the marks manifested, the stains too are empty and the marks tranquil. Therefore it is further called the pure mind. The last sentence concludes.

Chinese Source

（子）二釋無垢淨心。

Ru-Yi Translation

(K) Second, explaining the stainless pure mind.

Chinese Source

所言無垢淨心者，即是諸佛之體性。淨德之本實。雖具法爾違用之性，染熏息故，事染永泯。復備自性順用之能，淨熏滿故，事淨德顯。故言無垢。雖從熏顯，性淨之用非增。假遣昏雲，體照之功本具。復稱淨也。故言無垢淨心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: As for the stainless pure mind: it is the substance and nature of the buddhas, the original reality of pure virtues. Although it possesses the nature of the contrary function that is naturally so, because defiled perfuming has ceased, phenomenal defilement has vanished forever. It also possesses the capacity of the accordant function of its own nature; because pure perfuming is complete, the phenomenally pure virtues are manifest; therefore it is called "stainless." Although it is manifested through perfuming, the function of inherent purity is not increased; the dark clouds are dispelled only provisionally, and the work of the substance's illumination is originally possessed; so it is further called "pure." Therefore it is called the stainless pure mind.

Chinese Source

諸佛淨德，釋無垢也。體性本實，釋淨心也。體性者，全體淨性。本實者，本性如實。須知法界法爾，心性平等而有隨染隨淨之能。雖復隨緣，實具性淨不改之體。故染熏息滅，則事染永泯，淨熏滿足，則事淨德顯，名為無垢。實則淨熏，本自性淨德之用。故顯無別顯，全顯自性，非有新增。遣妄亦自體本具之功。故遣無可遣，但由照顯，假名為遣耳。故復名為淨心也。末句結成。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The pure virtues of the buddhas" explains "stainless"; "the substance and nature … the original reality" explains "pure mind." "Substance and nature" is the whole substance of the pure nature; "original reality" is the original nature being as it really is. One must know that the dharma-realm is naturally so: the mind-nature is equal and yet has the capacity to follow defilement and to follow purity. Although it follows conditions, it really possesses the substance of inherent purity that does not change. Therefore, when defiled perfuming ceases, phenomenal defilement vanishes forever, and when pure perfuming is complete, the phenomenally pure virtues are manifest; this is called stainless. In fact pure perfuming is originally the function of the pure virtues of its own nature. Therefore its manifesting is no separate manifesting; it wholly manifests its own nature and is not something newly increased. Dispelling the false is also the work originally possessed in its own substance. Therefore there is nothing to be dispelled in the dispelling; it is only because it is revealed by illumination that it is provisionally called dispelling. Therefore it is further called the pure mind. The last sentence concludes.

Chinese Source

（癸）三合結。

Ru-Yi Translation

(J) Third, concluding together.

Chinese Source

然依熏約用，故有有垢無垢之殊。就體談真，本無無染有染之異。即是平等實性，大總法門。故言真實性。問曰：既言有垢淨，亦應稱無垢染。答曰：亦有此義。諸佛違用，即是無垢染。但為令眾生捨染欣淨，是故不彰也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Yet in dependence on perfuming and in terms of function there is the distinction of with stains and stainless; speaking of the true in terms of substance, there is originally no difference of undefiled and defiled. This is the equal real nature, the great comprehensive dharma-gate; therefore it is called the real nature. Question: Since you speak of "pure with stains," you should also speak of "defiled without stains." Answer: This meaning also exists. The contrary function of the buddhas is "defiled without stains"; but in order to make sentient beings abandon defilement and delight in purity, it is not displayed.

Chinese Source

依熏約用故有殊者。此釋所以名為有垢無垢，乃就修德言也。就體談真本無異者。此釋所以皆稱為淨心，乃就性德言也。既無論有垢無垢，同此一體。則此體，即是平等不二之真實本性。故名為真實性。此性豎窮橫徧。能攝能生。故曰大總法門。問曰：自心本具染淨性用。既是有垢可言淨心，亦應無垢得稱染心。今有垢無垢，皆名淨心何耶？答之曰：諸佛倒駕慈航，隨形六道，可名為染。然雖示違用，而在塵不染，可稱無垢。則汝無垢染之說，亦許有此義也。但今欲教化眾生，返妄證真，須令捨染欣淨。由是義故。不言染名，單彰淨德耳。此義，具如上明因果法身中廣辨。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Because in dependence on perfuming and in terms of function there is a distinction": this explains why they are called "with stains" and "stainless"; it is said in terms of cultivated virtue. "Speaking of the true in terms of substance, there is originally no difference": this explains why both are called the pure mind; it is said in terms of inherent virtue. Since, whether with stains or stainless, it is the same one substance, this substance is the equal, nondual, real original nature; therefore it is called the real nature. This nature reaches to the end vertically and pervades horizontally; it can include and can give birth. Therefore it is called the great comprehensive dharma-gate. The question says: "One's own mind originally possesses the natures and functions of defilement and purity. Since what has stains can be called the pure mind, what is stainless should also be able to be called the defiled mind. Now why are both with stains and stainless called the pure mind?" The answer says: The buddhas, steering the ship of compassion back, follow forms through the six paths; this can be called defilement. Yet although they show the contrary function, they are in the dust but not defiled, and can be called stainless. Then your claim of "defiled without stains" is also allowed to have this meaning. But now, wishing to teach and convert sentient beings to turn back from the false and realize the true, one must make them abandon defilement and delight in purity. For this reason one does not speak of the name of defilement and displays only the pure virtues. This meaning is as discussed at length above in explaining the Dharma-body as cause and as fruit.

The Dependent Nature: The Pure Portion

Chinese Source

（壬）二別辨依他性。分二。（癸）初標章。二各釋。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(I) Second, distinguishing the dependent nature individually, in two: (J) first, heading the chapters; second, explaining each. Now the first.

Chinese Source

二明依他性者，亦有二種。一者，淨分依他性。二者，染分依他性。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Second, explaining the dependent nature: it too has two kinds: first, the dependent nature of the pure portion; second, the dependent nature of the defiled portion.

Chinese Source

即此凡聖同具之真實性體，以不覺自動故。生滅與不生滅和合，而成業識。若約其體而言，即是有垢淨心。若約相論。則在障之真，依熏淨顯者，為淨分依他性。能障之妄，依熏染現者，為染分依他性也。

Ru-Yi Translation

This very substance of the real nature possessed alike by ordinary beings and sages, because it moves of itself in nonawakening, [is where] what arises and ceases combines with what neither arises nor ceases and forms the karmic consciousness. Spoken of in terms of its substance, it is the pure mind with stains. If discussed in terms of marks, then the true within obstruction, manifesting as pure in dependence on perfuming, is the dependent nature of the pure portion; the false that obstructs, appearing as defiled in dependence on perfuming, is the dependent nature of the defiled portion.

Chinese Source

（癸）二各釋。即為二。（子）初釋淨分依他。二釋染分依他。初中二。（丑）初正釋。二料簡。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(J) Second, explaining each, which is in two: (K) first, explaining the dependent [nature] of the pure portion; second, explaining the dependent [nature] of the defiled portion. The first is in two: (L) first, explaining it directly; second, sorting out. Now the first.

Chinese Source

清淨分依他性者。即彼真如體具染淨二性之用，但得無漏淨法所熏故，事染之功斯盡。名為清淨。即復依彼淨業所熏故，性淨之用顯現。故名依他。所現即是所證三身淨土，一切自利利他之德是也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: The dependent nature of the pure portion: that substance of suchness possesses the functions of the two natures, defiled and pure. But because it has been perfumed by pure dharmas without outflows, the work of phenomenal defilement is exhausted, and it is called pure. And because, in dependence on the perfuming of that pure karma, the function of inherent purity is manifested, it is called dependent. What is manifested is the three bodies and pure lands that are realized, all the virtues of benefiting oneself and benefiting others.

Chinese Source

何謂清淨依他？釋之曰：真如本體，具有染淨二性用。但因在障之真，得淨法熏之，事染都盡。故名清淨。而淨用之能顯現，實依淨業所熏。故曰依他。無漏淨法者。貪瞋癡等煩惱，日夜由六根門頭，流注不絕，名之為漏。又煩惱能令人漏落於五道，亦名為漏。若修戒定慧。得離煩惱，是為無漏清淨之法。三身四淨土者。依一心之妙體證清淨法身，常寂光土。依一心之妙相，證圓滿報身。（開之為自受用報身，他受用報身。）實報方便二土。依一心之妙用，證千百億化身，凡聖同居淨土。法身自受用報身，常寂光土，為自利德。他受用及應化身，實報等土，皆利他德也。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the pure dependent [nature]? Explaining it, he says: the original substance of suchness possesses the functions of the two natures, defiled and pure. But because the true within obstruction is perfumed by pure dharmas, phenomenal defilement is entirely exhausted; therefore it is called pure. And the capacity of the pure function to manifest really depends on the perfuming of pure karma; therefore it is called dependent. "Pure dharmas without outflows": afflictions such as greed, anger, and folly flow out day and night through the gates of the six faculties without cease; this is called outflow. Further, afflictions can make people leak and fall into the five paths; this is also called outflow. If one cultivates precepts, concentration, and insight and is able to be free of afflictions, these are pure dharmas without outflows. The three bodies and four pure lands: relying on the wondrous substance of the one mind, one realizes the pure Dharma-body and the land of eternal tranquil light. Relying on the wondrous mark of the one mind, one realizes the perfect reward-body (opened into the reward-body of self-enjoyment and the reward-body of enjoyment for others) and the two lands of true reward and of expedients. Relying on the wondrous function of the one mind, one realizes the hundred thousand million transformation-bodies and the pure land where sages and ordinary beings dwell together. The Dharma-body, the reward-body of self-enjoyment, and the land of eternal tranquil light are virtues of benefiting oneself; the body of enjoyment for others, the response- and transformation-bodies, and the land of true reward and the others are all virtues of benefiting others.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Question: As for the function of inherent defilement, what is meant is that because defiled perfuming has been extinguished, it does not give rise to birth-and-death. Even so, after one becomes a buddha, is this nature entirely without function? Answer: Although this nature, being perfumed by [dharmas] without outflows, does not give rise to birth-and-death, yet because of the perfuming of the power of compassion and vows since the arousing of the mind, and further because the capacities of those who can be converted serve as conditions that perfume it, the function of showing the contrary also becomes manifest. "No pure vessel to perceive": one should know that it is sentient beings who abandon the Buddha, not the Buddha who abandons sentient beings. "Defiled virtues that can be seen": one should know that because the Buddha seeks sentient beings, sentient beings can seek the Buddha. For this reason, purity being complete does not prevent manifesting defiled virtues, while defilement being heavy does not allow revealing pure function. From this one can know that the arousing of the mind of the path really comes from delighting and wearying. Yet sentient beings have long been on the deluded road; if the Buddha-dharma did not constantly perfume them, delight and weariness would have no way to arise. This is why spreading the Buddha-dharma is superior in merit.

Ru-Yi Translation

The meaning of the three natures explained here and that in the preceding general and individual explanations each take something different, and each has a deep purport. Only by setting them side by side, one by one, to illuminate each other, can one see the Great Master's subtle intent. In the preceding general explanation, the real nature was spoken of only in terms of the pure virtues emerged from obstruction; the dependent nature in terms of the very substance of the true and false combined; the discriminated nature only in terms of deluded thinking and discrimination. This makes clear the meaning of following the stream back to the source: by turning back from the false one can realize the true. Now the real nature is spoken of in terms of the equal mind's substance; the dependent nature in terms of the substance being tied by defilement and purity; the discriminated nature in terms of what is manifested. Further, in the preceding individual explanation, the real nature was spoken of in terms of the difference between purity and stains; the dependent nature in terms of manifesting in dependence on perfuming; the discriminated nature in terms of the phenomenal functions of bestowing transformation in response to conditions and of discriminating and deluded grasping. Now, in speaking of the real nature, equality takes in difference. In speaking of the dependent nature: since the substance is tied by them, how could it not follow? Since it follows, it manifests in dependence on perfuming. This makes clear that the mind's substance is equal, neither awakening nor nonawakening; what further is there to tie it? In speaking of the discriminated nature: whether pure or foul, what is manifested is all illusory marks, nonexistent in substance and nature. This makes clear that it originally does not arise of itself and now does not cease; it is only the one mind, and its substance is without discrimination. In sum, the preceding individual explanation sought to reveal the difference of the virtuous and karmic functions of sages and ordinary beings; therefore it discussed mostly on the basis of differentiated phenomenal meanings. The present distinguishing together seeks to make clear the equal, ultimate substance of awakening; therefore it speaks by deeply tracing the real nature of suchness to its origin. Further, the first, speaking of the three natures in general in terms of following the stream back to the source, is to show ordinary beings a place to set their hands. Once one knows where to set one's hand, one must understand clearly the reasons for the differences between sages and ordinary beings; therefore next he explains the three natures individually, speaking mostly of the virtues and karma of sages and ordinary beings and their differentiated spheres.
