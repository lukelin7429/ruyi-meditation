The Dharma-Realm, the Dharma-Nature, and the Substance and Form of the Pure Mind

Part 4 finishes Dixian's commentary on the seven names of the one mind and begins the third main topic of the support of calming-and-contemplation, its "substance and form." He first explains the three meanings of "tathāgatagarbha" (what stores, what is stored, what can give birth), then the dharma-realm and the Dharma-nature, and closes the names with the reality-limit and the true mark. The substance and form are then distinguished in three ways, which Dixian matches with the three truths and the three contemplations: freedom from marks (entering emptiness from the provisional), "neither one nor different" (entering the provisional from emptiness), and the two kinds of tathāgatagarbha (the middle way). The long commentary on freedom from marks treats the tetralemma, the "hundred negations," and the eye that cannot see itself, and draws on the Sūtra of Perfect Awakening to present reciting Amitābha's name as "cultivating illusion with illusion." On the expedient for according and entering, Dixian explains how the discriminating sixth consciousness can be the instrument of cultivation while the pure mind is what is relied upon. The empty tathāgatagarbha is then defended against five doubts about ignorance and perfuming, and the part breaks off at the beginning of the nonempty tathāgatagarbha, with its inherent merits and the pure virtues that emerge from obstruction.

The Three Meanings of the Tathāgatagarbha (Commentary)

Chinese Source

有三義者。謂如來及藏，各有三義。初明能藏義。如來修圓性顯，故約果德法身以明能藏。眾生有性無修，故約性德淨心以明能藏。果德者。法身，般若，解脫為如來果上三德。性德者。正因，了因，緣因三佛性，為眾生性具三因。今言果德因心，並能包含染淨二性二事。蓋如來具淨性故。所以常樂我淨，而顯相好莊嚴。具染性故。所以能入六道，而現隨類應身。眾生具淨性故。所以能發覺除妄證真。具染性故。所以能起惑，造業招苦也。平等心中，隱含二性。對緣即應。非一非異。是之謂無所妨礙。此明能藏名藏之義。又能藏之體，聖凡平等。即名為如。隨緣生起，事用繁興故名為來。此明約能藏義名為如來之所以也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"There are three meanings": that is, "tathāgata" and "storehouse" each have three meanings. First it explains the meaning of what stores. The Tathāgata's cultivation is perfected and the nature is revealed; therefore it makes clear what stores in terms of the Dharma-body of fruition-virtue. Sentient beings have the nature but no cultivation; therefore it makes clear what stores in terms of the pure mind of inherent virtue. "Fruition-virtue": the Dharma-body, prajñā, and liberation are the three virtues on the Tathāgata's fruit. "Inherent virtue": the three buddha-natures — the correct cause, the revealing cause, and the conditioning cause — are the three causes that sentient beings inherently possess. Now it says that both the fruition-virtue and the causal mind can contain the two natures and the two phenomena of defilement and purity. For because the Tathāgata possesses the pure nature, he is permanent, blissful, self, and pure, and manifests the adornment of the marks and excellent features; because he possesses the defiled nature, he can enter the six paths and manifest response-bodies according to kind. Because sentient beings possess the pure nature, they can arouse awakening, remove the false, and realize the true; because they possess the defiled nature, they can give rise to delusion, create karma, and incur suffering. Within the equal mind the two natures are hidden and contained; meeting conditions, they respond at once; they are neither one nor different. This is called "without hindrance." This makes clear the meaning of "what stores is called the storehouse." Further, the substance of what stores is equal for sages and ordinary beings; this is called "thus." Arising in accord with conditions, phenomenal functions flourish abundantly; therefore it is called "come." This makes clear why, in terms of the meaning of what stores, it is called "thus-come" (tathāgata).

Chinese Source

次明所藏義。上以真心為能藏。染淨為所藏。此以無明為能藏。真心為所藏。謂之㲉藏者。卵內生機，具足飛騰之性，孵化即出。喻染中真體，具足恆沙功德，熏習即顯。此明所藏名藏之義也。藏體無異無相者。謂雖曰真心為無明妄想所覆故，名為所藏。然非真心，體別有異，體別有相。何則？全妄即真，故無所異。全真起妄，故別無相也。以其無異無相，故名為如。即此所藏之體，備具染淨二用，目之為來。此明約所藏義名為如來之所以也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next it explains the meaning of what is stored. Above, the true mind was what stores and defilement and purity were what is stored; here ignorance is what stores and the true mind is what is stored. It is called a "shell-store" because the vital potential within an egg fully possesses the nature of flying and soaring, and once incubated it emerges. This is a simile for the true substance within defilement, which fully possesses merits as numerous as the Ganges sands and is revealed once it is perfumed. This makes clear the meaning of "what is stored is called the storehouse." "The storehouse's substance being without difference and without marks" means: although it is said that the true mind is covered by ignorance and deluded thinking and is therefore called what is stored, it is not that the true mind's substance has some separate difference or some separate mark. Why? Because the whole of the false is the true, there is nothing different; because the whole of the true gives rise to the false, there is no separate mark. Because it is without difference and without marks, it is called "thus." The substance of this very thing that is stored is fully endowed with the two functions of defilement and purity; this is designated "come." This makes clear why, in terms of the meaning of what is stored, it is called "thus-come."

Chinese Source

三明能生義。以胎藏喻心體。以子喻染淨之用。胎藏以孕育功能，能生於子。喻心依染淨熏力，能生世出世間之法。是故下引楞伽，華嚴，觀無量壽三經證之。善不善因者，言其能生善惡業報也。心性是一，是不變體。能生種種果報，是隨緣用。正徧知海者。言如來正知，豎窮橫徧，如海之無底無涯。從心想生者。所謂是心是佛，是心作佛。此明能生名藏之義。染淨依心，心本平等為如。染淨之用，唯心能生為來。此明約能生義名為如來之所以也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, it explains the meaning of what can give birth. It uses the womb-store as a simile for the mind's substance and the child as a simile for the functions of defilement and purity. The womb-store, by its capacity of conception and nurture, can give birth to a child; this is a simile for the mind being able, in dependence on the perfuming forces of defilement and purity, to give birth to worldly and world-transcending dharmas. "Therefore" and what follows cites three sūtras — the Laṅkāvatāra, the Avataṃsaka, and the Contemplation of Amitāyus — to verify it. "The cause of the good and the not-good" speaks of its being able to give birth to good and evil karmic retribution. "The mind's nature is one" is the unchanging substance; "can give birth to various fruits of retribution" is the function that follows conditions. "The ocean of perfect and universal knowing" speaks of the Tathāgata's right knowledge, which exhausts the vertical and pervades the horizontal, like the ocean without bottom or shore. "Arises from the mind's own contemplation" is what is called "this mind is the buddha; this mind makes the buddha." This makes clear the meaning of "what can give birth is called the storehouse." Defilement and purity depend on the mind, and the mind being originally equal is "thus"; the functions of defilement and purity, which only the mind can give birth to, are "come." This makes clear why, in terms of the meaning of what can give birth, it is called "thus-come."

Explaining the Dharma-Realm

Chinese Source

（子）六釋法界。

Ru-Yi Translation

(K) Sixth, explaining the dharma-realm.

Chinese Source

問曰：云何復名淨心以為法界？答曰：法者，法爾故。界者，性別故。以此心體，法爾具足一切諸法。故言法界。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Question: Why further call the pure mind the dharma-realm? Answer: "Dharma" because it is naturally so; "realm" because of distinctions of nature. Because this mind's substance naturally fully possesses all dharmas, it is called the dharma-realm.

Chinese Source

法界二字。或約理體，或約事用。諸家釋義不同。以理事融通，性修不二，故可作種種釋也。如通常釋法曰諸法，是約體義。釋界曰界限，是約用義。此中以法爾釋法，即下文所云法爾具足。是就能具邊說，屬用。以性別釋界，即下文所云一切諸法，是就所具邊說，屬體。蓋以此心，天然有染淨二性，所以具足十法界差別因果。故曰法爾具足一切諸法。此名心為法界之義也。文並可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

The two characters 法界 ("dharma-realm") are taken sometimes in terms of the substance of principle and sometimes in terms of phenomenal function; the explanations of the various schools differ. Because principle and phenomena interpenetrate and nature and cultivation are not two, various explanations can be made. For example, ordinarily 法 is explained as "all dharmas," which is in terms of the meaning of substance, and 界 is explained as "boundary," which is in terms of the meaning of function. Here 法 is explained as "naturally so," that is, the "naturally fully possesses" of the text below; this is said from the side of what possesses and belongs to function. 界 is explained as "distinctions of nature," that is, the "all dharmas" of the text below; this is said from the side of what is possessed and belongs to substance. For this mind by nature has the two natures of defilement and purity, and therefore fully possesses the differentiated causes and fruits of the ten dharma-realms; therefore it says "naturally fully possesses all dharmas." This is the meaning of calling the mind the dharma-realm. The rest of the text can be understood.

Explaining the Dharma-Nature

Chinese Source

（子）七釋法性。

Ru-Yi Translation

(K) Seventh, explaining the Dharma-nature.

Chinese Source

問曰：云何名此淨心以為法性？答曰：法者，一切法。性者，體別義。以此淨心有差別之性，故能與諸法作體也。又性者，體實不改義。以一切法，皆以此心為體。諸法之相，自有生滅，故名虛妄。此心真實，不改不滅，故名法性也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Question: Why call this pure mind the Dharma-nature? Answer: "Dharma" is all dharmas; "nature" has the meaning of the distinctness of substance. Because this pure mind has differentiated natures, it can serve as the substance of all dharmas. Further, "nature" has the meaning of the substance being real and unchanging, because all dharmas take this mind as substance. The marks of dharmas have their own arising and ceasing, so they are called false. This mind is true and real, unchanging and unceasing; therefore it is called the Dharma-nature.

Chinese Source

釋此法性，義有兩重。初約不變隨緣釋。三千性相，俱名為法，故曰一切。體別者。心體不變，起差別用。今曰體別，順用而說也。謂諸法無體，唯此淨心具有染淨差別之性，能與諸法作體。故名此心以為法性也。次約隨緣不變釋。法仍前義。性者，體實不改。謂心體真實，性淨不改也。彼一切法，有生滅相。如依報有成住壞空，正報有生老病死，故皆名虛妄。唯此真心，既不改變，故無生滅。所以一切諸法，以之為體。故名此心以為法性也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The explanation of this Dharma-nature has two layers of meaning. First, it is explained in terms of being unchanging yet following conditions. The three thousand [realms] of natures and marks are all called dharmas; therefore it says "all." "Distinctness of substance": the mind's substance is unchanging yet gives rise to differentiated function. Now it says "distinctness of substance," speaking in accord with function. It means that dharmas have no substance; only this pure mind possesses the differentiated natures of defilement and purity and can serve as the substance of all dharmas; therefore this mind is called the Dharma-nature. Next, it is explained in terms of following conditions yet being unchanging. "Dharma" has the same meaning as before. "Nature" is the substance being real and unchanging; that is, the mind's substance is true and real, and its purity of nature does not change. Those dharmas all have marks of arising and ceasing: the dependent reward has formation, abiding, destruction, and emptiness, and the principal reward has birth, aging, sickness, and death; therefore they are all called false. Only this true mind, being unchanging, is without arising and ceasing; therefore all dharmas take it as substance. Therefore this mind is called the Dharma-nature.

Distinguishing the Substance and Form: Heading the Sections

Chinese Source

（壬）三辨體狀。分二。（癸）初標科略指。二依科廣釋。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(I) Third, distinguishing the substance and form, in two: (J) first, heading the sections and briefly indicating them; second, explaining extensively according to the sections. Now the first.

Chinese Source

次出體狀。所言體狀者，就中復有三種差別。一舉離相以明淨心。二舉不一不異以論法性。三舉二種如來藏以辨真如。雖復三種差別，總唯辨此淨心體狀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, setting out its substance and form. As for "substance and form," within it there are again three distinctions: first, citing its freedom from marks to make clear the pure mind; second, citing its being neither one nor different to discuss the Dharma-nature; third, citing the two kinds of tathāgatagarbha to distinguish suchness. Although there are three distinctions, all together they only distinguish the substance and form of this pure mind.

Chinese Source

何謂體狀？即形體相狀也。前出七名，皆是自心本來面目。吾人既聞其名，復知其義，不辨其形相可乎？世出世間一切法，差別之相甚多。若不辨明，必致誤認。且大乘止觀，必依止淨心而修。心體形相既未辨清，即無依止之境。云何修證？以此義故，本科所以廣辨。大眾即須悉心而聽也。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is substance and form? It is the shape of the substance and the appearance of the marks. The seven names set out above are all the original face of one's own mind. Having heard their names and further known their meanings, can we fail to distinguish their shape and appearance? Among all worldly and world-transcending dharmas, the differentiated marks are very many; if they are not distinguished clearly, one will certainly mistake them. Moreover, Mahāyāna calming-and-contemplation must be cultivated in reliance on the pure mind. If the shape and appearance of the mind's substance have not been distinguished clearly, there is no object to rely upon; how could one cultivate and realize? For this reason this section distinguishes it extensively. The assembly must listen with full attention.

Chinese Source

○就中三種差別，初明離相者，為從假入空。須知一切法可於有相中求。淨心必於離相中悟。《金剛經》云：若見諸相非相，即見如來。何以故？凡所有相，皆是虛妄故。諸相非相，即明離意。如來即是淨心也。次明不一不異者，為從空入假。離而不即，乃不一也。即而不離，乃不異也。既明離義，須更明即義，方能不墮偏空。所以古德又云：不見諸相非相，即見如來。此是明即義也。三明如來藏者，如來藏兼如實空如實不空而言。上言不一不異，尚恐學人自生隔別，故復雙明空不空義，則不即不離，融歸中道第一義諦矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

○ Among the three distinctions, the first, explaining freedom from marks, is entering emptiness from the provisional. You must know that all dharmas can be sought within marks, but the pure mind must be awakened to in freedom from marks. The Diamond Sūtra says: "If one sees all marks as not marks, one then sees the Tathāgata. Why? Because all that has marks is false." "All marks as not marks" makes clear the meaning of being apart; "the Tathāgata" is the pure mind. The next, explaining "neither one nor different," is entering the provisional from emptiness. Being apart and not identical is "not one"; being identical and not apart is "not different." Having made clear the meaning of being apart, one must further make clear the meaning of identity; only then does one not fall into one-sided emptiness. Therefore an ancient worthy also said: "Not seeing all marks as not marks, one then sees the Tathāgata." This makes clear the meaning of identity. The third, explaining the tathāgatagarbha: the tathāgatagarbha is spoken of as covering both the truly empty and the truly nonempty. Above, "neither one nor different" was spoken of, but fearing that students might still create separation for themselves, it further makes clear both the meanings of empty and nonempty; then, neither identical nor apart, they merge and return to the truth of the middle way of the first meaning.

Chinese Source

淨心，法性，真如，異名同體。今單舉離相以明淨心等者，何耶？曰：順文便故。蓋淨心之名，於離相義便。法性之名，於不一不異義便。（不一不異，為真實性故。）真如之名，於空不空義便。（起信論本約真如明二藏。今故以二種如來藏轉辨真如。）故順名偏舉耳，實則總唯辨此淨心。上來廣陳名義，名即名其體也。義即體之義也。得意者，聞名思義，便了得淨心體狀。若或未然。則恐偏執名義，不能會歸一體。故復於此融會辨釋，而令聞者忘言悟體，銷歸自性。此大師之苦心也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The pure mind, the Dharma-nature, and suchness are different names for the same substance. Why then does it cite freedom from marks alone to make clear the pure mind, and so on? Answer: because it follows the convenience of the text. For the name "pure mind" suits the meaning of freedom from marks; the name "Dharma-nature" suits the meaning of "neither one nor different" (because "neither one nor different" is the real nature); and the name "suchness" suits the meaning of empty and nonempty (the Awakening of Faith originally explains the two storehouses in terms of suchness; therefore here it uses the two kinds of tathāgatagarbha in turn to distinguish suchness). Therefore it cites each one-sidedly according to the name; in reality, all together it only distinguishes this pure mind. Above, the names and meanings were set out extensively; the name names its substance, and the meaning is the meaning of the substance. Those who grasp the intent, hearing the name and reflecting on the meaning, will then understand the substance and form of the pure mind. If not, it is feared that they will cling one-sidedly to names and meanings and be unable to bring them back to the one substance. Therefore here it again harmonizes, distinguishes, and explains, so that hearers may forget the words and awaken to the substance, returning it to their own nature. This is the Great Master's painstaking care.

Chinese Source

是故當知。淨心體狀本來離相。本與諸法不一不異。本是如實空如實不空。又復初句離是真諦。次句即是俗諦。三句不即不離，是中道第一義諦。七名中雖僅舉三名，實則淨心無量名字，以此三諦會其體狀，罄無不盡。觀其以如來藏釋真如，便可悟知名雖不一，理本互通。總唯辨此淨心而已。會得此旨，自性清淨心體狀，彷彿現前。則名名歸體，義義知宗。從此發足精進，便是由名字覺入觀行覺。而不著二邊，宛合中道，便是大乘圓頓行門，其造詣何可限量哉！末二句，結指總唯辨明心體，令聞者自悟淨心之意。上總標略指已竟。下復分科廣釋。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore you should know: the substance and form of the pure mind is originally free from marks; it is originally neither one with nor different from all dharmas; it is originally truly empty and truly nonempty. Further, the first phrase, being apart, is the truth of the real; the next phrase, identity, is the conventional truth; the third phrase, neither identical nor apart, is the truth of the middle way of the first meaning. Although only three of the seven names are cited, in reality the measureless names of the pure mind, when their substance and form are harmonized by these three truths, are exhausted without remainder. Seeing that it explains suchness by the tathāgatagarbha, one can awaken to the fact that although the names are not one, the principles are originally interconnected; all together it only distinguishes this pure mind. If one comprehends this purport, the substance and form of the mind pure in its own nature appear dimly before one. Then name after name returns to the substance, and meaning after meaning knows its cardinal point. Setting out from here with vigorous effort is going from awakening in name into awakening in contemplative practice; and not being attached to the two extremes, wholly according with the middle way, is the gate of practice of the perfect and sudden Mahāyāna. How could its attainment be measured! The last two phrases conclude by pointing out that all together it only distinguishes and makes clear the mind's substance, intending that hearers awaken to the pure mind for themselves. The general heading and brief indication above is complete. Below, it again divides into sections for extensive explanation.

Freedom from Marks: The Pure Mind Apart from All Marks

Chinese Source

（癸）二依科廣釋。自分為三。（子）初舉離相以明淨心。二舉不一不異以論法性。三舉二種如來藏以辨真如。初又二。（丑）初正明淨心離相。二巧示順入方便。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(J) Second, explaining extensively according to the sections, divided into three: (K) first, citing freedom from marks to make clear the pure mind; second, citing "neither one nor different" to discuss the Dharma-nature; third, citing the two kinds of tathāgatagarbha to distinguish suchness. The first is again in two: (L) first, directly explaining that the pure mind is free from marks; second, skillfully showing expedients for according and entering. Now the first.

Chinese Source

第一明離相者。此心即是第一義諦真如心也。自性圓融。體備大用。但是自覺聖智所知，非情量之能測也。故云言語道斷。心行處滅。不可以名名。不可以相相。何以故？心體離名相故。體既離名，即不可設名以談其體。心既絕相，即不可約相以辨其心。是以今欲論其體狀，實亦難哉！唯可說其所離之相，反相滅相而自契焉。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: First, explaining freedom from marks: this mind is the mind of suchness of the truth of the ultimate meaning. Its own nature is perfectly interfused; its substance is endowed with great function. It is known only by self-awakened holy wisdom and cannot be measured by the reckoning of feelings. Therefore it is said: the path of words is cut off, the place of the mind's activity is extinguished; it cannot be named by names, nor marked by marks. Why? Because the mind's substance is apart from names and marks. Since the substance is apart from names, one cannot set up names to speak of its substance; since the mind is beyond marks, one cannot rely on marks to distinguish the mind. He does not know that the mind's substance is equal: it does not reject nonexistence, nor does it reject existence, and it has no distinction between delusion and awakening. Then "since it can be nonexistent and yet be deludedly seen as existing, why could there not be no delusion and yet delusion arise perversely?" For "delusion" and "falsity" are both relative, and "seeing" and "arising" both belong to function. But the mind's substance is absolute and originally has no such thing; therefore it is called empty and pure. It is like the eye that, because of a cataract, sees flowers in the sky; yet in the pure eye there is originally no such thing. The empty tathāgatagarbha is also like this. "Fitting to set out here" means that for this doubt it is fitting to set out the simile of the flowers in the sky to make it clear.

（卯）三遣無明有體疑。

(N) Third, dispelling the doubt that ignorance has substance.

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, explaining the nonempty tathāgatagarbha. Within it there are two distinctions: first, explaining that it possesses the two dharmas of defilement and purity, to make clear that it is not empty; second, explaining the oneness and difference of the storehouse's substance, to explain its real existence. In the first, explaining the two dharmas of defilement and purity, first pure dharmas are explained, then defiled dharmas. Chinese Source

二種差別者。初明染淨二法，是約體具之用以明。二明藏體一異，是約具用之體以明。一異者。以用從體則一。是為理法界。以體從用則異。是為事法界。實有即不空義。無漏性功德法，是明具足淨性。出障淨法，是名具足淨事也。餘如下科廣明。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Two distinctions": the first, explaining the two dharmas of defilement and purity, makes clear [nonemptiness] in terms of the function possessed by the substance; the second, explaining the oneness and difference of the storehouse's substance, makes it clear in terms of the substance that possesses function. "Oneness and difference": with function following substance, they are one; this is the dharma-realm of principle. With substance following function, they are different; this is the dharma-realm of phenomena. "Real existence" is the meaning of nonemptiness. "Inherent meritorious dharmas without outflows" makes clear that it fully possesses the pure nature; "the pure dharmas that emerge from obstruction" means that it fully possesses pure phenomena. The rest is explained at length in the sections below.

Possessing Inherent Meritorious Dharmas without Outflows

Chinese Source

（寅）二隨科各釋。即為二。（卯）初明具染淨法。二明藏體一異。初中二。（辰）初明淨法。二明染法。初又二。（巳）初明具足無漏性功德法。二明具足出障淨法。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(M) Second, explaining each according to the sections, which is in two: (N) first, explaining that it possesses defiled and pure dharmas; second, explaining the oneness and difference of the storehouse's substance. The first is in two: (O) first, explaining pure dharmas; second, explaining defiled dharmas. The first is again in two: (P) first, explaining that it fully possesses inherent meritorious dharmas without outflows; second, explaining that it fully possesses the pure dharmas that emerge from obstruction. Now the first.

Chinese Source

第一具無漏性功德者。即此淨心，雖平等一味，體無差別。而復具有過恆沙數無漏性功德法。所謂自性有大智慧光明義故。真實識知義故。常樂我淨義故。如是等無量無邊性淨之法，唯是一心具有。如《起信論》廣明也。淨心具有此性淨法，故名不空。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: First, possessing inherent merits without outflows: although this pure mind is equal, of one flavor, with no differences in its substance, yet it possesses inherent meritorious dharmas without outflows more numerous than the sands of the Ganges — namely, the meaning of its own nature having the light of great wisdom, the meaning of true cognizance, the meaning of permanence, bliss, self, and purity. Such measureless and boundless inherently pure dharmas are possessed only by the one mind, as the Awakening of Faith explains at length. Because the pure mind possesses these inherently pure dharmas, it is called not empty.

Chinese Source

即此淨心者，謂即是吾人一念介爾之心。此之心體，性淨不改。故曰淨心。此一句統貫下文。平等者不二之義。因其不二，是為一味。既曰一味，故無差別。此二句約性體言。而復下約性用言。而復者。謂此心體，雖常平等，復常差別。過恆沙數，正明差別之義。無漏猶言不漏失。明其始終不變也。性功德法，猶言性淨功德。依此性淨之法，以自度度他。故曰功德。

Ru-Yi Translation

"This pure mind" means our single thought of mind, however slight. The substance of this mind, its purity of nature unchanging, is therefore called the pure mind. This one phrase runs through the whole text below. "Equal" means nondual. Because it is nondual, it is "of one flavor"; since it is of one flavor, it has "no differences." These two phrases are spoken in terms of the substance of the nature. "Yet" and what follows is spoken in terms of the function of the nature. "Yet" means that although this mind's substance is always equal, it is also always differentiated. "More numerous than the sands of the Ganges" precisely makes clear the meaning of differentiation. "Without outflows" is like saying "not leaking away"; it makes clear that it is unchanging from beginning to end. "Inherent meritorious dharmas" is like saying "merits of inherent purity." Relying on these inherently pure dharmas, one saves oneself and saves others; therefore they are called merits.

Chinese Source

所謂下引《起信論》，以釋性具過恆沙無漏功德法之義。自性二字標體。大智慧等三句，明體具之性德。大智慧者。即一切智，道種智，一切種智。此句是翻無明迷闇。故曰大智慧光明義。次句翻虛妄情見。故曰真實識知義。第三句翻無常、苦、無我、不淨。故曰常樂我淨義。（常樂我淨，為佛果四德。）如是等無量無邊句，明性淨之法，不可殫述，故以此句括之。上來諸義，皆此心本具之性德。故曰唯是一心具有。一心具有者。所謂理具三千，事造三千，兩重三千，同居一念是也。以上釋義之文，詳見《起信論》。故曰如《起信論》廣明。末二句。結顯上來所言，乃約心體具足淨性，以明不空也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Namely" and what follows cites the Awakening of Faith to explain the meaning of the inherently possessed meritorious dharmas without outflows more numerous than the Ganges sands. The two characters 自性 ("its own nature") designate the substance. The three phrases "the light of great wisdom" and so on make clear the inherent virtues possessed by the substance. "Great wisdom" is the wisdom that knows all things, the wisdom of the paths, and the wisdom knowing all modes. This phrase reverses the delusion and darkness of ignorance; therefore it says "the meaning of … having the light of great wisdom." The next phrase reverses false views of feeling; therefore it says "the meaning of true cognizance." The third phrase reverses impermanence, suffering, no-self, and impurity; therefore it says "the meaning of permanence, bliss, self, and purity." (Permanence, bliss, self, and purity are the four virtues of the buddha-fruit.) The phrase "such measureless and boundless" makes clear that the inherently pure dharmas cannot be fully described; therefore this phrase sums them up. All the meanings above are the inherent virtues originally possessed by this mind; therefore it says "possessed only by the one mind." "Possessed by the one mind" is what is called the two layers of three thousand — three thousand inherently possessed in principle and three thousand brought about in actual phenomena — dwelling together in a single thought. The text explaining these meanings is found in detail in the Awakening of Faith; therefore it says "as the Awakening of Faith explains at length." The last two phrases conclude by showing that what was said above makes clear nonemptiness in terms of the mind's substance fully possessing the pure nature.

Possessing the Pure Dharmas That Emerge from Obstruction

Chinese Source

（巳）二明具足出障淨法。分二。（午）初明果性惟心所具。二明能熏亦惟心所具。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(P) Second, explaining that it fully possesses the pure dharmas that emerge from obstruction, in two: (Q) first, explaining that the nature of the fruit is possessed only by the mind; second, explaining that what perfumes is also possessed only by the mind. Now the first.

Chinese Source

第二具出障淨德者。即此淨心，體具性淨功德，故能攝持淨業熏習之力。由熏力故，德用顯現。此義云何？以因地加行般若智業，熏於三種智性，令起用顯現。即是如來果德三種大智慧也。復以因地五波羅蜜等一切種行，熏於相好之性，令起用顯現。即是如來相好報也。然此果德之法，雖有相別，而體是一心。心體具此德故，名為不空。不就其心體義，明不空也。何以故？以心體平等，非空不空故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Second, possessing the pure virtues that emerge from obstruction: because this pure mind's substance possesses inherently pure merits, it can hold the force of the perfuming of pure karma; through the force of perfuming, the function of these virtues manifests. What does this mean? The karma of the prajñā-wisdom of preparatory practice in the causal ground perfumes the three kinds of wisdom-nature, causing them to arise as function and manifest; these are the three great wisdoms of the Tathāgata's fruition-virtue. Further, the five pāramitās and all other kinds of practice in the causal ground perfume the nature of the marks and excellent features, causing it to arise as function and manifest; this is the Tathāgata's reward of the marks and excellent features. Although these dharmas of fruition-virtue have distinct marks, their substance is the one mind; because the mind's substance possesses these virtues, it is called not empty — it is not in terms of the meaning of the mind's substance [itself] that it is explained as not empty. Why? Because the mind's substance is equal, neither empty nor not empty.

Translator's notes

Root text. Root-text units (2, 5, 8, 11, 15, 20, 24, 27, 30, 33, 36, 39, 42, 46, 49, 52) reuse `./dacheng-zhiguan/p1_body.md` (pairs 41–59) word for word. The root's pairs 42/43 are split where Dixian splits them (units 5, 8, 11); units 15 and 42 are cut at the root's own pair boundaries; unit 46/49 divide root pair 58 at "First, possessing inherent merits without outflows."

Variant in unit 30. Dixian's root reads 故佛常用而常寂; T1924 reads 故佛亦用而常寂. The root English ("the Buddha too functions yet is always tranquil") is kept. In unit 31 Dixian follows the Essentials Explaining in treating the doubled 有 of 何得不有有即非有 as redundant; the root English already reads it that way.

Attributions in unit 28. Dixian calls "Flowing on is birth-and-death; not flowing is nirvāṇa …" a verse of the Śūraṅgama Sūtra; it was not found in T945 (source not located). He assigns "The five aggregates are like an illusion, up to great parinirvāṇa is like an illusion …" to the Laṅkāvatāra; the closest wording ("if there were a dharma surpassing nirvāṇa, I would say that too is like an illusion") is in the Large Prajñāpāramitā (T223). Translated as he says.

Sūtra of Perfect Awakening (T842). The four quotations in unit 17 ("With the mind of the cycle of rebirth …"; "The mind [that sees things] as illusion is also left far behind …"; the fire-drill simile; "Knowing illusion is itself leaving it …") were checked in T842 and newly translated; Dixian quotes the fire-drill passage with the variant 鑽火兩木相因.
