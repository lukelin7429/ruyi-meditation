The Mirror Simile, the Names of the Dharma-Body, and the Mutual Inclusion of Phenomena

Part 6 carries the second main topic of the nonempty tathāgatagarbha, the oneness and difference of the storehouse's substance, through three and a half of its six sections. The first section, already argued by dharma in Part 5, is now restated by the simile of a bright mirror whose whole substance is present in every image, and proved by citations from the Avataṃsaka, the Awakening of Faith, and a sūtra on the one Dharma-body. The second section explains why a single Dharma-body bears the different names "of sentient beings" and "of buddhas," both from phenomena and from natures, and answers three doubts, including why the defiled side of suchness is never named. The third section treats suchness within obstruction and emerged from obstruction; here Dixian gives a long account of the three obstructions of affliction, karma, and retribution, and presents calming-and-contemplation, reciting the Buddha's name, and the Chan critical phrase as forms of pure perfuming. The fourth section, on the mutual inclusion and mutual identity of phenomena, refutes five doubts, including the confusion of the true mind with the Sāṃkhya "spiritual self," and the part ends in the middle of the root text's dialogue in which the visitor imagines a pore and a city.

Explanation by Simile: The Bright Mirror

Chinese Source

（未）二喻說。亦五。（申）初喻無差而差之理。二喻全理成事。三喻全事攝理。四喻全事攝事。五結顯差即無差。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(R) Second, explanation by simile, also in five: (S) first, the simile for the principle of being without difference yet differentiated; second, the simile for the whole of principle forming phenomena; third, the simile for the whole of phenomena including principle; fourth, the simile for the whole of phenomena including phenomena; fifth, concluding by revealing that difference is itself no difference. Now the first.

Chinese Source

○以文字語言，顯深微妙理，本是難事。是故諸佛說法，必加譬喻。喻者，曉訓之義。所謂月隱重山，舉扇以曉之。風息太虛，動樹以訓之。此亦如是。恐法說弗了，故復舉明鏡，以喻清淨妙心之圓融無礙也。因法立喻。分科故同。

Ru-Yi Translation

○ To reveal a deep, subtle, and wondrous principle by written words and speech is originally a difficult thing. Therefore, when the buddhas teach the Dharma, they always add similes. 喻 ("simile") means making understood and instructing. As is said: when the moon is hidden behind layered mountains, one raises a fan to make it understood; when the wind has stopped in the great void, one shakes a tree to instruct about it. This is also like that. Fearing that the explanation by dharma might not be understood, he further raises the bright mirror as a simile for the perfect interfusion without obstruction of the pure, wondrous mind. Since the simile is set up according to the dharma, the division of sections is the same.

Chinese Source

譬如明鏡。體具一切像性，各各差別不同。即是無差別之差別也。若此鏡體，本無像性差別之義者。設有眾色來對，像終不現。如彼熾火。雖復明淨，不能現像者。以其本無像性也。既見鏡能現像，定知本具像性。以是義故。此一明鏡，於一時中，俱能現於一切淨穢等像。而復淨像不妨於穢，穢像不妨於淨，無障無礙，淨穢用別。雖然有此像性像相之別，而復圓融不異，唯是一鏡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: It is like a bright mirror whose substance possesses the natures of all images, each distinct and different; this is difference without difference. If this mirror's substance originally lacked the meaning of differentiated image-natures, then even if many forms came before it, images would never appear. A blazing fire, though bright and clear, cannot manifest images, because it originally has no image-nature. Since we see that a mirror can manifest images, we know for certain that it originally possesses image-natures. For this reason, this one bright mirror can at a single time manifest all images, pure and foul. And the pure images do not hinder the foul, nor the foul the pure; without hindrance and without obstruction, the functions of pure and foul are distinct. Although there is this distinction between image-natures and image-marks, they are perfectly interfused and not different, only one mirror.

Chinese Source

明鏡具一切像之性。喻藏體具染淨之性。鏡體是一。無差別也。像性不同是異。有差別也。雖具一切像性，而鏡體平等一味。是謂無差而差。此總義也。下復別明。若此鏡下，謂明鏡若不具有像性，即無像相顯現。喻藏體若不具有染淨之性，即無染淨事現。復以熾火帖喻（帖與貼通。附益之義。舉火喻附鏡喻。益足以反顯鏡具像性之理。）熾火雖明，以本無像性故，不現像相。反顯鏡現像相，知其本具像性矣。以是義故下，言明鏡於一時中，淨像穢像，互現無妨。然雖有淨穢性相之別，而圓融不異，體唯一鏡。以喻藏體於一時中，淨事染事，不妨各現。然雖具染淨性事之別，而無礙圓融，唯是一心。則無差而差之理可明矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

The bright mirror possessing the natures of all images is a simile for the storehouse's substance possessing the defiled and pure natures. The mirror's substance being one is the undifferentiated; the image-natures being unlike is the different, the differentiated. Though it possesses the natures of all images, the mirror's substance is equal and of one flavor; this is called being without difference yet differentiated. This is the general meaning. Below, it is explained specifically. "If this mirror's substance" and what follows means: if the bright mirror did not possess image-natures, no image-marks would appear. This is a simile for: if the storehouse's substance did not possess defiled and pure natures, no defiled or pure phenomena would appear. Further, the blazing fire is added as a supplementary simile (帖 is interchangeable with 貼, meaning to attach and add: the fire simile is attached to the mirror simile, adding enough to reveal inversely the principle that the mirror possesses image-natures). Though a blazing fire is bright, because it originally has no image-nature, it does not manifest image-marks; this reveals inversely that, since a mirror manifests image-marks, we know it originally possesses image-natures. "For this reason" and what follows says that in the bright mirror, at a single time, pure images and foul images appear together without hindering each other; yet although there is the distinction of pure and foul natures and marks, they are perfectly interfused and not different, the substance being only one mirror. This is a simile for the storehouse's substance in which, at a single time, pure phenomena and defiled phenomena are not prevented from each appearing; yet although it possesses the distinction of defiled and pure natures and phenomena, it is unobstructed and perfectly interfused, only the one mind. Then the principle of being without difference yet differentiated can be made clear.

The Simile for the Whole of Principle Forming Phenomena

Chinese Source

（申）二喻全理成事。

Ru-Yi Translation

(S) Second, the simile for the whole of principle forming phenomena.

Chinese Source

何以故？謂以此鏡全體是一毛孔像性故。全體是一切毛孔像性故。如毛孔像性。其餘一一微細像性，一一粗大像性，一淨像性，一穢像性等，亦復如是，是鏡全體也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Why? Because this mirror's whole substance is the image-nature of a single pore, and its whole substance is the image-nature of all pores. As with the image-nature of a pore, so too with each other minute image-nature, each coarse and large image-nature, each pure image-nature, each foul image-nature, and so on: each is the mirror's whole substance.

Chinese Source

無論一切大小粗細淨穢等像。彼一一像現，皆是明鏡全體性現。是舉明鏡之性，全體而成一切像之性也。微細像如羊毛塵等。粗大像如山河等。淨像如四聖。穢像如六凡。此喻舉全藏體以成世出世間一切之事，以顯全理成事之義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Regardless of all images, large or small, coarse or fine, pure or foul, the appearance of each and every image is the appearance of the nature of the bright mirror's whole substance. This is taking up the nature of the bright mirror, whose whole substance forms the natures of all images. Minute images are like the dust on a sheep's hair and the like; coarse and large images are like mountains and rivers and the like. Pure images are like the four holy [realms]; foul images are like the six ordinary [realms]. This simile takes up the whole storehouse's substance forming all worldly and world-transcending phenomena, to reveal the meaning of the whole of principle forming phenomena.

The Simile for the Whole of Phenomena Including Principle

Chinese Source

（申）三喻全事攝理。

Ru-Yi Translation

(S) Third, the simile for the whole of phenomena including principle.

Chinese Source

是故若舉一毛孔像性。即攝其餘一切像性。如舉一毛孔像性即攝一切像性。舉其餘一一像性，亦復如是即攝一切像性也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Therefore, taking up the image-nature of one pore includes all the other image-natures. As taking up the image-nature of one pore includes all image-natures, so taking up each of the other image-natures likewise includes all image-natures.

Chinese Source

一毛孔像性，乃至一切像性，既皆攝明鏡全體之性。故舉一一像性，即攝其餘一切像性也。合法可知。此明全事攝理，故舉像性言。下明全事攝事，故舉像相言也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the image-nature of one pore, and so on up to all image-natures, each include the nature of the bright mirror's whole substance, taking up each image-nature includes all the other image-natures. Matching it with the dharma can be understood. This explains the whole of phenomena including principle, so it speaks of image-natures; below, explaining the whole of phenomena including phenomena, it speaks of image-marks.

The Simile for the Whole of Phenomena Including Phenomena

Chinese Source

（申）四喻全事攝事。

Ru-Yi Translation

(S) Fourth, the simile for the whole of phenomena including phenomena.

Chinese Source

又若舉一毛孔像相。即攝一切像相。如舉一毛孔像相即攝一切像相。舉其餘一一像相，亦復如是即攝一切像相。何以故？以一切像相，即以彼像性為體故。是故一切像性體融相攝故，一切像相亦即相融相攝也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Further, taking up the image-mark of one pore includes all image-marks. As taking up the image-mark of one pore includes all image-marks, so taking up each of the other image-marks likewise includes all image-marks. Why? Because all image-marks take those image-natures as substance. Therefore, because all image-natures are merged in substance and include each other, all image-marks too merge with and include each other.

Chinese Source

此科文相易知。至一相攝一一相之理，可以通俗共見之電光影戲譬之。若非相攝無礙者，以一小小紙片，何能於電光中現出山河人物等相哉？知此則事事相攝之理可明矣。或曰：此虛幻耳。嗟乎！正由凡夫妄想，不知此身，外洎山河虛空大地，咸是妙明真心中物，而妄執為實有。所以不能事事無礙耳。此之謂迷。此之謂無明。所以惑業苦三，頭出頭沒，循環不斷者，皆由於此。若了得一切皆是虛幻，無非電光影戲。則能知境虛，便是觀智。從此加用功行，即可轉凡入聖矣。大眾猛省。

Ru-Yi Translation

The appearance of the text of this section is easy to understand. As for the principle of one mark including each and every mark, it can be likened to the electric-light shadow plays [motion pictures] commonly seen by all. If there were no unobstructed mutual inclusion, how could a small piece of paper display marks of mountains, rivers, people, and things in the electric light? Knowing this, the principle of phenomena including phenomena can be made clear. Someone says: "That is only illusion." Alas! It is precisely because ordinary beings, with deluded thinking, do not know that this body, and outward to mountains, rivers, empty space, and the great earth, are all things within the wondrous, bright true mind, and falsely cling to them as really existing, that they cannot have phenomena unobstructed with phenomena. This is called delusion; this is called ignorance. That the three, delusion, karma, and suffering, keep surfacing and sinking, cycling without a break, is all due to this. If one understands that everything is illusory, nothing but an electric-light shadow play, then one can know that objects are empty; this is contemplative wisdom. Adding effort and practice from here, one can transform the ordinary and enter the holy. Assembly, wake up fiercely!

Concluding by Revealing That Difference Is Itself No Difference

Chinese Source

（申）五結顯差即無差。

Ru-Yi Translation

(S) Fifth, concluding by revealing that difference is itself no difference.

Chinese Source

以是譬故。一切諸佛，一切眾生，同一淨心如來之藏，不相妨礙，即應可信。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: By this simile, that all buddhas and all sentient beings share the one pure mind, the tathāgatagarbha, without hindering each other, should be believable.

Chinese Source

此如來藏。約眾生言為本明。如鏡為塵埋，故名為有垢淨心。約諸佛言是修明。如塵磨明顯，故稱為無垢淨心。然非今喻意。今喻但謂，明鏡雖現像差殊，而無障無礙，唯是一鏡。以喻藏體雖緣起各異，而無障無礙，唯一淨心。結成初明法界體一文中，一切眾生一切諸佛唯共一如來藏之義。以明差即無差也。

Ru-Yi Translation

This tathāgatagarbha, spoken of with regard to sentient beings, is original brightness, like a mirror buried in dust; hence it is called the pure mind with stains. Spoken of with regard to the buddhas, it is cultivated brightness, like the dust ground away and the brightness revealed; hence it is called the stainless pure mind. But that is not the meaning of the present simile. The present simile means only that, although the bright mirror manifests images that differ, it is without hindrance and without obstruction, only one mirror; this is a simile for the storehouse's substance which, although its conditioned arisings differ, is without hindrance and without obstruction, only the one pure mind. It concludes the meaning, in the text of the first section, "directly explaining that the substance of the dharma-realm is one," that all sentient beings and all buddhas share only one tathāgatagarbha, so as to make clear that difference is itself no difference.

Citing Proof: The Miscellaneous Flowers

Chinese Source

（未）三引證。又三。（申）初引雜華。（華嚴之異名。）二引起信。三引契經。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(R) Third, citing proof, again in three: (S) first, citing the Miscellaneous Flowers (another name for the Avataṃsaka); second, citing the Awakening of Faith; third, citing a sūtra. Now the first.

Chinese Source

是故經言。譬如明淨鏡。隨對面像現。各各不相知。業性亦如是。此義云何？謂明淨鏡者，即喻淨心體也。隨對者，即喻淨心體具一切法性，故能受一切熏習。隨其熏別，現報不同也。面者，即喻染淨二業也。像現者，即喻心體染淨二性，依熏力故，現染淨二報也。各各不相知者，即喻淨心與業果報，各不相知也。業者，染淨二業。合上面也。性者，即是真心染淨二性。合上明鏡具一切像性也。亦如是者，總結成此義也。又復長行問云：心性是一者。此據法性體融，說為一也。云何能生種種果報者。謂不解無差別之差別，故言云何能生種種果報也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Therefore a sūtra says: "It is like a bright, clear mirror: according to what is before it, the face's image appears; each does not know the other; the nature of karma is also like this." What does this mean? "A bright, clear mirror" is a simile for the substance of the pure mind. "According to what is before it" is a simile for the pure mind's substance possessing all dharma-natures, so that it can receive all perfuming and, according to the differences of perfuming, manifest different retributions. "The face" is a simile for the two kinds of karma, defiled and pure. "Image appears" is a simile for the two natures of defilement and purity of the mind's substance, which, in dependence on the force of perfuming, manifest the two retributions of defilement and purity. "Each does not know the other" is a simile for the pure mind and karmic retribution each not knowing the other. "Karma" is the two kinds of karma, defiled and pure, matching "the face" above. "Nature" is the two natures of defilement and purity of the true mind, matching the bright mirror possessing all image-natures above. "Is also like this" concludes and establishes this meaning as a whole. Further, where the prose question says, "The mind's nature is one," this is said to be one in terms of the Dharma-nature being merged in substance. "How can it give birth to various fruits of retribution?" — because [the questioner] does not understand difference without difference, he says, "How can it give birth to various fruits of retribution?"

Chinese Source

經偈中，約義有八。即一鏡、二明、三淨、四隨、五對、六面、七像、八現。此八義中，具三重能所。即明淨鏡為能現，像為所現。面為能對，明淨鏡為所對。鏡之明淨為能隨，面像為所隨也。明淨鏡，喻淨心藏體。開之則為三：鏡喻法身之體，明喻般若之相，淨喻解脫之用。體相用三大，絕對無雙，故具足一切法性，而能隨其熏別，顯現世出世果報不同也。面即喻業，對則喻熏，像喻果報，各各不相知者。謂鏡面像等，云何隨，云何對，云何現，互不相知。以喻心業果等之於隨對現，亦各不相知也。業性亦如是句下合法。在文可知。末復引長行，結成無差別之差別義。長行中心性是一，云何能生種種果報二句。是文殊菩薩問語。菩薩豈不知無差成差。以大悲故，為眾生問耳。凡一切經中，請法之語。權現之迹。皆同此理。不可不知。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the sūtra verse there are, by meaning, eight [elements]: first, "mirror"; second, "bright"; third, "clear"; fourth, "according to"; fifth, "before it"; sixth, "face"; seventh, "image"; eighth, "appears." Among these eight there are three layers of active and acted-upon: the bright, clear mirror is what manifests, and the image is what is manifested; the face is what is before it, and the bright, clear mirror is what it is before; the brightness and clarity of the mirror is what accords, and the face and image are what is accorded with. The bright, clear mirror is a simile for the pure mind, the storehouse's substance. Opened up, it becomes three: the mirror is a simile for the substance of the Dharma-body, "bright" for the mark of prajñā, "clear" for the function of liberation. The three greatnesses of substance, mark, and function are absolute and without peer; therefore it fully possesses all dharma-natures and can, according to the differences of perfuming, manifest different worldly and world-transcending fruits of retribution. The face is a simile for karma, being before it for perfuming, the image for the fruits of retribution. "Each does not know the other" means that the mirror, face, image, and so on do not know of one another how they accord, how they are before, how they appear. This is a simile for mind, karma, fruit, and so on likewise not knowing one another in according, being before, and appearing. The phrase "the nature of karma is also like this" and what follows matches it with the dharma; this can be understood from the text. At the end it further cites the prose to conclude the meaning of difference without difference. In the prose, the two phrases "The mind's nature is one; how can it give birth to various fruits of retribution?" are the words of the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī's question. Does a bodhisattva not know that no difference forms difference? Because of great compassion, he asks for the sake of sentient beings. In all the sūtras, words requesting the Dharma are traces of provisional manifestation and follow this same principle. One must know this.

Citing the Awakening of Faith

Chinese Source

（申）二引起信。

Ru-Yi Translation

(S) Second, citing the Awakening of Faith.

Chinese Source

此修多羅中喻意，偏明心性能生世間果報。今即通明能生世出世果，亦無所妨也。是故論云：三者用大。能生世間出世間善惡因果故。以此義故。一切凡聖一心為體，決定不疑也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: The meaning of the simile in this sūtra explains only the mind's nature giving birth to worldly fruits of retribution. Now there is no harm in explaining generally that it gives birth to both worldly and world-transcending fruits. Therefore the Treatise says: "Third, the greatness of function, because it can give birth to the good and evil causes and fruits of the world and beyond the world." For this reason, that all ordinary beings and sages take the one mind as substance is certain and beyond doubt.

Chinese Source

上來所引經偈，本是單喻心性能生世間因果。然即以之通明世出世因果，亦無所妨。是故下，引《起信論》以證其無妨也。用即體相用之用。世間通指六凡。出世間通指四聖。此十法界因果，皆由一心緣起。故曰能生。以一心能緣起十法界因果，故曰用大也。按今本《起信論》，此句無惡字。唐賢首國師《起信論義記》亦然。當別有據。吾輩讀古德書。凡遇異義之處，會其通，取其精可也。因此而起偏執鬬諍，大不可也。學者戒之。以此義故下，結成差即無差之義。

Ru-Yi Translation

The sūtra verse cited above originally was a simile only for the mind's nature giving birth to worldly causes and fruits. But there is no harm in using it to explain generally worldly and world-transcending causes and fruits; "Therefore" and what follows cites the Awakening of Faith to prove that there is no harm. "Function" is the function of substance, mark, and function. "The world" refers generally to the six ordinary [realms]; "beyond the world" refers generally to the four holy [realms]. The causes and fruits of these ten dharma-realms all arise by conditions from the one mind; hence "can give birth." Because the one mind can give rise by conditions to the causes and fruits of the ten dharma-realms, it is called the greatness of function. Note: in the present edition of the Awakening of Faith, this sentence lacks the word "evil," and so too does the Tang National Master Xianshou's Record of the Meaning of the Awakening of Faith. There must be some other basis. When we read the books of the ancient worthies, wherever we meet a different reading, we may grasp what they have in common and take their essence; to give rise to one-sided attachment and contention over it is greatly wrong. Students, take heed. "For this reason" and what follows concludes the meaning that difference is itself no difference.

Citing a Sūtra: One Dharma-Body

Chinese Source

（申）三引契經。

Ru-Yi Translation

(S) Third, citing a sūtra.

Chinese Source

又復經言。一切諸佛法身，唯是一法身者。此即證知一切諸佛，同一真心為體。以一切諸佛法身是一故，一切眾生及與諸佛，即同一法身也。何以故？修多羅為證故。所證云何？謂即此法身流轉五道，說名眾生。反流盡源，說名為佛。以是義故。一切眾生，一切諸佛。唯共一清淨心如來之藏平等法身也。此明第一圓融無礙法界法門竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Question: If you say that within the worldly truth each and every phenomenal mark is the whole substance of the truth of the real, then the true mind pervades all places. How does this differ in meaning from the spiritual self that non-Buddhists imagine to pervade all places? Answer: What non-Buddhists imagine is that there are dharmas outside the mind — large and small, far and near, the three times and the six paths, distinctly real — only that, because the spiritual self is subtle, vast, and great, it pervades all places like space. This is seeing that the marks of real phenomena differ from the spiritual self, and the mark of the spiritual self differs from real phenomena. Even if they imagine a self identical with phenomena, self and phenomena being one, they still grasp phenomena as real, and this and that do not merge. Within the Buddhadharma it is not like this. It knows that all dharmas are made by the mind; only because of the conditioned arising of the mind's nature, distinct marks are not absent; yet although the marks are distinct, they take only the one mind as substance. Because substance serves as function, it is said that the reality-limit reaches every place — it does not mean that there are real phenomena outside the mind and the mind pervades and is within them, which is called reaching.

西竺有一種黃髮外道，為九十六種外道中最高者。立二十五諦。諦者，謂其理真實也。第一為冥諦。第二十五為神我。神我為其主要之義。蓋計諸法從冥諦生，而神我即受用諸法者也。因彼初用苦功，遏捺六識妄想，反入七識。以為功已究竟，即執八識之見分為我。見此我微妙廣大，能徧於一切山河大地，（此即八識之相分。）因以為神，故名為神我也。又外道神我之計，復有二別。一者計異物是我。謂物我雖異，我能徧於一切物也。二者計即物是我。謂徧處是物，即徧一切處是我也。此等外道。如不謗佛，死後生非非想天，報終仍入輪廻。

Ru-Yi Translation

In India there was a kind of yellow-haired non-Buddhist, the highest among the ninety-six kinds of non-Buddhists, who set up twenty-five truths. "Truth" means that their principle is true and real. The first is the dark truth; the twenty-fifth is the spiritual self. The spiritual self is their principal doctrine. For they reckon that all dharmas are born from the dark truth, and the spiritual self is the one who enjoys all dharmas. Because at first they used ascetic effort to suppress the deluded thinking of the six consciousnesses, they instead entered the seventh consciousness; thinking their effort ultimate, they grasped the seeing portion of the eighth consciousness as self. Seeing this self as subtle, vast, and great, able to pervade all mountains, rivers, and the great earth (this is the image portion of the eighth consciousness), they took it as spirit; hence it is called the spiritual self. Further, the non-Buddhist speculation about the spiritual self has two kinds. First, reckoning that what is different from things is the self: that is, although things and self are different, the self can pervade all things. Second, reckoning that what is identical with things is the self: that is, what pervades all places is things, and that very pervading of all places is the self. If such non-Buddhists do not slander the Buddha, after death they are born in the heaven of neither perception nor non-perception; when the reward is exhausted, they still enter the cycle of rebirth.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The Great Master feared that people would not understand the principle that each and every phenomenal mark is the whole substance of the truth of the real, and would suspect that the true mind pervading all places and the spiritual self pervading all places do not differ in meaning; then they would be confused with non-Buddhists, which would do no small harm. Therefore he set up a question and answer to refute it. You must know that what non-Buddhists speculate — whether that the self pervades all, or that self and phenomena are one — always grasps phenomenal marks as really existing and grasps dharmas outside the mind. The Buddhadharma is not like this. It knows that objects are empty and that all those dharmas are made by the mind. Although, because of the conditioned arising of the mind-nature, differentiated phenomenal marks are not absent, in reality they take only the one mind as substance. Because it is the whole substance giving rise to function, it says "the reality-limit reaches every place." The text says, "it does not mean that there are real phenomena outside the mind and the mind pervades and is within them, which is called reaching": this directly explains that it is utterly unlike the non-Buddhists, who have dharmas outside the mind and speculate that a spiritual self pervades all places.

Root text: The śramaṇa said: "Close your eyes and recall in imagination a small pore on your body. Can you see it?" The visitor imagined a small pore and replied: "I already see it clearly." The śramaṇa said: "Close your eyes and recall in imagination a great city, several tens of li across. Can you see it?" The visitor imagined the city and replied: "I see it clearly in my mind." The śramaṇa said: "Do the pore and the city differ in size?" The visitor said: "They differ." The śramaṇa said: "Were the pore and the city just now only made by the mind?" The visitor said: "They were made by the mind." The śramaṇa said: "Does your mind have small and large?" The visitor said: "The mind has no form or mark; how could it be seen to have large and small?" The śramaṇa said: "When you imagined the pore, did you make it with a reduced bit of mind, or did you make it using the whole one mind?" The śramaṇa said: "When you imagined the great city, did you make it using only your own one mind, or did you also obtain other people's minds and spirits to make it together?" Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: The śramaṇa said: "Then the whole substance of the one mind makes only one small pore, and its whole substance can also make a great city. For this reason, taking up the small to take in the large, there is no large that is not small; taking up the large to include the small, there is no small that is not large. Because there is no small that is not large, the large enters the small and the large is not reduced; because there is no large that is not small, the small contains the large and the small is not increased.
