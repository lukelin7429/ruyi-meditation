Ultimate Awakening, the Four Buddha-Natures, and the Dharma-Body

Part 3 completes Dixian's commentary on "buddha-nature" and begins the remaining names of the one mind. It first finishes the stage of awakening in resemblance: the abiding ground of ignorance thins part by part from the first thought of setting out to cultivate, because the wisdom that knows objects to be illusory perfumes the mind while the perfuming force of subtle ignorance weakens. Dixian then follows the root text through awakening to a portion of the truth (the forty-one stages from the first abiding to equal awakening) and ultimate awakening, where the vajra unobstructed wisdom cuts off the last trace of ignorance and initial awakening unites with original awakening as nondiscriminating wisdom. The simile of still water illuminating inwardly closes the buddha-nature of wisdom; brief sections follow on the buddha-natures of reward and response, of emergence from obstruction, and of equality. Four doubts are then resolved: clinging to the nature and abandoning cultivation (with the six identities), whether nonawakening originally exists, spontaneity versus causes and conditions, and whether the mind is also the nature of ignorance. The part ends with the Dharma-body, explained as the mind's capacity to hold and manifest both defiled and pure dharmas, and with the root text on the tathāgatagarbha. Pure Land recitation is again offered as the practical path throughout.

Awakening in Resemblance: Verifying the Root from the Branches

Chinese Source

以此論之。非直果時迷事無明滅息，無明住地亦少分除也。若不分分漸除者，果時無明不得分分漸滅。但相微難彰，是故不說住地分滅也。今且約迷事無明滅後，以說住地漸滅因由。即知一念發修已來，亦能漸滅也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Considering it in this way, not only does ignorance in the fruit phase, deluded about phenomena, cease and come to rest, but the abiding ground of ignorance is also removed in small part. If it were not removed gradually, part by part, ignorance in the fruit phase could not cease gradually, part by part; but its marks are minute and hard to make visible, so the partial cessation of the abiding ground is not spoken of. Now, taking the time after the cessation of ignorance deluded about phenomena, I will explain the reasons for the gradual cessation of the abiding ground; from this you will know that from the first thought of setting out to cultivate, it is also able to cease gradually.

Chinese Source

以此論之下，牒前起後。謂根本細惑，（無明住地也。亦即子時無明。）雖不與枝末粗惑（即果時無明。）同滅。但粗惑既滅，細惑亦少分除。若不下，因末驗本略明其理。謂何以知其少分除耶？蓋果時無明，即是不了。子時無明，即是不覺。今既了達諸法心作，唯虛無實，圓解成就。非迷時全不覺知者可比。即此便是住地無明漸除之相。若不分分漸除，則是依然迷而不覺。必不能大開圓解，了達諸法是虛。即果時無明，何能分滅耶？然今但說果時無明滅，而不說住地分除者，以其覺相甚微。（習氣未除，即是覺相微也。）未能全彰，故不說耳。今且下，從後追前略明其由。謂今於果時無明滅後，知其住地漸滅者，以其圓解已成，證相似覺也。然則當其在觀行位，發於一念進修之心時，是心即是發覺初心。即可知其自彼時來，住地無明亦已漸滅矣。何況今至十信位耶？一念二字最要，言其發修已來，只此一念，更無雜念。若不如此，何能解成？

Ru-Yi Translation

"Considering it in this way" and what follows takes up what came before and raises what comes after. That is, although the fundamental subtle delusion (the abiding ground of ignorance, which is also ignorance in the seed phase) does not cease together with the branch coarse delusion (ignorance in the fruit phase), once the coarse delusion has ceased, the subtle delusion is also removed in small part. "If it were not" and what follows briefly explains the principle by verifying the root from the branches. That is: how do we know that it is removed in small part? Ignorance in the fruit phase is not understanding; ignorance in the seed phase is nonawakening. Now that one has penetrated that all dharmas are made by the mind, only illusory and without reality, perfect understanding is accomplished; this cannot be compared with the time of delusion, when there was no awareness at all. This itself is the mark of the gradual removal of the ignorance of the abiding ground. If it were not removed gradually, part by part, one would still be deluded and unawakened as before, and certainly could not greatly open perfect understanding and penetrate that dharmas are illusory; then how could ignorance in the fruit phase cease in part? Yet the reason it now speaks only of the cessation of ignorance in the fruit phase and does not speak of the partial removal of the abiding ground is that its mark of awakening is very minute (the habit-energies not yet being removed is what makes the mark of awakening minute) and cannot yet be fully made visible; therefore it is not spoken of. "Now" and what follows briefly explains the reason by tracing back from the later to the earlier. That is, the reason one now knows, after the cessation of ignorance in the fruit phase, that its abiding ground gradually ceases is that perfect understanding has been accomplished and one has realized awakening in resemblance. That being so, at the time when one was at the stage of contemplative practice and gave rise to a single thought of the mind to advance in cultivation, this mind was itself the initial mind of setting out to awaken. One can know that from that time on, the ignorance of the abiding ground had already been gradually ceasing — how much more now, on reaching the stage of the ten faiths! The two characters 一念 ("a single thought") are most essential: they say that from setting out to cultivate there is only this single thought, with no other mixed thoughts. If it were not so, how could understanding be accomplished?

Chinese Source

由此可知，持誦彌陀聖號，其要妙惟在一念。如能一念念佛，即能圓伏五住煩惱。若或未然，念既不一，則一邊念佛，一邊起煩惱，何能做到一心不亂？未免徒勞辛苦矣。又經教但言界內見思滅時，圓伏界外塵沙。（即見思細惑。）不言漸滅。今知但以相微難彰，所以不言。實則若非分分漸滅，即亦不能圓伏耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

From this we can know that in holding and reciting the holy name of Amitābha, the essential wonder lies only in a single thought. If one can recite the Buddha's name with a single thought, one can perfectly subdue the afflictions of the five abiding grounds. If not, the thought is not single: one recites the Buddha's name on one side and gives rise to afflictions on the other; how could one reach a single, undistracted mind? It cannot avoid being wasted toil. Further, the sūtras and teachings say only that when views and habituated thought within the realms cease, dust-and-sand outside the realms is perfectly subdued (that is, the subtle delusions of views and habituated thought); they do not say that it gradually ceases. Now we know that it is not spoken of only because its marks are minute and hard to make visible. In reality, if it did not cease gradually, part by part, it could not be perfectly subdued either.

Explaining the Reason for Cessation

Chinese Source

（戌）二正明滅由。

Ru-Yi Translation

(U) Second, explaining the reason for cessation proper.

Chinese Source

此義云何？謂以二義因緣故，住地無明業識等，漸已微薄。二義者何？一者，知境虛智熏心故，令舊無明住地習氣及業識等漸除也。何以故？智是明法，性能治無明故。二者，細無明虛執及虛境熏心故，雖更起無明住地等，即復輕弱。不同前迷境等所熏起者。何以故？以能熏微細故，所起不覺，亦即薄也。以此義故，住地無明業識等，漸已損滅也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: What does this mean? Because of the causes and conditions of two meanings, the ignorance of the abiding ground, karmic consciousness, and the rest gradually become faint and thin. What are the two meanings? First, the wisdom that knows objects to be illusory perfumes the mind, so that the old habit-energy of the abiding ground of ignorance, karmic consciousness, and the rest are gradually removed. Why? Because wisdom is a dharma of clarity, by nature able to counteract ignorance. Second, subtle ignorance, illusory grasping, and illusory objects perfume the mind, so that although the abiding ground of ignorance and the rest arise again, they are light and weak, not like those aroused by the earlier perfuming of [ignorance] deluded about objects and the rest. Why? Because what perfumes is minute, the nonawakening it arouses is also thin. For this reason the ignorance of the abiding ground, karmic consciousness, and the rest gradually diminish and cease.

Chinese Source

此義云何句，牒前標起。前云果時無明滅，住地亦少分除者，何故耶？以有二義為其因緣也。不然。住地及業識等，不能漸薄。等者，等於虛狀種子等也。云何二義。一約知境虛智熏心義。以明能熏之智是明，性能對治無明，故令原有之住地漸除。二約細無明虛執等熏心義。以明能熏之力輕弱，不同迷境力強，故新起之不覺亦薄。以有此二因緣義，所以住地無明等漸滅也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The phrase "What does this mean?" takes up the preceding and announces what follows. Earlier it said that when ignorance in the fruit phase ceases, the abiding ground is also removed in small part. Why is this? Because there are two meanings serving as its causes and conditions. Otherwise the abiding ground, karmic consciousness, and the rest could not gradually thin. "And the rest" includes the seeds of illusory forms and so on. What are the two meanings? The first is in terms of the meaning of the wisdom that knows objects to be illusory perfuming the mind. It makes clear that the wisdom that perfumes is clarity and by nature can counteract ignorance; therefore it causes the existing abiding ground to be gradually removed. The second is in terms of the meaning of subtle ignorance, illusory grasping, and the rest perfuming the mind. It makes clear that the force that perfumes is light and weak, unlike the strong force of [ignorance] deluded about objects; therefore the newly arising nonawakening is also thin. Because there are these two meanings of causes and conditions, the ignorance of the abiding ground and the rest gradually cease.

Chinese Source

○知境虛智，即無塵智。約迷境言，名為意識。在觀行位，此識漸知諸法唯虛，轉名知境虛智。至相似位，見思粗垢脫落，圓解成就，復轉名為無塵智。吾輩修行，宜常將所知之境虛道理，存於心中，反熏淨心。蓋知境虛是智。智乃明法如燈。無明則如闇。以燈照闇，無闇不明。古德云：「千年闇室，一燈能照。」可顯智性能破無明之理。無明既破，塵闇即無。故轉名無塵智也。

Ru-Yi Translation

○ The wisdom that knows objects to be illusory is the wisdom of no sense-objects. Spoken of in terms of being deluded about objects, it is called mind-consciousness. At the stage of contemplative practice, this consciousness gradually knows that all dharmas are only illusory, and it changes its name to the wisdom that knows objects to be illusory. On reaching the stage of resemblance, the coarse defilements of views and habituated thought fall away and perfect understanding is accomplished, and it changes its name again to the wisdom of no sense-objects. We who cultivate practice should always keep in our minds the principle, already known, that objects are illusory, and turn it back to perfume the pure mind. For knowing that objects are illusory is wisdom. Wisdom is a dharma of clarity, like a lamp; ignorance is like darkness. When a lamp shines on darkness, there is no darkness it does not brighten. An ancient worthy said: "A room dark for a thousand years — a single lamp can light it." This can show the principle that the nature of wisdom can break ignorance. Once ignorance is broken, the darkness of sense-objects is gone; therefore its name changes to the wisdom of no sense-objects.

Chinese Source

○細無明，即子時根本無明。虛執，即上文見有虛相，此執亦是妄想者是。虛境，即上文細無明妄想所執，以其細故，名為虛境者是。細無明等能熏力弱，所起不覺即薄者。譬如闇主不為愚僕所聳，橫暴即為損減是也。

Ru-Yi Translation

○ Subtle ignorance is fundamental ignorance in the seed phase. Illusory grasping is what the text above called "seeing that there are illusory marks; this grasping is also deluded thinking." Illusory objects are what the text above called "what is grasped by subtle ignorance and deluded thinking; because it is subtle, it is called illusory objects." That the perfuming force of subtle ignorance and the rest is weak, so that the nonawakening it arouses is thin, is like a dim-witted master who is not egged on by foolish servants: his violence is accordingly lessened.

Concluding the Earlier by Analogy with the Later

Chinese Source

（戌）三例後結前。

Ru-Yi Translation

(U) Third, concluding the earlier by analogy with the later.

Chinese Source

如迷事無明滅後，既有此義。應知一念創始發修之時，無明住地即分滅也。以其分分滅故，所起智慧分分增明，故得果時迷事無明滅也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Since this is so after the cessation of ignorance deluded about phenomena, you should know that at the time of a single thought first setting out to cultivate, the abiding ground of ignorance already ceases in part. Because it ceases part by part, the wisdom that arises grows clearer part by part; hence ignorance in the fruit phase, deluded about phenomena, is able to cease.

Chinese Source

此義者，指上文二義因緣而言。謂於果時迷事無明滅後，既以有此二義故，住地無明等漸已微薄。即可例知未滅以前（即是觀行位一念創始發修之時），亦必有能熏之智分明，及能熏之無明分弱二義，而使無明住地分滅也。以其下復換言以申明之。謂自一念創始發修以來。以住地分分滅故，所起智慧乃分分增明。不然。果時無明何由得滅，而登圓十信耶？此以結成上來因末驗本文中，若不分除不得分滅之義也。分分增明，謂自觀行覺至相似覺，分分增進。遂由知境虛智，轉為無塵智。智是明法，故曰增明也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"This" refers to the two meanings of causes and conditions in the text above. That is, since after the cessation of ignorance in the fruit phase, deluded about phenomena, the ignorance of the abiding ground and the rest gradually become faint and thin because of these two meanings, one can know by analogy that before its cessation (that is, at the time of a single thought first setting out to cultivate, at the stage of contemplative practice) there must also have been the two meanings — the wisdom that perfumes being partly clear and the ignorance that perfumes being partly weak — causing the abiding ground of ignorance to cease in part. "Because it" and what follows restates this in other words to make it clear. That is, since a single thought first set out to cultivate, because the abiding ground ceases part by part, the wisdom that arises grows clearer part by part. Otherwise, how could ignorance in the fruit phase cease, so that one ascends to the ten faiths of the perfect [teaching]? This concludes the meaning, in the text above on verifying the root from the branches, that if it were not removed in part, [the fruit] could not cease in part. "Grows clearer part by part" means advancing part by part from awakening in contemplative practice to awakening in resemblance, so that the wisdom that knows objects to be illusory changes into the wisdom of no sense-objects. Wisdom is a dharma of clarity; therefore it says "grows clearer."

Chinese Source

○行人修到相似位，為內凡位。從此更進，即轉凡入聖。即是轉入分真位。以南嶽大師之功行，尚復謙示僅居鐵輪。（即相似位也。一名六根清淨位。）可見由凡夫證入此位，實非易易。相似云者，言不過與常住真心相像而已，猶未證真也。故繼此而明分真覺。分真者，分分證真，亦曰分證。

Ru-Yi Translation

○ When the practitioner has cultivated as far as the stage of resemblance, this is the stage of the inner ordinary [beings]. Advancing further from here, one turns from the ordinary and enters the holy, that is, one turns and enters the stage of a portion of the truth. Even with the merit and practice of Great Master Nanyue, he humbly showed himself as dwelling only at the iron wheel. (That is, the stage of resemblance, also called the stage of the purity of the six faculties.) We can see that for an ordinary being to realize and enter this stage is truly not easy. "Resemblance" means only that it resembles the eternally abiding true mind; it has not yet realized the true. Therefore, following this, it explains awakening to a portion of the truth. "A portion of the truth" means realizing the true part by part; it is also called partial realization.

Awakening to a Portion of the Truth

Chinese Source

（未）四分真覺。

Ru-Yi Translation

(R) Fourth, awakening to a portion of the truth.

Chinese Source

自迷事無明滅後。業識及住地無明漸薄故，所起虛狀果報，亦轉輕妙。不同前也。以是義故，似識轉轉明利。似色等法，復不令意識生迷。以內識生外色塵等，俱細利故，無塵之智倍明。無明妄想極薄。還復熏心，復令住地無明業識習氣，漸欲向盡。所現無塵之智，為倍明了。如是念念，轉轉熏習故，無明住地垂盡。所起無塵之智，即能知彼虛狀果報，體性非有。本自不生，今即無滅，唯是一心。體無分別。以唯心外無法故，此智即是金剛無礙智也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: After ignorance deluded about phenomena has ceased, karmic consciousness and the ignorance of the abiding ground gradually thin, so that the illusory forms of retribution that arise also become lighter and finer, not like before. For this reason quasi-consciousness becomes ever clearer and sharper, and quasi-form and the other dharmas no longer cause mind-consciousness to give rise to delusion, because the inner consciousness that arises and the outer form-objects and the rest are all fine and sharp. The wisdom of no sense-objects grows doubly clear, ignorance and deluded thinking become extremely thin, and these in turn perfume the mind, again causing the habit-energy of the ignorance of the abiding ground and karmic consciousness gradually to approach exhaustion, and the wisdom of no sense-objects that appears becomes doubly clear. Through such perfuming, thought after thought, ever further, the abiding ground of ignorance is about to be exhausted, and the wisdom of no sense-objects that arises is then able to know that the substance and nature of those illusory forms of retribution are not existent — originally they do not arise of themselves, and now they do not cease; they are only the one mind, whose substance is without discrimination, because there are no dharmas outside the mind alone. This wisdom is the vajra unobstructed wisdom.

Chinese Source

此科文雖不多，而進修位次，共有四十一。即由初住以至等覺是也。（十住，十行，十回向，十地，加等覺，共四十一位。）大眾須知。何以進修要歷許多位次？實因凡夫自無始時，一念不覺。由是而子果無明，相熏相生，惑業苦三，連環不斷。直至於今，已歷塵沙劫數。病根深固，豈易拔除。所以返妄歸真，必須展轉熏修，展轉增進，方能拔盡病根也。可見吾人，但令一念不覺，回頭甚難。如給孤獨長者，造祇桓精舍時，掘地見蟻。舍利弗長老，天眼垂淚。悲其從毘婆尸佛時，即墮蟻身。至今第七佛釋迦出世，猶未得脫。嗟乎！一失足成千古恨。再回頭是百年身。以此蟻觀之，豈止百年。恐經過微塵點劫，若不得聞佛法，破我迷夢，那有回頭之望。

Ru-Yi Translation

Although the text of this section is not long, the stages of advancing cultivation number forty-one in all, namely from the first abiding up to equal awakening. (The ten abidings, the ten practices, the ten dedications, the ten grounds, and equal awakening make forty-one stages in all.) The assembly must know: why must advancing cultivation pass through so many stages? Truly because ordinary beings, from beginningless time, have had a single thought of nonawakening. From this, seed and fruit ignorance perfume and give birth to each other, and the three — delusion, karma, and suffering — are linked without break. Down to the present they have already passed through eons as numerous as dust and sand. The root of the illness is deep and firm; how could it be easily pulled out? Therefore, to turn back from the false and return to the true, one must perfume and cultivate step by step and advance step by step; only then can the root of the illness be pulled out completely. We can see that once we have a single thought of nonawakening, turning the head back is very difficult. For example, when the elder Anāthapiṇḍada was building the Jetavana Vihāra, ants were seen when the ground was dug. The elder Śāriputra, with his heavenly eye, shed tears, grieving that they had fallen into the bodies of ants from the time of Vipaśyin Buddha, and that now, when Śākyamuni, the seventh buddha, had appeared in the world, they still had not gotten free. Alas! "One misstep becomes a regret for a thousand ages; when one turns the head again, it is a body of a hundred years." Looking at it by these ants, how could it be only a hundred years? I fear they will pass through eons as numerous as fine dust motes; if they do not get to hear the Buddha-Dharma, which breaks our dream of delusion, what hope is there of turning back?

Chinese Source

然則我輩幸得人身又聞佛法。光陰易過，豈可蹉跎。又須知修到初住，方登不退。若不修圓頓法門，須歷三大阿僧祇劫，乃入初住。人身易失，惡緣又多，此長劫中，能保自己不退墮麼。然則奈何。幸有釋迦教我。彌陀接我。但能一心念佛，發願往生。盡此報身，徑赴蓮池海會，便是阿鞞拔致。阿鞞拔致者，不退之義。即初住位也。蓋淨土一宗，乃極圓極頓之法。一修一切修。不歷僧祇。亦無漸次。所以能徑登不退。我輩幸聞此法，安得不感謝佛恩乎！大眾思之，猛進猛進。

Ru-Yi Translation

That being so, we have fortunately obtained a human body and have also heard the Buddha-Dharma. Time passes easily; how can we let it slip away? Further, you must know that only on reaching the first abiding does one ascend to non-regression. If one does not cultivate the perfect and sudden dharma-gate, one must pass through three great asaṃkhyeya eons before entering the first abiding. The human body is easily lost, and evil conditions are many; in these long eons, can you guarantee that you yourself will not regress and fall? Then what is to be done? Fortunately Śākyamuni teaches us and Amitābha receives us. If only we recite the Buddha's name with a single mind and make the vow to be reborn, then at the end of this reward-body we go straight to the oceanlike assembly of the lotus pond: this is avaivartika. Avaivartika means non-regression; it is the stage of the first abiding. For the one school of Pure Land is a most perfect and most sudden Dharma: to cultivate one is to cultivate all. It does not pass through asaṃkhyeyas, nor does it have gradual stages; therefore one can ascend directly to non-regression. We have fortunately heard this Dharma; how can we not be grateful for the Buddha's kindness! Assembly, reflect on this: advance vigorously, advance vigorously.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "Further, although this mind's substance" on, it explains the capacity and support of the pure portion; following the text, it can be understood. "Measureless defiled dharmas": the mind's substance is covered by the obstructions of the afflictions of views and habituated thought, dust-and-sand, and ignorance, and by karma, suffering, and so on; therefore it says "measureless." "More numerous than the sands of the Ganges": the sands of the Ganges are extremely fine and their number is measureless; whenever the sūtras speak of the Ganges sands, it is a simile for a measureless number. "More numerous" means surpassing. "Without outflows" has the sense of perfect fulfillment. Using the word "although" above and "it fully possesses" below shows the meaning of following conditions yet being unchanging, what is called the purity of the nature not changing. These pure natures originally possess the merits of measureless pure dharmas without outflows; therefore it says "inherent meritorious dharmas." Now we ordinary beings, while in defilement, further have within our one mind perfect merits of inherent purity surpassing measureless numbers; the present moment is it. That a single thought fully possesses them is what is called three thousand inherently possessed in principle. After the initial mind of setting out to awaken, one perfumes it with pure karma, such as the four noble truths, the twelve links of conditioned arising, the thirty-seven factors of the path, up to loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity, the six perfections, the eighteen exclusive qualities, and so on: what is called three thousand brought about in actual phenomena. Yet pure karma is measureless; it can be summed up in the two words "blessing" and "wisdom." Because it is perfumed by this pure karma, the purity of the nature is gradually revealed. These pure natures can further take hold of this pure perfuming, manifest pure function, and accomplish the buddha-fruit. Yet these originally possessed measureless merits of inherent purity, together with the capacities and phenomenal functions of holding and manifesting, are also all in fact established alike in dependence on the one mind, and are neither one with nor different from the mind. The last sentence concludes. This concludes the meaning of the Dharma-body in terms of the pure portion. In contrast with the defiled portion, this is the Dharma-body emerged from obstruction, that is, the pure Dharma-body, the wondrous ultimate Dharma-body.

Chinese Source

問曰：云何復名此心為如來藏？答曰：有三義。一者，能藏名藏。二者，所藏名藏。三者，能生名藏。所言能藏者，復有二種。一者，如來果德法身。二者，眾生性德淨心。並能包含染淨二性，及染淨二事，無所妨礙。故言能藏名藏。藏體平等，名之為如。平等緣起，目之為來。此即是能藏名如來藏也。第二所藏名藏者。即此真心，而為無明㲉藏所覆藏故，名為所藏也。藏體無異無相，名之為如。體備染淨二用，目之為來。故言所藏名藏也。第三能生名藏者。如女胎藏，能生於子。此心亦爾。體具染淨二性之用，故依染淨二種熏力，能生世間出世間法也，是故經云：「如來藏者，是善不善因」。又復經言。心性是一，云何能生種種果報？又復經言。諸佛正徧知海，從心想而生也。故染淨平等，名之為如。能生染淨，目之為來。故言能生名如來藏也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Question: Why further call this mind the tathāgatagarbha? Answer: There are three meanings: first, what stores is called the storehouse; second, what is stored is called the storehouse; third, what can give birth is called the storehouse. As for "what stores," there are again two kinds: first, the Dharma-body of the Tathāgata's fruition-virtue; second, the pure mind of sentient beings' inherent virtue. Both can contain the two natures of defilement and purity and the two phenomena of defilement and purity without hindrance; therefore it is said that what stores is called the storehouse. The storehouse's substance being equal is called "thus" (如); its equal conditioned arising is designated "come" (來); this is what stores, called the tathāgatagarbha. Second, "what is stored is called the storehouse": this true mind is covered and stored by the shell-store of ignorance; therefore it is called what is stored. The storehouse's substance being without difference and without marks is called "thus"; the substance being endowed with the two functions of defilement and purity is designated "come"; therefore it is said that what is stored is called the storehouse. Third, "what can give birth is called the storehouse": just as a woman's womb-store can give birth to a child, so too with this mind. Its substance possesses the functions of the two natures of defilement and purity, and therefore, in dependence on the two perfuming forces of defilement and purity, it can give birth to worldly and world-transcending dharmas. Therefore a sūtra says: "The tathāgatagarbha is the cause of the good and the not-good." Further, a sūtra says: "The mind's nature is one; how can it give birth to various fruits of retribution?" Further, a sūtra says: "The ocean of perfect and universal knowing of all buddhas arises from the mind's own contemplation." Therefore defilement and purity being equal is called "thus"; being able to give birth to defilement and purity is designated "come"; therefore it is said that what can give birth is called the tathāgatagarbha.
