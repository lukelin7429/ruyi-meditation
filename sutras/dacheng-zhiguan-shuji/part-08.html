Tranquility and Function, Mind-Consciousness as What Relies, and the Refutation of Self-Realization

Part 8 completes the root text's "why it is relied upon" and works through most of "by what it is relied upon." Tranquility is the mind's substance apart from marks; function is its contrary and accordant capacities, so calming benefits oneself and contemplation benefits others. Answering the fear that function only prolongs birth-and-death, Dixian quotes Zhiyi on fire that the skilled need not put out: remove the illness, not the dharmas. The text then names mind-consciousness, the sixth consciousness, as what relies on the pure mind. Dixian sets out the eight consciousnesses and how each turns into wisdom, and maps the root text's account of calming and contemplation onto the stages of name, contemplative practice, resemblance, and partial realization, with notes on the four immeasurable minds, the four means of attraction, and the six perfections. Two refutations follow. Against the Lesser Vehicle, the pure mind must be relied upon because momentary thoughts cannot store what they learn. Against Mahāyāna practitioners who would use the pure mind alone, Dixian lays out six pairs of subject and object and traces four layers of questions. The part ends in the root text's examination of "realization in the dark," with self-realization refuted and realization by another under refutation.

Tranquility and Function

Chinese Source

問曰：何謂心體寂用二義？答曰：心體平等，離一切相，即是寂義。體具違順二用，即是用義。是故修習止行，即能除滅虛妄紛動，令此心體寂靜離相。即為自利。修習觀行，令此心用顯現繁興。即為利他。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Question: What are the two meanings of tranquility and function of the mind's substance? Answer: The mind's substance is equal and apart from all marks; this is the meaning of tranquility. The substance possesses the two functions, contrary and accordant; this is the meaning of function. Therefore, cultivating the practice of calming can remove and extinguish false agitation and make this mind's substance tranquil and apart from marks; this is benefiting oneself. Cultivating the practice of contemplation makes this mind's function manifest and flourish abundantly; this is benefiting others.

Chinese Source

平等二字，即是離一切相之意。心體本來非相非無相，離過絕非，真如平等。故曰即是寂義。違順二用，即前文所明染淨二性。一一凡聖心體，本具染淨性用。故曰即是用義。眾生無始不覺自動。妄想虛境，緣念不息，故致流轉。今修止行。凡有無等四句法，一切俱離。則妄動除滅，而證寂靜心體，得大自在。故曰即為自利。修習觀行。熏令違用繁興，則倒駕慈航，大悲度眾。熏令順用顯現，則大智圓明，功德滿足。故曰即為利他。又止觀二行，若通而論之。則約修證言，止觀無非自利。約教化言，止觀皆為利他。若別而論之。則止行顯體，於自利偏勝。觀行顯用，於利他偏多也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The word "equal" has the meaning of "apart from all marks." The mind's substance is originally neither mark nor no-mark; apart from faults and cut off from negations, it is suchness, equal. Therefore it says, "this is the meaning of tranquility." The two functions, contrary and accordant, are the two natures of defilement and purity explained in the earlier text. The mind's substance of each and every ordinary being and sage originally possesses the functions of the nature, defiled and pure; therefore it says, "this is the meaning of function." Sentient beings from beginningless time have moved of themselves in nonawakening; deluded thinking and illusory objects, thoughts taking objects without cease, have led to their flowing on. Now in cultivating the practice of calming, all the tetralemma dharmas of existence, nonexistence, and so on, are entirely left behind; then deluded movement is removed and extinguished, and one realizes the tranquil mind's substance and attains great sovereignty. Therefore it says, "this is benefiting oneself." In cultivating the practice of contemplation, when the contrary function is perfumed so that it flourishes abundantly, one steers the ship of compassion back and with great compassion brings beings across; when the accordant function is perfumed so that it manifests, great wisdom is perfectly bright and merit is complete. Therefore it says, "this is benefiting others." Further, as for the two practices of calming and contemplation, discussed in general: in terms of cultivation and realization, calming and contemplation are both benefiting oneself; in terms of teaching and converting, calming and contemplation are both for benefiting others. Discussed specifically: the practice of calming reveals the substance and leans more toward benefiting oneself; the practice of contemplation reveals function and is mostly for benefiting others.

Dispelling Doubts: Removing the Illness, Not the Dharmas

Chinese Source

（辛）二釋疑。

Ru-Yi Translation

(H) Second, dispelling doubts.

Chinese Source

問曰：修止觀者，為除生死。若令顯現繁興，此即轉增流浪。答曰：不然。但除其病，而不除法。病在執情，不在大用。是故熾然六道，權現無間，即是違用顯現。而復畢竟清淨，不為世染，智慧照明。故相好圓備，身心安住勝妙境界，具足一切諸佛功德。即是順用顯現也。此明止觀依止中，何故依止竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Question: Those who cultivate calming-and-contemplation do so to remove birth-and-death. If [the function] is made to manifest and flourish abundantly, this only increases the wandering. Answer: Not so. "Remove only the illness, and not the dharmas." The illness lies in the grasping of feelings, not in the great function. Therefore, blazing through the six paths and provisionally manifesting even in [the hell] without interval — this is the manifestation of the contrary function; and yet being ultimately pure, not defiled by the world, with wisdom illuminating, the marks and excellent features perfectly complete, body and mind abiding securely in supremely wondrous spheres, fully possessing all the merits of all buddhas — this is the manifestation of the accordant function. This completes the explanation, within the support of calming-and-contemplation, of why it is relied upon.

Chinese Source

問者以顯現繁興，濫同虛妄紛動。故來轉增生死流浪之疑。不知流轉生死，病在妄情紛動，執虛為實。豈在違順大用。故但除其情執之病，不妨大用繁興。若除病而并除一切世間出世間法。則沉空滯寂，何足為大乘？大師所以不然其說也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The questioner confuses "manifesting and flourishing abundantly" with false agitation, and so he raises the doubt that it only increases the wandering in birth-and-death. He does not know that the illness of flowing on in birth-and-death lies in the agitation of deluded feelings, grasping the illusory as real; how could it lie in the great functions, contrary and accordant? Therefore one removes only the illness of emotional grasping, and this does not prevent the great function from flourishing abundantly. If, in removing the illness, one also removed all worldly and world-transcending dharmas, one would sink into emptiness and stagnate in quiescence; how would that be worthy of the Mahāyāna? That is why the Great Master does not accept his claim.

Chinese Source

是故菩薩熾然六道，應以人天身得度者，即現人天身度之。應以三惡道身得度者，即現三惡道身度之。甚至大權示現於阿鼻地獄。即此便是違用顯現。豈與凡夫迷真逐妄，為業力所牽者同哉！所以雖現違用，而復畢竟清淨。以清淨故，所以在塵不染。以不染故，所以智慧照明。又復智慧照明，所以不為世染。不為世染，所以畢竟清淨。由是之故。雖其熾然六道。而復相好光明，圓明備足。雖權現無間。而其身心，仍復安住清淨勝妙境界。雖違用顯現，而即具足一切諸佛之性淨功德。具足功德者，所謂三德圓明是也。即此便是順用顯現。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore bodhisattvas blaze through the six paths. To those who should be brought across by the body of a human or a god, they manifest the body of a human or a god to bring them across; to those who should be brought across by a body of the three evil paths, they manifest a body of the three evil paths to bring them across; they even make a great provisional manifestation in the Avīci hell. This is the manifestation of the contrary function. How could it be the same as ordinary beings, deluded about the true and pursuing the false, who are dragged along by the force of karma? Therefore, though they manifest the contrary function, they are yet ultimately pure. Because they are pure, they are in the dust but not defiled; because they are not defiled, their wisdom illuminates. Further, because their wisdom illuminates, they are not defiled by the world; because they are not defiled by the world, they are ultimately pure. For this reason, though they blaze through the six paths, their marks, excellent features, and radiance are yet perfectly bright and fully complete; though they provisionally manifest in [the hell] without interval, their body and mind still abide securely in pure, supremely wondrous spheres; though the contrary function is manifest, they fully possess the merits of the pure nature of all buddhas. "Fully possessing merits" is what is called the perfect brightness of the three virtues. This is the manifestation of the accordant function.

Chinese Source

以此兩兩對勘，可知順用方能違用。違用即是順用。染即是淨。相即是空。故以淨現染，則染而非染。以空顯相，則相即無相。智者大師曰：火能燒人。得法術者，出入無礙，不須除火。故八萬四千煩惱，凡夫為之疲勞。諸佛菩薩以為佛事。蕅益大師曰：迷者被違順所用。達者能用違順。所云得法術者，修止觀之謂也。不須除火者，除病不除法之謂也。為之疲勞，所以法身流轉五道。以為佛事，所以生死即是湼槃。被違順用者，由於虛妄紛動。能用違順者，因其智慧照明也。明得此理，何須致疑？

Ru-Yi Translation

Comparing these pair by pair, we can know that only the accordant function can be the contrary function, and the contrary function is itself the accordant function; defilement is itself purity, and mark is itself emptiness. Therefore, manifesting defilement by purity, it is defiled and yet not defiled; revealing marks by emptiness, marks are themselves no-mark. Great Master Zhizhe said: "Fire can burn people; one who has gained the art goes in and out without obstruction and need not remove the fire. Therefore the eighty-four thousand afflictions weary ordinary beings, while the buddhas and bodhisattvas take them as buddha-work." Great Master Ouyi said: "The deluded are used by the contrary and the accordant; the penetrating can use the contrary and the accordant." "One who has gained the art" means one who cultivates calming-and-contemplation. "Need not remove the fire" means removing the illness but not the dharmas. "Weary them" is why the Dharma-body flows on through the five paths; "take them as buddha-work" is why birth-and-death is itself nirvāṇa. Being used by the contrary and the accordant comes from false agitation; being able to use the contrary and the accordant is because one's wisdom illuminates. If one understands this principle, why raise doubts?

Chinese Source

又文中病在執情，不在大用，畢竟清淨，智慧照明數句。極關緊要。何則？除執情而得清淨。所謂止也。以慧明而起大用。所謂觀也。又復因慧明故，乃得清淨。修觀即所以修止也。除情妄故，乃起大用。修止即所以修觀也。如此止觀並進。寂用圓成。則煩惱菩提，非一非異。生死湼槃，不入不住。乃問者但曰修止觀者，為除生死。是但知明體，而不知達用矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the several phrases in the text, "The illness lies in the grasping of feelings, not in the great function," "ultimately pure," and "with wisdom illuminating," are of the utmost importance. Why? Removing the grasping of feelings and attaining purity is what is called calming; giving rise to the great function through the brightness of wisdom is what is called contemplation. Further, because of the brightness of wisdom one attains purity: cultivating contemplation is precisely the means of cultivating calming. Because the false feelings are removed, the great function arises: cultivating calming is precisely the means of cultivating contemplation. When calming and contemplation advance together like this, and tranquility and function are perfectly accomplished, then afflictions and bodhi are neither one nor different, and birth-and-death and nirvāṇa are neither entered nor abided in. Yet the questioner only says, "Those who cultivate calming-and-contemplation do so to remove birth-and-death"; this is knowing only how to clarify the substance and not how to penetrate function.

Chinese Source

須知如來藏具足空不空二義。空即本體。不空即是大用。體必具用，用即是體。是為理事不二。吾人自性清淨心，本來如是圓融。如是無礙。是故修習止觀，必依止此心而修。方能體用全彰，理事圓顯耳。末句，總結止觀依止中，第二明何故依止義已竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

You should know that the tathāgatagarbha fully possesses the two meanings of empty and nonempty. The empty is the original substance; the nonempty is the great function. The substance necessarily possesses function, and function is itself the substance; this is principle and phenomena being nondual. The mind pure in its own nature of each of us is originally perfectly interfused like this and unobstructed like this. Therefore, in cultivating calming-and-contemplation, one must rely upon this mind in cultivating; only so can substance and function be fully displayed and principle and phenomena be perfectly revealed. The last sentence concludes the second meaning within the support of calming-and-contemplation, explaining why it is relied upon.

By What It Is Relied Upon: Mind-Consciousness

Chinese Source

（庚）三明以何依止。分三。（辛）初分科。二解釋。三總結。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(G) Third, explaining by what it is relied upon, in three: (H) first, dividing the sections; second, explaining; third, concluding as a whole. Now the first.

Chinese Source

次明以何依止。就中復有三門差別。一明以何依止體狀。二明破小乘人執。三明破大乘人執。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, explaining by what it is relied upon. Within this there are again three gates of distinction: first, explaining the substance and form of that by which it is relied upon; second, refuting the attachments of people of the Lesser Vehicle; third, refuting the attachments of people of the Mahāyāna.

Chinese Source

以者用也。上來所明自性清淨心，是止觀之所依止。今則明用孰依止淨心而修耶？須知能依止淨心修止觀者，即第六意識是也。分為三門，釋其所以。初明意識依止之體狀者。體狀猶言行相。即明修止觀時，其行相不能離意識作用也。二、三，皆除執病之文。先破小乘執。次破大乘執。蓋小乘人執意識為究竟，而不知淨心乃所依之本體。大乘人又執淨心能自知，而不了意識乃能修之妙用。此二種執，正是意識妄情。惜乎當人不自知耳。故須次第破之，然後大乘止觀，方得成就。自此以往，皆明修行法要。大眾其善聽善攝，善思惟之。

Ru-Yi Translation

"By" (以) means "using." What was explained above is that the mind pure in its own nature is what calming-and-contemplation relies upon; now it explains by using what one relies upon the pure mind to cultivate. You should know that what is able to rely upon the pure mind to cultivate calming-and-contemplation is the sixth consciousness, mind-consciousness. He divides it into three gates to explain why. First, explaining the substance and form of mind-consciousness's relying: "substance and form" is like saying "mode of activity"; that is, it explains that when one cultivates calming-and-contemplation, the mode of activity cannot depart from the working of mind-consciousness. The second and third are both texts removing the illness of attachment: first he refutes the attachments of the Lesser Vehicle, then the attachments of the Mahāyāna. For people of the Lesser Vehicle grasp mind-consciousness as ultimate and do not know that the pure mind is the original substance relied upon; people of the Mahāyāna further grasp that the pure mind can know itself, and do not understand that mind-consciousness is the wondrous function that cultivates. These two kinds of attachment are precisely the false feelings of mind-consciousness; it is a pity that the people concerned do not know it themselves. Therefore they must be refuted in order, and only then can Mahāyāna calming-and-contemplation be accomplished. From here on, everything explains the essentials of practice. Members of the assembly, listen well, take it in well, and reflect on it well.

（辛）二解釋。即為三。（壬）初明以何依止體狀。二明破小乘人執。三明破大乘人執。初中二。（癸）初總標。二別釋。今初。

The first is in two: (J) first, the general heading; second, the specific explanation. Now the first.

Chinese Source

初明以何依止體狀者。問曰：以何依止此心修止觀？答曰：以意識依止此心修行止觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: First, explaining the substance and form of that by which it is relied upon. Question: By what does one rely upon this mind to cultivate calming-and-contemplation? Answer: By mind-consciousness one relies upon this mind to cultivate calming-and-contemplation.

Chinese Source

意識，猶言意根之識。蓋第七末那，（末那，梵語翻為意。）為生起第六識之根。故約其生起之根則為意。約其分別之用則曰識。因名之曰意識也。欲明修行止觀，必用意識之故。不可不略知八識名相。蓋自最初一念不覺，真妄和合，而成藏識。此即阿梨耶，所謂第八識是也。名為藏者。謂其能受前七識之熏，執藏根身器界之種子故。其緣第八識之見分，執之為我者，即第七末那意。見聞覺知，攀緣分別者，即第六意識。其由五根發現於外者。則為眼識，耳識，鼻識，舌識，身識，所謂前五識是也。此八個識，成則俱成。起即同起。約用成八，約體為一也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Mind-consciousness" (意識) is like saying "the consciousness of the mind-faculty." For the seventh, manas (manas is a Sanskrit word, translated as "mind," 意), is the faculty that gives rise to the sixth consciousness. In terms of the faculty that gives rise to it, it is "mind"; in terms of its function of discrimination, it is called "consciousness"; hence it is named mind-consciousness. To explain why mind-consciousness must be used in cultivating calming-and-contemplation, one must know something of the names and marks of the eight consciousnesses. From the very first thought of nonawakening, the true and the false combined and became the storehouse consciousness; this is the ālaya, the so-called eighth consciousness. It is called the storehouse because it can receive the perfuming of the first seven consciousnesses and grasps and stores the seeds of the body with its faculties and of the vessel-world. What takes the seeing portion of the eighth consciousness as object and grasps it as self is the seventh, manas, [called] "mind." What sees, hears, perceives, and knows, clinging to objects and discriminating, is the sixth, mind-consciousness. Those that manifest outwardly through the five faculties are eye consciousness, ear consciousness, nose consciousness, tongue consciousness, and body consciousness, the so-called first five consciousnesses. These eight consciousnesses, when formed, are formed together; when arising, arise together. In terms of function they are eight; in terms of substance they are one.

Chinese Source

然凡夫之前五識，全仗意識功能，助成其用。藏識固是主人公。然但能為諸法所依之本，攝持諸法，不能自造善惡。所以謂之真妄和合者。即由真心不覺自動，妄分能所，而有末那之妄執，意識之妄情故耳。是故八個識中。力用之大，必推六七二識。然而第七末那，但能恆常審量，妄執我見。惟有第六意識，既明且利。無境不能緣。無事不能作。其力極強。其用極廣。故意識若轉，末那亦隨之而轉。若此二俱轉成智者，（轉意識成妙觀察智。轉末那成平等性智。）爾時藏識即轉為大圓鏡智，前五識亦轉為成所作智矣。今修止觀，即欲轉識成智耳。而意識若轉，諸識隨轉。所以修行止觀，必從意識下手者此也。若先明得此理。則於向後所說妙諦，庶幾易於領悟。

Ru-Yi Translation

But the first five consciousnesses of ordinary beings rely entirely on the capacity of mind-consciousness to help accomplish their function. The storehouse consciousness is certainly the master; yet it can only serve as the root on which all dharmas rely, gathering and holding all dharmas, and cannot itself create good or evil. That it is called the combination of the true and the false is because the true mind in nonawakening moves of itself and falsely divides into subject and object, and so there are the deluded grasping of manas and the false feelings of mind-consciousness. Therefore, among the eight consciousnesses, those of the greatest power and function must be considered the sixth and the seventh. Yet the seventh, manas, can only constantly deliberate and deludedly grasp the view of self; only the sixth, mind-consciousness, is both bright and sharp: there is no object it cannot take and no task it cannot do; its power is extremely strong and its function extremely broad. Therefore, if mind-consciousness turns, manas turns along with it. When these two have both turned into wisdom (mind-consciousness turning into the wondrous observing wisdom, and manas turning into the wisdom of the nature of equality), at that time the storehouse consciousness turns into the great perfect mirror wisdom, and the first five consciousnesses turn into the wisdom that accomplishes what is to be done. Now, cultivating calming-and-contemplation is precisely to turn consciousness into wisdom; and if mind-consciousness turns, all the consciousnesses turn along with it. This is why cultivating calming-and-contemplation must begin from mind-consciousness. If one first understands this principle, then the wondrous truths to be spoken of hereafter will perhaps be easy to comprehend.

The Practice of Calming

Chinese Source

（癸）二別釋。又二。（子）初明止行體狀。二明觀行體狀。初又二。（丑）初正明。二釋疑。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(J) Second, the specific explanation, again in two: (K) first, explaining the substance and form of the practice of calming; second, explaining the substance and form of the practice of contemplation. The first is again in two: (L) first, explaining it directly; second, dispelling doubts. Now the first.

Chinese Source

此義云何？謂以意識能知名義故。聞說一切諸法，自性寂靜，本來無相。但以虛妄因緣，故有諸法。然虛妄法，有即非有，唯一真心。亦無別真相可取。聞此說已。方便修習。知法本寂，唯是一心。然此意識如此解時，念念熏於本識，增益解性之力。解性增已。更起意識，轉復明利。知法如實。久久熏心故，解性圓明。自照己體，本唯真寂。意識即息。爾時本識轉成無分別智。亦名證智。以是因緣，故以意識依止真心修止行也。是故論言。以依本覺，故有不覺。依不覺故，而有妄心。能知名義。為說本覺。故得始覺。即同本覺。如實不有始覺之異也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: What does this mean? Mind-consciousness can know names and meanings. 此總結自證之說，不能成立也。《楞嚴經》云，縱令內守幽閑，亦是法塵分別影事。上來種種執計，皆不外此。故斥之曰：汝所言淨心自證，不起後念云云，無有道理。

Ru-Yi Translation

This concludes as a whole that the claim of self-realization cannot be established. The Śūraṅgama Sūtra says: "Even if one inwardly guards a secluded stillness, it is still the shadowy matter of discrimination of dharma-objects." All the grasping speculations above do not go beyond this. Therefore he rebukes him, saying: your claim that the pure mind realizes itself and does not give rise to later thoughts, and so on, is without principle.

Refuting Realization by Another

Chinese Source

（卯）二破他證。又二。（辰）初正破。二轉破。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(N) Second, refuting realization by another, again in two: (O) first, refuting directly; second, refuting the shift. Now the first.

Chinese Source

若言由他證者。是亦不然。何以故？心體自寂靜故。但以有六七識等，名之為他。由有此他，故說他心不證。是故乃可證他，何須以他證心也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: If you say it is realized by another, that too is not so. Why? Because the mind's substance is tranquil of itself. It is only because there are the sixth and seventh consciousnesses and so on, which are called "another," and because there is this "other," that the other mind is said not to realize. Therefore one may realize the other; why must the other be used to realize the mind?

Chinese Source

大師因破自證文中，有心不作意，即不能證，而作意即是意識，即名他證等語。恐不得意者，復顢頇計為由他所證。因破之曰：若言由他證者，是亦不然。心體本自寂靜，平等一如。豈得自心中，復有他心。但以不覺自動，而有六七識等。則對本寂自體言之，可名為他耳。由有此虛妄紛動之他心故，本寂之體，不能證得。故曰他心不證。以是之故。既是由自有他，乃可自心證他，他何能證自心耶？須知用意識修止觀者。為欲去其妄情，轉之成智。如意識轉為無塵智時，既不名為意識，便能了達自他無性，即亦不名為他。如是久久熏習，妄盡真顯，名為體證真如。非意識妄情，能證淨心也。譬如眼中有翳。去其幻翳，眼之本明方顯。眼喻淨心。本明喻心體寂照。翳喻意識妄情。以喻合法。可知意識不能證心矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the text refuting self-realization, the Great Master said that if the mind does not make an intention it cannot realize, and that making an intention is mind-consciousness and is called realization by another, and so on. Fearing that those who do not grasp the point might again muddle it into speculating that it is realized by another, he refutes it, saying: "If you say it is realized by another, that too is not so." The mind's substance is originally tranquil of itself, equal and thus; how could there be, within one's own mind, another mind? Only because it moves of itself in nonawakening are there the sixth, seventh, and other consciousnesses; and in relation to the originally tranquil self-substance, they can be called "another." Because there is this falsely agitated "other mind," the originally tranquil substance cannot be realized; therefore it says, "the other mind does not realize." For this reason, since the other comes from the self, the self-mind may realize the other; how could the other realize the self-mind? You should know that using mind-consciousness to cultivate calming-and-contemplation is in order to remove its false feelings and turn it into wisdom. When mind-consciousness turns into the wisdom of no sense-objects, for example, it is no longer called mind-consciousness, and it can understand that self and other have no nature; so it is no longer called "other" either. Perfuming for a long, long time in this way, the false is exhausted and the true revealed; this is called realizing suchness in substance. It is not that the false feelings of mind-consciousness can realize the pure mind. It is like a cataract in the eye: remove the illusory cataract, and the eye's original brightness appears. The eye is a simile for the pure mind; the original brightness, for the tranquil illumination of the mind's substance; the cataract, for the false feelings of mind-consciousness. Matching the simile with the dharma, we can know that mind-consciousness cannot realize the mind.

Chinese Source

（辰）二轉破。

Ru-Yi Translation

(O) Second, refuting the shift.

Chinese Source

若言心體雖復本寂，但以無始無明妄念熏故。有此妄念習氣在於心中，是故心體亦不證寂，故須他證者。何等方便，能除心中習氣，令心證也？若言更不起新念故，不熏益彼習氣，彼即自滅者。彼未滅間，有何所以，不起新念也？若無別法為對治者，彼諸習氣法應起念。若起念者，更益彼力也。以是義故。由他所證，亦無道理。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: If you say: "Although the mind's substance is originally tranquil, because it has been perfumed by beginningless ignorance and deluded thoughts, the habit-energy of these deluded thoughts is in the mind, and therefore the mind's substance does not realize tranquility either; so it must be realized by another" — by what expedient can the habit-energy in the mind be removed so that the mind realizes? If you say: "Because one no longer gives rise to new thoughts, that habit-energy is not perfumed and increased, and it ceases of itself" — while it has not yet ceased, what reason is there for not giving rise to new thoughts? If there were no separate dharma to counteract it, those habit-energies would as a matter of course give rise to thoughts; and if they give rise to thoughts, that further increases their power. For this reason realization by another is also without principle.
