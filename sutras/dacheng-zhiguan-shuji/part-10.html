The Substance and Form of Calming-and-Contemplation in the Defiled Three Natures

Part 10 ends the section on spheres and opens the third round, the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation: the actual method of practice. Dixian first answers the root text's question on the six consciousnesses, explaining direct perception, inference, and non-valid cognition, and why the Mahāyāna does not posit five separate mind-consciousnesses. The practice is then laid out on the defiled three natures in a fixed order. With the discriminated nature, one contemplates that the objects of the five aggregates and six sense-objects are taken as real only through ignorance; one then stops that grasping, and even the grasping of the contemplating thought. With the dependent nature, one sees that dharmas are illusory marks made by the mind and stops the grasping of those marks. The real nature takes four layers: dispelling the "absence" taken for the mind (the nature of no nature), dispelling a separate pure mind (no true nature), the fundamental samādhi of suchness, and calming and contemplation present together. Dixian teaches here as a meditation master, pausing and striking the ruler. A general sorting follows: the three non-natures, why the four layers are needed, why one may not jump to the third nature, and that only the grasping, not suchness, is removed. The part ends in the simile of the rabbit conjured on a handkerchief.

Distinguishing in Terms of the Dependent (continued)

Chinese Source

前總明三性中曰：在障之真，與染和合，名為阿梨耶識。此即是依他性。今謂在障之真，乃是淨分。即可名為真實性。無明染法，乃不淨分。而凡夫之虛相果報，皆由染法習氣種子而生。故即此便是分別性。至淨染和合無二，即依他性，其義易知。總之，三自性唯是一心。本來非一非三，即三即一。故可作種種釋。又此中所明，乃專約凡夫而言。因凡夫無明不覺，常住真心久已成為阿梨耶識。故一切眾生，無不在依他性中。其三自性，祇能就依他性中辨之。以明若不知循流溯源，即不能超凡入聖，證入常住真心。則亦終為在障有垢之凡夫已矣。此大師更出此義之深旨也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the preceding general explanation of the three natures it said: "The true within obstruction, combined with defilement, is called the ālaya consciousness; this is the dependent nature." Now it says that the true within obstruction is the pure portion and can be called the real nature; the defiled dharmas of ignorance are the impure portion; and the illusory marks and fruits of retribution of ordinary beings are all born from the habit-energy and seeds of defiled dharmas; therefore this is the discriminated nature. As for the pure and the defiled combined and nondual being the dependent nature, its meaning is easy to know. In sum, the three self-natures are only the one mind, originally neither one nor three, both three and one; therefore various explanations can be made. Further, what is explained here is spoken solely in terms of ordinary beings. Because of the ignorance and nonawakening of ordinary beings, the eternally abiding true mind has long since become the ālaya consciousness; therefore all sentient beings are without exception within the dependent nature, and their three self-natures can be distinguished only within the dependent nature. This makes clear that if one does not know how to follow the stream back to the source, one cannot transcend the ordinary and enter the holy and realize and enter the eternally abiding true mind; then one will in the end remain an ordinary being within obstruction, with stains. This is the deep purport of the Great Master's bringing out this further meaning.

The Doubt about the Six Consciousnesses

Chinese Source

（壬）三釋六識疑。

Ru-Yi Translation

(I) Third, explaining the doubt about the six consciousnesses.

Chinese Source

問曰：似識妄分別時，為是意識總能分別六塵，為六識各各自分別一塵。答曰：五識見塵時，各與意識俱時而起。如眼識見似色時，即是一意識俱時分別妄執也。餘識亦如是。是故意識總能分別妄執六塵。五識但能得五塵，不生分別妄執。問曰：妄執五塵為實者，為是五意識。為是第六意識。答曰：大乘中不明五意識與第六別。但能分別者，悉名意識。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Question: When quasi-consciousness deludedly discriminates, is it mind-consciousness that discriminates all six sense-objects, or does each of the six consciousnesses discriminate one sense-object by itself? Answer: When the five consciousnesses see sense-objects, each arises at the same time together with mind-consciousness. For example, when eye-consciousness sees quasi-form, one mind-consciousness at the same time discriminates and deludedly grasps; the other consciousnesses are likewise. Therefore mind-consciousness discriminates and deludedly grasps all six sense-objects. The five consciousnesses can only obtain the five sense-objects; they do not give rise to discrimination and deluded grasping. Question: When the five sense-objects are deludedly grasped as real, is it the five mind-consciousnesses or the sixth mind-consciousness? Answer: In the Mahāyāna it is not explained that the five mind-consciousnesses differ from the sixth; whatever can discriminate is all called mind-consciousness.

Chinese Source

上來自破大乘人執。至此已顯明八七六識中，以第六意識力用最大。故修行須從意識入手。唯意識與前五識，力用若何？尚未詳言。因更出此文以明之。問意以似識二字，本兼前五識而言。然則似識妄分別時，為是意識總能分別六塵耶？為意識與前五識，各自分別一塵耶？釋之曰：五識對塵之時，各與意識俱時而起。如眼識見似色時，但能見之而已。若其分別妄執，乃是同時俱起之意識作用。推之耳鼻舌身等識，可以例知。是故前五識，但能得五塵耳。惟意識總能分別六塵，妄執六塵也。得者。謂眼則見色，耳則聞聲，各各相得，不錯謬也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Above, from the refutation of the attachments of Mahāyāna people up to here, it has already been made clear that among the eighth, seventh, and sixth consciousnesses, the power and function of the sixth, mind-consciousness, is the greatest; therefore cultivation must set its hand to mind-consciousness. But the power and function of mind-consciousness and the front five consciousnesses have not yet been spoken of in detail. Therefore he brings out this further text to make it clear. The meaning of the question is that the two words "quasi-consciousness" originally include the front five consciousnesses. Then when quasi-consciousness deludedly discriminates, is it mind-consciousness that discriminates all six sense-objects? Or do mind-consciousness and the front five consciousnesses each discriminate one sense-object by themselves? Explaining it, he says: when the five consciousnesses face sense-objects, each arises at the same time together with mind-consciousness. For example, when eye-consciousness sees quasi-form, it can only see it; its discriminating and deluded grasping is the function of the mind-consciousness that arises together with it at the same time. The ear, nose, tongue, body, and other consciousnesses can be known by this example. Therefore the front five consciousnesses can only obtain the five sense-objects; only mind-consciousness discriminates all six sense-objects and deludedly grasps the six sense-objects. "Obtain" means that the eye sees form and the ear hears sound, each obtaining its own without error.

Chinese Source

茲約現比非三量之義，詳明其功能大小。須知前五識祇具現量。故但有自性分別，而無隨念計度之能。意識則通於三量。方能隨念計度，分別妄執。現量者。對所緣境，顯現量知。謂不加計度，便能識別也。如到眼便識得是色。入耳便識得是聲。初不隨念計度，所謂自性分別是也。至於黃乎？白乎？鐘聲乎？鼓聲乎？此已入於比量，而非現量。即為意識計度分別之力。非眼識耳識所能成辦矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, in terms of the meaning of the three means of cognition — direct perception, inference, and non-valid cognition — he makes clear in detail how great or small their capacities are. One must know that the front five consciousnesses have only direct perception; therefore they have only discrimination by their own nature and lack the ability of recollection and calculation. Mind-consciousness extends to all three means of cognition; therefore it can recollect and calculate, discriminate and deludedly grasp. "Direct perception" means cognizing the object taken as condition as it manifestly appears; that is, one can recognize it without adding calculation. For example, as soon as something reaches the eye, one recognizes that it is form; as soon as it enters the ear, one recognizes that it is sound; there is no recollecting or calculating at first. This is what is called discrimination by its own nature. As for "Is it yellow? Is it white? Is it the sound of a bell? Is it the sound of a drum?" — this has already entered inference and is not direct perception. It is the power of the calculating discrimination of mind-consciousness, not something eye-consciousness or ear-consciousness can accomplish.

Chinese Source

比量者。於不顯現之境，比例量度，而能正知。如以此例知彼。以前例知今。譬如見煙，例知有火是也。非量者。即是似現量，似比量。何謂似現量？如見泥團，即作泥團解。不知乃是眾塵和合之假法。非真現量也。何謂似比量？如見霧妄謂是煙，邪證有火。此皆錯亂分別，故曰非量。比量是意識之功。非量為意識之過。今用意識修行，乃用其比量正知，治其非量邪解。俾隨順證入淨心之現量耳。他如執虛境為實有，以及夢中境緣，醒時散亂心，皆為意識非量。看經聞法而得正解，則是比量。邪見妄解，便成非量。若作觀時，了了明明。及定中所緣之境，境與心應。皆意識之現量也。譬如修淨土者，定中得見彌陀。是為心境相應，即是現量。若不相應，非現量也。至於七識，祇有非我執我之非量。八識與前五識同，祇有現量。上來所說現比非三量，乃六根門頭尋常日用者。不可不辨明其功用，故詳為分晰言之。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Inference" means, with regard to an object that does not manifestly appear, measuring it by comparison and being able to know it correctly: for example, knowing that by this, or knowing the present by the past. It is like seeing smoke and inferring that there is fire. "Non-valid cognition" is pseudo-direct perception and pseudo-inference. What is pseudo-direct perception? For example, seeing a lump of clay and understanding it as a lump of clay, not knowing that it is a provisional dharma made of many particles combined; it is not true direct perception. What is pseudo-inference? For example, seeing mist and deludedly calling it smoke, and wrongly proving that there is fire. These are all confused discriminations; therefore they are called non-valid cognition. Inference is the merit of mind-consciousness; non-valid cognition is the fault of mind-consciousness. Now, in using mind-consciousness to practice, one uses its correct knowledge by inference to cure its wrong understanding by non-valid cognition, so that it accords with and realizes the direct perception of the pure mind. Besides this, grasping illusory objects as really existent, as well as the objects and conditions in dreams and the scattered mind when awake, are all non-valid cognition of mind-consciousness. Reading the sūtras and hearing the Dharma and gaining correct understanding is inference; wrong views and deluded understanding become non-valid cognition. When one is contemplating, clear and distinct, as well as the object taken as condition within concentration, the object and the mind corresponding — these are all direct perception of mind-consciousness. For example, when one who cultivates the Pure Land sees Amitābha within concentration, this is mind and object corresponding; it is direct perception. If they do not correspond, it is not direct perception. As for the seventh consciousness, it has only the non-valid cognition of grasping as self what is not self. The eighth consciousness, like the front five consciousnesses, has only direct perception. The three means of cognition described above — direct perception, inference, and non-valid cognition — are what is ordinarily used every day at the gates of the six faculties. Their functions must be distinguished clearly; therefore he has analyzed and explained them in detail.

次一番問答，是明大乘但立同時意識。不同小乘立五意識。五意識者。因小乘不知意識與前五識同時俱起。故計為前五識不但識現量境，亦識比量境。謂如眼見前塵之煙，此屬現量。名為眼識。而見煙比知有火，此屬比量。名為眼意識。若煙境已謝，緣念仍存。所謂緣念法塵中落謝影子者，乃屬於第六意識。餘耳鼻舌身等，類此可知。此小乘所立之義也。大乘立義，無此差別。但能分別前塵者。不論現塵，及落謝塵，皆屬第六意識作用。故悉名意識，不別立五意識之名。總之，意識通於三量，力用最大。故能作生死。能作涅槃。所以必須從意識著手進修者，此耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

The next round of question and answer makes clear that the Mahāyāna sets up only the simultaneous mind-consciousness and does not, like the Lesser Vehicle, set up five mind-consciousnesses. As for the five mind-consciousnesses: because the Lesser Vehicle does not know that mind-consciousness arises at the same time together with the front five consciousnesses, it reckons that the front five consciousnesses cognize not only objects of direct perception but also objects of inference. For example, the eye seeing the smoke of the present object belongs to direct perception and is called eye-consciousness; seeing the smoke and inferring that there is fire belongs to inference and is called eye-mind-consciousness. If the object of smoke has passed away and the thought taking it as condition still remains — what is called taking as condition the fallen-away shadow within dharma-objects — this belongs to the sixth, mind-consciousness. The ear, nose, tongue, body, and the rest can be known by analogy. This is the meaning set up by the Lesser Vehicle. The meaning set up by the Mahāyāna has no such distinction. Whatever can discriminate the present object, whether a present object or a fallen-away object, is all the function of the sixth, mind-consciousness; therefore all is called mind-consciousness, and the name "five mind-consciousnesses" is not set up separately. In sum, mind-consciousness extends to all three means of cognition and its power and function is the greatest; therefore it can make birth-and-death and can make nirvāṇa. This is the reason one must advance in cultivation by setting one's hand to mind-consciousness.

The Substance and Form of Calming-and-Contemplation

Ru-Yi Translation

(E) The third major section, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation, in three: (F) first, announcing in general; second, explaining individually; third, concluding in general. Now the first.

Chinese Source

次明第三止觀體狀。就中復有二番明義。一就染濁三性，以明止觀體狀。二就清淨三性，以明止觀體狀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, explaining the third, the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation. Within this there are again two rounds of explanation: first, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation in terms of the defiled and turbid three natures; second, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation in terms of the pure three natures.

Chinese Source

上來五番建立中。初明止觀依止，令行人先悟自心。然後依止修行，乃克成就。何以故？自性清淨心，為一切法之根本故。二明止觀境界。復開一心為三自性，又開三自性為淨染二種，以為所觀之境。何以故？若不定境，無從修心故。然此兩番，皆是理解。今第三番明止觀體狀，正屬行門。即的示吾人以修止觀之方法也。體狀即是行相。示以止觀行相，令行人依為模範故。上明境界，正為修行。彼既開染淨二種，故今亦有二番明義。此兩種修法。或隨修一種，或同時兼修，或先後修，皆得。今因文便先說染，後說淨耳。且以明凡夫雖已迷真成妄，而返妄即可證真矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the five rounds set up above, the first explained the support of calming-and-contemplation, so that practitioners would first awaken to their own mind; only after that, practicing in reliance on it, can they accomplish it. Why? Because the mind pure in its own nature is the root of all dharmas. The second explained the spheres of calming-and-contemplation: it further opened the one mind into the three self-natures, and further opened the three self-natures into the pure and defiled kinds, as the objects to be contemplated. Why? Because if the object is not fixed, there is no way to cultivate the mind. Yet these two rounds both belong to understanding of principle. Now the third round, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation, belongs properly to the gate of practice; that is, it shows us precisely the method of cultivating calming-and-contemplation. Substance and form is the mode of activity; it shows the mode of activity of calming-and-contemplation so that practitioners may rely on it as a model. Explaining the spheres above was precisely for the sake of practice. Since there it opened the two kinds, defiled and pure, so now too there are two rounds of explanation. Of these two methods of cultivation, one may cultivate either one, or cultivate both at the same time, or cultivate them one after the other; all are acceptable. Now, because it suits the text, he speaks of the defiled first and the pure afterward; this also makes clear that although ordinary beings have been deluded about the true and formed the false, by turning back from the false they can realize the true.

(F) Second, explaining individually, which is in two: (G) first, in terms of the defiled and turbid three natures; second, in terms of the pure three natures. The first is in three: (H) first, dividing the sections; second, explaining each; third, sorting out in general. Now the first.

Chinese Source

初就染濁三性中，復作三門分別。一依分別性以明。二約依他性以顯。三對真實性以示。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: First, within the defiled and turbid three natures, there are again three gates of distinction: first, explaining it in dependence on the discriminated nature; second, revealing it by way of the dependent nature; third, showing it with respect to the real nature.

Chinese Source

此中三門分科，乃循流溯源之一定次第。蓋修分別性，正為入依他性之方便。修依他性，正為入真實性之方便。真實性者，即自性清淨心是也。實為止觀依止之地。但自無明不覺，此性已與染分和合，成為依他。而依他。正由妄想分別而有。故必先從分別性，斬關而入。步步深進。乃能達到真實性究竟依止之地。所謂挽弓當挽強。擒賊先擒王也。修分別性，如宗下本分功夫。修依他性，如宗下重關功夫。修真實性時，即宗下所謂破末後牢關也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The division of these three gates into sections is the fixed order of following the stream back to the source. For cultivating the discriminated nature is precisely the expedient for entering the dependent nature; cultivating the dependent nature is precisely the expedient for entering the real nature. The real nature is the mind pure in its own nature; it is truly the ground on which calming-and-contemplation relies. But from ignorance and nonawakening this nature has combined with the defiled portion and formed the dependent; and the dependent exists precisely because of deluded thinking and discrimination. Therefore one must first break through the barrier from the discriminated nature and enter, advancing step by step deeper; only then can one reach the ground of ultimate reliance, the real nature. This is what is called "In drawing a bow, draw the strong one; in capturing bandits, first capture their king." Cultivating the discriminated nature is like the work of the fundamental matter in the [Chan] school; cultivating the dependent nature is like the work of the second barrier in the [Chan] school; cultivating the real nature is what the [Chan] school calls breaking through the final, tightly locked barrier.

The Discriminated Nature: Entering Calming from Contemplation

Chinese Source

（辛）二各釋。即為三。（壬）初約分別性。二約依他性。三約真實性。初中二。（癸）初從觀入止。二從止復觀。初又二。（子）初明觀。二明止。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

(H) Second, explaining each, which is in three: (I) first, in terms of the discriminated nature; second, in terms of the dependent nature; third, in terms of the real nature. The first is in two: (J) first, entering calming from contemplation; second, from calming returning to contemplation. The first is again in two: (K) first, explaining contemplation; second, explaining calming. Now the first.

Chinese Source

對分別性以明止觀體狀者，先從觀入止。所言觀者，當觀五陰及外六塵。隨一一法，悉作是念。我今所見此法，謂為實有形質堅礙，本來如是者。但是意識有果時無明故，不知此法是虛。以不知法是虛故，即起妄想，執以為實。是故今時意裏，確然將作實事。復當念言。無始已來，由執實故，於一切境界起貪瞋癡。造種種業。招生感死，莫能自出。作此解者，即名觀門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation with respect to the discriminated nature: first, from contemplation one enters calming. As for contemplation: one should contemplate the five aggregates and the six external sense-objects, and with regard to each and every dharma think thus: "This dharma I now see, which I take to be really existent, solid and resistant in form and substance, and to have been so from the beginning — it is only because mind-consciousness has ignorance in the fruit phase that I do not know this dharma is illusory. Because I do not know the dharma is illusory, deluded thinking arises and I grasp it as real. That is why now, in my mind, I firmly take it as a real thing." Further one should think: "Since beginningless time, because of grasping things as real, I have given rise to greed, anger, and folly toward all spheres and created all kinds of karma, calling forth birth and inviting death, unable to get out by myself." Making this understanding is called the gate of contemplation.

Chinese Source

觀者，即止之觀。止者，即觀之止。言有次第，修無先後。然若鈍根人不能止觀並運者，即無妨先觀後止。須知修觀，正為入止之方便。故曰從觀入止。若單修觀不修止。則妄念不息，何能了生死乎？此中觀法，可分為二。先觀諸法是虛。次觀執實之過。然言有前後，意則一貫。蓋以不知是虛，所以執實。以執實故，造業招苦。是故欲出生死之苦，便當知境是虛。昔我世尊，在鹿苑說法，先明苦諦。以凡夫不知畏因，猶知畏果。若知得內身外塵，無常苦果。則既明苦諦，方能修道諦，斷集諦也。今初修觀門，剋從五陰六塵入手者，正是此意。

Ru-Yi Translation

Contemplation is the contemplation that is calming; calming is the calming that is contemplation. In words there is an order; in cultivation there is no before and after. But if people of dull faculties cannot operate calming and contemplation together, there is no harm in contemplating first and calming afterward. One must know that cultivating contemplation is precisely the expedient for entering calming; therefore it says, "from contemplation one enters calming." If one cultivates only contemplation and not calming, deluded thoughts do not cease; how could one be finished with birth-and-death? But though in words there is before and after, the intent is one thread. Further, because sentient beings are right in the midst of delusion, if the object is not fixed as an expedient for contemplating the mind, from where would they set their hand? So next he opened the one mind into the three self-natures and explained the spheres of calming-and-contemplation. Further, he explained the substance and form of practice with respect to the three self-natures, to show how to realize and enter the three non-natures and recover one's own pure mind; this is the third, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation. Our Great Master has already explained it in detail, point by point, bringing it out to the full. Yet the three natures have no boundaries, and to raise one is to include them all; the places within them where the true and false merge with each other and nature and mark do not obstruct each other are deep and subtle. He was truly afraid that people long on the deluded road, though they hear the explanation by dharma, would still find it hard to understand clearly. Therefore he further speaks this simile to reveal it, intent on having practitioners see thoroughly the substance and form of the two gates of contemplation and calming; then, advancing in cultivation in reliance on them, they can reap twice the result with half the effort. Such skillful expedients are truly the Great Master's mind of compassion to the very bottom.

Ru-Yi Translation

This uses a simile for the three natures to explain contemplation. First, the handkerchief stands for the pure mind; the handkerchief originally having no rabbit stands for the mind being originally apart from marks. This is the dharma of the real nature. All marks are false; the self-nature is apart from marks, and therefore it is real. Next, the rabbit appearing stands for the marks of the six paths appearing; the rabbit appearing through illusion stands for marks appearing through deluded perfuming. This is the dharma of the dependent nature. Third, grasping the rabbit as real stands for grasping illusory marks as real; the foolish and the young without knowledge stand for mind-consciousness, deluded and confused. This is the dharma of the discriminated nature. Such a simile reveals it with the utmost clarity. "Therefore" and what follows cites the Tathāgatagarbha Sūtra as proof. "All dharmas" refers to the five aggregates, the six sense-objects, and so on. These are all manifested in dependence on perfuming; therefore it says "like illusions." This makes clear that dharmas of conditioned arising are all illusory existence. Contemplating and examining according to principle: the real is originally existent, like the handkerchief; the dependent is entirely provisional, like the illusory rabbit; the discriminated is entirely empty, like grasping the illusory rabbit as real. This is the gate of contemplation. Therefore it concludes, "This is a simile for the gate of contemplation of the three natures."
