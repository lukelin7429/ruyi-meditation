Notes Expounding the Dharma Gate of Mahāyāna Calming-and-Contemplation.

Dixian (諦閑, 1858–1932), abbot of Guanzong Monastery in Ningbo and the leading Tiantai teacher of his day, lectured line by line on the Dharma Gate of Mahāyāna Calming-and-Contemplation. His lay disciples wrote the lectures down in 1922–23, and Dixian revised them over eight years into these twenty fascicles. He reads the text through the Tiantai five layers of profound meaning, explains each phrase with sources from the sūtras and Tiantai masters, and turns repeatedly to practice — how the one mind is contemplated, and how calming and contemplation are carried into daily life.

The Text's Structure

Twenty fascicles in thirteen parts.

Part 1
Preface, the Five Layers of Profound Meaning, and the Opening Dialogue
Dixian (1858–1932), abbot of Guanzong Monastery in Ningbo, lectured on the Dharma Gate of Mahāyāna Calming-and-Contemplation (attributed to Huisi).
Translated
Part 2
Suchness, Buddha-Nature, and the Rise and Reversal of Delusion
Part 2 continues Dixian's commentary on the first round of the Dharma Gate, the support of calming-and-contemplation.
Translated
Part 3
Ultimate Awakening, the Four Buddha-Natures, and the Dharma-Body
Part 3 completes Dixian's commentary on "buddha-nature" and begins the remaining names of the one mind.
Translated
Part 4
The Dharma-Realm, the Dharma-Nature, and the Substance and Form of the Pure Mind
Part 4 finishes Dixian's commentary on the seven names of the one mind and begins the third main topic of the support of calming-and-contemplation, its "substance and form." He first explains the three meanings of "tathāgatagarbha" (what stores, what is stored, what can give birth), then the dharma-realm and the Dharma-nature, and closes the names with the reality-limit and the true mark.
Translated
Part 5
The Defiled Nature, Defiled Phenomena, and the One Tathāgatagarbha Shared by All
Part 5 continues Dixian's commentary on the nonempty tathāgatagarbha.
Translated
Part 6
The Mirror Simile, the Names of the Dharma-Body, and the Mutual Inclusion of Phenomena
Part 6 carries the second main topic of the nonempty tathāgatagarbha, the oneness and difference of the storehouse's substance, through three and a half of its six sections.
Translated
Part 7
Times and Eons, Counteracting Delusion, Shared and Unshared Marks, and Why the Mind Is Relied Upon
Part 7 finishes the long account of the nonempty tathāgatagarbha and opens the second question of the root text's support of calming-and-contemplation.
Translated
Part 8
Tranquility and Function, Mind-Consciousness as What Relies, and the Refutation of Self-Realization
Part 8 completes the root text's "why it is relied upon" and works through most of "by what it is relied upon." Tranquility is the mind's substance apart from marks; function is its contrary and accordant capacities, so calming benefits oneself and contemplation benefits others.
Translated
Part 9
Realizing the Other, Cultivating Without Thought, and the Three Self-Natures as Spheres
Part 9 finishes the long refutation of practitioners who would realize the pure mind without using mind-consciousness, and then opens the second of the root text's five rounds, the spheres of calming-and-contemplation.
Translated
Part 10
The Substance and Form of Calming-and-Contemplation in the Defiled Three Natures
Part 10 ends the section on spheres and opens the third round, the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation: the actual method of practice.
Translated
Part 11
The Substance and Form in the Pure Three Natures
Part 11 completes the third round, the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation, and opens the fourth.
Translated
Part 12
Cutting Off and Attaining, and the Activity of Calming-and-Contemplation
Part 12 finishes the fourth round, what calming-and-contemplation cuts off and attains, and gives the whole of the fifth, its activity, except for the closing verses.
Translated
Part 13
The Closing Verses, Calming-and-Contemplation in Bowing, Eating, and at the Latrine, and Dixian's Dedication
Part 13 is the last part of Dixian's lecture notes and covers the end of the root text.
Translated
