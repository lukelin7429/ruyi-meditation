Ten Gates for the Ten Wondrous Aspects

Zhanran (湛然, 711–782), the sixth patriarch of the Tiantai school, wrote these pages as part of his great sub-commentary on Zhiyi's Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sūtra, the Fahua Xuanyi Shiqian (T1717). Having finished commenting on the ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate — the wondrous of the object, of wisdom, of practice, and so on through merit and benefit — he paused to gather all ten into a form that could be contemplated, not only studied. The result is ten "gates of nonduality": form and mind, inner and outer, cultivation and nature, cause and fruit, defilement and purity, the dependent and the principal, self and other, the three kinds of action, the provisional and the real, and receiving and moistening. Each gate rests on the one before it, and all of them rest on a single claim: that the three thousand realms are complete in a single thought and are at once empty, provisional, and the middle. Later copyists lifted the passage out of the Shiqian and circulated it on its own, and in the Song dynasty it became the battleground between the "home mountain" and "off mountain" factions of Tiantai, above all through Zhili's commentary, the Shi buer men zhiyao chao (T1928). This translation follows the text alone and leaves those later readings to the notes.

Status: Part 1 of 1 · first edition Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T46, no. 1927 (Zhanran)

Title and Author

Chinese Source

十不二門

Ru-Yi Translation

The Ten Gates of Nonduality

Chinese Source

唐荊溪尊者湛然述

Ru-Yi Translation

Set forth by the Venerable Zhanran of Jingxi, of the Tang dynasty.

Why the Ten Wondrous Aspects Are Gathered into Ten Gates

Chinese Source

然此迹門，談其因果及以自他，使一代教門融通入妙故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, this trace gate discusses cause and fruit, and self and other, in order to make the teachings of the Buddha's entire lifetime merge and pass into the wondrous.

Chinese Source

凡諸義釋，皆約四教及以五味，意在開教，悉入醍醐。

Ru-Yi Translation

All of its explanations of meaning are given in terms of the Four Teachings and the Five Flavors; its intent is to open up the teachings so that all of them enter the ghee.

Chinese Source

觀心乃是教行樞機，仍且略點，寄在諸說或存或沒，非部正意，故縱有施設託事附法，或辯十觀列名而已。

Ru-Yi Translation

The contemplation of mind is the pivot of teaching and practice, yet here it is only briefly touched on, lodged within the various explanations — sometimes present, sometimes absent — because it is not the main intent of this work. So even where something is set up, relying on events or attaching to dharmas, or where the ten contemplations are discussed, it merely lists their names.

Chinese Source

所明理境、智行、位法，能化所化意在能詮，詮中咸妙。

Ru-Yi Translation

What it clarifies — the object of principle, wisdom and practice, stage and the [three] tracks — whether it concerns the one who transforms or those who are transformed, has its intent in what does the expressing; and within that expression, all is wondrous.

Chinese Source

為辯詮內始末自他故，具演十妙搜括一化，出世大意罄無不盡，故不可不了十妙大綱，故撮十妙為觀法大體。

Ru-Yi Translation

In order to set forth beginning and end, self and other, within what is expressed, it expounds the ten wondrous aspects in full, gathering up the whole of a single teaching career, so that the great intent of [the Buddha's] appearance in the world is exhausted, with nothing left out. Therefore one cannot fail to understand the broad outline of the ten wondrous aspects; and therefore I gather up the ten wondrous aspects as the overall framework of the method of contemplation.

Chinese Source

若解迹妙，本妙非遙，應知但是離合異耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one understands the wondrous of the trace, the wondrous of the origin is not far off; one should know that they differ only in how they are divided and combined.

Chinese Source

因果義一，自他何殊？故下文云，本迹雖殊、不思議一。

Ru-Yi Translation

Cause and fruit are one in meaning; how could self and other differ? So a later passage says: "Although origin and trace differ, they are inconceivably one."

Chinese Source

況體宗用秖是自他因果法故，況復教相秖是分別前之四章，使前四章與諸文永異，若曉斯旨則教有所歸。

Ru-Yi Translation

Moreover, the essence, the cardinal doctrine, and the function are simply the dharmas of cause and fruit for self and other; and further, the characteristics of the teachings simply distinguish the preceding four sections, so that those four sections are forever different from other texts. If one grasps this purport, the teachings have somewhere to return.

Chinese Source

一期縱橫不出一心，三千世間即空假中，理境乃至利益咸爾，則止觀十乘，成今自行因果；起教一章，成今化他能所。

Ru-Yi Translation

The whole of a teaching career, in its breadth and its length, does not go beyond a single mind; the three thousand worlds are identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle. From the object of principle up to merit and benefit, all are so. Thus the ten modes of calming-and-contemplation constitute, here, the cause and fruit of one's own practice; and the section on giving rise to the teaching constitutes, here, the agent and the recipients of transforming others.

Chinese Source

則彼此昭著，法華行成，使功不唐捐，所詮可識。故更以十門收攝十妙。何者？

Ru-Yi Translation

Then both are made plain, the practice of the Lotus is accomplished, effort is not spent in vain, and what is expressed can be recognized. Therefore I further use ten gates to gather in the ten wondrous aspects. Why?

Chinese Source

為實施權則不二而二，開權顯實則二而不二，法既教部咸開成妙，故此十門不二為目，一一門下以六即檢之。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the provisional is set up for the sake of the real, they are not two, yet two; when the provisional is opened to reveal the real, they are two, yet not two. Since the dharmas, the teachings, and the scriptural corpus have all been opened and made wondrous, these ten gates take "not two" as their name, and under each gate they are to be examined by means of the six identities.

Chinese Source

本門已廣引誠證，此下但直申一理，使一部經旨皎在目前。

Ru-Yi Translation

The origin gate has already cited trustworthy proofs extensively; below, I simply set forth the one principle directly, so that the purport of the whole sūtra shines clearly before one's eyes.

The Ten Gates Named

Chinese Source

一者、色心不二門，二者、內外不二門，三者、修性不二門，四者、因果不二門，五者、染淨不二門，六者、依正不二門，七者、自他不二門，八者、三業不二門，九者、權實不二門，十者、受潤不二門。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the gate of the nonduality of form and mind; second, the gate of the nonduality of inner and outer; third, the gate of the nonduality of cultivation and nature; fourth, the gate of the nonduality of cause and fruit; fifth, the gate of the nonduality of defilement and purity; sixth, the gate of the nonduality of the dependent and the principal; seventh, the gate of the nonduality of self and other; eighth, the gate of the nonduality of the three kinds of action; ninth, the gate of the nonduality of the provisional and the real; tenth, the gate of the nonduality of receiving and moistening.

Chinese Source

是中第一，從境妙立名；第二第三，從智行立名；第四，從位法立名；第五第六第七，從感應神通立名；第八第九，從說法立名；第十，從眷屬利益立名。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these, the first is named from the wondrous of the object; the second and third, from [the wondrous of] wisdom and of practice; the fourth, from [the wondrous of] stage and of the three tracks; the fifth, sixth, and seventh, from [the wondrous of] sympathetic response and of spiritual powers; the eighth and ninth, from [the wondrous of] teaching the Dharma; the tenth, from [the wondrous of] the retinue and of merit and benefit.

1. The Gate of the Nonduality of Form and Mind

Chinese Source

一、色心不二門者，且十如境乃至無諦，一一皆可總別二意，總在一念，別分色心。何者？

Ru-Yi Translation

1. The gate of the nonduality of form and mind. Take the objects from the ten suchnesses up to no truth: each of them can be understood in two senses, general and particular. In general, they lie in a single thought; in particular, they are divided into form and mind. How so?

Chinese Source

初十如中，相唯在色，性唯在心，體、力、作、緣義兼色心，因、果唯心，報唯約色；

Ru-Yi Translation

First, among the ten suchnesses: mark lies only in form; nature lies only in mind; substance, power, activity, and condition in meaning include both form and mind; cause and effect are mind only; retribution concerns form only.

Chinese Source

十二因緣苦業兩兼，惑唯在心；四諦則三兼色心，滅唯在心；二諦三諦皆俗具色心、真中唯心；一實及無，準此可見。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the twelve links of dependent origination, suffering and karma include both, while delusion lies only in mind. In the Four Noble Truths, three include both form and mind, while cessation lies only in mind. In the two truths and the three truths alike, the conventional contains form and mind, while the real and the middle are mind only. The one real [truth] and no [truth] can be seen by analogy with this.

Chinese Source

既知別已，攝別入總，一切諸法無非心性，一性無性三千宛然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having understood the particular, gather the particular into the general: all dharmas are none other than the nature of mind. The one nature is without nature, and the three thousand are fully present.

Chinese Source

當知心之色心即心名變，變名為造，造謂體用，是則非色非心而色而心，唯色唯心良由於此。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that the form and mind of the mind are, being mind, called transformation; transformation is called creating; creating means essence and function. Thus it is neither form nor mind, yet form and yet mind; "form only" and "mind only" are truly due to this.

Chinese Source

故知但識一念，遍見己他生佛。他生他佛尚與心同，況己心生佛寧乖一念？故彼彼境法，差差而不差。

Ru-Yi Translation

So we know: simply recognize a single thought, and you see everywhere oneself and others, beings and buddhas. If even other beings and other buddhas are the same as the mind, how much less could the beings and buddhas of one's own mind depart from a single thought? So each and every object and dharma is distinct in every case, and yet not distinct.

2. The Gate of the Nonduality of Inner and Outer

Chinese Source

二、內外不二門者，凡所觀境不出內外，外謂託彼依正色心，即空假中，即空假中妙，故色心體絕。

Ru-Yi Translation

2. The gate of the nonduality of inner and outer. The objects contemplated never go beyond inner and outer. "Outer" means relying on the form and mind of those dependent and principal [rewards], which are identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle. Being identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle is wondrous; therefore the essence of form and mind is cut off.

Chinese Source

唯一實性無空假中，色心宛然豁同真淨，無復眾生七方便異，不見國土淨穢差品，而帝網依正終自炳然。

Ru-Yi Translation

There is only the one real nature, without empty, provisional, or middle. Form and mind remain fully present, yet open out and are the same as true purity. There is no longer any difference between sentient beings and the seven kinds of expedient [practitioners], and no differing grades of pure and impure lands are seen; yet the dependent and principal [rewards] of Indra's net remain, in the end, brilliantly clear of themselves.

Chinese Source

所言內者，先了外色心一念無念，唯內體三千即空假中，是則外法全為心性。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for what is called "inner": first understand that outer form and mind are a single thought that is no thought; then only the inner essence — the three thousand — is identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle. Thus outer dharmas are wholly the nature of mind.

Chinese Source

心性無外攝無不周，十方諸佛法界有情，性體無殊一切咸遍，誰云內外、色心、己他？此即用向色心不二門成。

Ru-Yi Translation

The nature of mind has no outside; it gathers in everything, leaving nothing out. The buddhas of the ten directions and the sentient beings of the dharma-realm do not differ in the essence of their nature, which pervades all. Who would speak of inner and outer, form and mind, oneself and others? This is accomplished by using the preceding gate of the nonduality of form and mind.

3. The Gate of the Nonduality of Cultivation and Nature

Chinese Source

三、修性不二門者，性德秖是界如一念，此內界如三法具足，性雖本爾，藉智起修，由修照性，由性發修，存性則全修成性，起修則全性成修，性無所移、修常宛爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

3. The gate of the nonduality of cultivation and nature. Inherent virtue is simply the realms and suchnesses in a single thought; within it, the realms and suchnesses fully contain the three tracks. Though the nature is originally so, one relies on wisdom to give rise to cultivation: through cultivation one illuminates the nature, and from the nature cultivation is brought forth. In keeping to the nature, the whole of cultivation becomes the nature; in giving rise to cultivation, the whole of the nature becomes cultivation. The nature is not moved in the least, and cultivation is always fully present.

Chinese Source

修又二種：順修、逆修。順謂了性為行，逆謂背性成迷。

Ru-Yi Translation

Cultivation, further, is of two kinds: compliant cultivation and contrary cultivation. "Compliant" means understanding the nature and making it one's practice; "contrary" means turning one's back on the nature and becoming deluded.

Chinese Source

迷了二心，心雖不二，逆順二性，性事恒殊。

Ru-Yi Translation

Delusion and understanding are two minds. Though the mind is not two, the contrary and the compliant are two natures, and as nature and as event they always differ.

Chinese Source

可由事、不移心，則令迷修成了，故須一期迷了，照性成修，見性修心，二心俱泯。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is possible, by way of events and without moving the mind, to turn deluded cultivation into understanding. Therefore, over the whole span of delusion and understanding, one must illuminate the nature to accomplish cultivation, see the nature and cultivate the mind, until both minds dissolve.

Chinese Source

又了順修對性有離有合，離謂修性各三，合謂修二性一。修二各三，共發性三，是則修雖具九，九秖是三。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, the compliant cultivation of understanding, set against the nature, may be separated or combined. "Separated" means that cultivation and nature each have three; "combined" means that cultivation is two and nature is one. The two of cultivation each have three, and together they bring forth the three of the nature. Thus, although cultivation contains nine, the nine are only three.

Chinese Source

為對性明修故，合修為二，二與一性如水為波，二亦無二亦如波水。

Ru-Yi Translation

In order to clarify cultivation in relation to the nature, cultivation is combined into two. The two and the one nature are like water becoming waves; and the two are also not two, again like waves and water.

Chinese Source

應知性指三障，是故具三，修從性成，成三法爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that the nature points to the three obstacles; that is why it contains three. Cultivation is accomplished from the nature, and accomplishing the three is naturally so.

Chinese Source

達無修性、唯一妙乘，無所分別，法界洞朗。此由內外不二門成。

Ru-Yi Translation

When one penetrates that there is neither cultivation nor nature, there is only the one wondrous vehicle, with nothing to discriminate, and the dharma-realm is open and bright. This is accomplished through the gate of the nonduality of inner and outer.

4. The Gate of the Nonduality of Cause and Fruit

Chinese Source

四、因果不二門者，眾生心因既具三軌，此因成果名三涅槃，因果無殊始終理一。

Ru-Yi Translation

4. The gate of the nonduality of cause and fruit. Since the cause that is the mind of sentient beings already contains the three tracks, when this cause becomes the fruit it is called the three nirvāṇas. Cause and fruit do not differ; beginning and end are one in principle.

Chinese Source

若爾因德已具，何不住因？但由迷因各自謂實。

Ru-Yi Translation

If so, the virtues of the cause are already complete; why not abide in the cause? Only because, deluded about the cause, each [of cause and fruit] is taken to be real in itself.

Chinese Source

若了迷性實，唯住因故，久研此因，因顯名果。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one understands that the nature of delusion is real, then precisely because one abides only in the cause, one refines this cause for a long time, and when the cause becomes manifest it is called the fruit.

Chinese Source

秖緣因果理一，用此一理為因，理顯無復果名，豈可仍存因號？因果既泯，理性自亡，秖由亡智親疎，致使迷成厚薄。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is only because cause and fruit are one in principle that this one principle is used as the cause; when the principle is manifest, there is no longer even the name "fruit" — how could the label "cause" still remain? Once cause and fruit dissolve, the nature of principle itself vanishes. It is only because the wisdom that makes them vanish is nearer or farther that delusion comes to be thick or thin.

Chinese Source

迷厚薄故，強分三惑，義開六即，名智淺深。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because delusion is thick or thin, the three delusions are distinguished by force, the six identities are opened out in meaning, and wisdom is named shallow or deep.

Chinese Source

故如夢勤加、空名惑絕，幻因既滿鏡像果圓，空像雖即義同，而空虛像實，像實故稱理本有，空虛故迷轉成性，是則不二而二立因果殊，二而不二始終體一。

Ru-Yi Translation

So it is like diligent effort made in a dream: delusion is cut off in name, in emptiness. When the illusory cause is full, the mirror-image fruit is complete. Although emptiness and the image are identical and the same in meaning, emptiness is void while the image is real. Because the image is real, the principle is said to be originally present; because emptiness is void, delusion turns and becomes the nature. Thus, not two yet two, cause and fruit are established as different; two yet not two, beginning and end are one in essence.

Chinese Source

若謂因異果，因亦非因，曉果從因，因方克果。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one says the cause is different from the fruit, then the cause is not a cause either. Only when one understands that the fruit comes from the cause does the cause attain the fruit.

Chinese Source

所以三千在理同名無明，三千果成咸稱常樂。三千無改無明即明，三千並常俱體俱用。此以修性不二門成。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is why the three thousand, while in principle, are all alike called ignorance; when the three thousand are accomplished as the fruit, they are all called permanence and bliss. The three thousand are unchanged, and ignorance is itself illumination; the three thousand are all permanent, all essence and all function. This is accomplished by means of the gate of the nonduality of cultivation and nature.

5. The Gate of the Nonduality of Defilement and Purity

Chinese Source

五、染淨不二門者，若識無始，即法性為無明，故可了今即無明為法性。

Ru-Yi Translation

5. The gate of the nonduality of defilement and purity. If one recognizes that from beginningless time the Dharma-nature itself has been ignorance, then one can understand that now ignorance itself is the Dharma-nature.

Chinese Source

法性之與無明遍造諸法，名之為染，無明之與法性遍應眾緣，號之為淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Dharma-nature, together with ignorance, pervasively creating all dharmas, is called defilement; ignorance, together with the Dharma-nature, pervasively responding to the many conditions, is called purity.

Chinese Source

濁水清水，波濕無殊，清濁雖即由緣，而濁成本有，濁雖本有而全體是清，以二波理通、舉體是用，故三千因果俱名緣起。

Ru-Yi Translation

Muddy water and clear water do not differ as waves or as wetness. Although clear and muddy depend on conditions, the muddy, as it forms, is originally present; and although the muddy is originally present, its whole essence is clear. Because the principle of the two kinds of waves is shared, and the whole essence is function, the three thousand, as cause and fruit, are all called conditioned arising.

Chinese Source

迷悟緣起不離剎那，剎那性常，緣起理一，一理之內而分淨穢，別則六穢四淨，通則十通淨穢。

Ru-Yi Translation

The conditioned arising of delusion and awakening does not depart from the instant. The nature of the instant is permanent, and the principle of conditioned arising is one. Within the one principle, pure and impure are distinguished: in particular, six [realms] are impure and four are pure; in general, all ten are both pure and impure.

Chinese Source

故知剎那染體悉淨，三千未顯驗體仍迷，故相似位成六根遍照。

Ru-Yi Translation

So we know that the defiled essence of the instant is entirely pure; [but] while the three thousand are not yet manifest, the essence is shown to be still deluded. Therefore, at the stage of resemblance, the six faculties come to illuminate everywhere.

Chinese Source

照分十界，各俱灼然，豈六根淨人謂十定十？

Ru-Yi Translation

Their illumination distinguishes the ten realms, each fully and brilliantly clear. How could a person whose six faculties are purified say that the ten are fixed as ten?

Chinese Source

分真垂迹十界亦然，乃至果成等彼百界。

Ru-Yi Translation

One who has realized a portion of the truth lets fall traces in the ten realms in the same way; and so on, until, when the fruit is accomplished, it is equal to those hundred realms.

Chinese Source

故須初心而遮而照，照故三千恒具，遮故法爾空中，終日雙亡終日雙照，不動此念遍應無方，隨感而施淨穢斯泯。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore, from the beginner's mind onward, one must both negate and illuminate. Because one illuminates, the three thousand are always fully present; because one negates, they are naturally empty and the middle. All day long both vanish; all day long both are illuminated. Without stirring this thought, one responds everywhere without restriction, bestowing according to the appeal, and pure and impure thereby dissolve.

Chinese Source

亡淨穢故以空以中，仍由空中轉染為淨。由了染淨空中自亡，此以因果不二門成。

Ru-Yi Translation

Pure and impure vanish by means of the empty and the middle; and it is still through the empty and the middle that defilement is turned into purity. Through understanding defilement and purity, the empty and the middle themselves vanish. This is accomplished by means of the gate of the nonduality of cause and fruit.

6. The Gate of the Nonduality of the Dependent and the Principal

Chinese Source

六、依正不二門者，已證遮那一體不二，良由無始一念三千。

Ru-Yi Translation

6. The gate of the nonduality of the dependent and the principal. That Vairocana, already realized, is one essence and not two is truly due to the three thousand in a single thought from beginningless time.

Chinese Source

以三千中生陰二千為正，國土一千屬依，依正既居一心，一心豈分能所？

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the three thousand, the two thousand of beings and aggregates are the principal, and the one thousand of lands belong to the dependent. Since the dependent and the principal both reside in one mind, how could one mind be divided into subject and object?

Chinese Source

雖無能所，依正宛然，是則理性、名字、觀行，已有不二依正之相，故使自他因果相攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Although there is no subject and no object, the dependent and the principal are fully present. Thus already at [the identities of] principle, name, and contemplative practice there are the marks of the nondual dependent and principal; this is why self and other, cause and fruit, contain one another.

Chinese Source

但眾生在理，果雖未辦，一切莫非遮那妙境。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is only that sentient beings are at [the identity of] principle; although the fruit is not yet accomplished, everything is none other than Vairocana's wondrous object.

Chinese Source

然應復了，諸佛法體非遍而遍，眾生理性非局而局，始終不改、大小無妨，因果理同依正何別？

Ru-Yi Translation

But one should further understand: the Dharma-essence of the buddhas is not pervasive, yet pervasive; the principle-nature of sentient beings is not confined, yet confined. Beginning and end are unchanged, and large and small do not obstruct each other. Since cause and fruit are the same in principle, how could the dependent and the principal differ?

Chinese Source

故淨穢之土、勝劣之身，塵身與法身量同，塵國與寂光無異，是則一一塵剎一切剎，一一塵身一切身，廣狹勝劣難思議，淨穢方所無窮盡，若非三千空假中，安能成茲自在用。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore, among pure and impure lands and superior and inferior bodies, a body of dust is equal in measure to the Dharma-body, and a land of dust is no different from [the land of] tranquil light. Thus every dust-land is all lands, and every dust-body is all bodies; broad and narrow, superior and inferior, are hard to conceive; pure and impure places are without end. If not for the three thousand being empty, provisional, and the middle, how could this sovereign function be accomplished?

Chinese Source

如是方知生佛等，彼此事理互相收。此以染淨不二門成。

Ru-Yi Translation

Only in this way does one know that beings and buddhas are equal, and that each contains the other, in event and in principle. This is accomplished by means of the gate of the nonduality of defilement and purity.

7. The Gate of the Nonduality of Self and Other

Chinese Source

七、自他不二門者，隨機利他，事乃憑本。本謂一性具足自他，方至果位自即益他。

Ru-Yi Translation

7. The gate of the nonduality of self and other. In benefiting others according to their capacities, the event relies on the origin. "Origin" means that the one nature fully contains self and other; only on reaching the stage of the fruit is oneself identical to benefiting others.

Chinese Source

如理性三德、三諦三千，自行唯在空中，利他三千赴物。

Ru-Yi Translation

Take the three virtues of the principle-nature, and the three truths and the three thousand: in one's own practice they lie only in the empty and the middle; in benefiting others, the three thousand go forth to beings.

Chinese Source

物機無量不出三千，能應雖多不出十界，界界轉現不出一念，土土互生不出寂光。

Ru-Yi Translation

The capacities of beings are measureless, yet they do not go beyond the three thousand; those who respond are many, yet they do not go beyond the ten realms; realm after realm appears in turn, yet does not go beyond a single thought; land after land gives rise to one another, yet does not go beyond tranquil light.

Chinese Source

眾生由理具三千故能感，諸佛由三千理滿故能應，應遍機遍欣赴不差。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because sentient beings contain the three thousand in principle, they can make an appeal; because the buddhas have fulfilled the principle of the three thousand, they can respond. The response is everywhere, the capacities are everywhere, and the glad going-forth never misses.

Chinese Source

不然，豈能如鏡現像？鏡有現像之理，形有生像之性，若一形對不能現像，則鏡理有窮、形事不通。

Ru-Yi Translation

Otherwise, how could it be like a mirror showing images? The mirror has the principle of showing images; a form has the nature of producing an image. If there were a single form facing it whose image it could not show, then the mirror's principle would have a limit, and the form's event would not get through.

Chinese Source

若與鏡隔則容有是理，無有形對而不像者。

Ru-Yi Translation

If [the form] is cut off from the mirror, this may well be so; but there is no form that faces it and is not imaged.

Chinese Source

若鏡未現像由塵所遮，去塵由人磨，現像非關磨者，以喻觀法大旨可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

If the mirror has not yet shown an image, it is because dust covers it; removing the dust depends on someone polishing it; but the showing of the image has nothing to do with the one who polishes. Take this as a simile, and the main purport of the method of contemplation can be understood.

Chinese Source

應知理雖自他具足，必藉緣了為利他功。復由緣了與性一合，方能稱性施設萬端，則不起自性化無方所。此由依正不二門成。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that although principle fully contains self and other, it must rely on the conditioning and revealing [causes] to accomplish the work of benefiting others. And only when the conditioning and revealing [causes] are united as one with the nature can one, in accord with the nature, set up the myriad means; then, without rising from one's own nature, one transforms [beings] with no fixed place. This is accomplished through the gate of the nonduality of the dependent and the principal.

8. The Gate of the Nonduality of the Three Kinds of Action

Chinese Source

八、三業不二門者，於化他門事分三密，隨順物理得名不同，心輪鑒機、二輪設化，現身說法未曾毫差。

Ru-Yi Translation

8. The gate of the nonduality of the three kinds of action. In the gate of transforming others, the event is divided into the three secrets, which receive different names in accord with the principles of beings: the wheel of mind discerns capacities, and the other two wheels carry out the transforming, manifesting bodies and teaching the Dharma without the slightest error.

Chinese Source

在身分於真應，在法分於權實。二身若異，何故乃云即是法身？二說若乖，何故乃云皆成佛道？

Ru-Yi Translation

In body, it is divided into the true and the responsive; in Dharma, into the provisional and the real. If the two bodies were different, why is it said, "This is itself the Dharma-body"? If the two teachings were at odds, why is it said, "All attain the Buddha way"?

Chinese Source

若唯法身，應無垂世；若唯佛道，誰施三乘？

Ru-Yi Translation

If there were only the Dharma-body, there would be no coming down into the world; if there were only the Buddha way, who would bestow the three vehicles?

Chinese Source

身尚無身，說必非說，身口平等，等彼意輪，心色一如不謀而化，常冥至極稱物施為，豈非百界一心？

Ru-Yi Translation

Even the body is no body; the teaching is surely no teaching. Body and speech are equal, equal to the wheel of intention; mind and form are one suchness, transforming without deliberation, ever merged with the utmost, acting in accord with beings. Is this not the hundred realms in one mind?

Chinese Source

界界無非三業，界尚一念，三業豈殊？

Ru-Yi Translation

Every realm is none other than the three kinds of action. If even the realms are a single thought, how could the three kinds of action differ?

Chinese Source

果用無虧，因必稱果，若信因果，方知三密有本。

Ru-Yi Translation

The function of the fruit is without lack, and the cause must accord with the fruit. Only if one has faith in cause and fruit does one know that the three secrets have an origin.

Chinese Source

百界三業俱空假中，故使稱宜遍赴為果。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three kinds of action of the hundred realms are all empty, provisional, and the middle; that is why going everywhere, as is fitting, can be the fruit.

Chinese Source

一一應色、一一言音，無不百界三業具足，化復作化斯之謂歟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Every responsive form and every sound of speech is fully endowed with the three kinds of action of the hundred realms. Is this not what is meant by "a transformation that in turn makes transformations"?

Chinese Source

故一念凡心已有理性三密相海，一塵報色同在本理毘盧遮那，方乃名為三無差別。此以自他不二門成。

Ru-Yi Translation

So a single thought of an ordinary mind already has the ocean of marks of the three secrets of the principle-nature, and a single dust-mote of retributive form is likewise within Vairocana of the original principle. Only this is called "the three without distinction." This is accomplished by means of the gate of the nonduality of self and other.

9. The Gate of the Nonduality of the Provisional and the Real

Chinese Source

九、權實不二門者，平等大慧常鑒法界，亦由理性九權一實，實復九界、權亦復然，權實相冥，百界一念，不可分別任運常然。

Ru-Yi Translation

9. The gate of the nonduality of the provisional and the real. Great wisdom that is equal always discerns the dharma-realm. This, too, is because in the principle-nature nine are provisional and one is real; yet the real again [contains] the nine realms, and so too does the provisional. The provisional and the real merge with each other; the hundred realms are a single thought; they cannot be separated, and are spontaneously and always so.

Chinese Source

至果乃由契本一理，非權非實而權而實，此即如前心輪自在，致令身口赴權實機。

Ru-Yi Translation

Reaching the fruit comes from according with the one original principle: neither provisional nor real, yet provisional and yet real. This is just the sovereign wheel of mind described above, which causes body and speech to go forth to the capacities for the provisional and the real.

Chinese Source

三業一念無乖權實，不動而施豈應隔異？

Ru-Yi Translation

The three kinds of action are a single thought and do not depart from the provisional and the real. Bestowing without moving, how could there be any separation?

Chinese Source

對說即以權實立稱，在身即以真應為名，三業理同權實冥合。此以三業不二門成。

Ru-Yi Translation

With respect to teaching, the terms "provisional" and "real" are established; in body, the names are "true" and "responsive." The three kinds of action are the same in principle, and the provisional and the real merge as one. This is accomplished by means of the gate of the nonduality of the three kinds of action.

10. The Gate of the Nonduality of Receiving and Moistening

Chinese Source

十、受潤不二門者，物理本來性具權實，無始熏習或權或實，權實由熏理常平等，遇時成習行願所資，若無本因熏亦徒設，遇熏自異非由性殊。

Ru-Yi Translation

10. The gate of the nonduality of receiving and moistening. The principle of beings by nature originally contains the provisional and the real. Through perfuming since beginningless time, some are provisional and some real; the provisional and the real come from perfuming, while the principle is always equal. When the time comes, habit is formed, supported by practice and vows. If there were no original cause, perfuming would be set up in vain. Meeting with perfuming, they differ of themselves, not because their natures differ.

Chinese Source

性雖無殊必藉幻發，幻機幻感幻應幻赴，能應所化並非權實。

Ru-Yi Translation

Although the nature does not differ, it must rely on illusion to come forth: illusory capacity, illusory appeal, illusory response, illusory going-forth. The one who responds and those who are transformed are both neither provisional nor real.

Chinese Source

然由生具非權非實成權實機，佛亦果具非權非實為權實應，物機應契身土無偏，同常寂光無非法界。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet because beings contain what is neither provisional nor real, they become capacities for the provisional and the real; and the Buddha, too, containing in the fruit what is neither provisional nor real, makes provisional and real responses. When the capacities of beings and the response accord, body and land are without partiality; all are the same eternal tranquil light, none other than the dharma-realm.

Chinese Source

故知三千同在心地，與佛心地三千不殊，四微體同權實益等。此以權實不二門成。

Ru-Yi Translation

So we know that the three thousand are all within the mind-ground, no different from the three thousand of the Buddha's mind-ground. The four subtle elements are the same in essence, and the benefits of the provisional and the real are equal. This is accomplished by means of the gate of the nonduality of the provisional and the real.

Conclusion: Every Gate Opens onto All

Chinese Source

是故十門，門門通入，色心乃至受潤咸然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore, of the ten gates, each gate gives entry to all the others; from form and mind through receiving and moistening, all are so.

Chinese Source

故使十妙始終理一，如境本來具三，依理生解故名為智，智解導行行解契理，三法相符不異而異，而假立淺深設位簡濫。

Ru-Yi Translation

So the ten wondrous aspects are one in principle from beginning to end. For example, the object originally contains the three; understanding arises in reliance on principle, and so it is called wisdom; the understanding of wisdom guides practice, and practice and understanding accord with principle. The three tracks match one another, not different yet different; and so shallow and deep are provisionally set up, and stages are established to sift out false claims.

Chinese Source

三法秖是證彼三理，下之五章三法起用。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three tracks are simply the realization of those three principles; the five sections below are the three tracks giving rise to function.

Chinese Source

既是一念三千，即空假中，成故有用。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since it is three thousand realms in a single thought, identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle, once it is accomplished it has function.

Chinese Source

若了一念，十方三世諸佛之法本迹非遙。故重述十門，令觀行可識。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one understands a single thought, the Dharma of the buddhas of the ten directions and the three times, in origin and in trace, is not far off. Therefore I restate [the ten wondrous aspects] as ten gates, so that contemplative practice can be recognized.

Chinese Source

首題既爾，覽別為總，符文可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the opening title is so, take the particular as the general, and you will know it by matching it with the text.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Where the text comes from. These pages stand in Zhanran's Fahua Xuanyi Shiqian (T1717), fascicle 14, at the point where his commentary on the ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate is complete and the origin gate is about to begin. "This trace gate" in the opening line therefore means the trace-gate half of Zhiyi's Fahua Xuanyi, translated in full on this site. The ten wondrous aspects are named here as on that site: the wondrous of the object, of wisdom, of practice, of stage, of the three tracks, of sympathetic response, of spiritual powers, of teaching the Dharma, of the retinue, and of merit and benefit.

"The origin gate has already cited trustworthy proofs extensively." Read literally, 本門 is the origin gate; but in the Shiqian this passage comes before the origin-gate section. Alternatively, 本門 may be read as "the base text" (本文), that is, Zhiyi's Fahua Xuanyi itself, which cites scripture at length. The translation keeps the literal wording. (Added 2026-11-16: the CBETA text of the Shiqian itself, T1717 fascicle 14, reads 本文已廣引誠證, "the base text has already cited trustworthy proofs extensively," which supports the second reading. See [[法華玄義釋籤 英譯稿]], Part 14 notes, for the other differences between T1717 and T1927.)

"Relying on events or attaching to dharmas" (託事附法). Two of the three ways Tiantai commentary attaches contemplation of mind to a text: by taking a narrative event as a figure for contemplation, or by attaching contemplation to a doctrinal item. "The ten contemplations" are the ten modes of contemplation of the Mohe Zhiguan.

"Not two, yet two; two, yet not two" (不二而二，二而不二). The governing formula of the whole text. Setting up the provisional for the sake of the real produces a real "two" out of what is not two; opening the provisional to reveal the real shows that the two were never two. The translation keeps the mirror-image wording each time it recurs (Gates 4 and 6, and the conclusion).

"The essence, the cardinal doctrine, and the function … the characteristics of the teachings." These are four of Zhiyi's Five Sections (this site: Explaining the Name, Clarifying the Essence, Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine, Discussing the Function, Classifying the Teachings). "The ten modes of calming-and-contemplation" and "the section on giving rise to the teaching" refer to the Mohe Zhiguan, whose seventh section sets out the ten modes and whose ninth section (never delivered) was to be "giving rise to the teaching."

From the ten suchnesses to "no truth." These are the objects treated under the wondrous of the object in the Fahua Xuanyi: the ten suchnesses, the twelve links, the Four Noble Truths, the two truths, the three truths, the one real truth, and no truth. Gate 1 sorts each list into what belongs to form, to mind, or to both.

"The form and mind of the mind are, being mind, called transformation." The wording of 心之色心即心名變 is compressed and can be parsed more than one way; the translation keeps the order of the Chinese. Zhili's commentary makes this sentence central to his reading of "mind only," and his reading is not followed here.

"The essence of form and mind is cut off" (色心體絕). 絕 is the same word used for the "absolute" reading of "wondrous" (絕待妙) in the Fahua Xuanyi; the sense is that form and mind, as empty, provisional, and the middle, are beyond any relative description.

The seven kinds of expedient [practitioners] (七方便). The practitioners of the lesser and provisional paths, conventionally listed as humans and gods, hearers, solitary buddhas, and the bodhisattvas of the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct teachings.

Inherent virtue and the three tracks. "Inherent virtue" (性德) and its counterpart "cultivated virtue" (修德) are rendered as on this site's Fahua Xuanyi (the three tracks of inherent virtue / of cultivated virtue). The three tracks are the track of real nature, of contemplative illumination, and of resource-and-accomplishment; in Gate 3, "cultivation is two" refers to the latter two, and "nature is one" to the first.

"The nature points to the three obstacles." The three obstacles (三障) are usually afflictions, karma, and retribution. Zhanran's point is that the three of the nature are found within the three obstacles themselves, not apart from them.

"Over the whole span of delusion and understanding" (一期迷了). 一期 may also be read "for the time being": one provisionally distinguishes delusion and understanding in order to practice, until both dissolve.

"Only because, deluded about the cause, each [of cause and fruit] is taken to be real in itself" (但由迷因各自謂實). Alternatively: "each [person] takes [his or her own view] to be real." The bracketed words show the reading chosen.

"Delusion is cut off in name, in emptiness" (空名惑絕). The simile is of someone who works hard within a dream and then wakes: the severing of delusion is "empty," a matter of name, because the delusion was never substantial. Alternatively, 空名 may be read "is called, in vain, the severing of delusion."

"Ignorance is itself illumination" (無明即明). Gate 4 states and Gate 5 develops the identity of ignorance and the Dharma-nature. The two directions — "the Dharma-nature itself has been ignorance" and "ignorance itself is the Dharma-nature" — are translated as two distinct sentences, as in the Chinese.

"Six [realms] are impure and four are pure." Of the ten dharma-realms, the six from hell beings through gods are impure; hearers, solitary buddhas, bodhisattvas, and buddhas are pure.

The stage of resemblance and the six faculties. At the fourth of the six identities, identity in resemblance, the six sense faculties are purified, as in the Lotus Sūtra's chapter on the merits of the Dharma teacher (on this site, "The Purified Six Senses"). "A portion of the truth" is the fifth identity.

Negate and illuminate (遮／照). To negate is to see the three thousand as empty and the middle; to illuminate is to see them as fully present. "Both vanish" and "both are illuminated" (雙亡雙照) apply this pair to pure and impure at once.

"That Vairocana, already realized, is one essence and not two" (已證遮那一體不二). Alternatively: "Those who have realized [buddhahood] are of one essence with Vairocana, not two." 遮那 and 毘盧遮那 (Gate 8) both refer to Vairocana.

The dependent and the principal (依正). The "principal" reward is one's own body and mind; the "dependent" reward is the land and environment one lives in. Of the three kinds of world that make up the "three thousand," the worlds of beings and of the five aggregates are the principal, and the world of lands is the dependent; hence "two thousand" and "one thousand."

Tranquil light (寂光). The land of eternal tranquil light (常寂光土), the highest of the four Tiantai buddha-lands.

The conditioning and revealing [causes] (緣了). Two of the three causes of buddha-nature; the third, the correct cause, is "the nature" with which they unite. 了因 is rendered "the revealing cause," as in this site's Fahua Xuanyi, Fahua Wenju, and Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra.

The three secrets and the three wheels (三密, 三輪). The body, speech, and mind of a buddha are "secret" because unfathomable, and "wheels" because they roll over and crush afflictions. The wheel of mind (心輪) and the wheel of intention (意輪) name the same third member; the translation keeps the two Chinese terms distinct.

"This is itself the Dharma-body" and "All attain the Buddha way." The second is from the Lotus Sūtra's chapter on skillful means. The first echoes passages stating that the responsive body is itself the Dharma-body.

"A transformation that in turn makes transformations" (化復作化). A stock phrase for emanation bodies that themselves produce further emanations.

"The three without distinction" (三無差別). From the Avataṃsaka Sūtra: mind, buddha, and sentient beings — these three are without distinction.

Receiving and moistening (受潤). From the Lotus Sūtra's parable of the medicinal herbs: one rain falls, and each plant receives its moisture according to its kind. "The four subtle elements" (四微 — the visible, odor, taste, and the tangible) are, on this site's Fahua Xuanyi, the substrate common to flower and seed-pod; here they make the same point for rain and plants.

"The five sections below." The last five of the ten wondrous aspects (sympathetic response through merit and benefit), which concern transforming others and are said to be the function arising from the three tracks.

"Since the opening title is so" (首題既爾). Most naturally the title of the sūtra, The Lotus Sūtra of the Wondrous Dharma, whose single word "wondrous" Zhiyi has just unfolded into the ten wondrous aspects. Alternatively, the title of this section in the Shiqian.
