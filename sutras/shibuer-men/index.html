The Ten Gates of Nonduality.

Zhanran (湛然, 711–782) wrote these few pages inside his great sub-commentary on Zhiyi's Fahua Xuanyi. Having finished the ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate, he gathered all ten into ten gates of nonduality — form and mind, inner and outer, cultivation and nature, cause and fruit, defilement and purity, the dependent and the principal, self and other, the three kinds of action, the provisional and the real, and receiving and moistening — so that they could be contemplated and not only studied. Each gate rests on the one before it, and all of them rest on the three thousand realms in a single thought. Later copyists circulated the passage on its own, and in the Song dynasty it became the center of the home-mountain and off-mountain debate.

How to read this collection

The text is one continuous passage, translated here on a single page. It is easiest to read after the ten wondrous aspects of the Fahua Xuanyi, and it is the base text of Zhili's Pointing Out the Essentials, translated on this site. The same passage also appears, with a few textual variants, at the end of fascicle 14 of Zhanran's Explanatory Notes on the Profound Meaning.

Each gate ends by naming the gate it is "accomplished through," so the ten form a chain; reading them in order matters more than reading any one alone.
