Ten Habits, Six Crossings, One Wheel of Rebirth

Ānanda asks why hell, hungry ghosts, and animals exist at all, if every being's own nature is originally the same pure substance as a buddha's. The Buddha answers by tracing every destiny back to a single mechanism: the ratio, at the moment of death, of downward-pulling emotion to upward-lifting thought. Master Yuanying then unpacks, clause by clause, the ten habitual causes of hell and their six cross-referenced retributions across every sense-faculty, the ten kinds of hungry ghosts each craving drives a being toward, and the animal realm's own long accounting of debts repaid.

About This Fascicle

Fascicle 19 closed the sūtra's entire first major division on the cultivation of samādhi. Fascicle 20 opens a new one: having awakened to the path itself, Ānanda now asks why hell, hungry ghosts, animals, asuras, humans, and gods exist at all, if every being's true nature is originally the same pure substance the Buddha himself awakened to — citing three real, named cases (the nun Ratnakamalā, King Virūḍhaka, and the monk Sunakṣatra) who fell straight into hell, in their very living bodies, despite outward practice. The Buddha answers by tracing every destiny back to a single mechanism, first sketched in outline and then worked out in full: an "internal tendency" (attached, downward-pulling emotion) and an "external tendency" (aspiring, upward-lifting thought), whose shifting ratio in any one mind, at the moment of death, determines with mathematical precision exactly which of the seven destinies a being falls into. The fascicle then turns to hell in unsparing clause-by-clause detail — ten habitual causes (lust, greed, arrogance, anger, deceit, fraud, grudges, wrong views, false accusation, and litigation), each producing its own hell-image through six senses "crossing" into one another — followed by the ten kinds of hungry ghosts sorted by the specific craving that drove the original offense, and closes with the animal realm's own long, exact accounting of debts owed and repaid.

Quoted passages from the sūtra itself are marked Sūtra:; Yuanying's own explanation is marked Commentary:. This fascicle's sūtra text overlaps substantially with material the companion bare-sūtra project already translated in its own fascicle 8 — Ānanda's question, the internal/external tendency teaching, the emotion-and-thought passage, and (in condensed, summary form) the ten habitual causes, six retributions, ten hungry ghosts, and the animal realm. Wherever Yuanying quotes the identical CBETA wording already translated there, that existing translation is reused verbatim; because that companion project translates the sūtra alone and abbreviates the ten causes and six retributions to a representative first item plus a summary grid, this fascicle supplies, for the first time, the complete sūtra text and Yuanying's own extensive clause-by-clause commentary for every one of the ten causes and six retributions in full.

Ānanda Gives Thanks, and Asks Why the Seven Destinies Exist 丙二 助道別詳護定要法 · 丁初 談七趣勸離以警淹留 · 戊初 阿難請問

Yuanying frames this whole second major division of the sūtra in one sentence: everything before this was direct cultivation — Ānanda asking about samādhi, the Buddha answering concerning samādhi. Everything from here on is auxiliary practice, aiding samādhi's own success: first, discussing the seven destinies and urging that they be left behind, aiding samādhi through the precepts; second (fascicle 21 onward), distinguishing the five kinds of demonic states, aiding samādhi through wisdom.

Chinese Source

〖經〗即從座起，頂禮佛足。合掌恭敬，而白佛言：大威德世尊，慈音無遮，善開眾生，微細沉惑，令我今日，身心快然，得大饒益！

〖講〗此謝前述益，即從本座而起，頂禮佛之雙足，合掌恭敬，而白佛言：大威德世尊；十二類生，顛倒妄想，一時頓銷，故具折伏之大威。五十五位，增上妙理盡情吐露，故具攝受之大德。慈音無遮：遮者限也平等也：教不簡乎親疏，機不分乎勝劣。善開眾生，微細沉惑者：善巧方便，開示眾生，微細煩惱，以思惑修道位中所斷，行相難了，故曰微細。無始俱生無明，故曰沉惑。此皆破妄也。妄既破，而真自顯，喜菩提之有分，知涅槃之可修，從此生死無干，故得身心快然，得大饒益也。初謝前述益竟。

〖經〗世尊！若此妙明，真淨妙心，本來遍圓，如是乃至，大地草木，蝡動含靈，本元真如，即是如來，成佛真體。佛體真實！云何復有：地獄、餓鬼、畜生、脩羅、人、天等道？世尊！此道為復本來自有？為是眾生妄習生起？

〖講〗此總問諸趣。先據唯心真實。世尊，若此眾生，所具如來藏性：其體不變曰妙；其用隨緣曰明；從來無妄曰真；究竟不染曰淨；具此四種德相，曰妙心；本來周遍圓滿。妙心既已如是，乃至超略之詞，超四大、五陰、根、塵、識法，以及虛空，乃至大地草木（器世間），蝡動含靈（有情世間），本來原具，真如自性，即是十方如來，成佛真體，無二無別。佛體真實！云何復有：七趣之虛妄耶？等道，即仙道。世尊此等諸道，因何而有？為復真如體中本來自有耶？為是眾生心中妄習生起耶？若是真如體中，從本以來自有，則七趣不應虛妄；若是眾生心中，妄想習氣生起，則心外有法，與今現悟遍圓之義不符，所以未敢深領，而致問也。又前滿慈所問：清淨本然，云何忽生三種生續？此阿難所問，佛體真實，云何復有七趣虛妄？同耶異耶？答：前問三種生續，起於性覺必明，妄為明覺；今問七趣虛妄？由於情想差別，故見輪迴。若悟後文，精研七趣，皆是昏沉，諸有為相，妄想受生，妄想隨業，於妙圓明，無作本心，皆如空華，元無所著；但一虛妄，更無根緒，則二疑俱釋矣！二總問諸趣竟。

〖經〗世尊！如寶蓮香比丘尼，持菩薩戒，私行淫欲，妄言行淫，非殺非偷，無有業報。發是語已，先於女根，生大猛火，後於節節，猛火燒然，墮無間獄。琉璃大王，善星比丘，琉璃為誅瞿曇族姓；善星妄說一切法空；生身陷入，阿鼻地獄。此諸地獄，為有定處？為復自然，彼彼發業，各各私受？惟垂大慈，開發童蒙，令諸一切持戒眾生，聞決定義，歡喜頂戴，謹潔無犯。

〖講〗此淫報。舉現事以問地獄之報也。稱呼曰世尊！如寶蓮香比丘尼，持菩薩大戒，而又私行淫欲，既已破戒，且以妄言謗戒。謂行淫，非同殺生害命，非比偷劫財物，但是彼此交歡，現前無業，將來亦無果報。蓋私淫是破戒，妄言無報是破見；毀律誤人，罪惡彌甚；故感華報、果報，一時俱受。發是語已，先於女根，生大猛火者：淫欲屬火，故感女根生大猛火，業力增上，報不容緩也。後於節節，猛火燒然者：《烏芻章》云：說多淫人，成猛火聚，信不誣矣！此華報不足以償，故命終神識，徑墮地獄，而受果報矣！《指掌疏》云：下云九情一想，下洞火輪，身入風火，二交過地，輕生有間，重生無間。今寶蓮香，雖說淫欲無報，猶信殺偷有報，似是九情一想，受報應在風火二交過地。但以毀戒誤人，罪惡極重，故云墮無間地獄。

琉璃報復屬怒，善星邪見屬癡，亦舉現事，以問地獄之報。合上寶蓮香私行淫欲，總之淫、怒、癡三，為墮獄之因。為人在世，不可不慎；若復修行，更宜痛戒。琉璃王，是匿王太子，誅瞿曇族姓，以報一言之辱。惟以瞋怒增勝，殺戮太過，故致墮獄耳。琉璃王少時，定省外氏，適釋種為佛新造講堂，莊嚴燦爛，中設法座，佛尚未坐，而琉璃王竟坐其上。釋氏罵曰：此婢生物，汝有何福，敢於中坐？催逐令出。太子出已，語侍臣好苦言：釋種辱我至此，我紹位後，汝當告我，我起兵伐之。婢生物：因匿王求婚於釋氏，若不許，恐挾嫌伐我；若許，又非所願；後乃以婢女，充為釋種女嫁之。太子是其所生，此次來外婆家。及至長成，廢父自立；好苦來告大王，曾記釋種一言之辱乎？遂起兵伐釋種。目連請佛救度，佛乃默然。於是目連以缽盛諸釋種，至於天上。大兵既至，恣行殺戮。琉璃王有欲害佛，入見佛已，毛骨悚然。佛記七日之後，當入地獄。王懼，率領軍隊，乘船入海；時至，水中自然出火燒滅。大兵既退，目連見佛曰：當時請佛救度，因佛默默無語，我乃以缽盛救，送到天上，今則持歸。佛語之曰：定業不可轉，非汝神力，所能奈何！啟缽視之，皆化為血水矣！

遠因者：佛言往昔羅閱城中，有池多魚，天旱水乾，人向池中捕魚。內有魚王，一名麩，二名多舌，藏在泥中，池水既乾，泥覺有動，遂為人捕。時有小兒，手持竹竿，戲敲其頭三下。爾時捕魚人，今釋種是，麩魚琉璃王是，多舌好苦是，小兒即我身是。詳出《涅槃》，及《琉璃王經》。

善星，亦云善宿。《阿含經》云：是佛堂弟之子，或曰即調達之子。《涅槃》云：善星比丘，親近惡友，退失四禪，生惡邪見，作是說言：無佛無有涅槃，如來雖復為我說法，而我真實，謂無因果。即妄說一切法空。

《指掌疏》云：琉璃瞋怒殺人，已屬重罪，且所殺又非常人，乃瞿曇族姓。善星妄說法空，即是邪癡，若更為竊取利養，仍兼偷盜。二人之罪，俱為極重，故皆以生身陷獄。下云：純情即沉，入阿鼻地獄，若沉心中，有謗大乘，毀佛禁戒，誑妄說法，虛貪信施，濫膺恭敬，更生十方阿鼻地獄。今琉璃恣殺，善星逞妄，似是純情，固應沉於阿鼻地獄。且琉璃恣意殺戮，甚欲害佛，善星誑妄說法，虛貪信施，或亦更生十方阿鼻地獄。但生身陷入地獄，人所共見。陷後備受五無間報：一、趣果無間，不經中陰。二、形無間，一人亦滿獄城，多人亦滿。三、苦具無間，此種苦具用畢，再換他種。四、命無間，一日之中，萬生萬死，受罪死矣，巧風一吹，依舊復活。五、經劫無間，獄中具足一大劫，劫滿寄生他方地獄，再一劫，其罪未畢，轉寄他方。

敢問世尊，以上三人觀之，此諸地獄，為有一定處所，雖造業不同，皆歸此處，同受報耶？為復各自其然，而彼彼發業有異，各各私受其報耶？此問有二意：上問別業同受，後問別業別受，所以不明而疑也。惟願垂大慈悲，開發我等，幼童蒙昧；此乃當機，愧無大智，有類童蒙，懇求開發，正知正見，令諸一切持戒眾生，聞示決定義門，不致猶預，自然心生歡喜，身則頂戴，益加謹潔奉持，而無犯戒之事矣！初阿難請問竟。

〖經〗佛告阿難：快哉此問！令諸眾生，不入邪見。汝今諦聽，當為汝說。

〖講〗佛告阿難快哉者：合意之問，如上私行淫欲，恚怒恣殺，愚癡妄語，淫、怒、癡三、皆屬邪見；今以此問，而得發明。普令眾生，不入邪見，咸歸正道，豈不慶快於心哉！汝今諦實而聽，吾當為汝，分別解說，地獄同別之義也。初讚歎許說竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: "Ānanda then rose from his seat, bowed his head at the Buddha's feet, and, palms joined in reverence, said to the Buddha: 'Greatly awesome and virtuous World-Honored One, your compassionate voice holds nothing back, and has opened up the subtle, deeply buried confusions of sentient beings, so that my own body and mind today feel light and clear, greatly benefited!'"

Commentary: This gives thanks for the benefit described before. That is, he rose at once from his own seat, bowed his head at the Buddha's own two feet, and, palms joined in reverence, said to the Buddha: "Greatly awesome and virtuous World-Honored One" — since these twelve categories of birth, this distortion and false thought, were all at once dissolved entirely, he is thereby complete with this great awesomeness of subduing delusion; and since these fifty-five stages, this excellent, wondrous principle, were disclosed entirely, holding nothing back, he is thereby complete with this great virtue of gathering beings in. "Your compassionate voice holds nothing back" — "holds nothing back" means without limit, equally to all — this teaching makes no distinction between the near and the distant, and this capacity draws no line between the superior and the inferior. "Has opened up the subtle, deeply buried confusions of sentient beings" means that, through skillful means, he has disclosed to sentient beings this subtle affliction — this being exactly this same confused thought severed within the stage of cultivating the path, its own workings difficult to fully understand, hence "subtle"; and this beginningless, innate ignorance, hence "deeply buried." All of this refutes falseness. Once falseness is refuted, truth manifests of itself; rejoicing that bodhi now has its own share in him, and knowing that nirvāṇa may now be cultivated, from this point on birth and death no longer concern him — hence his body and mind feel light and clear, greatly benefited. This closes the first section, giving thanks for the benefit described before.

Sūtra: "World-Honored One, if this wondrous, bright, true, pure, wondrous mind is originally all-pervading and perfectly complete — extending even to the great earth, the grasses and trees, and every crawling, sentient thing, their original true suchness itself being the very true substance by which the Tathāgata becomes Buddha, that substance of buddhahood being real and genuine — then how can there still be the destinies of hell, hungry ghosts, animals, asuras, humans, and gods? World-Honored One, do these destinies exist inherently, from the very beginning, or do they arise from beings' own false habitual tendencies?"

Commentary: This poses this total question about every destiny, resting first on this same truth: that mind alone is real. World-Honored One, should this same tathāgatagarbha nature, possessed by every sentient being — its substance unchanging, hence "wondrous"; its function according with conditions, hence "bright"; from the very beginning without any falseness, hence "true"; and never, in the end, stained by anything, hence "pure" — complete with these four virtuous marks, hence "wondrous mind" — be originally all-pervading and perfectly complete. This wondrous mind being already so, "extending even to" is a phrase abbreviating what precedes: beyond the four elements, the five aggregates, the sense-faculties, sense-objects, and consciousness, and even beyond empty space itself, extending even to the great earth and the grasses and trees — the world of insentient vessels — and every crawling, sentient thing — the sentient world — all originally, alike, possess this same true suchness, their own nature, exactly this same true substance by which the Tathāgatas of the ten directions become Buddha, without any difference at all.

"That substance of buddhahood being real and genuine" — since this is truly the Buddha's own substance, it certainly ought to be real and genuine — "then how can there still be" these seven destinies, this falseness? ("Etc." here means the destiny of immortals.) World-Honored One, from what cause do all these various destinies exist? Do they exist inherently, from the very beginning, within the substance of true suchness itself? Or do they arise from some false habitual tendency within sentient beings' own minds? Should they exist inherently, from the very beginning, within the substance of true suchness, then these seven destinies ought not to be false at all; and should they arise from some false habitual tendency within sentient beings' own minds, then there would be some dharma existing outside of mind — which does not accord with this present understanding, already awakened to, of this same all-pervading, perfectly complete mind — and it is for this reason that he does not dare simply accept this without further question, and so raises this question.

One might further ask: earlier, Pūrṇamaitrāyaṇīputra asked: "if this is originally pure and so of itself, how does it suddenly give rise to these three kinds of continuity?" And now Ānanda asks: "if the substance of buddhahood is real and genuine, how can there still be these seven destinies, this falseness?" Are these the same question, or different? The answer: the earlier question concerned these three kinds of continuity, arising from "the nature of awareness is necessarily bright; because of this brightness, false awareness is thereby established." This present question concerns the falseness of these seven destinies, arising from the differing degrees of emotion and thought, and so one sees this cycle of rebirth. Should one awaken to what follows, carefully examining these seven destinies, one finds that all of them are nothing but confusion and heaviness, every conditioned mark, false thought receiving birth, false thought following karma — while, within this wondrous, perfect, bright, uncontrived original mind, all of it is, like a flower conjured in the sky, originally without any attachment to hold onto at all; simply this one falseness, with no further root or thread to trace — and so both doubts, this earlier one and this present one, are resolved together. This closes the second section, this total question about every destiny.

Sūtra: "World-Honored One: the bhikṣuṇī Ratnakamalā, who held the bodhisattva precepts yet secretly engaged in sexual misconduct, falsely claiming that such conduct was neither killing nor stealing and so carried no karmic retribution at all — no sooner had she spoken these words than a great fierce fire arose first from her own female organ, and then burned joint by joint through her entire body, until she fell into the unrelenting hell. So too King Virūḍhaka, and the monk Sunakṣatra: Virūḍhaka, for slaughtering the Gautama clan, and Sunakṣatra, for falsely claiming that all phenomena are empty, both sank, in their very living bodies, into the Avīci hell. Are these hells, then, fixed in some definite location? Or does each being's own karma arise naturally, each receiving it individually and privately? I beg only that you, in your great compassion, open the eyes of the untaught, so that all beings who keep the precepts may hear this settled teaching, and, joyfully upholding it, remain scrupulously pure and free of transgression."

Commentary: This is the retribution of lust — citing an actual event to raise this question about the retribution of hell. Addressing him as World-Honored One: the bhikṣuṇī Ratnakamalā upheld the great bodhisattva precepts, and yet secretly engaged in sexual misconduct — already breaking these precepts — and, further, spoke falsely, slandering the precepts themselves, claiming that this misconduct was not the same as killing a life, nor comparable to robbing another of their wealth, but merely a matter of mutual pleasure between two consenting parties, carrying no karma in the present and no fruit of retribution to come either. This private misconduct was itself a breaking of the precepts, and this false claim of no retribution was itself a breaking of correct view; violating the moral law and misleading others, this offense grew all the more severe — and so she came to suffer both the flower-retribution and the fruit-retribution at once, together, in a single moment. "No sooner had she spoken these words than a great fierce fire arose first from her own female organ" — sexual desire belongs to the element of fire, and so her own female organ came to feel this great fierce fire; the force of this karma only intensifying, its retribution allowing for no delay at all. "Then burned joint by joint through her entire body" — as the chapter on Ucchuṣma puts it: it is said that a person given greatly to lust becomes a mass of fierce fire — truly, this is no exaggeration! This flower-retribution alone was insufficient to repay this offense, and so, at the end of her life, her own consciousness fell straight into hell, there to receive this fruit-retribution as well. As the Detailed Commentary puts it: the later text speaks of nine parts emotion to one part thought sinking down through the fire-wheel, the body entering this place where wind and fire cross, lighter cases reborn in one of the interrupted hells, heavier cases reborn in the unrelenting hell itself. Now, this bhikṣuṇī Ratnakamalā, though she claimed that sexual misconduct carried no retribution, still believed that killing and stealing did carry retribution — and this seems to be exactly this case of nine parts emotion to one part thought, her retribution properly belonging to this place where wind and fire cross. Yet, because her breaking of the precepts misled others as well, her offense was of the very gravest kind, and so it is said that she fell into the unrelenting hell itself.

King Virūḍhaka and the monk Sunakṣatra — Virūḍhaka's retribution belongs to anger, and Sunakṣatra's belongs to delusion, wrong view — these, too, are actual events cited to raise this question about the retribution of hell. Together with Ratnakamalā's own secret misconduct above, altogether, lust, anger, and delusion, these three, are the very cause of falling into hell. Living in this world, one cannot afford not to be careful of these; and, all the more, one who cultivates practice ought to guard against them with the utmost severity.

King Virūḍhaka was the crown prince of King Prasenajit, and slaughtered the Gautama clan in order to avenge a single word of insult. This was simply because his own anger grew too strong, and his killing went too far, and so he came to fall into hell. When King Virūḍhaka was young, he went to pay respects to his mother's own family; the Śākyas, at that time, had just built a new lecture hall for the Buddha, magnificently adorned, with a Dharma-seat set up within it — the Buddha not yet having taken his seat there — and yet the young Virūḍhaka went ahead and sat upon it himself. The Śākyas rebuked him, saying: "You, born of a slave-girl — what merit have you, that you would dare sit in this place?" — and drove him out at once. Once outside, the young prince said to his attendant, Bad-Bitterness: "The Śākya clan has insulted me to this degree; once I inherit the throne, you must remind me of this, and I will raise an army to attack them." As for this "born of a slave-girl": King Prasenajit had once sought marriage with the Śākya clan; had they refused him, he feared they might harbor a grudge and attack him; yet, were they to agree, this too was not truly what they wished — and so, in the end, they gave him a servant-girl, raised as though she were a Śākya daughter, and married her to him instead. The prince was born of her, and it was on this occasion that he came to visit his mother's own family. Once he had grown to maturity, he deposed his own father and set himself upon the throne; Bad-Bitterness then came to remind the great king: do you still remember that single word of insult from the Śākya clan? And so he raised an army and attacked the Śākyas.

Maudgalyāyana asked the Buddha to rescue them, yet the Buddha remained silent. Maudgalyāyana then gathered a number of the Śākyas into his own begging-bowl and carried them up into the heavens. Once the great army arrived, they gave themselves over entirely to slaughter. King Virūḍhaka also wished to harm the Buddha; entering to see the Buddha, though, his own hair stood on end with dread. The Buddha then predicted that, after seven days, he would fall into hell. The king, afraid, led his army and set out by boat upon the sea; when the appointed time arrived, fire spontaneously arose from within the water and consumed him entirely. Once the great army had withdrawn, Maudgalyāyana came before the Buddha and said: "At that time, I asked the Buddha to rescue them; because the Buddha remained silent, I gathered them into my own bowl and sent them up to the heavens for safety — and now I have brought them back down again." The Buddha said to him: "Fixed karma cannot be altered — this was never within the power of your own spiritual might to prevent!" Opening the bowl to look inside, they found that all of them had already turned to blood and water.

As for the more distant cause of all this: the Buddha once said that, long ago, in the city of Rājagṛha, there was a pond full of fish; when a drought came and the pond's water dried up, people went into the pond to catch the fish. Among them was a king of the fish, named Fu, and a second named Many-Tongues, hiding themselves in the mud; once the pond's water had dried up, the mud, too, began to stir, and so they, too, were caught. At that time, there was a small boy who took a bamboo pole and playfully struck the fish-king's own head three times. At that time, the one catching the fish was the very same as these Śākyas today; the fish-king Fu was King Virūḍhaka himself; the fish Many-Tongues was Bad-Bitterness himself; and the small boy was the Buddha himself, in that same former life. This account is given in full in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra and in the Sūtra of King Virūḍhaka.

Sunakṣatra is also called Sunakṣatra. As the Āgama states: he was the son of the Buddha's own paternal cousin, or, some say, the son of Devadatta. As the Nirvāṇa Sūtra states: the monk Sunakṣatra drew close to evil companions, lost the fourth dhyāna, and gave rise to evil, deviant views, saying: there is no buddha, and no nirvāṇa; though the Tathāgata has, indeed, expounded the Dharma for my sake, I hold, in truth, that there is no cause and effect at all — this being exactly this same false claim that all phenomena are empty.

As the Detailed Commentary puts it: Virūḍhaka's own rage and slaughter already constitutes a most grave offense, and, further, those he killed were no ordinary people, but the very clan of Gautama himself. Sunakṣatra's false claim that all phenomena are empty is itself this same deviant delusion; and, should he further have stolen offerings for his own support, this would compound it with theft as well. The offenses of these two men, both alike, are of the very gravest kind — and so both, in their very living bodies, fell into hell. The later text states: with pure emotion alone, one sinks straight down, into the Avīci hell — and, should there also be, within that sinking mind, slander of the Great Vehicle, violation of the Buddha's own precepts, false preaching of the Dharma for gain, empty consumption of offerings made in good faith, or unearned acceptance of reverence, one is reborn again and again in Avīci hells throughout the ten directions. Now, Virūḍhaka's own unrestrained slaughter, and Sunakṣatra's own indulgence in falsehood, both seem to be cases of pure emotion, and so both, rightly, sink into the Avīci hell. And, further, Virūḍhaka's own wanton killing, even wishing greatly to harm the Buddha himself, and Sunakṣatra's own false preaching of the Dharma, emptily consuming offerings made in good faith, may, perhaps, also mean that both are reborn again and again in Avīci hells throughout the ten directions as well.

Yet that they fell into hell in their very living bodies is something everyone present alike witnessed. Once fallen there, they are subject in full to these five kinds of uninterrupted retribution: first, uninterrupted in destination and fruit — falling there without passing through any intermediate state at all. Second, uninterrupted in form — whether a single being or many beings, the hell-city is, alike, filled entirely. Third, uninterrupted in instruments of suffering — once one kind of instrument of torment has been used to its full extent, it is at once exchanged for another. Fourth, uninterrupted in life — within a single day, ten thousand births and ten thousand deaths; once one has died from this suffering, a skillful wind blows at once, and one is restored to life again, exactly as before. Fifth, uninterrupted across kalpas — a full great kalpa spent entirely within this one hell; once that kalpa is exhausted, one is sent to be reborn within another hell elsewhere, and, should this offense still not be exhausted after another full kalpa there, one is transferred to yet another elsewhere again.

May I dare to ask the World-Honored One: looking at these three people described above, do these hells possess some single, fixed location — so that, though the karma created by each differs, all alike return to this same place, receiving this same retribution together? Or does each unfold entirely on its own, this karma each gives rise to differing from the next, each receiving its own retribution individually and privately? This question carries two meanings: the first asks about differing karma yet a shared retribution; the latter asks about differing karma and a differing retribution as well — and so, this remaining unclear, this doubt arises. "I beg only that you, in your great compassion, open the eyes of the untaught" — "the untaught," referring to himself, this one directly addressed, ashamed of lacking great wisdom, resembling, in this, an untaught child, earnestly seeking to be opened to correct knowledge and correct view — "so that all beings who keep the precepts may hear this settled teaching," without any longer being caught in hesitation, naturally giving rise to a joyful mind, their own bodies reverently upholding it, all the more careful and pure in their practice, free of any transgression of the precepts at all. This closes the first section, Ānanda's own request.

Sūtra: "The Buddha said to Ānanda: 'Excellent, this question! It will keep beings from falling into wrong views. Listen carefully now, and I will explain.'"

Commentary: The Buddha telling Ānanda that this question is excellent — since this question, so fitting to the matter at hand, arising from the private misconduct, the wrathful and unrestrained slaughter, and the foolish, deluded false speech described above — lust, anger, and delusion, these three, all belong to deviant views — is now, because of this very question, brought fully to light, so that all sentient beings everywhere may be kept from falling into deviant views, and all alike return to the correct path — is this not, indeed, a cause for the deepest joy? "Listen carefully now, with full sincerity, and I will explain for you, distinguishing this matter of hell, its shared and its individual meaning, in full." This closes the first section, praising this question and granting to explain it.

Sūtra: "The Buddha said to Ānanda: 'Excellent, this question! It will keep beings from falling into wrong views. Listen carefully now, and I will explain. Ānanda, all beings are, in their true nature, originally pure — it is only because of false seeing that false habitual tendency arises, and from this, an internal tendency and an external tendency divide off from one another.'"

Commentary: This makes plain that true suchness, according with conditions, gives rise to falseness — this falseness being these seven destinies. Ānanda, these beings of all seven destinies, every sentient being, alike, possesses this same treasury-substance — this being truly, originally, their own inherent true suchness, buddha-nature, this pure, wondrous mind, free of every mixed defilement. Because of that single thought stirring falsely, this false seeing comes to be formed — that is, this present manifestation of ignorance — and so this false habitual tendency is given rise to; that is, this seed of mixed defilement, this habitual seed of ignorance taken as cause, bringing about these seven destinies, this falseness. This is exactly what you doubted: that the substance of buddhahood is real and genuine — how, then, could there be any falseness at all? — precisely this. What follows expounds at length this same falseness, disclosing this teaching on emotion and thought. Though these differ in terms of rising or falling, superior or inferior, in every case they amount to nothing but this same false habitual tendency. Sūtra: "'The internal tendency is what belongs within a being itself: because of every kind of attached love, false emotion arises, and once this emotion accumulates without rest, it gives rise to a kind of "water of love."'"

Commentary: The first sentence marks out this internal tendency's own realm — exactly what belongs within a being's own body. "Because of every kind of attached love" below reveals this internal tendency's own mind: because of these attachments and this love that arise upon this internal realm, this attached love is exactly this same emotion. As is commonly said in the world, joy, anger, grief, happiness, love, hatred, and desire — these are called the seven emotions, love being one among them; yet, altogether, all seven belong to love. How Emotion and Thought Decide a Destiny at the Moment of Death 辛二 約臨終別示升墜

〖經〗阿難！一切世間，生死相續，生從順習，死從變流，臨命終時，未捨煖觸，一生善惡，俱時頓現，死逆生順，二習相交。

〖講〗此示所感之果。意謂諸趣不同，不出情想二因，由內外情想輕重，故有昇沉差別也。阿難！一切有情，正報世間，生來死去，相續不斷，如旋火輪，未有休息。生從順習，死從變流者：一切眾生，莫不貪生惡死。生則從其隨順習氣，而造善惡等業；死則從其遷變流轉，而受異類等身。故臨命將終之時，六識皆已不行，而八識尚未離體，故未捨煖觸。若八識離體，煖相自盡，壽命已終。今則將死未死，現陰欲謝，中陰未生；當此之時，正在畏死求生之際，謂以死為逆，而欲避之；以生為順，而欲求之；則順逆二習，交相並發。則一生所作，一切善惡之業，隨其情想輕重，俱時感變而頓現焉。末二句，交光法師云：當在煖觸之下為順，恐是謄譯之訛耳。

〖經〗《華嚴經》云：譬如有人，將欲命終，見隨其業，所受報相。行惡業者，即現地獄、餓鬼、畜生，所有一切眾苦境界。作善業者，即現諸天宮殿，天眾綵女，種種衣服，具足莊嚴，悉皆妙好。身雖未死，而由業力，見如是事。故知地獄天堂，本無定處；身雖未死，唯心妄見。初臨終相現竟。

〖講〗其中者：即上四類之中，若能發善願，及存善心，願護持我佛法。或護禁戒，隨持戒人：如戒壇中，護戒善神之類；或護神咒，隨持咒者：如本經中，百靈護咒之類；或護禪定，及修禪之者，保綏（安也）法忍，如禪堂中，護靜善神之類；參禪遇魔，則所得法，不能忍可，今則保而安之。是等則親住如來法座之下。此明帶邪情，而有善願者。故八部之眾，皆以鬼神身，而得親近於佛，蒙佛授記者也。初昇而不墜竟。

〖經〗情想均等，不飛不墜，生於人間，想明斯聰，情幽斯鈍。

〖講〗此五情五想正相平等。不飛不墜者：由有五情，不能飛升，故上不及天仙鬼神；由有五想，不至沉墜，故下不在地獄三途。參合兩楹，故得生人世之間。想明斯聰，情幽斯鈍者：謂於總報，由具五想，想體明達，所以有聰利；如覺觀知識，推度事理，勝彼下趣也。由具五情，情體幽閉，所以有此暗鈍；如不有神通，不能飛舉，劣彼上趣也。二不升不墜竟。

〖經〗情多想少，流入橫生，重為毛群，輕為羽族。

〖講〗此墜畜生。情愛多，而勝想少者，是不均等。情多一分為六情，想少一分為四想，流入橫生。然橫生復有二類：若身之重者，則為毛群走獸，地行之類；若身之輕者，則為羽族，飛禽空行之類。

〖經〗七情三想，沈下水輪，生於火際，受氣猛火，身為餓鬼，常被焚燒。水能害己，無食無飲，經百千劫。

〖講〗此為餓鬼。七情三想，則沉於水輪之下，生於火輪之際，受猛火之氣分，結氣成形。身為餓鬼常被焚燒者：因受氣猛火之故。水能害己者：因見水變火之故，水反能加害於己也。此乃業力使然，隨識遷變，如天人見水是玻璃；魚龍見水是窟宅；人見水是水；餓鬼見水是火。由是無飲無食，經百千劫，常受飢虛之苦。

〖經〗九情一想，下洞火輪，身入風火，二交過地，輕生有間，重生無間，二種地獄。

〖講〗此為地獄。九情一想者：略輕取重，因重知輕故，文中少八情二想。想少於前，益不能飛，情多於前，愈沉於下。前在火際，今又透過火際，下洞火輪，以洞即透過義。身入風火，二交過地者：身即墜獄之身，入此風輪、火輪，二交過之地。此地有間無間，俱在其中，如八情二想，罪之輕者，生於有間；九情一想，罪之重者，生於無間；此二種地獄。然斯無間，但對在獄受苦言之，非對下之阿鼻地獄也。以既惟九情，罪惡未至極重；猶有一想，善根未至盡斷故。

〖經〗純情即沉入阿鼻地獄。若沉心中，有謗大乘，毀佛禁戒，誑妄說法，虛貪信施，濫膺恭敬，五逆十重，更生十方，阿鼻地獄。

〖講〗純情無想，唯墜不升，故臨命終時，即沉入阿鼻地獄。梵語阿鼻，此云無間，與前有異，乃罪惡極重者，所墜之處也。依《成論》略明，五種無間：一、趣果無間，捨身即生彼故。二、受苦無間，中無樂故。三、經時無間，定一劫故。四、命無間，中無絕故。五、形無間，身形縱廣八萬由旬，一人多人，皆遍滿故。若沉心中者：即純情心中，以及兼罪。或有毀謗大乘，如大慢婆羅門等；毀犯禁戒，如寶蓮香等；誑妄說法，如善星比丘等。以至無實行，而虛貪信施之資財。無實德，而濫膺四眾之恭敬，甚而五逆十重，無業不造，本獄不足以償，故云更生十方阿鼻地獄。

〖經〗《法華經》云：其人命終，入阿鼻地獄，具足一劫；劫盡更生十方世界阿鼻地獄；如是輾轉，至無數劫。《地藏經》云：墮無間獄，求出無期，此界壞時，寄生他界，皆此義也。二約臨終別示升墜竟。

〖經〗循造惡業，雖則自招，眾同分中，兼有元地。

〖講〗此結答前問。謂此諸地獄，為有定處，為復自然，彼彼發業，各各私受？今答云：一切眾生，循其自己所造惡業，雖則自業所感，還自來受；猶儒書所云：天作業猶可違，自作業不可活也。而眾所感，同分獄中，兼有本元，一定處所，此結答別業同報，不可謂於我無分。如地獄既爾，諸趣皆然。初略示情想為升墜根由竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: "Ānanda, the continuity of birth and death throughout the world is such that birth follows the stream of habitual tendency, and death is a reversal of that same stream. At the moment of death, before the body's last warmth has faded, an entire lifetime's good and evil appears all at once, in a single instant. Death runs against the ordinary current of life, and life runs with it — and it is exactly at this crossing point that these two habitual tendencies, of following and resisting, meet and interact."

Commentary: This shows the fruit felt at this moment, making plain that every destiny, however different, amounts to no more than these two causes, emotion and thought — and that it is because of this internal and external emotion and thought, heavier or lighter, that these differences of rising and sinking come to be. Ānanda, every sentient being, throughout this direct retribution of the world, is born and dies, dies and is born, in unbroken continuity, exactly like a wheel of fire turning round, never once resting. "Birth follows the stream of habitual tendency, and death is a reversal of that same stream" means that every sentient being, without exception, clings to life and abhors death: in birth, one follows this same habitual tendency, according with it, creating karma both good and evil; in death, one undergoes this same shifting and turning, taking on some other kind of body. And so, at the very moment life is about to end, the six consciousnesses have already ceased to function, yet the eighth consciousness has not yet departed the body — hence, "before the body's last warmth has faded." Were the eighth consciousness to depart the body, this warmth would, of itself, be entirely spent, and life would already have ended. Now, though, one is about to die and yet has not yet died, the present aggregate-body about to dissolve, the intermediate-state body not yet arisen; and, exactly at this moment, one stands precisely at the point of dreading death and seeking life — regarding death as something to be resisted, and wishing to avoid it; regarding life as something to be followed, and wishing to seek it — and so these two habitual tendencies, of following and of resisting, arise together, crossing one another. And so this entire lifetime's own deeds, every karma both good and evil, according to the weight of one's own emotion and thought, are all, together, felt and transformed, appearing all at once, suddenly, before one. As for the final two clauses: Master Jiaoguang holds that these ought properly to come just beneath "before the body's last warmth has faded" — this present ordering, he suspects, being simply an error introduced in copying and translation.

Sūtra: "As the Avataṃsaka Sūtra puts it: it is like a person about to die, who sees, following their own karma, the very mark of retribution they are to receive. One who has done evil karma sees, at once, hell, hungry ghosts, animals, every realm of suffering there is; one who has done good karma sees, at once, the palaces of the heavens, the heavenly host and its beautiful attendants, every kind of clothing, complete with every adornment, all alike wondrous and fine. Though the body has not yet died, through the power of this karma, one sees things exactly this way. One thus knows that hell and heaven, at root, possess no fixed location at all; though the body has not yet died, this is seen through mind alone, falsely. This closes the first section, the marks that appear at the moment of death."

Sūtra: "One who is pure thought takes flight, and is certain to be born in the heavens above — and if that rising mind is joined with merit, with wisdom, and with a pure vow as well, the mind opens of itself and the buddhas of the ten directions appear, so that one is reborn in any pure land according to one's own vow."

Commentary: "Thought" here means a settled mind contemplating and visualizing, not a scattered, disordered thought. This differs from that same thought spoken of in egg-birth: there, the reference was to this same false, defiled thought at the moment of receiving birth; here, the reference is to this same habitual thought of what is superior and wondrous, cultivated throughout one's own lifetime. Pure thought, this superior, wondrous realm, the spirit roaming beyond the ordinary, and so at once able to take flight, never falling — this must mean being born in the heavens above, taking up residence there. What follows, on pure emotion, is confined only to the very depths of Avīci; this pure thought, though, encompasses every heaven of all three realms — one thus sees that every heaven of these three realms is brought about entirely through this same wholesome karma of a thinking mind.

Should this pure thought within a rising mind be joined, in one's ordinary life, with the cultivation of both merit and wisdom — such as making offerings to the Buddha as meritorious karma, hearing the Dharma as wisdom-karma — and joined, too, with a pure vow — still wishing constantly to follow the Buddha in study, hoping to receive his own prophecy, and further wishing to be reborn there, vowing to behold the Buddha directly. As a rule, when life is about to end, one's own consciousness grows dim, and, gazing toward the ten directions, one sees only something like a gathered mass of ink, dim and obscure, not knowing where to go. Now, though, through this pure thought, together with the power of this merit and wisdom, and the power of this pure vow, the ground of mind naturally opens through, and one beholds the buddhas of the ten directions, every pure land there is — such as Amitābha's own land of bliss in the west, or Bhaiṣajyaguru's own lapis-lazuli land in the east; every direction, all appearing at once, superior and inferior each distinguished — and so one is thereby able to be reborn according to one's own vow. One thus sees that such beings, too, belong to this same pure thought, yet, joined further with merit, wisdom, and the power of vow, thereby surpass every heaven entirely.

Sūtra: "Where there is little emotion and mostly thought, one rises but not far, becoming a flying immortal, a powerful ghost-king, a flying yakṣa, or an earth-walking rākṣasa, roaming freely through the four heavens without obstruction —"

Commentary: Next, this properly discusses this mixed thought. "Little emotion and mostly thought" means that this superior thought is not entirely pure, mixed with a small trace of subtle emotion — and so, though able to rise lightly, this does not reach very far, since, vertically, it does not surpass the four heavens, and, horizontally, it does not go beyond the ring of iron mountains that bounds this world.

"Becomes a flying immortal" and what follows ought properly to be divided into four classes: this first ought to correspond to nine parts thought and one part emotion, as with the class of flying immortals described later. "A powerful ghost-king" ought to correspond to eight parts thought and two parts emotion, as with the class of mountain deities. "A flying yakṣa" ought to correspond to seven parts thought and three parts emotion, as with the class of ghost-commanders. "An earth-walking rākṣasa" ought to correspond to six parts thought and four parts emotion, as with the class of mountain and wilderness spirits. Yet this path of ghosts and spirits contains both the good and the evil: the evil are devoted solely to bringing calamity, and belong properly to the class of ghosts; the good are able, as well, to bring blessing, and extend also into the path of immortals — these powerful ghosts and spirits, spoken of here in terms of those able to bring blessing — all alike able to fly and soar, roaming through the four heavens, coming and going entirely without obstruction.

Sūtra: "— and among these, any who hold a good vow and good intention to protect this Dharma of mine, whether guarding the precepts and accompanying those who keep them, or guarding mantras and accompanying those who hold them, or guarding meditative concentration and preserving the forbearance of the Dharma, dwell in close attendance right beneath the Tathāgata's own seat."

Commentary: "Among these" refers to among these four classes above: should any give rise to a good vow and hold a good mind, wishing to protect and uphold the Buddha-dharma. "Or guarding the precepts, accompanying those who keep them" — such as the good spirits who guard the precepts, present at the ordination platform; "or guarding mantras, accompanying those who hold them" — such as the hundred numinous beings in this present sūtra who guard this mantra; "or guarding meditative concentration," and accompanying those who cultivate meditation, preserving and settling this forbearance of the Dharma — such as the good spirits who guard stillness, present within the meditation hall; should one, in meeting demonic obstruction while sitting in meditation, find the Dharma one has attained unable to be confirmed and accepted, these spirits now protect and settle this. Such as these dwell in close attendance right beneath the Tathāgata's own Dharma-seat. This makes plain those who carry some trace of deviant emotion, yet still hold a good vow. And so the assembly of the eight kinds of divine beings, though possessing a ghost or spirit body, are able to draw close to the Buddha, and receive the Buddha's own prophecy. This closes the first section, rising without falling.

Sūtra: "Where emotion and thought are equal, one neither rises nor falls, and is born among human beings — clarity of thought bringing intelligence, and murkiness of emotion bringing dullness."

Commentary: This is exactly five parts emotion and five parts thought, precisely equal to one another. Neither rising nor falling: because of these five parts emotion, one is unable to rise upward, and so does not reach as far as the immortals, ghosts, and spirits above; and because of these five parts thought, one does not sink downward either, and so does not fall among hell and the other two lower destinies below. Standing balanced between these two pillars, one thereby comes to be born amid this human world.

"Clarity of thought bringing intelligence, and murkiness of emotion bringing dullness" speaks of this total retribution: because of possessing these five parts thought, this substance of thought is clear and penetrating, and so one has this same sharp intelligence — such as awareness, reflection, and understanding, able to reason out both principle and affairs, superior to those of the lower destinies. And because of possessing these five parts emotion, this substance of emotion is murky and shut in, and so one has this same dull obscurity — such as lacking any spiritual power, unable to take flight, inferior to those of the higher destinies. This closes the second section, neither rising nor falling.

Sūtra: "Where there is much emotion and little thought, one flows down into the realm of creatures born sideways — heavier, becoming one of the furred kind; lighter, one of the feathered kind."

Commentary: This is falling into the animal realm. Attached emotion is much, and superior thought is little — this is imbalance. Emotion, the greater portion, makes six parts emotion; thought, the lesser portion, makes four parts thought — flowing down into this realm of creatures born sideways. Yet these creatures born sideways further divide into two classes: should the body be heavier, one becomes one of the furred kind, a running beast, one of those that walk the earth; should the body be lighter, one becomes one of the feathered kind, a bird, one of those that fly through the sky.

Sūtra: "Where there are seven parts emotion to three parts thought, one sinks down through the water-wheel, reborn at the very edge of fire, taking on the nature of fierce flame, the body becoming that of a hungry ghost, forever scorched by burning, harmed even by water, without food or drink for a hundred thousand kalpas."

Commentary: This is becoming a hungry ghost. Seven parts emotion, three parts thought, sinks down beneath the water-wheel, is born at the very edge of the fire-wheel, receives this same portion of fierce fire, this energy congealing into form. "The body becomes that of a hungry ghost, forever scorched by burning" is because of receiving this portion of fierce fire. "Harmed even by water" is because water itself is seen transformed into fire — water, in turn, becoming able to harm oneself. This is simply how karmic power brings this about, shifting according to one's own consciousness: just as a heavenly being sees water as crystal, and a fish or nāga sees water as its own dwelling-cave, and a human sees water as water, so a hungry ghost sees water as fire. And so, without food or drink, across a hundred thousand kalpas, one constantly undergoes this suffering of hunger and thirst.

Sūtra: "Where there are nine parts emotion to one part thought, one sinks down through the fire-wheel entirely, the body passing through the place where wind and fire cross — lighter, into one of the interrupted hells; heavier, into the unrelenting hell itself."

Commentary: This is falling into hell. "Nine parts emotion, one part thought" briefly takes the lighter to indicate the heavier, since, knowing the heavier, the lighter is known as well — the text here omitting the case of eight parts emotion, two parts thought. Thought is now less than before, and so one is all the more unable to rise; emotion is now more than before, and so one sinks all the further down. Before, one was at the very edge of fire; now, one passes through even this edge of fire, sinking down through the fire-wheel entirely — "sinking through" here carrying the sense of passing all the way through it. "The body enters this place where wind and fire cross" means that this same body, falling into hell, enters this place where the wind-wheel and the fire-wheel cross one another. Within this place, both the interrupted and the unrelenting exist together: as with eight parts emotion, two parts thought, this being the lighter offense, one is born in one of the interrupted hells; and as with nine parts emotion, one part thought, this being the heavier offense, one is born in the unrelenting hell itself — these being these two kinds of hell. Yet this "unrelenting" here speaks only in terms of undergoing suffering within hell itself, not in contrast to that Avīci hell spoken of below — since, possessing only these nine parts emotion, this offense has not yet reached its very gravest extreme; and, still possessing this one part thought, this root of goodness has not yet been entirely severed either.

Sūtra: "And where there is pure emotion alone, one sinks straight down into the Avīci hell — and if, within that sinking mind, there is also slander of the Great Vehicle, violation of the Buddha's own precepts, false preaching of the Dharma for gain, empty consumption of offerings made in good faith, or unearned acceptance of reverence, along with any of the five heinous or ten grave offenses, one is reborn again and again in Avīci hells throughout the ten directions."

Commentary: Pure emotion, without any thought at all, only falling, never rising, and so, at the very moment life comes to an end, one sinks straight down into the Avīci hell. The Sanskrit word Avīci is here translated "without interval" — differing from what came before — being exactly that place into which those of the very gravest offense fall. According to the Abhidharma-samuccaya, this is briefly explained through five kinds of "without interval": first, without interval in destination and fruit, since one takes on this body at once, without passing through any intermediate state; second, without interval in receiving suffering, since there is no joy in between at all; third, without interval across time, since a full kalpa is fixed and unbroken; fourth, without interval in life, since it does not cease in between; and fifth, without interval in form, whether one being or many beings, the body's own form filling this place completely, spreading across eighty thousand yojanas, in every case.

"Within that sinking mind" refers exactly to this mind of pure emotion, together with its accompanying offenses. There may be slander of the Great Vehicle, as with the greatly arrogant brāhmaṇa and others; violation of the precepts, as with Ratnakamalā and others; false preaching of the Dharma, as with the monk Sunakṣatra and others; and further, without any true practice at all, emptily consuming the wealth and resources offered in faith; without any true virtue at all, unjustly accepting the reverence of the fourfold assembly; and, further still, the five heinous and ten grave offenses, with no karma left uncommitted — such that this one original hell is insufficient to repay it, and so it is said: "reborn again and again in Avīci hells throughout the ten directions."

Sūtra: "As the Lotus Sūtra puts it: when such a person's life ends, they enter the Avīci hell, remaining there a full kalpa; once that kalpa is exhausted, they are reborn once more in the Avīci hell of some other world of the ten directions — turning, in this way, from one to the next, across kalpas beyond all counting. As the Sūtra of Kṣitigarbha puts it: falling into the unrelenting hell, there is no set time by which one may hope to emerge; and, when this present world itself is destroyed, one is sent to be reborn within some other world instead — both alike carrying this same meaning. This closes the second section, showing, specifically in terms of the moment of death, this rising and falling."

Sūtra: "Following whatever evil karma one has created, though this is indeed self-invited, within this shared portion of a common destiny, there is, at the same time, this same original ground."

Commentary: This concludes the answer to this earlier question. That is, do these hells possess some fixed location, or does each unfold entirely naturally, this karma each gives rise to differing from the next, each receiving its own retribution individually and privately? The answer now given is this: every sentient being, following whatever evil karma they themselves have created, though this is indeed brought about and felt through their own karma, still comes back to themselves to be received — exactly as the Confucian classics put it: calamity brought about by heaven may still be escaped, but calamity brought about by oneself cannot be survived at all. And yet, what is felt in common by the many, within this shared hell of common destiny, is joined, at the same time, to this same original, fixed location — this concluding the answer that, though karma differs from one being to the next, its retribution is shared in common, and one cannot say that this is a matter with no part in oneself at all. As it is with hell, so it is, alike, with every destiny. This closes the first section, briefly showing emotion and thought as the very root and cause of rising and falling.

〖經〗阿難！此等皆是，彼諸眾生，自業所感，造十習因，受六交報。

〖講〗上科略示情想，為升墜根由，此科詳示升墜，有因果差別。故呼阿難，而告之曰：此等因情想所感升墜之報，皆是彼諸眾生，自業之所感招，設無自業，雖本有地獄，誰能驅之入哉？此屬躡前，下乃標後，造十習之業因，受六交之果報。此雖因果各言，義實互具，以有因必有果，而果不離因也。初躡前標後竟。

〖經〗云何十因？阿難！一者淫習交接，發於相磨，研磨不休，如是故有大猛火光，於中發動。如人以手，自相摩觸煖相現前。二習相然，故有鐵床、銅柱諸事。是故十方，一切如來，色目行淫，同名慾火。菩薩見欲，如避火坑。

〖講〗首句總徵，寄居於此。阿難！一者淫習下，則別釋十因，以明感招十習；謂淫、貪、慢、瞋、詐、誑、怨、見、枉、訟。既云為習，即是業因。下之六報，即屬苦果。而貪慢等，仍帶惑名，應是兼惑業，故云習也。習有種習、現習，種習為種子屬惑，現習為現行屬業，由種子發生現行，故作諸業。

阿難！一者，淫習交接者：淫為萬惡之首，生死之根；一切眾生，皆以淫欲，而正性命，故首列焉。宿世淫欲熾盛，猶有餘習，發為現行，必須內根外境，男女交接，方得成業。發於相磨者：謂染心會合，彼此互相研磨，深求欲樂；研磨不休，則精血由是耗散。積淫成火，故有大猛火光，於自心中發動，預現地獄相也。則死後，招引業火，自可想而知矣。如人以手，自相摩觸，煖相現前者：此以例驗知，如有人以兩手掌，無故自相摩觸，由是故有煖相現前者，是也。

二習相然，故有鐵床，銅柱諸事。二習，謂種習現習之二，猶乾柴遇火，種習猶存，現習重增，互相熾然。由此命終，故感鐵床銅柱諸事。蓋前之大猛火光發動，心中之獄相初萌，此之鐵床銅柱，心中之業果成就矣。據觀佛三昧經云：銅柱地獄者，有一銅柱，狀如火山，高六百由旬，下有猛火，火上鐵床，上有刀輪，間有鐵嘴蟲鳥。有滅倫傷化，非時非處，犯不淨行者，命終則生銅柱之頂，猛火燄熾，焚燒其身，驚怖下視，見鐵床上，有端正女，若是女人，見端正男；心生愛著，從銅柱下，至鐵床上，男女二根，俱時火起，有鐵嘴蟲，從男女根入，自男女根出，一日一夜，九百億生，九百億死者是也。觀此則知，淫報之苦，悉由心現也。

是故十方，一切如來，色目行淫，同名慾火。菩薩見欲，如避火坑。是淫習為害之故，十方一切如來；向人形容謂之色，自己觀察謂之目，異口同音，同名慾火。淫慾火害，甚於猛火，遭之者必至喪身失命。菩薩見此淫欲之境，遠離回避，猶如火坑相似。《寶鏡疏》云：以故，諸佛同訶，審宜刻骨；菩薩共避，寧不驚心！嗚乎！欲之為火，其毒若此，惟願有智士女，幸勿自效撲燈蛾也。初淫習竟。

〖講〗貪，乃六種根本煩惱之一，本屬於惑，今云習者，以前生多貪，猶有種習，發為現行，重加現習，互相交計，彼此籌算方得成業。發於相吸者：謂互相吸取，以濟己私。吸攬不止者：由於貪得無厭，追求不息，如是故有積寒堅冰，於中凍冽。貪心屬水，吸取屬風，水若遇風，必至積寒，結為堅冰，於自心中，預現凍冽之相矣。如人以口，吸縮風氣，有冷觸生者：此以例驗知，如人以口空張，吸縮風氣，尚有冷觸發生者是也。

二習相陵，故有吒吒、波波、羅羅、青赤白蓮、寒冰等事。二習，謂種習猶存，現習重增，貪吸更甚，故曰相陵。由此命終，神識故感吒吒、波波、羅羅、青、赤、白蓮、寒冰等事﹒此吒吒、波波、羅羅、皆獄中寒逼罪人之苦聲；青、赤、白蓮，皆獄中凍冽罪人之形色；此乃八寒地獄之前六。準餘經，更有阿浮他，謂凍冽成瘡；泥羅浮他，謂肢節脫落，是八寒後二；以寒冰等事該之。前積堅冰，於中凍冽，心中之獄相初萌；此則寒逼之聲色現前，是業果成就矣。

是故十方，一切如來，色目多求，同名貪水。菩薩見貪，如避瘴海。是貪習為害之故，十方一切如來，向人形容謂之色，自己觀察謂之目，異口同音，此貪吸多求，同名貪水。謂貪之為害，勝於毒水，飲之者，必至腐腸爛冑。菩薩見此貪愛之境，莫不遠離，如避瘴海。瘴者癘氣也，凡山海熱處即有之，人若不知，誤吸其氣，便成瘧疾，遂至殞命也。二貪習竟。

〖經〗三者，慢習交陵，發於相恃，馳流不息。如是故有，騰逸奔波。積波為水，如人口舌，自相綿味，因而水發。二習相鼓，故有血河、灰河、熱砂、毒海、融銅、灌吞諸事。是故十方，一切如來，色目我慢，名飲癡水。菩薩見慢，如避巨溺。

〖講〗慢，亦六種根本煩惱之一，本屬於惑，今云習者，以前世多慢，猶有種習，發為現行，重加現習，互相交陵；彼此交相陵越，方得成業。發於相恃者：謂自恃豪姓大貴，有勢多財等，尊己卑他，其性高舉，馳心上流，不知止息。然我慢屬山，馳流屬水。山峙水馳，必致奔騰。如是故有，騰躍縱逸，奔馳之波，於自心中，預現積波為水之相矣！如人口舌，自相綿味，因而水發者：此以例驗知，如人以口中之舌，舐於上齶，綿絞其舌上之味，因而遂有口水發生是也。

二習相鼓，故有血河、灰河、熱砂、毒海、融銅、灌吞諸事。二習，謂種習猶存，現習重增，慢流益甚，故曰相鼓，謂鼓盪陵越也；由此命終，故感血河等報。《灌頂疏》云：獄有兩山，罪人走入，兩山忽合，如磨蓋壓，血肉遍流，如大河海，血水涌沸，男女萬數，出沒其中。灰河者：經律異相云：灰河地獄，縱廣深淺，五百由旬，灰湯涌沸，罪人入河，鐵刺刺身，膿血流出，痛苦萬狀。熱砂者：灌頂云：即黑砂地獄，熱風暴起，吹熱黑砂，來著罪人，燒皮徹骨。愚謂但言熱砂，不合水義，或熱砂連毒海為句；蓋以海沸熱砂，沒溺罪人，苦毒無量故也。融銅灌吞者：《地藏經》云：烊銅灌口，熱鐵澆身，萬死萬生，動經億劫；此皆以類相感，更有多事，故以諸字該之。前騰逸奔波，積波為水，心中之獄相初萌；此則血河灰河，融銅灌吞諸事，是業果成就矣。

是故十方，一切如來，色目我慢，名飲癡水。菩薩見慢，如避巨溺。是慢習為害之故，十方一切如來，色目我慢一事，名飲癡水。西域有水，飲之則癡迷顛倒，猶嶺海之貪泉，滇黔之啞泉相類。而菩薩見此自心之慢，非見他慢也，後皆倣此。如避巨溺：巨海洪濤，沉溺難出，有智之者，莫不嚴避也。三慢習竟。

〖經〗四者，瞋習交衝，發於相忤。忤結不息，心熱發火，鑄氣為金。如是故有：刀山、鐵橛、劍樹、劍輪、斧、鉞、鎗、鋸。如人銜冤，殺氣飛動。二習相擊，故有宮割、斬斫、剉、刺、槌、擊諸事。是故十方，一切如來，色目瞋恚，名利刀劍。菩薩見瞋，如避誅戮。

〖講〗瞋，亦六種根本煩惱之一，本屬於惑，今云習者，以宿生多瞋，猶餘種習，發為現行，重加現習，互相交衝，彼此衝突，方得成業。發於相忤者：謂忤逆侵犯。忤犯結恨，而不休息，不得報復，遂起瞋心；瞋是心中火，故曰心熱發火﹒鑄肺氣以為金，如是故有，刀山鐵橛等事。刀山者：聚刀為山；鐵橛者：《正脈》云：橛即棍也；劍樹者：豎劍為樹；劍輪者：團劍為輪；斧，劈屬，黃金飾斧為鉞；鎗，刺屬；鋸，解屬。於自心中，預現殺氣之相矣。如人銜冤，殺氣飛動者：此以例驗知，如世人銜冤，有欲報復，觀其形色，便有殺氣飛動者，是也。

二習相擊，故有宮割、斬斫、剉、刺、槌、擊諸事。二習，謂種習猶存，現習重增，瞋忤愈盛，益相攻擊，念念在殺。由是命終，神識故感宮割、斬斫、剉、刺、槌、擊諸事。《指掌疏》云：宮謂割其勢，名為宮割；斬謂斫其首，名為斬斫；剉謂折其體；刺謂穿其身；槌謂笞其背；擊謂杖其臀；此皆世刑引用耳。前之心熱發火，鑄氣為金，故有刀山鐵橛等，心中之獄相初萌；此則種種諸刑現前，是業果成就矣。

是故十方，一切如來，色目瞋恚，名利刀劍。菩薩見瞋，如避誅戮。是瞋習為害之故，十方一切如來，色目此瞋恚一事，名利刀劍，觸之即死。菩薩見此自心瞋恚之境，速宜遠之，如逃避天誅大戮，不敢有犯也。四瞋習竟。

〖經〗五者，詐習交誘，發於相調，引起不住，如是故有，繩木絞校，如水浸田，草木生長。二習相延，故有杻、械、枷、鎖、鞭、杖、檛、棒，諸事。是故十方，一切如來，色目奸偽，同名讒賊。菩薩見詐，如畏豺狼。

〖講〗詐，乃小隨煩惱之諂。今云習者，宿世慣行諂詐，猶有種習，發為現行，重加現習，交相哄誘，以成其業。發於相調者：謂諂詐不實，彼此欺瞞，發於相調，互為愚弄；由是引心令起，不肯止住，造業無休，則諂詐之術，愈出愈奇，能令人不覺不知，入其圈套。如是故有，繩木絞校：絞以繩引，謂絞而結之，令不得解；校以木局，謂校而匣之，令不得脫。此亦於自心，預現地獄之相矣。如水浸田，草木生長者：此以例驗知，如世間以水浸田中，能令草木，不覺不知，而自生長是也。

二習相延，故有杻、械、枷、鎖、鞭、杖、檛、棒，諸事。二習，謂種習猶存，現習重增，諂詐愈甚，更相延引，由此命終，神識故感在手之杻，在足之械，在項曰枷，在頸曰鎖，械枷即校。易曰：屨校滅趾，荷校滅耳，即此物也。在身則受鞭杖檛棒諸事。鞭策屬，杖條屬，檛箠屬，棒棍屬。前繩木絞校，心中之獄相初萌；此則杻械枷鎖等，是業果成就矣。

是故十方，一切如來，色目奸偽，同名讒賊。菩薩見詐，如畏豺狼。是詐習為害之故，十方一切如來，色目此奸詐虛偽，名為讒賊，以讒言哄誘，猶勝於賊，遇之者，莫不傾家蕩產。菩薩見自心之詐，回避不及，如畏豺狼。豺狼惡獸，故當避之。且能卜事，出必有獲。此如諂詐之徒，以術誘人，百發百中，亦猶是也。五詐習竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: "Ānanda, all of this — these are all what these very beings, of their own accord, have brought about through their own karma, creating ten habitual causes and receiving six cross-referenced retributions."

Commentary: The section above briefly showed emotion and thought as the very root and cause of rising and falling; this present section shows, in detail, that this rising and falling carries its own differences of cause and fruit. Hence, addressing Ānanda, the Buddha tells him: this retribution of rising and falling, felt through emotion and thought, is, in every case, brought about and summoned through these very beings' own karma. Were there no such karma of their own, then, though hell may originally exist, who could ever be driven to enter it? This carries forward what precedes; what follows now sets forth what comes next: creating the karmic cause of these ten habits, and receiving the karmic fruit of these six crossings. Though cause and fruit are spoken of separately here, in meaning they are, in truth, mutually inclusive of one another — for wherever there is a cause, there must be a fruit, and this fruit never departs from its cause. This closes the first section, carrying forward what precedes and setting forth what follows.

Sūtra: "What are these ten causes? Ānanda, first, the habit of lust, in its mutual contact, arises from mutual friction; this grinding, unceasing, gives rise to a great fierce blaze of fire stirring within it — just as, when a person rubs their own hands together, a sensation of warmth appears. The two kindle one another, and so there come to be beds of iron and pillars of copper. This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing sexual misconduct, alike calls it 'the fire of desire' — and why bodhisattvas regard desire exactly as they would a pit of fire, to be avoided at all costs."

Commentary: The first sentence poses this total question, situated here as an introduction. "Ānanda, first, the habit of lust" onward, then, distinguishes and explains each of these ten causes in turn, making plain how these ten habits — lust, greed, arrogance, anger, deceit, fraud, grudges, wrong views, false accusation, and litigation — come to be summoned. Since these are called "habits," they are exactly this same karmic cause; the six retributions that follow belong properly to this bitter fruit. Yet greed, arrogance, and the rest still carry the name of delusion as well, and so ought properly to be understood as combining both delusion and karma together — hence called "habits." A habit possesses both a seed-habit and a manifest habit: the seed-habit is this same seed, belonging to delusion; the manifest habit is this same manifestation, belonging to karma — and it is because this seed gives rise to this manifestation that every karma comes to be created.

Ānanda, first, the habit of lust, in its mutual contact: lust is the very foremost of every evil, the very root of birth and death; every sentient being, without exception, takes sexual desire as the very basis of their own life. And so it is listed here first. From some former life, this desire blazed fiercely, and its habit still remains, manifesting now in present activity; this must require an inward faculty and an outward object, male and female in mutual contact, before this karma can be formed. "Arises from mutual friction" means that a defiled mind comes together with another, each rubbing against the other, seeking pleasure ever more deeply; this grinding, unceasing, and so one's own essence and blood are, through this, worn away and dissipated. Lust accumulated becomes fire, and so there comes to be a great, fierce blaze of fire, stirring within one's own mind — this being a preview, already appearing, of this same mark of hell. That, at death, this summons this same karmic fire, one may readily infer from this alone. "Just as, when a person rubs their own hands together, a sensation of warmth appears" offers this as an illustrative proof: just as, should a person, for no reason at all, rub their own two palms together, this same sensation of warmth would duly appear — this is exactly the same.

"The two kindle one another, and so there come to be beds of iron and pillars of copper." These two habits refer to the seed-habit and the manifest habit together — like dry firewood meeting flame, the seed-habit still remaining even as the manifest habit intensifies further, each kindling the other in turn. And so, at the end of life, one comes to feel these various matters of the iron bed and the copper pillar. For that earlier great, fierce blaze of fire, stirring within, was only the first budding of this same hell-mark within the mind; this iron bed and copper pillar are the very accomplishment of this same karmic fruit within the mind. According to the Sūtra on the Samādhi of Buddha-Visualization, the copper-pillar hell contains a single copper pillar, shaped like a mountain of fire, six hundred yojanas in height, with fierce fire below it, and, atop this fire, an iron bed, and, above that, a wheel of blades, with iron-beaked worms and birds interspersed throughout. Whoever destroys the moral order, corrupts moral transformation, or commits impure acts at the wrong time or in the wrong place, is, at the end of life, born atop this copper pillar, its fierce flame blazing, burning their own body; terrified, they look down and see, upon the iron bed, a beautiful woman — or, should they themselves be a woman, a handsome man — and, the mind giving rise to love and attachment, they descend from the copper pillar down onto the iron bed, and, the moment their own male or female organ makes contact, fire arises at once from both, and iron-beaked worms enter through the male or female organ and emerge from it again — in a single day and night, undergoing nine hundred million births and nine hundred million deaths. Observing this, one thus knows that the suffering of this retribution of lust is, in every case, manifested through mind alone.

"This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing sexual misconduct, alike calls it 'the fire of desire' — and why bodhisattvas regard desire exactly as they would a pit of fire, to be avoided at all costs." Because of the harm caused by this habit of lust, every Tathāgata of the ten directions — describing it to others is called "describing"; observing it in themselves is called "seeing" — with one voice, though speaking in different tongues, alike name it "the fire of desire." The harm of this fire of desire is graver even than a raging flame — whoever meets it inevitably loses their own body and life. Bodhisattvas, seeing this realm of sexual desire, keep their distance and avoid it entirely, exactly as they would a pit of fire. As the Precious Mirror Commentary puts it: for this very reason, every buddha alike reproves it — one ought truly to engrave this upon one's very bones; and every bodhisattva alike avoids it — how could the heart not tremble at this! Alas! That desire should be a fire so poisonous as this — may every wise man and woman take heed, and never imitate the moth that flings itself against the lamp. This closes the first section, the habit of lust.

Sūtra: "Second, the habit of greed, in its mutual scheming, arises from mutual suction; this drawing in, without ceasing, gives rise to accumulated cold and solid ice, freezing bitterly within it — just as, when a person draws breath in through the mouth, a sensation of cold arises. The two overwhelm one another, and so there come to be the sounds of chattering teeth, chapped and bleeding lips, and lotuses blue, red, and white, together with ice and frost. This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing excessive craving, alike calls it 'the water of greed' — and why bodhisattvas regard greed exactly as they would a miasmic sea, to be avoided at all costs."

Commentary: Greed is one of the six root afflictions, belonging properly to delusion; called a "habit" here because, from a former life, greed was excessive, its seed-habit still remaining, now manifesting in present activity, this manifest habit added further still, each scheming against the other, each calculating against the other, before this karma can be formed. "Arises from mutual suction" means drawing in from one another, in order to satisfy one's own private wish. "This drawing in, without ceasing" means that, because of this greed, never satisfied, this pursuit never resting, there comes to be accumulated cold and solid ice, freezing bitterly within it. This mind of greed belongs to water, and this act of drawing in belongs to wind; when water meets wind, it inevitably accumulates cold and congeals into solid ice, this same mark of bitter freezing already previewed within one's own mind. "Just as, when a person draws breath in through the mouth, a sensation of cold arises" offers this as an illustrative proof: just as, should a person open their mouth wide and draw in breath, a sensation of cold would still arise from this alone — this is exactly the same.

"The two overwhelm one another, and so there come to be the sounds of chattering teeth, chapped and bleeding lips, and lotuses blue, red, and white, together with ice and frost." These two habits mean that the seed-habit still remains even as the manifest habit intensifies further, this drawing-in of greed growing ever more severe — hence, "overwhelm one another." And so, at the end of life, one's own consciousness comes to feel these matters — the sounds of chattering teeth, chapped and bleeding lips, and lotuses blue, red, and white, together with ice and frost. These sounds of chattering teeth and chapped, bleeding lips are all cries of suffering, uttered by those tormented by cold within hell; and these lotuses blue, red, and white are all the very color and shape of those frozen and split open by this same bitter cold within hell — these being the first six of the eight cold hells. According to other sūtras, there are, further, the hells of blistered sores, where the cold splits the flesh into open sores, and of flesh torn from the bone, where the very joints come apart entirely — these being the last two of these eight cold hells, all included here under "ice and frost, and the like." The earlier accumulated, solid ice, freezing bitterly within, was only the first budding of this same hell-mark within the mind; this present sound and appearance of this same cold, oppressing one, now manifest before one, is the very accomplishment of this same karmic fruit.

"This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing excessive craving, alike calls it 'the water of greed' — and why bodhisattvas regard greed exactly as they would a miasmic sea, to be avoided at all costs." Because of the harm caused by this habit of greed, every Tathāgata of the ten directions — describing it to others is called "describing"; observing it in themselves is called "seeing" — with one voice, though speaking in different tongues, this drawing-in and excessive craving alike named "the water of greed." That is, the harm caused by greed surpasses even poisoned water — whoever drinks it inevitably suffers a rotted intestine and a decayed stomach. Bodhisattvas, seeing this realm of covetous love, none fail to keep their distance from it, exactly as they would avoid a miasmic sea. A miasma is a pestilential vapor, found in every hot region of mountain and sea; should a person, unaware, mistakenly breathe in this vapor, they at once contract malarial fever, and so come to lose their own life. This closes the second section, the habit of greed.

Sūtra: "Third, the habit of arrogance, in its mutual encroachment, arises from mutual self-reliance; this racing current, never resting, gives rise to a surging, unrestrained flood — this accumulated surge becoming water — just as, when a person's own tongue presses against the roof of the mouth, savoring this again and again, saliva is thereby produced. The two beat upon one another, and so there come to be rivers of blood, rivers of ash, scorching sand, poisonous seas, and molten copper poured down the throat. This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing self-conceit, alike calls it 'drinking the water of delusion' — and why bodhisattvas regard arrogance exactly as they would a great flood, to be avoided at all costs."

Commentary: Arrogance, too, is one of the six root afflictions, belonging properly to delusion; called a "habit" here because, from a former life, arrogance was excessive, its seed-habit still remaining, now manifesting in present activity, this manifest habit added further still, each encroaching upon the other in turn — each mutually encroaching and overstepping the other, before this karma can be formed. "Arises from mutual self-reliance" means relying on one's own great and honored lineage, one's own power and abundant wealth, exalting oneself and demeaning others, this nature rising ever higher, this mind racing ever upward, never knowing when to stop. Now, self-conceit belongs to mountain, and this racing current belongs to water; where mountain stands and water races, this inevitably surges and floods. And so there comes to be this surging, unrestrained, racing flood, this same mark of accumulated surge becoming water, already previewed within one's own mind! "Just as, when a person's own tongue presses against the roof of the mouth, savoring this again and again, saliva is thereby produced" offers this as an illustrative proof: just as, should a person press their own tongue against the roof of the mouth, working this savor back and forth upon it, saliva would still, thereby, be produced — this is exactly the same.

"The two beat upon one another, and so there come to be rivers of blood, rivers of ash, scorching sand, poisonous seas, molten copper poured down the throat, and other such matters." These two habits mean that the seed-habit still remains even as the manifest habit intensifies further, this current of arrogance growing ever more severe — hence, "beat upon one another," meaning stirred and churned, encroaching and overstepping. And so, at the end of life, one comes to feel this retribution of these rivers of blood and the rest. As the Consecration Commentary puts it: this hell contains two mountains; the offender, running in between them, finds the two mountains suddenly close together, as though pressed beneath a grinding-stone, flesh and blood flowing everywhere, like a great river or sea, this blood-water surging and boiling, tens of thousands of men and women rising and sinking within it. As for this river of ash: as the Sūtra of Different Manifestations of the Scriptures and Vinaya puts it: the river-of-ash hell, in length, breadth, and depth, measures five hundred yojanas, its ash and hot water surging and boiling; the offender, entering this river, is pierced by iron thorns, pus and blood flowing out, suffering in every conceivable form. As for this scorching sand: as the Consecration Sūtra puts it, this is exactly the black-sand hell, where a fierce, hot wind suddenly rises, blowing hot, black sand that clings to the offender, burning through skin and bone alike. I would say, further, that speaking only of scorching sand does not, of itself, carry this sense of water; perhaps this ought to be read together with the poisonous sea that follows — this sea boiling with scorching sand, drowning the offender within it, this suffering and poison beyond all measure. As for this molten copper poured down the throat: as the Sūtra of Kṣitigarbha's Fundamental Vows puts it: molten copper is poured into the mouth, and burning iron is poured over the body, ten thousand deaths and ten thousand rebirths, passing through koṭis of kalpas in this way — all of this being drawn together, through this same kind of correspondence, and, since there are yet further such matters besides, gathered together under this word "and the rest." That earlier surging, unrestrained flood, this same accumulated surge becoming water, was only the first budding of this same hell-mark within the mind; this present river of blood, river of ash, molten copper poured down the throat, and every such matter, is the very accomplishment of this same karmic fruit.

"This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing self-conceit, alike names it 'drinking the water of delusion' — and why bodhisattvas regard arrogance exactly as they would a great flood, to be avoided at all costs." Because of the harm caused by this habit of arrogance, every Tathāgata of the ten directions names this single matter of self-conceit "drinking the water of delusion." In the Western Regions, there is said to be a certain water that, once drunk, brings on delusion and confusion, overturning the mind entirely — resembling the "spring of greed" at Ling Hai, or the "spring of muteness" in Yunnan and Guizhou. And bodhisattvas see this same arrogance within their own mind — not the arrogance of another; every case that follows should be understood the same way. "Exactly as they would a great flood": the vast ocean, its mighty waves, drowning one utterly, escape all but impossible — one of wisdom cannot but keep the strictest distance from it. This closes the third section, the habit of arrogance.

Sūtra: "Fourth, the habit of anger, in its mutual clashing, arises from mutual defiance; this defiance, knotted and unresolved, heats the mind and gives rise to fire, forging this vital energy into metal — and so there come to be mountains of blades, iron stakes, forests of swords, wheels of blades, axes, halberds, spears, and saws — just as, when a person nurses a grievance, a murderous impulse flares and stirs. The two strike against one another, and so there come to be castration, beheading, cutting, piercing, clubbing, and beating. This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing wrath, alike calls it 'a sharpened blade' — and why bodhisattvas regard anger exactly as they would execution itself, to be avoided at all costs."

Commentary: Anger, too, is one of the six root afflictions, belonging properly to delusion; called a "habit" here because, from a former life, anger was excessive, its seed-habit still remaining, now manifesting in present activity, this manifest habit added further still, each clashing with the other in turn — each mutually clashing and colliding, before this karma can be formed. "Arises from mutual defiance" means defying and encroaching upon one another. This defiance and encroachment, knotted into resentment, never resting; unable to take revenge, this then gives rise to a mind of anger — anger being this same fire within the mind, hence, "heats the mind and gives rise to fire" — forging the very breath of the lungs into metal, and so there come to be mountains of blades, iron stakes, and every such matter. A mountain of blades is blades gathered into a mountain; an iron stake — as the Standard Meaning Commentary notes, a stake here means a club; a forest of swords is swords set upright like trees; a wheel of blades is blades gathered into a circle; an axe belongs to the chopping class; a halberd is gold-adorned, an ornamented axe; a spear belongs to the stabbing class; and a saw belongs to the cutting class — all of this, this same mark of murderous impulse, already previewed within one's own mind! "Just as, when a person nurses a grievance, a murderous impulse flares and stirs" offers this as an illustrative proof: just as, should a person in this world nurse a grievance, wishing to take revenge, one could observe, in their very countenance, this murderous impulse already flaring and stirring — this is exactly the same.

"The two strike against one another, and so there come to be castration, beheading, cutting, piercing, clubbing, and beating, and every such matter." These two habits mean that the seed-habit still remains even as the manifest habit intensifies further, this defiance of anger growing ever more fierce, striking against one another all the more, thought after thought fixed on killing. And so, at the end of life, one's own consciousness comes to feel these matters of castration, beheading, cutting, piercing, clubbing, and beating. As the Detailed Commentary puts it: castration means cutting off the very organ, hence, "castration"; beheading means severing the very head, hence, "beheading"; cutting means breaking apart the very body; piercing means running the body through; clubbing means flogging the very back; and beating means striking the very buttocks — all of this simply drawn from the punishments used in this world. The earlier heating of the mind, giving rise to fire, forging this vital energy into metal, giving rise to mountains of blades, iron stakes, and the rest, was only the first budding of this same hell-mark within the mind; this present array of every such punishment, now manifest before one, is the very accomplishment of this same karmic fruit.

"This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing wrath, alike names it 'a sharpened blade' — and why bodhisattvas regard anger exactly as they would execution itself, to be avoided at all costs." Because of the harm caused by this habit of anger, every Tathāgata of the ten directions names this single matter of wrath "a sharpened blade" — to touch it is to die at once. And bodhisattvas, seeing this realm of wrath and anger within their own mind, ought swiftly to keep their distance from it — exactly as one would flee from execution by heaven's own decree, not daring to risk it. This closes the fourth section, the habit of anger.

Sūtra: "Fifth, the habit of deceit, in its mutual enticement, arises from mutual coaxing; this leading-on, never resting, gives rise to ropes and beams, bindings and cangues — just as water, seeping into a field, makes grass and trees grow, unnoticed and unknown. The two extend one another, and so there come to be manacles, fetters, cangues, chains, whips, rods, cudgels, and staves. This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing cunning fraud, alike calls it 'a slandering thief' — and why bodhisattvas fear deceit exactly as they would a jackal or wolf."

Commentary: Deceit is a minor derivative affliction, this same flattery. Called a "habit" here because, from a former life, one grew accustomed to flattery and deceit, its seed-habit still remaining, now manifesting in present activity, this manifest habit added further still, coaxing and luring one another in turn, in order to accomplish this karma. "Arises from mutual coaxing" means that, flattering and deceitful, insincere, each deceives the other, arising from this mutual coaxing, each making a fool of the other in turn; and so this mind is led on, unwilling to stop, this karma created without rest, and so this art of flattery and deceit grows ever more elaborate, ever more strange, able to draw a person in, unaware and unknowing, straight into its own trap. And so there come to be ropes and beams, bindings and cangues: binding with rope means binding and knotting it, so that it cannot be undone; confining with beam means confining and boxing it, so that it cannot escape — this, too, already previewing, within one's own mind, this same mark of hell. "Just as water, seeping into a field, makes grass and trees grow, unnoticed and unknown" offers this as an illustrative proof: just as, in this world, water seeping into a field is able to make grass and trees grow, unnoticed and unknowing, of their own accord — this is exactly the same.

"The two extend one another, and so there come to be manacles, fetters, cangues, chains, whips, rods, cudgels, and staves, and every such matter." These two habits mean that the seed-habit still remains even as the manifest habit intensifies further, this flattery and deceit growing ever worse, drawing one another on further still. And so, at the end of life, one's own consciousness comes to feel manacles upon the hands, fetters upon the feet, a cangue about the neck, and chains about the throat — fetters and cangues both being this same confining beam; as the Book of Changes puts it, "wearing the cangue that covers the toes" and "bearing the cangue that covers the ears" refer to exactly this same thing. Upon the body, one undergoes whipping, rods, cudgels, and staves — the whip belonging to the lashing class, the rod to the switching class, the cudgel to the beating class, and the staff to the clubbing class. The earlier ropes and beams, bindings and cangues, was only the first budding of this same hell-mark within the mind; this present array of manacles, fetters, cangues, and chains, is the very accomplishment of this same karmic fruit.

"This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing cunning fraud, alike names it 'a slandering thief' — and why bodhisattvas fear deceit exactly as they would a jackal or wolf." Because of the harm caused by this habit of deceit, every Tathāgata of the ten directions names this same cunning fraud and insincerity "a slandering thief" — for slanderous words, coaxing one in, surpass even a common thief; whoever meets with it loses their entire household and fortune, without exception. Bodhisattvas, seeing this deceit within their own mind, fear it as they would a jackal or wolf. The jackal and wolf are savage beasts, rightly to be feared and avoided; and yet they are also said to be able to divine coming events, so that, once they set out, they never fail to find their prey. This resembles, too, this same flatterer and deceiver, who, using this same art to entice others, succeeds in every single case. This closes the fifth section, the habit of deceit.

〖經〗六者，誑習交欺，發於相罔，誣罔不止，飛心造奸。如是故有，塵土、屎、尿，穢污不淨。如塵隨風，各無所見。二習相加，故有沒溺騰擲，飛墮漂淪諸事。是故十方，一切如來，色目欺誑，同名劫殺。菩薩見誑，如踐蛇虺。

〖講〗誑，乃小隨煩惱之一，亦屬於惑。今云習者：以宿世多誑，尚有種習，發為現行，重加現習，交相欺瞞，方得成業。發於相罔者：謂矯誑虛偽，發於誣罔。所以無者誣罔以為有，虛者誣罔以為實；誣罔不止，以為得計，遂飛動其心，造設奸謀，神出鬼沒，令人不覺，墮其計中。由其用心如是，故有塵土屎尿，穢污不淨，心中預現地獄之相矣。如塵隨風，各無所見者：此以例驗知，誣罔之計，令人迷惑；不知者，正如風捲塵沙，塵隨風勢，攪亂虛空，昏天黑地，令人對面各無所見是也。

二習相加，故有沒溺騰擲，飛墮漂淪諸事。二習，謂種習猶存，現習重增，誣罔愈甚，故曰相加。謂誑業倍造，如是故有，命終神識，見有沒溺騰擲，飛墮飄淪諸事。前塵土屎尿，穢污不淨，心中之獄相初萌；今見有沒溺等，業果成就矣。《指掌疏》云：沒溺者：似是沸屎地獄，謂屎尿沸溢，罪人沒溺於其中也。騰擲者：似是黑砂地獄，謂風吹黑砂，罪人騰擲於其中也。飛墜者：隨風上下之相；漂淪者：隨沸浮沉之相。《正脈》云：誑能陷害於人，故受沒溺；誑須飛心鼓揚，故受騰擲。前塵土屎尿，穢污不淨，心中之獄相初萌；今則沒溺騰擲等，是業果成就矣。

是故十方，一切如來，色目欺誑，同名劫殺。菩薩見誑，如踐蛇虺。是誑習為害之故，十方一切如來，色目此欺誑，害人自害，同於劫殺；謂劫財殺命，極言可畏也。菩薩見自心之誑，如踐踏蛇虺，蛇虺時常含毒螫人，踐之必受其害。問：誑之與詐，有何差別？答：詐謂誘他，期墮我術，明取其利；誑謂欺他，恐洩我機，暗取其利；皆屬妄語，而舉心不同也。六誑習竟。

〖經〗七者，怨（音冤）習交嫌，發於銜恨。如是故有，飛石投礫。匣貯車檻，甕盛囊撲。如陰毒人，懷抱畜惡。二習相吞，故有投擲、擒捉，擊射拋撮諸事。是故十方，一切如來，色目怨家，名違害鬼。菩薩見怨，如飲鴆酒。

〖講〗怨即怨恨，以宿世有怨，種習仍存，發為現行，重加現習，交相憎嫌，彼此懷怨，以成其業。發於銜恨者：謂銜怨不捨，懷恨在心，誓其報復。如是故有，飛石投礫，匣貯車檻，甕盛囊撲，於自心預形地獄之相矣。《指掌疏》云：飛石者：飛以石塊；投礫者：投以碎石；匣貯者：匣床盛貯；車檻者：車內檻禁；甕盛者：盛人於甕，而外以火灸，如昔人有請公入甕者是也。囊撲者：收入於囊，而舉以撲之，如秦始皇囊撲二弟之類是也。皆由銜怨害人，而害具即從自心中生也。如陰毒人，懷抱畜惡者：此以例驗知，正如陰毒之人，懷抱奸謀，心畜惡念，暗算害人者是也。

二習相吞，故有投擲、擒捉，擊射拋撮諸事。二習，謂種習猶存，現習更增，怨恨愈深，故曰相吞，即所謂恨不生噉也。如是故有，命終神識，見有投擲擒捉，擊射拋撮諸事。投擲者：礫投石擲；擒捉者：擒拏捉取；入匣車入甕囊也。擊射者：礫則擊其頭；石則射其身；拋撮者：拋撲令死，所謂囊撲者是也。撮折其身，所謂匣貯、車檻、甕盛者是也。始而心欲害人，究竟反為自害；懷怨者，宜知警矣。前飛石投礫等，心中之獄相初萌；今則投擲擒捉等，業果成就矣。

是故十方，一切如來，色目怨家，名違害鬼。菩薩見怨，如飲鴆酒。是怨習為害之故，十方一切如來，色目怨恨之家，名違害鬼；違背正理，暗中害人，極惡之鬼。而菩薩見此怨恨之心，不敢有犯。如飲鴆酒：鴆鳥最毒，羽毛瀝酒，飲之則腸寸寸斷矣！七怨習竟。

〖經〗八者，見習交明，如薩迦耶，見戒禁取，邪悟諸業，發於違拒，出生相反。如是故有，王使主吏，證執文籍，如行路人，來往相見，二習相交，故有勘問，權詐考訊，推鞠察訪，披究照明，善惡童子，手執文簿，辭辯諸事。是故十方，一切如來，色目惡見，同名見坑。菩薩見諸虛妄偏執，如臨毒壑。

〖講〗見：即惡見，於諸諦理，顛倒推求。見習者：以宿生諸見熾盛，猶有種習，發為現行，重加現習，彼此交相立破，欲明己見，故曰交明。此見行相差別有五：一、薩迦耶：此云有身：即是身見，執我我所。二、邊見：執斷執常。三、邪見：撥無因果。四、見取：非果計果，如以無想為涅槃之類。五、戒禁取：非因計因，如持牛戒狗戒，為生天因之類。然此五種，通稱惡見。文中見戒禁取句，應是見取與戒禁取，取字雙用。邊見背中，邪見背正，皆為邪悟。如上五見，各有所作，故云諸業；由此異執，故云發於違拒；互相是非，故云出生相反；不唯與正違拒，即其自類，亦自違拒；不唯他出之見相反，即與自生之見，亦多相反；如自語相違，自教相違之類。且而不肯從人就正，如是故有，王使主吏等。王使者：琰魔羅王之使者；主吏者：主掌簿書之官吏，證其所執之文籍，於自心中，預現其相矣。如行路人，往來相見者：此以喻驗知，如行路之人，彼此來往，互相看見，不可得避者是也。

二習相交，故有勘問，權詐考訊，推鞠察訪，披究照明，善惡童子，手執文簿，辭辯諸事。二習：謂種習猶存，現習重增，互相交對，辨別是非。由此命終，神識，見有勘問權詐等，謂始而勘校審問，權稱詐偽，此似王使事也。如不得其情，轉啟有司、逼考訊問、嚴推重鞠，從旁體察訪問，此似主吏事也。仍復不得其情，再披究生前見業，照明神識習氣，於時則有善惡童子，手執文簿，言辭辯別，載之甚詳，證之甚確，如此諸事，直得無理可申，地獄重罪，乃甘心領受矣。前王使主吏，證執文籍，心中之獄相初萌；今勘問權詐等，是業果成就矣。

是故十方，一切如來，色目惡見，同名見坑。菩薩見諸虛妄偏執，如臨毒壑。是見習為害之故，十方一切如來，形容觀察，同名見坑。謂此五種惡見，甚於深坑，一經誤入，即不能出。菩薩見諸虛妄偏執者：見理不真曰虛妄，自是他非曰偏執。如臨毒壑者：以邱壑中，有毒蛇等物，入之則必喪法身，傷慧命，急宜避之。八見習竟。

〖經〗九者，枉習交加，發於誣謗。如是故有，合山合石，碾磑耕磨。如讒賊人，逼枉良善。二習相排，故有押捺槌按，蹙漉衡度諸事。是故十方，一切如來，色目怨謗，同名讒虎。菩薩見枉，如遭霹靂。

〖講〗枉者：枉屈，謂駕禍逼人。今云習者，以宿世好枉，猶有種習，發為現行，再加現習，交相加逼，以成其業。發於誣謗者：所謂本無其事，誣之為有，謗以為實；如是故有，合山等物，已具自心。準《經律巽相》云：合山：謂大石山，兩山相對，罪人入中，山自然合，骨肉糜碎，山還故處；合石者：臥罪人大石之上，以石合之，如笮蒲萄相似。碾：即大熱鐵輪，轢諸罪人，令身破碎；磑：小磨也；耕：謂犁耕，破其舌也；磨：即石磨地獄，謂捉罪人撲熱石，舒布手足，以大熱石，壓其身上，迴旋而磨也；是有欲逼人，而逼己之具，先現自心，吁可畏也。如讒賊人，逼枉良善者：此以例驗知，如讒賊人。讒：譖也；賊：害也。讒譖為害，名讒賊人；逼壓無辜，枉害良善者是也。

二習相排，故有押捺槌按，蹙漉衡度諸事。二習：謂種習猶存，現習重增，互相排擠，枉害不止；由此命終，神識故感押抑揉捺，令其服罪也。設不服罪，蹙其身於囊袋，壓之而漉其血，名曰蹙漉；掛其身於權衡，秤稱輕重，尺量短長，名曰衡度。諸事者：以總其餘。前合山合石等，心中之獄相初萌；今押捺搥按等，是業果成就，自作自受矣。

是故十方，一切如來，色目怨謗，同名讒虎。菩薩見枉，如遭霹靂。是枉習為害之故，十方一切如來，色目怨害誣謗，同名讒虎；讒言傷害，惡過於虎。菩薩見枉屈之事，如遭霹靂。霹靂是迅雷擊物之聲，遭之者，莫不魂飛魄散矣！九枉習竟。

〖經〗十者，訟習交諠，發於藏覆，如是故有，鑑見照燭。如於日中，不能藏影。二習相陳，故有惡友，業鏡火珠，披露宿業，對驗諸事。是故十方，一切如來，色目覆藏，同名陰賊。菩薩觀覆，如戴高山，履於巨海。

〖講〗訟，乃諍訟；諠，即諠訴。今云習者：以宿生好訟，猶有種習，發為現行，重加現習，交相諠訴，以成其業。發於藏覆者：藏者，隱藏；覆者，蓋覆。隱藏己罪，蓋覆陰私。如是故有，鑑見照燭：謂鏡鑑，以見其宿業；珠照，以燭其心曲，使其不得隱藏；於自心中，預現其相矣。此以例驗知，如於日中，不能藏曲直之影者是也。

二習相陳，故有惡友，業鏡火珠，披露宿業，對驗諸事。二習：謂種習猶存，現習重增，訟覆交陳，如是故於命終，神識見有惡友，宿世同造之惡友，現前作證。業鏡者：能鑑宿業之鏡；火珠者：能照心曲之珠。使其披露宿業，對驗諸事，必顯然發明，分毫不能隱藏者。前鑑見照燭，心中之獄相初萌；今則惡友業鏡火珠等，是業果成就矣。

是故十方，一切如來，色目覆藏，同名陰賊。菩薩觀覆，如戴高山，履於巨海。是訟習為害之故，十方一切如來，色目覆藏一事，同名陰賊。謂家有陰賊，久必遭劫；喻覆藏己過，終必為害。此如來警惕於人者如此。菩薩觀此覆過之境，如頭戴高山，身履巨海，終不能出，故戴愈重，而溺愈深，以喻過愈覆，而罪愈重也。墜落極苦，無由得脫，此菩薩自相警惕者，又如此。蓋諍訟一事，聖賢共惕，急宜避之。文中二習相然，相陵等，以顯由因緣具足，而後事辦耳。

《正脈》云：下品往生者，雖具苦因，而火車相現，急急念佛，不成獄果；此所謂有因無緣，即不生也。但彼仗憑佛力，非己智分。則夫悟心之人，不但地獄，一切繫縛事業，平日固當努力，突絕其因，更記臨終，勿領其緣，有轉身處，則陰境現前，不隨他去，方於生死，少分得其自在，切須自忖！若也道力未充，未能作主，則念佛往生，更仗他力，萬無一失矣！生死關頭，故此詳敘，智者宜究心焉。二徵釋十因竟。

Sūtra: "Sixth, the habit of fraud, in its mutual deception, arises from mutual misleading; this false accusation, never ceasing, sends the mind flying to scheme and plot — and so there come to be dust, filth, excrement, and urine, foul and impure — just as dust, carried on the wind, leaves each person unable to see the other at all. Commentary: Fraud is one of the minor derivative afflictions, likewise belonging to delusion. Called a "habit" here because, from a former life, one was given greatly to fraud, its seed-habit still remaining, now manifesting in present activity, this manifest habit added further still, deceiving and misleading one another in turn, before this karma can be formed. "Arises from mutual misleading" means that this false pretense and insincerity arises from this mutual misleading — and so what does not exist is falsely claimed to exist, and what is empty is falsely claimed to be real; this false accusation never ceasing, taken as a clever scheme, the mind then sent flying, scheming and plotting, appearing and vanishing like a ghost, causing others, unaware, to fall straight into its trap. Because the mind works in this way, there come to be dust, filth, excrement, and urine, foul and impure — this same mark of hell already previewed within the mind. "Just as dust, carried on the wind, leaves each person unable to see the other at all" offers this as an illustrative proof: this scheme of false accusation confuses people; one who does not know it is exactly like sand and dust caught up by the wind, this dust following the wind's own force, churning up the empty sky, darkening heaven and earth alike, so that people facing one another can see nothing at all — this is exactly the same.

The earlier dust, filth, excrement, and urine, foul and impure, was only the first budding of this same hell-mark within the mind; now, this drowning, being tossed and hurled, and the rest, is the very accomplishment of this same karmic fruit.

Bodhisattvas, seeing this same fraud within their own mind, are as one who treads upon a viper — vipers constantly carry venom, and sting whoever provokes them; to tread upon one is inevitably to suffer its harm. This closes the sixth section, the habit of fraud.

Commentary: A grudge is exactly this same resentment and hatred; because of some grudge from a former life, this seed-habit still remaining, now manifesting in present activity, this manifest habit added further still, hating and resenting one another in turn, each harboring this grudge against the other, before this karma can be formed. "Arises from a nursed hatred" means nursing this grudge, unwilling to let it go, holding this hatred within the heart, vowing to take revenge. And so there come to be stones hurled and pebbles thrown, confinement in a case, imprisonment in a caged cart, sealing in an urn, and beating within a sack — this same mark of hell already previewed within one's own mind. As the Detailed Commentary puts it: hurling stones means hurling stone blocks; throwing pebbles means throwing broken stones; confinement in a case means being sealed within a chest or bed; imprisonment in a caged cart means being locked within a cart; sealing in an urn means placing a person within an urn and roasting them from outside with fire, as when, in ancient times, someone was invited to "enter the urn"; and beating within a sack means being gathered into a sack and then beaten, as when the First Emperor of Qin beat his own two younger brothers within sacks. All of this arises from nursing a grudge and harming another — and yet these very instruments of harm arise, in every case, from within one's own mind. "Just as a person of hidden poison secretly harbors and nurtures malice" offers this as an illustrative proof: exactly like a person of hidden poison, harboring some treacherous scheme, nursing an evil thought within the mind, secretly plotting to harm another — this is exactly the same.

"The two devour one another, and so there come to be hurling and seizing, striking, shooting, throwing, and grasping, and every such matter." These two habits mean that the seed-habit still remains even as the manifest habit intensifies further, this resentment and hatred growing ever deeper — hence, "devour one another," this being exactly what is meant by hating so deeply as to wish one could devour the other alive. And so there come to be, at the end of life, one's own consciousness seeing hurling and seizing, striking, shooting, throwing, and grasping, and every such matter. Hurling and seizing means throwing pebbles and hurling stones; seizing and capturing means grasping and taking hold, entering the case, entering the cart, entering the urn, entering the sack. Striking and shooting means that pebbles strike the head, and stones shoot the body; throwing and grasping means throwing to kill — this being exactly what is meant by beating within a sack — and grasping to break the body — this being exactly what is meant by confinement in a case, imprisonment in a caged cart, and sealing in an urn. At first, one's own mind wished only to harm another; in the end, this instead harms oneself — let all who harbor a grudge take heed of this, and beware! The earlier stones hurled and pebbles thrown, and the rest, was only the first budding of this same hell-mark within the mind; this present hurling and seizing, and the rest, is the very accomplishment of this same karmic fruit.

"This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing an enemy held in the heart, alike names it 'a harm-dealing ghost' — and why bodhisattvas regard grudges exactly as they would drinking poisoned wine." Because of the harm caused by this habit of grudges, every Tathāgata of the ten directions names this same household of resentment and hatred "a harm-dealing ghost" — turning against true principle, harming another in secret, this most evil of ghosts. And bodhisattvas, seeing this same mind of resentment and hatred, dare not transgress against it at all — exactly as one would drink poisoned wine: the poison-bird is the most venomous of all, its very feathers, steeped in wine, causing, once drunk, the very intestines to snap apart, section by section! This closes the seventh section, the habit of grudges.

Sūtra: "Eighth, the habit of wrong views, in its mutual assertion, is such as the view of a fixed self, the view of extremes, wrong view, clinging to views, and clinging to precepts wrongly held — every deviant realization arising, in each of these karmic activities, from mutual opposition, giving rise to contradiction — and so there come to be the king's own messengers and chief clerks, verifying and holding fast to written records — just as travelers on a road, coming and going, meet one another and cannot avoid being seen. The two cross one another, and so there come to be interrogation, deceitful cross-examination, pursuit and investigation, disclosure and clarification, and good and evil boy-attendants holding written ledgers in hand, arguing each case in detail. This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing evil views, alike calls them 'a pit of views' — and why bodhisattvas see every false, one-sided clinging exactly as they would approach a poisoned ravine."

Commentary: "View" means an evil view, a distorted, deluded search after every truth. Called a "habit" here because, from a former life, one's own views blazed fiercely, its seed-habit still remaining, now manifesting in present activity, this manifest habit added further still, each establishing and refuting the other's view in turn, each wishing to make their own view manifest — hence, "mutual assertion." This activity of view divides into five distinct kinds: first, the view of a fixed self — this is exactly the view of a self, clinging to a self and to what belongs to a self; second, the view of extremes — clinging either to annihilation or to eternity; third, wrong view — denying cause and effect entirely; fourth, clinging to views — mistaking what is not the fruit for the fruit, as when non-conceptual absorption is mistaken for nirvāṇa; and fifth, clinging to precepts wrongly held — mistaking what is not the cause for the cause, as when observing the vows of a cow or a dog is taken as the cause of rebirth in heaven. Yet all five of these, together, are called evil views. In the text, the phrase "clinging to views wrongly held" properly refers to both clinging to views and clinging to precepts wrongly held together, this word "clinging" used doubly for both. The view of extremes turns its back on the middle way; wrong view turns its back on what is correct — both, alike, are deviant realizations. Each of these five views, above, has its own particular activity, hence, "every karmic activity"; and, because of these differing views held to, hence, "arising from mutual opposition"; each holding the other to be mistaken, hence, "giving rise to contradiction" — not merely standing opposed to what is correct, but even opposed within their own same kind as well; not merely contradicting the view another puts forth, but even contradicting the view one has put forth oneself, as when one's own words contradict one another, or one's own teaching contradicts itself. And, unwilling to yield to another and accept correction, there come to be, in just this way, the king's own messengers and chief clerks, and the rest. The king's own messenger is exactly a messenger of King Yama; the chief clerk is exactly the officer in charge of records, verifying whatever written record one clings to — this same mark of hell already previewed within one's own mind. "Just as travelers on a road, coming and going, meet one another" offers this as an illustration to verify this: just as travelers on a road, coming and going in either direction, see one another mutually, unable to avoid it — this is exactly the same.

"The two cross one another, and so there come to be interrogation, deceitful cross-examination, pursuit and investigation, disclosure and clarification, and good and evil boy-attendants holding written ledgers in hand, arguing each case in detail, and every such matter." These two habits mean that the seed-habit still remains even as the manifest habit intensifies further, each cross-examining the other in turn, distinguishing right from wrong between them. And so, at the end of life, one's own consciousness comes to see interrogation and deceitful cross-examination — meaning that one is first examined and interrogated, feigning authority and pretending falsehood — this resembling this same matter of the king's own messenger. Should the truth still not be obtained this way, the matter is then referred further to the appropriate office, pressed and cross-examined, sternly pursued and gravely investigated, inquired into and observed from every side — this resembling this same matter of the chief clerk. Should the truth still not be obtained even then, one's own views and karma from this present life are disclosed and examined once more, this consciousness and its habitual tendency clarified and illuminated; and, at that time, there are good and evil boy-attendants, holding written ledgers in hand, arguing and distinguishing every word, recorded in the fullest detail, verified with the utmost certainty — and, faced with every such matter as this, there remains no argument left to make at all, and one is left with no choice but to willingly accept this grave offense of hell. The earlier king's own messengers and chief clerks, verifying and holding fast to written records, was only the first budding of this same hell-mark within the mind; this present interrogation, deceitful cross-examination, and the rest, is the very accomplishment of this same karmic fruit.

"This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing evil views, alike names them 'a pit of views' — and why bodhisattvas see every false, one-sided clinging exactly as they would approach a poisoned ravine." Because of the harm caused by this habit of wrong views, every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in every observation and description alike, names them "a pit of views" — meaning that these five kinds of evil view are graver even than a deep pit; once mistakenly fallen into, one can never climb back out again. "Bodhisattvas see every false, one-sided clinging" means that seeing this principle wrongly is called falseness, and holding oneself right while holding another wrong is called one-sided clinging. "Exactly as they would approach a poisoned ravine" means that, within such a ravine, there dwell venomous serpents and the like; whoever enters it must certainly lose the Dharma-body and injure the wisdom-life, and so ought urgently to avoid it. This closes the eighth section, the habit of wrong views.

Sūtra: "Ninth, the habit of false accusation, in its mutual imposition, arises from slander and defamation — and so there come to be mountains that close together, stones that press together, grinding wheels and millstones, plowing and grinding — just as a slandering thief forces the innocent into wrong. The two press against one another, and so there come to be pressing and squeezing, pounding and pressing down, crushing and draining, and weighing and measuring. This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing resentful slander, alike calls it 'a tiger that spreads lies' — and why bodhisattvas see false accusation exactly as they would being struck by lightning."

Commentary: False accusation means forcing a wrong upon another, bringing calamity down upon them by force. Called a "habit" here because, from a former life, one was given greatly to false accusation, its seed-habit still remaining, now manifesting in present activity, this manifest habit added further still, each imposing upon and pressing the other in turn, before this karma can be formed. "Arises from slander and defamation" means that, though the matter never originally existed at all, it is slandered into existing, and defamed into being taken as real — and so there come to be mountains that close together, and the rest, already inherent within one's own mind. According to the Sūtra of Different Manifestations of the Scriptures and Vinaya: mountains that close together means a great stone mountain, two mountains facing one another; the offender enters between them, and the mountains close of themselves, grinding flesh and bone to pulp, and then the mountains return to their original place. Stones that press together means the offender is laid beneath a great stone, and the stone is then pressed down upon them, exactly as one presses grapes beneath a press. Grinding wheels are exactly great, blazing-hot iron wheels, crushing every offender until their bodies break apart; millstones are small grinding-stones. Plowing means plowing with a blade, splitting open the tongue. Grinding is exactly the stone-mill hell, where the offender is seized and thrown upon a scorching stone, their hands and feet spread out, and a great, scorching stone is pressed down upon their body, turned round and round to grind them. This is exactly this same instrument used to force a wrong upon another, first manifesting within one's own mind, indeed a fearsome thing! "Just as a slandering thief forces the innocent into wrong" offers this as an illustrative proof: a slandering thief — slander means to defame; thief means to harm — defaming another to their harm is called a slandering thief; forcing and pressing down upon the innocent, wrongly harming the good and blameless — this is exactly the same.

"The two press against one another, and so there come to be pressing and squeezing, pounding and pressing down, crushing and draining, and weighing and measuring, and every such matter." These two habits mean that the seed-habit still remains even as the manifest habit intensifies further, pressing against one another mutually, this false accusation never ceasing. And so there come to be, at the end of life, one's own consciousness feeling this pressing and kneading down, forcing one to confess to this offense. Should one still refuse to confess, one's own body is squeezed into a sack and pressed, draining out one's own blood, called "crushing and draining"; and one's own body is hung upon a scale, weighed for its lightness or heaviness, measured for its shortness or length, called "weighing and measuring." "And every such matter" gathers in everything else besides. The earlier mountains that close together, stones that press together, and the rest, was only the first budding of this same hell-mark within the mind; this present pressing and kneading, pounding and pressing down, and the rest, is the very accomplishment of this same karmic fruit, each self-made and self-received.

"This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing resentful slander, alike names it 'a tiger that spreads lies' — and why bodhisattvas see false accusation exactly as they would being struck by lightning." Because of the harm caused by this habit of false accusation, every Tathāgata of the ten directions names this same resentful harm and slanderous defamation alike "a tiger that spreads lies" — slanderous words causing harm graver even than a tiger's own attack. Bodhisattvas, seeing this matter of forcing a wrong upon another, are as one struck by a bolt of lightning. Lightning is the sound of a sudden thunderclap striking something; whoever is struck by it, without exception, has their very spirit scattered in terror! This closes the ninth section, the habit of false accusation.

Sūtra: "Tenth, the habit of litigation, in its mutual clamor, arises from concealment — and so there come to be mirrors that reveal, and beacons that illuminate — just as, at high noon, one cannot hide one's own shadow. The two lay bare one another, and so there come to be evil companions, the karma-mirror, and the fire-pearl, exposing one's past deeds for cross-examination. This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing concealment, alike calls it 'a hidden thief' — and why bodhisattvas regard concealment as carrying a mountain on their back while wading through a vast sea."

Commentary: Litigation is exactly contention and dispute; clamor is exactly this same accusation, raised aloud. Called a "habit" here because, from a former life, one was given greatly to litigation, its seed-habit still remaining, now manifesting in present activity, this manifest habit added further still, contending and accusing one another in turn, before this karma can be formed. "Arises from concealment" — concealment means hiding away; covering means keeping something covered over — hiding one's own offense, covering over one's own secret wrongdoing. And so there come to be mirrors that reveal, and beacons that illuminate: mirrors that reveal one's own karma from past lives; beacons that illuminate the very depths of one's own heart, so that nothing may remain hidden at all — this same mark of hell already previewed within one's own mind. This offers an illustrative proof of this: exactly like the shadow of what is crooked or straight, unable to be hidden at high noon.

"The two lay bare one another, and so there come to be evil companions, the karma-mirror, and the fire-pearl, exposing one's past deeds for cross-examination, and every such matter." These two habits mean that the seed-habit still remains even as the manifest habit intensifies further, this litigation and concealment laid bare against one another in turn — and so, at the end of life, one's own consciousness sees an evil companion — an evil companion who, in some former life, committed the same offense together, now appearing to bear witness. The karma-mirror is a mirror able to reveal past karma; the fire-pearl is a pearl able to illuminate the very depths of the heart, causing one's own past karma to be exposed and every such matter cross-examined — and this must be brought to light plainly, without the slightest trace able to remain hidden at all. The earlier mirrors that reveal and beacons that illuminate, was only the first budding of this same hell-mark within the mind; this present evil companion, karma-mirror, and fire-pearl, and the rest, is the very accomplishment of this same karmic fruit.

"This is why every Tathāgata of the ten directions, in describing concealment, alike names it 'a hidden thief' — and why bodhisattvas regard concealment as carrying a mountain on their back while wading through a vast sea." Because of the harm caused by this habit of litigation, every Tathāgata of the ten directions names this single matter of concealment alike "a hidden thief" — meaning that a household harboring a hidden thief will, in time, certainly suffer plunder; likening this to how concealing one's own fault must, in the end, become a source of harm. This is exactly how the Tathāgata gives this warning to others. Bodhisattvas, observing this same matter of covering over one's own fault, are as one carrying a mountain upon their own head, wading through a vast ocean on foot — never, in the end, able to emerge from it; the heavier the load carried, the deeper one sinks — likening this to how the more one's own fault is covered over, the graver this same offense becomes. Falling to this utmost suffering, with no way ever to escape it — this, too, is how bodhisattvas give this same warning to themselves. This matter of contention and litigation is something every sage and worthy alike takes warning from — one ought urgently to avoid it. The text's own repeated phrases, "the two kindle one another," "the two overwhelm one another," and the rest, show that only once cause and condition are both complete does this matter come to pass.

As the Standard Meaning Commentary puts it: one born through the lower grade of rebirth, though complete with this same cause of suffering, has the mark of the fiery cart appear before them; urgently reciting the Buddha's name, this hell-fruit is not, in the end, brought about — this being exactly what is meant by having the cause without the condition, and so not being born there after all. Yet such a one relies upon the Buddha's own power, not on their own wisdom and discernment. As for one who has awakened to their own mind: not only hell, but every entangling affair throughout — one ought, in one's ordinary life, to make every effort to cut off this cause entirely; and, further, one should remember, when death approaches, not to receive this condition — should there be some way to turn the body aside, then, when this dark realm appears before one, one need not follow it, and so may attain, in birth and death, some small measure of freedom — this truly requires the most careful self-examination! Should one's own strength of the path not yet be sufficient, unable to remain master of oneself, then reciting the Buddha's name and seeking rebirth there, relying further on another's own power, will, without fail, never go wrong. This is the very juncture of birth and death, and so it is set forth here in such detail — let the wise take this to heart and investigate it well. This closes the second section, posing this question and explaining these ten causes.

〖經〗云何六報？阿難！一切眾生，六識造業，所招惡報，從六根出。

〖講〗首句總徵。阿難下略釋。阿難！一切眾生：此指極惡眾生，須受地獄之報。六識造業，所招惡報，從六根出者：造業招報，根識不離；無根則識無所依，無識則根無所別。今分言之：六識造業者：以識有了別，故能造業；是則六識為能招，而惡報為所招矣。然業報從根出者：以此六根，元為賊媒，乃賊出入之所；既造業時從此入，受報時仍從此出也。初總徵略釋竟。

〖經〗云何惡報從六根出？一者見報，招引惡果：此見業交，則臨終時，先見猛火，滿十方界，亡者神識，飛墜乘煙，入無間獄。

〖講〗首二句躡徵。所招惡報，當分兩種：一自業報，二交業報。自業報者：如見業所招，臨終先見鐵床銅柱等；交業報者：如見火燒聽，能為鑊湯洋銅等。見火既爾，聽水亦然，故云從六根出。如一根之識造業，連帶諸根之識，例如眼見嬌嬈之色，必令耳聞柔軟之聲，鼻嗅脂粉之香，舌談情愛之語，身圖細滑之觸，意戀愛欲之樂。故受報時從一根重者為正，諸根隨者為從，交相受報；如法律家，判罪而分首從，同時各有處分也。

一者見報、招引惡果者：此詳釋見報等。謂眼識造業，所招引之惡果，從眼根而出。然以果驗因，是因中眼識，及眼傢俱意識，造業偏多，故招引惡果，偏以眼根為正，餘根為從。此見業交者：即見業與餘業交作，故云此見業交，則地獄之因成矣。見覺屬火，故當臨終之時，先見猛火，遍滿十方世界；亡者神識，飛墜乘煙，入無間獄。火性上騰，故或飛；諸情所墜，故或墜。落於煙中，乘此煙氣，直入無間地獄。須知極善極惡，皆無中陰，故云直入也。初臨終見墜竟。

〖經〗發明二相：一者明見，則能遍見，種種惡物，生無量畏；二者暗見，寂然不見，生無量恐。

〖講〗發明二相者：既入獄中，仍依見業，發明二相：一者明見：由在世於明塵上，明目張膽造惡，了無忌憚；今則遍見惡物，火蛇火狗，牛頭獄卒之類，生無量畏懼之心。二者暗見：由在世於暗塵中，瞞心昧己造惡，全不知羞；今則寂然不見，昏天黑地，寂無所見之境，生無量恐怖之心。二本根發相竟。

〖經〗如是見火，燒聽：能為鑊湯、洋銅；燒息：能為黑煙、紫燄；燒味：能為燋丸、鐵糜；燒觸：能為熱灰、鑪炭；燒心：能生星火、迸灑，煽鼓空界。

〖講〗如是上來所說，見報之火。按下諸科，當先有本根受報之文，今補之：燒見能為鐵床銅柱。因中所見花容玉貌，今為見火所燒，眼中但見鐵床銅柱之色。因中所聞嬌聲愛語，今為見火所燒，耳中但聞鑊湯洋銅之聲。因中所嗅龍涎麝香，今為見火所燒，鼻中但嗅黑煙紫燄之氣。因中所嘗資身補劑，今為見火所燒，舌中但嘗焦丸鐵糜之味。因中所觸冰肌玉體，今為見火所燒，身中但受熱灰爐炭之觸。因中所思吳姬越豔，今為見火所燒，心中能生星火、迸灑，煽鼓空界。此皆流逸奔色之報，有如是等苦，可不戒哉！初見報竟。

〖經〗二者聞報，招引惡果：此聞業交，則臨終時，先見波濤，沒溺天地，亡者神識，降注乘流，入無間獄。

〖講〗二者聞報，招引惡果者：此聞報，謂耳識造業，所招引之惡果，從耳根而出。然以果驗因，是因中耳識，及耳家之俱意識，造業偏多；故招引惡果，偏以耳根為正，餘根為從。此聞業交者：即聞業與餘業交作，故云此聞業交；則地獄之因成矣。聞聽屬水，故當臨終之時，先見波濤，沒溺天地。亡者神識，降注乘流，入無間獄：聞業殊勝。故應波濤沒溺天地，心欲上昇，奈為諸情所墜，故降注乘流，愈沉愈下，入無間獄。初臨終見墜竟。

〖經〗發明二相：一者開聽，聽種種鬧，精神潰亂；二者閉聽，寂無所聞，幽魄沉沒。

〖講〗發明二相者：既入獄中，仍依聞業，發明二相：一者開聽：即動塵，由在世於動塵上，聞一言相犯，即百般施設，欲害其身；今者聽種種鬧，精神昏亂也。二者閉聽：即靜塵，由在世於靜塵中，不聞相犯之言，但心生疑慮，陷害於他；今則寂無所聞，幽魄沉沒。二本根發相竟。

〖經〗如是聞波，注聞：則能為責、為詰；注見：則能為雷、為吼、為惡毒氣；注息：則能為雨、為霧，灑諸毒蟲，周滿身體；注味：則能為膿、為血、種種雜穢；注觸：則能為畜、為鬼、為糞、為尿；注意：則能為電、為雹，摧碎心魄。

〖講〗如是聞波，因中聞一言侮辱，即種種詰責，逾於常情。今則注聞，則能為責罪詰情之事。因中聞一言譏毀，即怒目裂眥，震聲吐氣；今則注見，則能為雷為吼，為惡毒氣。因中聞說，花香酒氣，鼻識妄生貪著；今則注息，水隨氣變，為雨為霧，灑諸毒蟲，周滿身體。因中聞說，山珍海錯，舌識貪嘗其味，百計網羅，以供口腹；今則聽水注味，則能為膿為血，雜穢之物。因中聞說，嬌蛾美女，身識貪戀其觸；今為聽水所注，則能為畜為鬼，可畏之狀，為糞為尿，不淨之相。因中由聞聲作惡，設計圖謀，出其不意，而害人者；今為聽水所注，化為電雹摧碎心魄。此皆流逸奔聲之報，有如是等苦，可不戒哉！二聞報竟。

〖經〗三者嗅報，招引惡果：此嗅業交，則臨終時，先見毒氣，充塞遠近，亡者神識，從地湧出入無間獄。

〖講〗三者嗅報，招引惡果者：此嗅報，謂鼻識造業，所招引之惡果，從鼻根而出。然以果驗因，是因中鼻識，及鼻家之俱意識，造業偏多；故招引惡果，偏以鼻根為正，餘根為從。此嗅業交者：即嗅業與餘業交作，故云此嗅業交，則地獄之因成矣。嗅息屬氣，具出入息，吸則從外而入，呼則從內而出，故臨終業感，先見毒氣，充塞遠近。以嗅業遍造，故亡者神識，從地湧出。謂初見毒氣，入地避之；奈毒氣充塞於地，故復從地湧出。九情所墜，不覺又沉，直入無間地獄。初臨終見墜竟。

〖經〗發明二相：一者通聞，被諸惡氣，熏極心擾；二者塞聞，氣掩不通，悶絕於地。

〖講〗發明二相者：既入獄中，仍依嗅業，發明二相：一者通聞：由在世於通塵上，嗅龍麝珍饈之香而造業；今則嗅氣所衝，變諸惡氣，熏極難忍，心神擾亂。二者塞聞：於塞塵中，不欲嗅臭穢之物，依之造業；今則嗅氣所衝，氣塞不通，悶極氣絕，昏臥於地。二本根發相竟。

〖經〗如是嗅氣，衝息：則能為質、為履；衝見：則能為火、為炬；衝聽：則能為沒、為溺、為洋為沸；衝味：則能為餒、為爽；衝觸：則能為爛、為大肉山，有百千眼，無量咂食；衝思：則能為灰、為瘴，為飛沙礫，擊碎身體。

〖講〗如是嗅報之氣，因中貪嗅色塵之香而造罪；今則衝息，則能為質為履，以鼻根屬息，氣見息而益惡，故為質證其罪，履踐其形。因中貪視美女之色而造罪；今則衝見，以見覺屬火，氣見火而成燒，故為火為炬。因中貪聞嬌嬈之聲而造罪；今則衝聽，以聽聞屬水，氣見水而成溷，故為沒溺於洋湯沸屎之中。因中貪求厭飫之味而造罪；今則衝味，以氣見味而成臭，故為魚敗之餒，羹敗之爽。因中貪求情愛之觸而造罪；今則衝觸，以身根屬觸，氣見觸而成殺，故為綻裂爛壞，為大肉山，有百千眼，無量蛆蟲咂食。因中貪求欲樂之思而造罪；今則衝思，以思動屬風，氣遇風而成揚，故為揚灰潑瘴，飛沙擲礫，擊碎身體，皆帶揚氣；此皆流逸奔香之報，有如是等苦，可不戒哉！三嗅報竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: "What are these six retributions? Ānanda, every sentient being creates karma through these six consciousnesses, and the evil retribution thereby summoned issues forth through these same six sense-faculties."

Commentary: The first sentence poses this total question; what follows, addressing Ānanda, briefly explains it. Ānanda, all sentient beings here refers specifically to those beings of the very gravest evil, who must undergo this retribution of hell. "Creates karma through these six consciousnesses, and the evil retribution thereby summoned issues forth through these same six sense-faculties" means that creating karma and summoning retribution never depart from the sense-faculty and its consciousness together — without the sense-faculty, consciousness would have nothing to depend upon; and without consciousness, the sense-faculty would have nothing by which to discriminate anything at all. Speaking of these separately: it is consciousness that creates karma, since consciousness alone is able to discern and distinguish, and so is able to create karma — this making these six consciousnesses what summons this retribution. Yet karma and its retribution issue forth through the sense-faculty, since these six sense-faculties are, at root, the very accomplices of this thief, the very gateway through which this thief comes and goes — entering through them when karma is first created, and issuing forth through them again when this retribution is received. This closes the first section, this total question, briefly explained.

Sūtra: "How does this evil retribution issue forth through these six sense-faculties? First, the retribution of sight draws forth its own evil fruit. When this karma of sight comes due, at the moment of death, one first sees fierce fire filling the ten directions; the departed consciousness, falling as though riding on smoke, plunges into the unrelenting hell."

Commentary: The first two clauses carry forward this question. This evil retribution thereby summoned properly divides into two kinds: first, retribution through one's own particular karma; second, retribution crossing into other senses. Retribution through one's own particular karma is such as this karma of sight summoning, at the very moment of death, the sight of the iron bed and the copper pillar first, described above; retribution crossing into other senses is such as this same fire of seeing, crossing into hearing, becoming cauldrons of boiling water and molten copper, and the rest. Since seeing this fire happens this way, hearing this water happens the same way as well — hence, "issues forth through these same six sense-faculties." Just as the consciousness of one sense-faculty creates karma, it inevitably draws along with it the consciousness of every other sense-faculty as well — as when the eye beholds some seductive form, this inevitably causes the ear to hear some soft, alluring voice, the nose to smell the fragrance of rouge and powder, the tongue to speak words of love and desire, the body to seek some smooth, delicate touch, and the mind to cling to the pleasure of lustful desire. And so, when this retribution is received, whichever sense-faculty is heaviest becomes the principal offense, and every other sense-faculty that follows along becomes an accessory to it, each receiving this retribution crossing into the other — exactly as a court of law, in judging an offense, distinguishes the principal offender from the accessory, each, at the same time, receiving their own separate sentence.

"First, the retribution of sight draws forth its own evil fruit" explains, in detail, this retribution of sight and the rest. That is, this consciousness of the eye creates karma, and the evil fruit thereby drawn forth issues forth through the eye-faculty. Yet, examining this cause through its fruit, this is because, within this cause, the consciousness of the eye, together with the mind-consciousness that accompanies the eye, created karma to an especially great degree — and so this evil fruit thereby drawn forth takes the eye-faculty, specifically, as its principal offense, and every other sense-faculty as its accessory. "This karma of sight comes due" means that this karma of sight interacts together with every other karma — hence, "this karma of sight comes due" — and it is this that completes this very cause of hell. Sight, this faculty of seeing, belongs to fire, and so, at the very moment of death, one first sees fierce fire, filling the worlds of the ten directions entirely; the departed consciousness, falling as though riding on smoke, plunges into the unrelenting hell. The nature of fire rises upward, and so one may, at times, seem to fly; yet every attachment pulls one down as well, and so one, at times, falls — falling into this same smoke, riding upon this same smoky vapor, plunging straight into the unrelenting hell. One should know that both the very best and the very worst alike pass through no intermediate state at all — hence, "plunges straight" into it. This closes the first section, seeing this fall at the moment of death.

Sūtra: "Two aspects then appear: first, bright seeing, in which one sees every kind of evil thing everywhere, giving rise to boundless dread; second, dark seeing, utterly unseeing, giving rise to boundless terror."

Commentary: "Two aspects then appear" means that, having already entered into hell, one still relies on this same karma of sight to bring these two aspects to light. First, bright seeing: because, in this present life, upon this bright sense-object, one committed evil openly, brazenly, without the slightest scruple at all — now, one sees every kind of evil thing everywhere, fire-serpents and fire-dogs, ox-headed hell-wardens and the like, giving rise to a mind of boundless dread. Second, dark seeing: because, in this present life, within this dark sense-object, one committed evil while deceiving one's own conscience, entirely without any shame at all — now, one sees utterly nothing, heaven and earth both dark and obscured, this realm utterly still, with nothing to be seen at all, giving rise to a mind of boundless terror. This closes the second section, the root faculty giving rise to this mark.

Sūtra: "This same fire of seeing, crossing into hearing, becomes cauldrons of boiling water or molten copper; crossing into breath, becomes black smoke and violet flame; crossing into taste, becomes scorched pellets and iron gruel; crossing into touch, becomes hot ash and burning coals; crossing into the mind itself, scatters sparks that fan and churn the whole of empty space."

Commentary: This same fire of seeing, spoken of above — according to the sections that follow, there ought first to come a passage on this same root faculty's own particular retribution; here, this is supplied: this same burning of sight, too, is able to become the iron bed and the copper pillar. What was, within this cause, seen as flowering countenances and jade-like beauty, is now, burned by this same fire of seeing, seen, within the eye, as nothing but the very color of the iron bed and copper pillar. What was, within this cause, heard as soft voices and words of love, is now, burned by this same fire of seeing, heard, within the ear, as nothing but the very sound of cauldrons of boiling water and molten copper. What was, within this cause, smelled as ambergris and musk, is now, burned by this same fire of seeing, smelled, within the nose, as nothing but the very scent of black smoke and violet flame. What was, within this cause, tasted as nourishing delicacies and restorative tonics, is now, burned by this same fire of seeing, tasted, within the tongue, as nothing but the very flavor of scorched pellets and iron gruel. What was, within this cause, touched as skin like ice and a body like jade, is now, burned by this same fire of seeing, felt, within the body, as nothing but the very touch of hot ash and burning coals. What was, within this cause, thought of as the beauties of distant lands, is now, burned by this same fire of seeing, able to give rise, within the mind, to sparks that scatter and fly, fanning and churning the whole of empty space. All of this is the retribution of streaming outward, chasing after form — such suffering as this — how could one not take heed and guard against it! This closes the first section, the retribution of sight.

Commentary: This retribution of hearing means that this consciousness of the ear creates karma, and the evil fruit thereby drawn forth issues forth through the ear-faculty. "This karma of hearing comes due" means that this karma of hearing interacts together with every other karma — and it is this that completes this very cause of hell. Hearing, this faculty of listening, belongs to water, and so, at the very moment of death, one first sees great waves and floods, engulfing heaven and earth. The departed consciousness sinks downward, swept along on this current, plunging into the unrelenting hell: this same karma of hearing being especially strong, one ought, by rights, to see these waves and floods engulfing heaven and earth, one's own mind wishing to rise upward — yet, dragged down by every attachment, one instead sinks downward, swept along on this current, sinking ever deeper, ever further down, plunging into the unrelenting hell. This closes the first section, seeing this fall at the moment of death.

Sūtra: "Two aspects then appear: first, open hearing, in which one hears every kind of clamor, one's own spirit and mind thrown into confusion; second, closed hearing, utterly silent, hearing nothing at all, one's own hidden spirit sunk into oblivion."

Commentary: "Two aspects then appear": having already entered into hell, one still relies on this same karma of hearing to bring these two aspects to light. First, open hearing — this is exactly this moving sense-object: because, in this present life, upon this moving sense-object, hearing a single offending word was enough to provoke every kind of scheme, wishing to harm that very person's own body — now, one hears every kind of clamor, one's own spirit and mind thrown into confusion and disorder. Second, closed hearing — this is exactly this still sense-object: because, in this present life, within this still sense-object, though hearing no offending word at all, one's own mind still gave rise to suspicion and doubt, and, through this, harmed another — now, one is utterly silent, hearing nothing at all, one's own hidden spirit sunk into oblivion. This closes the second section, the root faculty giving rise to this mark.

Sūtra: "This same wave of hearing, pouring into hearing itself, becomes rebuke and interrogation; pouring into seeing, becomes thunder, roaring, and poisonous vapor; pouring into breath, becomes rain and mist, sprinkling every venomous creature over the whole of the body; pouring into taste, becomes pus, blood, and every kind of filth; pouring into touch, becomes beasts, ghosts, excrement, and urine; pouring into the mind itself, becomes lightning and hail, shattering the very heart and spirit."

Commentary: This same wave of hearing: within this cause, hearing a single insulting word brought every kind of accusation and rebuke, exceeding all ordinary bounds — now, pouring into hearing itself, it becomes this same matter of accusation, rebuke, and interrogation. Within this cause, hearing a single slanderous word caused the eyes to blaze with fury, the voice to thunder and the breath to heave — now, pouring into seeing, it becomes thunder, roaring, and poisonous vapor. Within this cause, hearing of fragrant flowers and the scent of wine caused the nose-consciousness to give rise, falsely, to craving attachment — now, pouring into breath, this water follows this same vital energy and transforms, becoming rain and mist, sprinkling every venomous creature over the whole of the body. Within this cause, hearing of mountain delicacies and rare seafood caused the tongue-consciousness to crave their taste, scheming by every means to satisfy one's own appetite — now, this water of hearing pours into taste, becoming pus, blood, and every kind of filthy thing. Within this cause, hearing of beautiful maidens and lovely women caused the body-consciousness to crave their touch — now, poured over by this same water of hearing, it becomes beasts and ghosts, fearsome forms, and excrement and urine, impure marks. Within this cause, because of what was heard, one plotted some evil scheme, catching another off guard to do them harm — now, poured over by this same water of hearing, this transforms into lightning and hail, shattering the very heart and spirit. All of this is the retribution of streaming outward, chasing after sound — such suffering as this — how could one not take heed and guard against it! This closes the second section, the retribution of hearing.

Smelling, this breath drawn in and out, belongs to vital energy — the in-breath drawn in from without, the out-breath sent forth from within — and so, when this karma comes due at death, one first sees poisonous vapor, filling every distance, near and far alike. Because this same karma of smell was so thoroughly created, the departed consciousness rises up out of the very ground — that is, upon first seeing this poisonous vapor, one enters the ground to escape it; yet, since this poisonous vapor fills the very ground as well, one rises up out of the ground once more. Pulled down by these nine parts emotion, unaware, one sinks once again, plunging straight into the unrelenting hell. This closes the first section, seeing this fall at the moment of death.

Sūtra: "Two aspects then appear: first, open smelling, in which one is enveloped by every evil vapor, choked to the point that the mind itself grows disturbed; second, blocked smelling, this vapor obstructing entirely, suffocating one until one collapses upon the ground."

Commentary: "Two aspects then appear": having already entered into hell, one still relies on this same karma of smell to bring these two aspects to light. First, open smelling: because, in this present life, upon this open sense-object, one smelled the fragrance of ambergris, musk, and every delicacy, and, through this, created karma — now, one is struck by this same smelling-vapor, transformed into every evil vapor, its stench overwhelming, unbearable, one's own spirit and mind thrown into disorder. Second, blocked smelling: within this closed sense-object, unwilling to smell anything foul or impure, and, through this, created karma — now, one is struck by this same smelling-vapor, this vapor obstructing entirely, suffocating one to the very point of collapse, lying senseless upon the ground. This closes the second section, the root faculty giving rise to this mark.

All of this is the retribution of streaming outward, chasing after fragrance — such suffering as this — how could one not take heed and guard against it! This closes the third section, the retribution of smell.

〖講〗四者味報，招引惡果者：準前眼、耳、鼻三根，既云見聞嗅報，此應云當報；今云味報者，從所嘗以為名也。此味報，謂舌識造業，所招引之惡果，從舌根而出。以根為賊媒，引識奔走，故招引惡果，唯舌根而出。此味業交者：即味業與餘業交作，故云此味業交，則地獄之因成矣。蓋舌屬金，以在生時，為此舌根，貪嘗滋味，網捕禽獸，魚鱉之屬，故當臨終之時，先見鐵網，猛燄熾烈，周覆世界，因味造殺，無處不到，故亡者神識，下透掛網。因見鐵網，又見猛燄，欲身透過；不意下透掛網，倒懸其頭，入無間獄。初臨終見墜竟。

〖經〗發明二相：一者吸氣，結成寒冰，凍冽肉身；二者吐氣，飛為猛火，焦爛骨髓。

〖講〗發明二相者：既入獄中，仍依味業，發明二相：一者吸氣，從外而入，其氣必寒，故結寒冰，而凍冽身肉。二者吐氣，從內而出，其氣必熱，故飛猛火，而焦爛骨髓。此由因中，貪食眾生身肉所感。二本根發相竟。

〖經〗如是嘗味，歷嘗則能為承、為忍；歷見：則能為然金石；歷聽：則能為利兵刃；歷息：則能為大鐵籠，彌覆國土；歷觸：則能為弓、為箭、為弩、為射；歷思：則能為飛熱鐵，從空雨下。

〖講〗如是嘗味，所感之報。因中貪食眾生身肉，令彼承當忍受，含冤莫訴；今則歷於舌根之嘗，亦令為承當，為忍受，伏罪無辭。因中見彼眾生之被殺；今則歷於眼根之見，為見火所燒，眼中但見然金爍石之色。因中聽彼眾生之受烹；今則歷於耳根之聽，為聽水所盪，耳中但聞為利兵刃之聲。因中嗅彼眾生之香氣；今則歷於鼻根之息，為息氣所蒸，鼻中但聞為大鐵籠，彌覆國土之氣。因中貪食眾生之血肉；今則歷於身根之觸，為身根所對，能為弓箭弩射之觸。因中貪食眾生之脂膏；今則歷於意根之思，意思屬風，則能為飛熱鐵，從空雨下。此皆流逸奔味之報，有如是等苦，可不戒哉！四味報竟。

〖經〗五者觸報，招引惡果：此觸業交，則臨終時，先見大山，四面來合，無復出路。亡者神識，見大鐵城，火蛇、火狗、虎、狼、獅子。牛頭獄卒，馬頭羅剎，手執鎗銷，驅入城門，向無間獄。

〖講〗五者觸報，招引惡果者：此觸報，謂身識造業，所招引之惡果，從身根而出；以根為賊媒，引識奔走，故招引惡果，從身根而出。此觸業交者：即觸業與餘業交作，故云此觸業交，則地獄之因成矣。然觸業最重者，莫如貪淫強逼令其喪志失節，無所逃避。故當臨終之時，先見大山，四面來合，無復出路；蓋自身地、水、火、風，即是四山。因中以此逼人，今則轉為逼己也。亡者神識，見大鐵城，方喜躲避有處，又見有火蛇火狗，虎狼獅子等，則不敢入；奈有牛頭獄卒，馬頭羅剎，手執鎗銷，驅逼使入，不得不入。既入城中，即無間地獄也。初臨終見墜竟。

〖經〗發明二相：一者合觸，合山逼體，骨肉血潰；二者離觸，刀劍觸身，心肝屠裂。

〖講〗發明二相者：既入獄中，仍依觸業，發明二相：一者合觸，謂貪於合觸造業，因中見他美貌如玉，強合成事；故感合山逼體，碎骨和肉，與血而俱潰也。二者離觸，謂貪於離觸造業，因中見他色衰愛弛，棄離不顧；故感刀劍觸壞其身，令其心之與肝，俱受屠裂，而分碎也。二本根發相竟。

〖經〗如是合觸，歷觸：則能為道、為觀、為廳、為案（疑是錯簡，應是為撞為擊、為剚為射）；歷見：則能為燒、為爇；歷聽：則能為撞、為擊、為剚、為射（疑是與上互錯，應換為道、為觀、為廳、為案）。歷息：則能為括、為袋、為考、為縛；歷嘗：則能為耕、為鉗、為斬、為截；歷思：則能為墜、為飛、為煎、為炙。

〖講〗如是觸業，所感之報。強合之觸，歷於身根，則能為撞為擊，為剚為射。蓋撞以杵觸，擊以杖觸，剚以刃觸（插刃於肉也），射以箭觸，皆身觸遇逼事也。《指掌疏》以此八字，與歷聽之文對換。甚屬有理，故今依之。強合之觸，歷於眼根，眼見屬火，火逼迫，則為爇為燒；逼住，則為爇；逼不住，則為燒。此皆眼見遇逼迫事也。強合之觸，歷於耳根，耳根聽聞，則為道為觀，為廳為案。蓋道為地獄之路，多叫苦聲。觀為獄主之宮（宮門兩觀），多傳呼聲；廳為理獄之所，多審罰聲；案為判罪之據，多判結聲：皆耳聞遇逼迫事也。強合之觸，歷於鼻根，以鼻息而遇逼迫，則能為括為袋，為考為縛。蓋括以布纏，袋以囊閉，考謂括而復考，縛謂袋而復縛，務令氣不得伸，皆鼻息遇逼迫事也。強合之觸，歷於舌根，以舌嘗而遇逼迫，則能為耕為鉗，為斬為截。耕謂犁其舌，鉗謂拔其舌，斬謂斫其根，截謂斷其半：此皆舌嘗遇逼迫事也。強合之觸，歷於意根，以意思而遇逼迫，則能為墜為飛，忽上忽下之狀，為煎為炙，時熱時燥之苦，此皆意思遇逼迫事也。此皆流逸奔觸之報，有如是等苦，可不戒哉！五觸報竟。

〖經〗六者思報，招引惡果：此思業交，則臨終時，先見惡風，吹壞國土。亡者神識，被吹上空，旋落乘風，墜無間獄。

〖講〗六者思報，招引惡果者：此思報，謂意識造業，所招引之惡果，從意根而出；以根為賊媒，引識奔走，故招引惡果，唯意根而出。此思業交者：即思業，與餘業交作，故云此思業交，則地獄之因成矣。蓋思屬風，有善有惡，善思則能成，惡思則能壞；今約惡業，故臨終時，先見惡風，吹壞國土。亡者神識，無所依止，被吹上空，旋復為九情所墜，故從空落，乘於風力，不覺轉入無間地獄。初臨終見墜竟。

〖經〗發明二相：一者不覺，迷極則荒，奔赴不息；二者不迷，覺知則苦，無量煎燒，痛深難忍。

〖講〗發明二相者：既入獄中，仍依思業，發明二相；以思業有覺不覺，故感報亦然。一者不覺，即滅法塵，以其不覺故，迷悶之極，心神荒亂，奔走不息。二者不迷（即覺），即生法塵，覺知則是苦境，無量煎燒，痛深難可忍受。二本根發相竟。

〖經〗如是邪思，結思：則能為方、為所；結見：則能為鑑、為證；結聽：則能為大合石、為冰、為霜、為土、為霧；結息：則能為大火車、火船、火檻；結嘗：則能為大叫喚、為悔、為泣；結觸：則能為大、為小，為一日中，萬生萬死，為偃、為仰。

〖講〗如是邪思，所感報風，結於現前意根。以意思而遇報風，則能為方隅，為處所，即是受罪處也。結見者：以思業報風，結於現前眼根。以眼見而遇報風，則能為業鏡之鑑，為惡友之證，令其分明也。結聽者：思業報風，結於現前耳根。以耳聞屬水，若遇報風，二力俱勝，鼓激過分，則能為大合石，若風寒水冷，則能為冰、為霜，若水勢劣風，風勢劣水，則能為土為霧，如微旱起塵，帶濕見霧等。結息者：思業報風，結於現前鼻根。鼻息屬風，風遇風而磨盪，則能為大火車、火船、火檻，皆獄中苦具也。結嘗者：思業報風，結於現前舌根。舌嘗貪味，味遇風而便失，則能為大叫喚，為悔為泣，皆譏渴逼惱聲也。結觸者：思業報風，結於現前身根。身觸從緣，風性無定，觸遇風而展舒，則為大身；觸遇風而侷促，則為小身。觸遇風而忽活，則能為生；觸遇風而忽斃，則能為死。一日之中，巧風所吹，萬生萬死，或被風吹，而面俯於地，則能為偃，或被風吹，而面嚮於天，故能為仰。此皆流逸奔法之報，有如是等苦，可不戒哉！三徵釋六報竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: "Fourth, the retribution of taste draws forth its own evil fruit. When this karma of taste comes due, at the moment of death, one first sees a net of iron, fiercely blazing, covering the whole world; the departed consciousness plunges downward, hung by the head from the net, into the unrelenting hell."

Commentary: Compared to the sight, hearing, and smell of the eye, ear, and nose above, this ought properly to be called "taste's own retribution"; here, though, it is named "the retribution of taste," named according to what is savored. This retribution of taste means that this consciousness of the tongue creates karma, and the evil fruit thereby drawn forth issues forth through the tongue-faculty. Since the sense-faculty is this same accomplice of the thief, drawing consciousness along in its own pursuit, this evil fruit thereby drawn forth issues forth solely through the tongue-faculty. "This karma of taste comes due" means that this karma of taste interacts together with every other karma — and it is this that completes this very cause of hell. The tongue belongs to metal, since, throughout life, this tongue-faculty craved every savor, netting and trapping birds and beasts, fish and turtles alike — and so, at the very moment of death, one first sees a net of iron, fiercely blazing, covering the whole world; because this killing, brought about through taste, reaches into every place without exception, the departed consciousness plunges downward, hung upon this net. Seeing this net of iron, and further seeing this fierce blaze, wishing to pass one's own body straight through it — yet, contrary to this wish, one instead plunges downward, hung by the head upon this net, plunging into the unrelenting hell. This closes the first section, seeing this fall at the moment of death.

Sūtra: "Two aspects then appear: first, drawing in breath, which congeals into bitter, freezing ice, this cold splitting open one's own flesh; second, expelling breath, which flies forth as fierce fire, scorching and rotting one's own very marrow."

Commentary: "Two aspects then appear": having already entered into hell, one still relies on this same karma of taste to bring these two aspects to light. First, drawing in breath, entering from without — this breath must be cold, and so congeals into bitter, freezing ice, this cold splitting open one's own flesh. Second, expelling breath, sent forth from within — this breath must be hot, and so flies forth as fierce fire, scorching and rotting one's own very marrow. This is felt because, within this cause, one craved to devour the very flesh of other sentient beings. This closes the second section, the root faculty giving rise to this mark.

Sūtra: "This same savoring of taste, passing through taste itself, becomes bearing and enduring; passing through seeing, becomes molten metal and blazing stone; passing through hearing, becomes sharp weapons and blades; passing through breath, becomes a great iron cage covering the whole land; passing through touch, becomes bows, arrows, crossbows, and volleys of fire; passing through thought, becomes burning, scorching iron, raining down from the empty sky."

Commentary: This retribution felt through this same savoring of taste: within this cause, one craved to devour the very flesh of other sentient beings, forcing them to bear this and endure it, their grievance unable to be voiced — now, this passes through the very tasting of the tongue-faculty, likewise made to bear this and endure it, submitting to this offense with no argument left at all. Within this cause, one watched these sentient beings being killed — now, this passes through the very seeing of the eye-faculty, burned by this same fire of seeing, the eye beholding nothing but the very color of molten metal and blazing stone. Within this cause, one listened to these sentient beings being boiled and cooked — now, this passes through the very hearing of the ear-faculty, churned by this same water of hearing, the ear hearing nothing but the very sound of sharp weapons and blades. Within this cause, one smelled the very fragrance of these sentient beings — now, this passes through the very breath of the nose-faculty, steamed by this same vapor of breath, the nose smelling nothing but the very scent of a great iron cage, covering the whole land. Within this cause, one craved to devour the very blood and flesh of these sentient beings — now, this passes through the very touch of the body-faculty, met by this same sense-faculty, becoming this same touch of bows, arrows, crossbows, and volleys of fire. Within this cause, one craved to devour the very fat of these sentient beings — now, this passes through the very thought of the mind-faculty; since this thought belongs to wind, it becomes burning, scorching iron, raining down from the empty sky. All of this is the retribution of streaming outward, chasing after flavor — such suffering as this — how could one not take heed and guard against it! This closes the fourth section, the retribution of taste.

Sūtra: "Fifth, the retribution of touch draws forth its own evil fruit. When this karma of touch comes due, at the moment of death, one first sees a great mountain closing in from all four sides, with no way out at all. The departed consciousness sees a great iron city, fire-snakes and fire-dogs, tigers, wolves, and lions; ox-headed hell-wardens and horse-headed rākṣasas, spears and pikes in hand, drive one through the gate toward the unrelenting hell."

Commentary: This retribution of touch means that this consciousness of the body creates karma, and the evil fruit thereby drawn forth issues forth through the body-faculty; since the sense-faculty is this same accomplice of the thief, drawing consciousness along in its own pursuit, this evil fruit thereby drawn forth issues forth through the body-faculty. "This karma of touch comes due" means that this karma of touch interacts together with every other karma — and it is this that completes this very cause of hell. Yet this being the very worst offense of touch, nothing surpasses forcing sexual desire upon another, destroying their own resolve and integrity, leaving them with nowhere to flee — and so, at the moment of death, one first sees a great mountain closing in from all four sides, with no way out at all; for one's own body's earth, water, fire, and wind are themselves these four mountains — within this cause, one used this to force another; now, this turns back to force oneself instead. The departed consciousness, just now rejoicing to find some place to hide, sees a great iron city, and, further, fire-snakes, fire-dogs, tigers, wolves, and lions — and dares not enter it; yet ox-headed hell-wardens and horse-headed rākṣasas, spears and pikes in hand, drive and force one to enter, and one has no choice but to enter. Once entered within this city, this is exactly the unrelenting hell itself. This closes the first section, seeing this fall at the moment of death.

Sūtra: "Two aspects then appear: first, contact through pressing together, in which the closing mountain presses upon the body, bone and flesh crushed, blood bursting forth; second, contact through cutting apart, in which sword and blade cut into the body, heart and liver alike torn and severed."

Commentary: "Two aspects then appear": having already entered into hell, one still relies on this same karma of touch to bring these two aspects to light. First, contact through pressing together: this refers to craving this contact of pressing together, and, through this, creating karma — within this cause, seeing another's beauty, like jade itself, one forced this union upon them — and so one now feels this closing mountain pressing upon the body, bone crushed and flesh mingled, together with blood, bursting forth entirely. Second, contact through cutting apart: this refers to craving this contact of cutting apart, and, through this, creating karma — within this cause, seeing another's beauty fade, one's own love growing cold, one abandoned and cast them aside without a thought — and so one now feels sword and blade cutting into and destroying the body, causing one's own heart and liver, both alike, to be torn and severed, broken apart entirely. This closes the second section, the root faculty giving rise to this mark.

Sūtra: "This same contact of pressing together, passing through touch itself, becomes striking with a pestle, beating with a rod, stabbing with a blade, and shooting with an arrow; passing through seeing, becomes burning and scorching; passing through hearing, becomes roads, watchtowers, halls, and courts of judgment; passing through breath, becomes binding and swaddling, torture and confinement; passing through taste, becomes plowing, wrenching, chopping, and severing; passing through thought, becomes falling, flying, frying, and roasting."

Commentary: This retribution felt through this same karma of touch: this forced union, passing through the body-faculty, becomes striking with a pestle, beating with a rod, stabbing with a blade, and shooting with an arrow. Striking is contact by a pestle; beating is contact by a rod; stabbing is contact by a blade — piercing the very flesh with a blade; and shooting is contact by an arrow — all of these being this same body's own contact, meeting this same force. As the Detailed Commentary notes, these eight characters ought properly to be exchanged with the text belonging to hearing below — and this seems quite reasonable, and so is followed here. This forced union, passing through the eye-faculty — since sight belongs to fire, and fire forces and presses — becomes scorching and burning: pressed but not yet consumed, this is scorching; pressed until consumed entirely, this is burning — all of this being this same eye's own seeing, meeting this same force. This forced union, passing through the ear-faculty, this hearing and listening, becomes roads, watchtowers, halls, and courts of judgment. A road is this same road within hell, filled with cries of suffering; a watchtower is this same palace of the lord of hell, its twin gate-towers filled with the sound of calling and summoning; a hall is this same place where hell's own cases are heard, filled with the sound of interrogation and punishment; and a court of judgment is this same basis on which an offense is sentenced, filled with the sound of judgment passed and sentence concluded — all of this being this same ear's own hearing, meeting this same force. This forced union, passing through the nose-faculty, this breath meeting this force, becomes binding and swaddling, torture and confinement. Binding is wrapped about with cloth; swaddling is sealed within a sack; torture means bound and then interrogated further; and confinement means sacked and then bound further still, in every case ensuring that one's own breath cannot extend at all — all of this being this same nose's own breath, meeting this same force. This forced union, passing through the tongue-faculty, this tasting meeting this force, becomes plowing, wrenching, chopping, and severing. Plowing means plowing the very tongue; wrenching means tearing out the very tongue; chopping means hacking at its very root; and severing means cutting it fully in half — all of this being this same tongue's own tasting, meeting this same force. This forced union, passing through the mind-faculty, this thought meeting this force, becomes falling and flying — the mark of suddenly rising, suddenly falling — and frying and roasting — this suffering of being, at turns, scorching hot and parched dry — all of this being this same mind's own thought, meeting this same force. All of this is the retribution of streaming outward, chasing after touch — such suffering as this — how could one not take heed and guard against it! This closes the fifth section, the retribution of touch.

Sūtra: "Sixth, the retribution of thought draws forth its own evil fruit. When this karma of thought comes due, at the moment of death, one first sees an evil wind, destroying the land entirely. The departed consciousness, blown up into the empty sky, turns and falls, riding upon this same wind, plunging into the unrelenting hell."

Commentary: This retribution of thought means that this consciousness of the mind creates karma, and the evil fruit thereby drawn forth issues forth through the mind-faculty; since the sense-faculty is this same accomplice of the thief, drawing consciousness along in its own pursuit, this evil fruit thereby drawn forth issues forth solely through the mind-faculty. "This karma of thought comes due" means that this karma of thought interacts together with every other karma — and it is this that completes this very cause of hell. Thought belongs to wind, and may be either good or evil: good thought is able to accomplish, and evil thought is able to destroy — here, spoken of in terms of this evil karma, at the moment of death, one first sees an evil wind, destroying the land entirely. The departed consciousness, with nothing left to rely upon, is blown up into the empty sky, and, once more pulled down by these nine parts emotion, falls back down from the sky, riding upon this same force of wind, unaware, turning and plunging into the unrelenting hell. This closes the first section, seeing this fall at the moment of death.

Sūtra: "Two aspects then appear: first, lack of awareness, this confusion reaching its very utmost, a frantic, ceaseless wandering; second, awareness itself, this knowing itself becoming suffering, endless burning and torment, pain too deep to bear."

Commentary: "Two aspects then appear": having already entered into hell, one still relies on this same karma of thought to bring these two aspects to light — since this karma of thought carries both awareness and its lack, this same retribution felt likewise follows suit. First, lack of awareness — that is, this mental object extinguished — since one lacks awareness, this confusion reaches its very utmost, one's own spirit and mind thrown into wild disorder, running and wandering ceaselessly. Second, awareness itself — that is, this mental object arising — this knowing itself becomes this same realm of suffering, endless burning and torment, pain too deep and too severe to ever bear. This closes the second section, the root faculty giving rise to this mark.

Sūtra: "This same deviant thought, congealing into thought itself, becomes a fixed place, a designated location; congealing into seeing, becomes a mirror of judgment and a witness to testify; congealing into hearing, becomes great colliding stones, ice, frost, mud, and fog; congealing into breath, becomes a great fiery cart, a fiery boat, and a fiery cage; congealing into taste, becomes great wailing cries, remorse, and weeping; congealing into touch, becomes growing large, growing small, ten thousand births and ten thousand deaths within a single day, lying face-down, and lying face-up."

Commentary: This same deviant thought, this retribution felt through this same wind: congealing into the mind-faculty as it presently stands — this thought meeting this wind of retribution, becomes a fixed place, a designated location — this being exactly the very place where this offense is punished. Congealing into seeing means that this same karma of thought, this wind of retribution, congeals into the eye-faculty as it presently stands — this seeing meeting this wind of retribution, becomes a mirror of judgment, and a witness of evil companions, making everything plain beyond doubt. Congealing into hearing means that this karma of thought, this wind of retribution, congeals into the ear-faculty as it presently stands — since hearing belongs to water, should this wind of retribution meet it, both forces at their fullest strength, stirred and churned to excess, this becomes great colliding stones; should the wind be cold and the water freezing, this becomes ice and frost; and should the water's force be weaker than the wind's, or the wind's force be weaker than the water's, this becomes mud and fog — as when a slight drought raises dust, or moisture meets it and fog appears. Congealing into breath means that this karma of thought, this wind of retribution, congeals into the nose-faculty as it presently stands — since the breath of the nose belongs to wind, wind meeting wind grinds and churns, becoming a great fiery cart, a fiery boat, and a fiery cage — all instruments of suffering found within hell. Congealing into taste means that this karma of thought, this wind of retribution, congeals into the tongue-faculty as it presently stands — the tongue's own tasting, craving flavor, this flavor lost the moment it meets this wind, and so becomes great wailing cries, remorse, and weeping — all these sounds of hunger, thirst, and desperate distress. Congealing into touch means that this karma of thought, this wind of retribution, congeals into the body-faculty as it presently stands — the body's own touch follows whatever condition it meets, this nature of wind having no fixed form; touch meeting this wind, stretched out, becomes a great body; touch meeting this wind, drawn in tight, becomes a small body; touch meeting this wind, suddenly revived, becomes life; and touch meeting this wind, suddenly extinguished, becomes death — within a single day, this skillful wind blowing, ten thousand births and ten thousand deaths; and, should this wind blow one's own face down toward the ground, this becomes lying face-down; and should this wind blow one's own face up toward the sky, this becomes lying face-up. All of this is the retribution of streaming outward, chasing after mental phenomena — such suffering as this — how could one not take heed and guard against it! This closes the third section, posing this question and explaining these six retributions.

〖經〗阿難！是名地獄，十因六果，皆是眾生，迷妄所造。

〖講〗此結前地獄十種之習因，六交之果報，若因若果，一一皆是，不了自心，迷於妄見，不達眾生相空；由此於諸眾生，起於妄情，十因六果，靡不資始乎此，故云所造。若能了知，循業妄發，所有因果，皆如夢中境界，夢時非無，及至於醒，了無所得也。

〖經〗若諸眾生，惡業同造，入阿鼻獄，受無量苦，經無量劫。

〖講〗上來所說地獄，因以習成，果以交報，大分因果，俱是如此。然地獄數有多少，苦有重輕，今略分析之。意謂若諸眾生，六根對十因，如是惡業，無不同造。前之所謂純情者，以此純情即沉，入阿鼻獄，此極重無間，分明獨為一獄，與下八無間有別，故留阿鼻不翻。入此獄中，諸苦備歷，故云受無量苦。若沉心中，有謗大乘等，劫盡更生十方阿鼻，故云經無量劫。

〖經〗六根各造，及彼所作，兼境兼根，是人則入八無間獄。

〖講〗此六根具造十因，但不同時，故云各造。及彼所作，兼境兼根者：謂除當根各造外，復兼餘境（即十因也）餘根故。是人則入八無間獄：即前九情一想，下洞火輪，身入風火，二交過地，輕生有間，重生無間，此即重生無間者也。

〖經〗身、口、意三作殺、盜、淫。是人則入十八地獄。

〖講〗此唯言身、口、意三，六根不交作故；唯言作殺、盜、淫，十因不圓造故。不交作、不圓造，各未盡其極，反顯上之墮阿鼻獄者，必是十因圓造，六根交作耳。入無間獄者，必是六根各造，十因互犯；是知此科之罪，較上輕微，對下則重；故曰是人則入十八地獄。準《泥犁經》，火獄有八，寒獄有十者是也。

〖經〗三業不兼，中間或為一殺一盜，是人則入三十六地獄。

〖講〗三業不兼者：謂身、口、意三，不全具也。中間或為：或者不定之辭，若錯落具陳，應有九句：一身口犯殺盜，二身口犯殺淫，三身口犯盜淫，四身意犯殺盜，五身意犯殺淫，六身意犯盜淫，七口意犯殺盜，八口意犯殺淫，九口意犯盜淫；方盡根境，各皆具二缺一之數也。是人則入三十六地獄：而受苦稍輕，劫數稍短矣。

〖經〗見見一根，單犯一業，是人則入，一百八地獄。

〖講〗見見一根者：於六根現見，止見一根。單犯一業者：於殺、盜、淫等，唯犯一業。是人則入一百八地獄：而受苦益輕，劫數益短矣。以上所論，因以圓兼者為重，單獨者為輕；果以因重者獄少，因輕者獄多也。二分別輕重竟。

〖經〗由是眾生，別作別造，於世界中，入同分地，妄想發生，非本來有。

〖講〗由是眾生，造業不同，受報亦別。如上五段，惡業不同，即別作別造也。所感果報，各從其類。於世界中，入差別同分地獄，以受其報也。此因前問，此諸地獄，為有定處，為復自然，彼彼發業，各各私受耶？故今答云：於世界中，入同分地，則非私受也明矣。前又疑問：此道為復本來自有，為是眾生妄習生起耶？今答云：妄想發生，非本來有，則是妄生也明矣。《正脈》云：夫生之由己，應悟滅亦由己。滅之何如？絕其惡業而已！學人慎勿聞其虛幻，遂忽略而不絕其業。當知虛幻，不但地獄，即今目前苦事，亦是虛幻，由前業力，宛然堅實，卒難得脫，卒得堪忍，豈可不自忖乎？是知佛慧，不可不領；而佛戒，亦不可不遵矣！初地獄趣竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: "Ānanda, these are named the ten causes and six fruits of hell — all of them brought about by sentient beings through their own confusion and falseness."

Commentary: This concludes what precedes: hell's ten kinds of habitual cause, formed into these six cross-referenced retributions as fruit — broadly speaking, both cause and fruit alike are, in every case, this same failure to understand one's own mind, this same confusion within false views, this same failure to penetrate the emptiness of every sentient being's own mark. Because of this, giving rise to this false emotion toward every sentient being, these ten causes and six fruits, without exception, all take their very origin from this — hence, "brought about." Should one be able to understand clearly that these arise falsely, following karma, then every cause and every fruit alike are exactly like the realm within a dream: while dreaming, this is not without reality; yet, once awakened, nothing at all is truly gained.

Sūtra: "Should sentient beings create this same evil karma together, in full, they enter the Avīci hell, undergoing boundless suffering across boundless kalpas."

Commentary: Everything said above concerning hell — its cause formed through habit, its fruit formed through crossing — broadly speaking, both cause and fruit alike are just this way. Yet the number of these hells varies, and their suffering differs in weight — let this now be briefly analyzed. The meaning is: should sentient beings, with these six sense-faculties matched against these ten causes, create this same evil karma in every combination, without exception, together — this being exactly what was earlier called "pure emotion," which, being pure emotion, sinks straight down into the Avīci hell, this gravest of all, the unrelenting hell, distinctly its own single hell, differing from the eight interrupted hells described below — hence the word Avīci is left untranslated here. Entering this hell, one undergoes every suffering in full — hence, "undergoing boundless suffering." And should there be, within that sinking mind, slander of the Great Vehicle and the rest, then, once one kalpa is exhausted, one is reborn once more in an Avīci hell of the ten directions — hence, "across boundless kalpas."

Sūtra: "Should each of the six sense-faculties create its own separate karma, and, in what is thereby done, both the sense-object and the sense-faculty crossed together, such a person enters one of the eight unrelenting hells."

Commentary: This means that these six sense-faculties, together, create these ten causes — yet not all at the same time — hence, "each creates its own." "And, in what is thereby done, both the sense-object and the sense-faculty crossed together" means that, beyond each sense-faculty's own particular creation, this is joined, further, with every other sense-object — that is, these same ten causes — and every other sense-faculty as well. "Such a person enters one of the eight unrelenting hells" refers exactly to what was described earlier: with nine parts emotion and one part thought, one sinks down through the fire-wheel entirely, the body entering this place where wind and fire cross, this being exactly this heavier case, reborn in the unrelenting hell.

Sūtra: "Should body, speech, and mind, these three, together commit killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct, such a person enters one of the eighteen hells."

Commentary: This speaks only of body, speech, and mind, these three, since these six sense-faculties do not all cross together in creating karma here; and it speaks only of committing killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct, since these ten causes are not all created in full here either. Not crossing together, and not created in full — in neither case has this reached its very utmost extreme — this, by contrast, reveals that whoever falls into the Avīci hell above must have created all ten causes in full, these six sense-faculties crossing together entirely; and whoever enters the unrelenting hell must have had each of the six sense-faculties create its own karma, these ten causes mutually transgressed. One thus knows that the offense described in this present section is lighter than what came before, yet graver than what follows — hence, "such a person enters one of the eighteen hells." According to the Sūtra on the Hells, there are eight hot hells and ten cold hells — these being exactly these eighteen.

Sūtra: "Should these three karmas not all combine together, but, among them, only killing alone, or stealing alone, be committed, such a person enters one of the thirty-six hells."

Commentary: "These three karmas not all combine together" means that body, speech, and mind, these three, are not all fully present together. "Among them, or" is a phrase of indeterminacy: were every possible combination spelled out in full, there ought to be nine such cases: first, body and speech committing killing and stealing; second, body and speech committing killing and sexual misconduct; third, body and speech committing stealing and sexual misconduct; fourth, body and mind committing killing and stealing; fifth, body and mind committing killing and sexual misconduct; sixth, body and mind committing stealing and sexual misconduct; seventh, speech and mind committing killing and stealing; eighth, speech and mind committing killing and sexual misconduct; and ninth, speech and mind committing stealing and sexual misconduct — only in this way are every sense-faculty and every sense-object, each, in every case, exhausted, two present and one lacking. "Such a person enters one of the thirty-six hells," the suffering undergone somewhat lighter, and the number of kalpas somewhat shorter.

Sūtra: "Should only a single sense-faculty be seen at work, and only a single karma alone be transgressed, such a person enters one of the hundred and eight hells."

Commentary: "Only a single sense-faculty be seen at work" means that, of these present six sense-faculties, only a single one alone is seen at work. "Only a single karma alone be transgressed" means that, of killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, and the rest, only a single karma alone is transgressed. "Such a person enters one of the hundred and eight hells," the suffering undergone all the lighter still, and the number of kalpas all the shorter still. Everything discussed above shows that, in terms of cause, what is complete and combined is graver, and what is solitary and alone is lighter; and, in terms of fruit, where the cause is graver, the hells are fewer, and where the cause is lighter, the hells are more numerous. This closes the second section, distinguishing these gradations of weight.

Sūtra: "And so it is that, because sentient beings, each creating their own separate karma, come, within this world, to enter this same shared portion of a common destiny — this arises entirely from false thought, and was never, from the very beginning, truly existent at all."

Commentary: Because of this, sentient beings, creating differing karma, receive differing retribution as well. As with the five gradations described above, this differing evil karma is exactly this same separate creation, each their own. The fruit thereby felt, each follows its own kind, entering, within this world, this distinct yet shared hell of common destiny, there to undergo this retribution. This answers this earlier question: do these hells possess some fixed location, or does each unfold entirely on its own, this karma each gives rise to differing from the next, each receiving its own retribution individually and privately? And so the answer now given is: entering, within this world, this same shared portion of common destiny — this is plainly not something received individually and privately at all.

And it was further doubted, earlier: do these destinies exist inherently, from the very beginning, or do they arise from beings' own false habitual tendencies? The answer now given is: this arises entirely from false thought, and was never, from the very beginning, truly existent at all — this being plainly this same false arising. As the Standard Meaning Commentary puts it: since this arising comes from oneself, one ought equally to awaken that its cessation, too, comes from oneself. And how does one bring it to cessation? Simply by severing this evil karma entirely! Let the student take care never to hear of this illusory, empty nature and, on that account, grow careless, failing to sever this karma. One should know that this illusory emptiness applies not only to hell, but that even this present suffering right before one's own eyes is, likewise, illusory and empty — yet, arising from this same power of past karma, it appears entirely solid and real, in the end difficult to escape, and difficult, in the end, to bear — how, then, could one fail to examine oneself over this? One thus knows that the Buddha's own wisdom must not go unheeded, and the Buddha's own precepts, likewise, must not go unheeded either! This closes the first section, the destiny of hell.

The Ten Kinds of Hungry Ghosts 辛二 諸鬼趣

Chinese Source

〖經〗復次，阿難！是諸眾生，非破律儀，犯菩薩戒，毀佛涅槃，諸餘雜業，歷劫燒然，後還罪畢，受諸鬼形。

〖講〗鬼者畏也，謂虛怯多畏，名之為鬼；此總標。地獄罪畢，入諸鬼趣，人皆錯認，合中陰與地獄，悉謂之鬼。今與辨明：一者、與中陰不同。蓋人之初死，極善極惡，皆不受中陰；若罪福皆劣，未即受生，倏然有身，名中陰身，此屬無而忽有之化生也。類多裸形三尺，自覺六根皆利，去來迅疾，無所隔礙，他觀如影而已。七日死而復生，長壽者不過七七，短者於二三七，即受生矣；此非鬼也。二者與地獄不同。地獄純是化生，而鬼趣則具胎、卵、濕、化四生，間有父母兄弟眷屬。但其勝者稱神，劣者為鬼；如下所列，十類是也。率皆不免饑虛，均屬餓鬼趣攝，此非地獄也。但與前之七情三想，沉下水輪，生於火際者不同；彼則由惡業直墮，此則由地獄餘報耳。

復次，阿難！是諸地獄，受罪眾生；非即謗也，破即犯也。不惟犯一切戒，而且謗一切戒為妄立，或言無罪無福，自陷陷人。律儀，通大小乘也。犯菩薩戒者：即大乘戒也，十重、四十八輕等；上二約毀戒言之。毀佛涅槃者：涅槃至理，大乘深教，今言毀者，或訶為虛妄，或斥為斷滅，此約毀乘言之。夫乘、戒二法，乃出苦之津樑，實成佛之樞要，互為緩急，尚且不可，何況俱毀？真為斷滅佛種矣！前云：若沉心中，有謗大乘，毀佛禁戒，即指此也。諸餘雜業者：此總指十習因，六交報等；諸餘雜業，墮在地獄，備嬰眾苦，如處猛火，故曰歷劫燒然。燒盡宿業之後，則前之純情無想，及九情一想等業，名為酬還罪畢。今重報雖畢，輕報當受，故受諸鬼形。初躡前起後竟。

〖經〗若於本因，貪物為罪，是人罪畢，遇物成形，名為怪鬼。

〖講〗此詳列諸鬼之趣。由前來造十習因，墮獄受報不同，上出為鬼，亦分十類：若於本因句，總貫十科，寄居於此：謂若於根本原因，以何種習造罪，今依餘習，成為何鬼。若於往昔，以貪求財物為罪者，是人受罪既畢，而出地獄，仍依貪習，遇物生貪，附之成形。故有依草附木，成精作怪之類，名為怪鬼。

〖經〗貪色為罪，是人罪畢，遇風成形，名為魃鬼。

〖講〗不言本因者，例上可知故。仍以貪名，貪為煩惱之首，冠一切業故，下皆準此。若於往昔，以貪求美色為罪者，是人受罪既畢，而出地獄，仍依淫習，心愛遊蕩，遇風成形，名為魃鬼。魃為女鬼，亦曰女妖，故呼女子之多淫者，為魃婦也。《神異經》云：魃鬼長二三尺，其行如風，所現之處必大旱。蓋以酷淫，則致陰陽不合，妖風能令雲雨不成也。

〖經〗貪惑為罪，是人罪畢，遇畜成形，名為魅鬼。

〖講〗若於往昔，以貪求誑惑為罪者，以是受罪既畢，而出地獄，仍依誑習，遇畜成形。故有狐狸野干，雞鼠成精之類，名為魅鬼。

〖經〗貪恨為罪，是人罪畢，遇蟲成形，名蠱毒鬼。

〖講〗若於往昔，貪求瞋恨為罪者，是人受罪既畢，而出地獄，仍依瞋習，懷恨在心，遇蟲成形。蟲謂毒蟲，如蟒蛇蜈蚣之類；附之蠱害於人，名蠱毒鬼。《灌頂》云：兩廣習妖術，令人成蠱脹者，即此鬼也。

〖經〗貪憶為罪，是人罪畢，遇衰成形，名為癘鬼。

〖講〗若於往昔，以貪憶宿怨為罪者，是人受罪既畢，而出地獄，仍依怨習，蓄惡欲雪，樂為衰敗，即遇衰成形。衰謂四時不正，陰陽衰敗之氣，散瘟行疫，名為癘鬼。

〖經〗貪傲為罪，是人罪畢，遇氣成形，名為餓鬼。

〖講〗若於往昔，貪求傲慢為罪者，是人受罪既畢，而出地獄，仍依慢習，常懷高舉，故遇氣成形。氣謂地上之氣，下有水火二輪，蒸熱發氣，升於虛空，希望高舉；故附之成形，無所主宰，不得祭祀，名為餓鬼。問：餓鬼，乃一趣總名，經云：腹大咽小，歷劫不聞漿水之名，常被焚燒，水能害己，何於慢者，獨受其苦耶？答：地獄十因，各隨業習，今受報既畢，仍依慢習，感報受生，遇氣成形，名為餓鬼。此乃總中之別，非獨苦耶？

〖經〗貪罔為罪，是人罪畢，遇幽為形，名為魘鬼。

〖講〗若於往昔，以求貪誣枉為罪者，是人受罪既畢，而出地獄，仍依枉習，趣逐暗昧，故即遇幽成形。幽謂幽隱暗昧，陰陽不分之氣，附此成形，乘睡魘人，令其氣不得伸，名為魘鬼。

〖經〗貪明為罪，是人罪畢，遇精為形，名魍魎鬼。

〖講〗若於往昔，以貪求邪見，妄作聰明為罪者，是人受罪既畢，而出地獄，仍依見習，而無正慧，故即遇精為形。精，謂日月精華之氣，附以成形，顯靈異於川澤，為魍魎鬼。魍，謂其形暗昧；魎，謂其形不定也。抱朴子曰：魍魎山精，形如小兒，獨足善犯人，又好學人聲，迷惑於人也。

〖經〗貪成為罪，是人罪畢，遇明為形，名役使鬼。

〖講〗若於往昔，以諂詐誘人，貪成己私為罪者，是人受罪既畢，而出地獄，仍依詐習，遇明為形。明：謂咒術也，附以成形，聽役使，以作禍福；不知因中，以詐成己，今為咒術之所役使也，名為役使鬼。

〖經〗貪黨為罪，是人罪畢，遇人為形，名傳送鬼。

〖講〗若於往昔，以貪求朋黨，助惡興訟，而為罪者，是人受罪既畢，而出地獄，仍依訟習，遇人為形。即環師所云，附巫祝而傳吉凶者，是也。良以因中結黨，傳遞隱暗之事，而訐露於人，今為鬼亦附人，發洩傳說，吉凶等事，即名傳送鬼。二詳列十類竟。

〖經〗阿難！是人皆以純情墜落，業火燒乾，上出為鬼。此等皆是自妄想業之所招引，若悟菩提，則妙圓明，本無所有。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: "Furthermore, Ānanda, these sentient beings, whether it be breaking the monastic discipline, transgressing the bodhisattva precepts, or reviling the Buddha's own nirvāṇa, and every other manner of mixed karma besides, are burned through kalpa upon kalpa — and, once this same offense is at last repaid in full, they come to take on the form of a ghost."

Commentary: "Ghost" means dread — that is, hollow, timid, and fearful, and so named a ghost; this states this generally. Once one's offense in hell is repaid in full, one enters this destiny of ghosts — and people commonly mistake this, conflating the intermediate state with hell, calling both alike "ghosts." Let this now be distinguished clearly. First, this differs from the intermediate state: for, at the very moment a person first dies, the very best and the very worst alike do not pass through this intermediate state at all; only should one's own merit and offense both be middling, not yet immediately reborn, does a body suddenly come to exist, named this intermediate-state body — this belonging to this same transformation-birth, arising suddenly from what was not there before. Such beings are, for the most part, naked, some three feet in stature, sensing that all six of their own sense-faculties are especially sharp, coming and going swiftly, without any obstruction at all, though appearing to others as nothing more than a shadow. Dying after seven days, they are reborn once more; the longest-lived last no more than forty-nine days, and the shortest, only fourteen or twenty-one — and then they are reborn. This, then, is not a ghost at all.

Second, this differs from hell: hell is entirely transformation-birth, while this destiny of ghosts is complete with all four kinds of birth — womb, egg, moisture, and transformation — at times possessing parents, siblings, and kin. Yet the superior among them are called spirits, and the inferior are called ghosts — as with the ten classes listed below. All, without exception, cannot escape hunger and emptiness, and all belong to this same destiny of hungry ghosts — this, then, is not hell either. Yet this differs, too, from that earlier case of seven parts emotion, three parts thought, sinking down beneath the water-wheel, born at the very edge of fire: that case falls directly, through evil karma alone; this present case arises, rather, from the remaining retribution left over from hell.

Furthermore, Ānanda, these sentient beings undergoing punishment in hell — "reviling" here means slandering; "breaking" here means transgressing. Not only transgressing every precept, but further slandering every precept as falsely established, or claiming that there is no offense and no merit at all, thereby sinking both oneself and others. "Monastic discipline" encompasses both the greater and lesser vehicles alike. "Transgressing the bodhisattva precepts" refers exactly to the precepts of the Great Vehicle — the ten grave and forty-eight lesser precepts, and the rest; these two speak in terms of violating the precepts. "Reviling the Buddha's own nirvāṇa": nirvāṇa, this ultimate principle, this profound teaching of the Great Vehicle — to revile it here means either denouncing it as empty falsehood, or condemning it as mere annihilation — this speaks in terms of reviling the vehicle itself. Now, vehicle and precept, these two dharmas, are exactly the very bridge by which one crosses beyond suffering, the very hinge by which buddhahood is truly accomplished; to treat either with more urgency than the other is already improper — how much more, then, to revile both together? This is truly to sever the Buddha's own seed entirely! The earlier text states: should there be, within that sinking mind, slander of the Great Vehicle, violation of the Buddha's own precepts — this is exactly what is meant here. "Every other manner of mixed karma" refers, in general, to these same ten habitual causes and six cross-referenced retributions, and the rest; every other manner of mixed karma, falling into hell, is subjected in full to every suffering there, as though dwelling within a raging fire — hence, "burned through kalpa upon kalpa." Once this past karma is burned away entirely, then that earlier karma of pure emotion without thought, and of nine parts emotion to one part thought, and the rest, is named repaid, this offense settled in full. Now, though this graver retribution is settled, this lighter retribution still remains to be undergone — and so one comes to take on the form of a ghost. This closes the first section, carrying forward what precedes and setting forth what follows.

Sūtra: "Should the very root cause be greed for objects, taken as this offense, then, once such a person's offense is repaid in full, encountering some object, they take shape from it — this is named the strange ghost."

Commentary: This lists, in detail, each destiny of these ghosts in turn. Having, in what preceded, created these ten habitual causes, falling into hell, undergoing this retribution differently according to each, now emerging as ghosts, these too divide into ten classes. The phrase "should the very root cause be" governs all ten sections together, situated here at the start: that is, according to whichever habit served as the root, original cause of this offense, one now, relying on this remaining habit, becomes whichever kind of ghost corresponds to it. Should one, in some former life, have taken greed for wealth and possessions as this offense, then, once such a person's own offense is repaid in full, emerging from hell, still relying on this same habit of greed, encountering some object gives rise to greed once more, attaching to it and taking shape from it. And so there come to be those who attach themselves to grass or wood, becoming spirits and working mischief, and the like — this being named the strange ghost.

Sūtra: "Greed for sensual beauty, taken as this offense — once such a person's offense is repaid in full, encountering wind, they take shape from it — this is named the drought-demon ghost."

Commentary: Not stating "the very root cause" here follows the pattern already established above, and so may be understood accordingly. This, too, is still named in terms of greed, since greed stands foremost among every affliction, crowning every karma there is — and every case that follows below should be understood the same way. Should one, in some former life, have taken greed for physical beauty as this offense, then, once such a person's own offense is repaid in full, emerging from hell, still relying on this same habit of lust, the mind loving to wander and roam freely, encountering wind, they take shape from it — this is named the drought-demon ghost. This drought-demon is a female ghost, also called a female specter — hence a woman given greatly to lust is called a "drought-demon wife." As the Sūtra of Spirits and Wonders puts it: this drought-demon ghost measures some two or three feet in length, moving as swiftly as the wind, and wherever it appears, a great drought is certain to follow. This is because such extreme lust causes yin and yang to fail to unite, this same demonic wind preventing clouds and rain from ever forming.

Sūtra: "Greed for deception, taken as this offense — once such a person's offense is repaid in full, encountering an animal, they take shape from it — this is named the charm ghost."

Commentary: Should one, in some former life, have taken greed for deceiving and confusing others as this offense, then, once such a person's own offense is repaid in full, emerging from hell, still relying on this same habit of fraud, encountering an animal, they take shape from it. And so there come to be foxes, wildcats, and jackals, chickens and rats that have become spirits, and the like — this being named the charm ghost.

Sūtra: "Greed for hatred, taken as this offense — once such a person's offense is repaid in full, encountering an insect, they take shape from it — this is named the venom-gu ghost."

Commentary: Should one, in some former life, have taken greed for anger and hatred as this offense, then, once such a person's own offense is repaid in full, emerging from hell, still relying on this same habit of anger, holding this hatred within the heart, encountering an insect, they take shape from it. "Insect" here means a venomous insect, such as the python, the snake, or the centipede; attaching itself to such creatures, it causes gu-poisoning, harming people — this being named the venom-gu ghost. As the Consecration Commentary puts it: the deviant arts practiced in the two Guang provinces, causing people to develop gu-swelling, are exactly this same ghost.

Sūtra: "Greed for brooding, taken as this offense — once such a person's offense is repaid in full, encountering decline, they take shape from it — this is named the plague ghost."

Commentary: Should one, in some former life, have taken greed for brooding over old grudges as this offense, then, once such a person's own offense is repaid in full, emerging from hell, still relying on this same habit of grudges, harboring evil, wishing to avenge this wrong, delighting in decline and misfortune, and so, encountering decline, they take shape from it. "Decline" means this untimely energy of the four seasons, this same energy of yin and yang in decay, spreading pestilence and carrying plague — this being named the plague ghost.

Sūtra: "Greed for pride, taken as this offense — once such a person's offense is repaid in full, encountering vital energy, they take shape from it — this is named the hungry ghost proper."

Commentary: Should one, in some former life, have taken greed for arrogance and pride as this offense, then, once such a person's own offense is repaid in full, emerging from hell, still relying on this same habit of arrogance, constantly harboring this wish to rise above others, and so, encountering vital energy, they take shape from it. "Vital energy" means the energy rising from the earth: below it lie the water-wheel and the fire-wheel, their heat steaming and giving off vapor, rising into the empty sky, aspiring to rise ever higher — and so, attaching to it, they take shape from it, possessing no ruler over themselves, receiving no offering or sacrifice — this being named the hungry ghost.

One might ask: "hungry ghost" is properly the general name for this entire destiny; the sūtra elsewhere states that such beings have swollen bellies and narrow throats, unable, across kalpas, even to hear the name of drink, constantly scorched by fire, harmed even by water — why, then, does only this class, born of arrogance, alone bear this suffering specifically? The answer: these ten causes of hell each follow their own particular karmic habit; now, having repaid this offense in full, still relying on this habit of arrogance, this retribution felt in being reborn, encountering vital energy, they take shape from it, and are named the hungry ghost. This is simply this same specific instance within this general class — how could it be the only one to suffer in this way?

Sūtra: "Greed for deceiving by scheming, taken as this offense — once such a person's offense is repaid in full, encountering darkness, they take shape from it — this is named the nightmare ghost."

Commentary: Should one, in some former life, have sought after this greed for false accusation as this offense, then, once such a person's own offense is repaid in full, emerging from hell, still relying on this same habit of false accusation, drawn toward darkness and obscurity, and so, encountering darkness, they take shape from it. "Darkness" means this hidden, obscure energy, where yin and yang cannot be distinguished; attaching to it, they take shape from it, pressing down upon a person while asleep, causing their own breath to be unable to move freely — this being named the nightmare ghost.

Sūtra: "Greed for [false] insight, taken as this offense — once such a person's offense is repaid in full, encountering an essence-spirit, they take shape from it — this is named the shade ghost."

Commentary: Should one, in some former life, have taken greed for deviant views, falsely posing as clever and wise, as this offense, then, once such a person's own offense is repaid in full, emerging from hell, still relying on this same habit of wrong views, lacking correct wisdom, and so, encountering an essence-spirit, they take shape from it. "Essence" here means this same energy, the very essence of the sun and moon; attaching to it, they take shape from it, manifesting strange, spiritual phenomena among rivers and marshes, becoming the shade ghost. "Shade" [魍] means that its form is dim and obscure; "shade" [魎] means that its form has no fixed shape. As Ge Hong puts it: this shade-ghost, this mountain-essence, takes the form of a small child, standing on a single foot, delighting in provoking people, and, further, fond of imitating the human voice, thereby confusing and misleading them.

Sūtra: "Greed for accomplishment, taken as this offense — once such a person's offense is repaid in full, encountering brightness, they take shape from it — this is named the servant ghost."

Commentary: Should one, in some former life, have used flattery and deceit to entice others, taking greed for accomplishing one's own private ends as this offense, then, once such a person's own offense is repaid in full, emerging from hell, still relying on this same habit of deceit, encountering brightness, they take shape from it. "Brightness" here means mantras and spells; attaching to them, taking shape from them, obeying whatever service is demanded of them, bringing about fortune or misfortune for others — not realizing that, within this cause, they used deceit to accomplish their own private ends, and are now, in turn, made to serve at the beck and call of these same mantras and spells — this being named the servant ghost.

Sūtra: "Greed for faction, taken as this offense — once such a person's offense is repaid in full, encountering a person, they take shape through them — this is named the courier ghost."

Commentary: Should one, in some former life, have taken greed for gathering factions, aiding evil and stirring up litigation, as this offense, then, once such a person's own offense is repaid in full, emerging from hell, still relying on this same habit of litigation, encountering a person, they take shape through them. This is exactly what Master Huan means by attaching to a medium or diviner, thereby transmitting good or ill fortune. This is simply because, within this cause, one gathered together in factions, passing along hidden, secret matters, exposing them to others; now, become a ghost, they likewise attach themselves to a person, giving voice to and transmitting matters of fortune and misfortune — this being named the courier ghost. This closes the second section, listing each of these ten classes in detail.

Sūtra: "Ānanda, all of these beings fall this way through pure emotion, their karmic fire burning them dry, so that they rise up out of the hells to become ghosts — every one of them self-summoned by their own false, deluded karma. Awaken to bodhi, and this wondrous, perfect brightness was never, in truth, anything at all."

Commentary: This traces the very root of this destiny of ghosts, cause and fruit together. Ānanda, these beings of these ten kinds of ghosts, every one of them, through this pure emotion without thought, fell into the Avīci hell; or, through nine parts emotion, one part thought, fell into one of the unrelenting hells; or, through eight parts emotion, two parts thought, fell into one of the interrupted hells; and it is only once this karmic fire, this suffering, has burned this water of false emotion dry that they rise up out of these hells to become ghosts. Among the three lower destinies, emerging from hell is, relatively speaking, a rising upward — this traces this very root. "Every one of these" below reveals this same falseness: every one of these ghosts, alike, arises entirely from one's own mind's false thought and distortion, following karma and manifesting accordingly — nothing more than this. Should one awaken to this true nature, bodhi itself, it is exactly as waking from a dream — there is nothing at all left to be gained. "Wondrous, perfect, bright": suffering unable to bind it is called wondrous; karma unable to obstruct it is called perfect; and delusion unable to obscure it is called bright — and so this whole destiny of ghosts is, at root, nothing at all! This closes the second section, this destiny of ghosts.

Chinese Source

〖經〗復次，阿難！鬼業既盡，則情與想，二俱成空，方於世間，與元負人，冤對相值，身為畜生，酬其宿債。

〖講〗此總標畜生趣。以其秉性愚蠢，不能自立，多賴於人，畜養而生，故名畜生。又名旁生，此道多屬覆身旁行，故曰旁生。復次，阿難！鬼業既盡者：諸鬼之業報，苦火之焚燒，燒得既盡，則情與想，二俱成空，即超出鬼趣情想，故曰二俱成空；此躡前也。方於下，此起後：方於世間，與彼元有負欠財物，或形命之人，冤家對頭，互相值遇，業債難逃，身為畜生，或被宰殺，或受驅使，償命償財，故曰酬其宿債。初躡前起後竟。

〖經〗物怪之鬼，物銷報盡，生於世間，多為梟類。

〖講〗物怪之鬼者：昔因貪習，遇物成形，名為怪鬼。今則所附之物已銷，所受之報亦盡，形謝苦終，生於世間。因有貪物為怪餘習，多為梟類。梟則附塊成形，即貪物餘習；以子食母，即怪鬼餘習；大率如是，故云多為。以一例餘，下則準此。

〖經〗風魃之鬼，風銷報盡；生於世間，多為咎徵，一切異類。

〖講〗風魃之鬼者：昔因淫習，遇風成形，名為魃鬼。今則所附之風已銷，所受之報亦盡，形謝苦終，生於世間。因有貪色，為魃餘習，多為咎徵，一切異類。咎徵者：乃凶事之前兆，如商羊舞水，石燕飛風等，即為魃鬼兆災餘習。異類者：或為色禽，或為淫獸，即貪色餘習；一切異類句，當屬此科。下第九科，多為休徵，與此同。十科均以類字，結尾也。

〖經〗畜魅之鬼，畜死報盡，生於世間，多為狐類。

〖講〗畜魅之鬼者：昔因誑習，遇畜成形，名為魅鬼。今則所附之畜已死，所受之報亦盡，形謝苦終，生於世間。因有貪誑為魅餘習，多為狐類。狐狸為妖獸，改形變幻，迷惑於人，即貪誑餘習。千歲狐為淫婦，百歲狐為美女，即魅鬼餘習。

〖經〗蟲蠱之鬼，蠱滅報盡，生於世間，多為毒類。

〖講〗蟲蠱之鬼者：昔因瞋習，遇蟲成形，名蠱毒鬼。今則所附之蟲已滅，所受之報亦盡。形謝苦終，生於世間。因有貪瞋，為蠱餘習，多為毒類，如蚖蛇蝮蠍，蜈蚣蚰蜓等，時時蓄毒，即貪瞋餘習；或無故便螫，或觸之乃傷，皆蠱鬼餘習。

〖經〗衰癘之鬼，衰窮報盡，生於世間，多為蛔類。

〖講〗衰癘之鬼者：昔因冤習，遇衰成形，名為癘鬼。今則所附之衰已窮，所受之報亦盡，形謝苦終，生於世間。因有貪憶，為癘餘習，多為蛔類。蛔，謂腸胃中蟲，因閉身中，即貪憶餘習；乘癩而生，致結蛔症，即為癘鬼餘習。

〖經〗受氣之鬼氣銷報盡，生於世間，多為食類。

〖講〗受氣之鬼者：昔因慢習，遇氣成形，名為餓鬼。今則所附之氣已銷，所受之報亦盡，形謝苦終，生於世間。因有貪慢，為餓餘習，多為食類。食類者：世人不知，凡屬有命，盡是貪生，以豬、羊、雞、鴨、魚、鱉之類，視為可食。由是傲慢，尊己陵他，恣意食噉，今亦以身肉，供人之食。斯則以報酬報，無怪其然也。

〖經〗綿幽之鬼，幽銷報盡，生於世間，多為服類。

〖講〗綿幽之鬼者：纏綿幽暗，昔因枉習，遇幽為形，名為魘鬼。今則所附之幽已銷，所受之報亦盡，形謝苦終，生於世間。因有貪枉，為魘餘習，多為服類。《指掌疏》引《異物誌》云：山鴞體有文色，土俗因形名之曰服，亦名隻狐，晝伏夜出，亦名禍鳥，鳴則有禍。蓋晝伏夜出，即貪枉餘習；鳴則有禍，即魘鬼餘習。又服類其義有二：一、為蠶蟲貂鼠等類，供人衣服。二、為驢馬駱駝等類，供人乘服。由其貪枉為罪，遇幽為魘之餘習；今為服類，酬償先債也。

〖經〗和精之鬼，和銷報盡，生於世間，多為應類。

〖講〗和精之鬼者：昔因見習，遇精為形，名魍魎鬼。今者，所和之精既銷，所受之報亦盡。形謝苦終，生於世間。因有貪明魍魎餘習，多為應類，如春燕秋鴻等，知時知節，即貪明餘習，忽南忽北，即魍魎餘習。

〖經〗明靈之鬼，明滅報盡，生於世間，多為休徵，一切諸類。

〖講〗明靈之鬼者：藉明顯靈之鬼，明即神咒，昔因詐習，遇明為形，名役使鬼。今則所附之明力已滅，所受之果報亦盡，形謝苦終，生於世間，多為休徵，一切諸類。休徵者：吉兆之前驗也。因有詐習，貪成役使餘習，如嘉鳳祥麟等。兆休明，徵聖治，即貪成餘習。諸類者：如靈禽文獸等，識語言，隨呼喚，即役使餘習。

〖經〗依人之鬼，人亡報盡，生於世間，多為循類。

〖講〗依人之鬼者：昔因訟習，貪黨為罪，遇人為形，名傳送鬼。環師謂：附巫祝而傳吉凶是也。今則所依之人既亡，所受之報亦盡，形謝苦終，生於世間。因有貪黨傳送餘習，多為循類。循者順也，為人畜養，循順於人，如犬鴿之類。良以因中結黨，洩露人之隱惡，次則為鬼，附巫傳送，是其餘習。今則為犬為鴿，黨惡受教，傳遞消息，亦餘習之使然也。問：如來總標，畜生原為酬債，竊觀此中，多有蕭散之物，如麟鳳鴻燕等類，畢竟酬何債耶？答：或被網羅售賣，或被籠繫玩好，或因捕而致命，或因閉而喪生，則何往而非酬債耶？二詳列十類竟。

〖經〗阿難！是等皆以業火乾枯，酬其宿債，旁為畜生。此等亦皆自虛妄業之所招引，若悟菩提，則此妄緣，本無所有。

〖講〗阿難！是等：是十類畜生等，皆以地獄、鬼趣，業報苦火燒盡，二趣妄情，今得乾枯，生於世間，償其宿債，披毛戴角，旁為畜生，亦為旁生，然旁生類寬，畜生類狹；以畜者養也，明其無力自活，待人畜養，不該有力自養者，故狹也。此等十類畜生，亦皆是虛妄惑業之所招引，自作自受也。若悟得菩提真心，本來無一物，而此業報，猶如空華，故謂妄緣，本無所有。清淨心中，無能無所，譬若晴明空，如果眼中有翳，自覺空裏華紅，空本無華，病眼妄見，則見空華時，華本不曾生，翳病若除，則見華滅，生既無生，滅何所滅，即此不生不滅，是自性菩提。菩提人人本有，祗因妄緣障之也。菩提如何悟得，只要能緣之心不起，所緣之境自無，所謂狂心若歇，歇即菩提，勝淨明心，本週法界。三推本顯妄竟。

〖經〗如汝所言：寶蓮香等，及琉璃王，善星比丘，如是惡業，本自發明，非從天降，亦非地出，亦非人與。自妄所招，還自來受。菩提心中，皆為浮虛，妄想凝結。

〖講〗此牒前問，如汝阿難所言，即問寶蓮香等三人墮獄。如是惡業者：前阿難偏問地獄，今如來總答三途，明其同一罪罰之苦趣故也。《正脈疏》云：本自發明者：良以眾生自心，如來藏中，無所不具，倘自循何等之業，即自發明何者之報。譬如米中，諸味皆具，成糖、成醋、成酒，隨其造時，即自發明，不從外得。非從下乃至還自來受，正明不從外得也。此意，學人還須著眼，不同世間所說，自惹官刑之意。蓋世間雖知禍是自招，而猶執官刑乃是外境。今表三途，皆是自心，變化妄境，全如夢中，並無外物；故云菩提心中，虛妄凝結。

《指掌疏》云：自妄所招，還自來受，顯是我自致。菩提心中，浮想凝結，顯本無實體。據前阿難有總別二問；初總問七趣云：此道為復本來自有，為是眾生妄習生起？此云自妄所招，還自來受，是答以妄習生起。菩提心中，浮想凝結，是答以非本來有。次別問地獄云：此諸地獄，為有定處？為復自然？彼彼發業，各各私受？此云：自妄所招，還自來受，是答以自然，彼彼發業，各各私受。皆為浮虛，妄想凝結，是答以縱有定處，亦屬浮想凝結。良以前云：眾同分中，兼有元地，似有一定之處。故今並遣言，眾生初以一念妄動，業識潛興，名為浮想。次以見分俄興，結為現境，是曰凝結。為七趣所共依；呼為同分，若眾生之自招，乃稱別業。一人發真歸元，十方虛空，悉皆銷殞；況諸地獄鬼畜等，又在虛空中耶？是菩提心中，都無所有，果其徹證，無勞更疑。若乃未悟先空，只恐誤人自誤。古德云：了即業障本來空，未了仍須還宿債，可弗慎歟！三畜生趣竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: "Furthermore, Ānanda, once a ghost's own karma is exhausted, both emotion and thought together become empty — and only then, within this world, does such a being meet the very person to whom they originally owed a debt, this enmity meeting face to face, taking the body of an animal to repay this old debt."

Commentary: This states generally this destiny of animals. Because their own nature is foolish and dull, unable to stand on their own, relying for the most part on human beings, raised and fed by them for their own survival — hence named domestic animals. Also named "beings born sideways," since this destiny, for the most part, belongs to those whose bodies are covered and who walk sideways — hence, "born sideways."

"Furthermore, Ānanda, once a ghost's own karma is exhausted" — once this karmic retribution of every ghost, this suffering, this burning fire, has burned itself out entirely, both emotion and thought together become empty — that is, transcending this same emotion and thought of the ghost-destiny — hence, "both together become empty." This carries forward what precedes. "And only then" below sets forth what follows: only then, within this world, does such a being meet, face to face, the very person to whom they originally owed money or possessions, or life itself, this enmity, this counterpart, meeting one another — this karmic debt impossible to escape — and so they take the body of an animal, whether slaughtered by that person, or made to labor at their command, repaying either life or wealth — hence, "to repay this old debt." This closes the first section, carrying forward what precedes and setting forth what follows.

Sūtra: "The strange ghost, once the object it attached to has dissolved and its retribution is exhausted, is born within this world, for the most part, as one of the owl-like kind."

Commentary: "The strange ghost" refers to that being who, in the past, because of this habit of greed, encountered some object and took shape from it, named the strange ghost. Now, the very object it attached to has dissolved, and the retribution it underwent is likewise exhausted, this form dissolved, this suffering ended, and it is born within this world. Because of this remaining habit of greed for objects that made it a strange ghost, it becomes, for the most part, one of the owl-like kind. The owl attaches itself to a clod of earth to take shape — this being exactly this remaining habit of greed for objects; and its young devour their own mother — this being exactly this remaining habit of the strange ghost. This holds, broadly, in most cases — hence, "for the most part." Taking this one case as an example for the rest, what follows below should be understood the same way.

Sūtra: "The drought-demon ghost, once the wind it attached to has dissolved and its retribution is exhausted, is born within this world, for the most part, as an ill-omened creature, every strange and unusual kind."

Commentary: "The drought-demon ghost" refers to that being who, in the past, because of this habit of lust, encountered wind and took shape from it, named the drought-demon ghost. Now, the very wind it attached to has dissolved, and the retribution it underwent is likewise exhausted, this form dissolved, this suffering ended, and it is born within this world. Because of this remaining habit of greed for beauty that made it a drought-demon, it becomes, for the most part, an ill-omened creature, every strange and unusual kind. An ill-omened creature is a portent foretelling misfortune, such as the goat-spirit dancing over water, or the stone swallow flying on the wind — this being exactly this remaining habit of the drought-demon ghost's own foretelling of calamity. "Every strange and unusual kind" means either a bird of unusual color, or a beast given to lust — this being exactly this remaining habit of greed for beauty; this phrase, "every strange and unusual kind," belongs properly to this present section. The ninth section below, on creatures of good omen, matches this same pattern. All ten sections alike conclude with this word "kind."

Sūtra: "The charm ghost, once the animal it attached to has died and its retribution is exhausted, is born within this world, for the most part, as one of the fox kind."

Commentary: "The charm ghost" refers to that being who, in the past, because of this habit of fraud, encountered an animal and took shape from it, named the charm ghost. Now, the very animal it attached to has died, and the retribution it underwent is likewise exhausted, this form dissolved, this suffering ended, and it is born within this world. Because of this remaining habit of greed for deceit that made it a charm ghost, it becomes, for the most part, one of the fox kind. Foxes are creatures of deceit, changing and transforming their own shape, confusing and misleading people — this being exactly this remaining habit of greed for deceit. A fox of a thousand years becomes a lustful woman, and a fox of a hundred years becomes a beautiful maiden — this being exactly this remaining habit of the charm ghost.

Sūtra: "The venom-gu ghost, once the insect it attached to has perished and its retribution is exhausted, is born within this world, for the most part, as one of the poisonous kind."

Commentary: "The venom-gu ghost" refers to that being who, in the past, because of this habit of anger, encountered an insect and took shape from it, named the venom-gu ghost. Now, the very insect it attached to has perished, and the retribution it underwent is likewise exhausted, this form dissolved, this suffering ended, and it is born within this world. Because of this remaining habit of greed for anger that made it a gu-ghost, it becomes, for the most part, one of the poisonous kind — such as the viper, the snake, the scorpion, the centipede, and the earwig, constantly harboring venom — this being exactly this remaining habit of greed for anger; whether striking without provocation, or wounding only when touched — both, alike, this remaining habit of this gu-ghost.

Sūtra: "The plague ghost, once the decline it attached to has run its course and its retribution is exhausted, is born within this world, for the most part, as one of the tapeworm kind."

Commentary: "The plague ghost" refers to that being who, in the past, because of this habit of grudges, encountered decline and took shape from it, named the plague ghost. Now, the very decline it attached to has run its course, and the retribution it underwent is likewise exhausted, this form dissolved, this suffering ended, and it is born within this world. Because of this remaining habit of greed for brooding that made it a plague-ghost, it becomes, for the most part, one of the tapeworm kind. The tapeworm is this same worm within the intestines and stomach; sealed away within another's own body — this being exactly this remaining habit of greed for brooding; arising through this same disease, giving rise to this same knotted parasitic affliction — this being exactly this remaining habit of the plague ghost.

Sūtra: "The hungry ghost proper, once the vital energy it attached to has dissolved and its retribution is exhausted, is born within this world, for the most part, as one of the kind bred for food."

Commentary: "The hungry ghost proper" refers to that being who, in the past, because of this habit of arrogance, encountered vital energy and took shape from it, named the hungry ghost. Now, the very energy it attached to has dissolved, and the retribution it underwent is likewise exhausted, this form dissolved, this suffering ended, and it is born within this world. Because of this remaining habit of greed for arrogance that made it a hungry ghost, it becomes, for the most part, one of the kind bred for food. As for this kind bred for food: people of this world do not realize that every living creature alike clings to its own life — and yet pigs, sheep, chickens, ducks, fish, and turtles are, in every case, regarded as fit to eat. Because of this same arrogance, exalting oneself and demeaning others, one indulges without restraint in devouring them — and now, in turn, one's own flesh and body are offered to feed others. This is simply retribution answering retribution — how could it be otherwise?

Sūtra: "The clinging, hidden ghost, once the darkness it attached to has dissolved and its retribution is exhausted, is born within this world, for the most part, as one of the kind put to service or use."

Commentary: "The clinging, hidden ghost" means clinging and lingering within darkness and obscurity — that being who, in the past, because of this habit of false accusation, encountered darkness and took shape from it, named the nightmare ghost. Now, the very darkness it attached to has dissolved, and the retribution it underwent is likewise exhausted, this form dissolved, this suffering ended, and it is born within this world. Because of this remaining habit of greed for false accusation that made it a nightmare-ghost, it becomes, for the most part, one of the kind put to service or use. As the Detailed Commentary cites from the Record of Strange Things: the mountain owl's own body bears distinctive markings, and local custom names it, according to its own form, the "garment-bird," also called the "solitary fox," hiding by day and emerging by night, also called the "calamity-bird," since its cry portends misfortune. Now, hiding by day and emerging by night is exactly this remaining habit of greed for false accusation; and its cry portending misfortune is exactly this remaining habit of the nightmare ghost. Further, this kind put to service carries two meanings: first, creatures such as the silkworm, the marten, and the rat, whose fur or silk supplies people's own clothing; and second, creatures such as the donkey, the horse, and the camel, who carry and bear people upon their own backs. Because of this greed for false accusation, taken as this offense, and this remaining habit of encountering darkness to become a nightmare ghost, one is now, in turn, made to serve in just this way, repaying this former debt.

Sūtra: "The shade ghost, once the essence-spirit it merged with has dissolved and its retribution is exhausted, is born within this world, for the most part, as one of the kind that answers to the seasons."

Commentary: "The shade ghost" refers to that being who, in the past, because of this habit of wrong views, encountered an essence-spirit and took shape from it, named the shade ghost. Now, the very essence-spirit it merged with has dissolved, and the retribution it underwent is likewise exhausted, this form dissolved, this suffering ended, and it is born within this world. Because of this remaining habit of greed for false insight, this remaining habit of the shade ghost, it becomes, for the most part, one of the kind that answers to the seasons — such as the swallow of spring, or the wild goose of autumn, each knowing the season and its own proper time — this being exactly this remaining habit of greed for false insight; and appearing suddenly in the south, then suddenly in the north — this being exactly this remaining habit of the shade ghost.

Sūtra: "The servant ghost, once the brightness it borrowed has been extinguished and its retribution is exhausted, is born within this world, for the most part, as a creature of good omen, every such kind."

Commentary: "The servant ghost" refers to a ghost that borrows brightness to manifest its own spiritual power — "brightness" here meaning mantras and spells — that being who, in the past, because of this habit of deceit, encountered brightness and took shape from it, named the servant ghost. Now, the very power of brightness it relied upon is extinguished, and the retribution it underwent is likewise exhausted, this form dissolved, this suffering ended, and it is born within this world, for the most part, as a creature of good omen, every such kind. A good omen is a sign foretelling coming fortune. Because of this remaining habit of deceit, this greed for accomplishing one's own ends, such as the auspicious phoenix or unicorn — foretelling good fortune, signaling a sage's own reign — this being exactly this remaining habit of greed for accomplishment. "Every such kind" refers to such creatures as the numinous bird or the patterned beast, understanding human speech, responding to human call — this being exactly this remaining habit of the servant ghost.

Sūtra: "The courier ghost, once the person it depended upon has died and its retribution is exhausted, is born within this world, for the most part, as one of the compliant kind."

Commentary: "The courier ghost" refers to that being who, in the past, because of this habit of litigation, took greed for faction as this offense, encountering a person and taking shape through them, named the courier ghost. As Master Huan puts it: attaching to a medium or diviner, thereby transmitting fortune or misfortune — this is exactly what is meant. Now, the very person it depended upon has died, and the retribution it underwent is likewise exhausted, this form dissolved, this suffering ended, and it is born within this world. Because of this remaining habit of greed for faction, of being a courier ghost, it becomes, for the most part, one of the compliant kind. "Compliant" means obedient — raised and fed by human beings, obedient and compliant toward them, such as the dog or the pigeon. This is simply because, within this cause, one gathered together in factions, exposing another's own hidden faults; and then, becoming a ghost, one attached oneself to a medium, transmitting fortune and misfortune — this being this same remaining habit. Now, becoming a dog or a pigeon, joining in some faction's own evil, receiving its instruction, carrying and transmitting its messages — this, too, is simply this same remaining habit, brought about in just this way.

One might ask: the Tathāgata states generally that becoming an animal is, at root, for the sake of repaying a debt — yet, examining this closely, many creatures here seem free and unburdened, such as the unicorn, phoenix, wild goose, and swallow — what debt, in the end, could these possibly be repaying? The answer: whether caught in a net and sold, or caged and kept as some prized pet, or captured and made to lose their own life, or confined and made to perish — where, then, could this ever fail to be some repayment of debt? This closes the second section, listing each of these ten classes in detail.

Sūtra: "Ānanda, all of these, through this same karmic fire dried up entirely, repaying this old debt, become animals, born sideways. These, too, are all summoned by this same false, deluded karma; and should one awaken to bodhi, this false condition, too, was never, at root, anything at all."

Commentary: Ānanda, these refer to these ten classes of animals and the rest, all of them, through this karmic retribution and suffering fire of hell and the ghost-destiny both, burned away entirely, this false emotion of these two destinies now dried up at last, born within this world, repaying this old debt, covered in fur or crowned with horns, becoming animals, born sideways — also called beings born sideways; yet "born sideways" is a broader category, and "animal" a narrower one, since "domestic" means raised and fed, showing that these beings lack the strength to sustain themselves alone, depending on human care — those not lacking such strength, able to sustain themselves alone, are not included, hence this narrower sense. These ten classes of animals, too, are all summoned through this same illusory, deluded karma, each self-made and self-received. Should one awaken to this true mind of bodhi, originally without a single thing, then this karmic retribution, too, is exactly like a flower conjured in the sky — hence this is called a false condition, never, at root, anything at all.

Within this pure mind, there is neither subject nor object at all — likened to a clear, bright sky: should there be a film over the eye, one senses, of oneself, flowers of red appearing within the sky — yet the sky itself never had any flower in it at all; this diseased eye falsely sees them. And so, at the very moment these sky-flowers are seen, these flowers themselves were never truly born at all; and should this diseased film be removed, this seeing of these flowers, too, then ceases — since there was never any true birth to begin with, what, then, could there ever be to truly cease? This unborn, unceasing nature is itself one's own original nature, bodhi itself. This bodhi is, in every person, originally already possessed — it is only obstructed by this false condition. How, then, is this bodhi awakened to? One need only cause this mind, capable of taking hold of conditions, to no longer arise, and this condition thereby taken hold of naturally ceases to exist — exactly what is meant by: once this maddened mind comes to rest, that very rest is bodhi itself, this most excellent, pure, bright mind, originally pervading the whole Dharma-realm. This closes the third section, tracing this very root, revealing this falseness.

Sūtra: "Just as you yourself have said: Ratnakamalā and the rest, together with King Virūḍhaka and the monk Sunakṣatra — such evil karma as this arises entirely of its own accord; it does not descend from heaven, nor does it emerge from the earth, nor is it given by any other person. It is summoned by one's own falseness, and comes back to be received by that very same self. Within this mind of bodhi, all of this is nothing but a floating, empty, false thought, congealed together."

Commentary: This restates this earlier question: exactly as you, Ānanda, yourself asked — that is, this question concerning how Ratnakamalā and these other two fell into hell. "Such evil karma as this" — since Ānanda's earlier question was directed specifically at hell, the Tathāgata now answers generally, in terms of all three lower destinies together, making plain that they share, alike, this same single realm of punishment and suffering.

As the Standard Meaning Commentary puts it: "arises entirely of its own accord" means, truly, that within a sentient being's own mind, this tathāgatagarbha, everything is inherently, completely possessed — and so, whatever karma one follows, this same retribution is, of itself, manifested and brought to light accordingly. It is like how, within rice, every flavor is already inherently present, becoming sugar, becoming vinegar, or becoming wine, according to however it is prepared — each, of itself, manifested and brought to light, never obtained from anything outside at all. Everything from "it does not" all the way through to "comes back to be received by that very same self" makes plain, precisely, that this is never obtained from anything outside. This meaning is one the student must take special care to grasp — it is not the same as what is commonly said in the world, that one brings some official punishment down upon oneself. For, though the world knows that calamity is self-invited, it still clings to the idea that official punishment itself is some external circumstance. Here, though, it is shown that all three lower destinies are, in every case, nothing but one's own mind's own false transformation, this false realm, exactly as within a dream, with nothing external to it at all — hence, "within this mind of bodhi, this falseness congeals together."

As the Detailed Commentary puts it: "summoned by one's own falseness, and comes back to be received by that very same self" makes plain that this is brought about by oneself. "Within this mind of bodhi, this floating thought congeals together" makes plain that this originally possesses no real substance at all. According to Ānanda's own earlier questions, there were two: general and specific. The first, this general question about these seven destinies, asked: do these destinies exist inherently, from the very beginning, or do they arise from beings' own false habitual tendencies? Here, "summoned by one's own falseness, and comes back to be received by that very same self" answers that they arise from this same false habitual tendency; and "within this mind of bodhi, this floating thought congeals together" answers that they were never, from the very beginning, truly existent at all. The second, this specific question about hell, asked: do these hells possess some fixed location, or does each unfold entirely on its own, this karma each gives rise to differing from the next, each receiving its own retribution individually and privately? Here, "summoned by one's own falseness, and comes back to be received by that very same self" answers that this unfolds entirely on its own, each karma giving rise to its own retribution, each received individually and privately; and "all of this is nothing but a floating, empty, false thought, congealed together" answers that, even should some fixed location exist, this, too, belongs to nothing but this same floating thought, congealed together. This is because the earlier text stated: within this shared portion of a common destiny, there is, at the same time, this same original ground — seeming to suggest some fixed location after all. And so this present passage now dispels this notion as well, saying that sentient beings, from the very first single thought stirring falsely, this karmic consciousness quietly arising, are named this floating thought; and next, this seeing-portion suddenly arising, congealing into this present realm, this is named congealing. This is what every one of the seven destinies alike depends upon in common — called, in this sense, a shared portion; yet, as each sentient being summons this individually, this is called their own separate karma. Should a single person turn back to truth, returning to this very source, the empty space of the ten directions is, at once, entirely dissolved away — how much more, then, hell, ghosts, animals, and the rest, themselves existing only within this same empty space? This is exactly why, within this mind of bodhi, there is nothing at all to be found — and, should one truly, thoroughly realize this, there is no need to entertain any further doubt about it at all. Yet should one merely claim this emptiness before ever truly awakening to it, this is much to be feared — one would only mislead others, and mislead oneself as well. As an ancient once said: once understood, karmic hindrance is, at root, empty; not yet understood, one must still repay this old debt owing — how could one fail to take the utmost care over this! This closes the third section, this destiny of animals.

Reading note: This closes the third of these seven destinies discussed in this fascicle — hell, hungry ghosts, and animals — and, with it, this fascicle's own detailed treatment of how these lower three destinies arise. Master Yuanying's outline marks three further destinies still ahead: immortals, the six heavens of the desire realm, and the form and formless realms beyond them, all still to come before this whole section on the seven destinies is complete. Notice how the closing exchange returns, precisely, to Ānanda's own two original questions from the very start of this fascicle — do these destinies exist inherently, and do these hells possess some fixed location — answering both, in the end, with the same single teaching this whole fascicle has been building toward: nothing here was ever given from outside; every destiny is one's own mind's own false, floating thought, congealed into a realm that never had any more reality than a flower conjured in the sky.
