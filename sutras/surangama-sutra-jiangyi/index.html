Master Yuanying · 1878–1953 · 24 fascicles

Studying the Śūraṅgama With Master Yuanying.

At sixty-eight, after more than forty years of study, Master Yuanying (圓瑛法師) began writing the commentary he considered the capstone of his life's work — finishing it at seventy-four, the year he died. This page is the entrance for reading his Lecture Notes on the Śūraṅgama Sūtra (楞嚴經講義) in English, fascicle by fascicle, alongside Ru-Yi's translation of the bare sūtra text.

Why this commentary

Master Yuanying was one of the most prominent Chinese Buddhist monks of the twentieth century — abbot of Tiantong and Qita monasteries, first chairman of the Chinese Buddhist Association, and a lifelong builder of schools and relief institutions. He studied the Śūraṅgama Sūtra for over forty years before writing a single line of this commentary, and regarded it as his life's central teaching project.

As far as we know, this is the first complete English translation of Yuanying's commentary specifically — distinct from the better-known English edition of the sūtra that draws on Master Hsuan Hua's teaching instead.

What each fascicle page includes

  • Yuanying's own ten-part outline (十門分別), kept as the page structure rather than replaced with new section divisions.
  • Quoted sūtra passages marked Sūtra:, Yuanying's own explanation marked Commentary:, matching the convention already used for the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra on this site.
  • A running glossary shared with the companion bare-sūtra translation project.

Read by Fascicle

Yuanying's fascicles are considerably longer than the sūtra's own ten fascicles, so each one appears here in installments rather than all at once. Fascicle 1's first nine installments — his entire tenfold preliminary analysis except the line-by-line commentary — are open now; the remaining entries link to the source text at bfnn.org in the meantime.

Translation Notes

Recurring terms and Yuanying's own outline, kept consistent across all 24 fascicles. See the full translation-notes.md in the site repository for the complete, growing list.

十門分別 — the tenfold analysisYuanying's overall organizing scheme, adapted from the Xianshou (Huayan) school's traditional ten gates of commentary.
密因 — the hidden causeThe unborn, unconditioned nature every being already possesses, prior to any cultivation.
了義 — complete meaningDistinguished by Yuanying in two specific ways: using the sense-root rather than discriminating consciousness, and according with true nature rather than clinging to appearances.
七種立題 — the seven ways sūtra titles are formedPerson / Dharma / Analogy, singly, in pairs, or all three — the classification Yuanying applies before analyzing this sūtra's own title.
三身 — the three bodiesDharmakāya, Saṃbhogakāya, and Nirmāṇakāya — invoked in Yuanying's gloss of "Tathāgata."
