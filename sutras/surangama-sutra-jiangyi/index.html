Studying the Śūraṅgama With Master Yuanying.

At sixty-eight, after more than forty years of study, Master Yuanying (圓瑛法師) began writing the commentary he considered the capstone of his life's work — finishing it at seventy-four, the year he died. This page is the entrance for reading his Lecture Notes on the Śūraṅgama Sūtra (楞嚴經講義) in English, fascicle by fascicle, alongside Ru-Yi's translation of the bare sūtra text.

Why this commentary

Master Yuanying was one of the most prominent Chinese Buddhist monks of the twentieth century — abbot of Tiantong and Qita monasteries, first chairman of the Chinese Buddhist Association, and a lifelong builder of schools and relief institutions. He studied the Śūraṅgama Sūtra for over forty years before writing a single line of this commentary, and regarded it as his life's central teaching project.

As far as we know, this is the first complete English translation of Yuanying's commentary specifically — distinct from the better-known English edition of the sūtra that draws on Master Hsuan Hua's teaching instead.

What each fascicle page includes

  • Yuanying's own ten-part outline (十門分別), kept as the page structure rather than replaced with new section divisions.
  • Quoted sūtra passages marked Sūtra:, Yuanying's own explanation marked Commentary:, matching the convention already used for the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra on this site.
  • A running glossary shared with the companion bare-sūtra translation project.

Read by Fascicle

Yuanying's fascicles are considerably longer than the sūtra's own ten fascicles. Fascicles 1–20 — the tenfold preliminary analysis, the four categories and seven great elements harmonized with the tathāgatagarbha, Ānanda's verse of gratitude, Pūrṇa's cosmogony questions, the complete teaching of the three aspects of the tathāgatagarbha, the parable of Yajñadatta, the sūtra's practical teaching on the two decisive meanings, the famous six-knotted cloth demonstration, all twenty-five disciples' testimonies to perfect penetration (Avalokiteśvara's own testimony spanning fascicles 15–16), Mañjuśrī's own verse selecting the ear-faculty as best suited to this world, the complete four clear instructions with the elaborate building of the ritual platform, the full account of the Śūraṅgama Mantra's structure, benefits, and protector deities, the complete fifty-five stages of the path from dry wisdom to wondrous enlightenment, and the detailed teaching on how emotion and thought decide rebirth among hell, hungry ghosts, and animals — are complete and open now; the remaining entries link to the source text at bfnn.org in the meantime.

Complete

Fascicle 1

The complete tenfold preliminary analysis, the entire preface section (序分), Ānanda's request, Yuanying's fascicle-by-fascicle roadmap of the sūtra, and his own textual-critical argument for relocating the Buddha's naming of the Śūraṅgama samādhi.

Complete

Fascicle 2: A Thief Mistaken for a Son

The seven-location search for the mind, and the Buddha's sharper refutation that the very searching mind itself is not the true mind — the two roots of true mind and false mind.

Complete

Fascicle 14: Gathering In All Six at Once

Thirteen more testimonies to perfect penetration, through the six consciousnesses and all seven great elements, closing with Mahāsthāmaprāpta's teaching on reciting the Buddha's name with body, speech, and mind gathered into one.

Complete

Fascicle 15: The One Who Observes the Observer

The opening of Avalokiteśvara's own testimony — the ancient Buddha's teaching of hearing, reflecting, and cultivating, the six-stage untying of the knot (with Yuanying's own account of his years of Chan practice), the thirty-two manifested bodies, and the fourteen kinds of fearlessness.

Complete

Fascicle 16: Turn That Hearing Back Upon Your Own Hearing

The four inconceivable virtues, the Buddha's own light confirming all twenty-five accounts, and Mañjuśrī's full verse of selection — closing the sūtra's entire twenty-five approaches to perfect penetration.

Complete

Fascicle 17: A Crooked Cause Brings a Twisted Result

Ānanda's request for a place of practice, the complete four clear instructions against sexual misconduct, killing, stealing, and false claims of attainment, the Śūraṅgama Mantra recommended, and the elaborate, symbol-laden building of the ritual platform for the hundred-day practice.

Complete

Fascicle 18: A Thousand Places Entreating, a Thousand Places Answered

The assembly's renewed request to hear the Śūraṅgama Mantra in full, the transformation-buddha's manifestation of light, the mantra's five-part structure, its uses for every buddha and every sentient being, and the closing assembly of deities vowing protection.

Complete

Fascicle 19: Two Distortions, and the True Path to Bodhi

The two root distortions of being and world, the twelve categories of birth, the three gradual stages, and all fifty-five stages of the path from dry wisdom to wondrous enlightenment in one continuous sweep — closing the sūtra's entire first major division on the cultivation of samādhi.

Complete

Fascicle 20: Ten Habits, Six Crossings, One Wheel of Rebirth

How the ratio of emotion to thought at death decides a being's destiny, the ten habitual causes and six cross-referenced retributions of hell in full clause-by-clause detail, the ten kinds of hungry ghosts, and the animal realm's own long accounting of debts owed and repaid.

Translation Notes

Recurring terms and Yuanying's own outline, kept consistent across all 24 fascicles. See the full translation-notes.md in the site repository for the complete, growing list.

十門分別 — the tenfold analysisYuanying's overall organizing scheme, adapted from the Xianshou (Huayan) school's traditional ten gates of commentary.
密因 — the hidden causeThe unborn, unconditioned nature every being already possesses, prior to any cultivation.
了義 — complete meaningDistinguished by Yuanying in two specific ways: using the sense-root rather than discriminating consciousness, and according with true nature rather than clinging to appearances.
七種立題 — the seven ways sūtra titles are formedPerson / Dharma / Analogy, singly, in pairs, or all three — the classification Yuanying applies before analyzing this sūtra's own title.
三身 — the three bodiesDharmakāya, Saṃbhogakāya, and Nirmāṇakāya — invoked in Yuanying's gloss of "Tathāgata."
