Nineteen Characters, Unpacked

Master Yuanying begins his commentary not with the sūtra's opening line, but with its title — all nineteen characters of it, taken apart phrase by phrase. Everything the sūtra will go on to teach, he argues, is already compressed into this one name.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: installments 1–4 of fascicle 1 (甲一–甲九 complete; 甲十 begun) Source: bfnn.org / NTU Buddhist Digital Library Installments so far: 甲一–甲九 complete; 甲十 序分 · 證信序 (the six perfections) begun

About This Installment

Master Yuanying organizes his entire commentary under a ten-part outline (十門分別), adapted from the Huayan patriarch Fazang's (賢首) traditional template. Where earlier commentators typically opened with a general summary of the sūtra's meaning before turning to the title, Yuanying moves the title exegesis to the very first position — so that, in his words, a reader "opens the book and immediately knows the meaning packed into the title." This page now covers the first nine of his ten preliminary parts in full: the title exegesis, why the Buddha taught this sūtra, its place among the piṭakas and vehicles, the depth of its doctrine, the substance of its teaching, its intended audience, its aims, when it was spoken, and the history of its translation into Chinese. Only the tenth and final part — his line-by-line commentary on the sūtra text itself — remains in progress; see the fascicle index for the full outline.

Quoted passages from the sūtra itself are marked Sūtra:; Yuanying's own explanation is marked Commentary:, following the convention already used on this site for the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra.

The Method, and the Title as a Whole 甲一 總釋名題

Chinese Source

今解此經，謹遵賢首十門解釋。考諸賢宗諸疏，而十門次序名目，亦不盡同，或具足十門，或略取數門，皆先述一經大意，乃以總釋名題、別解文義，兩門列後。今則略為變更，提總釋名題為第一，先釋經題，繼述綱要，後解經文，俾閱疏者，一開卷便知題中義理；聞經亦復如是，第一日即可聽講經題，此固一時之權變耳。以後科目，以天干地支標之，令易記憶，而便尋討。即分為十：甲初 總釋名題　二 起教因緣　三 藏乘攝屬　四 義理淺深　五 能詮教體　六 所被機宜　七 宗趣通別　八 說時前後　九 歷明傳譯　十 別解文義

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: In explaining this sūtra, I respectfully follow the tenfold method of the Xianshou (Fazang) school. Examining the various commentaries of that school, I find that the order and the names of the ten parts are not entirely uniform — some commentaries use the full ten, others only a few — but all of them first state the general meaning of the sūtra, and only afterward place the "general explanation of the title" and the "detailed explanation of the text" at the end.

Here I have made a small change: I place the general explanation of the title first. I explain the sūtra's title first, then set out its main outline, and only then explain the text itself — so that a reader who opens this commentary immediately understands the doctrine packed into the title. Likewise for hearing the sūtra taught: on the very first day, one can already hear the title explained. This is simply an expedient adjustment for the occasion. For the parts that follow, I label them with the Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, to make them easy to remember and easy to look up. There are ten in all:

Stem One — General Explanation of the Title. Two — The Causes and Conditions for Teaching This Sūtra. Three — Which Piṭaka and Vehicle It Belongs To. Four — The Relative Depth of Its Doctrine. Five — The Substance of the Teaching That Expresses It. Six — The Capacities of Those It Addresses. Seven — Its General and Specific Aims. Eight — When in the Buddha's Teaching Career It Was Spoken. Nine — The History of Its Transmission and Translation. Ten — Line-by-Line Explanation of the Text Itself.

Chinese Source

今初總釋名題　大佛頂，如來密因，修證了義，諸菩薩萬行，首楞嚴經。

題為全經之總，經乃一題之別；全經要義，萃於一題，欲識經中別義，須解法題總綱。凡釋題者，當知經家，既以題目冠列經前，而釋題者，自應據經而取其義。如密因、了義等，即當說是何等法，經中何文即是，未可儱侗拈弄，而與經文毫不相涉。若陡事論量文體，不依解釋文體，安能令文義雙暢乎？此題，乃佛自命五名中，結集者，揀擇重要，略取十九字，合成一題。前三字，分取第一題；中八字，全取第三題；後八字，分取第五題。名異諸經，故謂之別題。經之一字，凡是經藏，諸部同名為經，故謂之通題。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: Here begins the first part: the general explanation of the title. The title reads: Great Buddha's Crown, the Tathāgata's Hidden Cause, Cultivation and Realization of Complete Meaning, the Myriad Practices of All Bodhisattvas, the Śūraṅgama Sūtra.

The title is the summary of the whole sūtra; the sūtra is the detailed unfolding of the title. The essential meaning of the entire text is concentrated into this one title — so to understand the sūtra's particular meanings, one must first grasp the overall thread held in the title. Anyone explaining a sūtra's title should recognize that, since the compilers placed the title before the text, whoever explains that title must draw its meaning from the text itself. Terms like "hidden cause" or "complete meaning" must be shown to correspond to specific teachings and specific passages within the sūtra — they must not be handled loosely, as clever phrases with no real connection to the text. If one only debates the title as literary style, without grounding the explanation in the text, how could the meaning of the words ever come through clearly?

This title was chosen by the compilers from among five names the Buddha himself gave the sūtra elsewhere in the text, selecting what was essential and condensing it into nineteen characters. The first three characters are drawn from the first of those five names; the middle eight characters are taken in full from the third name; the last eight characters are drawn from the fifth name. Since this name differs from every other sūtra's title, it is called the "specific title." The single character "sūtra," however, is shared by every text in the sūtra-basket alike, and so it is called the "general title."

一切諸經，別名無量，按古德所判，不出七種立題：以人、法、喻三字，單字三種，雙字三種，具足一種。一單人：如《佛說阿彌陀》；二單法：如《大涅槃》；三單喻：如《梵網》；四人、法：如《地藏菩薩本願》，五人、喻：如《如來師子吼》；六法、喻：如《妙法蓮華》：七人、法、喻：如《大方廣佛華嚴》。此經以人、法為題。如來是果人，菩薩是因人，密因是理法，了義是教法，萬行是行法，首楞嚴是果法：故以人、法為題，亦可略兼於喻。以佛頂二字，非舉相似之物，比類發明，乃舉直稱法體之佛頂，以表勝妙，故曰略兼於喻。此依古判定，下逐句分釋：

Every sūtra has its own specific name, and these names are without number, but past masters have classified them as never exceeding seven basic patterns — built from the three elements of Person, Dharma, and Analogy, used singly (three patterns), in pairs (three patterns), or all three together (one pattern). First, Person alone: as in the Sūtra Spoken by the Buddha on Amitābha. Second, Dharma alone: as in the Great Nirvāṇa Sūtra. Third, Analogy alone: as in the Brahmā Net Sūtra. Fourth, Person and Dharma: as in the Sūtra of Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva's Original Vows. Fifth, Person and Analogy: as in the Sūtra of the Tathāgata's Lion's Roar. Sixth, Dharma and Analogy: as in the Wondrous Dharma Lotus Flower Sūtra. Seventh, Person, Dharma, and Analogy together: as in the Great, Vast, Buddha Flower Garland Sūtra.

This sūtra takes its title from Person and Dharma. "Tathāgata" is the person who has reached the fruit; "bodhisattvas" are persons still on the causal path. "Hidden cause" is a dharma of principle; "complete meaning" is a dharma of teaching; "myriad practices" is a dharma of conduct; "Śūraṅgama" is a dharma of the fruit attained. The title is therefore built from Person and Dharma — though it may also be said to touch lightly on Analogy as well. The two characters "Buddha's Crown" do not invoke some similar object for the sake of comparison; rather, they name outright the Buddha's own crown as the very substance being described, in order to express its surpassing wonder — which is why it is said to "touch lightly" on Analogy rather than fully constitute one. This follows the traditional classification. What follows is a phrase-by-phrase explanation.

Reading note: Yuanying opens by locating this sūtra's title within a standard classification every trained reader of Chinese Buddhist literature would recognize — the "seven ways of forming a title," built from Person, Dharma, and Analogy. This is not pedantry: it commits him, before he has explained a single term, to showing exactly which words in the title are "Person" and which are "Dharma" — a discipline he insists on explicitly ("must not be handled loosely... with no real connection to the text").

"Great Buddha's Crown" 大佛頂

Chinese Source

大佛頂三字，為能讚能表，下之四法，為所讚所表。大者，稱讚之詞，讚下四法，猶言大矣哉是經也！則知密因為大因，得成菩提故；了義為大義，稱實理說故；萬行為大行，如實修行故；楞嚴為大定，王三昧故：具此諸大，是為大經。首標大者，意令受持是經者，當依大教解大理；稱大理起大行；滿大行證大果，故以讚之。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: The three characters "Great Buddha's Crown" do the praising and the expressing; the four terms that follow are what is praised and expressed. "Great" is a word of praise, praising the four terms that follow — as if to say, "How great this sūtra is!" For we then know: the hidden cause is a great cause, since it enables the attainment of bodhi; complete meaning is a great meaning, since it speaks in accordance with true principle; the myriad practices are great practice, since they are cultivated in accordance with reality; and Śūraṅgama is a great samādhi, being the king of all samādhis. Possessing all these forms of greatness, this is indeed a great sūtra.

"Great" is placed first so that whoever receives and upholds this sūtra will, by relying on this great teaching, understand great principle; by according with great principle, give rise to great practice; and by fulfilling great practice, realize the great fruit. This is why the word praises it.

佛頂表顯之義。佛頂，則佛肉髻相上，無見頂相也，乃三十二相之第一相。肉髻在青螺紺髮正中，周圍紅色，狀如春山吐日。佛初生時，嵐毗尼林神，為佛乳母，棒持諦觀，不見其頂；又佛成道後，遊化波羅奈國，東方應持菩薩，欲窮佛頂，上歷恆沙佛土，終不能見。此不屬於有，而能放光化佛，又不屬於無，雙離有無，是之謂妙；表下四法，猶言妙矣哉是教也！則知密因為妙因，因心果覺，二不別故；了義為妙義，一門深入，六根清淨故；萬行為妙行，稱真如理，中中流入故；楞嚴為妙定，自性本具不假修成故；具此諸妙，是謂妙法。表以佛頂者，意令受持是經者，當依發妙耳門之妙教，悟如來藏性之妙理；從妙理起妙行；滿妙行證妙果即妙覺極果，圓滿菩提，歸無所得。故以表之。結集者，取此三字，冠於經題之首，令知所讚所表，必非權漸教也。

"Buddha's Crown" carries the sense of "expressing." The Buddha's crown refers to the fleshly protuberance (uṣṇīṣa) atop the Buddha's head — the invisible crown-mark, the first of the thirty-two major marks. This fleshly crown sits at the very center of his dark blue, spiral hair, ringed with red, shaped like a spring mountain giving birth to the rising sun.

When the Buddha was newly born, the forest spirit of Lumbinī, acting as his wet-nurse, held him and gazed at him closely, yet could not see the top of his head. And after the Buddha attained the path and was traveling and teaching in the kingdom of Vārāṇasī, the bodhisattva Apratihatavikramin, wishing to trace the Buddha's crown to its limit, ascended through as many buddha-lands as there are grains of sand in the Ganges, and still could not see it. This crown-mark, then, does not belong to the category of "existing" — and yet it can emit light and manifest transformation-buddhas; nor does it belong to the category of "not existing." Standing apart from both existence and non-existence at once — this is called "wondrous."

It expresses the four terms that follow — as if to say, "How wondrous this teaching is!" For we then know: the hidden cause is a wondrous cause, since the causal mind and the fruit of enlightenment are not, in the end, two different things; complete meaning is a wondrous meaning, since entering deeply through a single approach purifies all six sense-faculties at once; the myriad practices are wondrous practice, since they flow directly, moment after moment, in accordance with the principle of true suchness; and Śūraṅgama is a wondrous samādhi, since it is inherently possessed by one's own nature and is not something fabricated through cultivation. Possessing all these forms of wonder, this is indeed a wondrous Dharma.

"Buddha's Crown" is used to express this so that whoever receives and upholds this sūtra will, by relying on the wondrous teaching that opens through the ear faculty, awaken to the wondrous principle of the nature of the tathāgatagarbha; from that wondrous principle, give rise to wondrous practice; and by fulfilling that wondrous practice, realize the wondrous fruit — that is, the ultimate fruit of wondrous enlightenment, perfect bodhi, arriving at That Which Is Attained By No Attainment. This is why the compilers took these three characters and placed them at the very head of the title: so that readers would know that what is being praised and expressed here is, without question, no merely provisional or gradual teaching.

Reading note: Yuanying reads "Great" and "Buddha's Crown" as two distinct rhetorical moves rather than one descriptive phrase. "Great" (大) praises all four things named later in the title by calling each one "great" in turn. "Buddha's Crown" (佛頂) — a physical mark on the Buddha's body that, according to the legends Yuanying cites, no being can ever see the top of, even a bodhisattva ascending through countless world-systems — becomes his image for something that is neither simply existent nor simply nonexistent, and he uses that same paradox ("wondrous," 妙) to characterize the hidden cause, complete meaning, myriad practices, and Śūraṅgama in turn. The structure is: 大 (great) + 佛頂 (wondrous) each modify all four of the terms still to come.

"The Tathāgata's Hidden Cause" 如來密因

Chinese Source

如來密因：如來，是諸佛通號。佛有十號，如來為第一號，乃仿同先德號；以佛佛道同，後佛如先佛之再來，故曰如來，此約普通解釋。今按本經，終實教意，如為本覺，來為始覺。依本覺，不生滅之理性，起始覺，回光返照之觀智，依妙智證妙理，始覺與本覺合一，名究竟覺，方成佛道，方稱如來。更約三身釋之：身者，積聚之義。一、法身如來：梵語毗盧遮那，華言遍一切處，此積聚理法以為身；真如妙理，猶若虛空，遍一切處。經云：「常住妙明，不動周圓。求於去來，迷悟生死，了無所得。」即法身義。二、報身如來：梵語盧舍那，華言淨滿，此積聚智慧以為身；諸惑皆淨，智慧圓滿。經云：「明極即如來，」即報身義。三、應身如來：千百億化身，隨機應現，此積聚機緣以為身；如有可度機緣，即現八相成道。經云：「自覺已圓，能覺他者，如來應世。」即應身義。今連下密因二字，當屬報、應二身如來。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: "Tathāgata" is a title shared by all buddhas. A buddha has ten titles, of which "Tathāgata" is the first — meaning one who imitates and matches the honored ones who came before, since every buddha realizes the same path, and each later buddha is, in effect, an earlier buddha come again. This is the general explanation.

But according to this sūtra's own final, complete teaching, "thus" (如) refers to original enlightenment, and "come" (來) refers to initial enlightenment. Relying on the unborn, unceasing principle-nature of original enlightenment, one gives rise to the contemplative wisdom of initial enlightenment, which turns its light back upon its source; relying on this wondrous wisdom, one realizes this wondrous principle, until initial enlightenment and original enlightenment merge into one — this is called ultimate enlightenment, and only then is Buddhahood accomplished, only then is one truly called "Tathāgata."

This can be further explained through the three bodies. "Body" here means an accumulation. First, the dharmakāya Tathāgata: in Sanskrit, Vairocana, meaning "pervading every place" — a body accumulated from principle and dharma; the wondrous principle of true suchness, like empty space, pervades every place. The sūtra says: "Ever-abiding, wondrous, and bright; unmoving and perfectly complete. Search for its coming or going, its confusion or awakening, its birth or death — nothing at all can be found." This is the meaning of the dharmakāya.

Second, the saṃbhogakāya Tathāgata: in Sanskrit, Locana, meaning "pure and full" — a body accumulated from wisdom; every affliction entirely purified, wisdom perfectly complete. The sūtra says: "Brightness at its utmost — that is the Tathāgata." This is the meaning of the saṃbhogakāya.

Third, the nirmāṇakāya Tathāgata: manifesting in a hundred million transformation-bodies, appearing according to the capacities of beings — a body accumulated from causes and conditions of teaching; wherever there are beings ready to be taught, he manifests the eight aspects of accomplishing the path. The sūtra says: "One who, having awakened perfectly, is able to awaken others — this is the Tathāgata responding to the world." This is the meaning of the nirmāṇakāya.

Here, joined to the following two characters "hidden cause," "Tathāgata" refers to the Tathāgata in his saṃbhogakāya and nirmāṇakāya aspects.

密因，揀非事相修行，顯因可見者。而曰如來密因，即是十方如來，得成果覺，所依之因心；亦即一切眾生，所具之根性，為菩提涅槃，本元清淨之體；可為修證果覺之因地心。十方如來，皆依此不生不滅為本修因，然後圓成果地修證；眾生人人本具，迷而不覺，未能依之修證，故詣之密。又，此不特是因性，亦即是果性。以如來雖證極果，不離正因，所謂因該果海，果徹因源也。問：「既即果性，何復名因？」答：「須見此不生不滅之根性後，方是究竟果覺之因，更須依此圓湛不生滅性，成為因地心，稱性起修，始獲究竟果覺，即此一性，而能通因徹果，故如來破識顯根，即顯此密因也。」

"Hidden" distinguishes this from any cultivation carried out through visible outer forms — a cause that could simply be pointed to and seen. To speak of "the Tathāgata's hidden cause" is to speak of the very causal mind that the Tathāgatas of the ten directions rely upon to attain the fruit of enlightenment — and this is likewise the very nature already possessed by every sentient being: the originally pure substance of bodhi and nirvāṇa, which can serve as the causal-ground mind for cultivating and realizing the fruit of enlightenment.

The Tathāgatas of the ten directions all take this unborn, unceasing nature as the root cause of their cultivation, and only afterward perfectly accomplish realization at the level of the fruit. Every single sentient being already possesses it, yet, being deluded, fails to awaken to it, and so is unable to cultivate and realize by relying on it — which is why it is called "hidden."

Moreover, this is not only the nature of the cause; it is also the nature of the fruit. For although the Tathāgata has realized the ultimate fruit, he has never departed from the true cause — as it is said, the cause encompasses the whole ocean of the fruit, and the fruit penetrates all the way back to the source of the cause. One might ask: "If this is already the nature of the fruit, why call it a cause at all?" The answer: only after seeing this unborn, unceasing root-nature does it become the cause of ultimate enlightenment — one must further take this perfectly full, unborn nature and make it the causal-ground mind, cultivating in accordance with this very nature, before ultimate enlightenment can be attained. It is this single nature that runs through from cause all the way to fruit. This is why, when the Tathāgata refutes false consciousness and reveals the true faculty-nature later in this sūtra, he is revealing exactly this hidden cause.

又，密因二字，遣五種人過。密之一字，遣凡夫、外道、權教、小乘四種人過。以彼不達，密具不生滅之根性，即是成佛真因，反認意識為心，錯亂修習，塵劫劬勞，終無實果。第一卷文云：「諸修行人，不能得成無上菩提，乃至別成聲聞緣覺，及成外道，諸天魔王，及魔眷屬，皆由不知二種根本，錯亂修習，猶如煮沙，欲成嘉饌，縱經塵劫，終不能得。」佛欲令人捨妄本，而依真本也。因之一字，遣利根狂慧人過。以彼未明所具不生滅之根性，但是正因佛性，須假了、緣二因，正因方顯。遂乃自恃天真，本來是佛，頓捐修證，不依方便進修，終無得證。如礦雖是金，不假鍛煉，終久是礦，不能成金。

Furthermore, the two characters "hidden cause" correct the errors of five kinds of people. The word "hidden" alone corrects the errors of four kinds: ordinary unenlightened beings, followers of non-Buddhist paths, followers of provisional teachings, and followers of the Lesser Vehicle. All of these fail to realize that the unborn, unceasing root-nature, hidden within them, is itself the true cause of Buddhahood; instead they mistake the discriminating consciousness for mind, and, cultivating in confusion, labor through kalpas as numerous as dust motes without ever attaining any real fruit.

The sūtra says, later in this very fascicle: "All practitioners, unable to attain unsurpassed bodhi and instead attaining, at best, the separate fruits of śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha, or else becoming non-Buddhist practitioners, heavenly demon-kings, or the retinue of demons — all of this comes from not knowing the two roots, and cultivating in confusion; it is like cooking sand and hoping to produce a fine meal — though one labor through kalpas as numerous as dust motes, one will never succeed." The Buddha wishes, by this, to have people abandon the false root and rely instead on the true one.

The word "cause" alone corrects the error of a different kind of person: those of sharp faculties but reckless, unripe wisdom. Not having clearly understood that their possessed unborn, unceasing root-nature is only the correct cause of Buddha-nature — which still requires the two supporting causes, the "complete" and the "conditioning," before the correct cause can manifest — they instead rely on their own natural brilliance, imagining themselves already Buddhas from the start, and abruptly cast aside all cultivation and realization, refusing to advance by way of skillful means. In the end, they attain nothing. It is like ore that is, in truth, gold: without smelting, it remains ore forever, and never becomes gold.

Reading note: Yuanying's gloss of "Tathāgata" moves through three registers at once: the conventional title shared by all buddhas; a doctrinal reading specific to this sūtra ("thus" = original enlightenment, "come" = initial enlightenment, merging into "ultimate enlightenment"); and the classical three-bodies framework (dharmakāya, saṃbhogakāya, nirmāṇakāya). "Hidden cause" then does real philosophical work of its own: it names a nature every being already possesses but fails to recognize — and Yuanying is careful to note it corrects two opposite errors at once. "Hidden" corrects those who look for Buddhahood anywhere except this nature (ordinary beings, non-Buddhist paths, provisional teachings, the Lesser Vehicle, all mistaking "consciousness" for "mind"); "cause" corrects the opposite extreme — those of sharp intellect who, hearing that this nature is already complete, skip cultivation entirely, like ore that calls itself gold without ever being smelted.

"Cultivation and Realization of Complete Meaning" 修證了義

Chinese Source

修證了義：即稱密因，所起之修證也。……了義復含二意，與通常之解不同：一、用根不用識；用識，則以生滅心，為本修因，而求佛乘，不生不滅，無有是處。……故非了義。用根，則依不生滅，圓湛性成，然後圓成，果地修證。……故為了義。又特選耳根圓通……更是了義中之了義耳。……二、稱性不著相。著相之修，為事相之染修，著相之證，為新成之實證，未悟圓理，均非了義。稱性之修，乃從聞、思、修，入三摩地，如幻聞熏聞修，金剛三昧，但向一門深入，而得六根解脫，修則無修；稱性之證，生滅既滅，寂滅現前，乃發現其本有家珍，證亦無證……方為了義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: "Cultivation and realization of complete meaning" refers to the cultivation and realization that arise in accordance with this very hidden cause. ... "Complete meaning" here carries two senses not found in the ordinary use of the term.

First: relying on the sense-faculty rather than on consciousness. To rely on consciousness is to take the arising-and-ceasing mind as the root cause of cultivation while seeking the unborn, unceasing Buddha-vehicle — which cannot succeed. ... This, therefore, is not the teaching of complete meaning. To rely on the faculty is to accomplish perfect, deep clarity by relying on what is unborn and unceasing, and only then to perfectly accomplish cultivation and realization at the level of the fruit. ... This, therefore, is the teaching of complete meaning. This sūtra, moreover, specifically selects the perfect penetration of the ear faculty [as the means of entry] ... making it complete meaning within complete meaning itself.

Second: according with true nature rather than clinging to appearances. Cultivation that clings to appearances is a defiled cultivation carried out through outer forms; realization that clings to appearances is merely a newly fabricated, superficial realization — neither has awakened to the perfect principle, and neither counts as complete meaning. Cultivation that accords with true nature proceeds from hearing, reflecting, and cultivating into samādhi — hearing as if in a dream, cultivating that very hearing, entering the vajra samādhi — advancing deeply through a single approach until all six sense-faculties are liberated; such cultivation is, in truth, no cultivation at all. Realization that accords with true nature is one in which arising-and-ceasing having ceased, still cessation manifests of itself — uncovering the treasure that was one's own all along; such realization is, in truth, no realization at all. ... Only this counts as complete meaning.

Reading note: This is one of the more technical distinctions in the whole title, and Yuanying is explicit that it departs from how "complete meaning" (了義, often rendered elsewhere as "definitive meaning" or "ultimate teaching") is normally used. His two criteria are not about doctrinal content but about method: (1) does the practice work through the sense-faculty directly, or through discriminating consciousness? and (2) does it accord with one's true nature from the start, or does it cling to appearances and produce only an artificially manufactured result? By this second criterion, even a real attainment can fail to count as "complete meaning" if it was reached by clinging to appearances along the way. Both criteria will matter again in later fascicles, once Ānanda is taught to "enter through the ear faculty."

"The Myriad Practices of All Bodhisattvas" 諸菩薩萬行

Chinese Source

諸菩薩萬行：菩薩，梵語具云菩提薩埵，此方人有好略之習慣，簡稱菩薩。菩提譯云覺，薩埵譯云有情，乃大道心眾生之稱。今作三義釋之：一、已經覺悟我、法二空之有情；二、能覺法界，無量諸有情；三、智悲並運，自他兩利，運智，上求佛覺以自利；運悲，下度有情以利他。……而言諸者，通指五十五位也。

萬行，即稱圓通體，所起之無作妙行也。……其行應有無量，今言萬者，但明其多，非局定數也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: "Bodhisattva" is short for the full Sanskrit bodhisattva — people of this land, having a habit of preferring the concise, shorten it to "bodhisattva." "Bodhi" is translated as "awakening"; "sattva" is translated as "sentient being" — together, a term for a being whose heart is set on the great path. I explain this with three meanings: first, a being who has already awakened to the twin emptiness of self and dharmas; second, a being able to awaken countless other beings throughout the Dharma-realm; third, a being who carries wisdom and compassion together, benefiting both self and other — using wisdom to seek Buddhahood above, for one's own benefit, and using compassion to save sentient beings below, for the benefit of others. ... The word "all" (諸) refers, comprehensively, to all fifty-five stages of the bodhisattva path.

"Myriad practices" means the wondrous, unconstructed practices that arise in accordance with the substance of perfect penetration. ... Such practices are, properly speaking, without number; the word "myriad" here simply indicates their great quantity — it is not meant to fix them at any exact count.

"Śūraṅgama" 首楞嚴

Chinese Source

首楞嚴者，大定之總名也。圓含妙奢摩他、三摩、禪那三種別名，而成一定全體，迥不同於常途，工夫引起之定，亦不同於起心對境之定，此自性定耳。《涅槃經》佛自釋首楞嚴為「一切事究竟堅固」。而古德即明其為徹法底源，無動無壞。

今釋此定，二義料揀：一、此是圓定：不但獨取自心不動，乃統萬法，悉皆本來不動，為一定體。……經云：「常住妙明，不動周圓。」……二、此是妙定：正以性本自具，天然不動，不假修成，縱在迷位，其體如故……

合此圓妙二義，故為首楞嚴王三昧。自發解起行，直至歷位成佛，從始洎終，中間永無諸委曲相，亦無出退，非常途之定，有入、住、出。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: "Śūraṅgama" is the overall name for the great samādhi. It perfectly contains within itself the three separately named practices — wondrous śamatha, samādhi, and dhyāna — forming one single, complete body of samādhi, entirely unlike the ordinary kind of concentration brought about by deliberate effort, and unlike, too, the kind of concentration that arises by fixing the mind upon an object. This is, rather, the samādhi of one's own inherent nature.

In the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, the Buddha himself explains "Śūraṅgama" as meaning "the ultimate firmness of all things" — and past masters have made clear that this means penetrating all the way to the very source of every phenomenon, a ground that neither moves nor can be destroyed.

In explaining this samādhi, two distinctions should be made. First, it is a perfect samādhi: it is not merely that one's own mind alone is unmoving, but that the whole of the myriad dharmas is, from the very beginning, equally unmoving, forming a single body of samādhi. ... As the sūtra says: "Ever-abiding, wondrous, and bright; unmoving and perfectly complete." ... Second, it is a wondrous samādhi: precisely because this nature is inherently self-possessed and naturally unmoving, not something fabricated through cultivation — so that even while one is still deluded, its essence remains exactly as it is. ...

Combining these two senses, perfect and wondrous, this is why it is called the King Śūraṅgama Samādhi. From the first arising of understanding and the setting out on practice, all the way through every stage until Buddhahood is attained — from beginning to end, there is, in between, never any crooked or evasive quality, and no emerging or regressing either. This is unlike the ordinary kind of samādhi, which has an entering, an abiding, and an emerging.

Reading note: Yuanying's two technical criteria here — "perfect" (圓, encompassing all phenomena, not just one's own mind) and "wondrous" (妙, inherently present rather than manufactured by effort) — echo the same "great" / "wondrous" pairing he used earlier for "Great Buddha's Crown," now applied specifically to the nature of this one samādhi. His closing point is worth sitting with: an ordinary meditative state has a beginning, a middle, and an end — you enter it, abide in it, and eventually come out of it. The Śūraṅgama samādhi, on his account, has none of that shape, because it was never entered into a state to begin with; it is what was already the case, uncovered rather than constructed.

"Sūtra," and Why It Is Still Called a "Fascicle" 經 · 卷第一

Chinese Source

經之一字是通題，為能詮之文，即詮上四種實法。梵語修多羅，華言契經。上契諸佛所說之理，下契眾生可度之機。又此方聖教稱經，今譯契經，顯是西域聖教，具貫、攝、常、法四義。貫、則貫串所應知之義，令不散失故；攝、則攝受所應度之機，令得解脫故；常、則盡未來際，萬古不能易其說；法、則極十方界，眾生所應遵其軌。此經亦具四義……

卷第一，古來經書，多取軸之制度，舒之可能讀誦，卷之以便供奉，後人易制，未易其名，故仍稱卷。而第一者，數之始也，十卷玄文，次序居首。一總釋名題竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: The single character "sūtra" is the general title — the words that do the expressing, expressing the four substantial dharmas named above it. In Sanskrit, sūtra; in Chinese, "linking-scripture" (契經): linking upward with the principle spoken by all buddhas, and linking downward with the capacities of sentient beings who are to be saved by it. This land already had its own word, "classic" (經), for sacred teaching; translating it as "linking-scripture" makes clear that this is a sacred teaching from the Western Region, possessing four qualities: threading, gathering, constancy, and pattern. "Threading" (貫), because it threads together everything that should be known, so that none of it is scattered or lost. "Gathering" (攝), because it gathers in the capacities of those who are to be saved, so that they may attain liberation. "Constancy" (常), because throughout all future time, across ten thousand ages, its teaching can never be altered. "Pattern" (法), because throughout the ten directions, all sentient beings ought to follow its guiding thread. This sūtra likewise possesses all four qualities. ...

As for "Fascicle One": in ancient times, scriptures were generally made in the form of a scroll around an axle — unrolled, it could be read and recited; rolled up, it was convenient to store with reverence. Later generations changed the physical format but did not change the name, and so the word "fascicle" (卷, literally "scroll") is retained even now. "First" simply marks the beginning of the count: among the ten fascicles of this profound text, this one comes first in sequence.

This concludes the first part: the general explanation of the title.

Why the Buddha Taught This Sūtra 甲二 起教因緣

Chinese Source

法不孤起，起必有由。世間諸事，尚有因像，況無上佛法，豈無因緣耶？今明佛法因緣，有總、有別。總約一代時教，四十九年，或說大乘，或說小乘，或說頓教，或說漸教，無非顯理度生，所顯之理，即佛知見，眾生等有，迷不自知，佛則乘機應世，為其開示，令得悟入。《法華經》云：「如來為一大事因緣故，出現於世。」可見如來出世，即是一段度生之大因緣也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: No teaching arises on its own; wherever a teaching arises, there must be a reason for it. Even ordinary worldly affairs have their causes — how much more so the unsurpassed Buddha-dharma! Surely it, too, does not arise without cause or condition. I will now explain the causes and conditions for the Buddha-dharma under two headings: general and specific.

Speaking generally of the Buddha's forty-nine years of teaching as a whole: whether he taught the Great Vehicle or the Lesser, the sudden teaching or the gradual, it was always nothing but the revealing of a principle in order to save beings — and the principle revealed is precisely the Buddha's own knowledge and seeing, which every sentient being equally possesses, yet fails to recognize through delusion. The Buddha, accordingly, responds to the world as the occasion allows, opening and revealing this to beings so that they may awaken and enter it. The Lotus Sūtra says: "The Tathāgata appears in the world on account of one great cause and condition." This shows that the Tathāgata's appearance in the world is, in every case, this one great cause and condition of saving beings.

一、為開眾生佛之知見，使得清淨故，出現於世。佛知見，乃眾生六根中，所具見、聞、覺、知之性。此性即是佛性，人人本具，無奈埋沒於塵勞煩惱，垢染心中，雖有若無，幾如宅中寶藏；佛為開其本有家珍，使得離垢清淨故。

二、為欲示眾生佛之知見故，出現於世。佛之知見，眾生咸認惟佛獨有，而眾生無分，不知人人皆有，故佛為指示，尋常日用中，眼根見色，耳根聞聲，乃至意根知法，一一無非佛之知見。……

三、欲令眾生悟佛知見故，出現於世。既經如來開示，而眾生自當依教觀心，依理起行，但肯回光返照，照顧二六時中，見色、聞聲，乃至知法，畢竟是誰？照到日久月深，自有豁然貫通，悟明本來是佛時節，方信聖凡不二，生佛平等。

四、欲令眾生，入佛知見故，出現於世。眾生心光外洩，則名為出；若肯時時反觀內照，照到一心本源，則名為入；入無所入，即始覺智，照本覺理，照到惑淨智滿，轉八識成四智，是為入佛知見道故。此為如來出世一大事因緣，亦為諸教總因緣也。

First, the Buddha appears in the world in order to open sentient beings' own knowledge and seeing, so that they may attain purity. This knowledge and seeing is precisely the capacity for seeing, hearing, awareness, and knowing already possessed within sentient beings' six sense-faculties. This capacity is itself the Buddha-nature, which every single being already possesses from the start — yet it lies buried, helplessly, beneath the dust and affliction that defile the mind, so that, although present, it is as good as absent, almost like a treasure buried inside one's own house. The Buddha opens this original family treasure, so that beings may become free of defilement and pure.

Second, the Buddha appears in the world in order to show sentient beings this knowledge and seeing of the Buddha. Beings all assume that this knowledge and seeing belongs to the Buddha alone, and that they themselves have no share in it, not knowing that every single one of them already possesses it. And so the Buddha points it out: in the ordinary business of daily life, the eye-faculty seeing form, the ear-faculty hearing sound, and even the mind-faculty knowing mental objects — every one of these, without exception, is nothing other than the Buddha's own knowledge and seeing. ...

Third, the Buddha appears in the world in order to bring sentient beings to awaken to this knowledge and seeing of the Buddha. Once the Tathāgata has opened and revealed it, beings themselves should, in accordance with the teaching, contemplate their own mind and, in accordance with principle, put it into practice — simply being willing, at every hour of the day, to turn the light of attention back upon themselves and ask: in seeing form, hearing sound, and knowing mental objects, who, in the end, is doing this? Contemplated closely enough, for long enough, there naturally comes a moment of sudden, thorough penetration — awakening to the realization that one has been a Buddha all along — and only then does one truly believe that sage and ordinary being are not two, that sentient beings and Buddhas are equal.

Fourth, the Buddha appears in the world in order to bring sentient beings to enter this knowledge and seeing of the Buddha. When a being's mental light leaks outward, this is called "going out"; when one is willing, again and again, to turn one's contemplation inward until it reaches the original source of the one mind, this is called "entering." Entering where there is, in truth, nowhere to enter is precisely the wisdom of initial enlightenment illuminating the principle of original enlightenment — illuminating it until delusion is purified and wisdom complete, transforming the eight consciousnesses into the four wisdoms. This is what is meant by entering the path of the Buddha's knowledge and seeing. This, then, is the one great cause and condition of the Tathāgata's appearance in the world, and likewise the general cause and condition behind all of the Buddha's teachings.

別約本經因緣，交光法師開有十種，今則惟六：一、恃多聞忽定力；二、警狂慧護邪思；三、指真心顯根性；四、示性定勸實證；五、銷倒想除細惑；六、明二門利今後。

一、恃多聞忽定力：大凡利根之士，好務多聞，不勤定力，於聞、思、修，偏重聞慧，少及思、修，如人說食，終不能飽。故以阿難多聞，誤墮婬室發起大教。觀阿難歸佛所，頂禮悲泣，自述「恨無始來，一向多聞，未全道力」即定力，故慇懃啟請，十方如來所修，得成菩提之大定；足見多聞無功，不逮修習。後責阿難「汝雖歷劫，憶持如來，秘密妙嚴，不如一日修無漏業，遠離世間，憎愛二苦。」又偈云：「將聞持佛佛，何不自聞聞？」佛為恃多聞忽定力，故說此經。

二、警狂慧護邪思：世有大心凡夫，見理高妙，自恃天真，頓捐修證，玩留惡習，了不依佛方便之門，屈於欲魔，無力敵苦。如阿難為摩登伽邪咒所攝，心雖明了，力不自由，賴遇佛頂神咒，方得解脫。故自述見相發心，以佛相好，非欲愛所生為念，意顯婬愛，為定門之冤賊；大定，為破欲之將軍。偈云：「欲漏不先除，畜聞成過誤。」佛為警狂慧護邪思，故說此經。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: Turning now to the causes specific to this sūtra: Master Jiaoguang identified ten; I will reduce them to six. First, relying on broad learning while neglecting the power of samādhi. Second, warning against reckless wisdom, and guarding against wrong thought. Third, pointing out the true mind and revealing the nature of the sense-faculties. Fourth, showing the samādhi of inherent nature, and urging genuine realization. Fifth, dissolving inverted thinking and removing subtle delusion. Sixth, making clear two approaches that benefit both present and future generations.

First, relying on broad learning while neglecting the power of samādhi. Generally speaking, people of sharp faculties love to pursue broad learning but neglect the power of samādhi — among hearing, reflecting, and cultivating, they overweight the wisdom gained from hearing and give little attention to reflection and cultivation, like a person who talks about food but is never actually fed by it. This is exactly why this great teaching is set in motion by Ānanda's own broad learning leading him, by mistake, into a house of ill repute. Observe how, on returning to the Buddha, Ānanda bows and weeps, confessing in his own words that he "regrets that, from beginningless time, he devoted himself only to broad learning and never fully cultivated the power of the path" — that is, the power of samādhi — and so earnestly begs to be taught the great samādhi by which the Tathāgatas of the ten directions attain bodhi. This alone shows that broad learning accomplishes nothing without actual cultivation. Later in the sūtra, the Buddha reproaches Ānanda: "Though you have, for kalpas, memorized and retained the Tathāgata's secret and wondrous adornments of teaching, this does not compare to a single day spent cultivating the undefiled path, far removed from the two worldly sufferings of hatred and craving." And a verse says: "Why not turn that hearing which retains the Buddha's teaching back upon your own hearing?" The Buddha taught this sūtra on account of this fault of relying on broad learning while neglecting the power of samādhi.

Second, warning against reckless wisdom, and guarding against wrong thought. There are, in the world, ambitious ordinary people who glimpse lofty and wondrous principles, rely on their own natural brilliance, abruptly cast aside cultivation and realization, and carelessly retain their bad habits, refusing to rely on the Buddha's skillful gateways of practice — and so they are overcome by the demon of desire, powerless to withstand suffering. It is just so with Ānanda, seized by Mātaṅgī's evil spell: though his mind remained clear, he had no power to act freely, and only through the good fortune of encountering the Buddha's crown-spell did he obtain release. This is why the sūtra has him recount how his resolve first arose from seeing the Buddha's excellent marks, reflecting that they could not have come from desire and lust — the point being to show that lustful desire is the mortal enemy stationed at the very gate of samādhi, while the great samādhi is the general who defeats desire. As the verse says: "If the outflow of desire is not cleared away first, accumulated learning only becomes a further fault." The Buddha taught this sūtra to warn against reckless wisdom and guard against wrong thought.

三、指真心顯根性：一切眾生，多皆錯認肉團之心為心。……故阿難請說諸佛所修大定，佛即首告之曰：「一切眾生，從無始來，生死相續，皆由不知常住真心，性淨明體，用諸妄想，此想不真，故有輪轉。」由是乃有二次徵心，三番破識，十番顯見，佛意欲修大定，須以真心為本修因，顯真心，即大定之全體也。故應加徵問，看阿難是否錯認。……佛為指真心顯根性即如來密因，故說此經。

Third, pointing out the true mind and revealing the nature of the sense-faculties. Nearly all sentient beings mistake the fleshly, physical heart for the mind. This physical organ sits within the body, shaped rather like an inverted lotus — it is a mere provisional appearance, not the true mind at all, and has no capacity to know anything. When people hear this said, they are certain to protest endlessly: "But this mind can know, can think and discriminate — how can you say it has no capacity to know?" One should understand that such a person has made two errors at once: first, mistaking the physical heart for the true mind; second, now further mistaking the functioning of the deluded, discriminating mind for the functioning of that same physical organ. The physical heart is easy enough to refute as not being the true mind — for if this organ truly had the capacity to know, it should still know something even after death, since the organ itself remains; yet once a person has died, though the heart remains, it can no longer think or discriminate at all, which proves it was never the true mind. But the deluded, discriminating mind is far harder to refute as not being the true mind, because beings' delusion and attachment run so deep that the root of this delusion is difficult to pull out — and it is not only ordinary, unreflective people who make this mistake; even the provisional teachings and the Lesser Vehicle likewise mistake consciousness for mind.

This is why, when Ānanda asks to be taught the great samādhi cultivated by all buddhas, the Buddha's very first words to him are: "All sentient beings, from beginningless time, have continued through birth and death solely because they do not recognize the ever-abiding true mind, whose nature is pure and bright, and instead make use of false thinking; and because this thinking is not true, they continue turning in the wheel of transmigration." From this follow the two searches for the location of the mind, the threefold refutation of consciousness, and the tenfold revealing of the nature of seeing — for the Buddha's intent, in teaching the great samādhi, is that the true mind must be taken as the root cause of cultivation, and revealing the true mind is precisely the whole substance of the great samādhi itself. This is why the questioning must be pressed so insistently, to see whether Ānanda has, in fact, made this same mistake. ... The Buddha taught this sūtra in order to point out the true mind and reveal the nature of the sense-faculties — which is precisely the Tathāgata's hidden cause named in the title.

Reading note: Yuanying's third cause directly names the argument that runs through fascicle 1 of the sūtra itself — the "two searches for the location of the mind" and "threefold refutation of consciousness" are the seven-locations dialogue and the fist analogy already translated in full in Ru-Yi's bare-sūtra fascicle 1; the "tenfold revealing of the nature of seeing" is the King Prasenajit dialogue and its sequel translated in fascicle 2. Reading Yuanying's summary here alongside those pages shows exactly how a traditional commentator frames the same material: not as a story to follow, but as a two-step demolition (of the false mind) and construction (of the true one).

四、示性定勸實證：凡夫、外道、小乘、權宗，其所修行，各皆有定，而悉無究竟者。何也？以其徒慕真修，不諳真本，全用識心，錯亂修習。……推其病源，皆由以生滅心，為本修因，不能發明不生滅性也。經中阿難請定，如來首先三番破識者，即是決定，令捨生滅識心，撤去大定之障礙，後乃廣顯見性，不生滅、不動搖，決定令悟自性本定，依真常根性，成因地心，然後圓成，果地修證。……佛為示性定、勸實證，故說此經。

Fourth, showing the samādhi of inherent nature, and urging genuine realization. Ordinary beings, non-Buddhist practitioners, followers of the Lesser Vehicle, and followers of provisional teachings — each of these has some form of samādhi in their own practice, yet none of them reaches the ultimate. Why is this? Because they only long for genuine cultivation without actually knowing its true basis, and so rely entirely on the discriminating consciousness, cultivating in confusion. ... Tracing the root of this sickness, it always comes down to taking the arising-and-ceasing mind as the root cause of cultivation, which can never reveal the unborn, unceasing nature. This is why, when Ānanda asks to be taught samādhi, the Tathāgata's very first move is the threefold refutation of consciousness: this is precisely what decisively removes the arising-and-ceasing, discriminating mind, clearing away the obstruction to the great samādhi. Only afterward does he broadly reveal the nature of seeing — unborn, unceasing, unmoving — decisively bringing about the realization of the samādhi inherent in one's own nature, so that, relying on this true, ever-abiding faculty-nature as the causal-ground mind, cultivation and realization at the level of the fruit can then be perfectly accomplished. ... The Buddha taught this sūtra in order to show the samādhi of inherent nature and urge genuine realization.

五、銷倒想除細惑：良以眾生，元明失照，妄識紛亂，或迷心在身內；或認法居心外；或固執因緣，而繫縛權宗；或謬執自然，而馳騁外計；皆為倒想，足障真修。……但倒想先銷，細惑未盡，雖信諸法唯心，未徹唯心之本源；固知五大圓融，未了圓融之深故，仍能障乎性定。……佛為銷倒想除細惑，故說此經。

Fifth, dissolving inverted thinking and removing subtle delusion. This is because sentient beings, having lost the illumination of their own original brightness, fall into confused, false discrimination — some mistakenly locate the mind inside the body; some take mental objects to lie outside the mind; some cling rigidly to causes and conditions, binding themselves to provisional teachings; some mistakenly cling to spontaneity, running off after non-Buddhist theories. All of these are inverted thinking, fully capable of obstructing genuine cultivation. ... But even once this inverted thinking is dissolved at first, subtle delusion is not yet exhausted: one may believe that all dharmas are mind-only, yet not have penetrated all the way to the very source of mind-only; one may already know that the five great elements interfuse perfectly, yet not have fully understood the depth of that perfect interfusion — and this alone can still obstruct the samādhi of inherent nature. ... The Buddha taught this sūtra in order to dissolve inverted thinking and remove subtle delusion.

六、明二門利今後：惟有圓實教家，方能二門雙具。一、平等門：一心萬法，本原無差，平等一相，心為大總相法門體……二、方便門：於諸法中，分真、分妄；許破、許顯，乃有迷、悟；修、證，種種差別……本經雙具平等、方便二門，當機啟請即含此義。……佛為明二門利今後，故說此經。二起教因緣竟。

Sixth, making clear two approaches that benefit both present and future generations. Only the teaching of the complete and real vehicle can hold both approaches at once. The first is the approach of equality: the one mind and the ten thousand dharmas share, from their very origin, no difference at all — a single, equal mark, mind itself being the great, comprehensive substance underlying every teaching. ... The second is the approach of skillful means: within the various dharmas, true and false are distinguished; refutation and revelation are both permitted; and so there come to be all the various distinctions of delusion and awakening, cultivation and realization. ...

This sūtra holds both the approach of equality and the approach of skillful means at once, and this meaning is already contained in the very way Ānanda, the one addressed by the teaching, makes his request: having asked for the perfectly interfused great samādhi, he further begs for the very first steps of practice. ... The Buddha taught this sūtra in order to make clear these two approaches, for the benefit of present and future generations alike.

This concludes the second part: the causes and conditions for teaching this sūtra.

Six More Preliminaries, Briefly 甲三–甲八

Yuanying's remaining preliminary topics move faster than the first two — each answers a single classificatory question about the sūtra before the commentary turns, in 甲九 and 甲十, to the history of its translation and then its actual text.

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary (甲三, Which Piṭaka and Vehicle It Belongs To): Having established the great cause behind this sūtra, we must now ask which piṭaka and which vehicle it belongs to. Of the three piṭakas — sūtra expounding the training in samādhi, vinaya expounding the training in precepts, and śāstra expounding the training in wisdom — this sūtra, since Ānanda asks for samādhi and the Tathāgata answers with samādhi, properly belongs to the sūtra-piṭaka, though it touches on precepts and wisdom only as aids to samādhi. As for vehicle: of the Great and Lesser Vehicles, this sūtra belongs to the Great, since what the one addressed by the teaching requests, and what is granted, is purely bodhisattva practice. It belongs, more precisely, to the one vehicle held in common with other complete teachings, while also touching on the one vehicle of the distinct teaching — without thereby setting aside the fruits and precepts proper to the Lesser Vehicle, which it also touches on in passing.

已知此經，為大乘教法，未悉義理淺深，分齊如何？……若據五教，顯此經之分齊，經中多談法性，少及法相，縱說法相，亦會歸性……大分正屬終實之教……此則兼屬頓教……此則兼屬圓教……次約論辨義。依《起信論》……大分正齊心生滅門……後會通天臺……四義理淺深竟。

Commentary (甲四, The Relative Depth of Its Doctrine): Having established that this is a Great Vehicle teaching, we must ask how deep its doctrine reaches. Judged by the Xianshou school's five levels of teaching (elementary, initial, final, sudden, and perfect), this sūtra mostly speaks of the nature of dharmas rather than their particular characteristics — and even where it speaks of particular characteristics, it always brings the discussion back to their underlying nature. It belongs, for the most part, to the final, real teaching, while also touching on the sudden teaching and the perfect teaching at various points. Judged instead by the categories of the Awakening of Faith, it corresponds, for the most part, to the gate of mind's arising-and-ceasing, while also touching on the gate of mind's true suchness. Reconciled with the Tiantai school's own four teachings, the same correspondences hold under different names. This concludes the fourth part.

已知此經，義理甚深，未審何為教體？……今依賢首疏《起信論》，略作四門以明教體：一、隨相門……二、唯識門……三、歸性門……四、無礙門……此經四義交徹，四相無礙，三藏圓融，得以無礙為教體。五能詮教體竟。

Commentary (甲五, The Substance of the Teaching That Expresses It): What, then, is the substance in which the teaching itself is grounded? Following Fazang's commentary on the Awakening of Faith, four approaches may be distinguished: the approach of following outward appearance (sound, name, phrase, and text together); the approach of consciousness-only (all teaching, ultimately, not apart from consciousness); the approach of returning to nature (all teaching, ultimately, one with true suchness); and the approach of non-obstruction (mind and its objects, principle and phenomena, phenomena and phenomena, all interpenetrating without obstruction). Since this sūtra's four great meanings interpenetrate freely and its phenomena are mutually unobstructed, with the three piṭakas perfectly fused, its teaching may properly be said to be grounded in non-obstruction itself. This concludes the fifth part.

已知此經，能詮之體，未悉所被何機？今按本經，應分通、局。通：即普被群機……局、即揀擇當機……先明小乘四類：一、回心聲聞；二、並為緣覺；三、並及有學；四、兼為定性……次明帶病四類：一、認識為心；二、恃聞忽定；三、求他加被；四、恃性忘修……六所被機宜竟。

Commentary (甲六, The Capacities of Those It Addresses): This sūtra's audience should be understood in two ways: generally, and specifically. Generally, since it teaches that all sentient beings are, in principle, already Buddhas, its address is universal, extending to every being with a mind at all. Specifically, it particularly addresses four kinds of Lesser Vehicle practitioners turning toward the Great — śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, those still in training, and those fixed in a purely Lesser Vehicle disposition — as well as four kinds of practitioners carrying a particular "sickness": mistaking consciousness for mind, relying on learning while neglecting samādhi, hoping the Buddha's power alone will substitute for one's own effort, and relying on inherent nature while abandoning actual cultivation. Ānanda himself, in fact, exhibits all four of these very faults — which is exactly why he is chosen to set this whole teaching in motion. This concludes the sixth part.

已知此經，被機之廣，未悉何為宗趣？……此經若就通中圓實，以取宗趣，則以悟明心地為宗，證入果地為趣。局約本經，亦分總、別。總以圓定為宗，極果為趣……若明別意，以定宗趣，依交光法師，根據本經，應有六對：謂破、顯；偏、全；悟、入；體、用；行、位；分、滿也。七宗趣通別竟。

Commentary (甲七, Its General and Specific Aims): A school's "aim" (宗趣) names what a teaching centers on, and what it drives toward. Generally, this sūtra centers on awakening to the ground of mind, and drives toward realizing the fruit. Specifically, following Master Jiaoguang, six paired aims may be traced through the text in sequence, each pair's destination becoming the starting point of the next: refuting the false, then revealing the true; the partial view, then the complete; awakening, then entry; essence, then function; practice, then the stages of the path; partial realization, then its perfect fulfillment. This concludes the seventh part.

已知此經，宗趣圓極，未悉說自何時？今先明賢首三時……日出先照時……日升轉照時……日沒還照時……今按此經，義理因緣，通於前後，未能的指何時……如定欲判屬，則應從多分之經義，判歸中轉時，方等會。八說時前後竟。

Commentary (甲八, When in the Buddha's Teaching Career It Was Spoken): Following the Xianshou school's three periods of teaching — the sun's first light striking the highest peaks (the direct teaching of the Huayan assembly), its ascending light reaching progressively lower ground in three further stages (the expedient teachings, from the Āgamas through the Prajñā assemblies), and its final light returning to the peaks at sunset (the Lotus and Nirvāṇa assemblies) — this sūtra's own doctrine and circumstances run through more than one period, and no single moment can be fixed with certainty. If a determination must be made, the balance of the textual evidence places it in the middle, ascending period, among the Vaipulya assemblies. This concludes the eighth part.

Reading note: These six sections move quickly through classical Chinese Buddhist scholastic categories that any serious student of the tradition would recognize on sight — the three piṭakas, the two vehicles, the five (or four) levels of teaching, the fourfold analysis of doctrinal substance, the audience distinctions, the six paired aims, and the three periods of the Buddha's teaching career. Yuanying's own answers place the Śūraṅgama consistently at or near the highest tier available in each scheme — final/sudden/perfect doctrine, the complete vehicle, the Vaipulya period bordering on Prajñā and Lotus — which is itself a claim about the sūtra's stature relative to the rest of the canon, not merely a neutral classification exercise.

The History of Its Translation 甲九 歷明傳譯

Yuanying devotes the ninth preliminary topic to the four people responsible for bringing this sūtra into Chinese: the translator, the oral interpreter, the verifying monk, and the scholar-official who polished the prose.

Chinese Source

唐中天竺沙門般刺密諦譯。……般剌密諦，譯主別名，此云極量，乃才智僧也。……此經藏於龍宮，因龍勝菩薩，至龍宮說法，見龍藏中，有此一經，披閱之下，歎為希有，特默誦而出，以利閻浮眾生……譯主志益此方，初次匿經東來。被守邊官吏查獲，不許出國……乃用極細氈，書寫此經，剖膊潛藏，迨瘡口平復，再請出國，關吏搜查不著，乃得航海而來，於唐神龍元年達廣州……剖膊出經，譯成速回本國，以解邊吏之難。……夫譯主冒禁艱苦，不惜身命，正所謂重法輕身，功莫大焉！

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: "Translated by the Tang-dynasty monk Paramiti of central India." Paramiti, meaning "Utmost Measure," was a monk of great learning and ability. This sūtra, according to tradition, had long been kept in the dragon-king's palace; when the bodhisattva Nāgārjuna visited that palace to teach, he found this one sūtra among the dragon canon, was struck with wonder at it, and committed it to memory in secret so that he might bring its benefit to the beings of this world.

Paramiti, resolved to bring this teaching to China, first attempted to smuggle the text out of his own country, but was caught by border officials, who forbade its export. Undeterred, he wrote the sūtra out in miniature script on extremely fine silk, made an incision in his own arm, and hid the text inside; once the wound had healed, he requested leave to travel abroad once more, and this time passed the border inspection undetected, arriving by sea at Guangzhou in the first year of the Shenlong era of the Tang dynasty. There he cut open his arm, removed the sūtra, completed its translation, and hastened to return to his own country, so as to spare the border officials who had let him through any blame for his earlier attempt. That the translator braved such prohibition and hardship, sparing not even his own body, is truly what is meant by valuing the Dharma over one's own life — a merit beyond measure.

烏萇國沙門彌伽釋迦譯語。……彌伽釋迦，此云能降伏。譯語，即翻音，將梵音變成華語，定言詞成章句，厥功亦偉，故宜並列焉。

羅浮山南樓寺沙門懷迪證譯。……懷迪，取其常懷精進之意。證譯者，謂於音字之中，總為參詳校正。以師久習經論，備諳梵語，前二師雖兼美華文，以乍來此方，恐未盡善，經師證明，可謂盡美盡善，亦未可泯其功也。

"Oral translation by the monk Meghaśikha of Uḍḍiyāna," whose name means "Able to Subdue" — he rendered the Sanskrit sounds into spoken Chinese, fixing the wording into proper sentences, a contribution great enough to deserve its own place in the record.

"Verified by the monk Huaidi of Nanlou Monastery, Mount Luofu" — his name meaning one who "constantly cherishes diligence." To verify a translation means to review and correct both the sounds and the characters throughout. Having long studied the sūtras and treatises and being thoroughly versed in Sanskrit, he ensured that what the first two translators, however skilled, might have left imperfect on first arriving in this land, was brought to full and proper completion — a contribution likewise not to be forgotten.

"Recorded and polished by the disciple of the bodhisattva precepts, former Grand Master of Splendid Happiness, joint manager of affairs with the Secretariat-Chancellery, of Qinghe, Fang Rong." Minister Fang had a long-standing karmic connection with this sūtra: he first requested its translation, then took it down in writing, then polished its prose, and later submitted it to the imperial court. ... Fang Rong's contribution was not only to this sūtra but, in truth, a great contribution to the beings of this land as well. This concludes the ninth part.

Reading note: Yuanying preserves the traditional account of how this sūtra reached China — Paramiti's arm cut open twice to smuggle a silk manuscript across a closed border — as a matter of settled history, not legend to be filtered out. Whatever one makes of the story's literal details, the four names it fixes in place (Paramiti, Meghaśikha, Huaidi, Fang Rong) are also why the sūtra's Chinese prose reads as unusually polished for a translated text: three separate roles — rendering the sounds, verifying the meaning, and polishing the literary style — were kept distinct rather than left to one translator alone.

Beginning the Line-by-Line Commentary: The Six Perfections 甲十 別解文義 · 乙初 序分 · 丙初 證信序

Yuanying's tenth and final preliminary part is his actual commentary on the sūtra's text, which he divides, as is traditional, into three: the preface (序分), the main teaching (正宗分), and the transmission section (流通分). The preface itself divides into the "proof of faith" (證信序) — the opening formula every sūtra shares — and the "occasioning preface" (發起序) specific to this one, namely Ānanda's fall to Mātaṅgī's spell. This installment covers the proof of faith: Yuanying's phrase-by-phrase unpacking of "Thus have I heard: at one time, the Buddha was staying at Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍada's Grove, in the city of Śrāvastī" — traditionally read as six "perfections" (六成就) that together certify a sūtra's authenticity.

Chinese Source

〖經〗如是我聞：一時，佛在室羅筏城，祇桓精舍。

〖講〗如是乃指法之辭，我聞明授受之本；即指此經如是十卷玄文，乃我阿難親從佛聞，由佛傳授而我領受也。又如是者：信順之辭，即信成就；信則言如是，不信則言不如是。……信為道源功德母，長養一切諸善法。……是為信成就。我聞；即聞成就。我之一字，有四種不同：一、凡夫妄執之我；二、外道妄計神我；三、菩薩隨世假我；四、如來法身真我。今阿難稱我，乃隨順世間，假名稱我也。聞者，從耳根發耳識，聞佛聲教，由耳達心，故能記憶。……如是我聞，有四義：一、斷眾疑……二、秉佛囑……三、息諍論……四、異外教……以上皆就事解釋。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Thus have I heard: at one time, the Buddha was staying at Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍada's Grove, in the city of Śrāvastī.

Commentary: "Thus" is a word pointing to the teaching itself; "I heard" makes clear the basis on which it was received and transmitted — that is, this ten-fascicle profound text was heard by me, Ānanda, directly from the Buddha, and I received it as he transmitted it. "Thus" is also a word of faith and assent: it constitutes the perfection of faith. What is believed is called "thus"; what is not believed would be called "not thus." ... Faith is the source of the path, the mother that nourishes every wholesome quality — this is why it is called the perfection of faith.

"I heard" constitutes the perfection of hearing. The word "I" is used in four different senses: the falsely-clung-to self of the ordinary unenlightened person; the falsely-conceived divine self of non-Buddhist paths; the bodhisattva's provisional self, adopted in accordance with worldly convention; and the Tathāgata's true self, the Dharma-body. Here, Ānanda's use of "I" simply follows worldly convention, a provisional designation. "Heard" means that ear-consciousness arose from the ear-faculty, heard the Buddha's spoken teaching, and, passing from ear to mind, could thereby be remembered.

The formula "Thus have I heard" carries four purposes: first, to put an end to the assembly's doubts [when Ānanda first ascended the teaching-seat after the Buddha's death, resembling the Buddha so closely that the assembly briefly doubted whether the Buddha had returned, or Ānanda himself had become a buddha]; second, to fulfill the Buddha's own final instruction [on how every sūtra should open]; third, to put disputes to rest [since Ānanda, only a stream-enterer, might otherwise be challenged on his authority to speak]; fourth, to distinguish this teaching from other traditions [whose scriptures open instead with the words "was" or "will be"]. All of this explains the formula on the level of historical circumstance.

今更約理解釋，如是之法，按本經，如來藏妙真如性為如，如者不動之義，藏性遍滿虛空，充塞法界，湛然凝然，如如不動。一切事究竟堅固為是，是者無非之稱，將一切事相之法，悉心窮究，究到畢竟之處，即所謂徹法流之源底，全事即理，全相皆性，堅固不壞，無一物不是我心，無一法不是我體。

我聞：約理即以無我之真我，起不聞之真聞，聞如是之法，法法皆如，法法皆是，唯一如來藏性，為此經之理體，是為聞成就。一時：即時成就。世事會合，尚待昌期，大法弘宣，豈無嘉運？蓋必假良時，方成法益，師資道合說聽始終，謂之一時：不能定指何時……是為時成就。

Now, explained further on the level of principle: according to this very sūtra, "thus" refers to the wondrous, true, suchness-nature of the tathāgatagarbha itself — "thus" meaning "unmoving." This nature of the storehouse pervades the whole of empty space and fills the entire Dharma-realm, calm and still, thus and unmoving just as it is. "The ultimate firmness of all things" is what is meant by "is" — "is" here meaning "nothing that is otherwise." Investigate every phenomenon and every appearance to its very limit, all the way to its ultimate ground, and one arrives at what is called the true source beneath the flow of all things: every phenomenon is, in full, principle itself; every appearance is, in full, nature itself — firm and indestructible, so that there is not a single thing that is not one's own mind, not a single dharma that is not one's own substance.

"I heard," read on the level of principle, means: from the true self that is no-self, the true hearing that does not [ordinarily] hear arises, and hears this teaching of "thus" — every single dharma being "thus," every single dharma being "is" — this one nature of the tathāgatagarbha alone being the substance of principle underlying this whole sūtra. This is the perfection of hearing.

"At one time" constitutes the perfection of timing. Worldly affairs still require an auspicious convergence to come together — how much more so the proclamation of the great Dharma! It must, likewise, borrow the right occasion to bring about its benefit; the meeting of teacher and disciple, from the beginning of the teaching to its end, is what is meant by "one time" — it cannot be fixed to any single, nameable moment. ... This is the perfection of timing.

佛者：主成就，佛是說法主故。梵語佛陀，譯為覺者，乃大覺悟之者。今按本經，終實教意釋之，覺有三義，作二種解釋：一、本覺、始覺、究竟覺……二、自覺、覺他、覺滿……得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，名之為佛。佛是十種通號之一，乃指本土娑婆教主，即中天竺迦毗羅國，淨飯王太子……是為主成就。

在室羅筏城，祇桓精舍：處成就。……室羅筏城，即僑薩羅國都城……譯為豐德……祗者，具云祗陀，亦云逝多，譯為戰勝，乃匿王太子名……桓即是林。精舍，乃須達多長者，為佛建立……有他經，稱祗樹給孤獨園。祗樹，即祗陀林中諸樹；給孤獨，乃須達多長者之善名。……太子自起門樓，請佛出入，故曰祗樹給孤獨園。《楞嚴》大法得此勝地，可以宏宣，是為處成就。

"The Buddha" constitutes the perfection of the host, since the Buddha is the host who expounds the teaching. In Sanskrit, buddha, translated as "the awakened one" — one of great awakening. According to this sūtra's own final, complete teaching, "awakening" carries three senses, explained in two ways: first, original enlightenment, initial enlightenment, and ultimate enlightenment; second, self-awakening, awakening others, and the fullness of both — attaining unsurpassed, perfect, complete enlightenment, and thereby being called Buddha. "Buddha" is one of the ten universal titles, referring here specifically to the teacher of this our own Sahā world — the crown prince of King Śuddhodana, of the kingdom of Kapilavastu in central India. This is the perfection of the host.

"At Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍada's Grove, in the city of Śrāvastī" constitutes the perfection of place. Śrāvastī was the capital of the kingdom of Kośala, translated as "Land of Abundant Virtue." "Jeta" is short for Jetṛ, also rendered Jeta, meaning "victorious in battle" — the name of King Prasenajit's crown prince, so named because he was born just as his father returned in triumph from war. "Grove" refers to a forest of trees. "Vihāra" [monastery] was built for the Buddha by the elder Sudatta [Anāthapiṇḍada]. ... Other sūtras call it "Jeta's Grove, Anāthapiṇḍada's Park": "Jeta's Grove" refers to the trees within the grove belonging to Prince Jeta, and "Anāthapiṇḍada" ["Giver to the Orphaned and Solitary"] was the honorific name of the elder Sudatta, celebrated for his lifelong generosity to the destitute. [As the traditional story goes, the elder covered the prince's grove floor with gold to purchase it as a gift for the Buddha; the prince, moved by this devotion, donated the trees themselves, and together they built the monastery] — and the prince himself built its gatehouse, so as to welcome the Buddha in and out. Since the great teaching of the Śūraṅgama found so excellent a place in which to be proclaimed, this is the perfection of place.

Reading note: The "six perfections" (六成就) — faith, hearing, timing, host, place, and (in the next installment) assembly — are the traditional Chinese Buddhist reading of the formulaic opening every sūtra shares, treated not as mere boilerplate but as six separate certifications that together establish a text's authenticity: someone reliable heard it (信, 聞), at an identifiable-though-unfixed occasion (時), from a named and qualified teacher (主), in a real place (處), before witnesses (眾, next installment). Yuanying's double reading of "thus" and "I heard" — first as plain historical circumstance, then again as a statement about the tathāgatagarbha itself — is a move he repeats throughout the commentary: the same words certify the text as reliable testimony and, read more deeply, already state the sūtra's central doctrine before the narrative has even properly begun.
