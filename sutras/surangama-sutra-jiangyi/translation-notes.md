# Śūraṅgama Sūtra Jiǎngyì (Master Yuanying's Commentary) Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of Master Yuanying's (圓瑛法師) *Dà Fódǐng Shǒu Léngyán Jīng Jiǎngyì* (大佛頂首楞嚴經講義, "Lecture Notes on the Śūraṅgama Sūtra"). See the companion notes at [`sutras/surangama-sutra/translation-notes.md`](../surangama-sutra/translation-notes.md) for terminology shared with the bare-sūtra translation project.

## Who Yuanying was, and why this text

Master Yuanying (圓瑛, 1878–1953) was one of the most prominent Chinese Buddhist monks of the Republican era — abbot of Tiantong Monastery and Qita Monastery in Ningbo, first chairman of the Chinese Buddhist Association, and a founder of educational and relief institutions across China. By his own account in the preface to this work, he began studying the Śūraṅgama Sūtra at twenty-four, spent over forty years researching it, and did not begin writing this commentary until age sixty-eight, finishing it at seventy-four (completed 1953, the year of his death). It runs to 24 fascicles.

## The sūtra text embedded in the commentary

Yuanying's method, like many classical Chinese commentaries, quotes a passage of the sūtra and then explains it clause by clause. Following the convention already established on this site for the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra* (which interleaves sūtra and treatise passages), we mark the two layers inline:

- Chinese source: bold `〖經〗` for a quoted sūtra passage, bold `〖講〗` for Yuanying's own commentary.
- English translation: **Sūtra:** for the quoted passage, **Commentary:** for Yuanying's explanation.

Where Yuanying quotes a sūtra passage that Ru-Yi has already translated in the companion `surangama-sutra` project, we reuse that exact English wording for the quotation, so a reader comparing both projects sees one consistent translation of the sūtra itself.

## Yuanying's own outline (科判)

Yuanying organizes his entire commentary under a ten-part outline (十門分別), labeled with the Heavenly Stems (甲乙丙丁...) and, within each part, the Earthly Branches:

1. 總釋名題 — General explanation of the title
2. 起教因緣 — The causes and conditions for teaching this sūtra
3. 藏乘攝屬 — Which piṭaka and which vehicle it belongs to
4. 義理淺深 — The relative depth of its doctrine
5. 能詮教體 — The substance of the teaching that expresses it
6. 所被機宜 — The capacities of those it addresses
7. 宗趣通別 — Its general and specific aims
8. 說時前後 — When in the Buddha's teaching career it was spoken
9. 歷明傳譯 — The history of its transmission and translation
10. 別解文義 — Line-by-line explanation of the text itself (divided further into 序分, 正宗分, 流通分 — the preface, main teaching, and transmission sections)

We keep this outline as the page structure for each fascicle rather than inventing new section divisions, and preserve Yuanying's Heavenly-Stem/Earthly-Branch labels (甲一, 乙初, 丙初...) alongside an English translation of each heading.

## Core terminology

In addition to the shared sūtra-narrative terms in the companion notes:

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 十門分別 | the tenfold analysis | Yuanying's overall organizing scheme for the whole commentary (see outline above). |
| 賢首十門 | the Xianshou (Fazang) school's ten gates | The traditional Huayan-school template Yuanying adapts, with his own reordering (see his preface remarks on placing 總釋名題 first). |
| 密因 | the hidden cause | See companion notes. Yuanying glosses this at length as the unborn, unconditioned nature every being already possesses, as opposed to any nature contingent on cultivation. |
| 了義 | complete meaning | Yuanying distinguishes this from ordinary usage in two specific ways — see his gloss under 甲一: "using the [sense-]root rather than [discriminating] consciousness" and "according with [true] nature rather than clinging to appearances." Do not collapse this into a generic "profound teaching." |
| 七種立題 | the seven ways sūtra titles are formed | Person / Dharma / Analogy, singly, in pairs, or all three — the traditional classification scheme Yuanying applies to this sūtra's title before analyzing it. |
| 三身 | the three bodies | Dharmakāya (法身), Saṃbhogakāya (報身), Nirmāṇakāya (應身) — invoked in Yuanying's gloss of "Tathāgata." |
| 二種根本 | the two roots | Fascicle 2: the false root (妄本, the grasping/conditioned mind, root of birth and death) and the true root (真本, the identity of bodhi and nirvāṇa's originally pure substance). Shared with the companion sūtra project's 攀緣心 entry — keep "root" fixed, not "basis" or "source." |
| 識精 | the essential consciousness | Fascicle 2: the eighth consciousness' inherently luminous substance — "essential" translates 精 here (not "refined" or "pure"), since Yuanying glosses it as the single brightness underlying all six sense-faculties' functions. |
| 咄 | "Enough!" | The Buddha's single-syllable shout refuting Ānanda's last claim ("此非汝心" — "that is not your mind"). Render as an exclamation, not translated literally as a word of scolding like "tsk" or "bah," to preserve the sūtra's own dramatic force; Yuanying explicitly likens it to a lion's roar and a sword-stroke. |
| 七處徵心 | the seven-location search for the mind | The popular name for fascicle 2's central episode; Yuanying is careful to note this is technically seven refutations of proposed *locations*, not seven separate instances of "asking where the mind is" (徵心) — the sūtra only asks that question twice. Keep both the popular name and Yuanying's technical correction when discussing this passage. |
| 無生法忍 | the forbearance of the unborn nature of all things | Standard term (anutpattikadharmakṣānti); do not abbreviate to just "non-arising" — Yuanying's gloss stresses both that nothing is seen to arise *or* cease, and that this recognition must be "borne" (忍) as settled understanding, not merely observed in passing. |
| 十番顯見 | the ten displays of seeing | Fascicle 3 onward: the sūtra's positive counterpart to the seven-location search — ten separate demonstrations, one per fascicle-section, that seeing itself is the true mind. Fascicle 3 covers the first four: 顯見是心 (seeing is mind), 顯見不動 (seeing does not move), 顯見不滅 (seeing does not perish), 顯見不失 (seeing is never truly lost). Keep each display's own fixed English gloss consistent every time it recurs across fascicles. |
| 客塵 | guest and dust | Kauṇḍinya's own enlightenment image (fascicle 3): "guest" for whatever does not remain fixed (body, surroundings, the conditioned mind), "dust" for whatever stirs and moves — paired against "host" (主) and "space" (空) for what remains still and unmoving. Distinct from the *other* guest/inn image used later in the sūtra (a listener's conditioned mind as a lodging traveler) — both use guest/host imagery but illustrate different points; do not conflate the two when cross-referencing. |
| 帶妄顯真 | revealing truth while still carrying falseness | Yuanying's framing for the ten displays of seeing as a whole (fascicle 3): what is revealed at this stage is genuinely the true mind, but not yet fully purified of the two subtler kinds of false seeing (individual-karma and shared-karma false seeing, from the companion sūtra project's glossary) — comparable to the second moon: the moon itself is real, with only the pressing-finger's falseness layered on top. Only after all ten displays are complete does the sūtra strip this remaining trace away. |
| 顯見無還 / 顯見不雜 / 顯見無礙 / 顯見不分 | seeing has nowhere to return to / is not mixed with objects / is unobstructed / cannot be divided | Fascicle 4: displays five through eight of the ten. "Nowhere to return" uses the same "eight things returned" teaching as the companion sūtra project's glossary entry; keep "return" (not "revert" or "trace back") as the fixed verb throughout, since Yuanying's commentary plays on the guest/host sense of a borrowed object going back to its owner. |
| 見精明元 | the essence of seeing's own luminous root | Fascicle 4's fuller name for the "essence of seeing" (見精, already glossed in the companion sūtra project) — emphasizes it as the *root* (元) of the eighth consciousness's luminous clarity, not yet the fully purified true mind. Distinct from 識精元明 (the essential consciousness, originally bright) in the two-roots teaching — the two terms describe the same reality from slightly different angles; do not merge their glosses. |
| 是義 / 非是義 | "is this" / "is not this" | Fascicle 4's shorthand (in Mañjuśrī's question) for whether seeing and its objects are identical (是) or separate (非是) — resolved through the second-moon analogy already glossed in the companion sūtra project. Render as "is this" / "is not this" consistently, not "true" / "false," since the sūtra is asking about identity, not veracity. |
| 顯見超情 / 顯見離見 | seeing surpasses every category / seeing moves beyond seeing itself | Fascicle 5: the ninth and tenth (final) displays of seeing. The ninth systematically refutes both 自然 (spontaneity) and 因緣 (cause and condition) as explanations, closing with a four-round "negation of negation" (非因非緣，亦非自然，非不自然，無非不非，無是非是) — render each round literally in sequence, not collapsed into a single paraphrase, since Yuanying's commentary walks through them as four distinct logical moves. The tenth culminates in "見見之時，見非是見" ("seeing seeing itself is not seeing," already glossed in the companion sūtra project). |
| 帶妄顯真 / 剖妄出真 | revealing truth while still carrying falseness / stripping away falseness to reveal pure truth | The two-part structure of the sūtra's entire "pointing directly at the sense-faculty's nature" division (fascicles 3–5 of this commentary): the ten displays of seeing (帶妄顯真) still work with the essence of seeing (見精), which carries a trace of falseness; the section on individual-karma and shared-karma false seeing (剖妄出真, fascicle 5's close) strips that final trace away, arriving at pure, undefiled true seeing. |
| 別業妄見 / 同分妄見 | individual-karma false seeing / shared-karma false seeing | Already glossed in the companion sūtra project's fascicle 2 notes; fascicle 5 of this commentary adds the technical distinction that individual-karma seeing involves both delusion *and* karmic summons (依業招), while shared-karma seeing involves delusion alone (但由惑現) — keep this distinction explicit when explaining why the two are classified separately, not merely as "one person" versus "many people." |
| 會通四科 | harmonizing the four categories | Fascicle 6's opening framework: extending the ten displays of seeing's method to the five aggregates, six sense-entrances, twelve sense-fields, and eighteen elements, showing each to be, examined closely, the tathāgatagarbha. Render 會通 as "harmonizing," not "unifying" or "reconciling" — Yuanying's own gloss stresses that phenomenon and principle turn out to already be the same thing, not two things brought into agreement. |
| 三種破法 | the three refutation strategies | Yuanying's closing note in fascicle 6 (citing Master Jiaoguang / 交光法師's *Zhengmai* commentary): refuting by way of the *condition* (約緣破, removing fuel rather than fighting fire — used throughout the six sense-entrances), by way of *mutual dependency* (更互破, two things propping each other up — used for the twelve sense-fields), and by way of the *essential component* (從要破, as glue is essential to a bow — used for the eighteen elements). Keep these three methods distinguished by name when later fascicles apply them. |
| 蘊 / 陰 | heap / aggregate | Fascicle 6 notes both translations of *skandha*: 陰 ("covering," the older rendering, since the aggregates cover over the true nature) and 蘊 ("accumulation," the newer rendering, since conditioned things pile up together). The companion sūtra project's glossary already fixes "aggregate" as the English rendering throughout — this entry documents Yuanying's own etymological aside, not a change to that convention. |
| 處 / 入 | field / entrance | Fascicle 7's etymological aside on 十二處 (also called 十二入): "entrance" carries two senses — the entrance that enters (根, the faculty, since it enters and takes hold of its object) and the entrance that is entered (塵, the object, since it is the place an object enters into) — the identical duality already glossed for 六入 in fascicle 6, now extended to the twelve sense-fields. "Field" itself (處) is glossed as "a fixed place, a determinate location" — the notion the whole section's refrain ("俱無處所," "has no location at all") is built to dismantle. |
| 交床（牒）法 | the interlocking argument | Fascicle 7's name (in the eye-consciousness discussion) for a recurring rhetorical structure in the sūtra's prose: two refutations run in a crossed, alternating pattern — "if X changes, no boundary is established" set against "if X does not change, no boundary is established," each pair addressing an objection the other raises — "reaching both ends of the argument at once." Keep this name fixed when the pattern recurs in later fascicles. |
| 理事無礙法界 | the dharma-realm of the unobstructed interpenetration of principle and phenomena | Huayan technical term, quoted from Master Jiaoguang at the close of fascicle 7's harmonizing-the-four-categories section: the point at which every phenomenon is recognized as, in its entirety, principle itself, rather than principle and phenomena remaining two separate things brought into agreement (contrast 會通, "harmonizing," already glossed in fascicle 6). One of the ten approaches (十門) of Huayan doctrine; do not abbreviate to a generic "the realm where things aren't obstructed." |
| 獨影境 | isolated shadow-object | Fascicle 7's technical gloss (Yogâcāra terminology) for the mental object (法塵) in the mind-and-mental-phenomena sense-field: unlike the other four sense-objects, it is not a real external object but a "shadow" cast off by the five real sense-objects and taken in by mind-consciousness alone, sorted into wholesome, unwholesome, and neutral instances. Keep "shadow-object," not "mental image" or "representation," since Yuanying's own metaphor is specifically a cast shadow. |
| 三如來藏 | the three aspects of the tathāgatagarbha | Fascicle 8's threefold breakdown of true reality (實相), introduced before the seven great elements: the "empty" tathāgatagarbha (空如來藏, empty of every false appearance — the unchanging substance), the "non-empty" tathāgatagarbha (不空如來藏, complete with every phenomenon of the ten dharma-realms — the function following conditions), and the "empty-and-non-empty" tathāgatagarbha (空不空如來藏, substance and function manifest together, neither absent nor fixed). Keep all three names paired with their one-line gloss whenever cited, since later fascicles may invoke any one of the three on its own. |
| 理具 / 事造 | inherent in principle / brought about in fact | Fascicle 8's recurring distinction for each of the seven great elements: what a phenomenon already possesses, in full, as pure potential (理具— like a matchstick already containing fire's whole power, unstruck) versus what has actually manifested (事造 — the same matchstick, struck). The four categories (fascicles 6–7) were treated as 事造, already-manifested instances; the seven great elements are treated as 理具, inherent potential, hence each is introduced "within the tathāgatagarbha" (如來藏中) rather than "at root, the tathāgatagarbha" (本如來藏) — keep this preposition distinction intact in translation, since Yuanying flags it explicitly as marking the difference. |
| 帝網千珠 | Indra's net of a thousand jewels | Classical Huayan image (fascicle 8, on why the seeing-nature can serve as both the general characteristic underlying every phenomenon and one specific characteristic among the seven great elements): any one jewel in the net contains the reflection of every other, and every other converges in it — general and specific characteristic interchange freely depending on which jewel is taken as the reference point. |
| 得旨忘筌 | grasping the point and letting go of the means used to reach it | Fascicle 8's closing citation from the *Zhǐzhǎng* commentary (a Zhuangzi-derived idiom, literally "getting the fish and forgetting the trap"), applied to why consciousness is refuted mercilessly in the sūtra's opening fascicles yet welcomed as fully the tathāgatagarbha by fascicle 8's close — the refutation was always a means, not the destination. |
| 焦芽敗種 | scorched sprouts and rotten seeds | Fascicle 8's image (via the *Zhǐzhǎng* commentary) for those who attain only a fixed, one-sided realization (the śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha path) and never turn toward the Great Vehicle — seeds and sprouts with no capacity left to grow further. |
| 三細六麤 | the three subtle marks, the six coarse marks | Fascicle 9's inherited *Awakening of Faith* framework for how ignorance unfolds into a world: the three subtle marks (karmic mark 業相, turning mark 轉相 — the seeing-portion, and manifesting mark 現相 — the object first appearing) arise directly from "the nature of awareness is necessarily bright" (性覺必明，妄為明覺, already glossed in the companion sūtra project); the six coarse marks (intellect 智相, continuity 相續相, grasping-and-clinging 執取相, conceptualized names 計名字相, initiating karma 起業相, and suffering bound by karma 業繫苦相) build on these in turn, ending in actual karmic action. Yuanying credits this sūtra with making explicit *why* each mark gives rise to the next — a causal explanation he notes the *Awakening of Faith* itself never spells out. |
| 三種相續 | the three continuities | Fascicle 9's threefold answer to how the world, once suddenly arisen, keeps going: world-continuity (世界相續, wind/gold/fire/water building a physical world, ocean, continent, mountain, and forest each becoming the "seed" of the next), sentient-being continuity (眾生相續, the mechanics of conception and the four kinds of birth), and karma-continuity (業果相續, the cycle of mutual debt through killing, stealing, and sexual desire). Distinct from, but built out of, sentient-being continuity — see Yuanying's own note distinguishing the two (fascicle 9, closing the karma-continuity section). |
| 理事無礙 / 事事無礙 | the unobstructed interpenetration of principle and phenomena / of phenomena with one another | Fascicle 10's Huayan doctrinal pair, illustrated with concrete images: "one becomes countless, and countless become one" (the first); "the small appears within the large, the large within the small" (the second) — worked out further through "unmoving from the place of practice, it pervades the worlds of the ten directions" and "upon the tip of a single hair, a jeweled king's realm appears." Keep the two phrases distinguished — the first concerns principle's relation to phenomena, the second concerns phenomena's relation to each other. |
| 十玄門 | the ten profound gates | Huayan's classical list of ten ways phenomena interpenetrate without obstruction; fascicle 10 names two by name as it illustrates 理事無礙/事事無礙 — 一多相容門 ("one and many mutually containing each other") and 廣陝自在門 ("vastness and narrowness each at ease"). Keep each gate's own name attached when cited, rather than folding it into a generic "interpenetration," since later fascicles may cite others from the same list of ten. |
| 演若達多 | Yajñadatta | Already glossed in the companion sūtra project's notes, referencing the story's brief, unnamed appearance in that project's fascicle 4. Fascicle 10 of this commentary is where the story is actually told in full for the first time, with the name given — Yuanying glosses it as "received at the shrine" (祠接), since his parents prayed to the spirits at a shrine for his birth. Use this jiangyi fascicle, not the sūtra fascicle 4 excerpt, as the canonical full telling when cross-referencing. |
| 戲論 | idle theorizing | Fascicle 11: Yuanying's recurring term for Ānanda's (and Pūrṇa's) attachment to causation and spontaneity as competing explanations, and for mere erudition without practice generally. Keep "idle theorizing" as the fixed gloss wherever 戲論 stands alone as a noun; the one sūtra-quotation sentence reused verbatim from the companion sūtra project's fascicle 4 ("此句方名無戲論法") keeps that project's own established wording, "free of empty argument," for that specific line only — the two renderings describe the same underlying term and are not a site-wide inconsistency. |
| 二決定義 | the two decisive meanings | Already named in the companion sūtra project's fascicle 4 notes; fascicle 11 of this commentary is where Yuanying's own detailed exposition of both actually appears — the first (cause matching fruit, developed through the five turbidities and the teaching on clarifying them) and the second (resolving the knot exactly where it is tied, in the six sense-faculties). Treat this jiangyi fascicle as the canonical full unpacking of the term going forward, the way fascicle 10 became canonical for the Yajñadatta story. |
| 從根解結 | resolving the knot at the sense-faculty's own root | Fascicle 11's central practice image for the second decisive meaning: the six sense-faculties are themselves where the "knot" of affliction was tied (via the six consciousnesses acting as "accomplices to a thief," 六為賊媒), and so liberation must untie it there rather than elsewhere. Expect this image — and the companion knot-untying analogy ("someone who cannot see where a knot is tied can never know how to untie it") — to recur through the fascicles ahead, culminating in the six-knot / five-aggregate framework of Avalokiteśvara's own perfect penetration. |
| 澄濁 | clarifying turbidity | Fascicle 11's cultivation-stage teaching, entirely absent from the companion sūtra project (translated fresh here): the image of muddy water settled in a still vessel, its sediment sinking of itself. "The first subduing of the guest-dust afflictions" (初伏客塵煩惱) marks the ten-faiths stage, where the two obstructions' actual manifestation is merely subdued, corresponding to sand and mud merely settling; "the permanent severing of the root of ignorance" (永斷根本無明) marks full severing at the first ground, corresponding to the mud being removed entirely, leaving pure water. Note that "guest-dust" (客塵) here is a third, distinct usage from the two already logged under that entry above — here it names the two obstructions' actual manifestation specifically, not Kauṇḍinya's enlightenment image nor the conditioned-mind-as-lodging-traveler image; keep all three senses distinguished when cross-referencing. |
| 黏湛發見 / 脫黏內伏 | sticking to what is calm and giving rise to seeing / slipping loose from what it clings to and turning inward to subside | Fascicle 12's mechanism for how each of the six sense-organs forms in the first place: awareness "sticks" (黏) to a pair of opposed sense-objects — brightness/darkness for the eye, motion/stillness for the ear, passage/obstruction for the nose, blandness/change for the tongue, separation/contact for the body, arising/ceasing for the mind — and this sticking gives rise to that faculty's own essence (見精/聞精/etc.), which then further "sticks" to its object to form first the subtle ("superior") faculty and then the coarse, physical organ. Untying the knot reverses this exact mechanism: "slipping loose from what it clings to" (脫黏) is the technical undoing of this same sticking. Keep "sticking" as the fixed verb for 黏 throughout — it is a mechanical, almost adhesive image in Yuanying's own gloss, not a metaphor to be softened into "attaching" or "clinging" (which are reserved for 執/著 elsewhere in this glossary). |
| 六根互用 | the interchangeable use of the six sense-faculties | Fascicle 12's wondrous attainment following from untying even a single sense-faculty: faculty and object both let go of together, "a solitary, spiritual light shining forth alone," so that any one faculty can perform any other's function. Illustrated with six fixed examples, all named individuals in the assembly: Aniruddha sees without eyes (a heavenly eye earned after losing his sight to excessive sleep, then excessive diligence), the nāga Upananda hears through his horns, the goddess of the river Gaṅgā smells without a nose, Gavāṃpati tastes without an ordinary tongue, the space-god Śūnya feels touch with no body at all, and Mahākāśyapa — his mind-faculty "long since ceased" in the attainment of cessation — knows all things without depending on conscious thought. Keep this set of six paired names and faculties fixed when cited again, the way the fifty states of demonic interference or the twelve categories of birth are kept as fixed lists elsewhere on this site. |
| 根塵同源，縛脫無二 | the sense-root and its object share the same source; bondage and liberation are not two different things | Fascicle 12's closing formula (quoting the sūtra directly, reused from the companion project's fascicle 5) for why the Buddhas point to the six sense-faculties alone, never the sense-object or consciousness, as both the knot and its untying. Root and object are one source under two aspects (Yuanying glosses this via Yogâcāra's seeing-portion/appearing-portion pair, image: a snail's two horns, one head); consciousness is dismissed separately as merely false, "like flowers conjured in the sky," with no substance to serve as either knot or key. |
| 交蘆 | two leaning reeds | Already glossed in the companion sūtra project's notes (fascicle 5's central verse) as the image for how sense-root and sense-object have no independent nature apart from each other. Fascicle 12 of this commentary is where the prose teaching behind that verse is actually unpacked in full, with Yuanying drawing out three distinct points from the single image: mutual dependence (neither stands without the other), shared origin (one substance, not two), and the joint denial of every possible position (neither faculty, object, emptiness, nor existence can rightly be asserted alone). Use this jiangyi fascicle, not the sūtra fascicle 5 verse alone, as the canonical unpacking when cross-referencing. |
| 二十五圓通 | the twenty-five [approaches to] perfect penetration | The sūtra's central structural device, beginning in fascicle 13: twenty-five disciples, each asked the same question ("at your very first resolve, which one brought you to perfect penetration?"), testify in turn — six through the sense-objects, five through the sense-faculties, six through the six consciousnesses, seven through the great elements, and, last, Avalokiteśvara's own testimony through the ear-faculty (fascicles ahead). None is framed as superior in the telling itself; Mañjuśrī's own judgment among all twenty-five, singling out the ear-faculty as best suited to this particular world, comes only afterward. Disciple names follow the companion sūtra project's own established transliterations where it already lists them (its fascicle 5's condensed survey covers all twenty-five) — e.g. "Gandhavyūha, the Fragrance-Adorned Youth" (not "Gandhahastin"), "Kṣudrapanthaka" (not "Cūḍapanthaka") — even though this jiangyi project's own fuller canonical quotations of each testimony are translated fresh. |
| 結心 | the very center of the knot | Fascicle 13's image, within the six-knotted-cloth demonstration, for the middle way itself: pulling the knotted cloth left or right never unties it (the two extremes of clinging to existence and clinging to emptiness), only working at the knot's own center does ("if you wish to untie it, you must go to the very center of the knot," reused verbatim from the companion sūtra project). Yuanying is explicit that this is not a third position synthesized from emptiness and existence, nor one set apart from both, but simply the sense-faculty's own nature recognized as already free of both extremes. Expect this image to recur wherever the six knots are discussed in the fascicles ahead. |
| 事念 / 理念 | recollection through actual practice / recollection through principle | Fascicle 14's core distinction, in Yuanying's extended gloss on Mahāsthāmaprāpta's testimony, for two depths of reciting the Buddha's name (持名念佛): 事念 holds mind and Buddha's name together as subject and object, single-mindedly, word by word — sufficient on its own for rebirth in the Pure Land (middle three grades of the nine); 理念 turns this same recitation back to see that the recollecting mind and the Buddha recollected are not two ("apart from this mind there is no Buddha; apart from this Buddha there is no mind"), reaching the upper three grades. Yuanying stresses the four traditional forms of buddha-recollection (持名/觀像/觀想/實相) converge here — deepening from one to the next, yet 理念's own fruit runs back through all three of the others as well. Keep these two terms as the fixed pair; do not collapse into a single English "mindfulness of the Buddha." |
| 都攝六根，淨念相繼 | gathering in all six sense-faculties together at once, this pure recollection continuing without a break | Mahāsthāmaprāpta's own closing formula, fascicle 14 — among the single most quoted lines from this sūtra in Chinese Buddhist practice generally. Distinguish carefully from the ordinary sense-faculty testimonies: this is not cultivation through any one faculty, but the deliberate refusal to select any single faculty at all (我無選擇), gathering the "single, essential brightness" (一精明) that underlies all six at once by fixing it entirely on reciting the Buddha's name. Keep "gathering in" fixed for 攝 here (distinct from "gathering in" sentient beings, 攝受, used elsewhere in the same testimony for Mahāsthāmaprāpta drawing practitioners toward the Pure Land — same English verb, two distinct technical senses in the same passage; disambiguate by context when both appear together). |
| 三輪不思議 | the three inconceivable wheels | Fascicle 15: the bodhisattva's threefold inconceivable karmic function underlying Avalokiteśvara's thirty-two responsive bodies and fourteen kinds of fearlessness — the wheel of the body (manifesting whatever body brings liberation), the wheel of speech (expounding whatever dharma brings liberation), and the wheel of mind (discerning each being's own capacity with certainty, prior to and governing the other two). "Wheel" carries the sense of conveying and transporting beings across. |
| 三十二應 | the thirty-two manifested bodies | Fascicle 15: Avalokiteśvara's own fixed list of responsive bodies, from a Buddha's body down to a body matched to any non-human being, manifested however each being's own capacity calls for. Keep the list's own sixfold grouping (sagely vehicles / heavens / human realm / eight kinds of spirits / humans who cultivate as humans / non-human beings) intact when citing, the way the fifty states of demonic interference are kept as a fixed list elsewhere in this glossary. Partially overlaps but does not exactly match the Lotus Sūtra's own Universal Gate chapter list — Yuanying notes the two sūtras "each select and omit as they see fit" to arrive at the same number of thirty-two. |
| 十四無畏 | the fourteen kinds of fearlessness | Fascicle 15: Avalokiteśvara's own fixed list of fourteen protections bestowed on beings who call on this name, grouped by Yuanying into four: eight from danger (fire, water, rākṣasas, the sword, ghosts, chains, bandits, and the total of suffering itself), three from the three poisons (craving, aversion, delusion), two in answering prayer (for a son, for a daughter — the passage where Yuanying recounts his own birth story), and one through simply holding the name itself, equal in merit to holding sixty-two Ganges' sands of other bodhisattvas' names combined. |
| 四不思議 | the four inconceivable virtues | Fascicle 16: the close of Avalokiteśvara's testimony, closely paired with the thirty-two responses and fourteen kinds of fearlessness — countless heads, arms, and eyes manifested through the same body, together with boundless secret mantras (同體形咒); manifesting a different body and mantra for each occasion, bestowing fearlessness directly (異體形咒); moving beings, wherever the bodhisattva travels, to give up their own treasures unprompted (破貪感求, illustrated with the Lotus Sūtra's pearl-necklace episode); and making offerings equally to every buddha and to every sentient being's own request alike (供養佛生). Keep grouped under these four names when cited, the way the thirty-two responses and fourteen kinds of fearlessness are kept as fixed lists. |
| 文殊揀選偈 | Mañjuśrī's verse of selection | Fascicle 16: Mañjuśrī's long verse reply, commanded by the Buddha to judge among all twenty-five accounts of perfect penetration for Ānanda's sake and for beings of the final age. Structured in six movements Yuanying names explicitly: opening the selection at its source (the sea of awareness, and how world and self arise from it), examining and setting aside each of the other twenty-four gates in turn, selecting the ear-faculty alone (its three kinds of true reality — perfect, all-penetrating, ever-abiding — and the practice of turning hearing back upon itself), urging everyone to this same practice, reporting back to the Buddha, and a closing request for blessing. The sūtra text of this entire verse was already translated in the companion sūtra project's fascicle 6 as a single continuous verse-box; this jiangyi project's fascicle 16 breaks it into these same six movements to match Yuanying's own clause-by-clause commentary, reusing that existing translation verbatim throughout. |
| 楞嚴壇場 | the Śūraṅgama ritual platform | Fascicle 17: the elaborate, symbol-laden platform Ānanda is taught to build for the hundred-day practice — white bull's dung mixed with sandalwood and nine other kinds of incense plastered over cleared, yellow earth; an octagonal shape (the eightfold correct path folded into itself); a central lotus holding an alms-bowl of eighth-month dew; eight mirrors surrounding it (the eighth consciousness turned into great mirror wisdom) matched by eight more suspended above (causal stage already containing the fruit-stage ocean); sixteen offerings of milk-based food (the eightfold path's own flavor, doubled for self-cultivation and teaching others); images of Vairocana, Śākyamuni, Maitreya, Akṣobhya, Amitābha, and Avalokiteśvara at center, Vajragarbha and a set of guardian spirits at the sides. Yuanying reads every element symbolically, following the Precious Mirror Commentary (寶鏡疏) throughout as the one source that "interprets every symbol, from beginning to end, in a way that accords with the very method of cultivating perfect penetration" — keep this same symbolic mapping fixed if this platform is described again in a later fascicle. |

## Register

- Yuanying writes in a lucid, relatively modern classical Chinese (白話夾雜文言), not the denser style of Tang-dynasty commentarial writing — the English should likewise read as clear expository prose, not artificially archaic.
- Where Yuanying poses a rhetorical question-and-answer to unpack a term (a common move — e.g. "問：... 答：..."), preserve the question-and-answer form in English rather than flattening it into a single explanatory sentence; it is part of how he teaches.
- Mark every fascicle's status honestly. Given the size of each fascicle (Yuanying's fascicle 1 alone runs to roughly 40,000–50,000 Chinese characters — considerably longer than first estimated from the table of contents alone), a fascicle page may cover only one numbered section of Yuanying's ten-part outline at a time. Label each page clearly as covering "part of fascicle 1" (etc.) rather than implying the whole fascicle is finished.
