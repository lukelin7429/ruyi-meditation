Notes on the Tiantai Transmission of the Buddha-Mind Seal

This short treatise by Huaize (fl. early fourteenth century), a Tiantai monk of the Yuan dynasty, sets out in one continuous essay what the Tiantai school regards as the true "mind-seal" handed down from the Buddha. Its central claim, taken over from Zhili and Zhanran, is that the nature includes not only good but also evil (inherent evil), so that the ordinary deluded mind of the sixth consciousness, with all its cultivated evil, is itself the three thousand realms and need not be destroyed to reveal buddhahood. From this Huaize explains how the three causes, the three truths, and the threefold contemplation fit together, and why buddhas "cut off" cultivated evil while never cutting off inherent evil. He then traces the lineage from Śākyamuni through the twenty-four Indian ancestors to Huiwen, Huisi, Zhiyi, Guanding, Zhanran, and Zhili, rejects the Chan account of a "separate transmission outside the teaching," and argues that readings of "this very mind is the Buddha" that point only to a pure, true mind amount to the distinct teaching's "mere middle." The essay closes by distinguishing transmission for one's own practice from the entrusting of the Dharma to Kāśyapa for the sake of others.

Notes on the Tiantai Transmission of the Buddha-Mind Seal

Chinese Source

元傳天台宗教興教大師虎谿沙門懷則述

Ru-Yi Translation

Set forth by Huaize of Huxi, a śramaṇa, Great Master Xingjiao, who transmitted the teaching of the Tiantai school, Yuan dynasty.

Inherent Evil and the Meaning of Identity

Chinese Source

只一具字，彌顯今宗以性具善。他師亦知具惡緣了，他皆莫測。是知今家性具之功，功在性惡。若無性惡，必須破九界修惡顯佛界性善，是為緣理斷九。非今所論。故《止觀》所明十乘妙觀，觀於陰等十境三障四魔，一一皆成圓妙三諦。此乃發心立行之體格，豈有圓頓更過於此？初心修觀必先內心，故於三科揀却界入，復於五陰又除前四，的取識陰為所觀境，如去丈就尺、去尺就寸，是為總無明心。若就總明別，即第六識，如伐樹得根、灸病得穴，千枝百病自然消殞。若不入者，然後歷餘一心、例餘陰入，乃至九境待發方觀，不發不觀莫不咸爾。方顯九界三道修惡，當體即是性惡法門，性惡融通無法不趣，任運攝得佛界性善。修惡既即性惡，修惡無所破、性惡無所顯，是為全惡，是惡即義方成。是則今家明即永異諸師，以非二物相合、亦非背面相翻，直須當體全是方名為即。何須斷除煩惱生死，方顯佛界菩提涅槃耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Just the one word "include" makes our present school all the more evident. That the nature includes good, other masters also know; but that the conditioning and revealing [causes] include evil, none of them can fathom. Thus we know that the merit of our school's [teaching of] inherent inclusion lies in inherent evil. If there were no inherent evil, one would have to break the cultivated evil of the nine realms in order to reveal the inherent good of the buddha-realm; this is "taking principle as the object and severing the nine [realms]," which is not what we are discussing here. Therefore the wondrous contemplation of the ten modes explained in Calming and Contemplation contemplates the ten objects — the aggregates and the rest — and the three obstructions and four māras, and each and every one becomes the perfect and wondrous threefold truth. This is the essential framework for arousing the mind and establishing practice; how could anything perfect and sudden surpass it? At the initial mind, cultivating contemplation must begin with one's own mind. Therefore among the three categories one sets aside the realms and the entrances, and among the five aggregates one again removes the first four, taking precisely the aggregate of consciousness as the object contemplated — like setting aside the ten-foot measure for the foot, and the foot for the inch. This is the mind of ignorance in general. If one clarifies the specific within the general, it is the sixth consciousness — like felling a tree by getting at its root, or treating an illness with moxa by finding the right point: the thousand branches and hundred illnesses wither away of themselves. If one does not enter [in this way], one then passes through the remaining [dharmas] of the single mind and applies the same to the remaining aggregates and entrances; and the other nine objects are contemplated only when they arise — if they do not arise they are not contemplated. All are like this. Only then is it revealed that the cultivated evil of the three paths in the nine realms is, in its very substance, the Dharma-gate of inherent evil. Inherent evil fuses and penetrates, so that there is no dharma that does not tend toward it, and it effortlessly takes in the inherent good of the buddha-realm. Since cultivated evil is identical to inherent evil, in cultivated evil there is nothing to break, and in inherent evil there is nothing to reveal. This is "the whole of evil is evil"; only then is the meaning of identity accomplished. Thus our school's explanation of identity is forever different from that of the other masters: it is not two things joining together, nor back and front turning over into each other; only when [a thing] in its very substance is wholly [the other] is it called identity. Why must one cut off afflictions and birth-and-death before the bodhi and nirvāṇa of the buddha-realm can be revealed?

Inherent Good and Evil under Other Names

Chinese Source

又應須了此性善惡，在諸大乘立名不同、廣略有異。立名不同者，《華嚴》云：能隨染淨緣，遂分十法界，迷則十界俱染，悟則十界俱淨。十法界離合讀之，三因具足：三字合呼，九界為惡正因，佛界為善正因；十字獨呼、法界合呼，即了因；十法合呼、界字獨呼，即緣因。《法華》云：諸法實相不出權實。諸法是同體權中善惡緣了，實相是同體實中善惡正因。九界十如即惡緣因，佛界十如即善緣因。三轉讀之，了正不缺。《涅槃經》中，闡提、善人二人俱有性善性惡，名為善惡緣因。三因既妙，言緣必具了正，言了必具正緣，言正必具緣了。一必具三、三即是一，毋得守語害圓誣罔聖意。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, one should understand that this inherent good and evil is given different names in the various Mahāyāna [scriptures], and is treated more broadly or more briefly. As for the different names: the Avataṃsaka says, "It can follow defiled and pure conditions, and so divides into the ten dharma-realms; in delusion all ten realms are defiled, in awakening all ten realms are pure." Reading "ten dharma-realms" by separating and combining [its characters], the three causes are complete. Calling the three characters together, the nine realms are the correct cause of evil and the buddha-realm the correct cause of good. Calling "ten" alone and "dharma-realm" together, it is the revealing cause. Calling "ten dharmas" together and "realm" alone, it is the conditioning cause. The Lotus says, "the true mark of all dharmas"; this does not go beyond the provisional and the real. "All dharmas" are the conditioning and revealing [causes] of good and evil within the provisional of the same substance; "the true mark" is the correct cause of good and evil within the real of the same substance. The ten suchnesses of the nine realms are the conditioning cause of evil; the ten suchnesses of the buddha-realm are the conditioning cause of good. Reading it in the three turnings, the revealing and correct [causes] are not lacking. In the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, the icchantika and the good person both have inherent good and inherent evil; these are called the conditioning causes of good and evil. Since the three causes are wondrous, to speak of the conditioning necessarily includes the revealing and the correct; to speak of the revealing necessarily includes the correct and the conditioning; to speak of the correct necessarily includes the conditioning and the revealing. One necessarily includes three, and three are identical to one. One must not cling to the words, harming the perfect [teaching] and slandering the intent of the sages.

Chinese Source

若爾，九界三因，性染了因、性惡緣因，染惡不二是惡正因，豈唯局修？佛界三因，性善緣因、性淨了因，善淨不二即善正因。此性善惡，亦名性淨性穢，或名理明理暗，或名常無常雙寂之體。如《請觀音》，或單名毒害，毒害即性惡，皆一體之異名也。

Ru-Yi Translation

If so, then the three causes of the nine realms — inherent defilement as the revealing cause, inherent evil as the conditioning cause, and the nonduality of defilement and evil as the correct cause of evil — how could they be confined to cultivation alone? The three causes of the buddha-realm: inherent good is the conditioning cause, inherent purity is the revealing cause, and the nonduality of good and purity is the correct cause of good. This inherent good and evil is also called inherent purity and inherent impurity, or the brightness and darkness of principle, or the substance in which permanence and impermanence are both stilled. As in the Invoking Avalokiteśvara, it is sometimes simply called "poison and harm"; poison and harm is inherent evil. All are different names for a single substance.

The Three Thousand in a Single Thought of Cultivated Evil

Chinese Source

隨機利鈍廣略有異者，略則十界、廣則三千，故知善惡不出十界。十界性融互具成百界，界十如則成千如，假名一千。五陰一千、國土一千，如此三千現前一念修惡之心本來具足，非造作而成、非相生而然、非相含而然。一念不在前、三千不在後，一念不少、三千不多。須知情可破，法不可破，執法成病亦須破。是則善惡淨穢是法門理體，體本明淨不斷纖毫，是則斷證迷悟但約染淨而論。往人無擇法眼，情理不分、藥病不辨，纔聞空中名遮一相不立，便作斷滅而解；假觀名照三千宛然，定謂三千立法。若三諦俱遮，又如何立法耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

As for being treated more broadly or more briefly according to sharp or dull capacities: briefly, it is the ten realms; broadly, the three thousand. Thus we know that good and evil do not go beyond the ten realms. The ten realms, their natures fused, mutually include one another and make a hundred realms; each realm having the ten suchnesses makes a thousand suchnesses. Provisionally named [beings], a thousand; the five aggregates, a thousand; the lands, a thousand. These three thousand are originally complete in the present single thought, the mind of cultivated evil — not brought about by fabrication, not so by mutual generation, not so by mutual containment. The single thought is not before and the three thousand are not after; the single thought is not fewer and the three thousand are not more. One must know that feelings can be broken but dharmas cannot be broken; yet clinging to dharmas becomes an illness, and that too must be broken. Thus good and evil, purity and impurity, are the substance-in-principle of the Dharma-gates; that substance is originally bright and pure, and not a hair of it is cut off. Thus severing and realizing, delusion and awakening, are discussed only in terms of defilement and purity. People of the past lacked the eye that discerns dharmas: they did not separate feeling from principle, nor tell the medicine from the illness. As soon as they heard that the empty and the middle are called "negating," with not a single mark established, they understood it as annihilation; [hearing that] the contemplation of the provisional is called "illuminating," with the three thousand distinctly present, they concluded that the three thousand are dharmas to be established. If all three truths negate, how then are dharmas established?

Chinese Source

迷情須破，故用即空即假即中。達此一念修惡之心，即是三千妙境。修惡既即性惡，是理具三千，而此修惡便是妙事三千。但觀理具俱破俱立俱是法界，自然攝得事用三千。三千皆實相相宛然，事理本融非頭數法，不屬所破寧非所顯，故曰諸佛不斷性惡，闡提不斷性善。點此一意，眾滯自消。

Ru-Yi Translation

Deluded feelings must be broken; therefore one uses "identical to the empty, identical to the provisional, identical to the middle." Penetrate this single thought, the mind of cultivated evil: it is the wondrous object of the three thousand. Since cultivated evil is identical to inherent evil, it is the three thousand inherently included in principle, and this cultivated evil is itself the wondrous phenomenal three thousand. Just contemplate what is inherently included in principle — all broken, all established, all the dharma-realm — and one naturally takes in the three thousand of phenomenal function. The three thousand are all the true mark, their marks distinctly present; phenomena and principle are originally fused and are not dharmas to be counted one by one. Not belonging to what is broken, how could they not be what is revealed? Hence it is said, "The buddhas do not cut off inherent evil; the icchantika does not cut off inherent good." Once this one point is made, the many blockages dissolve of themselves.

Severing and Not Severing

Chinese Source

問曰：闡提與佛斷何等善惡？答：闡提斷修善盡、修惡滿足，諸佛斷修惡盡、修善滿足。問：修善修惡既是妙事，乃屬所顯，何名所破？答：修善惡即性善惡，無修善惡可論，斯是斷義，故諸佛斷修惡盡，闡提斷修善盡。修善惡既即性善惡，修善惡何嘗斷？斯不斷義。斷與不斷，妙在其中。問：闡提不斷性善，修善得起。諸佛不斷性惡，還起修惡否？答：闡提不達性善，為善所染，故修善得起，廣治諸惡。諸佛能達於惡，故於惡自在，惡不復起。廣用諸惡化度眾生，妙用無染，名惡法門。雖無染礙之相，而有性具之相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What good and evil have the icchantika and the Buddha cut off? Answer: The icchantika has cut off cultivated good completely and is full of cultivated evil; the buddhas have cut off cultivated evil completely and are full of cultivated good. Question: Since cultivated good and cultivated evil are wondrous phenomena and belong to what is revealed, why are they called what is broken? Answer: Cultivated good and evil are identical to inherent good and evil, so there is no cultivated good and evil to speak of; this is the meaning of severing. Hence the buddhas have cut off cultivated evil completely, and the icchantika has cut off cultivated good completely. Since cultivated good and evil are identical to inherent good and evil, when have cultivated good and evil ever been cut off? This is the meaning of not severing. Severing and not severing — the wonder lies within them. Question: The icchantika does not cut off inherent good, so cultivated good can arise. The buddhas do not cut off inherent evil; do they then give rise to cultivated evil again? Answer: The icchantika does not penetrate inherent good and is defiled by good; therefore cultivated good can arise and broadly cure the various evils. The buddhas are able to penetrate evil; therefore they are free with regard to evil, and evil does not arise again. They broadly use the various evils to transform and deliver beings, a wondrous function without defilement; this is called the Dharma-gate of evil. Although there is no mark of defilement or obstruction, there is the mark of inherent inclusion.

The Three Truths, Three Contemplations, and Three Functions

Chinese Source

博地但理名字，初聞觀行未顯驗體仍迷，六根似發，初住分見，妙覺果成，究竟明顯，是則理須親證，其相方彰。如曹公相隱解衣方見，事可比知。如孫劉相顯，瞭然在目。又如全波為濕、全濕為波，波相易識濕性難彰。如此事理宛有三用，只一事理三千即空性，了因即假性，緣因即中性。正因三諦若不性具即義，何由可成？非但三千即三諦，亦乃三諦即三千。故云中諦者統一切法，真諦者泯一切法，俗諦者立一切法。三千即中，以中為主，即一而三，名為本有所觀妙境。以空假即中，三皆屬性中，即空假還歸二修。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ordinary beings of the common ground have it only in principle and in name. At first hearing and in contemplative practice it is not yet manifest, and its substance is still obscured to verification; at [the purity of] the six faculties, a resemblance of it arises; at the first abiding, a portion is seen; at wondrous awakening, the fruit is accomplished and it is ultimately clear and manifest. Thus the principle must be realized personally before its marks become evident. It is like Lord Cao, whose marks were hidden and could be seen only when he took off his clothes — the matter can be known by comparison; and like Sun and Liu, whose marks were manifest and plain to the eye. Again, it is like the whole wave being wetness and the whole of wetness being the wave: the mark of the wave is easy to recognize, the nature of wetness hard to make evident. Thus phenomena and principle distinctly have three functions. The one three thousand of phenomena and principle — identical to the empty, it is the inherent revealing cause; identical to the provisional, the inherent conditioning cause; identical to the middle, the inherent correct cause. If the three truths were not inherently included, how could the meaning of identity be accomplished? Not only are the three thousand identical to the three truths; the three truths are also identical to the three thousand. Hence it is said: "The truth of the middle unifies all dharmas; the truth of the real dissolves all dharmas; the conventional truth establishes all dharmas." The three thousand identical to the middle, with the middle as primary, the one being identical to the three: this is called the originally existing wondrous object that is contemplated. Since the empty and the provisional are identical to the middle, all three belong to the nature as the middle; [yet] the empty and the provisional in themselves return to the two [aspects of] cultivation.

Chinese Source

三千即空，以空為主，名全性起修，是為因中能觀妙觀。以假中皆空，三皆屬觀空，即假中還歸用。境三千即假，以假為主，名為果上解脫大用。以中空即假，三皆屬用；假即中空，還歸境觀。只一三法，各對二明，論乎三境。三觀三用不即不離、不縱不橫，即遮即照，二義同時玄妙深絕，如三點伊一不相混，三不相離名大涅槃。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three thousand identical to the empty, with the empty as primary: this is called "the whole nature giving rise to cultivation," and is the wondrous contemplation that contemplates, in the cause. Since the provisional and the middle are both empty, all three belong to contemplation as the empty; [yet] the provisional and the middle in themselves return to function and object. The three thousand identical to the provisional, with the provisional as primary: this is called the great function of liberation upon the fruit. Since the middle and the empty are identical to the provisional, all three belong to function; [yet] as the provisional is identical to the middle and the empty, they return to object and contemplation. It is just one threefold dharma, each explained in relation to the other two. In discussing the three objects, the three contemplations, and the three functions: they are neither identical nor separate, neither vertical nor horizontal; negating and at once illuminating, the two meanings simultaneous, profound, wondrous, deep and cut off [from words] — like the three dots of the Siddhaṃ letter i: the one is not confused with the others, and the three not being separate is called great nirvāṇa.

Chinese Source

今就能觀論乎三觀，所觀即是三諦。言三觀者，以即空故破染礙情，一相不立，顯此三千同一性故。一切即一，方能同居一念派之彌合，故如眾珠咸趣一珠，畢竟清淨非斷無空。以即假故互具互攝，諸相宛然，顯此三千不失自體一即一切。雖復同居一念，即之彌分，故如一珠影入眾珠，不可思議非賴緣假。以即中故，顯此三千非一非一切、非分非合，雙遮二邊無有二相，雙照二諦空假宛然，豈同但中不具諸法？

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, discussing the three contemplations in terms of that which contemplates, what is contemplated is the three truths. As for the three contemplations: because [each] is identical to the empty, it breaks the feelings of defilement and obstruction, with not a single mark established, revealing that these three thousand share one nature. All being identical to one, they can dwell together in a single thought; the more they branch out, the more they merge. Thus it is like many pearls all converging in one pearl: ultimately pure, not an emptiness of annihilation and nonexistence. Because [each] is identical to the provisional, they mutually include and mutually take in one another, all marks distinctly present, revealing that these three thousand do not lose their own substance — one being identical to all. Though they dwell together in a single thought, the closer they are, the more distinct; thus it is like the reflection of one pearl entering many pearls: inconceivable, not a provisional [existence] dependent on conditions. Because [each] is identical to the middle, it reveals that these three thousand are neither one nor all, neither divided nor combined; doubly negating the two extremes, there are no two marks; doubly illuminating the two truths, the empty and the provisional are distinctly present. How could this be the same as the mere middle, which does not include all dharmas?

Chinese Source

一空一切空，三觀皆空，總空觀。一假一切假，三觀皆假，總假觀。一中一切中，三觀皆中，總中觀。是則終日破相，諸法皆成；終日立法，纖塵必盡；終日絕待，二邊熾然。是為即破即立、即立即破，非破非立而破而立。亦名即遮即照、即照即遮，非遮非照而遮而照。說雖次第，行在一時。若爾，無理不立、無情不破，豈與斷無之空、賴緣之假、出二邊中同日而語耶？故曰唯佛與佛乃能究盡，稻麻二乘恒沙菩薩，並不能知斯義少分。

Ru-Yi Translation

One empty, all empty: the three contemplations are all empty — the comprehensive contemplation of the empty. One provisional, all provisional: the three contemplations are all provisional — the comprehensive contemplation of the provisional. One middle, all middle: the three contemplations are all the middle — the comprehensive contemplation of the middle. Thus, breaking marks all day long, all dharmas are accomplished; establishing dharmas all day long, every speck of dust is surely exhausted; all day long beyond relativity, the two extremes blaze. This is breaking that is establishing and establishing that is breaking, neither breaking nor establishing and yet breaking and establishing. It is also called negating that is illuminating and illuminating that is negating, neither negating nor illuminating and yet negating and illuminating. Though the explanation is sequential, the practice is at one time. If so, there is no principle not established and no feeling not broken; how could it be spoken of in the same breath as an emptiness of annihilation and nonexistence, a provisional dependent on conditions, or a middle lying outside the two extremes? Hence it is said, "Only buddha together with buddha is able to fully fathom it": those of the two vehicles as many as rice and hemp, and bodhisattvas as many as the sands of the Ganges, are all unable to know even a small part of this meaning.

The Lineage of the Mind-Seal

Chinese Source

如此三千，通依諸部，的在《法華》。蓋由昔經，一有兼帶之過、二有隔偏之失。今經非但純一無雜，復能開麁即妙。題稱妙法，良在茲焉。是知用此絕待妙法為觀體者，方譬日光不與暗共。此乃終窮究竟極說，是為佛祖正傳心印，佛以是傳之於迦葉，迦葉以是傳之於阿難，乃至二十四代傳之於師子比丘，師子遇難不得其傳焉。是為金口祖承，皆見而知之者，出《付法傳》。

Ru-Yi Translation

These three thousand rely in general on all the [scriptural] divisions, but lie precisely in the Lotus. For the earlier sūtras, first, have the fault of being combined and accompanied, and second, have the failing of being separated and partial. This sūtra is not only pure and unmixed, but also able to open the coarse so that it is identical to the wondrous. That its title says "Wondrous Dharma" truly lies here. Thus we know that only one who takes this absolute wondrous Dharma as the substance of contemplation is like sunlight, which does not coexist with darkness. This is the final, exhaustive, ultimate teaching; it is the mind-seal correctly transmitted by the buddhas and ancestors. The Buddha transmitted it to Kāśyapa; Kāśyapa transmitted it to Ānanda; and so on through twenty-four generations, down to the bhikṣu Siṃha. Siṃha met with calamity, and it was not transmitted [further]. This is the ancestral succession from the Golden Mouth; all of them are those who "knew it by seeing," as given in the Account of the Transmission of the Dharma-Treasury.

The End of Notes on the Tiantai Transmission of the Buddha-Mind Seal

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

"Just the one word 'include'": the opening sentences repeat Zhili's Record on the Profound Meaning of Guanyin (觀音玄義記). CBETA punctuates 「彌顯今宗以性具善。他師亦知具惡緣了」; the translation follows the reading 「彌顯今宗。以性具善，他師亦知；具惡緣了，他皆莫測」, which is how Zhili's sentence runs.

"The whole of evil is evil" (全惡是惡): read with Zhili's Zhiyao chao (T1928), which says of other schools that "since they cannot [hold that] the whole of evil is evil, their meaning of identity is never accomplished." Alternatively: "This is 'the whole of evil'; only when evil is identical is the meaning accomplished."

"It can follow defiled and pure conditions" (能隨染淨緣，遂分十法界): attributed to the Avataṃsaka, but the wording is not found in the sūtra; it appears as a quoted "sūtra" line in T1885 (Commentary on the Verses on the Dharma-Realm Contemplation). Source in the Avataṃsaka not located.

"Lord Cao . Sun and Liu": from Mohe Zhiguan (T1911), where Cao Cao's hidden marks and the manifest marks of Sun Quan and Liu Bei illustrate the difference between hidden and evident features.

"The truth of the middle unifies all dharmas .": the same three lines appear in T1973 and T2037; earliest source not located.

Re-punctuation in the three-causes sentence: CBETA has 「即空性，了因即假性，緣因即中性。正因三諦若不性具即義」; the translation reads 「即空性了因，即假性緣因，即中性正因。三諦若不性具，即義何由可成」. Likewise 「即假中還歸用。境三千即假」 is read as 「還歸用境。三千即假」, which matches the parallel clauses.

"Lust is itself the path": the Great Treatise (T1509) has a verse from the Sūtra on the Non-Activity of All Dharmas in slightly different wording (婬欲即是道，恚癡亦如是；如此三事中，無量諸佛道). Huaize quotes a later standard form.

"Snapping the fingers, scattering flowers .": a paraphrase of verses in the Lotus, "Skillful Means" chapter (site: ".or even lowered the head slightly"); the phrase 彈指散華 is used in the Profound Meaning (T1716) for the conditioning cause.

"Only buddha together with buddha is able to fully fathom it": Lotus, "Skillful Means"; wording follows the site's Fahua Xuanyi and Lotus pages. "Rice and hemp . Ganges sands" is from the same chapter's verses (site: "as numerous as rice, hemp, bamboo, and reeds").

"Now the great treasure of the Dharma-king has come of itself": Lotus, "Belief and Understanding"; the site has "this treasure of the Dharma-king has come to us of itself."

"I have now entrusted the peerless true Dharma": Nirvāṇa Sūtra; wording follows the site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra page. The Account of the Transmission of the Dharma-Treasury (付法藏因緣傳, T2058) has the second quotation.

"Having never heard at all the name of inherent evil": Zhanran, Fahua wenju ji (T1719), quoted by Zhili in the Zhiyao chao.

"Whether in a field or a village . on a tree or a stone": the same pair appears in Guanding's preface to the Profound Meaning (site: "what a field or a village can preserve . what a tree or a stone can preserve").

Names: Zhang'an = Guanding; the "two Weis" = Fahua Zhiwei and Tiangong Huiwei; Zuoxi = Xuanlang; Jingxi = Zhanran; Luoxi = Yiji; King Qian = Qian Chu of Wuyue; Siming = Zhili; Liang Jingzhi = Liang Su; Liu Zihou = Liu Zongyuan. Huaize refers to himself as "Ze" (則) in the last unit.

Doubtful passages: 「故曰說法據此故施開自在」 is unclear and translated literally; 「教觀復還無行江浙」 — 無 is probably a copying error (for 盛 or similar); 「不盡度耳」 is read as "was not all brought across [into Chinese]."
