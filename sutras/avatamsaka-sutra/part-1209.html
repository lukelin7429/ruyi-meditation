The Garden of Fragrant Shoots

With his father's permission, the prince rides out to a garden in a jewelled carriage drawn by five hundred court ladies, surrounded by an escort of hundreds of thousands of myriads, along roads lined with towers stocked with everything a traveller could possibly need.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1209 — fascicle 75 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

The Garden of Fragrant Shoots

The prince's outing to a garden becomes an occasion for a procession on the same scale as everything else in this story — six different kinds of escort, each numbering hundreds of thousands of myriads.

Chinese Source

「爾時，太子受父王教，與十千采女詣香芽園遊觀戲樂。太子是時，乘妙寶車，其車具有種種嚴飾，置大摩尼師子之座而坐其上；五百采女各執寶繩牽馭而行，進止有度，不遲不速；百千萬人持諸寶蓋，百千萬人持諸寶幢，百千萬人持諸寶幡，百千萬人作諸妓樂，百千萬人燒諸名香，百千萬人散諸妙華，前後圍遶而為翊從。道路平正，無有高下，眾寶雜華散布其上；寶樹行列，寶網彌覆，種種樓閣延袤其間。其樓閣中，或有積聚種種珍寶，或有陳列諸莊嚴具，或有供設種種飲食，或有懸布種種衣服，或有備擬諸資生物，或復安置端正女人，及以無量僮僕侍從；隨有所須，悉皆施與。

Ru-Yi Translation

"At that time, the prince, receiving his royal father's instruction, went together with ten thousand court ladies to the Garden of Fragrant Shoots, to journey there for pleasure and delight. At that time, the prince rode in a wondrous jewelled carriage, that carriage adorned with every kind of ornament, upon which was set a great maṇi-jewel lion-seat, and he sat upon it; five hundred court ladies each took hold of jewelled cords and drew the carriage forward, their advance and halting perfectly measured, neither too slow nor too fast; hundreds of thousands of myriads of people held every kind of jewelled canopy, hundreds of thousands of myriads of people held every kind of jewelled banner, hundreds of thousands of myriads of people held every kind of jewelled pennant, hundreds of thousands of myriads of people made every kind of music, hundreds of thousands of myriads of people burned every kind of famed incense, and hundreds of thousands of myriads of people scattered every kind of wondrous flower, surrounding him before and behind as his attendant escort. The roads were level and even, with no rise or fall at all, and every kind of jewel and mingled flower was scattered over them; jewelled trees stood in ranks, jewelled nets covered everything above, and towers of every kind stretched out along the way. Within those towers, in some there were gathered every kind of rare treasure; in some there were arrayed every kind of ornament; in some there were laid out every kind of food and drink; in some there hung every kind of clothing; in some there were prepared every kind of necessary implement; and in some there were stationed women of upright beauty, together with measureless young attendants and servants — and whatever was needed, all of it was given freely.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

百千萬人持諸寶蓋⋯⋯百千萬人散諸妙華 — hundreds of thousands of myriads of people held every kind of jewelled canopy⋯⋯hundreds of thousands of myriads of people scattered every kind of wondrous flower. Counted directly, this single sentence repeats 百千萬人, hundreds of thousands of myriads of people, six times in a row, each clause naming a different task — canopies, banners, pennants, music, incense, flowers — before the procession has even reached the road it will travel.
