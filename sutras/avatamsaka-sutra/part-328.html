The King Who Withholds Nothing

With the sixth dedication now formally complete, Vajradhvaja Bodhisattva — who has spoken this entire chapter — surveys the ten directions and the assembly, and by the Buddha's own spiritual power begins a verse retelling everything the dedication has already said in prose: a bodhisattva who appears as a king unequaled in worldly rank, giving away his food and clothing, his own head and eyes, his tongue, his wife and children, his kingship, and his entire retinue, all with a mind that never once regrets.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 328 — the sixth dedication retold in verse, part one Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A King Unequaled in Worldly Rank

Where the prose of the sixth dedication moved through named categories of gifts one by one, the verse compresses them into a single continuous image: a bodhisattva-king, virtuous and unassailable, who gives away everything from his daily meal to his own flesh and bone without the smallest flicker of regret.

Chinese Source

「菩薩現身作國王，於世位中最無等，
福德威光勝一切，普為群萌興利益。
其心清淨無染著，於世自在咸遵敬，
弘宣正法以訓人，普使眾生獲安隱。
現生貴族昇王位，常依正教轉法輪，
稟性仁慈無毒虐，十方敬仰皆從化。
智慧分別常明了，色相才能皆具足，
臨馭率土靡不從，摧伏魔軍悉令盡。
堅持淨戒無違犯，決志堪忍不動搖，
永願蠲除忿恚心，常樂修行諸佛法。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The bodhisattva manifests himself as a king, unequaled in worldly rank; his merit, virtue, and majestic light surpass all others, and he universally brings benefit to every living being. His mind is pure and free of any defilement; sovereign over the world, he is honored and revered by all; he broadly proclaims the true Dharma to instruct people, and universally causes sentient beings to find peace and security. Born into a noble clan, he ascends to the throne, and forever relying on the true teaching, he turns the wheel of the Dharma; his nature is kind and compassionate, without any cruelty, and beings in the ten directions revere him and are all transformed by him. His wisdom and discernment are forever clear, his form and abilities entirely complete; as he governs the whole land, there is none who does not submit, and he subdues every army of Māra, destroying it entirely. He firmly upholds pure precepts without any transgression, his resolve steadfast in forbearance, never wavering; he forever vows to eliminate any mind of anger and hatred, forever delighting in cultivating the Dharma of every buddha."

Chinese Source

飲食香鬘及衣服，車騎床褥座與燈，
菩薩悉以給濟人，并及所餘無量種。
為利益故而行施，令其開發廣大心，
於尊勝處及所餘，意皆清淨生歡喜。
菩薩一切皆周給，內外所有悉能捨，
必使其心永清淨，不令暫爾生狹劣。
或施於頭或施眼，或施於手或施足，
皮肉骨髓及餘物，一切皆捨心無吝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Food and drink, fragrant garlands, and clothing, carriages and mounts, bedding, seats, and lamps — the bodhisattva provides all of these to help people, together with immeasurable other kinds of things besides. For the sake of benefiting them he practices giving, causing them to open up a vast and expansive mind; toward those of the most exalted station and toward all others alike, his mind remains entirely pure, giving rise to joy. The bodhisattva provides for everyone entirely, giving away everything he has, both what is internal and what is external, and is certain to keep his mind forever pure, never once, even briefly, letting a narrow or lesser mind arise. Sometimes he gives away his head, sometimes his eyes, sometimes his hands, sometimes his feet; his skin, flesh, bones, and marrow, and every other thing besides — he gives all of it away, his mind free of any stinginess.

Chinese Source

菩薩身居大王位，種族豪貴人中尊，
開口出舌施群生，其心歡喜無憂戀。
以彼施舌諸功德，迴向一切諸眾生，
普願藉此勝因緣，悉得如來廣長舌。
或施妻子及王位，或施其身作僮僕，
其心清淨常歡喜，如是一切無憂悔。
隨所樂求咸施與，應時給濟無疲厭，
一切所有皆能散，諸來求者普滿足。

Ru-Yi Translation

The bodhisattva, his person seated upon the great throne of a king, of a noble and eminent clan, the most honored among people, opens his mouth and puts forth his tongue to give it to every living being, his mind joyful, free of any sorrow or longing. With the merit of that giving of his tongue, he dedicates it to all sentient beings, universally vowing that, by means of this excellent causal condition, they may all attain the Tathāgata's own broad and long tongue. Sometimes he gives away his wife and children along with his kingship, sometimes he gives his own person to become a servant; his mind remains pure and forever joyful, and in all of this he never once feels sorrow or regret. Whatever is delighted in and sought, he gives it all away entirely, providing for people in a timely manner without any fatigue; everything he has, he is able to give it all away, so that every one who comes seeking is universally satisfied.

Chinese Source

為聞法故施其身，修諸苦行求菩提，
復為眾生捨一切，求無上智不退轉。
以於佛所聞正法，自捨其身充給侍，
為欲普救諸群生，發生無量歡喜心。
彼見世尊大導師，能以慈心廣饒益，
是時踊躍生歡喜，聽受如來深法味。
菩薩所有諸善根，悉以迴向諸眾生，
普皆救護無有餘，永使解脫常安樂。
菩薩所有諸眷屬，色相端嚴能辯慧，
華鬘衣服及塗香，種種莊嚴皆具足。
此諸眷屬甚希有，菩薩一切皆能施，
專求正覺度群生，如是之心無暫捨。
菩薩如是諦思惟，備行種種廣大業，
悉以迴向諸含識，而不生於取著心。

Ru-Yi Translation

For the sake of hearing the Dharma, he gives away his own body, cultivating every manner of ascetic practice in seeking enlightenment; and again, for the sake of sentient beings, he relinquishes everything, seeking unsurpassed wisdom without ever regressing. Because he hears the true Dharma in the presence of a buddha, he gives his own body over entirely to serve and attend upon him, and for the sake of universally rescuing every living being, gives rise to immeasurable joy in his mind. When he sees the World-Honored One, the great guide, who with a mind of kindness broadly brings benefit to all, at that moment he leaps with joy, rejoicing, and listens to and receives the Tathāgata's profound flavor of the Dharma. Every wholesome root that the bodhisattva possesses, he dedicates entirely to sentient beings, universally rescuing and protecting them all without exception, forever causing them to attain liberation and constant happiness. Every member of the bodhisattva's own retinue is dignified and fine in form, eloquent and wise, adorned entirely with garlands of flowers, clothing, and fragrant perfumes, every manner of adornment fully complete. Though this retinue is exceedingly rare and precious, the bodhisattva is able to give every one of them away entirely, single-mindedly seeking true enlightenment in order to deliver every living being, and this mind of his is never abandoned, not even for a moment. The bodhisattva, reflecting truly in this way, fully practices every manner of vast and great deed, dedicating all of it entirely to every sentient being, without ever giving rise to a mind of grasping or attachment.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The verse recap intensifies rather than simply repeats the prose it summarizes — where the prose spoke of giving one's tongue only obliquely within a catalogue, the verse lingers on it, and adds a specific dedication that had no prose counterpart: giving one's tongue away so that all beings might one day attain the Tathāgata's own broad, long tongue, one of the thirty-two marks of a buddha's body.
