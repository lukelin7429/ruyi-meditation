Lotuses as Broad as a Chariot Wheel

The tower keeps showing more: pearl necklaces that pour out fragrant water of eight excellent qualities, lapis lazuli shining with a hundred thousand lights, and the four kinds of lotus, each bearing measureless flowers as broad as a hand, a cubit, or a chariot wheel, and every flower filled with images of men, women, boys, girls, Śakra, Brahmā, the eight classes of beings, and the two vehicles, all with palms joined. The Tathāgata sits cross-legged with the thirty-two marks; the pure lapis lazuli ground shows worlds and buddhas at every step; the jeweled trees show half-body images in every branch and fruit; and the half-moons of every tower send out the light of suns, moons, and stars.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1315 — fascicle 79 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Lotuses as Broad as a Chariot Wheel

Necklaces, lotuses, the seated Tathāgata, the lapis ground, the trees, and the half-moons — every surface of the tower shows images and gives off light.

Chinese Source

又見彼真珠瓔珞，常出香水，具八功德；瑠璃、瓔珞，百千光明，同時照耀；幢、幡、網、蓋，如是等物，一切皆以眾寶莊嚴。又復見彼優鉢羅華、波頭摩華、拘物頭華、芬陀利華，各各生於無量諸華，或大一手，或長一肘，或復縱廣猶如車輪，一一華中皆悉示現種種色像以為嚴飾。所謂：男色像、女色像、童男色像、童女色像、釋、梵、護世、天、龍、夜叉、乾闥婆、阿脩羅、迦樓羅、緊那羅、摩睺羅伽、聲聞、緣覺及諸菩薩。如是一切眾生色像，皆悉合掌，曲躬禮敬。亦見如來結跏趺坐，三十二相莊嚴其身。又復見彼淨瑠璃地，一一步間，現不思議種種色像。所謂：世界色像、菩薩色像、如來色像及諸樓閣莊嚴色像。又於寶樹枝、葉、華、果一一事中，悉見種種半身色像。所謂：佛半身色像、菩薩半身色像，天、龍、夜叉，乃至護世、轉輪聖王、小王、王子、大臣、官長，及以四眾半身色像。其諸色像，或執華鬘，或執瓔珞，或持一切諸莊嚴具；或有曲躬合掌禮敬，一心瞻仰，目不暫捨；或有讚歎，或入三昧。其身悉以相好莊嚴，普放種種諸色光明，所謂：金色光明、銀色光明、珊瑚色光明、兜沙羅色光明、帝青色光明、毘盧遮那寶色光明、一切眾寶色光明、瞻波迦華色光明。又見諸樓閣半月像中，出阿僧祇日月星宿種種光明普照十方。

Ru-Yi Translation

He saw, moreover, those necklaces of pearls constantly pouring out fragrant water possessing the eight excellent qualities; the lapis lazuli and the necklaces shining at once with a hundred thousand lights; and the banners, pennants, nets, canopies, and every such thing all adorned with every jewel. And again he saw those utpala flowers, padma flowers, kumuda flowers, and puṇḍarīka flowers, each of them giving rise to measureless flowers, some as large as a hand, some a cubit in length, and some as broad and long as a chariot wheel, and within each and every flower every kind of image was displayed as its adornment. That is to say: images of men, images of women, images of boys, images of girls, and Śakra, Brahmā, the world-protectors, devas, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and the bodhisattvas. All these images of every kind of being had their palms joined, bending low in reverence. He saw also the Tathāgata seated cross-legged, his body adorned with the thirty-two marks. And again he saw that pure lapis lazuli ground, in the space of each and every step, displaying every kind of inconceivable image. That is to say: images of worlds, images of bodhisattvas, images of tathāgatas, and images of the adornments of the towers. And in each and every branch, leaf, flower, and fruit of the jeweled trees he saw every kind of half-body image. That is to say: half-body images of buddhas, half-body images of bodhisattvas, and half-body images of devas, nāgas, yakṣas, and so on up to the world-protectors, wheel-turning sage kings, lesser kings, princes, great ministers, officials, and the four assemblies. These images held garlands of flowers, or held necklaces, or held every article of adornment; some bent low with palms joined in reverence, gazing up single-mindedly, their eyes never leaving him for a single moment; some praised him, and some entered samādhi. Their bodies were all adorned with the excellent marks and sent forth every color of light universally, that is to say: light of the color of gold, light of the color of silver, light of the color of coral, light of the color of tuṣāra, light of the color of indranīla, light of the color of the Vairocana jewel, light of the colors of every jewel, and light of the color of the campaka flower. He saw, moreover, within the half-moon images of the towers, asaṃkhyeya lights of every kind, of the sun, the moon, and the stars and constellations, coming forth and shining universally through the ten directions.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

兜沙羅色光明、瞻波迦華色光明 — two of the eight colors of light are named by transliteration. 兜沙羅 is read as tuṣāra, a word for frost or snow, so that the light is of a pale, frosty color; the identification follows the usual reading of the three characters and is offered as the translator's. 瞻波迦 is a variant spelling of the flower written 薝蔔迦 in fascicle seventy-eight, and both are rendered campaka, so that the yellow-flowered tree keeps a single name across the two fascicles.

半身色像 — the images seen in the branches, leaves, flowers, and fruit of the jeweled trees are 半身, half-body, the upper half of a figure rising from the surface as if from a frame, in contrast to the full 色像 that the lotuses and the lapis ground display. The lotus images form a list of eighteen kinds of being, from men, women, boys, and girls through Śakra, Brahmā, and the world-protectors to the eight classes and the two vehicles, ending with the bodhisattvas; every one of them is shown with palms joined.
