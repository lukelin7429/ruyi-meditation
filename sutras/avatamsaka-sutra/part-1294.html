Free of Five Terrors

A new pattern of paired similes begins — an ordinary medicine's own power set directly against its match in the mind of bodhi — running from freedom from five terrors through a snake-repelling remedy to a tree whose bark regrows however often it is taken.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1294 — fascicle 78 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Free of Five Terrors

Seven paired similes open this new pattern — each one first describing an ordinary medicine's own power, then matching it directly to what the mind of bodhi accomplishes for a bodhisattva.

Chinese Source

「善男子！譬如有人，得無畏藥，離五恐怖。何等為五？所謂：火不能燒，毒不能中，刀不能傷，水不能漂，煙不能熏。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，得一切智菩提心藥，貪火不燒，瞋毒不中，惑刀不傷，有流不漂，諸覺觀煙不能熏害。「善男子！譬如有人，得解脫藥，終無橫難。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，得菩提心解脫智藥，永離一切生死橫難。「善男子！譬如有人，持摩訶應伽藥，毒蛇聞氣，即皆遠去。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，持菩提心大應伽藥，一切煩惱諸惡毒蛇，聞其氣者，悉皆散滅。「善男子！譬如有人，持無勝藥，一切怨敵無能勝者。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，持菩提心無能勝藥，悉能降伏一切魔軍。「善男子！譬如有人，持毘笈摩藥，能令毒箭自然墮落。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，持菩提心毘笈摩藥，令貪、恚、癡、諸邪見箭自然墮落。「善男子！譬如有人，持善見藥，能除一切所有諸病。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，持菩提心善見藥王，悉除一切諸煩惱病。「善男子！如有藥樹，名：珊陀那，有取其皮以塗瘡者，瘡即除愈；然其樹皮，隨取隨生，終不可盡。菩薩摩訶薩從菩提心生一切智樹亦復如是，若有得見而生信者，煩惱業瘡悉得消滅，一切智樹初無所損。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son! Just as, when a person obtains the fearless medicine, they are freed from five kinds of terror — what are the five? Namely: fire cannot burn them, poison cannot afflict them, a blade cannot wound them, water cannot sweep them away, and smoke cannot choke them — a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: having obtained the medicine of the mind of bodhi that is all-knowing wisdom, the fire of craving cannot burn them, the poison of anger cannot afflict them, the blade of delusion cannot wound them, the stream of existence cannot sweep them away, and the smoke of discursive thought cannot choke or harm them. Good son! Just as, when a person obtains the medicine of liberation, they never meet with sudden calamity, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: having obtained the wisdom-medicine of liberation that is the mind of bodhi, they forever leave behind every sudden calamity of birth-and-death. Good son! Just as, when a person carries the great yìngqié medicine, every venomous serpent that catches its scent at once flees far away, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: carrying the great yìngqié medicine that is the mind of bodhi, every venomous serpent of affliction that catches its scent entirely scatters and vanishes. Good son! Just as, when a person carries the unconquerable medicine, no enemy whatsoever is able to defeat them, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: carrying the unconquerable medicine that is the mind of bodhi, they are entirely able to subdue every army of māra. Good son! Just as, when a person carries the pí-jí-mó medicine, it causes every poisoned arrow to fall away of itself, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: carrying the pí-jí-mó medicine that is the mind of bodhi, they cause the arrows of craving, anger, delusion, and every wrong view to fall away of themselves. Good son! Just as, when a person carries the well-seen medicine, it removes every sickness there is, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: carrying the well-seen king of medicine that is the mind of bodhi, they remove every sickness of affliction entirely. Good son! Just as there is a medicinal tree named Shāntuónà, and one who takes its bark and applies it to a wound finds the wound healed at once — yet its bark, taken again and again, keeps regrowing and is never exhausted — the tree of all-knowing wisdom a bodhisattva-mahāsattva grows from the mind of bodhi is likewise: if there is one who beholds it and gives rise to faith, the wound of their karma born of affliction is entirely dissolved away, while the tree of all-knowing wisdom itself suffers no loss at all.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

如有藥樹，名：珊陀那 — 珊陀那 and 應伽 in this Part both read as phonetic transliterations, but no Sanskrit or Pali source term can be identified with confidence from the passage alone; per this project's own standing rule against inventing unverified reconstructions, both are kept here as pinyin (Shāntuónà, yìngqié) rather than assigned guessed Sanskrit names — the same treatment already given to 毘笈摩 in Part 1291.

This Part opens a new pattern of paired similes — an ordinary person's medicine set directly against a bodhisattva's own mind of bodhi — distinct from the single-image chain that ran through Parts 1290–1293. Seven pairs here, each introduced by 善男子！譬如有人 ("Good son! Just as, when a person...") and answered by 菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是 ("a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise"), counted directly against the source text.
