Some See Him Live Eighty Years

The great gāthā continues through the many different forms in which beings see the buddha: predicting the enlightenment of bodhisattvas, entering nirvāṇa after measureless kalpas or only now attaining bodhi, dwelling in the palaces of Brahmā, Māra, and every heaven, and, in the presence of the World-Honored Dīpaṃkara, scattering flowers and spreading out his own hair as an offering. Some see a body vast as Sumeru, some see it seated unmoving and filling boundless worlds; some see his halo the span of a single fathom, others see it filling every land. Some see him live eighty years, others measureless kalpas. His wisdom knows the three times in a single thought, and he turns the wheel of dharma with the ten powers, the four fearlessnesses, and the four unobstructed eloquences, teaching the four truths, the twelve links, and every dharma-gate in a single voice that each being understands according to its own faculties, shaking every land without ever frightening a single being.

Some See Him Live Eighty Years

Twenty more stanzas of the great gāthā — the buddha's many lifespans and dwellings, his offering before Dīpaṃkara, and the wisdom and teaching that turn the wheel of dharma in a single voice.

Chinese Source

或見如來無量壽，
與諸菩薩授尊記，
而成無上大導師，
次補住於安樂剎。

或見無量億千劫，
作佛事已入涅槃；
或見今始成菩提，
或見正修諸妙行。

或見如來清淨月，
在於梵世及魔宮，
自在天宮化樂宮，
示現種種諸神變。

或見在於兜率宮，
無量諸天共圍遶，
為彼說法令歡喜，
悉共發心供養佛。

或見住在夜摩天，
忉利護世龍神處，
如是一切諸宮殿，
莫不於中現其像。

於彼然燈世尊所，
散華布髮為供養，
從是了知深妙法，
恒以此道化群生。

或有見佛久涅槃，
或見初始成菩提；
或有住於無量劫，
或見須臾即滅度。

身相光明與壽命，
智慧菩提及涅槃，
眾會所化威儀聲，
如是一一皆無數。

或現其身極廣大，
譬如須彌大寶山；
或見跏趺不動搖，
充滿無邊諸世界。

或見圓光一尋量，
或見千萬億由旬，
或見照於無量土，
或見充滿一切剎。

或見佛壽八十年，
或壽百千萬億歲，
或住不可思議劫，
如是展轉倍過此。

佛智通達淨無礙，
一念普知三世法，
皆從心識因緣起，
生滅無常無自性。

於一剎中成正覺，
一切剎處悉亦成，
一切入一一亦爾，
隨眾生心皆示現。

如來住於無上道，
成就十力四無畏；
具足智慧無所礙，
轉於十二行法輪。

了知苦集及滅道，
分別十二因緣法；
法義樂說辭無礙，
以是四辯廣開演。

諸法無我無有相，
業性不起亦無失，
一切遠離如虛空，
佛以方便而分別。

如來如是轉法輪，
普震十方諸國土，
宮殿山河悉搖動，
不使眾生有驚怖。

如來普演廣大音，
隨其根欲皆令解，
悉使發心除惑垢，
而佛未始生心念。

或聞施戒忍精進，
禪定般若方便智，
或聞慈悲及喜捨，
種種音辭各差別。

或聞四念四正勤，
神足根力及覺道，
諸念神通止觀等，
無量方便諸法門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Some see the Tathāgata of Immeasurable Life
bestowing the honored prediction upon the bodhisattvas,
so that they become the unsurpassed great guide,
next in succession, abiding in the land of ease and happiness.

Some see him, after measureless koṭis of thousands of kalpas
of performing buddha-work, entering nirvāṇa;
some see him only now accomplishing bodhi,
some see him rightly cultivating every wondrous practice.

Some see the Tathāgata, pure as the moon,
within the palace of Brahmā and the palace of Māra,
within the palace of the Paranirmita-vaśavartin and the Nirmāṇarati heavens,
displaying every kind of spiritual transformation.

Some see him within the palace of Tuṣita,
surrounded together by measureless devas,
expounding the dharma to them and bringing them joy,
so that all together they give rise to the mind and make offerings to the buddha.

Some see him abiding in the Yāma Heaven,
in the Trāyastriṃśa Heaven, among the world-protectors, the nāgas, and the spirits;
throughout every one of these palaces,
there is none in which his image does not appear.

In the presence of the World-Honored Dīpaṃkara,
he scattered flowers and spread out his hair as an offering;
from that time he understood the profound and wondrous dharma,
and has ever since used this path to teach every kind of being.

Some see the buddha having long since entered nirvāṇa,
some see him only just now accomplishing bodhi;
some see him abiding for measureless kalpas,
some see him passing into cessation in but a moment.

His bodily marks, his light, and his lifespan,
his wisdom, his bodhi, and his nirvāṇa,
his assemblies, those he transforms, his bearing, and his voice—
of each and every one of these there are countless numbers.

Some see him display a body extremely vast,
just like Sumeru, the great jeweled mountain;
some see him seated cross-legged, unmoving and unshaken,
filling boundless worlds.

Some see his round halo of light the measure of a single fathom,
some see it thousands of myriads of koṭis of yojanas,
some see it shining upon measureless lands,
some see it filling every land there is.

Some see the buddha's life eighty years,
some see it a hundred thousand myriad koṭis of years,
some see him abide for inconceivable kalpas,
and further still, doubling and redoubling beyond even this.

A buddha's wisdom penetrates everywhere, pure and unobstructed;
in a single thought he universally knows the dharmas of the three times,
all of them arising from the causes and conditions of mind and consciousness,
arising and ceasing, impermanent, and without any nature of their own.

Within a single land he accomplishes perfect enlightenment,
and within every land it is likewise accomplished;
entering all of them, in each and every one it is just the same,
displayed entirely according to the minds of sentient beings.

The Tathāgata abides in the unsurpassed path,
having accomplished the ten powers and the four fearlessnesses;
fully endowed with wisdom, obstructed by nothing,
he turns the wheel of dharma in its twelve aspects.

He fully understands suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path,
and discerns the dharma of the twelve links of dependent origination;
with unobstructed eloquence in dharma, in meaning, in joyful speech, and in diction,
he widely opens and expounds it through these four kinds of eloquence.

Every dharma is without self and without any mark,
the nature of karma neither arises nor is lost,
and all of it is as far removed as empty space;
and yet the buddha, through skillful means, still distinguishes and discerns it.

Thus does the Tathāgata turn the wheel of dharma,
universally shaking every land of the ten directions;
palaces, mountains, and rivers all tremble and stir,
and yet he never lets sentient beings feel any fright or fear.

The Tathāgata universally sends forth his vast and great voice,
causing every being to understand according to their own faculties and desires,
causing all of them to give rise to the mind and remove their defiling stains,
and yet the buddha has never once given rise to a thought of doing so.

Some hear of giving, of the precepts, of patience, and of diligence,
of dhyāna, of prajñā, of skillful means, and of wisdom;
some hear of loving-kindness, of compassion, of sympathetic joy, and of equanimity;
each kind of sound and diction differing from the rest.

Some hear of the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right efforts,
the bases of spiritual power, the faculties, and the powers, and the factors of the path of awakening;
of every kind of mindfulness, spiritual power, calming, and insight,
and every measureless gate of skillful means.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

於彼然燈世尊所，散華布髮為供養 — this couplet recalls the well-known account of a past life in which the bodhisattva who would become Śākyamuni bought flowers to offer to the buddha Dīpaṃkara and spread his own hair over the mud so the buddha would not have to step in it, receiving in return the prediction of his own future buddhahood; 然燈 is kept as Dīpaṃkara, the form already established on this site. The stanza folds the whole episode into two lines, offering and understanding, without naming the flowers or the mud directly, and the translation follows the source in leaving those details implicit.

十力四無畏……十二行法輪……四辯 — three technical sets appear in close succession: the ten powers and the four fearlessnesses, already established in this translation; 十二行法輪, the wheel of dharma turned in its twelve aspects, referring to the traditional three turnings of the four truths, each turning bearing four aspects; and 四辯, the four kinds of unobstructed eloquence, in dharma, in meaning, in joyful speech, and in diction, matching the site's established "unobstructed" vocabulary for 無礙.
