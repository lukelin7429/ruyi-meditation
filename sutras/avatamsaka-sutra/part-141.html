No Defilement, No Purity

Fascicle twenty opens directly into the seventh practice, free of attachment — a bodhisattva who makes offerings to countless buddhas across kalpas beyond telling, seeing every land, every teaching, every assembly, and clinging to none of it, because within the buddha-dharma there is no defilement and no purity, no darkness and no brightness at all.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 141 — the practice free of attachment begins Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

The Seventh Practice: Free of Attachment

Chinese Source

「佛子！何等為菩薩摩訶薩無著行？佛子！此菩薩以無著心，於念念中，能入阿僧祇世界，嚴淨阿僧祇世界。於諸世界，心無所著，往詣阿僧祇諸如來所，恭敬禮拜，承事供養。以阿僧祇華、阿僧祇香、阿僧祇鬘，阿僧祇塗香、末香、衣服、珍寶、幢幡、妙蓋，諸莊嚴具各阿僧祇以用供養；如是供養，為究竟無作法故，為住不思議法故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Son of the Buddha! What is a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own practice free of attachment? Son of the Buddha, this bodhisattva, with a mind free of attachment, is able, moment after moment, to enter asaṃkhyeya worlds, and to adorn and purify asaṃkhyeya worlds. Toward every world, their mind clings to nothing at all, as they go to the place of asaṃkhyeya tathāgatas, revering and bowing to them, attending upon and making offerings to them. With asaṃkhyeya flowers, asaṃkhyeya incense, asaṃkhyeya garlands, asaṃkhyeya anointing incense, powdered incense, garments, treasures, banners, streamers, and wondrous canopies, and every kind of adorning implement, each in asaṃkhyeya number, do they make offerings; such offerings as this are made for the sake of the ultimate, unconditioned dharma, and for the sake of abiding in the inconceivable dharma.

Chinese Source

於念念中，見無數佛；於諸佛所，心無所著；於諸佛剎，亦無所著；於佛相好，亦無所著；見佛光明，聽佛說法，亦無所著；於十方世界，及佛菩薩所有眾會，亦無所著；聽佛法已，心生歡喜，志力廣大，能攝、能行諸菩薩行，然於佛法，亦無所著。

Ru-Yi Translation

Moment after moment, they see countless buddhas; toward the place of every buddha, their mind clings to nothing at all; toward every buddha-land, likewise they cling to nothing; toward the buddha's own excellent marks, likewise they cling to nothing; seeing the buddha's own light and hearing the buddha teach the Dharma, likewise they cling to nothing; toward the worlds of the ten directions, and every assembly belonging to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, likewise they cling to nothing. Having heard the buddha-dharma, their mind gives rise to joy, their resolve and strength vast and expansive, able to gather in and able to practice every bodhisattva conduct — and yet, toward the buddha-dharma itself, they likewise cling to nothing.

Chinese Source

此菩薩於不可說劫，見不可說佛出興於世，一一佛所，承事供養，皆悉盡於不可說劫，心無厭足；見佛聞法，及見菩薩眾會莊嚴，皆無所著；見不淨世界，亦無憎惡。何以故？此菩薩如諸佛法而觀察故。諸佛法中，無垢、無淨、無闇、無明，無異、無一，無實、無妄，無安隱、無險難，無正道、無邪道。

Ru-Yi Translation

This bodhisattva, throughout kalpas beyond telling, sees buddhas beyond telling appearing within the world, and at the place of each and every buddha, attends upon and makes offerings to them, exhausting kalpas beyond telling entirely, their mind never once wearied or satiated; seeing the buddhas and hearing the Dharma, and seeing the adornment of the bodhisattvas' own assemblies, they cling to none of it at all; seeing impure worlds, likewise they feel no aversion or hatred. Why is this? Because this bodhisattva contemplates in accordance with the very dharma of every buddha. Within the buddha-dharma, there is no defilement and no purity, no darkness and no brightness, no difference and no oneness, no truth and no falsehood, no security and no peril, no right path and no wrong path.

Chinese Source

菩薩如是深入法界，教化眾生，而於眾生不生執著；受持諸法，而於諸法不生執著；發菩提心，住於佛住，而於佛住不生執著；雖有言說，而於言說心無所著；入眾生趣，於眾生趣心無所著；了知三昧，能入能住，而於三昧心無所著；往詣無量諸佛國土，若入、若見、若於中住，而於佛土心無所著，捨去之時亦無顧戀。

Ru-Yi Translation

The bodhisattva, entering deeply into the Dharma-realm in this way, teaches and transforms living beings, and yet gives rise to no attachment toward living beings; upholds and retains every dharma, and yet gives rise to no attachment toward any dharma; gives rise to the mind of bodhi, abiding in the Buddha's own abode, and yet gives rise to no attachment toward the Buddha's own abode; though they engage in speech, their mind clings to nothing toward that very speech; entering the destinies of living beings, their mind clings to nothing toward those destinies of living beings; thoroughly understanding samādhi, able to enter it and able to abide within it, and yet their mind clings to nothing toward that very samādhi; going to immeasurable buddha-lands, whether entering them, seeing them, or abiding within them, their mind clings to nothing toward those buddha-lands, and when they depart from them, they harbor no longing or attachment either.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The list of paired opposites the buddha-dharma is said to be free of — "no defilement, no purity, no darkness, no brightness, no difference, no oneness" — is not a denial that impure worlds exist; the very next sentence confirms the bodhisattva sees them. The point instead is that revulsion is not a fixed feature of dharmas themselves, only of a mind that has not yet let go of grasping — which is exactly what this seventh practice, named for its freedom from attachment, is training.
