A Body of Pure Wisdom

The treasury of giving turns from narrative back toward reflection for its final four kinds — releasing attachment to the merit of past buddhas, to rebirth in the lands of future buddhas, and to every heaven and vehicle of the present — before its most demanding kind, ultimate giving, in which the bodhisattva contemplates the body's own impurity in unflinching detail before giving it, without a single thought of regret, to whoever lacks eyes, ears, or limbs.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 169 — the treasury of giving concludes Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Past, Future, Present, and the Ultimate Gift

The final four kinds of giving turn from the wheel-turning king's narratives back toward the bodhisattva's own contemplation: releasing every fixed notion of the merit accumulated by buddhas past, every longing for rebirth among buddhas to come, and every reaction — whether delight or aversion — toward the vast catalog of heavens and vehicles that make up the present. Ultimate giving then closes the treasury by returning, one final time, to the body itself.

云何為菩薩過去施？此菩薩聞過去諸佛菩薩所有功德，聞已不著，了達非有，不起分別，不貪不味，亦不求取，無所依倚；見法如夢，無有堅固；於諸善根，不起有想，亦無所倚；但為教化，取著眾生，成熟佛法，而為演說；又復觀察：『過去諸法，十方推求都不可得。』作是念已，於過去法，畢竟皆捨。是名：過去施。云何為菩薩未來施？此菩薩聞未來諸佛之所修行，了達非有，不取於相，不別樂往生諸佛國土，不味不著，亦不生厭；不以善根迴向於彼，亦不於彼而退善根，常勤修行，未曾廢捨；但欲因彼境界攝取眾生，為說真實，令成熟佛法；然此法者非有處所、非無處所，非內、非外，非近、非遠。復作是念：『若法非有，不可不捨。』是名：未來施。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the bodhisattva's giving with regard to the past? This bodhisattva hears of every merit possessed by the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the past, and upon hearing of it, does not become attached; understanding that it does not truly exist as any fixed thing, they give rise to no discrimination toward it, feel neither craving nor relish for it, and neither seek after it nor grasp at it, relying upon nothing at all. They see every dharma as like a dream, without any solidity; and toward every wholesome root, they give rise to no notion of its fixed existence, and rely upon nothing there either. It is only for the sake of teaching and transforming living beings who cling to such things that they expound it, bringing the buddha's own Dharma to ripening. They further contemplate: "Seeking for the dharmas of the past throughout the ten directions, none of them can ever be found." Having so reflected, they give up entirely and completely every dharma of the past. This is called giving with regard to the past. What is the bodhisattva's giving with regard to the future? This bodhisattva hears of the practice to be cultivated by every buddha of the future, and understands that it does not truly exist as any fixed thing; they do not grasp at any characteristic of it, nor do they specially delight in being reborn within any buddha's own land because of it, feeling neither relish nor attachment toward it, and yet also giving rise to no aversion toward it. They do not dedicate their wholesome roots toward that future, nor do their wholesome roots regress on account of it; they forever cultivate and practice diligently, never once abandoning or setting it aside. It is only that, relying upon that very domain, they wish to gather in living beings, expounding the truth for them, and bringing the buddha's own Dharma to ripening; and yet this very Dharma has no fixed place where it exists, nor any place where it does not exist, is neither internal nor external, and is neither near nor far. They further reflect: "If a dharma does not truly exist, then there is nothing that need not be given up." This is called giving with regard to the future.

云何為菩薩現在施？此菩薩聞四天王眾天、三十三天、夜摩天、兜率陀天、化樂天、他化自在天、梵天、梵身天、梵輔天、梵眾天、大梵天、光天、少光天、無量光天、光音天、淨天、少淨天、無量淨天、遍淨天、廣天、少廣天、無量廣天、廣果天、無煩天、無熱天、善見天、善現天、色究竟天，乃至聞聲聞、緣覺具足功德。聞已，其心不迷、不沒、不聚、不散，但觀諸行如夢不實，無有貪著；為令眾生，捨離惡趣，心無分別，修菩薩道，成就佛法，而為開演。是名：現在施。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the bodhisattva's giving with regard to the present? This bodhisattva hears of the heaven of the Four Heavenly Kings, the Trāyastriṃśa Heaven, the Yāma Heaven, the Tuṣita Heaven, the Nirmāṇarati Heaven, the Paranirmitavaśavartin Heaven, the Brahmā Heaven, the Heaven of Brahmā's Own Body, the Heaven of Brahmā's Ministers, the Heaven of Brahmā's Retinue, the Great Brahmā Heaven, the Heaven of Light, the Heaven of Lesser Light, the Heaven of Immeasurable Light, the Ābhāsvara Heaven, the Pure Heaven, the Heaven of Lesser Purity, the Heaven of Immeasurable Purity, the Śubhakṛtsna Heaven, the Vast Heaven, the Heaven of Lesser Vastness, the Heaven of Immeasurable Vastness, the Bṛhatphala Heaven, the Heaven Without Affliction, the Heaven Without Heat, the Heaven of Skillful Vision, the Heaven of Skillful Appearance, and the Akaniṣṭha Heaven, and further hears of the merit fully possessed by every śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha. Upon hearing all of this, their mind is neither confused nor sunk down, neither gathered up nor scattered, but simply observes every conditioned activity as like a dream, unreal, without any craving or attachment toward it. It is for the sake of causing living beings to abandon and depart from the evil destinies that, their mind free of any discrimination, they cultivate the bodhisattva path, bring the buddha's own Dharma to accomplishment, and expound it for them. This is called giving with regard to the present.

云何為菩薩究竟施？佛子！此菩薩，假使有無量眾生或有無眼、或有無耳、或無鼻舌及以手足，來至其所，告菩薩言：『我身薄祜，諸根殘缺。唯願仁慈，以善方便，捨己所有，令我具足。』菩薩聞之，即便施與；假使由此，經阿僧祇劫，諸根不具，亦不心生一念悔惜。但自觀身，從初入胎，不淨微形，胞段諸根，生老病死；又觀此身，無有真實，無有慚愧，非賢聖物，臭穢不潔，骨節相持，血肉所塗，九孔常流，人所惡賤。作是觀已，不生一念愛著之心。復作是念：『此身危脆，無有堅固。我今云何而生戀著？應以施彼，充滿其願。如我所作，以此開導一切眾生，令於身心不生貪愛，悉得成就清淨智身。』是名：究竟施。是為菩薩摩訶薩第六施藏。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the bodhisattva's ultimate giving? Son of the Buddha! This bodhisattva — suppose there were immeasurable living beings, some without eyes, some without ears, some without a nose or tongue, or without hands or feet, who came before them and said to the bodhisattva: "My own body is thin in fortune, and my faculties are maimed and incomplete. I beg only that you, out of your own humane compassion, through your own skillful means, give up what you yourself possess, and cause me to become whole." Upon hearing this, the bodhisattva gives it at once; and even were this to continue for asaṃkhyeya kalpas, their own faculties remaining incomplete, they would never give rise to a single thought of regret or reluctance. They simply contemplate their own body: from the very moment of entering the womb, an impure and minute form, its bodily parts and faculties gradually formed, subject to birth, old age, sickness, and death. They further contemplate this body: that it possesses nothing true or real within it, has no shame or conscience, is not the possession of the worthy and the noble, is foul, filthy, and impure, held together by bone and joint, smeared over with flesh and blood, its nine orifices forever leaking, an object of loathing and contempt to others. Having so contemplated, they give rise to not a single thought of love or attachment toward it. They further reflect: "This body is precarious and fragile, without any solidity at all. Why, then, should I give rise to any fond attachment toward it? I should give it to that one, and fulfill their wish entirely. Just as I have done, so too, by this very deed, may I guide and instruct every living being, causing them to give rise to no craving or attachment toward body or mind, and causing every one of them to accomplish a body of pure wisdom." This is called ultimate giving. This is the bodhisattva-mahāsattva's sixth treasury: the treasury of giving.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The treasury closes by naming, one by one, every heaven in the traditional cosmology — the four heavens of desire, then heaven after heaven of the form realm, all the way to Akaniṣṭha at its summit — only to say the bodhisattva hears every one of them named and remains "neither confused nor sunk down, neither gathered up nor scattered." What began, three Parts ago, as sharing a meal with tiny creatures in one's own body ends here, in ultimate giving, by contemplating that very body in its full, unflattering physical detail — not to feel disgust for its own sake, but so that giving it away, when someone actually asks, involves no attachment left to overcome.
