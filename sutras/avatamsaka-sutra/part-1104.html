A World of Pure and Foul Together

She answers the question about her liberation by first saying who could possibly understand it — nine kinds of person, and no gods, humans, or followers of the lesser vehicles among them. Then she opens the past, and the world she opens is a mixed one.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1104 — an ancient world Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Three Questions

The verse ends and the prose resumes with the same eleven characters that opened the same moment with the previous teacher. What has changed is the number of questions.

Chinese Source

爾時，善財童子說此頌已，白普救眾生妙德夜神言：「天神！今此解脫甚深希有！其名何等？得此解脫其已久如？修何等行而得清淨？」夜神言：「善男子！是處難知，諸天及人、一切二乘所不能測。何以故？

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, when Good-Wealth had finished speaking these verses, he said to the night-spirit Wondrous Virtue Who Universally Rescues Beings: "Heavenly spirit! This liberation is profound and rare indeed. What is it called? How long is it since you attained this liberation? And by cultivating what practice did it come to be pure?" The night-spirit said: "Good son, this place is hard to know; gods and humans and all those of the two vehicles alike cannot fathom it. Why is that?

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

爾時，善財童子說此頌已，白 — at that time, when Good-Wealth had finished speaking these verses, he said. These first eleven characters are word for word the ones that opened the same moment with Joyful Eye in Part 1094, and the middle question here, 得此解脫其已久如, how long is it since you attained this liberation, is those same eight characters again. But there he asked two questions and here he asks three, and the new one is the last: 修何等行而得清淨, by cultivating what practice did it come to be pure. The pilgrim is learning to ask about the work rather than only the date.

A thing worth noticing about all three questions is what is missing from them. In Part 1100, Joyful Eye told him to come here and ask 菩薩云何入菩薩行、淨菩薩道 — how a bodhisattva enters the bodhisattva practices and purifies the bodhisattva path. Neither 入菩薩行 nor 淨菩薩道 occurs anywhere in this fascicle. He does not ask the assigned question. What he asks instead grows out of what he has just witnessed, and the word 淨, purify, survives from the assignment into his third question as if by memory rather than obedience.

Nine Kinds of Person Whose Ground This Is

Rather than name her liberation, she first draws a boundary around who is capable of standing where she stands. Nine clauses, each ending 者境界故 — because it is the domain of one who.

Chinese Source

此是住普賢菩薩行者境界故，住大悲藏者境界故，救護一切眾生者境界故，能淨一切三惡八難者境界故，能於一切佛剎中紹隆佛種不斷者境界故，

Ru-Yi Translation

Because this is the domain of one who abides in the practices of the bodhisattva Samantabhadra; the domain of one who abides in the treasury of great compassion; the domain of one who rescues and guards every being; the domain of one able to purify all three evil destinies and eight difficulties; the domain of one able, in every buddha-land, to continue and make flourish the buddha-lineage unbroken;

Chinese Source

能住持一切佛法者境界故，能於一切劫修菩薩行成滿大願海者境界故，能於一切法界海以清淨智光滅無明闇障者境界故，能以一念智慧光明普照一切三世方便海者境界故。我承佛力，今為汝說。

Ru-Yi Translation

the domain of one able to uphold and sustain every dharma of the buddhas; the domain of one able, throughout every eon, to cultivate the bodhisattva practices and fulfil the ocean of great vows; the domain of one able, throughout every ocean of the dharma-realm, to extinguish the darkening hindrance of ignorance with the pure light of wisdom; and the domain of one able, by the light of wisdom in a single thought, to illuminate universally every ocean of skilful means throughout the three times. Sustained by the Buddha's power, I shall now speak of it for you.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Nine clauses, each closing 者境界故 — because it is the domain of one who. The first three describe a person by where they stand: abiding in Samantabhadra's practices, abiding in the treasury of great compassion, rescuing and guarding beings. The remaining six all open with 能, able to, and describe a person by what they can do. The turn from position to capacity is the whole answer to Good-Wealth's third question. What makes the liberation pure is not a state one occupies but a set of things one can actually perform.

諸天及人、一切二乘所不能測 — gods and humans and all those of the two vehicles alike cannot fathom it. She has just been addressed as 天神, a heavenly spirit, and her first act is to say that gods cannot understand this. The exclusion includes her own apparent rank. Whatever she is, it is not the thing Good-Wealth has just called her.

我承佛力，今為汝說 — sustained by the Buddha's power, I shall now speak of it for you. The formula concedes the difficulty rather than dissolving it. She does not say the nine clauses were an exaggeration; she says she will speak anyway, and borrows the power to do it. Everything that follows in this fascicle is delivered under that borrowing.

Pure and Foul Together

The past opens, as these accounts always do, with an eon, a world, and a count of buddhas. What is unusual is the world's construction.

Chinese Source

「善男子！乃往古世，過佛剎微塵數劫，爾時有劫，名：圓滿清淨，世界名：毘盧遮那大威德，有須彌山微塵數如來於中出現。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, in an age long past, more eons ago than there are fine motes of dust in a buddha-land, there was an eon named Perfect and Complete Purity, and a world named Vairocana's Great Majestic Virtue, in which tathāgatas as numerous as the fine motes of dust of Mount Sumeru appeared.

Chinese Source

其佛世界，以一切香王摩尼寶為體，眾寶莊嚴，住無垢光明摩尼王海上。其形正圓，淨穢合成，一切嚴具帳雲而覆其上，一切莊嚴摩尼輪山千匝圍遶。

Ru-Yi Translation

That buddha-world had for its very substance the maṇi jewel of the king of every fragrance; it was adorned with every jewel, and it rested upon an ocean of maṇi kings of stainless light. Its shape was perfectly round, and it was formed of the pure and the foul combined; canopy-clouds of every adornment covered it above, and mountains of maṇi wheels of every adornment encircled it a thousand times round.

Chinese Source

有十萬億那由他四天下皆妙莊嚴，或有四天下惡業眾生於中止住，或有四天下雜業眾生於中止住，或有四天下善根眾生於中止住，或有四天下一向清淨諸大菩薩之所止住。

Ru-Yi Translation

It held a hundred thousand koṭis of nayutas of sets of four continents, all of them wondrously adorned. In some sets of four continents, beings of evil deeds dwelt; in some sets of four continents, beings of mixed deeds dwelt; in some sets of four continents, beings with roots of goodness dwelt; and some sets of four continents were dwelt in by great bodhisattvas wholly and only pure.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

其形正圓，淨穢合成 — its shape was perfectly round, and it was formed of the pure and the foul combined. Four characters of geometry and four of morality, in the same breath and the same grammar, as though how a world is shaped and what it is made of were the same kind of fact. This translation met exactly that pairing three parts ago. In Part 1101 the vision in the gap between two night-spirits sorted worlds first by defilement and purity and then, without marking the seam, by whether they lay level, face-down, or on their side. Here the two scales have merged into one sentence. The character pair 淨穢 occurs only this once in the whole fascicle.

The name of the eon is 圓滿清淨, Perfect and Complete Purity, and the world inside it is 淨穢合成, made of the pure and the foul together. That is not a contradiction the text is embarrassed by. A perfectly pure age is one in which foulness is included rather than excluded, because there is nowhere else to put it and no one left to put it there.

The four continents are then sorted by their inhabitants into four grades — 惡業, evil deeds; 雜業, mixed deeds; 善根, roots of goodness; and 一向清淨諸大菩薩, great bodhisattvas wholly and only pure. The phrase 一向清淨 appears twice in this fascicle and nowhere else: once in Part 1101 as the seventh kind of world, and once here as the fourth kind of continent. The taxonomy Good-Wealth was shown in a speck of dust turns out to be the floor plan of the world he is about to be told a story about.

Rice Without Ploughing

Out of a hundred thousand koṭis of nayutas of continents, the story needs one. It sits on the eastern rim, and the description of it is almost entirely about trees.

Chinese Source

「此界東際輪圍山側，有四天下，名：寶燈華幢。國界清淨，飲食豐足，不藉耕耘而生稻粱；宮殿樓閣悉皆奇妙；諸如意樹處處行列，

Ru-Yi Translation

"At the side of the encircling mountain on the eastern rim of that world there was a set of four continents named Banner of Jewelled Lamps and Flowers. Its lands were pure and its food and drink abundant; rice and grain grew there without depending on any ploughing; its palaces and towered pavilions were every one of them rare and wondrous; wish-fulfilling trees stood in rows in every place;

Chinese Source

種種香樹恒出香雲，種種鬘樹恒出鬘雲，種種華樹常雨妙華；種種寶樹出諸奇寶，無量色光周匝照耀；諸音樂樹出諸音樂，隨風吹動演妙音聲；日月光明摩尼寶王普照一切，晝夜受樂無時間斷。

Ru-Yi Translation

every kind of incense-tree constantly sent forth clouds of incense; every kind of garland-tree constantly sent forth clouds of garlands; every kind of flower-tree constantly rained down wondrous flowers; every kind of jewel-tree sent forth rare jewels whose measureless coloured light shone all around; and the music-trees sent forth every kind of music, playing wondrous sounds as the wind moved through them. Maṇi jewel kings of the light of sun and moon shone universally over everything, so that delight was received by day and by night without a moment's interruption.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Six kinds of tree are named — 如意樹 wish-fulfilling, 香樹 incense, 鬘樹 garland, 華樹 flower, 寶樹 jewel, and 音樂樹 music — and each is given a verb of production: sending out clouds, raining flowers, putting forth jewels, playing sounds. The land is described almost entirely by what grows on it and gives itself away. Even the music is agricultural; it is what the trees do when the wind arrives.

不藉耕耘而生稻粱 — rice and grain grew there without depending on any ploughing. The detail is older than this sūtra and belongs to the standard description of the northern continent, where nobody farms and food appears of itself. Placed here, at the opening of a story about a night-spirit's ancient past, it does a plainer job: it clears the labour out of the way. Nothing that happens in this land will be explicable by anyone having had to work for a living.

晝夜受樂無時間斷 — delight was received by day and by night without a moment's interruption. The teller is a night-spirit. She has spent four parts of this fascicle being praised for lights that end darkness, and the first world she chooses to describe from her own past is one where day and night make no difference at all to how much joy there is in it.

Part 1104 Complete

This continues chapter thirty-nine, 入法界品, in fascicle seventy. Part 1105 narrows from the four continents to a single country within Jambudvīpa, named Jewelled Flower Lamp, where a wheel-turning king is born by transformation inside a lotus.
