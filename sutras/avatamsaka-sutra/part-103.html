Two Further Tens That Please Every Buddha

Dharma Wisdom continues with two more lists of ten dharmas that cause every buddha to rejoice — the first built from qualities of practice, the second entirely a list of things a bodhisattva abides settled within, from non-negligence to a wisdom that sees every dharma as empty space.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 103 — the ten dharmas that please every buddha Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

The First Ten: Diligence, Patience, and a Mind That Leans on Nothing

Chinese Source

「佛子！菩薩摩訶薩復有十法，能令一切諸佛歡喜。何等為十？一者，精進不退；二者，不惜身命；三者，於諸利養無有希求；四者，知一切法皆如虛空；五者，善能觀察，普入法界；六者，知諸法印，心無倚著；七者，常發大願；八者，成就清淨忍智光明；九者，觀自善法，心無增減；十者，依無作門，修諸淨行。佛子！是為菩薩住十種法，能令一切如來歡喜。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva-mahāsattva has, moreover, ten further dharmas that are able to cause every buddha to rejoice. What are the ten? First, diligence that never turns back. Second, never begrudging their own body or life. Third, seeking nothing at all in the way of gain or offerings. Fourth, knowing that every dharma is just like empty space. Fifth, skillfully able to contemplate, universally entering the Dharma-realm. Sixth, knowing the seal of every dharma, their mind leaning on nothing at all. Seventh, forever giving rise to great vows. Eighth, accomplishing the pure light of patience and wisdom. Ninth, contemplating their own wholesome dharmas with a mind that neither increases nor diminishes them. Tenth, relying on the gate of non-action, cultivating every pure practice. Son of the Buddha, these are the ten dharmas a bodhisattva abides in that are able to cause every Tathāgata to rejoice."

The Second Ten: Abiding Settled

Chinese Source

「佛子！復有十法，能令一切諸佛歡喜。何者為十？所謂：安住不放逸；安住無生忍；安住大慈；安住大悲；安住滿足諸波羅蜜；安住諸行；安住大願；安住巧方便；安住勇猛力；安住智慧，觀一切法皆無所住，猶如虛空。佛子！若諸菩薩住此十法，能令一切諸佛歡喜。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Son of the Buddha! There are, moreover, ten further dharmas that are able to cause every buddha to rejoice. What are the ten? Namely: abiding settled in non-negligence; abiding settled in the patient acceptance of the unborn; abiding settled in great loving-kindness; abiding settled in great compassion; abiding settled in the fulfillment of every perfection; abiding settled in every practice; abiding settled in great vows; abiding settled in skillful means; abiding settled in courageous power; and abiding settled in wisdom, contemplating that every dharma dwells upon nothing at all, just like empty space. Son of the Buddha, if bodhisattvas abide in these ten dharmas, they are able to cause every buddha to rejoice."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The second list's every item begins with the same word, 安住, "abiding settled" — a repeated formula that turns the list itself into something closer to a recitation than an inventory. It is worth noticing what closes it: after loving-kindness, compassion, vows, and skillful means, the very last item is a wisdom that sees every dharma as resting on nothing at all, exactly like empty space. The chapter keeps returning to this pairing — vigorous, specific practice on one side, and a mind that clings to none of it on the other.
