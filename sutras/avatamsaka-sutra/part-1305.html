An Army Rises into the Sky

Five paired similes close the run of mixed images before the long series on vajra begins: a pure maṇi jewel that a cataract-clouded eye sees as impure, a medicine held by a spell that heals whoever sees or hears of it, ambrosia kept always at hand, a mechanical wooden figure that falls apart without its pegs, and the wheel-turning king's aloeswood named Elephant-Treasury, whose burning lifts his fourfold army into the sky.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1305 — fascicle 78 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

An Army Rises into the Sky

Five more paired similes continue the pattern — a jewel misjudged, a medicine that heals at a glance, ambrosia, a wooden figure, and an aloeswood incense that ends with the bodhisattva's wholesome roots leaving the three realms altogether.

Chinese Source

「善男子！譬如清淨摩尼妙寶，眼有翳故見為不淨。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心寶亦復如是，無智不信謂為不淨。「善男子！譬如有藥，為呪所持，若有眾生見、聞、同住，一切諸病皆得消滅。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心藥亦復如是，一切善根、智慧、方便、菩薩願智共所攝持，若有眾生見、聞、同住、憶念之者，諸煩惱病悉得除滅。「善男子！譬如有人，常持甘露，其身畢竟不變不壞。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，若常憶持菩提心露，令願智身畢竟不壞。「善男子！如機關木人，若無有楔，身即離散，不能運動。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，無菩提心，行即分散，不能成就一切佛法。「善男子！如轉輪王，有沈香寶，名曰：象藏；若燒此香，王四種兵悉騰虛空。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心香亦復如是，若發此意，即令菩薩一切善根永出三界，行如來智無為空中。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Just as a pure and wondrous maṇi jewel is seen as impure by an eye that has a cataract, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own jewel that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: those without wisdom and without faith declare it to be impure. Good son! Just as there is a medicine held by a spell, such that any sentient being who sees it, hears of it, or dwells alongside it has every illness extinguished, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own medicine that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: held together by every wholesome root, by wisdom, by skillful means, and by the bodhisattva's wisdom of vows, so that any sentient being who sees it, hears of it, dwells alongside it, or recollects it has every illness of affliction entirely removed. Good son! Just as a person who constantly keeps ambrosia has a body that in the end neither changes nor decays, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: if they constantly keep in recollection the ambrosia of the mind of bodhi, they cause the body of vow and wisdom to be in the end indestructible. Good son! Just as a mechanical wooden figure, if it lacks its pegs, falls apart at once and cannot move, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: without the mind of bodhi, their practice scatters at once and cannot accomplish any buddha-dharma. Good son! Just as a wheel-turning king has an aloeswood treasure named Elephant-Treasury, and when this incense is burned the king's fourfold army rises entirely into the sky, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own incense that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: when this intention is aroused, it at once causes every wholesome root of the bodhisattva to depart forever from the three realms and to move within the unconditioned sky of the Tathāgata's wisdom.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

象藏 — the aloeswood treasure of the wheel-turning king is named on the same pattern as the 海藏, Ocean-Treasury, jewel of Part 1298, and is rendered Elephant-Treasury to keep that pattern visible. 四種兵, the king's fourfold army, is rendered with the same phrase the site has used for it before; the simile has the whole force lifted into the sky by the burning of a single incense, which the passage matches to every wholesome root leaving the three realms at once.

翳 — the cataract in the first simile is the same word rendered cataract earlier in this chapter, where it was the world's cataract of delusion; here it is a fault in the viewer rather than in the jewel, and the bodhisattva's side of the simile makes the fault explicit as 無智不信, the absence of wisdom and of faith.
