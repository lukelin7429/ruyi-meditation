The Names of the Tathāgata Begin

With the Chapter on Vairocana complete, the sūtra opens a new assembly: the Buddha, having just accomplished awakening beneath the bodhi tree in Magadha, is now seated in the Hall of Universal Light. Ten bodhisattvas — Mañjuśrī foremost among them — arrive from ten directions, each from a distinct world with a distinct buddha. Mañjuśrī then opens the teaching that gives this chapter its name: within this very world alone, the Buddha is known by ten thousand different names, and the same is true of every world in every direction, without end.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 34 — opens fascicle 12 Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

The Second Assembly Opens

Chinese Source

爾時，世尊在摩竭提國阿蘭若法菩提場中，始成正覺，於普光明殿坐蓮華藏師子之座，妙悟皆滿，二行永絕；達無相法，住於佛住；得佛平等，到無障處；不可轉法，所行無礙；立不思議，普見三世。與十佛剎微塵數諸菩薩俱，莫不皆是一生補處，悉從他方而共來集，普善觀察諸眾生界、法界、世界、涅槃界，諸業果報、心行次第、一切文義，世、出世間，有為、無為，過、現、未來。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, the World-Honored One, within the awakening-site of the Araṇyaka Dharma in the country of Magadha, having just accomplished true awakening, sat within the Hall of Universal Light upon the lion-throne of the Lotus-Treasury. His wondrous awakening was fully complete, and the two conducts were forever severed; he had penetrated the markless Dharma, and abided in the very abode of a buddha; he had attained the equality of every buddha, and reached the place without any obstruction; his Dharma could not be turned back, and his every conduct was unobstructed; he had established the inconceivable, and universally beheld the three periods of time. He was together with bodhisattvas as numerous as the fine motes of dust in ten buddha-lands, none of whom were anything but bound to buddhahood in their very next birth, all of whom had come together, gathering from other directions, universally and excellently contemplating the realm of every living being, the Dharma-realm, the realm of worlds, the realm of nirvāṇa, the recompense of every karmic fruit, the sequence of every mental conduct, the meaning of every text, the mundane and the transcendent, the conditioned and the unconditioned, and the past, present, and future.

Chinese Source

時，諸菩薩作是思惟：「若世尊見愍我等，願隨所樂，開示佛剎、佛住、佛剎莊嚴、佛法性、佛剎清淨、佛所說法、佛剎體性、佛威德、佛剎成就、佛大菩提。如十方一切世界諸佛世尊，為成就一切菩薩故，令如來種性不斷故，救護一切眾生故，令諸眾生永離一切煩惱故，了知一切諸行故，演說一切諸法故，淨除一切雜染故，永斷一切疑網故，拔除一切希望故，滅壞一切愛著處故，說諸菩薩十住、十行、十迴向、十藏、十地、十願、十定、十通、十頂，及說如來地、如來境界、如來神力、如來所行、如來力、如來無畏、如來三昧、如來神通、如來自在、如來無礙、如來眼、如來耳、如來鼻、如來舌、如來身、如來意、如來辯才、如來智慧、如來最勝。願佛世尊，亦為我說！」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, the bodhisattvas had this thought: "If the World-Honored One would look upon us with pity, we pray that, according to what we delight in, he would open and reveal the buddha-land, the buddha's own abode, the buddha-land's adornment, the buddha's own Dharma-nature, the buddha-land's purity, the Dharma the Buddha teaches, the buddha-land's own essence and nature, the Buddha's majestic virtue, the buddha-land's accomplishment, and the Buddha's own great bodhi — just as every buddha, every World-Honored One, of every world of the ten directions does, in order to bring every bodhisattva to accomplishment, in order to cause the lineage of the Tathāgata to never be severed, in order to rescue and protect every living being, in order to cause every living being to forever leave behind every affliction, in order to thoroughly understand every conduct, in order to proclaim every dharma, in order to purely remove every defilement, in order to forever sever every net of doubt, in order to uproot every hope and expectation, in order to extinguish and destroy every place of love and attachment — teaching the bodhisattva's ten abodes, ten practices, ten dedications, ten treasuries, ten grounds, ten vows, ten samādhis, ten spiritual penetrations, and ten crowns, and teaching the Tathāgata's own ground, the Tathāgata's own domain, the Tathāgata's own spiritual power, the Tathāgata's own conduct, the Tathāgata's own power, the Tathāgata's own fearlessness, the Tathāgata's own samādhi, the Tathāgata's own spiritual transmutation, the Tathāgata's own sovereign mastery, the Tathāgata's own unobstructedness, the Tathāgata's own eye, the Tathāgata's own ear, the Tathāgata's own nose, the Tathāgata's own tongue, the Tathāgata's own body, the Tathāgata's own mind, the Tathāgata's own eloquence, the Tathāgata's own wisdom, and the Tathāgata's own supremacy. We pray that the Buddha, the World-Honored One, would likewise teach this for our sake!"

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This is the sūtra's second assembly, and the frame shifts accordingly: the vast opening scene at the awakening-site — the bodhi tree, the throne, the boundless assembly of world-lords — now narrows into a single hall, and the bodhisattvas' unspoken request lists, in one long breath, nearly every major teaching category the rest of this eighty-fascicle sūtra will eventually unfold: the ten abodes, ten practices, ten dedications, ten grounds, and more, each destined to become its own chapter many fascicles from now. What follows in this chapter answers only the first, most basic of these questions — not yet what the Buddha teaches, but what he is even called.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Mañjuśrī's own arrival, from the east, from a world named Golden Color under a buddha named Unshakable Wisdom, marks his entry into the sūtra proper — he will remain one of its two central bodhisattva voices (alongside Samantabhadra) through much of what follows. The nine companions who arrive alongside him from the other nine directions — Foremost in Awakening, Foremost in Wealth, Foremost in Treasure, and the rest — will each, in coming chapters, pose questions or receive teachings of their own; their names are not decorative but functional, previewing the roles they are about to play.

Mañjuśrī Opens the Teaching

Chinese Source

爾時，文殊師利菩薩摩訶薩，承佛威力，普觀一切菩薩眾會而作是言：「此諸菩薩甚為希有！諸佛子！佛國土不可思議，佛住、佛剎莊嚴、佛法性、佛剎清淨、佛說法、佛出現、佛剎成就、佛阿耨多羅三藐三菩提皆不可思議。何以故？諸佛子！十方世界一切諸佛，知諸眾生樂欲不同，隨其所應，說法調伏，如是乃至等法界、虛空界。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Mañjuśrī Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva, sustained by the Buddha's own power, universally beheld the whole assembly of bodhisattvas, and spoke these words: "These bodhisattvas are utterly rare indeed! Buddha's children! The buddha-land is inconceivable; the buddha's own abode, the buddha-land's adornment, the buddha's own Dharma-nature, the buddha-land's purity, the Dharma the Buddha teaches, the Buddha's own appearance, the buddha-land's accomplishment, and the Buddha's own unsurpassed, perfect awakening are all inconceivable. Why is this so? Buddha's children! Every buddha of every world of the ten directions, knowing that the delight and desire of every living being is not the same, teaches the Dharma and tames them according to what is fitting for each — and it is thus, reaching even the whole extent of the Dharma-realm and the realm of empty space.

Chinese Source

「諸佛子！如來於此娑婆世界諸四天下，種種身、種種名、種種色相、種種修短、種種壽量、種種處所、種種諸根、種種生處、種種語業、種種觀察，令諸眾生各別知見。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Buddha's children! The Tathāgata, throughout every set of four continents within this Sahā world, has every kind of body, every kind of name, every kind of form and mark, every kind of length of life, every kind of measure of lifespan, every kind of place, every kind of faculty, every kind of place of birth, every kind of verbal karma, and every kind of way of being contemplated, causing every living being to know and see him each in their own distinct way.

Chinese Source

「諸佛子！如來於此四天下中，或名：一切義成，或名：圓滿月，或名：師子吼，或名：釋迦牟尼，或名：第七仙，或名：毘盧遮那，或名：瞿曇氏，或名：大沙門，或名：最勝，或名：導師……。如是等，其數十千，令諸眾生各別知見。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Buddha's children! The Tathāgata, within this set of four continents, is variously named: Every Aim Accomplished, or named the Full and Perfect Moon, or named the Lion's Roar, or named Śākyamuni, or named the Seventh Sage, or named Vairocana, or named the Gautama Clan, or named the Great Śramaṇa, or named the Supreme, or named the Guide... Such as these, their number ten thousand, causing every living being to know and see him each in their own distinct way.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Notice that "Śākyamuni" and "Vairocana" appear here as two names among ten, listed with the same weight as "the Seventh Sage" or "the Gautama Clan" — not as a special case to be singled out, but as one more instance of the very pattern being taught. This is worth pausing on given what Part 21 already established: that this Sahā world's own buddha is Vairocana himself. Here, in the plainest possible terms, the sūtra treats the identity of "Śākyamuni" and "Vairocana" as already settled and unremarkable — simply two of the ten thousand names by which the beings of this one set of four continents happen to know the very same buddha.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Ten worlds, ten thousand names each, for what the teaching insists is fundamentally the same buddha in every case — the point is not that these are different beings, but that "name" itself is entirely a function of the audience receiving it, not a fixed property of the one being named. Chengguan's commentary reads this chapter as a direct continuation of the cosmological argument running since fascicle six: if the Buddha's body already pervades every world without division, then of course no single name could ever be adequate to it — a name is always local, addressed to beings who share a language and a world, while what it names is not.

Part 35 zooms outward to the scale of the whole Sahā world itself, and the worlds that surround it.
