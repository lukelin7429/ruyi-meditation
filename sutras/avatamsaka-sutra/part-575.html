Kalpas of Praise for a Single Tathāgata

After the long litanies of Parts 573 and 574, the verses return to their earlier four-line form. A single body multiplies into unspeakable heads, tongues, and sounds, each still praising a buddha whose merit outlasts every kalpa spent on it — and then the scale narrows again, to lotus-flower worlds folded into a single mote of dust.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 575 — praise that outlasts every kalpa spent giving it Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Single Body, Every Sound Praising

The verses trace a single body multiplying into unspeakable heads, then tongues, then sounds — every one of them still not enough kalpas to exhaust a buddha's merit.

Chinese Source

不可言說一切劫，讚不可說諸功德，不可說劫猶可盡，不可說德不可盡。
不可言說諸如來，不可言說諸舌根，歎佛不可言說德，不可說劫無能盡。
十方所有諸眾生，一切同時成正覺，於中一佛普能現，不可言說一切身。

此不可說中一身，示現於頭不可說；此不可說中一頭，示現於舌不可說；此不可說中一舌，示現於聲不可說；此不可說中一聲，經於劫住不可說。
如一如是一切佛，如一如是一切身，如一如是一切頭，如一如是一切舌，如一如是一切聲，不可說劫恒讚佛，不可說劫猶可盡，歎佛功德無能盡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Unspeakable are all the kalpas needed to praise unspeakable merits; yet unspeakable kalpas could still be exhausted, while unspeakable merit could never be exhausted.
Unspeakable are the tathāgatas, unspeakable are their tongue-faculties; praising the buddha's unspeakable virtue, unspeakable kalpas could never exhaust it.
Every being in the ten directions, all attaining right enlightenment at the same moment — within that, a single buddha is able to manifest universally, in unspeakable bodies, every one.

Within that unspeakable number of bodies, from a single body, unspeakable heads are displayed; within that unspeakable number of heads, from a single head, unspeakable tongues are displayed; within that unspeakable number of tongues, from a single tongue, unspeakable sounds are displayed; within that unspeakable number of sounds, from a single sound, unspeakable kalpas are abided in.
As with one, so with every buddha; as with one, so with every body; as with one, so with every head; as with one, so with every tongue; as with one, so with every sound — through unspeakable kalpas constantly praising the buddha, unspeakable kalpas could still be exhausted, while praising the buddha's merit could never be exhausted.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

不可說劫猶可盡，不可說德不可盡 — unspeakable kalpas could still be exhausted, while unspeakable merit could never be exhausted. This line, repeated at the close of both stanzas above, draws a line the entire chapter has been circling since the doubling ladder of Part 567: the largest nameable quantity of time is still finite enough to run out. Merit is not being called a bigger number than the unspeakably unspeakable — it is being placed in a different category altogether, one that exhaustion does not reach.

Lotus Worlds in a Single Mote of Dust

The scale reverses again, folding unspeakable lotus-flower worlds — each with its own tathāgata — into a single mote of dust, then multiplying that dust across an unbounded ocean of lands.

Chinese Source

一微塵中能悉有，不可言說蓮華界，一一蓮華世界中，賢首如來不可說。
乃至法界悉周遍，其中所有諸微塵，世界若成若住壞，其數無量不可說。
一微塵處無邊際，無量諸剎普來入，十方差別不可說，剎海分布不可說。

一一剎中有如來，壽命劫數不可說，諸佛所行不可說，甚深妙法不可說，神通大力不可說，無障礙智不可說，入於毛孔不可說，毛孔因緣不可說，成就十力不可說，覺悟菩提不可說，入淨法界不可說，獲深智藏不可說。
種種數量不可說，如其一切悉了知；種種形量不可說，於此靡不皆通達。
種種三昧不可說，悉能經劫於中住，於不可說諸佛所，所行清淨不可說。
得不可說無礙心，往詣十方不可說，神力示現不可說，所行無際不可說，往詣眾剎不可說，了達諸佛不可說，精進勇猛不可說，智慧通達不可說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within a single mote of dust, unspeakable lotus-flower realms can be entirely contained; and within each and every one of those lotus-flower worlds, the Tathāgata Foremost in Virtue is unspeakably present.
Extending to pervade the entire dharma-realm, every mote of dust found within it, whether worlds are forming, abiding, or decaying — their number is measureless and unspeakable.
Within a single mote of dust, without any boundary or edge, measureless lands universally come and enter; the distinctions of the ten directions are unspeakable, and the distribution of that ocean of lands is unspeakable.

Within each and every one of those lands there is a tathāgata, whose lifespan in kalpas is unspeakable; every buddha's practice is unspeakable; the profound and wondrous dharma is unspeakable; spiritual power and great strength are unspeakable; unobstructed wisdom is unspeakable; entering into pores is unspeakable; the causes and conditions of those pores are unspeakable; accomplishing the ten powers is unspeakable; awakening to bodhi is unspeakable; entering the pure dharma-realm is unspeakable; and attaining the profound treasury of wisdom is unspeakable.
Every manner of number and measure is unspeakable, yet all of it is entirely known just as it is; every manner of form and dimension is unspeakable, yet none of it goes without complete comprehension.
Every manner of samādhi is unspeakable, and one is able to abide within it through every kalpa; at the places of unspeakable buddhas, the purity of practice there is unspeakable.
Attaining an unspeakable, unobstructed mind, journeying to the ten directions is unspeakable; displaying spiritual power is unspeakable; the boundlessness of one's practice is unspeakable; journeying to every land is unspeakable; thoroughly comprehending every buddha is unspeakable; diligence and valor are unspeakable; and the thorough penetration of wisdom is unspeakable.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

種種數量不可說，如其一切悉了知 — every manner of number and measure is unspeakable, yet all of it is entirely known just as it is. This is the pivot the chapter keeps returning to: unspeakable does not mean unknown. A bodhisattva's knowledge is said to reach every one of these unspeakable quantities completely, even though no language, including the doubling ladder that opened this chapter, could ever state them.
