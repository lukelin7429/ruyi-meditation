Ten Kinds of Body

Assemblies beyond telling emerge at every single hair's-tip, and the bodhisattva answers every distinct question without fear, timidity, doubt, or error — and completes the ninth practice with ten kinds of body, becoming through them a dwelling, a rescuer, a refuge, a guide, a teacher, a lamp, and a light for every living being.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 149 — the practice of wholesome dharmas concludes Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Without Fear, Without Error

Chinese Source

復次，假使一毛端處，念念出不可說不可說道場眾會；一切毛端處，皆亦如是。盡未來劫，彼劫可盡，眾會無盡。是諸眾會，於念念中，以各別言辭，各別所問；菩薩於一念中，悉能領受，無怖無怯，無疑無謬，而作是念：『設一切眾生以如是語業俱來問我，我為說法無斷無盡，皆令歡喜，住於善道；復令善解一切言辭，能為眾生說種種法，而於言語無所分別。假使不可說不可說種種言辭而來問難，一念悉領，一音咸答，普使開悟，無有遺餘。以得一切智灌頂故，以得無礙藏故，以得一切法圓滿光明故，具足一切智智故。』

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, suppose that at a single hair's-tip, moment after moment, there emerged assembly-grounds beyond telling, beyond all telling; and at every single hair's-tip, entirely in the same way. Through kalpas to the very end of the future, those kalpas could reach their end, and yet those assemblies would never be exhausted. Those very assemblies, moment after moment, each with their own distinct words, each with their own distinct question; the bodhisattva, in a single thought, is entirely able to receive and understand all of it, without fear, without timidity, without doubt, and without error, and reflects in this way: "Suppose every living being, through such verbal karma as this, were all to come at once to ask of me — I would teach the Dharma for them without break and without end, causing everyone to rejoice, and to abide upon the wholesome path; and further cause them to skillfully understand every kind of speech, able to teach every kind of dharma for the sake of living beings, while making no discrimination at all regarding language. Suppose, moreover, that every kind of speech beyond telling, beyond all telling, were to come to question and challenge me — in a single thought I would receive and understand it all, and with a single sound answer every one of them, universally causing them to open to understanding, with nothing at all left over. This is because I have attained the consecration of all-knowing wisdom, because I have attained the unobstructed treasury, because I have attained the fully perfected light of every dharma, and because I am fully endowed with the very wisdom of all-knowing wisdom."

Chinese Source

佛子！此菩薩摩訶薩安住善法行已，能自清淨，亦能以無所著方便而普饒益一切眾生，不見有眾生得出離者。如於此三千大千世界，如是乃至於不可說三千大千世界，變身金色，妙音具足，於一切法無所障礙而作佛事。

Ru-Yi Translation

Son of the Buddha, this bodhisattva-mahāsattva, having abided settled in this practice of wholesome dharmas, is able to purify themselves, and is also able, through skillful means free of attachment, to universally benefit every living being, and yet sees no living being at all who has attained release. Just as within this great trichiliocosm, so too within trichiliocosms beyond telling, transforming their own body to a golden color, their wondrous voice fully endowed, they perform the Buddha's own work, meeting no obstruction at all in any dharma.

Ten Kinds of Body

Chinese Source

佛子！此菩薩摩訶薩成就十種身。所謂：入無邊法界非趣身，滅一切世間故；入無邊法界諸趣身，生一切世間故；不生身，住無生平等法故；不滅身，一切滅、言說不可得故；不實身，得如實故；不妄身，隨應現故；不遷身，離死此生彼故；不壞身，法界性無壞故；一相身，三世語言道斷故；無相身，善能觀察法相故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Son of the Buddha, this bodhisattva-mahāsattva accomplishes ten kinds of body. Namely: a body that enters the boundless Dharma-realm beyond every destiny, because it extinguishes every worldly thing; a body that enters the boundless Dharma-realm within every destiny, because it is born within every worldly thing; an unborn body, because it abides in the unborn, equal Dharma; an unceasing body, because every kind of cessation, spoken of, can never actually be found; a non-illusory body, because it attains what is exactly as real; a non-false body, because it manifests in accordance with what is fitting; an unshifting body, because it leaves behind dying here and being born there; an indestructible body, because the very nature of the Dharma-realm can never be destroyed; a body of a single mark, because the path of language across the three periods of time is cut off; and a markless body, because it is skillfully able to contemplate the marks of every dharma.

Chinese Source

菩薩成就如是十種身，為一切眾生舍，長養一切善根故；為一切眾生救，令其得大安隱故；為一切眾生歸，與其作大依處故；為一切眾生導，令得無上出離故；為一切眾生師，令入真實法中故；為一切眾生燈，令其明見業報故；為一切眾生光，令照甚深妙法故；為一切三世炬，令其曉悟實法故；為一切世間照，令入光明地中故；為一切諸趣明，示現如來自在故。佛子！是名菩薩摩訶薩第九善法行。菩薩安住此行，為一切眾生作清涼法池，能盡一切佛法源故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The bodhisattva, having accomplished such ten kinds of body as this, becomes a dwelling for every living being, because they nurture and grow every wholesome root; becomes a rescuer for every living being, because they cause them to attain great security; becomes a refuge for every living being, because they become a great place of reliance for them; becomes a guide for every living being, because they cause them to attain unsurpassed release; becomes a teacher for every living being, because they cause them to enter into the true Dharma; becomes a lamp for every living being, because they cause them to clearly see their own karmic recompense; becomes a light for every living being, because they cause the most profound, wondrous Dharma to be illumined; becomes a torch for every one of the three periods of time, because they cause beings to awaken to and understand the true Dharma; becomes an illumination for the whole world, because they cause beings to enter into the ground of light; and becomes a brightness for every destiny, because they manifest the Tathāgata's own sovereign freedom. Son of the Buddha, this is called the bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own ninth practice, the practice of wholesome dharmas. The bodhisattva, abiding settled in this practice, becomes a cool and refreshing pool of Dharma for every living being, because they are able to exhaust the very source of every buddha-dharma.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The ten kinds of body listed here work in deliberate pairs and near-opposites — a body entering the Dharma-realm beyond every destiny paired with one entering within every destiny, an unborn body paired with an unceasing one — building toward a single point: none of the ordinary categories (born or unborn, real or unreal, moving or fixed) actually applies cleanly. What survives that dissolution is entirely practical: ten roles a bodhisattva plays for living beings — dwelling, rescuer, refuge, guide, teacher, lamp, light, torch, illumination, brightness — each one a form of the same single dhāraṇī-based eloquence turned toward concrete, ordinary need.
