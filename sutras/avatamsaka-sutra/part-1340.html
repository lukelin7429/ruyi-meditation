Like the Moon Waxing and Waning

The great gāthā continues through the buddha appearing as a magistrate who judges cases, a minister who governs unseen, a petty king scattered among countless others, a flying wheel-turning emperor, and then rising through the heavens themselves: the four world-protecting kings, the great king of Trāyastriṃśa in the Hall of the True Dharma, the kings of Yāma, Tuṣita, Nirmāṇarati, and Paranirmita-vaśavartin, the assembly of Brahmā, and at last the Akaniṣṭha Heaven, arriving and departing in a manner none can trace. Then two similes for how the Tathāgata's own unobstructed wisdom brings every being to maturity: an illusionist displaying every kind of illusory deed, and the pure moon in the sky, waxing and waning, its image cast in every river and pool, outshining every star, just as the buddha's own moon of wisdom is reflected in the minds of bodhisattvas and outshines the śrāvakas.

Like the Moon Waxing and Waning

The buddha's forms among rulers and every heaven, and the first two similes for his unobstructed wisdom — the illusionist and the moon.

Chinese Source

或現上服以嚴身，
首戴華冠蔭高蓋，
四兵前後共圍遶，
誓眾宣威伏小王。

或為聽訟斷獄官，
善解世間諸法務，
所有與奪皆明審，
令其一切悉欣伏。

或作大臣專弼輔，
善用諸王治政法，
十方利益皆周遍，
一切眾生莫了知。

或為粟散諸小王，
或作飛行轉輪帝，
令諸王子采女眾，
悉皆受化無能測。

或作護世四天王，
統領諸龍夜叉等，
為其眾會而說法，
一切皆令大欣慶。

或為忉利大天王，
住善法堂歡喜園，
首戴華冠說妙法，
諸天覲仰莫能測。

或住夜摩兜率天，
化樂自在魔王所，
居處摩尼寶宮殿，
說真實行令調伏。

或至梵天眾會中，
說四無量諸禪道，
普令歡喜便捨去，
而莫知其往來相。

或至阿迦尼吒天，
為說覺分諸寶華，
及餘無量聖功德，
然後捨去無知者。

如來無礙智所見，
其中一切諸眾生，
悉以無邊方便門，
種種教化令成熟。

譬如幻師善幻術，
現作種種諸幻事；
佛化眾生亦如是，
為其示現種種身。

譬如淨月在虛空，
令世眾生見增減，
一切河池現影像，
所有星宿奪光色。

如來智月出世間，
亦以方便示增減，
菩薩心水現其影，
聲聞星宿無光色。

Ru-Yi Translation

Some see him displaying fine robes to adorn his body,
his head crowned with a flower garland and shaded by a lofty canopy,
surrounded before and behind by a fourfold army,
proclaiming his might before the assembly and subduing lesser kings.

Some see him as a magistrate who hears disputes and judges cases,
well versed in every worldly matter of law,
examining clearly every grant and every seizure,
so that all are entirely pleased and submit.

Some see him as a great minister devoted to assisting his king,
skillfully applying every method of governance,
benefiting the ten directions universally,
and yet no sentient being is able to understand it.

Some see him as one of the countless petty kings scattered like grains of millet,
some as a flying wheel-turning emperor,
causing every prince and every palace maiden
to be transformed by him, none able to fathom it.

Some see him as one of the four world-protecting heavenly kings,
governing the nāgas, the yakṣas, and the rest,
expounding the dharma to their assemblies,
causing all of them to rejoice greatly.

Some see him as the great deva-king of Trāyastriṃśa,
abiding in the Hall of the True Dharma and the Garden of Joy,
his head crowned with a flower garland, expounding the wondrous dharma,
so that the devas who gaze up at him in homage are unable to fathom it.

Some see him abiding in the Yāma Heaven, the Tuṣita Heaven,
and the realms of the Nirmāṇarati and Paranirmita-vaśavartin kings,
dwelling within palaces of maṇi jewels,
expounding the true practice so as to tame them.

Some see him arrive within the assembly of the Brahmā heaven,
expounding the four immeasurables and every path of dhyāna,
causing them all to rejoice and then departing,
and none can know the manner of his coming or going.

Some see him arrive in the Akaniṣṭha Heaven,
expounding for them the jeweled flowers of the factors of awakening
and every other measureless noble merit,
and then depart, with none who know it.

All the sentient beings within the domain
seen by the Tathāgata's own unobstructed wisdom,
he brings entirely to maturity through every kind of teaching,
by boundless gates of skillful means.

Just as an illusionist, skilled in the art of illusion,
displays and performs every kind of illusory deed,
so too does the buddha transform sentient beings,
displaying for them every kind of body.

Just as the pure moon in the sky
causes the beings of the world to see it wax and wane,
its image appearing in every river and pool,
and it outshines the color of every star,

so too does the Tathāgata's own moon of wisdom appear in the world,
likewise showing its waxing and waning through skillful means;
its reflection appears in the water of the bodhisattvas' own minds,
while it outshines the śrāvakas, its stars, of their light and color.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

或作……或為……或至 — the run of nine stanzas from the fine-robed lord to the Akaniṣṭha Heaven forms a single ascending sequence, moving from human rulers, a magistrate, a minister, a petty king, a wheel-turning emperor, up through the heavens in their standard order, the four world-protectors, Trāyastriṃśa, Yāma, Tuṣita, Nirmāṇarati, Paranirmita-vaśavartin, Brahmā, and finally Akaniṣṭha, the highest heaven of form named earlier in this translation. 善法堂 is kept as the Hall of the True Dharma, the form already established on this site for Śakra's own hall in Trāyastriṃśa.

譬如淨月在虛空……如來智月出世間 — the moon simile is given as a matched pair of stanzas, the moon itself and then its application to the Tathāgata, each ending on the same note, that it outshines the stars; the translation keeps the parallel structure, ending the first stanza's list with 所有星宿奪光色 unresolved into its own clause so that the second stanza's 聲聞星宿無光色 answers it directly, śrāvakas standing in the place the stars held before.
