The Same Ten, Differently Practiced

This is one of the most consequential passages in the chapter, and it is easy to read past. The ten courses of wholesome action do not lead anywhere in particular. Practiced at their highest degree, the very same ten produce the śrāvaka vehicle, or the pratyekabuddha vehicle, or a bodhisattva's vast practice, or every buddha-dharma — and what decides between them is not the conduct but the mind undertaking it.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 416 — the three vehicles from one set of actions, and the fruits of the unwholesome ten Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

One Set of Actions, Four Outcomes

Each outcome is introduced with the identical phrase — these same ten courses at their highest degree — and then differentiated only by a string of clauses about the mind. The conduct is held constant on purpose, so that everything doing the work is visible.

Chinese Source

「佛子！此菩薩摩訶薩復作是念：『十不善業道，是地獄、畜生、餓鬼受生因；十善業道，是人、天乃至有頂處受生因。又此上品十善業道，以智慧修習，心狹劣故，怖三界故，闕大悲故，從他聞聲而解了故，成聲聞乘。又此上品十善業道，修治清淨，不從他教，自覺悟故，大悲方便不具足故，悟解甚深因緣法故，成獨覺乘。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Child of the Buddha! This bodhisattva mahāsattva thinks further in this way: 'The ten courses of unwholesome action are the cause of birth in the hells, among animals, and among hungry ghosts; the ten courses of wholesome action are the cause of birth among humans and gods, on up to the summit of existence. Further, these same ten courses of wholesome action at their highest degree, cultivated with wisdom — because the mind is narrow and inferior, because one dreads the three realms, because great compassion is lacking, and because understanding is arrived at through hearing another's voice — become the śrāvaka vehicle. Further, these same ten courses of wholesome action at their highest degree, cultivated and purified — because one does not follow another's teaching but awakens for oneself, because great compassion and skillful means are not complete, and because one awakens to and understands the profound dharma of causes and conditions — become the pratyekabuddha vehicle.

Chinese Source

又此上品十善業道，修治清淨，心廣無量故，具足悲愍故，方便所攝故，發生大願故，不捨眾生故，希求諸佛大智故，淨治菩薩諸地故，淨修一切諸度故，成菩薩廣大行。又此上品十善業道，一切種清淨故，乃至證十力、四無畏故，一切佛法皆得成就。是故我今等行十善，應令一切具足清淨；如是方便，菩薩當學。』

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, these same ten courses of wholesome action at their highest degree, cultivated and purified — because the mind is broad and measureless, because pity and compassion are complete, because they are gathered in by skillful means, because great vows are given rise to, because beings are not abandoned, because one seeks the great wisdom of the buddhas, because the bodhisattva grounds are purified and prepared, and because all the pāramitās are cultivated in purity — become the vast practice of a bodhisattva. Further, these same ten courses of wholesome action at their highest degree — because they are purified in every mode, on up to the realization of the ten powers and the four fearlessnesses — bring every buddha-dharma to accomplishment. Therefore I now practice the ten wholesome courses equally, and should cause them all to be complete and pure. Such is the skillful means a bodhisattva ought to learn.'

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The differentiating clauses for the first two vehicles are stated as lacks rather than errors. The śrāvaka's mind is narrow, dreads the three realms, wants for great compassion, and takes its understanding from another's voice; the pratyekabuddha awakens without a teacher but is not complete in compassion and skillful means. Nothing here is called wrong. The same actions are simply carrying less, and arrive correspondingly nearer.

Which makes the bodhisattva's list worth reading closely, since it is longer than the other two together. Breadth of mind, complete compassion, skillful means, great vows, not abandoning beings, seeking a buddha's wisdom, purifying the grounds, cultivating the pāramitās — eight clauses, and not one of them is a further action to perform. They describe the disposition inside which the ten courses are already being practiced.

是故我今等行十善 — therefore I now practice the ten wholesome courses equally. The conclusion the bodhisattva draws is not to practice something more advanced. It is to practice these same ten, evenly, without letting any of them be the one that is neglected.

What Each Offence Leaves Behind

The reflection turns to the other side of the same principle. The unwholesome ten are graded by degree into three destinies, and then — for those who do return to a human birth — each offence is given two specific consequences, which read less like punishments than like the shape a life takes when it has been lived a certain way.

Chinese Source

「佛子！此菩薩摩訶薩又作是念：『十不善業道，上者地獄因，中者畜生因，下者餓鬼因。於中，殺生之罪能令眾生墮於地獄、畜生、餓鬼；若生人中，得二種果報，一者短命，二者多病。偷盜之罪亦令眾生墮三惡道；若生人中，得二種果報，一者貧窮，二者共財不得自在。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Child of the Buddha! This bodhisattva mahāsattva thinks further in this way: 'Of the ten courses of unwholesome action, the highest degree is a cause of the hells, the middling a cause of the animal realm, and the lowest a cause of the hungry ghosts. Among them, the offence of killing is able to make beings fall into the hells, the animal realm, or the hungry ghosts; and should they be born among humans, they obtain two kinds of fruit — the first, a short life; the second, much sickness. The offence of stealing likewise makes beings fall into the three evil destinies; and should they be born among humans, they obtain two kinds of fruit — the first, poverty; the second, having no freedom over property held in common.

Chinese Source

邪婬之罪亦令眾生墮三惡道；若生人中，得二種果報，一者妻不貞良，二者不得隨意眷屬。妄語之罪亦令眾生墮三惡道；若生人中，得二種果報，一者多被誹謗，二者為他所誑。兩舌之罪亦令眾生墮三惡道；若生人中，得二種果報，一者眷屬乖離，二者親族弊惡。

Ru-Yi Translation

The offence of sexual misconduct likewise makes beings fall into the three evil destinies; and should they be born among humans, they obtain two kinds of fruit — the first, a spouse who is not faithful; the second, not obtaining the household they would wish. The offence of false speech likewise makes beings fall into the three evil destinies; and should they be born among humans, they obtain two kinds of fruit — the first, being much slandered; the second, being deceived by others. The offence of divisive speech likewise makes beings fall into the three evil destinies; and should they be born among humans, they obtain two kinds of fruit — the first, a household that falls apart; the second, kin who are corrupt.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The two fruits assigned to each offence are not arbitrary penalties; each is the offence itself experienced from the receiving end. One who deceived is deceived; one who divided households finds their own falling apart; one who slandered is slandered. The list reads as a description of what a habit looks like once it has become a world rather than as a schedule of sentences.

Note also that the destinies are graded by degree rather than by which offence was committed — the highest degree of any of the ten leads to the hells, the lowest to the hungry ghosts. Intensity decides where, and the particular offence decides what shape the residue takes afterwards.
