Vimuktacandra Asks Again

Vajragarbha has said the world cannot receive this teaching, and so he keeps silent. Vimuktacandra Bodhisattva does not let the matter rest. He describes the assembly that is actually present — purified, undeluded, no longer taking its understanding secondhand — and asks again. Vajragarbha answers that his silence was never about this assembly at all, but about those elsewhere who would hear and be harmed by their own doubt. So Vimuktacandra asks a third time, with a simile drawn from the schoolroom.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 401 — the second and third requests, and the simile of the alphabet Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

This Assembly Is Ready

Vimuktacandra's first move is to answer Vajragarbha's objection on its own terms. Vajragarbha said the world cannot receive it; Vimuktacandra replies by describing exactly who is sitting here — beings who have served countless buddhas, who are free of stain, and who, crucially, no longer take their understanding of the Dharma on anyone else's authority. Whatever may be true of the world at large, these listeners can realize what is said.

Chinese Source

爾時，解脫月菩薩聞是說已，白金剛藏菩薩言：「佛子！今此眾會皆悉已集，善淨深心，善潔思念，善修諸行，善集助道，善能親近百千億佛，成就無量功德善根，捨離癡惑，無有垢染，深心信解，於佛法中不隨他教。善哉佛子！當承佛神力而為演說，此諸菩薩於如是等甚深之處皆能證知。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then Vimuktacandra Bodhisattva, having heard this said, addressed Vajragarbha Bodhisattva, saying: "Child of the Buddha! This assembly has now gathered here in full — well purified in the profound mind, well cleansed in their thinking, well cultivated in every practice, well gathered in the aids to the path, well able to attend upon a hundred thousand million buddhas, accomplished in measureless wholesome roots of merit. They have cast off delusion and confusion; they are without stain or defilement; their faith and understanding reach the profound mind; and within the Buddha's Dharma they do not follow the teaching of another. Excellent, child of the Buddha! Sustained by the Buddha's own spiritual power, you should now expound it — for these bodhisattvas are every one of them able to realize and know a place as profound as this."

Chinese Source

爾時，解脫月菩薩欲重宣其義而說頌曰：
「願說最安隱，菩薩無上行，分別於諸地，智淨成正覺。
此眾無諸垢，志解悉明潔，承事無量佛，能知此地義。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then Vimuktacandra Bodhisattva, wishing to state this meaning again, spoke in verse, saying:
"We beg you to speak of that most secure and peaceful thing, the unsurpassed practice of a bodhisattva — discriminate it ground by ground, whereby wisdom is made pure and perfect awakening accomplished. This assembly is without any stain at all; their resolve and understanding are wholly bright and clean; they have served and attended upon measureless buddhas, and they are able to know the meaning of these grounds."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

"Within the Buddha's Dharma they do not follow the teaching of another" (於佛法中不隨他教) is the decisive credential in this list. It does not mean these bodhisattvas are unteachable. It means their conviction is no longer borrowed — they have verified the Dharma in their own experience rather than holding it on report. That is precisely the qualification Vajragarbha's objection requires, and Vimuktacandra places it last, where the weight falls.

Not This Assembly, But Others

Vajragarbha's reply concedes everything Vimuktacandra has claimed. Yes, this assembly is ready. His silence was never a judgment on the listeners in front of him — it was concern for those of lesser understanding who would encounter the teaching and be injured by their own reaction to it. Doubt about something this profound is not a harmless mistake; it carries consequences that outlast the moment.

Chinese Source

爾時，金剛藏菩薩言：「佛子！雖此眾集善淨思念，捨離愚癡及以疑惑，於甚深法不隨他教；然有其餘劣解眾生，聞此甚深難思議事，多生疑惑，於長夜中受諸衰惱。我愍此等，是故默然。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then Vajragarbha Bodhisattva said: "Child of the Buddha! Though this gathered assembly is well purified in its thinking, having cast off both folly and doubt, and though within the most profound Dharma they do not follow the teaching of another — still there are other beings, of inferior understanding, who upon hearing a matter so profound and so hard to conceive would give rise to much doubt and confusion, and would through the long night undergo every manner of decline and affliction. It is out of pity for such as these that I have remained silent."

Chinese Source

爾時，金剛藏菩薩欲重宣其義而說頌曰：
「雖此眾淨廣智慧，甚深明利能決擇，其心不動如山王，不可傾覆猶大海。
有行未久解未得，隨識而行不隨智，聞此生疑墮惡道，我愍是等故不說。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then Vajragarbha Bodhisattva, wishing to state this meaning again, spoke in verse, saying:
"Though this assembly is pure and of vast wisdom, most profound, bright and keen, able to discern and decide; though their minds are unmoving as the king of mountains, and cannot be overturned, being like the great ocean — yet there are those whose practice is not yet long and whose understanding is not yet won, who move along with consciousness and do not move along with wisdom. Hearing this, they would give rise to doubt and fall into the evil destinies; it is out of pity for such as these that I do not speak."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

"Who move along with consciousness and do not move along with wisdom" (隨識而行不隨智) names the exact fault, and it is not stupidity. Consciousness here is the discriminating mind that sorts what it meets into the familiar categories it already owns. A teaching that exceeds those categories will be judged false by that faculty, confidently and immediately. Wisdom is the capacity to sit with what has not yet been assimilated. Vajragarbha is not protecting a secret; he is declining to hand someone an instrument they will use against themselves.

As the Alphabet Is the Root of Writing

Vimuktacandra asks a third time, and this time he changes the ground of the argument. His earlier appeals were about the audience's readiness; this one is about the teaching's own necessity. The ten grounds are not one topic among many that might be deferred until conditions improve. They are the first thing a bodhisattva does and the root from which every buddha-quality grows — and so he reaches for the plainest possible image, the one every child learns first.

Chinese Source

爾時，解脫月菩薩重白金剛藏菩薩言：「佛子！願承佛神力分別說此不思議法，此人當得如來護念而生信受。何以故？說十地時，一切菩薩法應如是，得佛護念。得護念故，於此智地能生勇猛。何以故？此是菩薩最初所行，成就一切諸佛法故。譬如書字、數說，一切皆以字母為本、字母究竟，無有少分離字母者。佛子！一切佛法皆以十地為本，十地究竟修行成就，得一切智。是故，佛子！願為演說！此人必為如來所護，令其信受。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then Vimuktacandra Bodhisattva addressed Vajragarbha Bodhisattva once again, saying: "Child of the Buddha! I beg you, sustained by the Buddha's own spiritual power, to discriminate and speak this inconceivable Dharma — for such people will be held in mind and guarded by the Tathāgata, and will give rise to belief and acceptance. And why is this? Because when the ten grounds are being taught, it is the very nature of the Dharma of all bodhisattvas that it should be so, and they receive the Buddha's holding them in mind. And because they are so held in mind, within this ground of wisdom they are able to give rise to courage. And why is this? Because this is the very first thing a bodhisattva practices, and it accomplishes every buddha-dharma. It is like the writing of letters and the reckoning of numbers — all of it takes the letters of the alphabet as its root and the letters of the alphabet as its completion, and there is not the least part of it that stands apart from the letters of the alphabet. Child of the Buddha! Every buddha-dharma takes the ten grounds as its root; when the ten grounds are cultivated to completion and accomplished, all-knowing wisdom is attained. Therefore, child of the Buddha, I beg you to expound it! Such people will surely be guarded by the Tathāgata, and be brought to believe and accept it."

Chinese Source

爾時，解脫月菩薩欲重宣其義而說頌曰：
「善哉佛子願演說，趣入菩提諸地行！十方一切自在尊，莫不護念智根本。
此安住智亦究竟，一切佛法所從生，譬如書數字母攝，如是佛法依於地。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then Vimuktacandra Bodhisattva, wishing to state this meaning again, spoke in verse, saying:
"Excellent, child of the Buddha — we beg you to expound the practice of the grounds by which one enters upon awakening! Among all the sovereign lords of the ten directions there is not one who does not hold in mind this root of wisdom. To abide in this wisdom is itself its completion, and from it every buddha-dharma is born. Just as writing and reckoning are subsumed within the letters of the alphabet, so do the buddha-dharmas rest upon the grounds."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The simile is more exact than it first appears. The letters of the alphabet are not a preliminary that literacy leaves behind — every word ever written, including the most sophisticated, is still nothing but those letters arranged. So the text says the alphabet is both root and completion (為本、字母究竟). The ten grounds are not a beginner's syllabus superseded by higher teaching later; they are what buddhahood is made of, all the way up. This is why Vimuktacandra can say the teaching cannot reasonably be withheld — deferring it does not delay one topic, it withholds the alphabet from someone who wants to read.

Note also the shift in what is being asked. Vimuktacandra no longer argues that the listeners are strong enough. He argues that they will be held in mind and guarded by the Tathāgata (得如來護念) — that the protection Vajragarbha is trying to provide by silence is already provided, from a source neither of them has to supply.

Part 401 Complete

This continues the opening of the Ten Grounds chapter. Chapter 26 continues in Part 402 as the whole assembly of great bodhisattvas, with one voice together, joins Vimuktacandra's request in verse — praising Vajragarbha's undefiled wisdom and eloquence, declaring themselves free of doubt, and likening their longing for this Dharma to the thirsty longing for cool water and the hungry for fine food. The Buddha then answers them with light.
