Perfumed in a Single Day

The paired similes turn to fragrance and trees: black sandalwood whose single zhu perfumes a whole small thousand-world system, white sandalwood that cools every feverish affliction, Mount Sumeru's own color, the pārijāta tree in three successive images, and a coconut tree on an island that never stops giving.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1299 — fascicle 78 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Perfumed in a Single Day

Seven more paired similes continue the pattern — two sandalwoods, the color of Mount Sumeru, three successive images drawn from the pārijāta tree, and a coconut tree whose every part is used by beings without pause.

Chinese Source

「善男子！譬如天上黑栴檀香，若燒一銖，其香普熏小千世界，三千世界滿中珍寶所有價直皆不能及。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心香亦復如是，一念功德普熏法界，聲聞、緣覺一切功德皆所不及。「善男子！如白栴檀，若以塗身，悉能除滅一切熱惱，令其身心普得清涼；菩薩摩訶薩菩提心香亦復如是，能除一切虛妄、分別、貪、恚、癡等諸惑熱惱，令其具足智慧清涼。「善男子！如須彌山，若有近者，即同其色。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心山亦復如是，若有近者，悉得同其一切智色。「善男子！譬如波利質多羅樹，其皮香氣，閻浮提中若婆師迦、若薝蔔迦、若蘇摩那，如是等華所有香氣皆不能及。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心樹亦復如是，所發大願功德之香，一切二乘無漏戒定、智慧解脫、解脫知見諸功德香悉不能及。「善男子！譬如波利質多羅樹，雖未開華，應知即是無量諸華出生之處。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心樹亦復如是，雖未開發一切智華，應知即是無數天人眾菩提華所生之處。「善男子！譬如波利質多羅華，一日熏衣，薝蔔迦華、婆利師華、蘇摩那華雖千歲熏亦不能及。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心華亦復如是，一生所熏諸功德香，普徹十方一切佛所，一切二乘無漏功德百千劫熏所不能及。「善男子！如海島中生椰子樹，根、莖、枝、葉及以華果，一切眾生恒取受用無時暫歇。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心樹亦復如是，始從發起悲願之心，乃至成佛，正法住世，常時利益一切世間無有間歇。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Just as, with the black sandalwood incense found in the heavens, if one burns but a single zhu of it, its fragrance universally perfumes a small thousand-world system, and the worth of all the precious treasures that fill a trichiliocosm cannot equal it, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own incense that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: the merit of a single thought universally perfumes the dharma-realm, and every merit of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas is unable to equal it. Good son! Just as white sandalwood, when it is rubbed upon the body, is able to remove and extinguish every feverish affliction and to bring cool refreshment to body and mind alike, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own incense that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: it is able to remove the feverish affliction of falsity, discrimination, greed, hatred, delusion, and every such deluding passion, and to bring them the full coolness of wisdom. Good son! Just as, with Mount Sumeru, whoever draws near to it at once takes on its color, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own mountain that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: whoever draws near to it entirely attains the same color of all-knowing wisdom. Good son! Just as, with the pārijāta tree, the fragrance of its bark is such that no fragrance possessed by the vārṣika, the campaka, the sumanā, or any flower like these in Jambudvīpa can equal it, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own tree that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: the fragrance of merit from the great vows it gives rise to cannot be equaled by any fragrance of merit of the two vehicles, whether of their moral discipline and concentration without outflows, of their wisdom and liberation, or of their knowledge and vision of liberation. Good son! Just as, with the pārijāta tree, even while it has not yet opened its flowers, one should know that it is the very place from which measureless flowers are born, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own tree that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: even while it has not yet opened the flower of all-knowing wisdom, one should know that it is the very place from which the flowers of bodhi of countless assemblies of devas and humans are born. Good son! Just as a pārijāta flower perfumes a garment in a single day so thoroughly that campaka flowers, vārṣika flowers, and sumanā flowers, even perfuming it for a thousand years, are unable to equal it, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own flower that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: the fragrance of every merit it perfumes within a single lifetime pervades universally to the abode of every buddha in the ten directions, and the merit without outflows of the two vehicles, even perfuming for a hundred thousand kalpas, is unable to equal it. Good son! Just as, with a coconut tree that grows on an island in the sea, its roots, trunk, branches, and leaves together with its flowers and fruit are constantly taken up and made use of by every sentient being, never pausing even for a moment, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own tree that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: from the moment it first gives rise to the mind of compassionate vow, up through the attainment of buddhahood and the abiding of the true dharma in the world, it constantly benefits every world at all times without any interruption.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

若燒一銖 — 銖 (zhu) is a very small unit of weight in the Chinese system, so the point of the first simile is that even the smallest measurable quantity of this incense, once burned, outreaches a whole small thousand-world system. The word is kept as zhu rather than converted to a modern measure.

波利質多羅樹 — the pārijāta tree, the flowering tree of the heavens, is used for three successive similes in this part: the fragrance of its bark, its power to produce flowers before it has even bloomed, and the single day in which one of its flowers perfumes a garment. The three flower names it is measured against are transliterations: 婆師迦 vārṣika, 薝蔔迦 campaka, and 蘇摩那 sumanā. The first of these appears under two spellings within this same passage, 婆師迦 in the fourth simile and 婆利師 in the sixth; both are treated as the same flower and rendered vārṣika.
