A Prince from the Moment of Birth

The paired similes turn to what comes first and what cannot be matched: clean water taken before any medicine is mixed, the life-faculty protected before the body, a life cut off that can benefit no one, an ocean nothing can destroy, a sun no star can outshine, and three images of a prince who is honored by every great minister from the moment of his birth, before he holds any power at all.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1304 — fascicle 78 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Prince from the Moment of Birth

Eight more paired similes continue the pattern — three on what must come first, two on what cannot be destroyed or outshone, and three successive images of a newborn prince.

Chinese Source

「善男子！譬如有人，調和藥汁，必當先取好清淨水。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，欲修菩薩一切行願，先當發起菩提之心。「善男子！如人護身，先護命根。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，護持佛法，亦當先護菩提之心。「善男子！譬如有人，命根若斷，不能利益父母、宗親。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，捨菩提心，不能利益一切眾生，不能成就諸佛功德。「善男子！譬如大海，無能壞者。菩提心海亦復如是，諸業煩惱、二乘之心所不能壞。「善男子！譬如日光，星宿光明不能映蔽。菩提心日亦復如是，一切二乘無漏智光所不能蔽。「善男子！如王子初生，即為大臣之所尊重，以種性自在故。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，於佛法中發菩提心，即為耆宿久修梵行聲聞、緣覺所共尊重，以大悲自在故。「善男子！譬如王子，年雖幼稚，一切大臣皆悉敬禮。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，雖初發心修菩薩行，二乘耆舊皆應敬禮。「善男子！譬如王子，雖於一切臣佐之中未得自在，已具王相，不與一切諸臣佐等，以生處尊勝故。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，雖於一切業煩惱中未得自在，然已具足菩提之相，不與一切二乘齊等，以種性第一故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Just as a person who blends a medicinal juice must first take good, clean water, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: wishing to cultivate every practice and vow of a bodhisattva, they must first give rise to the mind of bodhi. Good son! Just as a person protecting their body first protects the life-faculty, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: protecting and upholding the buddha-dharma, they must likewise first protect the mind of bodhi. Good son! Just as a person whose life-faculty is cut off is unable to benefit their parents and relatives, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: abandoning the mind of bodhi, they are unable to benefit any sentient being and unable to accomplish the merits of the buddhas. Good son! Just as the great ocean is something nothing can destroy, the ocean of the mind of bodhi is likewise: no karma or affliction and no mind of the two vehicles is able to destroy it. Good son! Just as the light of the sun cannot be outshone by the light of the stars and constellations, the sun of the mind of bodhi is likewise: it cannot be outshone by the light of the wisdom without outflows of any follower of the two vehicles. Good son! Just as a prince, from the moment of his birth, is honored by the great ministers because of the sovereign freedom of his lineage, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: arousing the mind of bodhi within the buddha-dharma, they are at once honored together by the elders among the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas who have long cultivated pure conduct, because of the sovereign freedom of their great compassion. Good son! Just as a prince, though still young in years, is reverenced by every one of the great ministers, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: though they have only just aroused the mind and begun to cultivate the bodhisattva's practices, the elders of the two vehicles should all reverence them. Good son! Just as a prince, though he has not yet attained sovereign freedom over all the ministers and aides, already possesses the marks of a king and is not the equal of any minister or aide, because of the supremacy of his place of birth, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: though they have not yet attained sovereign freedom over all karma and afflictions, they already fully possess the marks of bodhi and are not the equal of any follower of the two vehicles, because their lineage is foremost.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

王子 — the prince supplies the last three similes of this part in succession, and each grounds the honor he receives in something other than power he has exercised: the sixth in 種性, his lineage; the seventh in nothing but his birth, since he is still a child; and the eighth in 生處, his place of birth, which gives him the marks of a king before he has any authority over the ministers. The bodhisattva's side of the eighth simile closes on 種性第一, the lineage that is foremost, so that the whole set turns on where one is born rather than what one has yet done.

命根 — the life-faculty appears in two successive similes, the second and third, first as what a person protects before anything else and then as what, once cut off, leaves a person unable to benefit even their own parents and relatives. The two are rendered with the same term so that the pairing of protection and loss stays visible in English.
