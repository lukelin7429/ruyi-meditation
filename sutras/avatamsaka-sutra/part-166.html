What the World Cannot Answer

The fifth treasury turns from inward reflection to a catalog of learning itself — the formula of dependent origination, the worldly and transcendent dharmas, the conditioned and unconditioned, the thirty-seven factors of awakening — before closing on the classic set of questions the Buddha himself left undetermined, from whether the world has an end to how many living beings there are.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 166 — the treasury of learning Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Dependent Origination, and Every Category of Dharma

Unlike the treasuries before it, learning is defined not by a motive or a memory but by an inventory: the formula "because this exists, that exists," the five aggregates as worldly dharmas, the path to liberation as transcendent dharmas, and the full sweep of what is conditioned, unconditioned, and categorically answerable — everything, in short, that a bodhisattva must come to know in order to teach it truly.

Chinese Source

「佛子！何等為菩薩摩訶薩聞藏？此菩薩知是事有故是事有，是事無故是事無；是事起故是事起，是事滅故是事滅；是世間法，是出世間法；是有為法，是無為法；是有記法，是無記法。何等為是事有故是事有？謂：無明有故行有。何等為是事無故是事無？謂：識無故名色無。何等為是事起故是事起？謂：愛起故苦起。何等為是事滅故是事滅？謂：有滅故生滅。何等為世間法？所謂：色、受、想、行、識。何等為出世間法？所謂：戒、定、慧、解脫、解脫知見。何等為有為法？所謂：欲界、色界、無色界、眾生界。何等為無為法？所謂：虛空、涅槃、數緣滅、非數緣滅、緣起法性住。何等為有記法？謂：四聖諦、四沙門果、四辯、四無所畏、四念處、四正勤、四神足、五根、五力、七覺分、八聖道分。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Son of the Buddha! What is the bodhisattva-mahāsattva's treasury of learning? This bodhisattva knows: because this thing exists, that thing exists; because this thing does not exist, that thing does not exist; because this thing arises, that thing arises; because this thing ceases, that thing ceases; this is a worldly dharma, this is a transcendent dharma; this is a conditioned dharma, this is an unconditioned dharma; this is a dharma to be answered categorically, this is a dharma to be left undetermined. What is meant by 'because this thing exists, that thing exists'? It means: because ignorance exists, volitional formations exist. What is meant by 'because this thing does not exist, that thing does not exist'? It means: because consciousness does not exist, name-and-form does not exist. What is meant by 'because this thing arises, that thing arises'? It means: because craving arises, suffering arises. What is meant by 'because this thing ceases, that thing ceases'? It means: because becoming ceases, birth ceases. What is meant by worldly dharmas? Namely: form, feeling, perception, volitional formations, and consciousness. What is meant by transcendent dharmas? Namely: moral discipline, concentration, wisdom, liberation, and the knowledge and vision of liberation. What is meant by conditioned dharmas? Namely: the realm of desire, the realm of form, the formless realm, and the realm of living beings. What is meant by unconditioned dharmas? Namely: empty space, nirvāṇa, cessation through discernment, cessation not through discernment, and the abiding nature of dependently originated dharmas. What is meant by dharmas to be answered categorically? Namely: the four noble truths, the four fruits of the śramaṇa, the four unhindered eloquences, the four fearlessnesses, the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right efforts, the four bases of spiritual power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of awakening, and the eightfold noble path.

Chinese Source

何等為無記法？謂：世間有邊，世間無邊，世間亦有邊亦無邊，世間非有邊非無邊；世間有常，世間無常，世間亦有常亦無常，世間非有常非無常；如來滅後有，如來滅後無，如來滅後亦有亦無，如來滅後非有非無；我及眾生有，我及眾生無，我及眾生亦有亦無，我及眾生非有非無；過去，有幾如來般涅槃？幾聲聞、辟支佛般涅槃？未來，有幾如來？幾聲聞、辟支佛？幾眾生？現在，有幾佛住？幾聲聞、辟支佛住？幾眾生住？何等如來最先出？何等聲聞、辟支佛最先出？何等眾生最先出？何等如來最後出？何等聲聞、辟支佛最後出？何等眾生最後出？何法最在初？何法最在後？世間從何處來，去至何所？有幾世界成？有幾世界壞？世界從何處來，去至何所？何者為生死最初際？何者為生死最後際？是名無記法。菩薩摩訶薩作如是念：『一切眾生於生死中，無有多聞，不能了知此一切法。我當發意，持多聞藏，證阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，為諸眾生說真實法。』是名：菩薩摩訶薩第五多聞藏。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is meant by dharmas to be left undetermined? Whether the world has an end, whether the world has no end, whether the world both has an end and has no end, whether the world neither has an end nor has no end; whether the world is eternal, whether the world is not eternal, whether the world is both eternal and not eternal, whether the world is neither eternal nor not eternal; whether the Tathāgata exists after extinction, whether the Tathāgata does not exist after extinction, whether the Tathāgata both exists and does not exist after extinction, whether the Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist after extinction; whether the self and living beings exist, whether the self and living beings do not exist, whether the self and living beings both exist and do not exist, whether the self and living beings neither exist nor do not exist; in the past, how many Tathāgatas have entered parinirvāṇa? How many śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas have entered parinirvāṇa? In the future, how many Tathāgatas will there be? How many śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas? How many living beings? In the present, how many buddhas now abide? How many śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas now abide? How many living beings now abide? Which Tathāgata appeared first of all? Which śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha appeared first of all? Which living being appeared first of all? Which Tathāgata will appear last of all? Which śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha will appear last of all? Which living being will appear last of all? Which dharma comes first of all? Which dharma comes last of all? From where does the world come, and to where does it go? How many worlds have come into being? How many worlds have been destroyed? From where does the world come, and to where does it go? What is the very first limit of birth-and-death? What is the very final limit of birth-and-death? These are called the dharmas to be left undetermined. The bodhisattva-mahāsattva reflects in this way: "Every living being, within birth-and-death, lacks extensive learning, and is unable to understand all of these dharmas. I shall resolve to uphold the treasury of extensive learning, realize unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, and expound the true and real Dharma for the sake of living beings." This is called the bodhisattva-mahāsattva's fifth treasury: the treasury of learning.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The "dharmas to be left undetermined" here are the classic set the Buddha himself famously declined to answer directly — whether the world has an end, whether it is eternal, whether the Tathāgata exists after death — questions that assume categories too coarse for the reality they try to describe. What is notable is that the treasury of learning does not omit them as unworthy of study; it catalogs them alongside the four noble truths and the factors of awakening as things a bodhisattva must know exist as a category, precisely so as to recognize why they resist a yes-or-no answer rather than mistaking that resistance for the Buddha's own ignorance.
