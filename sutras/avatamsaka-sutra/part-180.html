Praised by a Hundred Million Dharmas of Praise

Eleven kinds of bodhisattva, each named for the hand that offers — Flower-Hand, Fragrance-Hand, Garment-Hand, Jewel-Hand, and more — rain their own offering down in turn. Heavenly sons hold the throne aloft with their own bodies, and a final list of bodhisattva qualities closes the entire description with a single summary formula naming what the throne was made from.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 180 — hand-bodhisattvas, heavenly sons, and the throne's closing summary Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Eleven Hands, Each Its Own Offering

The passage closes its long description of the throne and the assembly around it with two final images — bodhisattvas named for the very hand that offers, each raining down exactly what that hand is named for, and heavenly sons literally holding the throne aloft with their own bodies — before a ten-item summary formula gathers everything said across seven Parts into a single sentence.

Chinese Source

百萬億華手菩薩，雨一切華；百萬億香手菩薩，雨一切香；百萬億鬘手菩薩，雨一切鬘；百萬億末香手菩薩，雨一切末香；百萬億塗香手菩薩，雨一切塗香；百萬億衣手菩薩，雨一切衣；百萬億蓋手菩薩，雨一切蓋；百萬億幢手菩薩，雨一切幢；百萬億幡手菩薩，雨一切幡；百萬億寶手菩薩，雨一切寶；百萬億莊嚴手菩薩，雨一切莊嚴具。百萬億諸天子，從天宮出，至於座所；百萬億諸天子，以淨信心，并宮殿俱；百萬億生貴天子，以身持座；百萬億灌頂天子，舉身持座。

Ru-Yi Translation

A hundred million Flower-Hand Bodhisattvas, raining down every kind of flower; a hundred million Fragrance-Hand Bodhisattvas, raining down every kind of fragrance; a hundred million Garland-Hand Bodhisattvas, raining down every kind of garland; a hundred million Powdered-Incense-Hand Bodhisattvas, raining down every kind of powdered incense; a hundred million Anointing-Incense-Hand Bodhisattvas, raining down every kind of anointing incense; a hundred million Garment-Hand Bodhisattvas, raining down every kind of garment; a hundred million Canopy-Hand Bodhisattvas, raining down every kind of canopy; a hundred million Banner-Pole-Hand Bodhisattvas, raining down every kind of banner-pole; a hundred million Banner-Hand Bodhisattvas, raining down every kind of banner; a hundred million Jewel-Hand Bodhisattvas, raining down every kind of jewel; and a hundred million Adornment-Hand Bodhisattvas, raining down every kind of adorning implement. A hundred million heavenly sons, coming forth from their own heavenly palaces, arriving at the very place of the throne; a hundred million heavenly sons, with a pure and faithful mind, coming together with their own palaces; a hundred million heavenly sons of noble birth, holding up the throne with their own bodies; and a hundred million consecrated heavenly sons, raising their whole bodies to hold up the throne.

Chinese Source

百萬億思惟菩薩，恭敬思惟；百萬億生貴菩薩，發清淨心；百萬億菩薩，諸根悅樂；百萬億菩薩，深心清淨；百萬億菩薩，信解清淨；百萬億菩薩，諸業清淨；百萬億菩薩，受生自在；百萬億菩薩，法光照耀；百萬億菩薩，成就於地；百萬億菩薩，善能教化一切眾生。百萬億善根所生，百萬億諸佛護持，百萬億福德所圓滿，百萬億殊勝心所清淨，百萬億大願所嚴潔，百萬億善行所生起，百萬億善法所堅固，百萬億神力所示現，百萬億功德所成就，百萬億讚歎法而以讚歎。

Ru-Yi Translation

A hundred million contemplating bodhisattvas, reverently reflecting; a hundred million bodhisattvas of noble birth, giving rise to a pure mind; a hundred million bodhisattvas, every faculty delighted and at ease; a hundred million bodhisattvas, their deep mind entirely pure; a hundred million bodhisattvas, their faith and understanding entirely pure; a hundred million bodhisattvas, every one of their karmic actions entirely pure; a hundred million bodhisattvas, sovereign and free in taking birth; a hundred million bodhisattvas, the light of the Dharma shining forth from them; a hundred million bodhisattvas, accomplished upon their own ground; and a hundred million bodhisattvas, skillfully able to teach and transform every living being. Born from a hundred million wholesome roots, upheld and protected by a hundred million buddhas, brought to full perfection by a hundred million kinds of merit, made pure by a hundred million supreme minds, adorned and purified by a hundred million great vows, arising from a hundred million wholesome practices, made firm by a hundred million wholesome dharmas, manifested by a hundred million spiritual powers, and accomplished through a hundred million merits — it was with a hundred million dharmas of praise that it was praised.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The final sentence functions as the closing bracket to everything described since the throne first appeared seven Parts ago: ten separate causes — wholesome roots, the buddhas' own protection, merit, pure mind, great vows, wholesome practice, wholesome dharma, spiritual power, and accomplished merit — each stated as the source of a hundred million somethings, before the very last clause admits that even praise itself was offered a hundred million times over. Naming the heavenly sons who "hold up the throne with their own bodies" is a small, physical detail amid all this abstraction — a reminder that despite the scale, this is still a throne someone has to carry.
