The Question List Closes, the Ten Bases Begin

The question list turns, in its final stretch, to the traditional stages of a buddha's own life — descending from Tuṣita, entering the womb, the first smile, the seven steps, the great departure, the site of awakening, subduing māra, turning the dharma-wheel, and parinirvāṇa — before Samantabhadra finally answers, opening with the ten things a bodhisattva-mahāsattva relies upon.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 686 — the question list closes, the ten bases begin Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

From a Buddha's Whole Life to the First Ten Answers

The question list's closing stretch retells the traditional stages of a buddha's biography as a sequence of questions rather than a narrative — birth, smile, seven steps, renunciation, awakening, the turning of the wheel, and final nirvāṇa — before Samantabhadra begins answering at last, opening not with a single response but with the first of what becomes a long series of ten-fold lists: the ten things a bodhisattva relies upon.

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Samantabhadra Bodhisattva spoke to Universal Wisdom and the other bodhisattvas: "Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva-mahāsattva has ten kinds of basis. What are these ten? Namely: taking the mind of awakening as their basis, because they never forget it; taking a good spiritual friend as their basis, because they are harmonized with them as one; taking roots of goodness as their basis, because they cultivate and increase them; taking the pāramitās as their basis, because they practice them to completion; taking every dharma as their basis, because they ultimately transcend through them; taking their great vow as their basis, because it increases their awakening; taking every practice as their basis, because they accomplish every one of them completely; taking every bodhisattva as their basis, because they share one and the same wisdom; taking making offerings to every buddha as their basis, because their faith is thereby made pure; and taking every tathāgata as their basis, because his teaching, like a loving father's, never ceases. These are the ten. If bodhisattvas abide in this dharma, they become a basis for the tathāgata's own unsurpassed great wisdom."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

以一切如來為依，如慈父教誨不斷故 — taking every tathāgata as their basis, because his teaching, like a loving father's, never ceases. The list of ten bases climbs from an inward resolve (the mind of awakening) through relationships and practices to end on this image of unbroken paternal instruction — a notably warm, domestic comparison for a text that has just spent an enormous list cataloguing cosmic-scale abstractions, and a reminder that the chapter's method is to answer even its most sweeping questions with something recognizably human.
