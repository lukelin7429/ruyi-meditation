Every Heaven, Each in Its Own Posture

The eight classes of guardian kings — deva, nāga, yakṣa, gandharva, asura, garuḍa, kiṃnara, and mahoraga — each respond in one distinctive gesture: gazing without blinking, cutting off pride, holding a ribbon in the beak. Then, heaven by heaven, from Trāyastriṃśa up through the Brahmā heavens to Akaniṣṭha at the very summit, every realm of the traditional cosmology bows toward the throne in its own particular way.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 179 — guardian kings and every heaven respond Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

One Gesture per Class, One Posture per Heaven

Where the previous Part gave every stage of the bodhisattva path a single defining response, this one does the same for the assembled hosts of the cosmos: each of the eight guardian classes gets one physical gesture entirely its own, and then every heaven, ascending from the realm of desire through every tier of the realm of form, bows, prostrates, or gazes in its own particular manner.

百萬億諸天王，恭敬禮拜；百萬億龍王，諦觀無厭；百萬億夜叉王，頂上合掌；百萬億乾闥婆王，起淨信心；百萬億阿脩羅王，斷憍慢意；百萬億迦樓羅王，口銜繒帶；百萬億緊那羅王，歡喜踊躍；百萬億摩睺羅伽王，歡喜瞻仰；百萬億世主，稽首作禮；百萬億忉利天王，瞻仰不瞬；百萬億夜摩天王，歡喜讚歎；百萬億兜率天王，布身作禮；百萬億化樂天王，頭頂禮敬；百萬億他化天王，恭敬合掌；百萬億梵天王，一心觀察；百萬億摩醯首羅天王，恭敬供養；

Ru-Yi Translation

A hundred million heavenly kings, reverently paying homage; a hundred million nāga kings, gazing intently without ever growing weary; a hundred million yakṣa kings, joining their palms above their own heads; a hundred million gandharva kings, giving rise to a pure and faithful mind; a hundred million asura kings, cutting off every thought of arrogance and pride; a hundred million garuḍa kings, holding silk ribbons in their beaks; a hundred million kiṃnara kings, leaping for joy; and a hundred million mahoraga kings, gazing up in joyful reverence. A hundred million lords of the world, bowing their heads in homage; a hundred million kings of the Trāyastriṃśa Heaven, gazing upward without blinking; a hundred million kings of the Yāma Heaven, joyfully praising and extolling; a hundred million kings of the Tuṣita Heaven, prostrating their whole body in homage; a hundred million kings of the Nirmāṇarati Heaven, bowing their heads in reverent homage; a hundred million kings of the Paranirmitavaśavartin Heaven, reverently joining their palms; a hundred million kings of the Brahmā Heaven, single-mindedly observing; and a hundred million kings of the Maheśvara Heaven, reverently making offerings.

Ru-Yi Translation

A hundred million bodhisattvas, raising their voices in praise; a hundred million heavenly maidens, single-mindedly making offerings; a hundred million gods of shared vows, leaping for joy; a hundred million gods who once dwelled together in ages past, extolling with wondrous voices; a hundred million gods of Brahmā's own body, prostrating their whole body in reverent homage; a hundred million gods of Brahmā's ministers, joining their palms above their heads; a hundred million gods of Brahmā's retinue, encircling and attending as guards; a hundred million gods of the Great Brahmā Heaven, extolling and proclaiming immeasurable merit; a hundred million gods of the Heaven of Light, prostrating all five limbs to the ground; a hundred million gods of the Heaven of Lesser Light, proclaiming and praising how rare it is for a buddha to appear in the world; a hundred million gods of the Heaven of Immeasurable Light, bowing toward the Buddha from afar; a hundred million gods of the Ābhāsvara Heaven, extolling how utterly rare it is to behold the Tathāgata; a hundred million gods of the Pure Heaven, coming here together with their own palaces; a hundred million gods of the Heaven of Lesser Purity, with a pure mind, bowing their heads in homage; a hundred million gods of the Heaven of Immeasurable Purity, wishing to behold the Buddha, casting themselves down; a hundred million gods of the Śubhakṛtsna Heaven, reverently honoring him, drawing near to make offerings; a hundred million gods of the Vast Heaven, recollecting their own wholesome roots from ages past; a hundred million gods of the Heaven of Lesser Vastness, giving rise, before the Tathāgata, to a sense of how rare this is; a hundred million gods of the Heaven of Immeasurable Vastness, resolutely honoring him, giving rise to every wholesome deed; a hundred million gods of the Bṛhatphala Heaven, bowing their bodies in reverence; a hundred million gods of the Heaven Without Affliction, their faculty of faith firm and solid, reverently paying homage; a hundred million gods of the Heaven Without Heat, joining their palms and holding the Buddha in mind, their feeling never satisfied or weary; a hundred million gods of the Heaven of Skillful Vision, bowing their head and face in homage; a hundred million gods of the Heaven of Skillful Appearance, intent on making offerings to the Buddha, their mind never slackening or resting; a hundred million gods of the Akaniṣṭha Heaven, reverently bowing their heads in homage; a hundred million gods of every kind, all greatly rejoicing, raising their voices in praise; a hundred million gods of every kind, each skillfully reflecting, adorning the scene; and a hundred million bodhisattva-gods, guarding and upholding the Buddha's own throne, its adornment never ceasing.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The physical detail given to each response is what keeps twenty-eight heavens and eight guardian classes from blurring into a single undifferentiated crowd: a garuḍa king holds a silk ribbon in its beak, gods of the Heaven of Immeasurable Purity cast themselves bodily downward wishing to see the Buddha, and gods of Brahmā's retinue simply encircle the throne as guards rather than bowing at all. The heavens climb in the same order the cosmology always gives them, ending at Akaniṣṭha, "the ultimate realm of form" — the ceiling of the form realm itself, beyond which the sūtra does not need to go, since even here the response is still bowing, homage, and reverence rather than anything more exalted.
