The Space Between Two Night-Spirits

The pilgrimage has stopped travelling — the next teacher is sitting in the same assembly. So the sūtra takes the few paces between them and opens that gap instead, filling it with motes of earth and water and fire, each one holding worlds that form and fall apart.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1101 — fascicle 70 opens Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Vow Made Standing Still

Fascicle seventy opens not with a road but with a vow. Good-Wealth has a few paces to walk, and he uses them to promise that he will never again be far from a teacher.

Chinese Source

爾時，善財童子於喜目觀察眾生夜神所，聞普喜幢解脫門，信解趣入，了知隨順，思惟修習，念善知識所有教誨，心無暫捨，諸根不散，一心願得見善知識，普於十方勤求匪懈，

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Good-Wealth, at the place of the night-spirit Joyful Eye Observing Beings, had heard the liberation-gate of the Banner of Universal Joy. He believed and understood it and set out into it; he comprehended it and accorded with it; he pondered it and cultivated it. He kept in mind every instruction his good spiritual friends had given him, his mind not laying them aside for an instant, his faculties not scattering; with a single mind he vowed to see his good spiritual friends, seeking them diligently throughout the ten directions and never slackening;

Chinese Source

願常親近生諸功德，與善知識同一善根，得善知識巧方便行，依善知識入精進海，於無量劫常不遠離。作是願已，往詣普救眾生妙德夜神所。

Ru-Yi Translation

vowing always to draw near to them and so bring forth every merit, to share one and the same root of goodness with his good spiritual friends, to attain the skillful and expedient practice of his good spiritual friends, to enter the ocean of vigor in reliance upon his good spiritual friends, and through measureless eons never to be far from them. Having made this vow, he went to where the night-spirit Wondrous Virtue Who Universally Rescues Beings was.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The phrase 善知識, good spiritual friend, occurs five times in these two rows, and each occurrence carries a different preposition: 念善知識所有教誨 — keep their instruction in mind; 願得見善知識 — vow to see them; 與善知識同一善根 — share one root of goodness with them; 得善知識巧方便行 — attain their skillful practice; 依善知識入精進海 — enter the ocean of vigor in reliance on them. The vow works through the whole grammar of a relationship: remembering, meeting, sharing, receiving, depending.

於無量劫常不遠離 — through measureless eons never to be far from them. Good-Wealth is at this moment perhaps a few paces from his next teacher, who is in the same assembly as the one he has just left. He is making a vow about measureless eons at a distance of a few steps, which is the sort of disproportion this chapter has stopped apologising for.

A Light Named the Lamp of Wisdom

Where Joyful Eye began by having Good-Wealth recall past lives, this teacher begins with a light from between her brows — and the light does not stop at showing him something. It enters him.

Chinese Source

時，彼夜神為善財童子示現菩薩調伏眾生解脫神力，以諸相好莊嚴其身，於兩眉間放大光明，名：智燈普照清淨幢，無量光明以為眷屬，其光普照一切世間。照世間已，入善財頂，充滿其身。善財爾時即得究竟清淨輪三昧。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, that night-spirit displayed for Good-Wealth the liberating spiritual power by which a bodhisattva tames beings. Adorning her body with every excellent mark, from between her two brows she sent forth a great light named the Pure Banner of the Lamp of Wisdom Universally Illuminating, attended by measureless lights, and that light shone universally over every world. Having shone over the worlds, it entered the crown of Good-Wealth's head and filled his whole body. At that moment Good-Wealth attained the samādhi of the Ultimate Pure Wheel.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

其光普照一切世間。照世間已，入善財頂 — that light shone universally over every world; having shone over the worlds, it entered the crown of Good-Wealth's head. The sequence matters. The light goes out to the whole world first and only afterwards comes back into one person. It is not a private beam aimed at a pilgrim; it is a universal illumination that happens to end in him, and what he then sees will be everything it touched.

智燈普照清淨幢 — the Pure Banner of the Lamp of Wisdom Universally Illuminating. A lamp, a shining, and a banner in a single name. The lamp is what the light is, the shining is what it does, and the banner is how it is seen from far off — which is the standard Huayan habit of building one name out of substance, function, and sign.

What Is in the Gap

The samādhi's first gift is not a vision of distant buddha-lands. It is a vision of the small volume of air between where one night-spirit stands and where the other does.

Chinese Source

得此三昧已，悉見二神兩處中間，所有一切地塵、水塵及以火塵，金剛摩尼眾寶微塵，華香、瓔珞、諸莊嚴具，如是一切所有微塵，一一塵中各見佛剎微塵數世界成壞。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having attained this samādhi, he saw in full, in the space between the two places where the two spirits stood, every mote of earth, every mote of water, and every mote of fire there was; the fine motes of vajra and of maṇi and of every jewel; the fine motes of flowers and incense, of necklaces and every adornment. Among all such motes as these, within each single mote he saw worlds as numerous as the fine motes of dust in a buddha-land coming into being and falling apart.

Chinese Source

及見一切地、水、火、風諸大積聚。亦見一切世界接連，皆以地輪任持而住。種種山海、種種河池、種種樹林、種種宮殿，所謂：天宮殿、龍宮殿、夜叉宮殿，乃至摩睺羅伽、人、非人等宮殿屋宅，地獄、畜生、閻羅王界一切住處，諸趣輪轉，生死往來，隨業受報，各各差別，靡不悉見。

Ru-Yi Translation

He saw as well the massed accumulations of the great elements — earth, water, fire, and wind. He saw too that every world is joined on to the next, all of them upheld and kept in place by the earth-wheel: every kind of mountain and sea, every kind of river and pool, every kind of grove and forest, every kind of palace — namely, the palaces of gods, the palaces of nāgas, the palaces of yakṣas, and so on to the palaces and dwellings of mahoragas, of humans and of non-humans; the hells, the animals, and every abode in the realm of King Yama; the turning of beings through the destinies, their going and coming in birth and death, and their receiving of recompense according to their deeds, each one distinct from the others — there was none of it he did not see.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

悉見二神兩處中間 — he saw in full the space between the two places where the two spirits stood. This is the sentence the whole passage turns on. In Part 1100 the sūtra pointedly stopped sending Good-Wealth anywhere, naming the next teacher as sitting 此眾會中, in this very assembly. Having removed the journey, it now opens up the only thing left — the gap — and finds inside it every mote of dust there is, each one holding worlds that form and fall apart. The pilgrimage has not shrunk. It has changed direction, from across to within.

地塵、水塵及以火塵 — motes of earth, motes of water, motes of fire. Only three of the four great elements get motes here; wind is missing, and appears one sentence later in 一切地、水、火、風諸大積聚, where the topic is massed accumulations rather than particles. The omission is exact rather than careless — wind is the element that has no grain to it.

The word 塵 appears seven times in the first row, and it is doing two different jobs: it names the actual specks of earth, water, fire, jewel, flower, and ornament lying in the space between the two spirits, and it also supplies the measure — 佛剎微塵數, as numerous as the fine motes of dust in a buddha-land — for the worlds found inside each speck. The unit of counting is also the thing counted.

Ten Kinds of World

The vision ends with a taxonomy. Purity and defilement are graded first, and then, abruptly, the list stops talking about moral quality and starts talking about which way up a world is lying.

Chinese Source

又見一切世界差別。所謂：或有世界雜穢，或有世界清淨，或有世界趣雜穢，或有世界趣清淨，或有世界雜穢清淨，或有世界清淨雜穢，或有世界一向清淨，或有世界其形平正，或有覆住，或有側住。

Ru-Yi Translation

He saw, too, the differences among all worlds. Namely: some worlds are defiled; some worlds are pure; some worlds tend toward defilement; some worlds tend toward purity; some worlds are defiled and then pure; some worlds are pure and then defiled; some worlds are purely and only pure; some worlds are level and even in shape; some rest face-down; and some rest on their side.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The list holds ten clauses, each opening with 或有. The first eight name their subject — 或有世界, some worlds — and the last two drop it, 或有覆住、或有側住, some rest face-down, some rest on their side. The ellipsis is where the list changes topic: the first seven items are about defilement and purity, and the last three are about geometry. A world can be graded morally or positionally, and the sūtra runs the two scales together without marking the seam.

或有世界雜穢清淨，或有世界清淨雜穢 — some worlds are defiled and then pure; some worlds are pure and then defiled. Two clauses made of the same two words in opposite order. Chinese lets the pairing sit there without a verb, and the reading that fits the surrounding taxonomy is sequence — a world that was foul and became clean is a different kind of world from one that was clean and became foul, even though at any given moment the two may be indistinguishable. A world's history is part of what it is.

或有覆住 — some rest face-down. The phrase is standing Huayan cosmology; in Part 536, Samantabhadra's samādhi includes the gate of knowing buddha-lands 覆住、仰住, resting face-down and face-up. What Good-Wealth is being shown in the gap between two night-spirits is not a private revelation but the ordinary furniture of this sūtra's universe, seen at last from inside a speck of dust.

Part 1101 Complete

This opens chapter thirty-nine, 入法界品, in fascicle seventy. Part 1102 continues the vision — in all those worlds and all those destinies Good-Wealth sees this same night-spirit appearing before beings in whatever shape and speech they can receive, and a long catalogue begins of the fears from which she frees them.
