Touched Upon the Crown

The buddhas who share Vajra Banner Bodhisattva's own name bestow upon him ten distinct gifts — from immeasurable wisdom to unbroken eloquence — before each extending their right hand to touch the crown of his head. The moment this touch is complete, Vajra Banner Bodhisattva rises from his samādhi and turns to address the assembled bodhisattvas directly.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 209 — the buddhas' gifts, Vajra Banner rises from samādhi Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Ten Gifts, Then a Touch Upon the Crown

The buddhas' bestowal takes the form of a list — ten distinct gifts in total, from immeasurable wisdom and unhindered eloquence through to unbroken eloquence for expounding every dharma in every place — before the scene moves to a single gesture: each of the countless buddhas reaches out with their own right hand and touches the crown of Vajra Banner Bodhisattva's head. It is this touch, not the gifts themselves, that draws him up out of samādhi and turns him to face the bodhisattvas gathered to hear him speak.

Chinese Source

爾時，諸佛即與金剛幢菩薩無量智慧，與無留礙辯，與分別句義善方便，與無礙法光明，與如來平等身，與無量差別淨音聲，與菩薩不思議善觀察三昧，與不可沮壞一切善根迴向智，與觀察一切法成就巧方便，與一切處說一切法無斷辯。何以故？入此三昧善根力故。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, the buddhas at once bestowed upon Vajra Banner Bodhisattva immeasurable wisdom, bestowed unhindered eloquence, bestowed skillful means in discerning the meaning of every phrase, bestowed unhindered light of the Dharma, bestowed a body equal to the Tathāgata's own, bestowed immeasurable and distinct pure voices, bestowed the bodhisattva's own inconceivable and skillfully observing samādhi, bestowed the wisdom of dedication whose every wholesome root can never be destroyed, bestowed the skillful means that accomplish the observation of every dharma, and bestowed unbroken eloquence to expound every dharma in every place. For what reason? Because of the power of the wholesome roots from having entered this very samādhi.

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, the buddhas each extended their own right hand and touched the crown of Vajra Banner Bodhisattva's head. Once Vajra Banner Bodhisattva had received this touch upon his crown, he at once rose from his concentration, and addressed the bodhisattvas, saying:

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The hand-upon-the-crown gesture recalls the Vedic and later Buddhist practice of abhiṣeka, a consecration or anointing that marks a formal transfer of authority — here performed not by a single teacher but by the entire assembly of buddhas at once, upon a bodhisattva who already shares each of their names. The ten gifts that precede it read almost as a checklist of what a teacher of this scale requires: not just wisdom, but the eloquence, skillful means, and inexhaustible speech needed to make an inconceivable teaching sayable at all.
