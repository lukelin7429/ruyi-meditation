Why I Was Born Here

Born into so honored a family, the bodhisattva knows every dharma to be like a reflected image, a transformation, a dream, an illusion, a mirage, and so despises no world, clings to no state of existence, tires of no being, and fears no kalpa. Then Maitreya speaks of himself: his body is born throughout the whole dharma-realm in the forms, voices, names, and bearing of every being; he was born in Jambudvīpa, in a brāhmaṇa family in a named village, to bring back those who once practiced with him and have fallen from the mind of bodhi, and to free his kin and the brāhmaṇas from pride of caste; and he will end this life and be born in Tuṣita for reasons he lists one by one, ending with the promise that when he attains bodhi Good-Wealth and Mañjuśrī will both see him.

Why I Was Born Here

What the bodhisattva knows and is therefore freed from, and Maitreya's own account of his body, his birth, and his coming death.

Chinese Source

「善男子！菩薩摩訶薩生於如是尊勝家已，知一切法如影像故，於諸世間無所惡賤；知一切法如變化故，於諸有趣無所染著；知一切法無有我故，教化眾生心無疲厭；以大慈悲為體性故，攝受眾生不覺勞苦；了達生死猶如夢故，經一切劫而無怖畏；了知諸蘊皆如幻故，示現受生而無憂厭；知諸界、處同法界故，於諸境界無所壞滅；知一切想如陽焰故，入於諸趣不生倒惑；達一切法皆如幻故，入魔境界不起染著；知法身故，一切煩惱不能欺誑；得自在故，於一切趣通達無礙。「善男子！我身普生一切法界，等一切眾生差別色相，等一切眾生殊異言音，等一切眾生種種名號，等一切眾生所樂威儀，隨順世間教化調伏；等一切清淨眾生示現受生，等一切凡夫眾生所作事業，等一切眾生想，等一切菩薩願，而現其身充滿法界。「善男子！我為化度與我往昔同修諸行，今時退失菩提心者；亦為教化父母、親屬；亦為教化諸婆羅門，令其離於種族憍慢，得生如來種性之中。——而生於此閻浮提界、摩羅提國、拘吒聚落、婆羅門家。善男子！我住於此大樓閣中，隨諸眾生心之所樂，種種方便教化調伏。善男子！我為隨順眾生心故，我為成熟兜率天中同行天故，我為示現菩薩福智變化莊嚴；超過一切諸欲界故，令其捨離諸欲樂故，令知有為皆無常故，令知諸天盛必衰故，為欲示現將降生時大智法門；與一生菩薩共談論故，為欲攝化諸同行故，為欲教化釋迦如來所遣來者令如蓮華悉開悟故，於此命終，生兜率天。善男子！我願滿足，成一切智，得菩提時，汝及文殊俱得見我。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Once a bodhisattva-mahāsattva has been born into so honored a family as this, because he knows that all dharmas are like reflected images, there is nothing in any world that he despises; because he knows that all dharmas are like transformations, there is no state of existence to which he clings; because he knows that all dharmas are without self, he teaches and transforms sentient beings with a mind that never wearies; because great loving-kindness and compassion are his very nature, he gathers in sentient beings without feeling the toil; because he understands that birth-and-death is like a dream, he passes through every kalpa without fear; because he knows that the aggregates are all like an illusion, he shows himself taking birth without sorrow or weariness; because he knows that the elements and the sense-bases are the same as the dharma-realm, there is no domain in which he suffers destruction; because he knows that every notion is like a mirage, he enters the destinies without giving rise to inverted delusion; because he understands that all dharmas are like an illusion, he enters the domain of Māra without giving rise to defiled attachment; because he knows the dharma-body, no affliction is able to deceive him; and because he has attained sovereign freedom, he penetrates every destiny without obstruction. Good son! My body is born universally throughout the whole dharma-realm, matching the differing forms of every sentient being, matching the distinct voices of every sentient being, matching the manifold names of every sentient being, and matching the bearing that every sentient being delights in, and in accord with the world it teaches, transforms, and tames; matching every pure being it shows itself taking birth, matching every ordinary being it performs their works, matching the notions of every sentient being and matching the vows of every bodhisattva, it manifests itself, filling the dharma-realm. Good son! In order to transform and deliver those who once cultivated the practices together with me and have now fallen away from the mind of bodhi; and also to teach and transform my father and mother and my kin; and also to teach and transform the brāhmaṇas, so that they leave behind the pride of caste and are born into the lineage of the tathāgatas, I was born here in the realm of Jambudvīpa, in the land of Móluótí, in the village of Jūzhā, in a brāhmaṇa family. Good son! I dwell within this great tower, teaching, transforming, and taming sentient beings by every kind of skillful means according to what their minds delight in. Good son! Because I accord with the minds of sentient beings, because I bring to ripeness the devas of the same practice in the Tuṣita Heaven, and because I display the transformations and adornments of a bodhisattva's merit and wisdom; because these surpass the whole realm of desire, because they cause beings to abandon the pleasures of desire, because they cause them to know that everything conditioned is impermanent, because they cause them to know that the flourishing of the devas must decline, and because I wish to display the gate of the dharma of great wisdom at the time of my coming descent to birth; because I converse together with the bodhisattvas of a single remaining birth, because I wish to gather in and transform those of the same practice, and because I wish to teach and transform those sent by Śākyamuni Tathāgata so that they all awaken like lotus flowers opening, I shall end this life here and be born in the Tuṣita Heaven. Good son! When my vows are fulfilled and I accomplish all-knowing wisdom, at the time I attain bodhi, you and Mañjuśrī will both see me.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

知……故 — the first paragraph is eleven clauses long, each a piece of knowledge and the freedom that follows from it, and every clause ends in 故, because. The knowledge is stated first and the consequence second in the Chinese, and the translation keeps that order, because knowing that dharmas are like reflected images, there is nothing he despises, rather than reversing it into cause and effect. The likenesses used are the sūtra's familiar set, reflected image, transformation, dream, illusion twice, and mirage, applied here not to the vision in the tower but to the bodhisattva's whole conduct in the world.

摩羅提國、拘吒聚落 — Maitreya names his birthplace a second time and more precisely, adding a village, 拘吒, to the country named in Part 1320, and adding that the family was a brāhmaṇa one. Both place names are plainly transliterations, and both are left in pinyin, Móluótí and Jūzhā, on the same principle as before: the Chinese characters do not by themselves secure a Sanskrit form, and a transparent placeholder is preferred to a guess. The paragraph on Tuṣita gives nine reasons ending in 故 for his coming death and rebirth there, together with two further clauses of purpose introduced by 為 that lack the final 故, and the translation renders each of them as its own because.
