Neither Going Nor Coming

She sees every buddha of the three times in complete detail — and then explains, in ten paired clauses, why none of what she sees can be said to arrive, depart, exist, or not exist. Then she cultivates the four dhyānas, and every one of them is about somebody else.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1084 — neither going nor coming Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Everything Seen, Nothing Held

Nine categories of what she sees about each buddha, all of them specific and all of them different — and then one clause that refuses to make anything of it.

Chinese Source

「善男子！我得菩薩解脫，名：寂靜禪定樂普遊步。普見三世一切諸佛，亦見彼佛清淨國土、道場、眾會、神通、名號、說法、壽命、言音、身相，種種不同，悉皆明覩而無取著。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, I have attained a bodhisattva liberation named the Universal Roaming Step in the Joy of Tranquil Meditation. I see universally every buddha of the three times, and see also those buddhas' pure lands, their places of awakening, their assemblies, their spiritual powers, their names, their teaching of the dharma, their lifespans, their speech and sounds, and their bodily marks, each kind different from the next — all of them I behold clearly, and yet without grasping or attachment.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

名：寂靜禪定樂普遊步 — named the Universal Roaming Step in the Joy of Tranquil Meditation. This name is worth setting beside the sūtra's own first fascicle, where the same night-spirit appears in the opening assembly and is recorded as having attained 寂靜禪定樂大勇健解脫門 — a liberation-gate of the great valor of the joy of tranquil meditative absorption. The first five characters are identical. The teacher Good-Wealth has now reached was introduced, with almost this same liberation, more than a thousand parts ago.

悉皆明覩而無取著 — all of them I behold clearly, and yet without grasping or attachment. 明覩, beholding clearly, and 無取著, not grasping, are placed in the same clause by 而, and yet. The detail is not reduced in order to achieve the non-attachment; she keeps the lifespans, the names, the voices and the bodily marks, and does not take hold of any of it.

Ten Negations, Each With Its Reason

Not one of the ten is left as an assertion. Each negation is followed by 故, because — and the reasons do not all run in the same direction.

Chinese Source

何以故？知諸如來非去，世趣永滅故；非來，體性無生故；非生，法身平等故；非滅，無有生相故；非實，住如幻法故；非妄，利益眾生故；非遷，超過生死故；非壞，性常不變故；一相，言語悉離故；無相，性相本空故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is this? Because I know that the tathāgatas do not go, since the destinies of the world are forever extinguished for them; that they do not come, since their own essential nature is unborn; that they are not born, since the dharma-body is equal; that they are not extinguished, since there is no mark of birth in them; that they are not real, since they abide in dharmas that are like a conjuring; that they are not false, since they benefit beings; that they do not shift, since they have passed beyond birth and death; that they do not decay, since their nature is constant and unchanging; that they are of one mark, since language is wholly left behind; and that they are without mark, since nature and mark are originally empty.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

非實，住如幻法故；非妄，利益眾生故 — they are not real, since they abide in dharmas that are like a conjuring; they are not false, since they benefit beings. This is the pivot of the whole series. The denial of reality is argued from emptiness; the denial of falsity is argued from usefulness. The sūtra does not resolve the pair into a higher term — it lets the two reasons stand side by side, one metaphysical and one entirely practical.

一相，言語悉離故；無相，性相本空故 — they are of one mark, since language is wholly left behind; they are without mark, since nature and mark are originally empty. The last two items contradict each other on their face, one asserting a single mark and the next asserting none. Read with their reasons, they are two different denials of plurality: the first removes the distinctions language makes, the second removes the ground for making them.

Four Dhyānas, Each About Someone Else

The four meditative absorptions are the most private practice in Buddhism. Here three of the four take a being's condition as their object, and the fourth is about extinguishing somebody's fever.

Chinese Source

「善男子！我如是了知一切如來時，於菩薩寂靜禪定樂普遊步解脫門，分明了達，成就增長，思惟觀察，堅固莊嚴，不起一切妄想分別，大悲救護一切眾生，

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, when I know every tathāgata in this way, then in the bodhisattva liberation-gate of the Universal Roaming Step in the Joy of Tranquil Meditation I plainly and fully penetrate, accomplish and increase, contemplate and examine, and firmly adorn it; I give rise to no false imagining or discrimination at all; and with great compassion I rescue and protect every being.

Chinese Source

一心不動，修習初禪；息一切意業，攝一切眾生，智力勇猛，喜心悅豫，修第二禪；思惟一切眾生自性，厭離生死，修第三禪；悉能息滅一切眾生眾苦熱惱，修第四禪。

Ru-Yi Translation

With a single mind unmoving, I cultivate the first dhyāna. Stilling every mental act, gathering in every being, my power of wisdom valiant and my mind of joy gladdened, I cultivate the second dhyāna. Contemplating the self-nature of every being and growing weary of and detached from birth and death, I cultivate the third dhyāna. Able to still and extinguish the manifold suffering and feverish distress of every being, I cultivate the fourth dhyāna.

Chinese Source

增長圓滿一切智願，出生一切諸三昧海，入諸菩薩解脫海門，遊戲一切神通，成就一切變化，以清淨智普入法界。

Ru-Yi Translation

I increase and fulfill the vow of all-knowing wisdom, bring forth every ocean of samādhi, enter the ocean-gates of the bodhisattvas' liberations, play freely in every spiritual power, accomplish every transformation, and with pure wisdom enter universally into the dharma-realm.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

思惟一切眾生自性，厭離生死，修第三禪 — contemplating the self-nature of every being and growing weary of and detached from birth and death, I cultivate the third dhyāna. In the standard accounts the dhyānas are graded by the internal factors present at each level — applied thought, joy, pleasure, equanimity. This passage regrades them by what the meditator is attending to, and in three of the four cases it is beings. The fourth, 悉能息滅一切眾生眾苦熱惱, able to still and extinguish the manifold suffering and feverish distress of every being, replaces the classical equanimity with someone else's relief.

遊戲一切神通 — play freely in every spiritual power. 遊戲, play, recurs through this whole stretch of the sūtra as the word for a bodhisattva's exercise of power, and it sits here immediately after four dhyānas and before 以清淨智普入法界, entering the dharma-realm with pure wisdom. The sequence runs from stillness through play into universality without marking any of the transitions as difficult.

Thirteen Notions for Those Who Stay Home

Her method with the comfortable is to install a list of thoughts, and the list is unsparing — including one that is simply the name of a flesh-eating demon.

Chinese Source

「善男子！我修此解脫時，以種種方便成就眾生。所謂：於在家放逸眾生，令生不淨想、可厭想、疲勞想、逼迫想、繫縛想、羅剎想、無常想、苦想、無我想、空想、無生想、不自在想、老病死想。自於五欲不生樂著，亦勸眾生不著欲樂，唯住法樂，出離於家，入於非家。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, when I cultivate this liberation, I bring beings to accomplishment by every kind of skillful means. That is to say, in beings who dwell at home in licentiousness I cause to arise the notion of the impure, the notion of the repugnant, the notion of exhaustion, the notion of oppression, the notion of bondage, the notion of a rākṣasa, the notion of impermanence, the notion of suffering, the notion of no-self, the notion of emptiness, the notion of the unborn, the notion of being unfree, and the notion of old age, sickness and death. I myself give rise to no delight or attachment toward the five desires, and I likewise urge beings not to cling to the pleasures of desire but to abide only in the joy of the dharma, to leave the home behind and enter the homeless state.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

羅剎想 — the notion of a rākṣasa. Among twelve otherwise doctrinal notions — impermanence, suffering, no-self, emptiness — sits one that is simply a monster. It is the same device the city spirit Great God used in Part 1071, showing the angry 如羅剎等飲血噉肉; here the rākṣasa is not displayed but planted as a way of seeing what one is already attached to.

出離於家，入於非家 — to leave the home behind and enter the homeless state. 非家, non-home, is a rare formation, and the parallelism is exact: 家 and 非家, home and not-home. What she urges is not a different dwelling but the negation of the one they have — and she prefaces it by saying she takes no pleasure in the five desires herself.
