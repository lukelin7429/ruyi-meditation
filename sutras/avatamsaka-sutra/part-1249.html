Twenty-Two Gates of Letters

The youth begins the teaching his own name promised — not a story or a vow this time, but an alphabet, where intoning each single syllable opens its own distinct gate of prajñāpāramitā.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1249 — fascicle 76 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Twenty-Two Gates of Letters

This is the first half of the famous forty-two-letter teaching shared by the Avataṃsaka and Prajñāpāramitā traditions alike — the Siddham syllabary in which every single sound, intoned on its own, unlocks a named gate of wisdom.

Chinese Source

唱阿字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：以菩薩威力入無差別境界；唱多字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：無邊差別門；唱波字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：普照法界；唱者字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：普輪斷差別；唱那字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：得無依無上；唱邏字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：離依止無垢；唱柂輕呼字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：不退轉方便；唱婆蒲我切字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：金剛場；唱荼徒解切字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名曰：普輪；唱沙史我切字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名為：海藏；唱縛房可切字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：普生安住；唱哆都我切字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：圓滿光；唱也以可切字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：差別積聚；唱瑟吒字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：普光明息煩惱；唱迦字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：無差別雲；唱娑蘇我切字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：降霔大雨；唱麼字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：大流湍激眾峯齊峙；唱伽上聲輕呼字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：普安立；唱他他可切字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：真如平等藏；唱社字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：入世間海清淨；唱鎖字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：念一切佛莊嚴；唱柂字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名：觀察揀擇一切法聚；

Ru-Yi Translation

When I intone the syllable a (阿), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named By a Bodhisattva's Own Power, Entering the State Free of Every Distinction; when I intone the syllable duo (多), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named The Gate of Boundless Distinctions; when I intone the syllable bo (波), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named Universally Illumining the Dharma-Realm; when I intone the syllable zhe (者), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named The Universal Wheel That Severs Every Distinction; when I intone the syllable na (那), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named Attaining What Is Unsupported and Unsurpassed; when I intone the syllable luo (邏), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named Free of Every Reliance, Utterly Stainless; when I intone the syllable yi (柂, pronounced lightly), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named The Skillful Means of Non-Retrogression; when I intone the syllable po (婆, as glossed), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named The Vajra Site; when I intone the syllable tie (荼, as glossed), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named The Universal Wheel; when I intone the syllable shuo (沙, as glossed), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named The Treasury of the Ocean; when I intone the syllable fe (縛, as glossed), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named Universally Arising, Abiding in Peace; when I intone the syllable duo (哆, as glossed), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named Perfect and Complete Light; when I intone the syllable ye (也, as glossed), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named The Accumulation of Every Distinction; when I intone the syllable sezha (瑟吒), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named Universal Light That Quells Every Affliction; when I intone the syllable jia (迦), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named The Cloud Free of Every Distinction; when I intone the syllable suo (娑, as glossed), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named Sending Down a Great Rain; when I intone the syllable mo (麼), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named The Great Current's Rushing Torrent, Where Every Peak Stands Equal; when I intone the syllable jia (伽, pronounced lightly, rising tone), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named Universal Establishment; when I intone the syllable te (他, as glossed), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named The Treasury of Suchness's Own Equality; when I intone the syllable she (社), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named Entering the Purity of the World's Own Ocean; when I intone the syllable suo (鎖), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named Recollecting the Adornment of Every Buddha; when I intone the syllable tuo (柂, unmarked, contrasting with its lighter reading above), I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā named Contemplating and Discerning Every Gathering of Dharmas.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

唱阿字時，入般若波羅蜜門⋯唱柂字時，入般若波羅蜜門 — when I intone the syllable a, I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā⋯when I intone the syllable tuo, I enter the gate of prajñāpāramitā. This is the well-known forty-two-letter dharma gate (四十二字門) found in both the Avataṃsaka and the Prajñāpāramitā sūtras, built on the Siddham syllabary tradition scholars call Arapacana. Several of the Chinese characters here carry a fanqie (反切) gloss — a pair of characters whose sounds combine to fix an exact pronunciation — because the text needs a precise, unambiguous syllable rather than any of a character's more common readings. Reconstructing the exact historical Sanskrit value of each letter is a specialized question this translation does not attempt to settle; each syllable below is rendered by its plain modern reading, with the original character kept alongside it, so nothing is asserted beyond what the text itself gives.
