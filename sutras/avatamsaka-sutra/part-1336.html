A Single Drop from the Ocean of Merit

Samantabhadra speaks a short verse of four stanzas, urging the assembly to listen with an undistracted mind, comparing the Tathāgata to the udumbara flower that blooms but once in an age. The bodhisattvas, thirsting to hear more, reflect together on Samantabhadra's own pure nature and the truth of everything he says. Samantabhadra, adorned with merit and wisdom like a lotus untouched by the three realms, promises to speak of no more than a single drop from the ocean of a buddha's merit, and begins a vast gāthā: a buddha's wisdom pervades every mind yet never stirs a thought, and what each being sees of a buddha differs entirely according to their own karma and purity of mind, some seeing him seated everywhere at once and others not seeing him across measureless kalpas, some hearing his voice and others not, some seeing the buddha of Immeasurable Life surrounded by Avalokiteśvara and every other assembly of a buddha's land.

A Single Drop from the Ocean of Merit

A short verse on the rarity of meeting a buddha, the bodhisattvas' thirst to hear more, and the opening ten stanzas of the great gāthā that follows.

Chinese Source

爾時，普賢菩薩摩訶薩即說頌言：「汝等應除諸惑垢，
一心不亂而諦聽；
我說如來具諸度，
一切解脫真實道。

出世調柔勝丈夫，
其心清淨如虛空，
恒放智日大光明，
普使群生滅癡暗。

如來難可得見聞，
無量億劫今乃值，
如優曇華時一現，
是故應聽佛功德。

隨順世間諸所作，
譬如幻士現眾業，
但為悅可眾生心，
未曾分別起想念。」

爾時，諸菩薩聞此說已，一心渴仰，唯願得聞如來世尊真實功德，咸作是念：「普賢菩薩具修諸行，體性清淨，所有言說皆悉不虛，一切如來共所稱歎。」作是念已，深生渴仰。爾時，普賢菩薩功德智慧具足莊嚴，猶如蓮華不著三界一切塵垢，告諸菩薩言：「汝等諦聽，我今欲說佛功德海一滴之相。」即說頌言：

「佛智廣大同虛空，
普遍一切眾生心，
悉了世間諸妄想，
不起種種異分別。

一念悉知三世法，
亦了一切眾生根，
譬如善巧大幻師，
念念示現無邊事。

隨眾生心種種行，
往昔諸業誓願力，
令其所見各不同，
而佛本來無動念。

或有處處見佛坐，
充滿十方諸世界，
或有其心不清淨，
無量劫中不見佛。

或有信解離憍慢，
發意即得見如來；
或有諂誑不淨心，
億劫尋求莫值遇。

或一切處聞佛音，
其音美妙令心悅；
或有百千萬億劫，
心不淨故不聞者。

或見清淨大菩薩，
充滿三千大千界，
皆已具足普賢行，
如來於中儼然坐。

或見此界妙無比，
佛無量劫所嚴淨；
毘盧遮那最勝尊，
於中覺悟成菩提。

或見蓮華勝妙剎，
賢首如來住在中，
無量菩薩眾圍遶，
皆悉勤修普賢行。

或有見佛無量壽，
觀自在等所圍遶，
悉已住於灌頂地，
充滿十方諸世界。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Samantabhadra Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva then spoke a verse, saying: "You should remove every defiling stain,
and with a single, undistracted mind attentively listen;
I shall speak of the Tathāgata's every pāramitā,
the true path of every liberation.

He who has gone beyond the world, gentle and supreme among men,
his mind pure like empty space,
forever sends forth the great light of the sun of wisdom,
universally causing every being to extinguish the darkness of delusion.

The Tathāgata is one rarely seen or heard of,
and only now, after measureless koṭis of kalpas, have you encountered him;
like the udumbara flower that shows itself but once in its season,
therefore you should listen to the buddha's own merit.

He accords with the world in every action he performs,
just as an illusionist manifests every kind of deed,
doing so only to please the minds of sentient beings,
never once giving rise to a discriminating thought."

At that time, the bodhisattvas, having heard this spoken, thirsted single-mindedly for more, wishing only to hear of the World-Honored Tathāgata's own true merit, and all together thought this thought: "Samantabhadra Bodhisattva has fully cultivated every practice, his very nature pure, and everything he has spoken is entirely without falsehood, praised together by every tathāgata." Having thought this thought, they thirsted for it all the more deeply. At that time, Samantabhadra Bodhisattva, fully adorned with merit and wisdom, like a lotus flower untouched by any defiling stain of the three realms, addressed the bodhisattvas, saying, "You should now listen attentively; I shall now speak of the mark of a single drop from the ocean of a buddha's merit." And then he spoke a verse, saying:

"A buddha's wisdom is vast and great, the same as empty space,
universally pervading the mind of every sentient being;
it fully understands every false notion of the world,
yet never gives rise to any manner of differentiating thought.

In a single thought it entirely knows the dharmas of the three times,
and likewise understands the faculties of every sentient being;
just like a great and skillful illusionist,
in thought after thought it displays boundless things.

According to the manifold conduct of sentient beings' minds,
and through the power of their past karma and their vows,
what each of them sees differs one from another,
though the buddha himself has never once stirred a thought.

Some see the buddha seated everywhere at once,
filling every world of the ten directions;
some, because their minds are not pure,
do not see the buddha throughout measureless kalpas.

Some, with faith and understanding, free of pride and conceit,
attain the sight of the Tathāgata the moment they give rise to the intention;
some, with a flattering, deceitful, and impure mind,
seek for koṭis of kalpas without ever once encountering him.

Some hear the buddha's voice in every place,
its sound wondrous and beautiful, delighting the mind;
some, throughout hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of kalpas,
because their minds are not pure, never hear it at all.

Some see pure and great bodhisattvas
filling a whole trichiliocosm,
every one of them fully endowed with Samantabhadra's own practice,
with the Tathāgata seated solemnly in their midst.

Some see this world wondrous beyond compare,
adorned and purified by the buddha through measureless kalpas;
Vairocana, the most supreme and honored one,
awakens within it and accomplishes bodhi.

Some see the supreme and wondrous land of the lotus flower,
where the Tathāgata Bhadraśrī abides,
surrounded by measureless assemblies of bodhisattvas,
every one of them diligently cultivating Samantabhadra's own practice.

Some see the buddha Immeasurable Life,
surrounded by Avalokiteśvara and others,
all of them already abiding on the ground of consecration,
filling every world of the ten directions.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

佛功德海一滴之相 — Samantabhadra frames the entire gāthā that follows, one of the longest single passages of verse in the sūtra, as no more than a single drop out of the whole ocean of a buddha's merit; the fourteen stanzas given here are only its opening. Each stanza is a self-contained couplet-pair on the theme of 或見……或見……, some see this, some see that, contrasting what a pure mind perceives against what an impure one misses entirely, whether the buddha's form, his voice, or the land he is seen to occupy.

無量壽 and 觀自在 — the buddha named here is rendered Immeasurable Life, the same English form this site has used before for the identical two characters when they appear as a name; the sūtra does not here write 阿彌陀, the transliterated name this site renders Amitābha elsewhere, so the two are kept distinct rather than merged into one spelling. 觀自在 is Avalokiteśvara, the form already established on this site since the bodhisattva's own chapter earlier in the sūtra, and 賢首如來 continues to be Bhadraśrī, kept consistent with Part 1329 and earlier.
