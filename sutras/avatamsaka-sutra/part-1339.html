Eighty-Four Thousand Remedies

The great gāthā continues: in a single voice the buddha teaches every class of being, nāgas, devas, humans and non-humans, each according to its own kind, meeting the eighty-four thousand kinds of affliction with the eighty-four thousand remedies for them, the ten precepts for the newly virtuous and the cessation of nirvāṇa for those already ripe, the three gates of liberation for the timid, dependent origination for the solitary, and the sound of the Great Vehicle for the pure and vast of heart, so that one land hears the One Vehicle, another two, three, four, or five, and another measureless vehicles, all of it the Tathāgata's own skillful means. The buddha's voice, like the light from his own face, fills every world equally, understood differently by each mind, and his very body appears as a śramaṇa with shaved head and kāṣāya robe, as an emaciated brāhmaṇa skilled in every worldly art, deep in every dhyāna and liberation, all so that beings may be brought to abide in the buddha's own path.

Eighty-Four Thousand Remedies

Twenty more stanzas of the great gāthā — the buddha's single voice meeting every affliction with its own remedy, and his body appearing as a śramaṇa and a brāhmaṇa among every form beings themselves take.

Chinese Source

龍神八部人非人，
梵釋護世諸天眾，
佛以一音為說法，
隨其品類皆令解。

若有貪欲瞋恚癡，
忿覆慳嫉及憍諂，
八萬四千煩惱異，
皆令聞說彼治法。

若未具修白淨法，
令其聞說十戒行；
已能布施調伏人，
令聞寂滅涅槃音。

若人志劣無慈愍，
厭惡生死自求離；
令其聞說三脫門，
使得出苦涅槃樂。

若有自性少諸欲，
厭背三有求寂靜；
令其聞說諸緣起，
依獨覺乘而出離。

若有清淨廣大心，
具足施戒諸功德，
親近如來具慈愍，
令其聞說大乘音。

或有國土聞一乘，
或二或三或四五，
如是乃至無有量，
悉是如來方便力。

涅槃寂靜未曾異，
智行勝劣有差別；
譬如虛空體性一，
鳥飛遠近各不同。

佛體音聲亦如是，
普遍一切虛空界，
隨諸眾生心智殊，
所聞所見各差別。

佛以過去修諸行，
能隨所樂演妙音，
無心計念此與彼，
我為誰說誰不說。

如來面門放大光，
具足八萬四千數；
所說法門亦如是，
普照世界除煩惱。

具足清淨功德智，
而常隨順三世間，
譬如虛空無染著，
為眾生故而出現。

示有生老病死苦，
亦示住壽處於世；
雖順世間如是現，
體性清淨同虛空。

一切國土無有邊，
眾生根欲亦無量；
如來智眼皆明見，
隨所應化示佛道。

究竟虛空十方界，
所有人天大眾中，
隨其形相各不同，
佛現其身亦如是。

若在沙門大眾會，
鬀除鬚髮服袈裟，
執持衣鉢護諸根，
令其歡喜息煩惱。

若時親近婆羅門，
即為示現羸瘦身，
執杖持瓶恒潔淨，
具足智慧巧談說。

吐故納新自充飽，
吸風飲露無異食，
若坐若立不動搖，
現斯苦行摧異道。

或持彼戒為世師，
善達醫方等諸論，
書數天文地眾相，
及身休咎無不了。

深入諸禪及解脫，
三昧神通智慧行，
言談諷詠共嬉戲，
方便皆令住佛道。

Ru-Yi Translation

The eight classes, nāgas and devas, humans and non-humans,
Brahmā, Śakra, the world-protectors, and every host of devas—
the buddha teaches the dharma in a single voice,
and causes each according to its own kind to understand.

If there is greed, hatred, and delusion,
resentment, concealment, stinginess, envy, pride, and flattery,
the eighty-four thousand differing afflictions—
he causes all of them to hear the remedy taught for each.

If one has not yet cultivated the white and pure dharma in full,
he causes them to hear the ten precepts of conduct taught;
for one already able to give and to tame others,
he causes them to hear the sound of quiescent nirvāṇa.

If a person's resolve is weak and without compassion,
despising birth-and-death and seeking only to escape it,
he causes them to hear the three gates of liberation taught,
so that they attain the bliss of nirvāṇa beyond suffering.

If one is by nature of few desires,
turning from the three states of existence to seek quiescence,
he causes them to hear dependent origination taught,
so that they find release through the pratyekabuddha vehicle.

If one has a pure and vast mind,
fully endowed with the merits of giving and moral discipline,
drawing near to the Tathāgata with compassion complete,
he causes them to hear the sound of the Great Vehicle.

Some lands hear of the One Vehicle,
some of two, some of three, some of four or five,
and so on up to measureless vehicles,
every one of them the Tathāgata's own skillful means.

Nirvāṇa's own quiescence is never different,
though wisdom and practice, superior and inferior, differ;
just as empty space is one in its own nature,
while birds flying through it go far or near, each their own way.

A buddha's own voice is likewise:
universally pervading the whole realm of space,
yet according to the differing minds and wisdom of sentient beings,
what is heard and what is seen each differ.

Through the practices the buddha cultivated in ages past,
he is able to send forth a wondrous voice according to what beings delight in,
with no mind that reckons this against that,
no thought of for whom he speaks and for whom he does not.

From the Tathāgata's own face there streams a great light,
fully numbering eighty-four thousand;
the gates of dharma he speaks are likewise,
universally illuminating the world and removing its afflictions.

Fully endowed with pure merit and wisdom,
and yet ever according with the three worlds,
just as empty space is without any taint,
he appears in the world for the sake of sentient beings.

He shows the suffering of birth, old age, sickness, and death,
and also shows a life that abides and dwells in the world;
though he appears thus, according with the world,
his own nature remains pure, the same as empty space.

Every land is without limit,
and the faculties and desires of sentient beings are likewise measureless;
the Tathāgata's eye of wisdom sees all of it clearly,
and shows the buddha's own path according to what each should be taught.

Throughout the whole realm of space and the ten directions,
among every assembly of devas and humans there is,
each with its own differing form and appearance,
and the buddha's own displayed body is likewise.

If among an assembly of śramaṇas,
he shaves his head and beard and wears the kāṣāya robe,
holding his own robe and bowl, guarding his own faculties,
causing them joy and the quieting of their afflictions.

If at times he draws near to the brāhmaṇas,
he displays for them a lean and emaciated body,
holding a staff and a vessel, ever pure and clean,
fully endowed with wisdom and skilled in every discourse.

Exhaling the old and inhaling the new, satisfied by this alone,
breathing the wind and drinking the dew with no other food,
whether seated or standing, never wavering or moving,
he displays such ascetic practice to overcome the other paths.

Or he upholds their own precepts and becomes a teacher to the world,
well versed in medicine and in every kind of treatise,
in numbers, in astronomy, in the marks of the earth,
and in every sign of good and ill fortune of the body, leaving nothing unknown.

Deeply entering every dhyāna and every liberation,
the practice of samādhi, spiritual power, and wisdom,
joining in speech, in song, and in every kind of play together,
through skillful means he causes all to abide in the buddha's own path.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

八萬四千 — the number appears twice in this stretch, once for the afflictions themselves and once for the rays of light from the Tathāgata's own face that meet them, each ray matched to its own gate of dharma; the translation keeps the same phrase, eighty-four thousand, both times, following the site's established rendering of this recurring number throughout the sūtra.

或有國土聞一乘，或二或三或四五 — the stanza on the One Vehicle names a graded series, one, two, three, four or five, and then measureless, and the translation keeps the whole series rather than summarizing it, since the point of the stanza is precisely that the same teaching is heard as a different number of vehicles by different lands. The two portraits that follow, of the buddha appearing among the śramaṇas with shaved head and kāṣāya robe and among the brāhmaṇas with an emaciated body and ascetic practice, match the two audiences this chapter has already named together earlier in the sūtra.
