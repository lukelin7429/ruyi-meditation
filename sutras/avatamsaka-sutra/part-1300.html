One Lamp Lights a Hundred Thousand

The paired similes continue: a medicinal juice named hāṭaka that turns a thousand liang of copper into true gold, a small fire that grows fiercer with whatever it burns, a single lamp that lights a hundred thousand more and is never diminished, a lamp that breaks a hundred thousand years of darkness, a wick fed with oil, and the golden crown of the king of the Paranirmita-vaśavartin Heaven.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1300 — fascicle 78 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

One Lamp Lights a Hundred Thousand

Six more paired similes continue the pattern — a gold-making medicine, a spreading fire, three successive images of a lamp, and a heavenly crown that no ornament of the desire realm can match.

Chinese Source

「善男子！如有藥汁，名：訶宅迦，人或得之，以其一兩變千兩銅，悉成真金，非千兩銅能變此藥。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，以菩提心迴向智藥，普變一切業惑等法，悉使成於一切智相，非業惑等能變其心。「善男子！譬如小火，隨所焚燒，其焰轉熾。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心火亦復如是，隨所攀緣，智焰增長。「善男子！譬如一燈，然百千燈，其本一燈無減無盡。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心燈亦復如是，普然三世諸佛智燈，而其心燈無減無盡。「善男子！譬如一燈，入於闇室，百千年闇悉能破盡。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心燈亦復如是，入於眾生心室之內，百千萬億不可說劫諸業煩惱、種種闇障悉能除盡。「善男子！譬如燈炷，隨其大小而發光明；若益膏油，明終不絕。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心燈亦復如是，大願為炷，光照法界；益大悲油，教化眾生，莊嚴國土，施作佛事，無有休息。「善男子！譬如他化自在天王，冠閻浮檀真金天冠，欲界天子諸莊嚴具皆不能及。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，冠菩提心大願天冠，一切凡夫、二乘功德皆不能及。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Just as there is a medicinal juice named hāṭaka, and should a person obtain it, a single liang of it transforms a thousand liang of copper so that all of it becomes true gold, while a thousand liang of copper cannot transform this medicine, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: with the medicine of the wisdom of dedication that is the mind of bodhi, they universally transform every dharma of karma, delusion, and the like, causing all of them to become the marks of all-knowing wisdom, while karma, delusion, and the like cannot transform that mind. Good son! Just as a small fire, whatever it burns, grows ever fiercer in its flame, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own fire that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: whatever it takes as its object, the flame of its wisdom increases. Good son! Just as a single lamp lights a hundred thousand lamps, and the original lamp is neither diminished nor exhausted, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own lamp that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: it universally lights the wisdom-lamps of the buddhas of the three times, yet its own mind-lamp is neither diminished nor exhausted. Good son! Just as a single lamp, entering a dark room, is able to break apart entirely a darkness of a hundred thousand years, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own lamp that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: entering within the chamber of sentient beings' minds, it is able to remove entirely the karma and afflictions and every kind of dark obscuration of a hundred thousand myriad koṭis of ineffable kalpas. Good son! Just as a lamp's wick gives off light according to its size, and if oil is added, the light never fails, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own lamp that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: with great vows as its wick, its light illuminates the dharma-realm; and adding the oil of great compassion, it teaches and transforms sentient beings, adorns lands, and performs buddha-work without any rest. Good son! Just as the king of the Paranirmita-vaśavartin Heaven wears a heavenly crown of true Jambu-river gold that no ornament of the devaputras of the desire realm can equal, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: wearing the heavenly crown of great vows that is the mind of bodhi, no merit of any ordinary being or of the two vehicles can equal it.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

訶宅迦 — the three characters are read here as a transliteration of hāṭaka, a Sanskrit word for gold, which suits a juice whose whole function is to turn copper into gold; the identification is offered as the translator's reading rather than as a settled equivalence. 兩 (liang) is kept as a unit name in the same way that 銖 (zhu) was kept in the previous part: the simile turns on the ratio of one liang against a thousand liang, not on the modern weight of either.

The lamp supplies three successive similes here, each drawing out a different property: a lamp that lights others without loss, a lamp that ends a long darkness at once, and a wick whose light continues for as long as oil is supplied. The last of these is the one the passage expands into the bodhisattva's own vows as wick and great compassion as oil.
