Verse, Stanzas One to Twenty

Good-Wealth recasts the entire teaching as verse, naming Maitreya directly in the very first stanza, then running through nineteen more — each building an image across three lines before closing on who this great dwelling-place belongs to.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1275 — fascicle 77 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Verse, Stanzas One to Twenty

Twenty stanzas of seven-character lines open Good-Wealth's verse, each one naming a quality of the tower's true resident before closing on the same refrain — "this is the dwelling-place of."

Chinese Source

此是大悲清淨智，
利益世間慈氏尊，
灌頂地中佛長子，
入如來境之住處。

一切名聞諸佛子，
已入大乘解脫門，
遊行法界心無著，
此無等者之住處。

施戒忍進禪智慧，
方便願力及神通，
如是大乘諸度法，
悉具足者之住處。

智慧廣大如虛空，
普知三世一切法，
無礙無依無所取，
了諸有者之住處。

善能解了一切法，
無性無生無所依，
如鳥飛空得自在，
此大智者之住處。

了知三毒真實性，
分別因緣虛妄起，
亦不厭彼而求出，
此寂靜人之住處。

三解脫門八聖道，
諸蘊處界及緣起，
悉能觀察不趣寂，
此善巧人之住處。

十方國土及眾生，
以無礙智咸觀察，
了性皆空不分別，
此寂滅人之住處。

普行法界悉無礙，
而求行性不可得，
如風行空無所行，
此無依者之住處。

普見惡道群生類，
受諸楚毒無所歸，
放大慈光悉除滅，
此哀愍者之住處。

見諸眾生失正道，
譬如生盲踐畏途，
引其令入解脫城，
此大導師之住處。

見諸眾生入魔網，
生老病死常逼迫，
令其解脫得慰安，
此勇健人之住處。

見諸眾生嬰惑病，
而興廣大悲愍心，
以智慧藥悉除滅，
此大醫王之住處。

見諸群生沒有海，
沈淪憂迫受眾苦，
悉以法船而救之，
此善度者之住處。

見諸眾生在惑海，
能發菩提妙寶心，
悉入其中而濟拔，
此善漁人之住處。

恒以大願慈悲眼，
普觀一切諸眾生，
從諸有海而拔出，
此金翅王之住處。

譬如日月在虛空，
一切世間靡不燭，
智慧光明亦如是，
此照世者之住處。

菩薩為化一眾生，
普盡未來無量劫，
如為一人一切爾，
此救世者之住處。

於一國土化眾生，
盡未來劫無休息，
一一國土咸如是，
此堅固意之住處。

十方諸佛所說法，
一座普受咸令盡，
盡未來劫恒悉然，
此智海人之住處。

Ru-Yi Translation

"This is the pure wisdom of great compassion,
the honored Maitreya who benefits the world,
a buddha's own eldest son upon the ground of consecration —
the dwelling-place of one who has entered the very domain of the tathāgatas.

Every buddha-child renowned by name,
who has already entered the great vehicle's own gate of liberation,
who travels through the dharma-realm with a mind unattached —
this is the dwelling-place of one without equal.

Giving, precepts, forbearance, diligence, meditation, and wisdom,
skillful means, the power of vow, and spiritual penetration —
every such pāramitā of the great vehicle,
this is the dwelling-place of one who has fulfilled them all.

Wisdom vast and broad as empty space,
universally knowing every dharma of the three times,
unobstructed, without reliance, grasping at nothing —
this is the dwelling-place of one who understands every state of existence.

Able to fully understand every dharma,
without nature, without birth, without any reliance,
like a bird flying through space in perfect freedom —
this is the dwelling-place of one of great wisdom.

Understanding the true nature of the three poisons,
discerning that they arise falsely from conditions,
yet never wearying of them or seeking escape from them —
this is the dwelling-place of one who is still.

The three gates of liberation, the eightfold noble path,
the aggregates, sense-bases, elements, and dependent origination —
able to contemplate all of these without hastening toward quiescence,
this is the dwelling-place of one skilled in means.

Every land of the ten directions and every sentient being,
all contemplated with unobstructed wisdom,
understood in nature to be empty, without discrimination —
this is the dwelling-place of one at peace.

Traveling universally through the dharma-realm without any obstruction,
yet seeking the very nature of that traveling, it cannot be found,
like wind moving through space, going nowhere at all —
this is the dwelling-place of one without reliance.

Universally seeing every kind of being in the evil destinies,
suffering every torment with nowhere to turn,
he releases the light of great loving-kindness and dissolves it all away —
this is the dwelling-place of one who feels their sorrow.

Seeing every sentient being who has lost the right path,
like one born blind treading a fearful road,
he leads them until they enter the city of liberation —
this is the dwelling-place of the great guide.

Seeing every sentient being caught in māra's net,
constantly pressed by birth, old age, sickness, and death,
he brings them to liberation and grants them comfort —
this is the dwelling-place of the courageous one.

Seeing every sentient being afflicted by the sickness of delusion,
he raises a mind of vast and great compassion,
and with the medicine of wisdom dissolves it all away —
this is the dwelling-place of the great king of physicians.

Seeing every being sunk within the ocean of existence,
drowning, pressed by sorrow, receiving every suffering,
he rescues them all with the ship of the dharma —
this is the dwelling-place of one who ferries beings safely across.

Seeing every sentient being within the ocean of delusion,
he is able to raise the wondrous jewel-mind of bodhi,
and enters into it entirely to rescue and draw them out —
this is the dwelling-place of the skillful fisherman.

Always with the eye of great vow and loving compassion,
he universally beholds every sentient being,
and draws them out from the ocean of every state of existence —
this is the dwelling-place of the garuḍa king.

Just as the sun and moon stand in empty space,
and there is no world they fail to illuminate,
so too is the light of his own wisdom —
this is the dwelling-place of one who illuminates the world.

For the sake of transforming a single sentient being,
a bodhisattva exhausts every measureless kalpa yet to come,
and just as for one person, so it is for every being —
this is the dwelling-place of one who saves the world.

Within a single land he transforms sentient beings,
without rest throughout every kalpa yet to come,
and every single land is treated just the same —
this is the dwelling-place of one whose resolve is firm.

Every dharma spoken by every buddha of the ten directions,
he receives them all universally in a single seat and exhausts them entirely,
and so it remains throughout every kalpa yet to come —
this is the dwelling-place of one whose wisdom is an ocean.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

此是大悲清淨智，利益世間慈氏尊 — the very first line of Good-Wealth's verse names 慈氏尊, "the honored Maitreya," directly: this entire teaching turns out to have been describing Maitreya himself, who has not yet appeared in the story but is now named for the first time. Twenty stanzas, twenty occurrences of the refrain 此⋯之住處 ("this is the dwelling-place of"), each built on four seven-character lines — stanza count and line count both verified directly against the source text, and every stanza carries exactly three 、
、 breaks on both sides of the table.

The refrain shifts subject every stanza — a bodhisattva of unequaled wisdom, a physician, a guide for the blind, a fisherman drawing beings from an ocean, a garuḍa, the sun and moon — all epithets circling back to the same one figure named in the opening line.
