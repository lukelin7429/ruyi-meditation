An Aspiration Forever Secure

With the eighth dedication now formally complete, Vajradhvaja Bodhisattva again surveys the ten directions and, by the Buddha's own power, speaks a verse retelling what the dedication of true suchness has already said in prose — an aspiration forever secure, mindfulness firm and free of delusion, and karma that comes to share the very mark, nature, and boundlessness of true suchness itself.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 355 — the eighth dedication retold in verse, part one Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Karma That Shares Suchness's Own Nature

The verse retraces the prose's central claim in condensed form: not only does the dedication resemble true suchness, but karma itself — ordinarily the most conditioned, most entangled category in Buddhist thought — comes to share suchness's mark, its nature, its very boundlessness, "and yet within it there is no bondage or attachment at all."

Chinese Source

「菩薩志樂常安住，正念堅固離癡惑，
其心善軟恒清涼，積集無邊功德行。
菩薩謙順無違逆，所有志願悉清淨，
已得智慧大光明，善能照了一切業。
菩薩思惟業廣大，種種差別甚希有，
決意修行無退轉，以此饒益諸群生。
諸業差別無量種，菩薩一切勤修習，
隨順眾生不違意，普令心淨生歡喜。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The bodhisattva's aspiration and joy forever remain secure, his true mindfulness firm, leaving behind delusion and confusion; his mind is gentle and soft, forever cool and tranquil, gathering boundless deeds of merit. The bodhisattva is humble and accommodating, never contrary or resistant, every aspiration and vow of his entirely pure; he has already attained the great light of wisdom, skillfully able to illuminate and comprehend every karmic act. The bodhisattva reflects that karma is vast and great, its various distinctions exceedingly rare and wondrous; his resolve to cultivate and practice never regresses, and with this he benefits every living being. The distinctions among karmic acts are of immeasurable kinds, and the bodhisattva diligently cultivates and practices every one of them; according with sentient beings, never contrary to their wishes, he universally causes their minds to become pure and give rise to joy.

Chinese Source

已昇調御人尊地，離諸熱惱心無礙，
於法於義悉善知，為利群生轉勤習。
菩薩所修眾善行，無量無數種種別，
於彼一切分別知，為利群生故迴向。
以妙智慧恒觀察，究竟廣大真實理，
斷諸有處悉無餘，如彼真如善迴向。
譬如真如遍一切，如是普攝諸世間，
菩薩以此心迴向，悉令眾生無所著。

Ru-Yi Translation

He has already ascended to the ground of the Tamer, the most honored among people, leaving behind every feverish distress, his mind unobstructed; regarding both the Dharma and its meaning, he skillfully knows them all, and for the benefit of every living being, he turns ever more diligently to their cultivation. Every wholesome practice the bodhisattva cultivates is immeasurable, innumerable, and of every kind of distinction; regarding all of them, he discerningly knows each one, and for the benefit of every living being, he dedicates them. With wondrous wisdom he forever contemplates the ultimate, vast, true and real principle, severing every place of existence entirely without remainder, dedicating skillfully just as true suchness itself does. Just as true suchness pervades everything, so too does he universally gather in every world; with this mind the bodhisattva dedicates, causing every sentient being to be attached to nothing at all.

Chinese Source

菩薩願力遍一切，譬如真如無不在，
若見不見念悉周，悉以功德而迴向。
夜中隨住晝亦住，半月一月亦隨住，
若年若劫悉住中，真如如是行亦然。
所有三世及剎土，一切眾生與諸法，
悉住其中無所住，以如是行而迴向。
譬如真如本自性，菩薩如是發大心，
真如所在無不在，以如是行而迴向。

Ru-Yi Translation

The power of the bodhisattva's vow pervades everything, just as true suchness is present everywhere without exception; whether seen or unseen, his thought pervades entirely, and he dedicates all of it as merit. Abiding throughout the night, and abiding throughout the day as well, abiding likewise throughout the half-month and the month, abiding entirely within the year and the kalpa alike — just as true suchness is so, this practice is likewise so. Throughout the three times and every land, throughout every sentient being and every dharma, he abides entirely within them all while abiding nowhere at all, dedicating with this very practice. Just as true suchness is originally its own nature, so too does the bodhisattva give rise to this great mind; wherever true suchness is present, he too is present without exception, dedicating with this very practice.

Chinese Source

譬如真如本自性，其中未曾有一法，
不得自性是真性，以如是業而迴向。
如真如相業亦爾，如真如性業亦爾，
如真如性本真實，業亦如是同真如。
譬如真如無邊際，業亦如是無有邊，
而於其中無縛著，是故此業得清淨。
如是聰慧真佛子，志願堅固不動搖，
以其智力善通達，入於諸佛方便藏。

Ru-Yi Translation

Just as true suchness is originally its own nature, and within it there has never once been a single dharma, so the absence of any fixed nature is itself the true nature, and it is with this very karma that he dedicates. Just as true suchness has its own mark, so too does karma; just as true suchness has its own nature, so too does karma; just as true suchness's own nature is originally true and real, karma is likewise so, the same as true suchness. Just as true suchness is without any boundary or limit, so too is karma without any boundary; and yet within it there is no bondage or attachment at all, and it is for this reason that this karma attains purity. Such a wise and intelligent true child of the Buddha, his aspiration and vow firm and unshaken, with the power of his wisdom skillfully comprehends and enters into the treasury of skillful means of every buddha.

Chinese Source

覺悟法王真實法，於中無著亦無縛，
如是自在心無礙，未曾見有一法起。
如來法身所作業，一切世間如彼相，
說諸法相皆無相，知如是相是知法。
菩薩住是不思議，於中思議不可盡，
入此不可思議處，思與非思皆寂滅。
如是思惟諸法性，了達一切業差別，
所有我執皆除滅，住於功德無能動。

Ru-Yi Translation

He awakens to the true and real Dharma of the King of Dharma, within it neither attached nor bound; his mind, sovereign and free in this way, unobstructed, has never once seen a single dharma arise. The karma performed by the Tathāgata's own Dharma-body is, throughout every world, just as that same mark; expounding that every mark of every dharma is itself markless, to know this mark in this way is to know the Dharma. The bodhisattva abides within this inconceivable state, and within it, contemplation itself can never be exhausted; entering this inconceivable place, both thought and non-thought alike become quiescent. Reflecting in this way on the nature of every dharma, he fully comprehends every distinction of karma; every grasping at a self is entirely extinguished, and he abides in merit, moved by nothing at all.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

"To know this mark in this way is to know the Dharma" closes the verse's most philosophically dense stretch with an unusually direct equation: markless-ness is not a difficult truth to be grasped alongside ordinary knowing, but is itself what knowing the Dharma actually consists of. Everything the verse has said about suchness and karma sharing one nature converges here into a single working definition of understanding.
