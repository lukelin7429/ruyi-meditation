Equal Practice, Every Buddha's Domain

The dedication litany continues its long expansion, moving through purity in body, speech, and mind, wholesome karmic fruit, and every buddha-land adorned and purified, before reaching some of its most vivid images: the sixty distinct qualities of a buddha's own voice, the fearless lion's roar of a hero among people, and the turning of an inexhaustible, never-regressing wheel of the Dharma.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 335 — purity in body, speech, and mind, the buddha's voice and lion's roar Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Sixty Voices, One Lion's Roar

Amid the litany's steady repetition of "universally pure" and "universally fulfilled," two images break through with unusual specificity: the buddha's voice, said to possess sixty distinct qualities, each word "true and sincere, entirely worthy of trust," and the lion's roar — the fearless, unhesitating proclamation traditionally associated with a buddha's own teaching, here dedicated to every sentient being alike.

Ru-Yi Translation

So that all sentient beings may abide in equal practice, gathering wholesome dharmas entirely to fulfillment; so that all sentient beings may skillfully enter every buddha's domain, pervading it entirely; so that all sentient beings' actions of body, speech, and mind may universally become pure; so that all sentient beings' wholesome karmic fruit may universally become pure; so that all sentient beings may fully comprehend every dharma, universally pure; so that all sentient beings may fully comprehend the true meaning, universally pure; so that all sentient beings may cultivate every supreme practice, universally pure; so that all sentient beings may accomplish every great vow of a bodhisattva, universally pure; so that all sentient beings may realize and attain every merit and wisdom, universally pure; so that all sentient beings may accomplish every wholesome root of the same essence, dedicating it to give rise to the vehicle of all-wisdom, universally fulfilled; so that all sentient beings may adorn and purify every buddha-land, universally fulfilled; so that all sentient beings may behold every buddha without any attachment, universally fulfilled; so that all sentient beings may possess every excellent mark, adorned with merit, universally fulfilled; so that all sentient beings may attain the sixty kinds of vocal quality, speaking words that are true and sincere, entirely worthy of trust, adorned with hundreds of thousands of kinds of Dharma, the Tathāgata's own unobstructed, meritorious, wondrous voice entirely fulfilled; so that all sentient beings may accomplish the ten powers, adorned with an unobstructed, equal mind; so that all sentient beings may attain every buddha's inexhaustible light of the Dharma, every eloquence universally fulfilled; so that all sentient beings may attain the unsurpassed, fearless lion's roar of a hero among people; so that all sentient beings may attain all-wisdom, turning the inexhaustible, never-regressing wheel of the Dharma; so that all sentient beings may comprehend every dharma, revealing and expounding it, universally fulfilled; so that all sentient beings may cultivate pure and wholesome dharmas at the right time, universally fulfilled; so that all sentient beings may accomplish the guide's unsurpassed jewel of the Dharma, equally pure; so that all sentient beings may be universally fulfilled in a single adornment, immeasurable adornments, great adornments, and the adornments of every buddha; so that all sentient beings may equally enter every domain of the three times, pervading it entirely; so that all sentient beings may all be able to journey to every buddha-land, listening to and receiving the true Dharma everywhere without exception; so that all sentient beings' wisdom and benefit may be revered by the world, equal to a buddha's own; so that all sentient beings, with all-wisdom, may know every dharma, universally fulfilled; so that all sentient beings may practice unshakable karma, attaining an unobstructed fruit, universally fulfilled; so that all sentient beings' every faculty may all attain spiritual powers, able to know the faculties of every sentient being; so that all sentient beings may attain undifferentiated, equal wisdom, universally pure regarding the single-mark dharma; so that all sentient beings may accord without contradiction with the true principle, every wholesome root entirely complete; so that all sentient beings may become entirely clear and proficient in every bodhisattva's sovereign spiritual power.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

"Words that are true and sincere, entirely worthy of trust" sits inside a much longer clause about the buddha's sixty-fold voice, but it names something the whole litany has been doing all along: this entire stretch of the seventh dedication is itself a list of trustworthy speech, each clause a small promise about what the bodhisattva's dedication is actually for.
