Twenty-One Minds

Twenty-one similes name the exact mind Good-Wealth should bring to serving any good spiritual friend — earth, vajra, a mountain, a servant, a bridge, and, twice over, the very outcastes his own society looked down on.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1266 — fascicle 77 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Twenty-One Minds

Where the last Part gave ten images of what a good spiritual friend is, this one gives twenty-one images of what the student's own mind should become — most of them animals, tools, and laborers, and two of them figures society ranked at the very bottom.

Chinese Source

「復次，善男子！汝承事一切善知識，應發如大地心，荷負重任無疲倦故；應發如金剛心，志願堅固不可壞故；應發如鐵圍山心，一切諸苦無能動故；應發如給侍心，所有教令皆隨順故；應發如弟子心，所有訓誨無違逆故；應發如僮僕心，不厭一切諸作務故；應發如養母心，受諸勤苦不告勞故；應發如傭作心，隨所受教無違逆故；應發如除糞人心，離憍慢故；應發如已熟稼心，能低下故；應發如良馬心，離惡性故；應發如大車心，能運重故；應發如調順象心，恒伏從故；應發如須彌山心，不傾動故；應發如良犬心，不害主故；應發如旃荼羅心，離憍慢故；應發如犗牛心，無威怒故；應發如舟船心，往來不倦故；應發如橋梁心，濟渡忘疲故；應發如孝子心，承順顏色故；應發如王子心，遵行教命故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Moreover, good son! In serving every good spiritual friend, you should give rise to a mind like the earth, because it bears every heavy burden without ever growing weary; a mind like vajra, because its resolve is firm and cannot be destroyed; a mind like Cakravāḍa Mountain, because no suffering whatsoever can move it; a mind like an attendant, because it follows every instruction given; a mind like a disciple, because it never contradicts any teaching received; a mind like a servant, because it never tires of any task; a mind like a foster-mother, because it endures every hardship without ever complaining of fatigue; a mind like a hired laborer, because it never contradicts whatever instruction it receives; a mind like a remover of filth, because it is free of arrogance; a mind like ripened grain, because it is able to bow low; a mind like a good horse, because it is free of a vicious nature; a mind like a great cart, because it is able to carry heavy loads; a mind like a well-tamed elephant, because it forever submits and follows; a mind like Sumeru, because it never tilts or wavers; a mind like a good dog, because it never harms its master; a mind like a caṇḍāla, because it is free of arrogance; a mind like a gelded ox, because it is without fierce anger; a mind like a boat, because it goes back and forth without ever tiring; a mind like a bridge, because it carries others across, forgetting its own fatigue; a mind like a filial son, because it reads and follows its parent's every expression; a mind like a prince, because it complies with and carries out every royal command."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

應發如除糞人心，離憍慢故⋯應發如旃荼羅心，離憍慢故 — a mind like a remover of filth, because it is free of arrogance⋯a mind like a caṇḍāla, because it is free of arrogance. Twenty-one minds in all, counted directly from the source text. Two of them share the identical reason, word for word — and both are drawn from the two lowest, most despised classes of labor and birth in the very society this sūtra was composed within. The text does not soften this: it holds up exactly the figures its own world scorned as the model for how to regard a teacher.
