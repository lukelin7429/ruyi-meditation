Like the Ocean, Like Mount Sumeru

Untouched by the wealth surrounding him, Good-Wealth finds King Great Light seated at a crossroads on a throne of wish-fulfilling maṇi-lotus, described through nine similes that move from a mountain of gold to an island filled entirely with the jewels of wisdom.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1021 — like the ocean, like Mount Sumeru Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Untouched, and Single-Pointed

Surrounded by wealth beyond counting, Good-Wealth's own mind remains free of attachment to any of it — his single desire is simply to see his good spiritual friend, and that desire alone leads him to the crossroads where King Great Light sits.

Chinese Source

爾時，善財童子於此一切珍寶妙物，乃至男女、六塵境界，皆無愛著，但正思惟究竟之法，一心願樂見善知識。漸次遊行，見大光王去於所住樓閣不遠四衢道中，坐如意摩尼寶蓮華藏廣大莊嚴師子之座，紺瑠璃寶以為其足，金繒為帳，眾寶為網，上妙天衣以為茵蓐。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Good-Wealth, amid all these wondrous jeweled things, and even amid every realm of the six sense-objects — men and women included — held no attachment to any of it, but rightly reflected only on the ultimate dharma, his mind single-pointed and joyfully longing to see his good spiritual friend. Journeying onward by stages, he saw King Great Light, not far from the tower where he dwelt, seated at a crossroads upon a vast and magnificently adorned lion-throne formed from a treasury of wish-fulfilling maṇi-lotuses, its legs made of dark blue lapis lazuli, its canopy of golden silk, its net of every kind of jewel, and its cushion of the finest heavenly garments.

Nine Similes for a King

The king's own body, marked with the thirty-two and eighty marks of a great being, is described through nine similes in succession — mountain, sun, moon, Brahmā, ocean, snow mountain, cloud, sky, and Sumeru — before settling on the image of an island filled entirely with jewels of wisdom.

Chinese Source

其王於上結跏趺坐，二十八種大人之相、八十隨好而以嚴身；如真金山，光色熾盛；如淨空日，威光赫奕；如盛滿月，見者清涼；如梵天王，處於梵眾；亦如大海，功德法寶無有邊際；亦如雪山，相好樹林以為嚴飾；亦如大雲，能震法雷，啟悟群品；亦如虛空，顯現種種法門星象；如須彌山，四色普現眾生心海；亦如寶洲，種種智寶充滿其中。

Ru-Yi Translation

The king sat cross-legged upon it, his body adorned with the twenty-eight marks of a great being and the eighty minor marks; like a mountain of true gold, his radiance blazed forth; like the sun in a clear sky, his majestic light shone brilliantly; like the full moon, whoever saw him felt cooled and refreshed; like the Brahmā king amid his own Brahmā-host; also like the great ocean, his treasury of meritorious dharma without any bound; also like the snow mountain, his excellent marks adorning him like a forest; also like a great cloud, able to thunder forth the dharma and awaken every kind of being; also like empty space, manifesting every kind of dharma-gate like constellations of stars; like Mount Sumeru, its four colors universally reflected in the ocean of every being's own mind; and also like an island of jewels, filled entirely with every kind of jewel of wisdom.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

二十八種大人之相 — the twenty-eight marks of a great being. Where most descriptions of a buddha's own body in this sūtra name the standard thirty-two marks, the Chinese source gives this king twenty-eight — a small but deliberate variation, keeping his splendor visibly short of a buddha's own, however dazzling the nine similes that follow.
