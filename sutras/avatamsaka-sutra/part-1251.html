Every Worldly Art

The youth's own humility formula turns out to be the most worldly catalogue this journey has offered — writing, medicine, exorcism, gemology, geography, and statecraft — all of it named as only a fraction of what a true bodhisattva masters.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1251 — fascicle 76 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Every Worldly Art

No prior humility formula in this journey has reached this far into the concrete and the everyday — healing, treasure, geography, weather, harvest, and statecraft all named in a single breath before the youth ever gets to sending Good-Wealth onward.

Chinese Source

「善男子！我唯知此善知眾藝菩薩解脫。如諸菩薩摩訶薩，能於一切世、出世間善巧之法，以智通達到於彼岸；殊方異藝，咸綜無遺；文字、算數，蘊其深解；醫方、呪術，善療眾病；有諸眾生，鬼魅所持，怨憎呪詛，惡星變怪，死屍奔逐，癲癇、羸瘦，種種諸疾，咸能救之，使得痊愈；又善別知金玉、珠貝、珊瑚、瑠璃、摩尼、硨磲、雞薩羅等一切寶藏，出生之處，品類不同，價直多少；村營鄉邑、大小都城、宮殿苑園、巖泉藪澤，凡是一切人眾所居，菩薩咸能隨方攝護；又善觀察天文地理、人相吉凶、鳥獸音聲、雲霞氣候、年穀豐儉、國土安危，如是世間所有技藝，莫不該練，盡其源本；又能分別出世之法，正名辨義，觀察體相，隨順修行，智入其中，無疑、無礙、無愚暗、無頑鈍、無憂惱、無沈沒、無不現證。而我云何能知能說彼功德行？「善男子！此摩竭提國，有一聚落，彼中有城，名：婆呾那；有優婆夷，號曰：賢勝。汝詣彼問：菩薩云何學菩薩行、修菩薩道？」時，善財童子頭面敬禮知藝之足，遶無數匝，戀仰辭去。向聚落城，至賢勝所，禮足圍遶，合掌恭敬，於一面立，白言：「聖者！我已先發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，而未知菩薩云何學菩薩行？云何修菩薩道？我聞聖者善能誘誨，願為我說！」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son! I know only this bodhisattva's own liberation-gate of Skilled in Every Art. As for the bodhisattva-mahāsattvas, who are able, through wisdom, to penetrate every skillful worldly and transcendent-worldly dharma all the way to its far shore; who master every extraordinary art from every distant land, without a single one left out; who hold a profound understanding of writing and arithmetic; who are skilled in medicine and incantation, healing every kind of illness; who are able to rescue and bring to full recovery every being possessed by ghosts or demons, cursed by hatred and malediction, afflicted by evil stars and strange calamities, pursued by a wandering corpse, or suffering from epilepsy, wasting sickness, and every other kind of disease; who are, moreover, skilled in distinguishing gold and jade, pearls and shells, coral, lapis lazuli, maṇi-jewels, mother-of-pearl, and kesara-gems, together with every kind of treasure — knowing where each is found, its every distinct grade, and its exact worth; who can, throughout every village and hamlet, every great and small city, every palace and garden, every crag and spring and marsh and moor — wherever any multitude of people dwells — protect and provide for them according to what each place requires; who are, moreover, skilled in observing astronomy and geography, the fortunate and unfortunate signs of the human countenance, the cries of birds and beasts, the clouds and mists and weather, the abundance or scarcity of the year's own harvest, and the safety or peril of every land — mastering every worldly art such as these, down to its very root and source; and who are, moreover, able to discern the transcendent-worldly dharma, rightly naming it and distinguishing its meaning, contemplating its essence and characteristics, cultivating it in accordance with itself, their wisdom entering into it entirely — free of doubt, free of obstruction, free of ignorance's darkness, free of dullness, free of anguish, free of drowning, and with nothing at all left unrealized — how could I possibly know, or possibly speak, of their own virtuous practice? Good son! In this land of Magadha, there is a settlement, and within it a city, named Vatana; there is a laywoman there, called Worthy Superior. Go to her, and ask: how does a bodhisattva study the bodhisattva's own practice, and cultivate the bodhisattva's own path?" At that time, Good-Wealth bowed his head in reverence at the feet of Skilled in Every Art, circled him countless times, gazed up at him with longing and devotion, and took his leave. He made his way to that settlement's city, arrived at Worthy Superior's own place, bowed at her feet, circled her, stood reverently to one side with his palms joined, and said: "Noble one! I have already given rise to the mind of anuttara-samyak-sambodhi, yet I do not yet know how a bodhisattva studies the bodhisattva's own practice, or how one cultivates the bodhisattva's own path. I have heard that you, noble one, are skillfully able to guide and instruct — I beg you, teach me!"

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

又善別知金玉、珠貝、珊瑚、瑠璃、摩尼、硨磲、雞薩羅等一切寶藏 — who are, moreover, skilled in distinguishing gold and jade, pearls and shells, coral, lapis lazuli, maṇi-jewels, mother-of-pearl, and kesara-gems, together with every kind of treasure. Every humility formula so far in this journey has praised bodhisattvas for spiritual attainments beyond the speaker's own reach; this one, alone, praises them for knowing the going price of a pearl.
