Indescribably Indescribable

The offering litany continues through this sūtra's classic ten-term escalation of scale — immeasurable, boundless, without equal, uncountable, inexpressible, inconceivable, unmeasurable, indescribable, and finally indescribably indescribable — applied first to flowers, garlands, and perfumed unguents, and finally to an extensive series of maṇi-jewel offerings whose every category, down to the palace itself, exceeds description twice over.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 248 — escalating numerical qualifiers, jeweled and maṇi offerings Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Ladder of Superlatives

Each material in this passage receives a distinct rung on the same ladder of scale this sūtra applies elsewhere to kalpas and lifetimes — immeasurable, boundless, without equal, uncountable, inexpressible, inconceivable, unmeasurable, indescribable — climbing one step higher with every new substance, until maṇi-jewel offerings arrive at the ladder's final and highest rung, indescribably indescribable, repeated across an entire catalogue of forms from banner to palace.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The device "speaking extensively, up to" is the sūtra's own shorthand, not a summary imposed on it — the text itself signals that the full nineteen-item sequence established earlier (canopy through palace) repeats identically for each material, and abbreviates by naming only the first and last term. Rendering this device transparently, rather than silently expanding or silently compressing it further, preserves both the passage's actual content and the specific literary technique the sūtra uses to manage its own scale.
