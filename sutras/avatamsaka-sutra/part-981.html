The Universal Adornment Gate of Prajñāpāramitā

Loving-Conduct names her liberation and its source at once — thirty-six Ganges' sands of buddhas, each one teaching her through an entirely different gate, so that nothing any single buddha taught was ever repeated by another — before beginning to describe what entering this gate has actually opened up.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 981 — the Universal Adornment Gate of Prajñāpāramitā Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Thirty-Six Ganges' Sands, Never Repeating

Loving-Conduct's own claim is remarkable in its precision — not simply that many buddhas taught her, but that each one used a completely different gate, so that the sum of their teaching, rather than any single repeated lesson, is what actually built this liberation.

Chinese Source

爾時，童女告善財言：「善男子！此是般若波羅蜜普莊嚴門，我於三十六恒河沙佛所求得此法。彼諸如來各以異門，令我入此般若波羅蜜普莊嚴門；一佛所演，餘不重說。」善財白言：「聖者！此般若波羅蜜普莊嚴門境界云何？」童女答言：「善男子！我入此般若波羅蜜普莊嚴門，隨順趣向，思惟觀察，憶持分別時得普門陀羅尼，百萬阿僧祇陀羅尼門皆悉現前。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, the maiden said to Good-Wealth: "Good son, this is the Universal Adornment Gate of Prajñāpāramitā; I sought and attained this dharma at the places of thirty-six Ganges' sands of buddhas. Each of those tathāgatas, through a different gate, caused me to enter this Universal Adornment Gate of Prajñāpāramitā; what one buddha expounded, none of the others repeated." Good-Wealth said: "Noble one, what is the state of this Universal Adornment Gate of Prajñāpāramitā?" The maiden replied: "Good son, when I enter this Universal Adornment Gate of Prajñāpāramitā, following and moving toward it, reflecting upon and contemplating it, calling it to mind, upholding, and discerning it, I attain the dhāraṇī of the universal gate, and a hundred million asaṃkhyeyas of dhāraṇī-gates all appear before me entirely.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

一佛所演，餘不重說 — what one buddha expounded, none of the others repeated. This detail sets Loving-Conduct apart from Rest-and-Release, who recalled thirty-six Ganges' sands of buddhas served in fascicle sixty-four without specifying what made each encounter distinct. Here, non-repetition itself is named as part of the teaching.
