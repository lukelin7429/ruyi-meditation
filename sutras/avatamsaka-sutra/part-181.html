The Same Throne, in Every World

The sūtra steps back from its own description to make an explicit claim: the throne just described in such overwhelming detail is not unique to this world — every Tuṣita heavenly king, in every world there is, prepared exactly this same throne, in exactly this same way, down to the very feeling of reverence involved. The King of Tuṣita then goes forth, with countless heavenly sons and maidens, to welcome the Tathāgata in person.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 181 — the throne prepared identically everywhere, and the welcome begins Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Not One Throne, but Every Throne Alike

After seven Parts describing this single throne down to its windows and its fragrances, the sūtra reveals what the description was actually about: not a singular, unrepeatable object, but a pattern repeated identically by every Tuṣita heavenly king in every world — the same throne, the same adornment, the same reverence, "all entirely the same." The King of Tuṣita then leads the welcome in person, joined by an assembly raining down clouds of offering from their own bodies.

Chinese Source

如此世界兜率天王，奉為如來，敷置高座；一切世界兜率天王，悉為於佛，如是敷座，如是莊嚴，如是儀則，如是信樂，如是心淨，如是欣樂，如是喜悅，如是尊重，如是而生希有之想，如是踊躍，如是渴仰，悉皆同等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Just as the king of the Tuṣita Heaven in this very world reverently prepared this lofty throne for the Tathāgata, so too did the kings of the Tuṣita Heaven in every world, all alike for the sake of the Buddha, spread out just such a throne, adorn it in just such a way, follow just such a ceremony, hold just such faith and delight, purify their mind in just such a way, feel just such joy, just such happiness, just such reverence, give rise to just such a sense of how rare this is, feel just such exhilaration, and hold just such thirsting reverence — every one of them entirely the same.

Chinese Source

爾時，兜率天王為如來敷置座已，心生尊重，與十萬億阿僧祇兜率天子奉迎如來；以清淨心，雨阿僧祇色華雲，雨不思議色香雲，雨種種色鬘雲，雨廣大清淨栴檀雲，雨無量種種蓋雲，雨細妙天衣雲，雨無邊眾妙寶雲，雨天莊嚴具雲，雨無量種種燒香雲，雨一切栴檀沈水堅固末香雲。諸天子眾各從其身出此諸雲時，百千億阿僧祇兜率天子，及餘在會諸天子，眾心大歡喜，恭敬頂禮；阿僧祇天女，踊躍欣慕，諦觀如來。兜率宮中不可說諸菩薩眾，住虛空中，精勤一心，以出過諸天諸供養具，供養於佛，恭敬作禮。阿僧祇音樂一時同奏。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, the King of the Tuṣita Heaven, having already prepared this throne for the Tathāgata, gave rise to a reverent mind, and together with a hundred thousand million asaṃkhyeyas of Tuṣita heavenly sons, went forth to welcome the Tathāgata; with a pure mind, they rained clouds of asaṃkhyeya-colored flowers, rained clouds of inconceivably colored fragrance, rained clouds of garlands of every color, rained clouds of vast and pure sandalwood, rained clouds of immeasurable and various canopies, rained clouds of fine and wondrous heavenly garments, rained clouds of boundless wondrous jewels, rained clouds of heavenly adorning implements, rained clouds of immeasurable and various burning incense, and rained clouds of every kind of sandalwood and firm aloeswood powdered incense. As every one of these heavenly sons manifested these very clouds each from their own body, a hundred thousand million asaṃkhyeyas of Tuṣita heavenly sons, together with every other heavenly son present in the assembly, their minds all greatly rejoicing, reverently bowed their heads in homage; asaṃkhyeya heavenly maidens, leaping with joyful longing, gazed intently upon the Tathāgata. Within the Tuṣita palace, indescribable multitudes of bodhisattvas, abiding within empty space, diligently and single-mindedly brought forth offerings surpassing every heavenly one, making offerings to the Buddha, and reverently paying homage. Asaṃkhyeya kinds of music played together all at once.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

"All entirely the same" is the sūtra's own explicit statement of what its earlier chapters showed by demonstration — that this world's own experience of the Buddha's teaching is not privileged or unique but identically mirrored across every world there is. What makes the offering here distinct is where it comes from: the heavenly sons "manifested these very clouds each from their own body," so the flowers and fragrances raining down are not fetched from somewhere but generated directly from the very beings making the offering.
