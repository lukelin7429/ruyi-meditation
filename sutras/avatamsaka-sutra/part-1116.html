The Entertainer Named Lovely Face

She has been a wheel-turning king, an asura king, a sage with sixty thousand followers. For the last buddha of that world she is a singer in the street — and that is the encounter that ends with a light from between his brows.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1116 — the last buddha of that world Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Song and Dance in the Street

The catalogue skips to its end, and the body she is wearing for the last of the five hundred is the lowest-ranking one in the whole account.

Chinese Source

善男子！如是次第，其最後佛，名：充滿虛空法界妙德燈；我為妓女，名曰：美顏，見佛入城，歌舞供養；承佛神力，踊在空中，以千偈頌讚歎於佛；

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son, and so in due succession, until the last of those buddhas, named Lamp of Wondrous Virtue Filling Empty Space and the Dharma-Realm. I was a female entertainer, named Lovely Face. Seeing the Buddha enter the city, I made an offering of song and dance; and sustained by the Buddha's spiritual power I leapt up into the sky and praised the Buddha in a thousand verses;

Chinese Source

佛為於我，放眉間光，名：莊嚴法界大光明，遍觸我身；我蒙光已，即得解脫門，名：法界方便不退藏。

Ru-Yi Translation

and the Buddha, for my sake, sent out a light from between his brows, named Great Light Adorning the Dharma-Realm, which touched my body all over. Having received that light, I straightaway attained a liberation-gate named Treasury of Non-Regression in the Skilful Means of the Dharma-Realm.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

我為妓女，名曰：美顏，見佛入城，歌舞供養 — I was a female entertainer, named Lovely Face; seeing the Buddha enter the city, I made an offering of song and dance. The word 妓 in Tang usage means a woman who performs — a singer and dancer — and the act described is exactly that. Set at the end of a list in which she was twice a wheel-turning king, an asura king, and a sage with sixty thousand followers, the entertainer is the last and least-ranked of the bodies, and she is given a name about her looks. She makes the only offering she has, which is her craft, performed in the road as a procession goes past.

佛為於我，放眉間光 — the Buddha, for my sake, sent out a light from between his brows. The phrase 眉間 occurs three times in fascicle seventy and the three make a circuit. In Part 1101 this night-spirit sent a light 於兩眉間 into Good-Wealth's crown; in Part 1103 he praised her for it, 見尊眉間相，放大清淨光; and here, a very long time ago, a buddha did it to her because she sang in the street. What she does to her pilgrim is what was once done to her, and the sūtra puts the original at the bottom of the social scale.

以千偈頌讚歎於佛 — I praised the Buddha in a thousand verses. Good-Wealth's praise of her in Part 1103 ran to twenty-one stanzas. A professional singer produces a thousand. The detail is not a boast; it is simply what a person with that training does when moved, and it is the reason the light came.

Not One Letter Forgotten

The whole world is then summed up in a sentence, and what she claims for herself is not attainment but memory.

Chinese Source

「善男子！此世界中，有如是等佛剎微塵數劫，一切如來於中出現；我皆承事，恭敬供養；彼諸如來所說正法，我皆憶念，乃至不忘一文一句。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, in this world there have been eons such as these, as numerous as the fine motes of dust of a buddha-land, and in them every tathāgata appeared. I attended upon them all and made offerings to them reverently; and of the true dharma those tathāgatas spoke, I kept every word in mind, not forgetting so much as a single letter or a single phrase.

Chinese Source

於彼一一諸如來所，稱揚讚歎一切佛法，為無量眾生廣作利益；於彼一一諸如來所，得一切智光明，現三世法界海，入一切普賢行。

Ru-Yi Translation

At the place of each single one of those tathāgatas I extolled and praised every buddha-dharma and worked wide benefit for measureless beings; and at the place of each single one of those tathāgatas I attained the light of all-knowing wisdom, made manifest the ocean of the dharma-realm of the three times, and entered into every practice of Samantabhadra.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

乃至不忘一文一句 — not forgetting so much as a single letter or a single phrase. After a catalogue of bodies and buddhas running over motes-of-dust-many eons, the achievement she names is retention. Not insight, not power, not merit — that she has not lost a word. For a tradition whose scriptures were carried in memory for centuries before they were written down, this is not a modest claim dressed up as a large one. It is the large claim stated plainly.

The clause 於彼一一諸如來所, at the place of each single one of those tathāgatas, is used twice in a single sentence, and the two halves it governs are not the same kind of thing. The first is what she did for others — extolling the dharma, benefiting beings. The second is what she got — the light of all-knowing wisdom, the ocean of the three times, Samantabhadra's practices. The sūtra puts giving before receiving in the syntax and declines to say that one caused the other.

Because of the Light

Her prose answer closes on a circle. She is asked nothing, and answers anyway, and the answer is the same as the premise.

Chinese Source

「善男子！我依一切智光明故，於念念中見無量佛；既見佛已，先所未得、先所未見普賢諸行，悉得成滿。何以故？以得一切智光明故。」爾時，普救眾生夜神，欲重明此解脫義，承佛神力，為善財童子而說頌言：

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, because I rely upon the light of all-knowing wisdom, in thought after thought I see measureless buddhas; and having seen those buddhas, the practices of Samantabhadra that I had not previously attained and had not previously seen were all of them brought to fulfilment. Why is that? Because I have attained the light of all-knowing wisdom." At that time the night-spirit Who Universally Rescues Beings, wishing to make the meaning of this liberation clear once more, sustained by the Buddha's spiritual power, spoke verses for Good-Wealth, saying:

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

我依一切智光明故 … 何以故？以得一切智光明故 — because I rely upon the light of all-knowing wisdom … Why is that? Because I have attained the light of all-knowing wisdom. The phrase occurs three times in four lines and the explanation is identical to the thing it explains. Read as argument this is a circle. Read as it is meant, it is a refusal: there is nothing underneath this to appeal to, and the question 何以故 has run out of places to go. The sūtra uses the same rhetorical move whenever it reaches a floor.

先所未得、先所未見普賢諸行，悉得成滿 — the practices of Samantabhadra that I had not previously attained and had not previously seen were all of them brought to fulfilment. Samantabhadra has been in this account from the beginning: he told her to mend a statue, he outshone her city, his vows were what she contemplated, his body held every bodhisattva she saw. Her attainment is stated, at the very end, as his practices completing themselves in her. She never claims a practice of her own.

Part 1116 Complete

This continues chapter thirty-nine, 入法界品, in fascicle seventy. Part 1117 begins her closing verses, which open by telling Good-Wealth to listen, and then return to the very beginning — how she first gave rise to the mind of awakening, and what she sought when she did.
