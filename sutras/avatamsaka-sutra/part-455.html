Great Lands Made Small at a Thought

Fascicle thirty-eight opens as the deva kings, who have been listening for seven grounds, rain down offerings and sing what they have understood. Then Vimuktacandra asks for the eighth, and Vajragarbha's answer arrives at the hinge of the whole chapter — the forbearance of the non-arising of dharmas.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 455 — the devas' praise, the request, and the forbearance attained Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Fascicle Thirty-Eight Opens

The Chinese heading, as CBETA gives it, carries both the fascicle line and the chapter's fifth division; the eighth ground is named at once, before a word of it is spoken.

Chinese Source

大方廣佛華嚴經卷第三十八
于闐國三藏實叉難陀奉制譯

Ru-Yi Translation

The Great and Vast Buddha's Flower Ornament Sūtra, Fascicle Thirty-Eight
Translated by imperial command by Śikṣānanda, Tripiṭaka Master of the Kingdom of Khotan
Chapter Twenty-Six, The Ten Grounds, Part Five
The Eighth Ground

What the Devas Sing

Their offering comes first — flowers, banners, canopies, incense, garlands, jewelled robes, all set with maṇi — and then a heavenly orchestra, and then the verse. What they praise is not the seven grounds just expounded but the spiritual power they have seen while listening.

Chinese Source

是時天王及天眾，聞此勝行皆歡喜，為欲供養於如來，及以無央大菩薩，
雨妙華幡及幢蓋，香鬘瓔珞與寶衣，無量無邊千萬種，悉以摩尼作嚴飾。
天女同時奏天樂，普發種種妙音聲，供養於佛并佛子，共作是言而讚歎：
「一切見者兩足尊，哀愍眾生現神力，令此種種諸天樂，普發妙音咸得聞。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the deva kings and the deva multitudes, hearing of these surpassing practices, all rejoiced; and wishing to make offerings to the Tathāgata and to the numberless great bodhisattvas, they rained down fine flowers and pennants and banners and canopies, fragrant garlands and necklaces and jewelled robes, measureless and boundless, in thousands upon ten-thousands of kinds, all of them adorned and set with maṇi. The daughters of the devas at the same moment struck up celestial music, sending forth everywhere every kind of wondrous sound, making offerings to the Buddha and to the Buddha's children, and together they spoke these words in praise:
"Honored One of Two Feet, seer of all things, in pity for beings you display spiritual power, causing these many kinds of celestial music to send forth wondrous sound everywhere, so that all may hear.

Chinese Source

於一毛端百千億，那由他國微塵數，如是無量諸如來，於中安住說妙法。
一毛孔內無量剎，各有四洲及大海，須彌鐵圍亦復然，悉見在中無迫隘。
一毛端處有六趣，三種惡道及人天，諸龍神眾阿脩羅，各隨自業受果報。
於彼一切剎土中，悉有如來演妙音，隨順一切眾生心，為轉最上淨法輪。

Ru-Yi Translation

At the tip of a single hair, a hundred thousand koṭis — nayutas of lands, as many as their dust-motes — and measureless Tathāgatas such as these abide within it, expounding the wondrous Dharma. Within a single pore are measureless lands, each with its four continents and its great oceans, and Sumeru and the encircling mountains of iron likewise, all seen within it and none of them crowded. At the tip of a single hair are the six destinies, the three evil paths and humans and devas, the hosts of nāgas and spirits and the asuras, each receiving the fruit of their own deeds. And within all of those lands there is a Tathāgata setting forth the wondrous sound, according with the minds of all beings, turning for them the supreme and purest Dharma-wheel.

Chinese Source

剎中種種眾生身，身中復有種種剎，人天諸趣各各異，佛悉知已為說法。
大剎隨念變為小，小剎隨念亦變大，如是神通無有量，世間共說不能盡。」
普發此等妙音聲，稱讚如來功德已，眾會歡喜默然住，一心瞻仰欲聽說。
時解脫月復請言：「今此眾會皆寂靜，願說隨次之所入，第八地中諸行相！」

Ru-Yi Translation

Within those lands are the bodies of every kind of being, and within those bodies again are lands of every kind; humans and devas and all the destinies, each one different — the Buddha, knowing them all, expounds the Dharma for them. A great land, at a thought, is transformed into a small one; a small land, at a thought, is transformed into a great one; spiritual power such as this is without measure, and all the world speaking together could not exhaust it." Having sent forth everywhere such wondrous sounds as these, and having praised the Tathāgata's merits, the assembly rejoiced and remained silent, gazing up single-mindedly, wishing to hear what would be said. Then Vimuktacandra asked again, saying: "Now this assembly is wholly at rest — may you expound what is entered next in order, the aspects of practice within the eighth ground!"

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

悉見在中無迫隘 — all seen within it and none of them crowded. 迫隘 is a word for the pressure of too many things in too small a space, and its denial is what makes the whole hair-tip image work. The claim is not that the lands are miniaturized but that smallness never became a constraint in the first place.

剎中種種眾生身，身中復有種種剎 — within lands are bodies, and within bodies again are lands. The devas have understood the containment as running both ways, and this is precisely what earns their praise a place here: what they sing is the eighth ground's own mode of seeing, arriving in the mouths of onlookers before the ground itself is taught.

The Forbearance of Non-Arising

Vajragarbha's answer begins not with the eighth ground but with the seventh brought to completion, and then runs in a single unbroken sentence to the attainment that makes the eighth possible.

Chinese Source

爾時，金剛藏菩薩告解脫月菩薩言：「佛子！菩薩摩訶薩於七地中，善修習方便慧，善清淨諸道，善集助道法。大願力所攝，如來力所加，自善力所持，常念如來力、無所畏、不共佛法，善清淨深心思覺，能成就福德智慧，大慈大悲不捨眾生，入無量智道，入一切法，本來無生、無起、無相、無成、無壞、無盡、無轉、無性為性，初、中、後際皆悉平等，無分別如如智之所入處，離一切心、意、識分別想，無所取著猶如虛空，入一切法如虛空性，是名：得無生法忍。

Ru-Yi Translation

Then Vajragarbha Bodhisattva said to Vimuktacandra Bodhisattva: "Child of the Buddha! A bodhisattva mahāsattva, within the seventh ground, has well cultivated skillful means and wisdom, has well purified all the paths, and has well gathered the dharmas that aid the path. Held by the power of great vows, supported by the power of the Tathāgata, upheld by the power of their own goodness; constantly recollecting the Tathāgata's powers, fearlessnesses, and unshared buddha-dharmas; their profound mind's thought and awareness well purified; able to bring merit and wisdom to accomplishment; with great loving-kindness and great compassion not forsaking beings; entering the path of measureless wisdom; entering all dharmas as originally unarisen, without origination, without characteristics, without formation, without destruction, without exhaustion, without turning, and having no nature for their nature; the beginning, the middle and the end alike being entirely the same; that place which the undiscriminating wisdom of suchness enters; apart from all the discriminating notions of mind, thought and consciousness; grasping at nothing, like open space; entering all dharmas as being of the nature of open space — this is called attaining the forbearance of the non-arising of dharmas.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

無性為性 — having no nature for their nature. The phrase is deliberately self-consuming, and Chinese lets it stand as four characters where English needs a small paradox to carry it. It is not that dharmas lack a nature and that is the end of the matter; the lack is what functions as the nature.

無生法忍 is rendered here as the forbearance of the non-arising of dharmas, keeping 忍 as forbearance rather than the commoner "acceptance". The character has carried patience under injury all through this chapter — the third of the pāramitās, and the 順忍 of the sixth ground — and the continuity matters: what is borne here is not an insult but the fact that nothing has ever arisen.

Part 455 Complete

This opens Acalā, the Immovable Ground, the eighth of the Ten Grounds, in fascicle thirty-eight. Part 456 continues as the forbearance takes effect — the bodhisattva enters the eighth ground, where all effort of body, speech and mind falls away, and the buddhas of the ten directions must rouse them from a stillness in which, left to themselves, they would enter final quiescence and the Buddha's work would come to an end.
