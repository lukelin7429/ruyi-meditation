Pure Joy, Pure Equanimity, Four Immeasurables Complete

Ten kinds of pure joy move from giving rise to the mind of awakening to delighting in every austerity of the bodhisattva path. Ten kinds of pure equanimity then close the entire four-part sequence of loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity, ending on a bodhisattva who holds two very different kinds of sentient being with exactly the same patience, waiting for each one's own right moment.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 774 — pure joy, pure equanimity, four immeasurables complete Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Neither High Nor Low, Neither Early Nor Late

The final entries of pure equanimity name two very different beings — one whose faculties have already matured yet who cannot know the supreme dharma, one whom the bodhisattva has taught since past ages and who can only be tamed upon reaching the buddha-ground — and hold them with exactly the same patience, ranking neither above the other and waiting, without favor, for each one's own moment.

Ru-Yi Translation

"Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva-mahāsattva has ten kinds of pure joy. What are these ten? Namely: the pure joy of giving rise to the mind of awakening; the pure joy of giving up everything possessed; the pure joy of never rejecting sentient beings who violate precepts, but teaching and bringing them to accomplishment; the pure joy of being able to bear with sentient beings who commit evil, vowing to rescue and deliver them; the pure joy of giving up the body to seek the dharma without ever giving rise to regret; the pure joy of personally giving up sensual pleasure, forever delighting in the joy of the dharma; the pure joy of causing every sentient being to give up the pleasure of worldly livelihood, forever delighting in the joy of the dharma; the pure joy of seeing every buddha and revering and making offerings to them without ever growing weary, the dharma-realm equal throughout; the pure joy of causing every sentient being to delight in entering and emerging, with playful ease, from meditative absorption, liberation, and samādhi; and the pure joy of the mind delighting in fully carrying out every austerity in accordance with the bodhisattva path, realizing the sage's own still, unmoving, unsurpassed concentration and wisdom. These are the ten. If bodhisattvas abide within this dharma, they attain the tathāgata's own unsurpassed, vast, pure joy.

「佛子！菩薩摩訶薩有十種清淨捨。何等為十？所謂：一切眾生恭敬供養，不生愛著清淨捨；一切眾生輕慢毀辱，不生瞋恚清淨捨；常行世間，不為世間八法所染清淨捨；於法器眾生待時而化，於無法器亦不生嫌清淨捨；不求二乘學、無學法清淨捨；心常遠離一切欲樂、順煩惱法清淨捨；不歎二乘、厭離生死清淨捨；遠離一切世間語，非涅槃語、非離欲語、不順理語、惱亂他語、聲聞獨覺語，略說乃至一切障菩薩道語皆悉遠離清淨捨；或有眾生，根已成熟發生念慧而未能知最上之法，待時方化清淨捨；或有眾生，菩薩往昔已曾教化至於佛地方可調伏，彼亦待時清淨捨；菩薩摩訶薩於彼二人，無高無下，無取無捨，遠離一切種種分別，恒住正定，入如實法，心得堪忍清淨捨。是為十。若諸菩薩安住其中，則得如來無上廣大清淨捨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva-mahāsattva has ten kinds of pure equanimity. What are these ten? Namely: the pure equanimity of never giving rise to attachment even when every sentient being reveres and makes offerings; the pure equanimity of never giving rise to hatred even when every sentient being treats them with contempt and disgrace; the pure equanimity of constantly moving through the world without being stained by the eight worldly dharmas; the pure equanimity of waiting for the right time to transform sentient beings who are vessels for the dharma, while never once feeling aversion toward those who are not; the pure equanimity of never seeking the dharma of training or beyond training belonging to the two lesser vehicles; the pure equanimity of the mind forever staying far from every sensual pleasure and every dharma that accords with affliction; the pure equanimity of never praising the two lesser vehicles' own weariness of and departure from birth and death; the pure equanimity of staying far from every worldly speech — speech that is not about nirvāṇa, not about freedom from desire, not in accordance with principle, speech that troubles and disturbs others, the speech of śrāvakas and solitary awakened ones, and, in brief, every kind of speech that obstructs the bodhisattva path, staying entirely far from all of it; the pure equanimity of waiting for the right time to transform those sentient beings whose faculties have already matured, giving rise to mindfulness and wisdom, and yet who are not yet able to know the supreme dharma; and the pure equanimity of also waiting for the right time for those sentient beings whom the bodhisattva has already taught in past ages, who can only be tamed upon reaching the buddha-ground. A bodhisattva-mahāsattva, toward these two kinds of people, holds neither high regard nor low, neither grasping nor rejecting, staying far from every kind of discrimination, forever abiding in right concentration, entering the dharma exactly as it truly is, their mind attaining the pure equanimity of acceptance. These are the ten. If bodhisattvas abide within these, they attain the tathāgata's own unsurpassed, vast, pure equanimity.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

於彼二人，無高無下，無取無捨 — toward these two kinds of people, holds neither high regard nor low, neither grasping nor rejecting. The closing line of this entire four-part sequence resolves nothing by choosing between the two beings just described; it simply states that neither is ranked above the other, closing loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity together on a refusal to distinguish rather than on any final preference.
