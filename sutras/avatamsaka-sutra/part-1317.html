As One Who Sees in a Dream

The inventory of the tower pauses, and the sūtra explains how Good-Wealth is able to see all of it in every single object: through the power of recollection that never forgets, a pure eye that sees the ten directions, an unobstructed wisdom of observation, and the sovereign wisdom and vast understanding of the bodhisattvas. Then come two similes. A sleeper sees cities and palaces, mountains and rivers, parents and relatives, the great ocean and Mount Sumeru, even his own body grown to hundreds of thousands of yojanas, and on waking knows it was a dream yet remembers everything clearly. A person at the point of death, still alive, already sees the hells or the heavens that their own deeds have prepared.

As One Who Sees in a Dream

The powers behind Good-Wealth's seeing, and the first two of the similes by which the sūtra explains it — the dream and the moment before death.

Chinese Source

爾時，善財童子得不忘失憶念力故，得見十方清淨眼故，得善觀察無礙智故，得諸菩薩自在智故，得諸菩薩已入智地廣大解故，於一切樓閣一一物中，悉見如是及餘無量不可思議自在境界諸莊嚴事。譬如有人，於睡夢中見種種物，所謂：城邑、聚落、宮殿、園苑、山林、河池、衣服、飲食乃至一切資生之具；或見自身父母兄弟、內外親屬；或見大海須彌山王，乃至一切諸天宮殿、閻浮提等四天下事；或見其身形量廣大百千由旬，房舍、衣服悉皆相稱，謂於晝日經無量時不眠不寢受諸安樂。從睡覺已，乃知是夢，而能明記所見之事。善財童子亦復如是，以彌勒菩薩力所持故，知三界法皆如夢故，滅諸眾生狹劣想故，得無障礙廣大解故，住諸菩薩勝境界故，入不思議方便智故，能見如是自在境界。譬如有人，將欲命終，見隨其業所受報相：行惡業者，見於地獄、畜生、餓鬼所有一切眾苦境界，或見獄卒手持兵仗或瞋或罵囚執將去，亦聞號叫、悲歎之聲，或見灰河，或見鑊湯，或見刀山，或見劍樹，種種逼迫，受諸苦惱；作善業者，即見一切諸天宮殿無量天眾、天諸采女，種種衣服具足莊嚴，宮殿、園林盡皆妙好。身雖未死，而由業力見如是事。善財童子亦復如是，以菩薩業不思議力，得見一切莊嚴境界。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Good-Wealth the youth, because he had attained the power of recollection that never forgets, because he had attained the pure eye that sees the ten directions, because he had attained the unobstructed wisdom of skillful observation, because he had attained the sovereign wisdom of the bodhisattvas, and because he had attained the vast and great understanding of the bodhisattvas who have already entered the ground of wisdom, saw within each and every object of all the towers every such adornment as these, and measureless other inconceivable domains of sovereign freedom besides. It is just as when a person sees every kind of thing in a dream, that is to say, cities and villages, palaces and gardens, mountains and forests, rivers and pools, garments and food and drink, and even every necessity of life; or sees his own father and mother, brothers, and relatives near and distant; or sees the great ocean and Sumeru, king of mountains, and even the palaces of all the devas and the affairs of Jambudvīpa and the rest of the four continents; or sees his own body grown vast in size to hundreds of thousands of yojanas, with houses and garments all in proportion to it, and supposes that by day he has passed a measureless time without sleeping or lying down, enjoying every ease. Once he wakes from sleep he knows that it was a dream, and yet is able to recall clearly the things he saw. Good-Wealth the youth was likewise: because he was sustained by the power of Maitreya Bodhisattva, because he knew that the dharmas of the three realms are all like a dream, because he had extinguished the narrow and inferior notions of sentient beings, because he had attained an unobstructed, vast, and great understanding, because he abided in the supreme domain of the bodhisattvas, and because he had entered the inconceivable wisdom of skillful means, he was able to see such domains of sovereign freedom as these. It is just as when a person about to die sees the signs of the retribution they are to receive according to their karma. One who has done evil deeds sees every domain of suffering that belongs to the hells, the animals, and the hungry ghosts; or sees the hell-wardens, weapons in hand, glaring or cursing as they seize and drag them away; and hears also the sounds of howling and lamentation; or sees the river of ashes, or sees the cauldrons of boiling water, or sees the mountain of knives, or sees the sword-trees, pressed on every side and undergoing every torment. One who has done good deeds at once sees the palaces of all the devas, measureless hosts of devas and celestial maidens, fully adorned with every kind of garment, and palaces and gardens all wondrously fine. Though their body has not yet died, through the power of karma they see such things as these. Good-Wealth the youth was likewise: through the inconceivable power of a bodhisattva's karma, he was able to see every domain of adornment.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

故 — the two sentences that frame the first simile are each built on a chain of reasons ending in 故, because. The opening sentence gives five of them, each a power or understanding Good-Wealth has attained, before it reaches its verb, 悉見, he saw all; the sentence that applies the dream to Good-Wealth gives six, the first of which is that he is sustained by Maitreya's power and the second that he knows the dharmas of the three realms to be like a dream. The translation keeps every because rather than folding the reasons into a list, so that the weight of each sentence falls, as in the source, on its final clause.

譬如有人 — with the dream and the deathbed the sūtra begins a run of similes for the manner of Good-Wealth's seeing, and these two are its first pair. Both turn on the same point: the things seen are not present in the ordinary way, the dream-city is not there and the hell or heaven has not yet been entered, and yet the seeing is exact, remembered clearly on waking in the one case and produced by the force of one's own karma in the other. In both similes the sūtra insists that the seer is wholly alive and awake to what is seen, 身雖未死, the body not yet dead, and 而能明記, able to recall it clearly.
