As the Dharma-Realm Is, So Too Is This Dedication

A hymn-like passage compares this dedication directly to ten qualities of the dharma-realm itself — immeasurable, boundless, unceasing, of a single nature, pure — each quality mirrored in what the dedication accomplishes for sentient beings. It closes with fourteen vows, each opening "may, through these wholesome roots," building toward a single wish: that every sentient being forever beholds every buddha, present before them, never once abandoned for even a moment.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 377 — the dedication mirrors the dharma-realm, and fourteen vows to behold every buddha Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Ten Qualities Mirrored

Each line follows the same pattern — as the dharma-realm is some quality, so too is this dedication of wholesome roots — pairing an abstract quality of ultimate reality with a concrete effect: because the dharma-realm is of a single nature, this dedication shares that single nature of wisdom with every sentient being; because the dharma-realm cannot be destroyed, this dedication causes every bodhisattva to never destroy any pure practice.

Ru-Yi Translation

"Child of the Buddha! This bodhisattva mahāsattva further dedicates his wholesome roots in this way, namely: 'As the dharma-realm is immeasurable, so too is this dedication of wholesome roots; the wisdom attained through it is, in the end, never measurable. As the dharma-realm is boundless, so too is this dedication of wholesome roots; beholding every buddha, there is no boundary to it. As the dharma-realm is without limit, so too is this dedication of wholesome roots; going forth to every buddha-land, there is no limit set upon it. As the dharma-realm is without border, so too is this dedication of wholesome roots; cultivating a bodhisattva's own practice throughout every world, there is no border to it. As the dharma-realm is unceasing, so too is this dedication of wholesome roots; abiding in omniscience, it is forever without cessation. As the dharma-realm is of a single nature, so too is this dedication of wholesome roots; it shares that same single nature of wisdom with every sentient being. As the dharma-realm's own nature is pure, so too is this dedication of wholesome roots; it causes every sentient being to attain ultimate purity. As the dharma-realm accords with all things, so too is this dedication of wholesome roots; it causes every sentient being to entirely accord with Samantabhadra's own practice and vow. As the dharma-realm is adorned, so too is this dedication of wholesome roots; it causes every sentient being to be adorned by Samantabhadra's own practice. As the dharma-realm cannot be destroyed, so too is this dedication of wholesome roots; it causes every bodhisattva to never destroy any pure practice.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This dedication takes its very name from the dharma-realm — "equal to the boundless dharma-realm" — and this passage makes that equality explicit, quality by quality, rather than leaving it as a title alone. Ten times over, a metaphysical fact about ultimate reality is made to justify a concrete promise about what the dedication does for sentient beings, as though the dharma-realm's own nature were itself the argument.

Fourteen Vows to Behold Every Buddha

The passage turns to a rapid sequence of vows, each opening "may, through these wholesome roots" — moving from the bodhisattva's own swift entry into omniscience to a repeated wish for every sentient being: that they forever behold every buddha, their minds joyful, pure, unattached, and never once regressing.

Ru-Yi Translation

"Child of the Buddha! This bodhisattva mahāsattva further dedicates these wholesome roots in this way, namely: 'May, through these wholesome roots, I serve every buddha and bodhisattva, causing them all to rejoice; may, through these wholesome roots, I swiftly attain entry into the nature of omniscience; may, through these wholesome roots, I cultivate omniscience everywhere, throughout every place; may, through these wholesome roots, I cause every sentient being to forever be able to go before and behold every buddha; may, through these wholesome roots, I cause every sentient being to forever behold every buddha, able to perform a buddha's own work; may, through these wholesome roots, I cause every sentient being to forever attain the beholding of a buddha, never giving rise to a mind of laziness or negligence regarding a buddha's own work; may, through these wholesome roots, I cause every sentient being to forever attain the beholding of a buddha, their minds joyful and pure, never regressing; may, through these wholesome roots, I cause every sentient being to forever attain the beholding of a buddha, their minds well able to comprehend; may, through these wholesome roots, I cause every sentient being to forever attain the beholding of a buddha, never giving rise to attachment; may, through these wholesome roots, I cause every sentient being to forever attain the beholding of a buddha, fully comprehending without obstruction; may, through these wholesome roots, I cause every sentient being to forever attain the beholding of a buddha, accomplishing Samantabhadra's own practice; may, through these wholesome roots, I cause every sentient being to forever behold every buddha, present before them, never once abandoned for even a moment; may, through these wholesome roots, I cause every sentient being to forever behold every buddha, giving rise to a bodhisattva's own immeasurable powers; may, through these wholesome roots, I cause every sentient being to forever behold every buddha, forever never forgetting any dharma.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Beholding a buddha is treated here as a wish with an unusually long tail of consequences — not one vow but nine consecutive variations on it, each specifying a different quality that beholding should produce: joy, comprehension, non-attachment, unbroken presence, memory. Seeing a buddha, the repetition suggests, is not a single event but something that must be secured against every way it could go wrong.
