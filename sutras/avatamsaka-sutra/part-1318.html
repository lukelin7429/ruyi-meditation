At a Snap of the Fingers He Rose

The similes for Good-Wealth's seeing run on: one possessed by a ghost who answers whatever is asked, one possessed by a nāga who takes a short while for months and years, a Brahmā palace named Adorned Treasury in which a whole trichiliocosm is seen without confusion, a monk in the totality-absorption whose object appears before him in every posture, a city of gandharvas seen in the sky, the palaces of yakṣas and of humans in one place yet not mixed, the great ocean, and an illusionist. Then Maitreya withdraws his power, enters the tower, snaps his fingers, and tells Good-Wealth to rise, for the nature of dharmas is like this, like an illusion, a dream, a shadow, a reflected image. At the sound Good-Wealth rises from samādhi.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1318 — fascicle 79 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

At a Snap of the Fingers He Rose

The remaining similes for the seeing, and the moment the vision ends — Maitreya's finger-snap, his one sentence on the nature of dharmas, and Good-Wealth's rising from samādhi.

Chinese Source

譬如有人，為鬼所持，見種種事，隨其所問，悉皆能答。善財童子亦復如是，菩薩智慧之所持故，見彼一切諸莊嚴事，若有問者，靡不能答。譬如有人，為龍所持，自謂是龍，入於龍宮，於少時間，自謂已經日月年載。善財童子亦復如是，以住菩薩智慧想故，彌勒菩薩所加持故，於少時間謂無量劫。譬如梵宮，名：莊嚴藏，於中悉見三千世界一切諸物不相雜亂。善財童子亦復如是，於樓觀中，普見一切莊嚴境界種種差別不相雜亂。譬如比丘，入遍處定，若行、若住、若坐、若臥，隨所入定，境界現前。善財童子亦復如是，入於樓觀，一切境界悉皆明了。譬如有人，於虛空中見乾闥婆城具足莊嚴，悉分別知，無有障礙；譬如夜叉宮殿與人宮殿，同在一處而不相雜，各隨其業，所見不同；譬如大海，於中悉見三千世界一切色像；譬如幻師，以幻力故，現諸幻事種種作業。善財童子亦復如是，以彌勒菩薩威神力故，及不思議幻智力故，能以幻智知諸法故，得諸菩薩自在力故，見樓閣中一切莊嚴自在境界。爾時，彌勒菩薩摩訶薩即攝神力入樓閣中，彈指作聲，告善財言：「善男子！起，法性如是，此是菩薩知諸法智因緣聚集所現之相。如是自性，如幻、如夢、如影、如像，悉不成就。」爾時，善財聞彈指聲，從三昧起。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is just as when a person possessed by a ghost sees every kind of thing and is able to answer whatever he is asked. Good-Wealth the youth was likewise: because he was sustained by the wisdom of the bodhisattvas, he saw every one of those adornments, and if anyone were to ask him about them, there was nothing he could not answer. It is just as when a person possessed by a nāga supposes himself to be a nāga, enters the nāga palace, and in a short while supposes that days, months, and years have already passed. Good-Wealth the youth was likewise: because he abided in the notion of the bodhisattvas' wisdom, and because he was empowered by Maitreya Bodhisattva, in a short while he took it to be measureless kalpas. It is just as, in the Brahmā palace named Adorned Treasury, every thing in a trichiliocosm is seen within it without confusion. Good-Wealth the youth was likewise: within the tower he saw universally every domain of adornment in all its manifold differences, without confusion. It is just as when a monk enters the totality-absorption, and whether walking, standing, sitting, or lying down, the object of whichever absorption he has entered appears before him. Good-Wealth the youth was likewise: having entered the tower, every domain was entirely clear to him. It is just as when a person sees in the sky a city of gandharvas fully adorned and discerns it all without obstruction; just as the palaces of yakṣas and the palaces of humans occupy one and the same place without mixing, so that what is seen differs according to each one's karma; just as within the great ocean every image of a trichiliocosm is seen; and just as an illusionist, through the power of illusion, manifests every kind of illusory work. Good-Wealth the youth was likewise: because of the majestic spiritual power of Maitreya Bodhisattva, and because of the power of inconceivable illusion-like wisdom, because he was able to know all dharmas through illusion-like wisdom, and because he had attained the sovereign power of the bodhisattvas, he saw within the tower every domain of adornment and sovereign freedom. At that time, Maitreya Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva withdrew his spiritual power, entered the tower, snapped his fingers, and said to Good-Wealth, "Good son! Rise. The nature of dharmas is like this; this is the mark manifested by the gathering together of the causes and conditions of the bodhisattvas' wisdom that knows all dharmas. Such is its intrinsic nature, like an illusion, like a dream, like a shadow, like a reflected image; none of it is accomplished." At that time, Good-Wealth, hearing the sound of the snapped fingers, rose from samādhi.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

譬如 — this part carries eight further similes for the manner of Good-Wealth's seeing, which with the dream and the deathbed of the previous part makes ten in the whole run. The first four each receive their own application, 善財童子亦復如是, Good-Wealth was likewise; the last four, the city of gandharvas, the two kinds of palace, the great ocean, and the illusionist, are given together and answered by a single application built on four reasons. The translation keeps that difference in pacing, applying the first four one by one and the last four in a group, as the source does.

彈指 — Maitreya's snapped fingers frame the whole vision. At the opening of this fascicle, 彈指出聲, the sound of his fingers opened the door of the tower; here, 彈指作聲, it ends the samādhi in which the tower was seen. Between the two snaps lies the whole description of the tower, and Maitreya's one sentence on rising, 法性如是, the nature of dharmas is like this, followed by the four likenesses of illusion, dream, shadow, and reflected image, is the only comment the sūtra makes on it before the questions begin.
