Nineteen Fears, and Nineteen Reasons

Hell-beings are relieved of torment in four words. Animals in five. Human beings take a list of nineteen fears, most of which no animal could have — and then the sūtra gives nineteen reasons for the whole rescue.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1102 — fascicle 70 in progress Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Five Kinds of Relief

The vision in the gap between the two night-spirits now fills with her work. She appears in every destiny, shaped to whoever is looking, and the relief she brings is measured exactly to what each kind of being suffers.

Chinese Source

如是等一切世界一切趣中，悉見此普救眾生夜神，於一切時一切處，隨諸眾生形貌、言辭、行解差別，以方便力普現其前，隨宜化度，令地獄眾生免諸苦毒，令畜生眾生不相食噉，令餓鬼眾生無有飢渴，令諸龍等離一切怖，令欲界眾生離欲界苦，

Ru-Yi Translation

In all such worlds as these and throughout all their destinies, he saw this night-spirit Who Universally Rescues Beings, at every time and in every place, in accord with the differences among beings in shape and appearance, in speech, and in practice and understanding, appearing universally before them by the power of skillful means and teaching and delivering them as suited each. She causes beings in the hells to escape every bitter torment; causes beings among the animals not to devour one another; causes beings among the hungry ghosts to be without hunger and thirst; causes the nāgas and the like to be free of every fear; causes beings of the desire realm to be free of the desire realm's suffering;

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Five clauses beginning 令 stand in this row — hell-beings, animals, hungry ghosts, nāgas, and the beings of the desire realm — and the sixth, human beings, has to wait for a row of its own, because the list of what humans are afraid of runs to nineteen items. The proportion is the argument. What a hell-being needs is stated in four characters, 免諸苦毒, escape from bitter torment. What an animal needs is stated in five, 不相食噉, that they not devour one another. Human fear needs nineteen entries and most of them have no physical cause at all.

隨諸眾生形貌、言辭、行解差別，以方便力普現其前 — in accord with the differences among beings in shape and appearance, in speech, and in practice and understanding, she appears universally before them. Three axes of difference, and they ascend: what a being looks like, what language it speaks, and how it practises and understands. A rescuer who matched only the first two would be a shapeshifter. Matching the third is what makes her a teacher.

What Human Beings Are Afraid Of

The sixth clause opens into a catalogue. It starts where anyone's fear starts — the dark, gossip, death — and then keeps going into territory only a practitioner can enter.

Chinese Source

令人趣眾生離暗夜怖、毀呰怖、惡名怖、大眾怖、不活怖、死怖、惡道怖、斷善根怖、退菩提心怖、遇惡知識怖、離善知識怖、墮二乘地怖、種種生死怖、異類眾生同住怖、惡時受生怖、惡種族中受生怖、造惡業怖、業煩惱障怖、執著諸想繫縛怖，如是等怖悉令捨離。

Ru-Yi Translation

causes beings in the human destiny to be free of the fear of the dark of night, the fear of being reviled, the fear of a bad reputation, the fear of a great assembly, the fear of not surviving, the fear of death, the fear of an evil destiny, the fear of cutting off one's roots of goodness, the fear of turning back from the mind of awakening, the fear of meeting a bad friend, the fear of being parted from a good spiritual friend, the fear of falling to the stage of the two vehicles, the fear of every kind of birth and death, the fear of dwelling together with beings of another kind, the fear of being born in an evil age, the fear of being born into an evil clan, the fear of committing evil deeds, the fear of the hindrance of deeds and afflictions, and the fear of the bondage of clinging to notions — all such fears as these she causes them to cast off.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The list holds nineteen fears, and its third through seventh items — 惡名怖、大眾怖、不活怖、死怖、惡道怖 — are exactly the classic set of five fears this sūtra has given before, in Part 697, as the fears a bodhisattva leaves behind on the day they give rise to the mind of awakening: the fear of not surviving, of a bad reputation, of death, of an evil destiny, and of a great assembly's own majestic authority. Here they sit unannounced in the middle of a longer list, with the dark of night and gossip before them and the whole of a practitioner's anxiety after.

The turn comes at the eighth item, 斷善根怖, the fear of cutting off one's roots of goodness. From there the catalogue leaves the world entirely: turning back from the mind of awakening, meeting a bad friend, being parted from a good spiritual friend, falling to the stage of the two vehicles. These are fears one has to have earned. A being who never set out cannot be afraid of turning back, and the sūtra is quietly telling its reader that some of the worst fears arrive only after the good news.

離善知識怖 — the fear of being parted from a good spiritual friend. Good-Wealth has just spent the opening of this fascicle vowing 於無量劫常不遠離, never through measureless eons to be far from his good spiritual friends. The vision he is now being shown names that very separation as one of the nineteen terrors a night-spirit removes. He made a vow against it; she takes it away.

Every Manner of Birth

Having gone through the destinies, the vision widens once more to the standing formula for every possible kind of being — sorted not by where they live but by how they are born and whether they perceive.

Chinese Source

又見一切眾生，卵生、胎生、濕生、化生，有色、無色，有想、無想，非有想、非無想，普現其前，常勤救護，

Ru-Yi Translation

He saw, too, that before all sentient beings — those born from eggs, born from a womb, born from moisture, and born by transformation; those with form and those without form; those with perception, those without perception, and those neither with perception nor without it — she appears universally, constantly and diligently rescuing and guarding them:

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

卵生、胎生、濕生、化生，有色、無色，有想、無想，非有想、非無想 — the standing formula, which this translation has met before at Part 406 among the bodhisattva's great vows. Four modes of birth, then two grades of form, then the three positions of perception. The final term is a single category, neither perceiving nor non-perceiving, even though the Taishō punctuation sets a mark inside it; it names the summit of the formless attainments, and it is included here so that the list reaches beings so refined that ordinary rescue has no handle on them.

Nineteen Reasons

Then, instead of describing the rescue further, the sūtra explains it — nineteen clauses, every one of them closing with 故, because. The first ten build her; the last nine spend her.

Chinese Source

為成就菩薩大願力故，深入菩薩三昧力故，堅固菩薩神通力故，出生普賢行願力故，增廣菩薩大悲海故，得普覆眾生無礙大慈故，得普與眾生無量喜樂故，得普攝一切眾生智慧方便故，得菩薩廣大解脫自在神通故，嚴淨一切佛剎故，

Ru-Yi Translation

so as to perfect the power of a bodhisattva's great vows; so as to enter deeply into the power of a bodhisattva's samādhi; so as to make firm the power of a bodhisattva's spiritual penetrations; so as to bring forth the power of Samantabhadra's practices and vows; so as to broaden the ocean of a bodhisattva's great compassion; so as to attain the unobstructed great kindness that covers all beings; so as to attain the giving of measureless joy and delight to all beings; so as to attain the wisdom and skillful means that gather in every being; so as to attain a bodhisattva's vast liberation and sovereign spiritual penetrations; so as to adorn and purify every buddha-land;

Chinese Source

覺了一切諸法故，供養一切諸佛故，受持一切佛教故，積集一切善根修一切妙行故，入一切眾生心海而無障礙故，知一切眾生諸根教化成熟故，淨一切眾生信解除其惡障故，破一切眾生無知黑闇故，令得一切智清淨光明故。

Ru-Yi Translation

so as to awaken fully to every dharma; so as to make offerings to every buddha; so as to receive and uphold every teaching of the buddhas; so as to accumulate every root of goodness and cultivate every wondrous practice; so as to enter the ocean of the minds of all beings without obstruction; so as to know the faculties of all beings and so teach and ripen them; so as to purify the faith and understanding of all beings and clear away their evil hindrances; so as to shatter the dark ignorance of all beings; and so as to cause them to obtain the pure light of all-knowing wisdom.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Nineteen clauses, each ending in 故 — ten in the first row and nine in the second. The opening four all end 力故, by the power of: great vows, samādhi, spiritual penetrations, and Samantabhadra's practices and vows. Four more then open with 得, so as to attain. The shape of the first half is acquisitive; the shape of the second half is not. The last four clauses all turn on 一切眾生 — entering their minds, knowing their faculties, purifying their understanding, shattering their ignorance — and the nineteenth gives the whole series away by not being about her at all, 令得一切智清淨光明故, so as to cause them to obtain the pure light of all-knowing wisdom.

The passage answers a question nobody asked out loud. Good-Wealth has just watched her save beings from nineteen fears, and the natural response is gratitude. What the sūtra supplies instead is motive — and the motive is not pity. She rescues beings in order to perfect vows, deepen samādhi, and make her powers firm, exactly as much as she rescues them in order to shatter their ignorance. Compassion here is not the opposite of self-cultivation; it is the method of it.

She Puts Her Own Shape Back On

The display ends. She sets aside the adorned body she had taken up for the demonstration — and the sentence takes care to say what she does not set aside.

Chinese Source

時，善財童子見此夜神如是神力不可思議甚深境界，普現調伏一切眾生菩薩解脫已，歡喜無量，頭面作禮，一心瞻仰。時，彼夜神即捨菩薩莊嚴之相，還復本形，而不捨其自在神力。爾時，善財童子恭敬合掌，却住一面，以偈讚曰：

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, having seen this night-spirit's spiritual power, her inconceivable and profound domain, and her bodhisattva liberation that universally manifests and tames all beings, Good-Wealth rejoiced beyond measure; he bowed his head and face in homage and gazed up at her with a single mind. Then that night-spirit put off the adorned form of a bodhisattva and returned to her original shape — yet without putting off her sovereign spiritual power. At that time, Good-Wealth reverently joined his palms, withdrew and stood to one side, and praised her in verse, saying:

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

即捨菩薩莊嚴之相，還復本形，而不捨其自在神力 — she put off the adorned form of a bodhisattva and returned to her original shape, yet without putting off her sovereign spiritual power. The character 捨 appears twice in one sentence, once affirmed and once denied, and the whole theology of these night-spirit chapters sits in the gap between them. The splendour was a costume; the power was not. What she goes back to being is a night-spirit — an ordinary local deity of the dark, in the ordinary shape Good-Wealth first saw — who happens to have lost nothing at all.

却住一面 — he withdrew and stood to one side. The gesture is as old as the Pali canon, where a visitor sits down to one side before speaking. After a vision containing every world in every mote of dust, Good-Wealth does the plainest thing available to him: he steps back, joins his palms, and starts to sing.
