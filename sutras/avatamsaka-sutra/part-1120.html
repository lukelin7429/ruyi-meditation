Four Questions Asked Only Once

Good-Wealth reaches the fourth night-spirit, states what he wants with four verbs instead of the usual two, and then — having been told the name of her liberation — asks four questions in a form that appears nowhere else in the eighty fascicles.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1120 — fascicle 71 opens Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

He Arrives With Four Verbs

Fascicle seventy-one opens where seventy ended, with the shortest journey in the pilgrimage. What has grown is not the distance but the request.

Chinese Source

爾時，善財童子於普救眾生妙德夜神所，聞菩薩普現一切世間調伏眾生解脫門，了知信解，自在安住；而往寂靜音海夜神所，頂禮其足，遶無數匝，於前合掌而作是言：「聖者！我已先發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，我欲依善知識，學菩薩行，入菩薩行，修菩薩行，住菩薩行。唯願慈哀，為我宣說：菩薩云何學菩薩行？云何修菩薩道？」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Good-Wealth, at the place of the night-spirit Wondrous Virtue Who Universally Rescues Beings, had heard the bodhisattva liberation-gate of Universally Manifesting in Every World and Taming Beings; he comprehended it, believed and understood it, and abided in it with sovereign freedom. Then he went to where the night-spirit Ocean of Tranquil Sound was, bowed his head at her feet, circled her countless times, and standing before her with palms joined said: "Noble one, I have already given rise to the mind of anuttara-samyak-sambodhi, and I wish, relying upon a good spiritual friend, to learn the bodhisattva practices, to enter the bodhisattva practices, to cultivate the bodhisattva practices, and to abide in the bodhisattva practices. I ask only that out of kindness and compassion you expound for me: how does a bodhisattva learn the bodhisattva practices? How does a bodhisattva cultivate the bodhisattva path?"

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

學菩薩行，入菩薩行，修菩薩行，住菩薩行 — to learn the bodhisattva practices, to enter them, to cultivate them, and to abide in them. Four verbs on one object, and one of them has a history. In Part 1100 Joyful Eye told Good-Wealth to go and ask about 入菩薩行、淨菩薩道, entering and purifying — and he never asked it; neither phrase occurs anywhere in fascicle seventy. Here 入菩薩行 surfaces at last, on the first page of the next fascicle, and it occurs exactly once in this fascicle too. It is not in his question. It is in his description of what he wants, which is a different and more honest place for it.

菩薩云何學菩薩行？云何修菩薩道 — how does a bodhisattva learn the bodhisattva practices, and cultivate the bodhisattva path? After four verbs in the preamble, the question itself uses two, and they are the two this chapter has been using all along. The escalation happens in what he says he wants; the asking stays plain. A pilgrim who has met thirty-five teachers has learned to keep his question simple.

Her Gate, and Four Questions

She praises the question, names her liberation in a single line, and stops. So he asks something he has never asked anyone.

Chinese Source

時，彼夜神告善財言：「善哉善哉！善男子！汝能依善知識求菩薩行。「善男子！我得菩薩念念出生廣大喜莊嚴解脫門。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time that night-spirit said to Good-Wealth: "It is good, it is good, good son, that you are able to rely upon a good spiritual friend in seeking the bodhisattva practices. Good son, I have attained the bodhisattva liberation-gate of Bringing Forth Vast Joy and Adornment in Thought After Thought.

Chinese Source

」善財言：「大聖！此解脫門為何事業？行何境界？起何方便？作何觀察？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good-Wealth said: "Great sage, what work does this liberation-gate do? What domain does it range in? What skilful means does it set in motion? What does it contemplate?"

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

為何事業？行何境界？起何方便？作何觀察 — what work does it do, what domain does it range in, what skilful means does it set in motion, what does it contemplate? Each of these four phrasings occurs exactly once in the whole of the eighty-fascicle T279, and all four occurrences are in this one sentence. Good-Wealth has heard thirty-five teachers name a liberation-gate and has never before asked what one is for. The four questions are practical in a way nothing he has asked has been: not what it is, but what it does, where it works, what it deploys, and what it looks at.

He addresses her as 聖者, noble one, at the start of the exchange and as 大聖, great sage, four lines later. Both vocatives are standard in this chapter and the shift is not marked. But it falls exactly at the moment she has told him she possesses something, and it is the sort of small upgrade a person makes without deciding to.

念念出生廣大喜莊嚴 — Bringing Forth Vast Joy and Adornment in Thought After Thought. The gate of the previous night-spirit was about manifesting and taming; this one is about joy. Keep the word in view: the nineteen-item list that follows ends on 皆受喜樂心, a mind that all beings should receive joy and ease, and the gate's name is the list's destination.

Nine Minds She Raised

Her answer is not a definition. It is a list of what she once wanted — and the first nine items are all about the condition of her own mind.

Chinese Source

夜神言：「善男子！我發起清淨平等樂欲心，我發起離一切世間塵垢清淨堅固莊嚴不可壞樂欲心，我發起攀緣不退轉位永不退轉心，我發起莊嚴功德寶山不動心，我發起無住處心，我發起普現一切眾生前救護心，我發起見一切佛海無厭足心，我發起求一切菩薩清淨願力心，我發起住大智光明海心，

Ru-Yi Translation

The night-spirit said: "Good son, I gave rise to a mind of pure and equal aspiration; I gave rise to a mind of aspiration pure, firm, adorned and indestructible, free of all the world's dust and defilement; I gave rise to a mind that takes the stage of non-regression as its object and never regresses; I gave rise to a mind unmoving as an adorned jewel-mountain of merit; I gave rise to a mind that dwells nowhere; I gave rise to a mind that appears universally before all beings to rescue and guard them; I gave rise to a mind never sated with seeing the ocean of all buddhas; I gave rise to a mind that seeks the pure power of every bodhisattva's vows; I gave rise to a mind abiding in the ocean of the light of great wisdom;

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

我發起無住處心 — I gave rise to a mind that dwells nowhere. Five items in, between a mind unmoving as a jewel-mountain and a mind that appears before every being, the list puts a mind with no location at all. The two clauses on either side of it are about being fixed and about being everywhere, and they need the one between them: a mind that could be pinned anywhere could not do either.

攀緣不退轉位永不退轉心 — a mind that takes the stage of non-regression as its object and never regresses. The verb 攀緣, to grasp at or take as object, is usually a fault in this literature — it is what a mind does to things it should let alone. Here the object grasped is non-regression itself, and the grasping is what produces it. The sūtra is comfortable turning a vice into a method by changing only what it is aimed at.

Ten Minds for Beings

At the tenth item the subject changes and never changes back. Every remaining clause is about somebody else, and they walk from grief to joy.

Chinese Source

我發起令一切眾生超過憂惱曠野心，我發起令一切眾生捨離愁憂苦惱心，我發起令一切眾生捨離不可意色、聲、香、味、觸、法心，我發起令一切眾生捨離愛別離苦、怨憎會苦心，我發起令一切眾生捨離惡緣、愚癡等苦心，我發起與一切險難眾生作依怙心，我發起令一切眾生出生死苦處心，我發起令一切眾生捨離生、老、病、死等苦心，我發起令一切眾生成就如來無上法樂心，我發起令一切眾生皆受喜樂心。

Ru-Yi Translation

I gave rise to a mind to bring all beings across the wilderness of grief and distress; I gave rise to a mind to make all beings cast off sorrow, grief, suffering and vexation; I gave rise to a mind to make all beings cast off displeasing forms, sounds, scents, tastes, touches and dharmas; I gave rise to a mind to make all beings cast off the suffering of parting from what they love and the suffering of meeting what they hate; I gave rise to a mind to make all beings cast off evil conditions, folly, and sufferings of that kind; I gave rise to a mind to be a support and refuge for all beings in peril and hardship; I gave rise to a mind to bring all beings out of the place where birth-and-death is suffered; I gave rise to a mind to make all beings cast off the sufferings of birth, ageing, sickness and death; I gave rise to a mind to make all beings accomplish the tathāgata's unsurpassed joy in the dharma; and I gave rise to a mind that all beings should receive joy and ease.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Nineteen clauses open 我發起, I gave rise to, and they break nine against ten. The first nine describe a mind she was making; from 我發起令一切眾生超過憂惱曠野心 onward, every one of the remaining ten is about beings. Nothing marks the seam. The list simply stops being about her, and that unannounced turn is the whole answer to the four questions he asked — what the gate does is this, and where it works is other people.

愛別離苦、怨憎會苦 … 生、老、病、死等苦 — the suffering of parting from what one loves, of meeting what one hates, and the sufferings of birth, ageing, sickness and death. Six of the classical eight sufferings appear in this list; the missing two, 求不得 and 五陰熾盛, not getting what one seeks and the burning of the five aggregates, are nowhere in the passage. Six is enough for the point being made. This is not a doctrinal enumeration but a practical one, and the items in it are the ones a night-spirit would actually meet at night.

超過憂惱曠野 — across the wilderness of grief and distress. Suffering is given a geography here, and the verb is one of crossing. Of the ten clauses about beings, seven ask that they 捨離 or 出, cast off or get out of, something; one asks that she be their 依怙, support and refuge; and the last two ask that they receive something — the tathāgata's joy in the dharma, and joy and ease. A list that begins by getting people out of a wasteland ends by handing them something, and the thing it hands them is the name of her liberation.

Part 1120 Complete

This opens chapter thirty-nine, 入法界品, in fascicle seventy-one. Part 1121 continues her answer — having raised those minds, she teaches, and the teaching is sorted by what she finds people attached to: their houses, their parents, their spouses, their palaces.
