Ten Kinds of Tireless Mind

Answering the monks' own vow, Mañjuśrī teaches ten kinds of mind that never wearies — in gathering roots of goodness, in serving buddhas, in seeking dharma, in practicing every pāramitā — closing on a single being's maturation multiplied across the whole realm of sentient beings, still without a trace of fatigue.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 891 — ten kinds of tireless mind Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Mind That Never Wearies

Each of the ten items names an entire path of practice — gathering merit, serving every buddha, mastering every pāramitā, purifying every land, teaching every being — and attaches to it the same four words: the mind knows no weariness. The refrain itself becomes the teaching.

Chinese Source

爾時，文殊師利菩薩告諸比丘言：「比丘！若善男子、善女人，成就十種趣大乘法，則能速入如來之地，況菩薩地！何者為十？所謂：積集一切善根，心無疲厭。見一切佛承事供養，心無疲厭。求一切佛法，心無疲厭。行一切波羅蜜，心無疲厭。成就一切菩薩三昧，心無疲厭。次第入一切三世，心無疲厭。普嚴淨十方佛剎，心無疲厭。教化調伏一切眾生，心無疲厭。於一切剎一切劫中成就菩薩行，心無疲厭。為成熟一眾生故，修行一切佛剎微塵數波羅蜜，成就如來十力；如是次第，為成熟一切眾生界，成就如來一切力，心無疲厭。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Mañjuśrī Bodhisattva said to the monks: "Monks! If a good son or good daughter accomplishes ten kinds of dharma that lead toward the Great Vehicle, they will then be able to swiftly enter the tathāgata's own ground — how much more so the bodhisattva's own ground! What are these ten? Namely: in gathering every root of goodness, their mind knows no weariness. In beholding every buddha and attending upon and making offerings to them, their mind knows no weariness. In seeking every buddha's own dharma, their mind knows no weariness. In practicing every pāramitā, their mind knows no weariness. In accomplishing every bodhisattva samādhi, their mind knows no weariness. In entering, one after another, every one of the three times, their mind knows no weariness. In universally adorning and purifying every buddha-land throughout the ten directions, their mind knows no weariness. In teaching, transforming, and taming every sentient being, their mind knows no weariness. In accomplishing the bodhisattva's own practice throughout every land and every eon, their mind knows no weariness. For the sake of bringing a single sentient being to maturity, they cultivate pāramitās as numerous as every mote of dust in every buddha-land, and accomplish the tathāgata's own ten powers; and so, one after another, for the sake of bringing every realm of sentient beings to maturity, they accomplish every one of the tathāgata's own powers — and in all this their mind knows no weariness.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

為成熟一眾生故……如是次第，為成熟一切眾生界 — for the sake of bringing a single sentient being to maturity … and so, one after another, for the sake of bringing every realm of sentient beings to maturity. The tenth item scales the whole list at once: the same tireless effort spent on one being is asked to repeat itself across the entire realm of sentient beings, without the mind ever registering fatigue.

What Ten Kinds of Faith Give Rise To

Mañjuśrī closes by naming the consequences of holding these ten minds with deep faith: not a single reward but an entire reordering of a person's own destiny, out of birth and death and into the tathāgata's own family.

Chinese Source

「比丘！若善男子、善女人，成就深信，發此十種無疲厭心，則能長養一切善根，捨離一切諸生死趣，超過一切世間種姓，不墮聲聞、辟支佛地，生一切如來家，具一切菩薩願，學習一切如來功德，修行一切菩薩諸行，得如來力，摧伏眾魔及諸外道，亦能除滅一切煩惱，入菩薩地，近如來地。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Monks! If a good son or good daughter accomplishes deep faith and gives rise to these ten kinds of tireless mind, they will then be able to nurture and grow every root of goodness, abandon and depart from every destiny of birth and death, surpass every worldly lineage, and never fall into the ground of the śrāvaka or the pratyekabuddha; they will be born into the family of every tathāgata, be endowed with every bodhisattva vow, learn every tathāgata's own merit, cultivate every one of the bodhisattva's own practices, attain the tathāgata's own power, subdue every māra and every non-Buddhist teacher, and be able as well to extinguish every affliction, enter the bodhisattva's own ground, and draw near to the tathāgata's own ground."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

生一切如來家 — born into the family of every tathāgata. The ten tireless minds are framed here not as a means to some later reward but as the very thing that reconstitutes a person's lineage: away from the destinies of birth and death, and into the tathāgata's own household.
