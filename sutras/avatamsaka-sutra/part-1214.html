Born from a Lotus Flower

The prince has asked twice; now the maiden's mother answers, not by cataloguing her daughter's virtues but by telling their origin — a bathing pool, a night ending into sunrise, and a thousand-petaled lotus splitting open to reveal a girl already whole.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1214 — fascicle 75 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Born from a Lotus Flower

Where the prince asked about parentage and conduct, the mother answers first with an origin more marvelous than either — an account of the very morning her daughter was born.

Chinese Source

「爾時，女母為其太子而說頌言：「『太子汝應聽，
我今說此女，
初生及成長，
一切諸因緣。

太子始生日，
即從蓮華生，
其目淨修廣，
肢節悉具足。

我曾於春月，
遊觀娑羅園，
普見諸藥草，
種種皆榮茂。

奇樹發妙華，
望之如慶雲；
好鳥相和鳴，
林間共歡樂。

同遊八百女，
端正奪人心，
被服皆嚴麗，
歌詠悉殊美。

彼園有浴池，
名曰蓮華幢；
我於池岸坐，
采女眾圍遶。

於彼蓮池內，
忽生千葉華，
寶葉瑠璃莖，
閻浮金為臺。

爾時夜分盡，
日光初出現，
其蓮正開剖，
放大清淨光。

其光極熾盛，
譬如日初出，
普照閻浮提，
眾歎未曾有。

時見此玉女，
從彼蓮華生，
其身甚清淨，
肢分皆圓滿。

此是人間寶，
從於淨業生，
宿因無失壞，
今受此果報。

紺髮青蓮眼，
梵聲金色光，
華鬘眾寶髻，
清淨無諸垢。

肢節悉具足，
其身無缺減，
譬如真金像，
安處寶華中。

毛孔栴檀香，
普熏於一切；
口出青蓮香，
常演梵音聲。

此女所住處，
常有天音樂；
不應下劣人，
而當如是偶。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, the maiden's mother spoke a hymn for the prince's sake, saying: "Prince, you should listen;
I will now tell of this daughter —
her birth and her growth,
and every cause and condition of it.

Prince, on the very day of her birth,
she came forth straight from a lotus flower;
her eyes were pure and broad,
and her every limb was already complete.

Once, in a month of spring,
I went to wander and view the Sāla Grove;
I saw everywhere every kind of medicinal herb,
every one of them flourishing and lush.

The wondrous trees put forth their marvelous blossoms,
and to behold them was like gazing on auspicious clouds;
the beautiful birds called to one another in harmony,
and throughout the grove all rejoiced together.

Eight hundred women wandered there together with me,
each one upright and fine enough to seize the heart;
every one of their garments was resplendent and fine,
and their singing was, every note of it, wondrously beautiful.

In that garden there was a bathing pool,
named Lotus Banner;
I sat upon the bank of that pool,
surrounded on every side by my attendant women.

Within that lotus pool,
there suddenly arose a thousand-petaled flower,
its petals jeweled and its stem of lapis lazuli,
its calyx wrought of Jambūnada gold.

At that time the night watch had just ended,
and the sun's light had only now appeared;
the lotus, at that very moment, split fully open,
and let loose a great and utterly pure light.

That light blazed with the utmost brilliance,
just like the sun at its very rising;
it universally illumined the whole of Jambudvīpa,
and everyone marveled at what they had never before beheld.

Then I saw this jewel-woman,
born forth from that very lotus flower;
her body was utterly pure,
and her every limb was perfectly complete.

This is a treasure among humankind,
born from pure karma;
the causes laid down in past lives were never lost or destroyed,
and now she receives this very fruit of retribution.

Her hair a deep indigo-blue, her eyes like the blue lotus,
her voice the Brahmā-voice, her light the hue of gold;
a flower garland and a topknot set with every jewel,
utterly pure, without a single stain.

Every one of her limbs and joints was already complete,
her body without the slightest lack or blemish,
just like an image of true gold,
set in place within a jeweled flower.

From every pore of her skin came the fragrance of sandalwood,
universally perfuming everything around her;
and from her mouth came the fragrance of the blue lotus,
forever giving voice to the Brahmā-sound.

Wherever this daughter dwells,
there is always heavenly music;
no lowly person ought to be matched
as a partner to one such as this.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

從彼蓮華生 — born forth from that very lotus flower. The maiden's own hymn in Part 1212 praised her body, her wisdom, and her indifference to the crowds who desired her, but never said where any of it came from. Her mother's hymn answers that silence: a bathing pool named Lotus Banner, a night ending into sunrise, a thousand-petaled flower splitting open in a blaze of light, and inside it, already whole, the daughter she is about to offer to the prince.
