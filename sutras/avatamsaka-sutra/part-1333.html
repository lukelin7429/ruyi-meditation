Not One Word Won Without Giving

Samantabhadra continues: he adorned buddha-lands with a mind of great compassion, rescued and protected sentient beings, taught them to maturity, made offerings to the buddhas, served good spiritual friends, and in seeking the true dharma proclaimed and upheld it widely, giving up everything within and without, never begrudging even his own body and life; an ocean of kalpas could not exhaust the telling of it, and the ocean of kalpas itself could be exhausted before this could. In his whole ocean of dharma there is not a single word, not a single phrase, that was not won by giving away a wheel-turning king's throne, or won by giving away everything he possessed; and all that he sought was sought only to rescue every sentient being, with the single thought: may sentient beings hear this dharma, may the light of wisdom universally illuminate the world, may I open and reveal the wisdom that transcends the world, may sentient beings all attain peace and happiness, may the merits possessed by every buddha be everywhere extolled. Such causes and conditions of ages past as these could not be exhausted in the telling, through kalpas as numerous as the fine motes of dust of ineffably ineffable buddha-lands.

Not One Word Won Without Giving

Samantabhadra's adorning of buddha-lands, his rescue of beings, and the five vows he made in seeking the dharma.

Chinese Source

「善男子！我莊嚴佛土，以大悲心，救護眾生，教化成就，供養諸佛，事善知識；為求正法，弘宣護持，一切內外悉皆能捨，乃至身命亦無所吝。一切劫海說其因緣，劫海可盡，此無有盡。「善男子！我法海中，無有一文，無有一句，非是捨施轉輪王位而求得者，非是捨施一切所有而求得者。善男子！我所求法，皆為救護一切眾生，一心思惟：『願諸眾生得聞是法，願以智光普照世間，願為開示出世間智，願令眾生悉得安樂，願普稱讚一切諸佛所有功德。』我如是等往昔因緣，於不可說不可說佛剎微塵數劫海，說不可盡。「是故，善男子！我以如是助道法力、諸善根力、大志樂力、修功德力、如實思惟一切法力、智慧眼力、佛威神力、大慈悲力、淨神通力、善知識力故，得此究竟三世平等清淨法身，復得清淨無上色身，超諸世間，隨諸眾生心之所樂而為現形，入一切剎，遍一切處，於諸世界廣現神通，令其見者靡不欣樂。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son! I adorned buddha-lands, and with a mind of great compassion rescued and protected sentient beings, teaching and transforming them to maturity, making offerings to the buddhas, and serving good spiritual friends; and in seeking the true dharma I widely proclaimed and upheld it, able to give up everything within and without, never begrudging even my own body and life. Every ocean of kalpas could tell of these causes and conditions, and the ocean of kalpas could be exhausted, but this could never be exhausted. Good son! In my ocean of dharma there is not a single word and not a single phrase that was not sought and won by giving away a wheel-turning king's throne, and not a single word and not a single phrase that was not sought and won by giving away everything I possessed. Good son! What I sought the dharma for was entirely for the sake of rescuing and protecting every sentient being, thinking single-mindedly: 'May sentient beings attain the hearing of this dharma; may the light of wisdom universally illuminate the world; may I open and reveal the wisdom that transcends the world; may sentient beings all attain peace and happiness; may the merits possessed by every buddha be everywhere extolled.' Such causes and conditions of ages past as these of mine could not be exhausted in the telling, through kalpas as numerous as the fine motes of dust of ineffably ineffable buddha-lands.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

無有一文，無有一句 — the claim is exact and twice repeated: not a single word or phrase in Samantabhadra's whole ocean of dharma, 我法海中, was won except by giving away a wheel-turning king's throne, or by giving away everything he possessed. The two clauses are parallel in the source, 非是捨施……而求得者 twice, and the translation keeps both rather than collapsing them into one, since the second widens the first from a throne to everything without exception.

一心思惟 — the five things Samantabhadra wished for are marked off in the source by 『 』, the same inner-quotation marks the sūtra uses elsewhere for a vow spoken within a speech, and each of the five opens with 願, may. The translation keeps all five as a single unbroken wish, in the source's order: that beings hear the dharma, that the light of wisdom shine on the world, that the wisdom beyond the world be revealed, that beings attain peace, and that the merits of every buddha be extolled.
