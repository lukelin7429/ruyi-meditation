You Might Become an Obstacle

Two full hymns of praise have asked the prince to accept the maiden. His answer is neither yes nor no — a vow to give away everything he owns, down to his own limbs, and a warning that the two women before him may not be able to bear the cost.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1216 — fascicle 75 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

You Might Become an Obstacle

Having led the maiden and her mother into the Garden of Fragrant Shoots, the prince finally speaks — not a word of acceptance, but the full scope of his own vow, and a warning about what it will demand of them.

Chinese Source

「爾時，太子入香芽園已，告其妙德及善現言：『善女！我趣求阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，當於盡未來際無量劫，集一切智助道之法，修無邊菩薩行，淨一切波羅蜜，供養一切諸如來，護持一切諸佛教，嚴淨一切佛國土，當令一切如來種性不斷，當隨一切眾生種性而普成熟，當滅一切眾生生死苦置於究竟安樂處，當淨治一切眾生智慧眼，當修習一切菩薩所修行，當安住一切菩薩平等心，當成就一切菩薩所行地，當令一切眾生普歡喜；當捨一切物，盡未來際行檀波羅蜜，令一切眾生普得滿足衣服飲食、妻妾男女、頭目手足，如是一切內外所有，悉當捨施，無所吝惜。當於爾時，汝或於我而作障難：施財物時，汝心吝惜；施男女時，汝心痛惱；割肢體時，汝心憂悶；捨汝出家，汝心悔恨。』

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, once the prince had entered the Garden of Fragrant Shoots, he said to Wondrous Virtue and Wholesome Manifestation: "Good women! I am hastening toward the attainment of anuttara-samyak-sambodhi. Throughout measureless kalpas, to the very end of the future, I shall gather every dharma that assists the path to all-knowing wisdom, cultivate the boundless practices of a bodhisattva, purify every pāramitā, make offerings to every tathāgata, uphold and protect the teaching of every buddha, and adorn and purify every buddha-land. I shall cause the lineage of every tathāgata to never be cut off, cause every being to reach maturity according to their own nature, extinguish the suffering of birth-and-death for every being and set them in the ultimate place of peace and joy, purify the eye of wisdom of every being, practice every practice a bodhisattva practices, abide firmly in the equal mind of every bodhisattva, accomplish every ground a bodhisattva traverses, and cause every being to universally rejoice. I shall relinquish every possession, and to the very end of the future practice the pāramitā of giving, causing every being to be fully satisfied — with clothing and food, with wife and consort, with son and daughter, with head, eyes, hands, and feet. All such things, whether within me or belonging to me, I shall entirely give away, begrudging nothing at all. And at that time, you might perhaps become an obstacle and a hindrance to me: when I give away wealth and possessions, your heart may begrudge it; when I give away son and daughter, your heart may be pained and distressed; when I cut away my own limbs, your heart may be troubled and grieved; and when I leave you to go forth into homelessness, your heart may be filled with regret and resentment."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

汝或於我而作障難 — you might perhaps become an obstacle and a hindrance to me. The prince's first words after two full hymns of praise are not acceptance but a warning, naming four tests in ascending order — money, then children, then his own body, then finally his leaving them for renunciation entirely — as if measuring the promises just made against what fulfilling them would actually cost the two women who made them.
