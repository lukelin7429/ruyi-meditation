Universal Wisdom's Question List Continues

Without a single pause, Universal Wisdom's questions move from samādhi and the gateways of liberation through ten kinds of unobstructed function, decisive understanding, and habitual tendency, before arriving at a striking turn: questions about the bodhisattva's own foot, hand, belly, and armor, as if the whole practice were about to be described as a body in its own right.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 685 — the question list continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

From Unobstructed Function to a Bodhisattva's Own Body

Eighty-eight more questions continue without any break in the sentence that began the chapter — moving through liberation, ten kinds of unobstructed function tied to beings, lands, dharmas, and vows in turn, decisive understanding of the world and of sentient beings, and then, abruptly, the foot, the hand, the belly, and a set of armor, as the questions begin to describe practice in bodily terms.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

何等為足？何等為手？何等為腹？何等為藏？何等為心？何等為被甲？何等為器仗？ — what is the foot? What is the hand? What is the belly? What is the storehouse? What is the mind? What is donning armor? What is a weapon? After eighty questions built almost entirely from abstract nouns — function, understanding, tendency, dharma — the list turns without warning to a bodhisattva's own body and equipment, a shift in register that suggests the answers to come will describe spiritual practice as something closer to a physical stance than a set of ideas.
