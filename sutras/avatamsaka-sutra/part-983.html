Every Dharma, Every World, Pure and Impure

This stretch of dhāraṇī-gates moves from teaching and compassion through every vehicle's own dharma, then opens an entire sequence devoted to worlds — formed, destroyed, pure, defiled, level, uneven — before turning finally to a long list built entirely around a buddha's own dharma-wheel.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 983 — every dharma, every world, pure and impure Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Every Vehicle's Dharma, Every Kind of World

After compassion and skillful means comes a fourfold division of dharma itself — buddha, bodhisattva, śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, and worldly — followed immediately by a run of worlds distinguished only by their own condition: formed or destroyed, pure or defiled, level or uneven, coarse or subtle.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

於垢世界現淨陀羅尼門、於淨世界現垢陀羅尼門 — the dhāraṇī-gate of manifesting purity within a defiled world, the dhāraṇī-gate of manifesting defilement within a pure world. These two gates sit directly beside each other, each the mirror image of the other, as though purity and defilement were never fixed properties of a world at all, only two different things any world can be made to show.

Twenty-Three Gates, All Turning on a Single Wheel

The final stretch of this list returns entirely to buddhas — their body, light, voice, assembly, samādhi, and above all their own dharma-wheel, named, distinguished, explained, and turned across nine separate gates before the sequence widens out to a buddha's full life, birth to nirvāṇa.
