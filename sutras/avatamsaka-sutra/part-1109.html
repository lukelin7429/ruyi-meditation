Samantabhadra Outshines a Proud City

A whole city is vain about its own good looks. Samantabhadra's remedy is not a sermon. He manifests a body more beautiful than any of theirs and a light beside which none of theirs can be seen at all.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1109 — a cure for vanity Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Beauty Nobody Can Look Past

The buddha's work is summed up in a sentence, and then the story goes back to the city Part 1105 left mid-decline. The problem there is unchanged, and the solution is not an argument.

Chinese Source

「善男子！彼普智寶焰妙德幢如來，現如是不思議自在神力轉法輪時，於彼一一諸世界中，隨其所應，念念調伏無量眾生。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, when that tathāgata, Banner of Wondrous Virtue and the Jewel-Flame of Universal Wisdom, displayed such inconceivable and sovereign spiritual power and turned the dharma-wheel, in each single one of those worlds, according to what each required, in thought after thought he tamed measureless beings.

Chinese Source

「時，普賢菩薩知寶華燈王城中眾生，自恃色貌及諸境界，而生憍慢陵蔑他人；化現妙身，端正殊特，往詣彼城，放大光明，普照一切，令彼聖王及諸妙寶、日月星宿、眾生身等一切光明悉皆不現，譬如日出眾景奪曜，亦如聚墨對閻浮金。

Ru-Yi Translation

"At that time the bodhisattva Samantabhadra knew that the beings in the royal city of Jewelled Flower Lamp were relying on their own looks and their own circumstances, and so giving rise to arrogance and treating others with contempt. He therefore manifested a wondrous body, upright and extraordinary, and went to that city. He sent out a great light that shone universally over everything, so that the light of the sage-king, of all their wondrous jewels, of the sun and moon and stars and constellations, and of the bodies of beings — every light there was — could no longer be seen at all. It was as when the sun rises and robs every other glow of its brightness; it was as a lump of ink set beside Jambū-river gold.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

自恃色貌及諸境界，而生憍慢陵蔑他人 — relying on their own looks and their own circumstances, and so giving rise to arrogance and treating others with contempt. This is the diagnosis of the disease Part 1105 showed us catching. There, one person told another 我身端正，汝形鄙陋, my body is well-formed and your shape is base and ugly, and lifespan and beauty began to shrink for everyone. Here the sūtra names the illness in its clinical form and adds the second cause the anecdote did not mention — 諸境界, one's circumstances. It is not only faces. It is everything one happens to have.

化現妙身，端正殊特 — he manifested a wondrous body, upright and extraordinary. The remedy is startling, and it is worth refusing the temptation to make it gentler than it is. Samantabhadra does not arrive and explain that beauty is impermanent. He arrives better-looking. The word 端正, well-formed, is the very word the proud man used of himself in Part 1105. The cure for taking oneself to be the most beautiful thing present is to be shown something more beautiful, and the sūtra evidently thinks this works where a lecture would not.

譬如日出眾景奪曜，亦如聚墨對閻浮金 — as when the sun rises and robs every other glow of its brightness; as a lump of ink set beside Jambū-river gold. Two similes for one event, and they differ in temper. The first is impersonal — the sun does not humiliate the stars, it simply comes up. The second is unkind: beside river gold, what you were proud of is a lump of ink. The sūtra offers both, and lets the reader decide how the citizens are meant to feel.

Who Is That

The city's response is the only one available to it. Nobody repents; everybody asks what just happened, and the categories they reach for are the wrong ones.

Chinese Source

時，諸眾生咸作是言：『此為是誰？為天？為梵？今放此光，令我等身所有光色皆不顯現。』種種思惟，無能解了。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time all those beings said together: "Who is this? Is it a god? Is it a Brahmā? He has sent out this light, so that the light and colour our own bodies possess can no longer be seen at all." They pondered it in every way, and none of them could understand it.

Chinese Source

「爾時，普賢菩薩在彼輪王寶宮殿上虛空中住，而告之言：『大王當知，今汝國中，有佛興世，在普光明法雲音幢菩提樹下。』

Ru-Yi Translation

"At that time the bodhisattva Samantabhadra stood in the sky above the wheel-turning king's jewelled palace and said to him: 'Great king, you should know that in your country now a buddha has arisen in the world, beneath the bodhi tree Banner of the Sound of the Dharma-Cloud of Universal Light.'

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

此為是誰？為天？為梵 — who is this? Is it a god? Is it a Brahmā? Their two guesses are the two highest things their cosmology offers, and both are wrong. Part 1104 has already supplied the correction in advance, where this same night-spirit told Good-Wealth that 諸天及人、一切二乘所不能測, gods and humans and both vehicles alike cannot fathom this. A city that can only ask whether something is a god or a Brahmā is a city that has no word for a bodhisattva yet.

The message Samantabhadra finally delivers has nothing to do with the light. He has flattened the vanity of an entire city, and what he says is a single piece of news — 今汝國中，有佛興世, in your country now a buddha has arisen in the world — with an address attached. The whole display was to make one sentence audible. Nothing is said about pride at all; the beings who were preening a moment ago are simply given somewhere else to look.

A Daughter Makes a Vow

One person in the city responds to the sight itself rather than to the news. She wants what she is looking at, and then, mid-vow, she changes what it is she wants it for.

Chinese Source

時，聖王女——蓮華妙眼，見普賢菩薩所現色身光明自在，及聞身上諸莊嚴具所出妙音，心生歡喜，作如是念：『願我所有一切善根，得如是身、如是莊嚴、如是相好、如是威儀、如是自在。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the sage-king's daughter, Lotus Wondrous Eye, seeing the sovereign freedom of the bodily form and light that Samantabhadra manifested, and hearing the wondrous sound issuing from the adornments upon his body, rejoiced in her heart and thought to herself: 'By all the roots of goodness I possess, may I obtain such a body as this, such adornment as this, such excellent marks as these, such bearing as this, such sovereign freedom as this.

Chinese Source

今此大聖，能於眾生生死長夜黑闇之中放大光明，開示如來出興於世；願令於我亦得如是，為諸眾生作智光明，破彼所有無知黑闇。願我所在受生之處，常得不離此善知識。』

Ru-Yi Translation

This great sage is able, in the long night and darkness of beings' births and deaths, to send out a great light and reveal that a tathāgata has arisen in the world. May it be granted to me to do likewise — to be a light of wisdom for all beings and to break whatever ignorant darkness they have. And may I, in whatever place I am reborn, never be apart from this good spiritual friend.'

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

得如是身、如是莊嚴、如是相好、如是威儀、如是自在 — such a body, such adornment, such excellent marks, such bearing, such sovereign freedom. Five clauses, each opening 如是, like this. The first thing the girl wants is exactly what her city wanted: to look like that. The vow begins as the same vanity that Samantabhadra came to cure, aimed at a better object — and then it turns. 願令於我亦得如是，為諸眾生作智光明, may it be granted to me to do likewise, to be a light of wisdom for all beings. She started by wanting the body and ends by wanting the work.

能於眾生生死長夜黑闇之中放大光明 — able, in the long night and darkness of beings' births and deaths, to send out a great light. The girl is describing Samantabhadra, and she is also describing, without knowing it, the job of a night-spirit. The teacher telling this whole story is 普救眾生妙德, a spirit of the dark whose entire office is to give light in the long night. Whoever this girl grew up to become, the vow she makes is for the work that the voice narrating it now performs.

願我所在受生之處，常得不離此善知識 — may I, in whatever place I am reborn, never be apart from this good spiritual friend. Fascicle seventy opened, in Part 1101, with Good-Wealth making the same vow in almost the same words: 於無量劫常不遠離, through measureless eons never to be far from them. The pilgrim listening and the girl in the story have now made one vow between them, separated by more eons than there are motes of dust in a buddha-land.

One textual difficulty should be stated plainly rather than smoothed over. Part 1105 named the wheel-turning king's daughter 普智焰妙德眼, and the sūtra will use that name again later in this fascicle; here the daughter is 蓮華妙眼, Lotus Wondrous Eye. The Taishō text carries no variant reading at this point. Whether these are two daughters or two names for one, T279 does not say, and this translation does not decide for it.
