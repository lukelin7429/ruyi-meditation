Banners of Wisdom

The gift now becomes a banner: countless jeweled poles hung with jeweled silk pennants, crowned with a crescent-moon jewel of Jambū-river gold outshining the sun, given to every buddha, to stūpas after a buddha's parinirvāṇa, to the treasures of Dharma and saṃgha, and to whoever comes asking. Ten dedication-vows follow, each raising a different banner — of wholesome roots, of unbreakable wisdom, of the Nārāyaṇa strength that topples worldly pride — culminating in the Tathāgata's own banner, which destroys all ninety-six kinds of heterodox view.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 282 — giving banners and pennants, opening and ten dedication-vows Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Banner Raised Against the World

Unlike the seat or the canopy, a banner is not primarily something that shelters — it is something that announces and asserts. That shift in character carries straight into the dedication: these ten vows speak of establishing, raising, and holding banners against Māra's activity, against worldly pride, against ninety-six varieties of wrong view. A banner-gift, it turns out, dedicates toward standing firm rather than covering over.

Chinese Source

「佛子！菩薩摩訶薩或施種種上妙幢幡。眾寶為竿，寶繒為幡，種種雜綵以為其幢；寶網垂覆，光色遍滿；寶鐸微搖，音節相和；奇特妙寶形如半月，閻浮檀金光逾皦日，悉置幢上；隨諸世界業果所現，種種妙物以為嚴飾。如是無數千萬億那由他諸妙幢幡，接影連輝遞相間發，光明嚴潔周遍大地，充滿十方虛空法界一切佛剎。菩薩摩訶薩淨心信解，以如是等無量幢幡，或施現在一切諸佛及佛滅後所有塔廟，或施法寶，或施僧寶，或施菩薩、諸善知識，或施聲聞及辟支佛，或施大眾，或施別人；諸來求者，普皆施與。以此善根如是迴向，所謂：『願一切眾生皆能建立一切善根福德幢幡，不可毀壞；願一切眾生建一切法自在幢幡，尊重愛樂，勤加守護；願一切眾生常以寶繒書寫正法，護持諸佛菩薩法藏；願一切眾生建高顯幢，然智慧燈普照世間；願一切眾生立堅固幢，悉能摧殄一切魔業；願一切眾生建智力幢，一切諸魔所不能壞；願一切眾生得大智慧那羅延幢，摧滅一切世間慢幢；願一切眾生得智慧日大光明幢，以智日光普照法界；願一切眾生具足無量寶莊嚴幢，充滿十方一切世界供養諸佛；願一切眾生得如來幢，摧滅一切九十六種外道邪見。』是為菩薩摩訶薩施幢幡時善根迴向，為令一切眾生得甚深高廣菩薩行幢及諸菩薩神通行幢清淨道故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Child of the Buddha! The bodhisattva mahāsattva may also give every kind of supreme and wondrous banner and pennant. Every jewel forms the pole, jeweled silk forms the pennant, every kind of mixed and brilliant color forms the banner itself; a jeweled net hangs down and covers it, its light and colors filling everything; jeweled bells sway gently, their notes harmonizing together; a rare and wondrous jewel shaped like a crescent moon, its Jambū-river gold light outshining even the sun, is set atop the banner; manifesting in accordance with the karmic fruit of every world, it is adorned with every kind of wondrous thing. Such countless thousands of ten-thousands of koṭis of nayutas of wondrous banners and pennants, their reflected light joined and interwoven, radiating in alternation, their brightness and purity spread across the great earth, filling the realm of empty space, the Dharma-realm, and every buddha-land in the ten directions. The bodhisattva mahāsattva, with a pure mind of faith and understanding, with such immeasurable banners and pennants as these, gives them to every buddha now present and to every stūpa and shrine remaining after a buddha's parinirvāṇa; or gives them to the treasure of the Dharma; or gives them to the treasure of the saṃgha; or gives them to bodhisattvas and every good spiritual friend; or gives them to śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas; or gives them to the great assembly; or gives them to a single individual; to whoever comes seeking them, they give them to them all entirely. With this wholesome root, they dedicate it in this way, namely: 'May all sentient beings all be able to establish every banner and pennant of wholesome roots and merit, indestructible; may all sentient beings establish every banner and pennant of mastery over the Dharma, honored and cherished, diligently guarded and protected; may all sentient beings forever write out the true Dharma upon jeweled silk, upholding and protecting the Dharma-treasury of every buddha and bodhisattva; may all sentient beings establish a lofty and conspicuous banner, kindling the lamp of wisdom to universally illumine the world; may all sentient beings raise up a firm banner, able to entirely destroy every activity of Māra; may all sentient beings establish the banner of the power of wisdom, unbreakable by any Māra; may all sentient beings obtain the Nārāyaṇa banner of great wisdom, destroying every banner of worldly pride; may all sentient beings obtain the great, luminous banner of the sun of wisdom, its wisdom-sun light universally illumining the Dharma-realm; may all sentient beings be fully endowed with the immeasurable jewel-adorned banner, filling every world in the ten directions to make offerings to the buddhas; may all sentient beings obtain the Tathāgata's own banner, destroying every one of the ninety-six kinds of heterodox, mistaken view.' This is the bodhisattva mahāsattva's dedication of wholesome roots when giving banners and pennants, so that all sentient beings may obtain the profound, lofty, and vast banner of bodhisattva practice, and the banner of every bodhisattva's spiritual power and pure path of practice.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The vow "may all sentient beings forever write out the true Dharma upon jeweled silk" stands out among the ten: the pennant itself is not just held aloft but written on, becoming a carrier of scripture. A banner announces from a distance; here it also transmits a text — protection and transmission collapsed into a single gesture.
