Naming the Great Numbers

Fascicle forty-five opens a new chapter with a new kind of question: not what a bodhisattva does, but how large a number can be named at all. The Bodhisattva Mind King asks the Buddha to explain the asaṃkhyeya and the string of superlatives beyond it, and the Buddha begins to answer with a ladder of number-names, each one the square of the name before it.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 567 — chapter thirty opens, the doubling ladder begins Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Question About Numbers

Chapter thirty, 阿僧祇品, opens fascicle forty-five. The Bodhisattva Mind King asks the Buddha to explain a string of terms — asaṃkhyeya, measureless, boundless, unequalled, uncounted, unreckoned, inconceivable, immeasurable, unspeakable, unspeakably unspeakable — that earlier fascicles have already used again and again to describe kalpas, buddha-lands, and beings, without ever pausing to define them.

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, the Bodhisattva Mind King said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! The buddha-tathāgatas speak of the asaṃkhyeya, the measureless, the boundless, the unequalled, the uncounted, the unreckoned, the inconceivable, the immeasurable, the unspeakable, and the unspeakably unspeakable. World-Honored One, what are the asaṃkhyeya, up to the unspeakably unspeakable?" The Buddha said to the Bodhisattva Mind King: "Excellent, excellent! Good son! Wishing to cause every world to enter into the meaning of number known to the Buddha, you now ask this of the Tathāgata, the Worthy One, the Perfectly Enlightened One. Good son! Listen closely, listen closely, and reflect on this well! I shall explain it for you." At that time, the Bodhisattva Mind King assented and received the teaching. The Buddha said: "Good son!

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

云何阿僧祇乃至不可說不可說耶 — what are the asaṃkhyeya, up to the unspeakably unspeakable? Fascicle forty-four's closing verses ended on a claim that a bodhisattva's merit exceeds every count. This chapter takes that claim and asks what counting itself can reach before it gives out — and answers not with a proof, but with a name for every rung of the ladder, all the way up.

The Doubling Begins

The Buddha's answer takes the form of a chain: a hundred lakṣas make one koṭi, and from there, each new number is simply the one before it multiplied by itself. Forty rungs of that ladder appear here — the rest continues in Part 568.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

俱胝俱胝為一阿庾多 — a koṭi of koṭis make one ayuta. Every step in this ladder after the first repeats a single name twice and equates it with the next: not "many of A" but "A of A's," which reads as A multiplied by itself. That squaring, carried out over a hundred and twenty-three rungs, is what makes this list mathematically notorious — it does not merely grow large, it grows large by repeated squaring, reaching magnitudes no ordinary count could approach.

Where a name in this ladder is an ordinary Chinese word — the Supreme, the Boundary, the Weighed — it is translated directly. Where it is a string of Chinese characters used purely for their sound, carrying no meaning of its own, this translation renders it as a systematic romanization of that sound (Pinpoluo, Jinjieluo, Agaluo…), since no prior English reconstruction of these names is drawn on here. koṭi, ayuta, and nayuta are kept as already-established loanwords, consistent with how earlier fascicles have used them.
