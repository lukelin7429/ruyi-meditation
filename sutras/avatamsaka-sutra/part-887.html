Mañjuśrī Departs with a Vast Retinue

After the long description of bodhisattvas teaching in every form and setting, the sūtra turns at last to Mañjuśrī himself, who emerges from his own pavilion accompanied by the spirits of earth, water, fire, wind, day, and night, and the eight classes of divine and semi-divine beings who traditionally guard the dharma.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 887 — Mañjuśrī departs with a vast retinue Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

An Entire Cosmology in Attendance

This retinue is not incidental: nature-spirits governing earth, water, fire, wind, day, night, space, ocean, mountain, and city walk alongside nāga kings, yakṣa kings, gandharva kings, and every other class of the eight kinds of divine and semi-divine beings that traditionally protect the dharma — the entire cosmos, in effect, accompanying Mañjuśrī as he prepares to leave.

Ru-Yi Translation

In some cases speaking of every worldly craft and trade; in other cases speaking of the lamp of wisdom that illuminates the world; in other cases speaking of what every sentient being's own karmic power has adorned; in other cases speaking of the establishment of every vehicle's own stages throughout every land in the ten directions; and in other cases speaking of the domain of every dharma illuminated by the lamp of wisdom — teaching and bringing every sentient being to accomplishment, and yet never departing from this very place of the tathāgata within the Jeta Grove. At that time, Mañjuśrī, the youth, emerged from the Well-Abiding Pavilion, together with measureless bodhisattvas walking alongside him, and the vajra spirits who constantly attend and guard him, the body-spirits who universally make offerings to every buddha for the sake of sentient beings, the foot-walking spirits who, having long ago given rise to firm vows, constantly follow after him, the earth-lord spirits who delight in hearing the wondrous dharma, the water-lord spirits who constantly cultivate great compassion, the fire-lord spirits whose wisdom-light blazes bright, the wind-lord spirits crowned with maṇi jewels, the direction-lord spirits skilled in every ceremony throughout the ten directions, the night-lord spirits who single-mindedly and diligently extinguish the darkness of ignorance, the day-lord spirits who, with an undivided, untiring mind, make manifest the sun of the Buddha, the space-lord spirits who adorn the entire dharma-realm and every realm of empty space, the ocean-lord spirits who universally deliver sentient beings beyond every ocean of existence, the mountain-lord spirits who constantly and diligently accumulate roots of goodness that aid the path toward all-knowing wisdom, lofty and vast as a mountain, and the city-lord spirits who constantly and diligently guard the city of every sentient being's own mind of awakening — together with the great nāga kings who constantly and diligently guard the unsurpassed dharma-city of all-knowing, all-seeing wisdom, the yakṣa kings who constantly and diligently guard every sentient being, the gandharva kings who constantly cause sentient beings' own joy to grow, the kumbhāṇḍa kings who constantly and diligently eliminate the realm of hungry ghosts, the garuḍa kings who constantly vow to rescue every sentient being out of every ocean of existence, the asura kings who vow to accomplish every tathāgata's own body, rising high above the world, the mahoraga kings who, seeing the Buddha, rejoice and bow reverently with respect, the great deva kings who constantly weary of birth and death and constantly delight in seeing the Buddha, and the great Brahmā kings who honor the Buddha, praise him, and make offerings to him.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

諸大龍王……諸大梵王 — from the great nāga kings through the great Brahmā kings, this passage names, in sequence, the traditional eight classes of divine and semi-divine beings said to protect the dharma wherever it is taught: nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, kumbhāṇḍas, garuḍas, asuras, mahoragas, and devas. Their gathering here, all at once, marks Mañjuśrī's own departure as an event the entire cosmos takes part in.
