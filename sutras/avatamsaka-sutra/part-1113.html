The Root Was a Broken Statue

The Buddha tells the girl her own history, and it goes back further than the story we have been hearing. Ten great eons earlier, in an age with no buddha alive in it, somebody asked her to mend a damaged image.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1113 — an older life still Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Ten Vows Out of an Ocean

Having been given thirty kinds of mind, she uses them on one object — Samantabhadra's vows — and produces vows of her own, of which ten are named.

Chinese Source

一心思惟普賢大願，發一切如來十佛剎微塵數願海：願嚴淨一切佛國，願調伏一切眾生，願遍知一切法界，願普入一切法界海，願於一切佛剎盡未來際劫修菩薩行，願盡未來際劫不捨一切菩薩行，願得親近一切如來，

Ru-Yi Translation

With a single mind she contemplated Samantabhadra's great vows, and brought forth oceans of vows of every tathāgata, as numerous as the fine motes of dust of ten buddha-lands: the vow to adorn and purify every buddha-realm; the vow to tame all beings; the vow to know the whole dharma-realm through and through; the vow to enter universally into every ocean of the dharma-realm; the vow, in every buddha-land, to cultivate the bodhisattva practices to the end of all future eons; the vow, to the end of all future eons, never to abandon any bodhisattva practice; the vow to draw near to every tathāgata;

Chinese Source

願得承事一切善友，願得供養一切諸佛，願於念念中修菩薩行增一切智無有間斷……。發如是等十佛剎微塵數願海，成就普賢所有大願。

Ru-Yi Translation

the vow to attend upon every good friend; the vow to make offerings to every buddha; and the vow, in thought after thought, to cultivate the bodhisattva practices and increase all-knowing wisdom without interruption — and so on. Bringing forth oceans of vows such as these, as numerous as the fine motes of dust of ten buddha-lands, she accomplished every one of Samantabhadra's great vows.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Ten vows are named and the total is 十佛剎微塵數願海, oceans of vows as numerous as the motes of dust in ten buddha-lands — the same device the samādhi list used in Part 1112, a sample with the total stated after it. What is worth noticing is the neighbourhood of the eighth. The seventh vow is to draw near to every tathāgata and the ninth is to make offerings to every buddha; between them stands 願得承事一切善友, the vow to attend upon every good friend. The good spiritual friend is not a lesser stage on the way to buddhas. In this list they are filed together.

一心思惟普賢大願 — with a single mind she contemplated Samantabhadra's great vows. Her own vows are not invented. They are made by looking at somebody else's and taking them, which is precisely what a reader of this sūtra is being invited to do with the vows now being read.

The Buddha Tells Her Her Own Story

What the Buddha then teaches her is not doctrine. It is her biography, and hearing it is what awakens her.

Chinese Source

「時，彼如來復為其女開示演說發心已來所集善根、所修妙行、所得大果，令其開悟成就如來所有願海，一心趣向一切智位。

Ru-Yi Translation

"At that time that tathāgata further revealed and expounded for that girl the roots of goodness she had gathered since first giving rise to the mind of awakening, the wondrous practices she had cultivated, and the great fruits she had obtained — causing her to awaken, to accomplish every ocean of a tathāgata's vows, and with a single mind to set out toward the station of all-knowing wisdom.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

彼如來復為其女開示演說發心已來所集善根 — that tathāgata further revealed and expounded for that girl the roots of goodness she had gathered since first giving rise to the mind of awakening. Consider the arrangement. A night-spirit is telling Good-Wealth the story of her past; inside that story, a buddha tells a girl the story of her past; and what the girl is awakened by is hearing it. The form of the teaching and the form of the fascicle are the same thing. Good-Wealth is at this moment receiving the treatment the girl received, from the girl.

Ten Great Eons Before That

And the biography goes back further than the tale we have been following — to an age when no buddha was alive, and there was nothing to serve but a broken image.

Chinese Source

「善男子！復於此前，過十大劫，有世界，名：日輪光摩尼，佛號：因陀羅幢妙相。此妙眼女，於彼如來遺法之中，普賢菩薩勸其修補蓮華座上故壞佛像；既修補已而復彩畫，既彩畫已復寶莊嚴，發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, ten great eons before even this, there was a world named Maṇi of the Sun-Disc's Light, whose buddha was called Wondrous Marks of Indra's Banner. This girl Wondrous Eye, within that tathāgata's bequeathed teaching, was urged by the bodhisattva Samantabhadra to repair a broken buddha-image upon a lotus seat. Having repaired it, she then painted it in colours; having painted it, she then adorned it with jewels; and she gave rise to the mind of anuttara-samyak-sambodhi.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

普賢菩薩勸其修補蓮華座上故壞佛像 — the bodhisattva Samantabhadra urged her to repair a broken buddha-image upon a lotus seat. This is where the whole thing starts. Not a vow in a buddha's presence, not a vision, not a samādhi — a repair job on a damaged statue. The phrase 於彼如來遺法之中 places it precisely: within that tathāgata's bequeathed teaching, meaning after his parinirvāṇa, in an age with no living buddha in it. There was nothing else available to serve. She served the thing that was left.

既修補已而復彩畫，既彩畫已復寶莊嚴 — having repaired it she then painted it in colours; having painted it she then adorned it with jewels. Three acts, chained by two hinges of the form 既…已, having done this. The sequence is the ordinary order of restoring an image, and the sūtra records each step. Nobody is transfigured; nobody flies. Then, at the end of the third clause, she gives rise to the mind of unsurpassed awakening — and the reader is left to notice that it came after the work rather than before it.

The story is now three layers deep in the past: Good-Wealth is being told of a wheel-turning king's age, inside which a buddha told a girl of an age ten great eons earlier still. And at the bottom of the three layers is a broken statue nobody was tending.

She Says the Word I

Then the grammar changes. She has been calling her subject 此妙眼女, this girl Wondrous Eye. Without any announcement, she starts saying I.

Chinese Source

「善男子！我念過去，由普賢菩薩善知識故，種此善根。從是已來，不墮惡趣，常於一切天王、人王種族中生，端正可喜，眾相圓滿，令人樂見，常見於佛，常得親近普賢菩薩；乃至於今，示導開悟，成熟於我，令生歡喜。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, I recall that in the past, because of the good spiritual friend Samantabhadra, this root of goodness was planted. From that time until now I have not fallen into the evil destinies; I have always been born among the families of deva-kings and human kings, well-formed and pleasing, complete in every mark, a delight for people to look upon; I have always seen buddhas, and I have always been able to draw near to the bodhisattva Samantabhadra — who even to this day instructs and guides and awakens me, brings me to ripeness, and causes joy to arise in me.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

我念過去 — I recall that in the past. One sentence earlier the same person was 此妙眼女, this girl Wondrous Eye, described from outside. Here she is 我, I. The night-spirit gives away the ending of her own story in a pronoun, before the formal question 於意云何, what do you think, which comes in the next part. The disclosure is grammatical before it is explicit, which is how a person actually lets something slip.

端正可喜，眾相圓滿，令人樂見 — well-formed and pleasing, complete in every mark, a delight for people to look upon. The word 端正 occurs six times in fascicle seventy, and following it through the fascicle is a small education. It described the king's thousand sons and his court women and his queen; then in Part 1105 it was in the mouth of the man who said 我身端正，汝形鄙陋, my body is well-formed and your shape is base and ugly; then in Part 1109 it described the body Samantabhadra manifested to cure that city of exactly this vanity. Here, at the sixth and last occurrence, it is the karmic result of mending a statue. The sūtra has not condemned beauty at any point. It has only been rearranging where beauty comes from.

常於…常見…常得 — three clauses opening 常, always: always born among kings, always seeing buddhas, always able to draw near Samantabhadra. In Part 1109 the girl's vow was 願我所在受生之處，常得不離此善知識, may I in whatever place I am reborn never be parted from this good spiritual friend. The third 常 here is that vow reported as fulfilled, in the same two characters, 常得.

Part 1113 Complete

This continues chapter thirty-nine, 入法界品, in fascicle seventy. Part 1114 asks the question the whole account has been building toward — who those people were — and answers that the wheel-turning king is Maitreya, his queen is a night-spirit living not far from here, and the girl was the speaker herself.
