A Hand Across a Hundred and Ten Yojanas

The last fascicle of the sūtra opens with Good-Wealth on the road again, following Maitreya's instruction through more than a hundred and ten cities to the city of Sumanā in the land of Universal Gate, where he stands at the gate thinking of Mañjuśrī and searching for him. Mañjuśrī does not come. From a hundred and ten yojanas away he stretches out his right hand, lays it on Good-Wealth's head, and speaks of what is lost to one who leaves the root of faith, whose mind is weak and regretful, who is content with a little merit. Then he gives Good-Wealth asaṃkhyeya gates of the dharma and boundless dhāraṇīs, vows, samādhis, powers, and wisdom, sets him in the site of Samantabhadra's practice and in his own abode, and withdraws without ever having appeared.

A Hand Across a Hundred and Ten Yojanas

Good-Wealth's return to Mañjuśrī, and the teacher who reaches him without being seen.

Chinese Source

爾時，善財童子依彌勒菩薩摩訶薩教，漸次而行，經由一百一十餘城已，到普門國蘇摩那城，住其門所，思惟文殊師利，隨順觀察，周旋求覓，希欲奉覲。是時，文殊師利遙伸右手，過一百一十由旬，按善財頂，作如是言：「善哉善哉！善男子！若離信根，心劣憂悔，功行不具，退失精勤，於一善根心生住著，於少功德便以為足，不能善巧發起行願，不為善知識之所攝護，不為如來之所憶念，不能了知如是法性、如是理趣、如是法門、如是所行、如是境界；若周遍知、若種種知、若盡源底，若解了、若趣入、若解說、若分別、若證知、若獲得，皆悉不能。」是時，文殊師利宣說此法，示教利喜，令善財童子成就阿僧祇法門，具足無量大智光明，令得菩薩無邊際陀羅尼、無邊際願、無邊際三昧、無邊際神通、無邊際智，令入普賢行道場，及置善財自所住處；文殊師利還攝不現。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Good-Wealth the youth, following the instruction of Maitreya Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva, traveled on gradually, and having passed through more than a hundred and ten cities, came to the city of Sumanā in the land of Universal Gate. He stood at its gate, thinking of Mañjuśrī, observing in accord with him, searching all around for him, and wishing to behold him. At that time, Mañjuśrī stretched out his right hand from afar, across a hundred and ten yojanas, laid it upon Good-Wealth's head, and spoke these words: "Excellent, excellent! Good son! If one leaves the root of faith, and the mind is weak, sorrowful, and regretful, and merit and practice are not complete, and one falls away from diligence, and the mind dwells and clings to a single wholesome root, and one is content with a little merit, unable skillfully to give rise to practices and vows, not gathered in and protected by good spiritual friends, not kept in mind by the tathāgatas, then one is unable to understand such a nature of dharmas, such a principle, such a gate of the dharma, such a practice, and such a domain; whether to know them completely, or to know them in their many kinds, or to reach their very depths, or to understand them, or to enter them, or to explain them, or to distinguish them, or to realize them, or to attain them, one is unable to do any of it." At that time, Mañjuśrī, proclaiming this dharma, instructed, taught, benefited, and gladdened him, causing Good-Wealth the youth to accomplish asaṃkhyeya gates of the dharma and to be fully endowed with the measureless light of great wisdom, causing him to attain the bodhisattva's boundless dhāraṇīs, boundless vows, boundless samādhis, boundless spiritual powers, and boundless wisdom, causing him to enter the site of awakening of Samantabhadra's practice, and setting Good-Wealth in his own abode; and then Mañjuśrī withdrew and did not appear.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

遙伸右手，過一百一十由旬 — Mañjuśrī reaches Good-Wealth with a hand stretched across a hundred and ten yojanas, the same number as the cities Good-Wealth has just passed, 一百一十餘城, and the same as the count of good spiritual friends he has visited, which the sūtra fixed at 一百一十 in fascicle seventy-eight. The passage does not say that Mañjuśrī came or was seen; he speaks, gives, places, and 還攝不現, withdraws and does not appear, and the translation keeps that absence rather than supplying an appearance.

若離信根 — Mañjuśrī's speech is a single conditional sentence: nine faults, from leaving the root of faith to not being kept in mind by the tathāgatas, and then the whole range of what is thereby lost, nine modes of knowing in a run of 若 clauses, ending 皆悉不能, one is unable to do any of it. The translation keeps it as one sentence so that the weight of the long protasis lands, as in the source, on the four final characters.
