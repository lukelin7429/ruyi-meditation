Ten Great Treasuries of Dharma

The similes finish, and then she does something this sūtra almost never does — tells the reader what to do with them. Good-Wealth asks how one practises to get such a thing, and the answer is the complete set of ten perfections.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1126 — the ten perfections Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Sun, Moon, Suchness, Echo

Eleven images to close the run. Three of them are things with no substance of their own — a shadow, an echo, a reflection — and they sit in the middle.

Chinese Source

此解脫者猶如淨日，能破眾生無知暗故；此解脫者猶如滿月，滿足廣大福德海故；此解脫者猶如真如，悉能周遍一切處故；此解脫者猶如自影，從自善業所化出故；此解脫者猶如呼響，隨其所應為說法故；此解脫者猶如影像，隨眾生心而照現故；此解脫者如大樹王，開敷一切神通華故；此解脫者猶如金剛，從本已來不可壞故；此解脫者如如意珠，出生無量自在力故；此解脫者如離垢藏摩尼寶王，示現一切三世如來諸神力故；此解脫者如喜幢摩尼寶，能平等出一切諸佛法輪聲故。

Ru-Yi Translation

this liberation is like a pure sun, because it is able to break the darkness of beings' ignorance; this liberation is like a full moon, because it fills up the vast ocean of merit; this liberation is like suchness, because it is able to pervade every place entirely; this liberation is like one's own shadow, because it comes forth by transformation out of one's own good deeds; this liberation is like an echo, because it teaches the dharma according to what each requires; this liberation is like a reflected image, because it shines forth according to the minds of beings; this liberation is like a great tree-king, because it opens out the flowers of every spiritual penetration; this liberation is like vajra, because from the very beginning it has been indestructible; this liberation is like a wish-fulfilling jewel, because it brings forth measureless sovereign powers; this liberation is like the maṇi jewel-king Stainless Treasury, because it displays the spiritual powers of every tathāgata of the three times; this liberation is like the maṇi jewel Banner of Joy, because it is able to send out equally the sound of the dharma-wheel of every buddha.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

猶如自影，從自善業所化出故 — like one's own shadow, because it comes forth by transformation out of one's own good deeds. Of all the images in this run of twenty-five, this is the one that cannot be admired from outside. A shadow is not something a person has; it is something a person casts, and it goes where they go. The reason attached to it — that it comes out of one's own good deeds — makes the simile a statement about karma in its plainest form: what you have done is walking around with you, in your shape.

猶如呼響，隨其所應為說法故 — like an echo, because it teaches the dharma according to what each requires. An echo has no message of its own; it gives back the shape of what was called into it. Put beside Part 1121's thirty-seven diagnoses, where every teaching was determined by what she found the person holding, the image is exact. She is not a source of doctrine. She is what a question sounds like when it comes back.

Three of these eleven are insubstantial things — 自影 a shadow, 呼響 an echo, 影像 a reflection — and they are consecutive, sitting between suchness and a great tree. The sequence runs from 真如, suchness itself, the most solid thing this literature knows, straight into three things that are nothing but the shape of something else, and then back out to a tree, vajra, and jewels. The sūtra does not rank these. Being like suchness and being like an echo are both true of the same gate.

Think On These Images

Then she stops and, unusually, says what the similes were for. And Good-Wealth asks the one question he has not yet asked.

Chinese Source

善男子！我今為汝說此譬諭，汝應思惟，隨順悟入。」爾時，善財童子白寂靜音海夜神言：「大聖！云何修行，得此解脫？」夜神言：「善男子！菩薩修行十大法藏，得此解脫。何等為十？

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son, I have now spoken these similes for you; you should ponder them, and in accord with them awaken and enter." At that time Good-Wealth said to the night-spirit Ocean of Tranquil Sound: "Great sage, how does one practise so as to gain this liberation?" The night-spirit said: "Good son, a bodhisattva practises ten great treasuries of dharma and gains this liberation. What are the ten?

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

我今為汝說此譬諭，汝應思惟，隨順悟入 — I have now spoken these similes for you; you should ponder them, and in accord with them awaken and enter. The word 譬諭, simile or analogy, occurs once in fascicle seventy-one and thirty-three times across the eighty fascicles; 隨順悟入 occurs twice in the whole sūtra. What is unusual is not the vocabulary but the gesture. Having given twenty-five images, she names them as images and issues instructions for their use. Most of this chapter shows; this sentence tells, and what it tells is that the pondering is the reader's job.

云何修行，得此解脫 — how does one practise so as to gain this liberation? These eight characters occur exactly once in the whole of T279, and this is it. Good-Wealth's four questions in Part 1120 asked what the gate does; this one asks what he has to do. Having been given the work, the domain, the means, the object of contemplation, and twenty-five similes, the pilgrim finally asks for the practice — and the answer is going to be shorter and more ordinary than anything that preceded it.

Ten Great Treasuries

And the answer is the standard list. After a fascicle of oceans and pores and jewel-kings, what one actually does is the ten perfections, in order.

Chinese Source

一修布施廣大法藏，隨眾生心悉令滿足；二修淨戒廣大法藏，普入一切佛功德海；三修堪忍廣大法藏，能遍思惟一切法性；四修精進廣大法藏，趣一切智恒不退轉；五修禪定廣大法藏，能滅一切眾生熱惱；六修般若廣大法藏，能遍了知一切法海；

Ru-Yi Translation

First, one cultivates the vast dharma-treasury of giving, so that according to the minds of beings all are satisfied. Second, one cultivates the vast dharma-treasury of pure discipline, entering universally into the ocean of every buddha's merit. Third, one cultivates the vast dharma-treasury of forbearance, able to contemplate everywhere the nature of all dharmas. Fourth, one cultivates the vast dharma-treasury of vigour, going toward all-knowing wisdom and never regressing. Fifth, one cultivates the vast dharma-treasury of meditative concentration, able to extinguish the burning distress of all beings. Sixth, one cultivates the vast dharma-treasury of wisdom, able to know fully and everywhere the ocean of all dharmas.

Chinese Source

七修方便廣大法藏，能遍成熟諸眾生海；八修諸願廣大法藏，遍一切佛剎、一切諸眾生海，盡未來劫修菩薩行；九修諸力廣大法藏，念念現於一切法界海、一切佛國土，成等正覺常不休息；十修淨智廣大法藏，得如來智，遍知三世一切諸法無有障礙。善男子！若諸菩薩安住如是十大法藏，則能獲得如是解脫，清淨增長，積集堅固，安住圓滿。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, one cultivates the vast dharma-treasury of skilful means, able to bring to ripeness everywhere the ocean of all beings. Eighth, one cultivates the vast dharma-treasury of vows, pervading every buddha-land and the whole ocean of beings, cultivating the bodhisattva practices to the end of all future eons. Ninth, one cultivates the vast dharma-treasury of powers, in thought after thought appearing throughout the whole ocean of the dharma-realm and in every buddha-land, accomplishing perfect awakening without ever resting. Tenth, one cultivates the vast dharma-treasury of pure knowledge, gaining the tathāgata's wisdom and knowing everywhere all dharmas of the three times without any obstruction. Good son, if bodhisattvas abide securely in these ten great dharma-treasuries, then they are able to obtain such a liberation as this, purified and increasing, gathered and made firm, abiding and complete."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

布施, 淨戒, 堪忍, 精進, 禪定, 般若, 方便, 諸願, 諸力, 淨智 — giving, discipline, forbearance, vigour, meditation, wisdom, skilful means, vows, powers, knowledge. This is the complete list of ten perfections, in its standard order, with nothing added and nothing left out. Part 1121 is worth putting beside it: there she handed out perfections as remedies for particular faults, and only six of the ten appeared — 忍, 精進, 禪, 般若, 力, 智. The four that were missing there, 施, 戒, 方便 and 願, are the first two and the seventh and eighth here. What she dispenses to others is whichever one answers their defect. What she cultivates herself is all ten.

The answer is startlingly ordinary, and that is the point of where it is placed. Good-Wealth has just heard that this liberation is bottomless, is the universal gate, is like suchness and like an echo and like the maṇi jewel-king Stainless Treasury. He asks how one gets it, and is told: giving, discipline, patience, effort, concentration, wisdom, and the four that follow. The sūtra's vast machinery is not an alternative to the basic curriculum. It is what the basic curriculum turns out to have been.

清淨增長，積集堅固，安住圓滿 — purified and increasing, gathered and made firm, abiding and complete. Six characters in three pairs, closing the answer. Each pair puts a process beside a state: purified and growing, accumulated and solid, settled and whole. Nothing in the phrasing suggests a moment of arrival; the liberation is described as something that keeps being got.
