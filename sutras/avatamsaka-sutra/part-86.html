What Is Pure Practice, If None of These Ten Things Are?

Fascicle seventeen opens with a single, sharp question: how does a bodhisattva who has left home and taken robes actually attain pure practice? Dharma Wisdom Bodhisattva answers not by describing what pure practice looks like, but by examining ten candidates one by one — the body, its actions, speech, the mind, even the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha themselves — and showing that not one of them, taken alone, could be it.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 86 — chapter 16 opens, the tenfold examination Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Devaputra's Question

Mindfulness Devaputra asks how a bodhisattva who has renounced the household life actually attains pure practice, and Dharma Wisdom Bodhisattva answers by naming ten things a practitioner might mistake for the practice itself: the body, bodily action, speech, verbal action, the mind, mental action, the Buddha, the Dharma, the Sangha, and the precepts.

Chinese Source

爾時，正念天子白法慧菩薩言：「佛子！一切世界諸菩薩眾，依如來教，染衣出家。云何而得梵行清淨，從菩薩位逮於無上菩提之道？」

法慧菩薩言：

「佛子！菩薩摩訶薩修梵行時，應以十法而為所緣，作意觀察。所謂：身、身業、語、語業、意、意業、佛、法、僧、戒。應如是觀：為身是梵行耶？乃至戒是梵行耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Mindfulness Devaputra said to Dharma Wisdom Bodhisattva: "Son of the Buddha! The bodhisattvas throughout every world, in accordance with the Tathāgata's own teaching, dye their robes and leave the household life. How, then, do they attain a pure practice of brahmacarya, and, from the bodhisattva's own stage, arrive at the path of unsurpassed bodhi?"

Dharma Wisdom Bodhisattva said:

"Son of the Buddha! When a bodhisattva mahāsattva cultivates the practice of brahmacarya, he should take ten dharmas as his own object, and turn his attention to contemplate them. Namely: the body, bodily action, speech, verbal action, the mind, mental action, the Buddha, the Dharma, the Sangha, and the precepts. He should contemplate in this way: is the body itself the practice of brahmacarya? — all the way to: is the precepts themselves the practice of brahmacarya?

The Body, Its Deeds, Speech, and the Mind

Each candidate is taken in turn and shown to be inadequate on its own terms: if the body itself were the practice, the practice would be no better than a corpse; if bodily action were it, the practice would be nothing more than walking and sitting; if speech or the mind's own activity were it, the practice would collapse into mere breath, or into dreams.

Chinese Source

若身是梵行者，當知梵行則為非善、則為非法、則為渾濁、則為臭惡、則為不淨、則為可厭、則為違逆、則為雜染、則為死屍、則為蟲聚。若身業是梵行者，梵行則是行住坐臥、左右顧視、屈伸俯仰。若語是梵行者，梵行則是音聲風息、唇舌喉吻、吐納抑縱、高低清濁。若語業是梵行者，梵行則是起居問訊、略說、廣說、諭說、直說、讚說、毀說、安立說、隨俗說、顯了說。若意是梵行者，梵行則應是覺、是觀、是分別、是種種分別、是憶念、是種種憶念、是思惟、是種種思惟、是幻術、是眠夢。若意業是梵行者，當知梵行則是思想、寒熱、飢渴、苦樂、憂喜。

Ru-Yi Translation

If the body itself were the practice of brahmacarya, one should know that the practice of brahmacarya would then be unwholesome, would then be contrary to the Dharma, would then be turbid, would then be foul and rank, would then be impure, would then be something to be loathed, would then be something to be shunned, would then be defiled, would then be a corpse, and would then be a swarm of worms. If bodily action itself were the practice of brahmacarya, the practice of brahmacarya would then simply be walking, standing, sitting, and lying down, looking left and right, bending and stretching, bowing and raising the head. If speech itself were the practice of brahmacarya, the practice of brahmacarya would then simply be sound and the breath of wind, the lips, the tongue, the throat and mouth, exhaling and inhaling, holding back and letting loose, high and low, clear and murky. If verbal action itself were the practice of brahmacarya, the practice of brahmacarya would then simply be greeting others upon rising and settling, speaking briefly, speaking at length, speaking by analogy, speaking directly, speaking in praise, speaking in disparagement, speaking to establish something, speaking in accordance with convention, and speaking to make something clear. If the mind itself were the practice of brahmacarya, the practice of brahmacarya would then have to be initial thought, sustained thought, discrimination, every kind of discrimination, recollection, every kind of recollection, reflection, every kind of reflection, illusory conjuring, and sleep and dreaming. If mental action itself were the practice of brahmacarya, one should know that the practice of brahmacarya would then simply be thought and conception, cold and heat, hunger and thirst, suffering and pleasure, sorrow and joy.

Not the Buddha, the Dharma, the Sangha, or the Precepts Either

The examination does not spare even the Three Jewels themselves: is any single aggregate, mark, or spiritual power really "the Buddha"? Is cessation, or nirvāṇa, or the inexpressible really "the Dharma"? Is any single stage of attainment really "the Sangha"? Is the ordination platform, the shaved head, or the robe really "the precepts"? Each question is left open, unanswered by anything the list itself supplies.

Chinese Source

若佛是梵行者，為色是佛耶？受是佛耶？想是佛耶？行是佛耶？識是佛耶？為相是佛耶？好是佛耶？神通是佛耶？業行是佛耶？果報是佛耶？若法是梵行者，為寂滅是法耶？涅槃是法耶？不生是法耶？不起是法耶？不可說是法耶？無分別是法耶？無所行是法耶？不合集是法耶？若僧是梵行者，為預流向是僧耶？預流果是僧耶？一來向是僧耶？一來果是僧耶？不還向是僧耶？不還果是僧耶？阿羅漢向是僧耶？阿羅漢果是僧耶？三明是僧耶？六通是僧耶？若戒是梵行者，為壇場是戒耶？問清淨是戒耶？教威儀是戒耶？三說羯磨是戒耶？和尚是戒耶？阿闍梨是戒耶？鬀髮是戒耶？著袈裟衣是戒耶？乞食是戒耶？正命是戒耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

If the Buddha himself were the practice of brahmacarya — is form itself the Buddha? Is feeling itself the Buddha? Is perception itself the Buddha? Is volition itself the Buddha? Is consciousness itself the Buddha? Is the major marks themselves the Buddha? Is the minor marks themselves the Buddha? Is spiritual power itself the Buddha? Is karmic conduct itself the Buddha? Is the fruit of recompense itself the Buddha? If the Dharma itself were the practice of brahmacarya — is tranquil cessation itself the Dharma? Is nirvāṇa itself the Dharma? Is non-arising itself the Dharma? Is non-origination itself the Dharma? Is the inexpressible itself the Dharma? Is non-discrimination itself the Dharma? Is non-action itself the Dharma? Is that which does not come together and assemble itself the Dharma? If the Sangha itself were the practice of brahmacarya — is one heading toward stream-entry itself the Sangha? Is the fruit of stream-entry itself the Sangha? Is one heading toward once-returning itself the Sangha? Is the fruit of once-returning itself the Sangha? Is one heading toward non-returning itself the Sangha? Is the fruit of non-returning itself the Sangha? Is one heading toward arhatship itself the Sangha? Is the fruit of arhatship itself the Sangha? Is the threefold knowledge itself the Sangha? Is the sixfold spiritual power itself the Sangha? If the precepts themselves were the practice of brahmacarya — is the ordination platform itself the precepts? Is the questioning as to purity itself the precepts? Is the teaching of deportment itself the precepts? Is the threefold proclamation of the karman itself the precepts? Is the preceptor himself the precepts? Is the instructing teacher himself the precepts? Is the shaving of the head itself the precepts? Is the wearing of the kāṣāya robe itself the precepts? Is begging for alms itself the precepts? Is right livelihood itself the precepts?

Nothing at All to Grasp — and Thus, Pure Practice

Having exhausted the list, the teaching turns the question back on itself: since nothing at all in the body, in cultivation, or in the Dharma can be grasped, where could the practice of brahmacarya possibly come from, or belong to, or be done by anyone at all? It is precisely this finding nothing to grasp — not locating some eleventh candidate — that the teaching names as pure practice itself.

Chinese Source

如是觀已，於身無所取，於修無所著，於法無所住；過去已滅，未來未至，現在空寂；無作業者，無受報者；此世不移動，彼世不改變。此中何法名為梵行？梵行從何處來？誰之所有？體為是誰？由誰而作？為是有，為是無？為是色，為非色？為是受，為非受？為是想，為非想？為是行，為非行？為是識，為非識？如是觀察，梵行法不可得故，三世法皆空寂故，意無取著故，心無障礙故，所行無二故，方便自在故，受無相法故，觀無相法故，知佛法平等故，具一切佛法故，如是名為清淨梵行。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Having contemplated in this way, there is nothing at all to grasp in the body, nothing at all to cling to in cultivation, nothing at all to abide in within the Dharma; the past has already ceased, the future has not yet arrived, and the present is empty and tranquil; there is no doer of any deed, and no receiver of any recompense; this world does not shift or move, and that world does not change or alter. Within all of this, what dharma at all could be called the practice of brahmacarya? From what place does the practice of brahmacarya come? To whom does it belong? Whose own substance is it? By whom is it done? Is it existent, or is it non-existent? Is it form, or is it not form? Is it feeling, or is it not feeling? Is it perception, or is it not perception? Is it volition, or is it not volition? Is it consciousness, or is it not consciousness? Contemplating in this way: because the dharma of brahmacarya cannot be found at all; because the dharmas of the three periods of time are all empty and tranquil; because the mind has nothing at all to grasp or cling to; because the mind is without any obstruction at all; because the practice one engages in is without any duality; because skillful means are sovereign and unhindered; because one receives the markless dharma; because one contemplates the markless dharma; because one knows that the Buddha's own dharmas are equal; and because one is fully endowed with every single buddha-dharma — it is in this very way that it is called the pure practice of brahmacarya."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This is the same method the sūtra has used before, from Mañjuśrī's dialogues in chapter ten to the ninth abode's own teaching two chapters ago — but here applied with unusual thoroughness to something intensely practical: the actual daily life of a monastic, robe and shaved head and ordination platform included. The chapter's method is not to find the true answer hidden among the ten candidates, nor to declare that pure practice is impossible; it is to show that the very framing — "which single thing among these is it?" — cannot be satisfied by any of them, and that seeing this clearly is itself what purifies the practice. Nothing about the actual conduct of monastic life changes; what changes is the grasping that would make any one part of it into an object to be clung to as "the practice" itself.
