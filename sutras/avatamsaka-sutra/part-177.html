A Hundred Million Sounds, Each Praising Something Different

The palace's ornament finally becomes sound: banner-poles hung with bells, flowers, and garments give way to conches, drums, harps, and mṛdaṅgas playing all at once — and then, in the passage's final turn, to a hundred million distinct sounds, each one praising a specific aspect of the Dharma rather than simply repeating the same note of praise over and over.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 177 — music and the sounds of praise Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

From Music to Meaning

The description moves from ornament to sound: banner-poles bearing bells, silks, fragrance, and flowers, and then instruments — conches, drums, harps, mṛdaṅgas — playing together at once, some struck, some sounding of their own accord. The passage's final and longest stretch turns this music into something more specific: not a wash of pleasant noise but a hundred million distinct sounds, each one praising a named aspect of the Dharma in turn.

Chinese Source

百萬億天鈴幢，百萬億金網幢，出微妙音；百萬億天繒幢，眾彩具足；百萬億香幢，垂布香網；百萬億華幢，雨一切華；百萬億天衣幢，懸布妙衣；百萬億天摩尼寶幢，眾寶莊嚴；百萬億天莊嚴具幢，眾具校飾；百萬億天鬘幢，種種華鬘，四面行布；百萬億天蓋幢，寶鈴和鳴，聞皆歡喜。百萬億天螺，出妙音聲；百萬億天鼓，出大音聲；百萬億天箜篌，出微妙音；百萬億天牟陀羅，出大妙音；百萬億天諸雜樂，同時俱奏；百萬億天自在樂，出妙音聲，其聲普遍一切佛剎；百萬億天變化樂，其聲如響，普應一切；百萬億天鼓，因於撫擊，而出妙音；百萬億天如意樂，自然出聲，音節相和；百萬億天諸雜樂，出妙音聲，滅諸煩惱。

Ru-Yi Translation

A hundred million heavenly bell-banners and a hundred million golden-net banners, sending forth a subtle and wondrous sound; a hundred million heavenly silk banners, fully endowed with every color; a hundred million fragrance-banners, hanging down nets of fragrance; a hundred million flower-banners, raining down every kind of flower; a hundred million heavenly garment-banners, hanging out wondrous garments; a hundred million heavenly maṇi-jewel banners, adorned with every kind of treasure; a hundred million heavenly banners of adorning implements, decorated with every kind of ornament; a hundred million heavenly garland-banners, every kind of flower garland arrayed on all four sides; a hundred million heavenly canopy-banners, their jeweled bells ringing in harmony, so that whoever heard them all rejoiced. A hundred million heavenly conches, sending forth a wondrous sound; a hundred million heavenly drums, sending forth a great sound; a hundred million heavenly harps, sending forth a subtle and wondrous sound; a hundred million heavenly mṛdaṅgas, sending forth a great and wondrous sound; a hundred million heavenly instruments of every kind, all playing together at once; a hundred million heavenly instruments of sovereign mastery, sending forth a wondrous sound, its voice pervading every buddha-land universally; a hundred million heavenly instruments of transformation, their sound like an echo, universally resounding in answer to everything; a hundred million heavenly drums, struck and beaten, sending forth a wondrous sound; a hundred million heavenly instruments of wish-fulfillment, sounding forth of their own accord, their notes harmonizing together; and a hundred million heavenly instruments of every kind, sending forth a wondrous sound, extinguishing every affliction.

Ru-Yi Translation

A hundred million sounds delightful to the mind, extolling and praising the offerings made; a hundred million vast and expansive sounds, extolling and praising service rendered; a hundred million profound sounds, extolling and praising cultivation and practice; a hundred million wondrous sounds of every kind, extolling the buddha's own karmic fruit; a hundred million subtle and minute sounds, extolling the principle of things exactly as they are; a hundred million unobstructed and true sounds, extolling the buddha's own original conduct; a hundred million pure sounds, extolling and praising the offerings made to every buddha in ages past; a hundred million sounds of the Dharma-gate, extolling and praising every buddha's own most supreme fearlessness; a hundred million immeasurable sounds, extolling every bodhisattva's own inexhaustible merit; a hundred million sounds of the bodhisattva grounds, extolling and praising the opening up and revealing of the conduct corresponding to every bodhisattva ground; a hundred million uninterrupted sounds, extolling the buddha's own merit as never once interrupted; a hundred million accordant sounds, extolling and proclaiming the conduct of beholding the buddha; a hundred million profound sounds of the Dharma, extolling the principle corresponding to the unobstructed wisdom of every dharma; a hundred million vast and expansive sounds, their voice filling every buddha-land entirely; a hundred million unobstructed and pure sounds, according with each mind's own delight, causing every one of them to rejoice entirely; a hundred million sounds that dwell in none of the three realms, causing whoever hears them to enter deeply into the very nature of dharmas; a hundred million joyful sounds, causing whoever hears them to have a mind free of any obstruction, with deep faith and reverence; a hundred million sounds of the buddha's own domain, wherever their voice comes forth, entirely able to open up and reveal the meaning of every dharma; a hundred million sounds of dhāraṇī, skillfully proclaiming every distinct phrase of the Dharma, resolving and clarifying the Tathāgata's own secret treasury; and a hundred million sounds of every dharma, their voice harmonious and free-flowing, perfectly attuned to every kind of music.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Where the earlier catalogs of nets, curtains, and flowers named a hundred million of the same kind of thing, this final catalog of sounds names a hundred million different objects of praise — offerings, service, cultivation, karmic fruit, the bodhisattva grounds, the Tathāgata's own secret treasury — each getting its own sound rather than sharing a generic one. The music has, in effect, become instruction: a hall filled with a hundred million kinds of teaching, arriving not as words but as something one simply hears.
