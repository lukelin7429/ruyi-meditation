Attached to Nothing Within the Dedication

Vajra Banner Bodhisattva opens his closing verse on the third dedication, gathering every kind of bliss the bodhisattva has attained — of body, of eye and ear, of every faculty — and dedicating it entirely toward every living being. The verse states its own governing paradox directly: although the bodhisattva dedicates for the sake of every living being, they cling to nothing at all within the dedication itself.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 243 — Vajra Banner Bodhisattva's third closing verse Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Every Bliss, Dedicated, Clung to By No One

The verse's opening stanzas move through an inventory of bliss — the bodily faculty, the eye, ear, nose, and tongue, whatever supreme and wondrous happiness the bodhisattva has attained — each dedicated in turn toward every living being. Its clearest statement of the dedication's own character arrives at the midpoint: dedicating for the sake of every living being while clinging to nothing at all within the act of dedicating itself, a distinction the verse insists on rather than lets pass unremarked.

Chinese Source

「彼諸菩薩摩訶薩，修過去佛迴向法，
亦學未來現在世，一切導師之所行。
於諸境界得安樂，諸佛如來所稱讚，
廣大光明清淨眼，悉以迴向大聰哲。
菩薩身根種種樂，眼耳鼻舌亦復然，
如是無量上妙樂，悉以迴向諸最勝。
一切世間眾善法，及諸如來所成就，
於彼悉攝無有餘，盡以隨喜益眾生。
世間隨喜無量種，今此迴向為眾生，
人中師子所有樂，願使群萌悉圓滿。
一切國土諸如來，凡所知見種種樂，
願令眾生皆悉得，而為照世大明燈。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Those bodhisattva mahāsattvas cultivate the very dedication-dharma of every buddha of the past; they likewise study the conduct of every Guiding Teacher of the future and present worlds. Attaining peace and happiness amid every domain, praised by every buddha and Tathāgata; their vast and radiant, pure eye — they dedicate all of it entirely toward the greatly wise and perceptive. The bodhisattva's own bodily faculty and every kind of bliss, and likewise the eye, ear, nose, and tongue; immeasurable supreme and wondrous bliss such as this — they dedicate all of it entirely toward the most supreme ones. Every wholesome dharma throughout the world, and whatever every Tathāgata has accomplished; they gather all of it in entirely, leaving nothing out, and through rejoicing in it entirely, benefit living beings. Rejoicing in the world's immeasurable kinds of merit, they now dedicate this for the sake of living beings; whatever bliss the Lion Among Humankind possesses, may every sprouting being come to perfectly fulfill it. Every Tathāgata throughout every land, whatever bliss they know and see of every kind; may living beings all attain it entirely, and become the great bright lamp that illuminates the world.

Chinese Source

菩薩所得勝妙樂，悉以迴向諸群生，
雖為群生故迴向，而於迴向無所著。
菩薩修行此迴向，興起無量大悲心，
如佛所修迴向德，願我修行悉成滿。
如諸最勝所成就，一切智乘微妙樂，
及我在世之所行，諸菩薩行無量樂，
示入眾趣安隱樂，恒守諸根寂靜樂，
悉以迴向諸群生，普使修成無上智。
非身語意即是業，亦不離此而別有，
但以方便滅癡冥，如是修成無上智。
菩薩所修諸行業，積集無量勝功德，
隨順如來生佛家，寂然不亂正迴向。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whatever supreme and wondrous bliss the bodhisattva has attained, they dedicate all of it entirely toward every kind of living being; although they dedicate for the sake of every living being, they cling to nothing at all within the dedication itself. The bodhisattva, cultivating and practicing this dedication, gives rise to an immeasurable mind of great compassion; just as the merit of dedication the Buddha himself has cultivated, may my own cultivation and practice be entirely fulfilled. Just as every most supreme one has accomplished, the subtle and wondrous bliss of the vehicle of all-knowing wisdom; and whatever I myself practice while in the world, the immeasurable bliss of every bodhisattva's own conduct, the peaceful and secure bliss of entering every destiny to teach, the tranquil bliss of forever guarding every faculty, I dedicate all of it entirely toward every kind of living being, universally causing them to cultivate and accomplish unsurpassed wisdom. It is not that body, speech, and mind are themselves identical with karma, nor is there anything separate from these apart from them; but rather, through skillful means, extinguishing the darkness of delusion, one cultivates and accomplishes unsurpassed wisdom in just this way. Every conduct and karmic deed the bodhisattva cultivates accumulates immeasurable supreme merit; according with the Tathāgata, born into the household of the Buddha, tranquil and undisturbed, they dedicate correctly.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

"It is not that body, speech, and mind are themselves identical with karma, nor is there anything separate from these apart from them" applies, in miniature, the same non-identity-non-separation formula this sūtra has used elsewhere for karma and its retribution, and for the Buddha's body and his spiritual power — a recurring refusal to let two things that seem intimately connected collapse into strict identity or clean separation.
