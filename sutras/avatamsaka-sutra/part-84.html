Youth, Prince, and Consecration, the Ten Abodes Complete

The great hymn's final three movements restate the last of the Ten Abodes: the eighth, with a body, speech, and mind entirely without fault and a spiritual power that journeys to countless buddha-lands; the ninth, entering the Dharma-king's own domain — his palace, his consecration, his repose; and the tenth, reaching a wisdom so vast that not even a Dharma-prince bodhisattva can fully know it, completing the abode sequence of Dharma Wisdom's entire hymn.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 84 — the great hymn's abode sequence completes, 8–10 of 10 Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

The Eighth Abode: Youthful Innocence, in Verse

A body, speech, and mind entirely without fault, taking birth according to his own will, journeying to countless buddha-lands and serving countless buddhas without any weariness at all.

Chinese Source

「第八菩薩童真住，身語意行皆具足，
一切清淨無諸失，隨意受生得自在。
知諸眾生心所樂，種種意解各差別，
及其所有一切法，十方國土成壞相。

逮得速疾妙神通，一切處中隨念往，
於諸佛所聽聞法，讚歎修行無懈倦。
了知一切諸佛國，震動加持亦觀察，
超過佛土不可量，遊行世界無邊數。

阿僧祇法悉諮問，所欲受身皆自在，
言音善巧靡不充，諸佛無數咸承事。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"The bodhisattva of the eighth abode, Youthful Innocence, has bodily, verbal, and mental conduct all fully complete, entirely pure and without any fault at all, taking birth according to his own will, sovereign and unhindered. Knowing every living being's own mind and what it delights in, their every manifold intent and understanding each differing, and every dharma there is belonging to them, and the marks of forming and destruction of the lands of the ten directions.

Attaining a swift and wondrous spiritual power, going wherever he thinks of within every single place, listening to and receiving the Dharma at the place of every buddha, extolling and cultivating it without any weariness or fatigue at all. Understanding entirely every single buddha-land, shaking it, sustaining it with his own power, and likewise contemplating it, transcending buddha-lands beyond all measure, journeying throughout worlds beyond all boundless number.

Inquiring into asaṃkhyeya dharmas entirely, sovereign and unhindered over whatever body he wishes to receive, his speech and voice skillful, with nothing at all left unfulfilled, serving and attending upon countless buddhas, every one of them."

The Ninth Abode: The Dharma-Prince, in Verse

Understanding worldly and ultimate truth entirely, this bodhisattva enters the Dharma-king's own domain — his palace, his consecration, his repose — cultivating right mindfulness until every single buddha manifests before him.

Chinese Source

「第九菩薩王子住，能見眾生受生別，
煩惱現習靡不知，所行方便皆善了。
諸法各異威儀別，世界不同前後際，
如其世俗第一義，悉善了知無有餘。

法王善巧安立處，隨其處所所有法，
法王宮殿若趣入，及以於中所觀見。
法王所有灌頂法，神力加持無怯畏，
宴寢宮室及歎譽，以此教詔法王子。

如是為說靡不盡，而令其心無所著，
於此了知修正念，一切諸佛現其前。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"The bodhisattva of the ninth abode, the Dharma-Prince, is able to see the distinctions in how living beings receive birth, and there is nothing at all he does not know of the arising of afflictions and their habitual tendencies; every skillful means he practices, he skillfully understands entirely. Every dharma is each distinct, every deportment differs, every world is not the same, and their own past and future limits differ — exactly as they are, both worldly truth and ultimate truth, he skillfully understands entirely, with nothing at all left over.

The Dharma-king's own skillful place of establishment, and, according to that very place, whatever dharma there is; the Dharma-king's own palace, whether entering into it, and likewise whatever is observed and seen within it. The Dharma-king's own Dharma of consecration, his spiritual power sustaining without any timidity or fear at all, his repose within the palace hall, and his own praise — by means of these, the Dharma-prince is instructed and taught.

Thus is it proclaimed, with nothing at all left unexhausted, causing his own mind to cling to nothing at all; understanding this and cultivating right mindfulness, every single buddha manifests before him."

The Tenth Abode: Consecration, in Verse

The final abode reaches a wisdom that shakes and illumines countless worlds, sustains and adorns them, and opens the Dharma to countless living beings — a domain of body and spiritual power that the verse declares, once again, unknowable even to a Dharma-prince bodhisattva. This completes the great hymn's own restatement of all ten abodes.

Chinese Source

「第十灌頂真佛子，成滿最上第一法，
十方無數諸世界，悉能震動光普照。
住持往詣亦無餘，清淨莊嚴皆具足，
開示眾生無有數，觀察知根悉能盡。

發心調伏亦無邊，咸令趣向大菩提，
一切法界咸觀察，十方國土皆往詣。
其中身及身所作，神通變現難可測，
三世佛土諸境界，乃至王子無能了。

一切見者三世智，於諸佛法明了智，
法界無礙無邊智，充滿一切世界智，
照耀世界住持智，了知眾生諸法智，
及知正覺無邊智，如來為說咸令盡。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"The tenth abode's own true son of the Buddha, Consecration, fully accomplishes the most supreme and foremost dharma; the countless worlds of the ten directions, he is entirely able to shake, and his light universally illumines them. Sustaining them and going to them, likewise with nothing at all left over, purely adorning them, everything entirely complete; opening up and revealing the Dharma to countless living beings, contemplating and knowing their own faculties entirely and completely.

Giving rise to their resolve and taming and subduing them, likewise without any boundary at all, causing every one of them to head toward the great bodhi; the whole Dharma-realm entirely contemplated, the lands of the ten directions all entirely visited. Within this, his own body and what his body does, his spiritual powers and transformative manifestations, are hard to fathom at all; the domains of buddha-lands throughout the three periods of time — not even a Dharma-prince is able to understand them.

The wisdom of the one who sees all, the wisdom of the three periods of time, the wisdom that clearly understands every buddha-dharma, the unobstructed and boundless wisdom of the Dharma-realm, the wisdom that fills every world entirely, the wisdom that illumines the world, the wisdom that sustains it, the wisdom that understands living beings and every dharma, and the boundless wisdom that knows the perfectly awakened one — the Tathāgata proclaims these for him, causing every one of them to be entirely exhausted."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The verse's tenth abode closes the entire hymn's own abode sequence by echoing the prose almost word for word — the wisdom that even a Dharma-prince cannot know, the tenfold list of the buddhas' own wisdom given here as a compressed catalogue rather than the prose's fuller enumeration. Read across all ten abodes together, the hymn's own proportions tell a story the prose alone did not make as visible: ninety-two lines for the first stirring of the awakening-mind, and roughly a dozen apiece for everything that follows, all the way to consecration itself. The distance from resolve to full realization, measured in verse, turns out to be far shorter than the distance from not yet having resolved to having resolved at all.
