Windows and Doorways Made of Maṇi

The lion-throne's description reaches its architectural details — windows and doorways formed entirely of maṇi jewels, a sovereign mind-jewel granting whatever is wished, and necklace after necklace of pearl, lapis lazuli, and maṇi hanging down on every side — before closing with a hundred million kinds of fragrance, each perfuming the ten directions or every buddha-land without ever once fading.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 175 — the lion-throne's description concludes Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

From Windows of Jewel to Fragrance Without End

The throne's catalog moves from ribbons and inset ornaments to its very architecture — windows, doorways, and a treasury all formed of named varieties of maṇi jewel — before turning to necklace after necklace draped around it. The description closes, fittingly, on scent: a hundred million kinds of fragrance, several of them explicitly said never to fade, perfuming not just the throne's own hall but every buddha-land there is.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The sovereign mind-jewel that grants "whatever is sought exactly as wished" is the cintāmaṇi of Buddhist and broader Indian legend — a wish-fulfilling jewel — placed here as one item among a hundred million others rather than singled out for special comment, which is itself a kind of comment: on a throne built to this scale, even a wish-fulfilling jewel is simply one more variety of maṇi. The fragrances that close the passage make an unusually explicit claim — several are said to perfume not merely the room but "every buddha-land," and to do so "never once ceasing or fading," extending the throne's own scale from the merely vast to the literally endless.
