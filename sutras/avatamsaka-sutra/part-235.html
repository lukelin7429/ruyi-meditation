Ten Kinds of Buddha-Bliss

Vajra Banner Bodhisattva opens the third dedication — the dedication equal to every buddha — describing a bodhisattva who accords with and studies the very path of dedication followed by every buddha of the past, future, and present. Beholding every form and every touch, pleasant or unpleasant, without love or hatred, their mind attains sovereign freedom; they then dedicate this very peace toward ten kinds of buddha-bliss, from the Buddha's inconceivable abiding to conduct that never changes or alters.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 235 — the third dedication opens, ten kinds of buddha-bliss Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Neither Love Nor Hatred

The third dedication begins not with a vow but with a description of equanimity: the bodhisattva, following the very path every buddha has walked, beholds every form and every touch — pleasant or unpleasant alike — without giving rise to love or hatred, and their mind attains sovereign freedom. Only once this freedom brings its own peace and happiness does the bodhisattva turn outward again, dedicating that very peace toward the growth of ten distinct kinds of bliss belonging to every buddha.

Chinese Source

「佛子！云何為菩薩摩訶薩等一切佛迴向？「佛子！此菩薩摩訶薩隨順修學去、來、現在諸佛世尊迴向之道。如是修學迴向道時，見一切色乃至觸法若美、若惡，不生愛憎，心得自在；無諸過失，廣大清淨；歡喜悅樂，離諸憂惱；心意柔軟，諸根清涼。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Child of the Buddha! What is called the bodhisattva mahāsattva's dedication equal to every buddha? Child of the Buddha! This bodhisattva mahāsattva accords with and studies the very path of dedication followed by every buddha, every World-Honored One, of the past, future, and present. When studying and cultivating the path of dedication in just this way, beholding every form, and even every object of touch, whether pleasant or unpleasant, they give rise to neither love nor hatred, their mind attaining sovereign freedom; free of every fault, vast and expansive, entirely pure; joyful and delighted, leaving behind every worry and affliction; their mind and intention gentle and soft, every one of their faculties cool and refreshed.

Chinese Source

佛子！菩薩摩訶薩獲得如是安樂之時，復更發心迴向諸佛，作如是念：『願以我今所種善根，令諸佛樂轉更增勝，所謂：不可思議佛所住樂、無有等比佛三昧樂、不可限量大慈悲樂、一切諸佛解脫之樂、無有邊際大神通樂、最極尊重大自在樂、廣大究竟無量力樂、離諸知覺寂靜之樂、住無礙住恒正定樂、行無二行不變異樂。』

Ru-Yi Translation

Child of the Buddha! When the bodhisattva mahāsattva obtains peace and happiness such as this, they further give rise to the resolve to dedicate it toward every buddha, giving rise to this thought: 'May the wholesome roots I now plant cause the bliss of every buddha to grow ever more supreme — namely: the inconceivable bliss of the Buddha's own abiding, the unequaled bliss of the Buddha's own samādhi, the immeasurable bliss of great loving-kindness and compassion, the bliss of liberation belonging to every buddha, the boundless bliss of great spiritual power, the most utterly honored bliss of great sovereign freedom, the vast and ultimate bliss of immeasurable power, the tranquil bliss free of every perception and cognition, the bliss of abiding in the unobstructed abode, forever in right concentration, and the bliss of practicing non-dual conduct, never changing or altering.'

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The dedication's very name — equal to every buddha — is enacted before it is explained: the bodhisattva's own peace, attained through equanimity toward form and touch, is offered not as a gift given upward to a superior, but as a wish that the buddhas' own bliss might increase, as though peace understood deeply enough naturally seeks to multiply itself in whoever already embodies it most fully.
