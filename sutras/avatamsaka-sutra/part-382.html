Every Sentient Being Attains a Pure, Wondrous Voice

One of the longest single vows in this dedication so far: forty-four distinct qualities of voice, wished upon every sentient being at once — soft as a heavenly drum, pervading every buddha-land, unbound by any world, fearless, and finally, a voice able to universally awaken every sentient being sleeping through the long night of ignorance.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 382 — forty-four qualities of a pure, wondrous voice Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Forty-Four Qualities of Voice

The list moves from the sensory (soft, lovable, joyful) to the cosmic (pervading every buddha-land, filling the whole dharma-realm) to the doctrinal (a voice that is a treasury of wisdom for the inexpressible letters and phrases of the Dharma, a voice of vajra words), before closing on the most concrete image in the whole passage: a voice that reaches sentient beings still asleep, deep in the long night.

Ru-Yi Translation

"Child of the Buddha! This bodhisattva mahāsattva further dedicates the wholesome roots born from the giving of Dharma in this way, namely: causing every sentient being to attain a pure, wondrous voice, to attain a soft voice, to attain a voice like a heavenly drum, to attain an immeasurable, countless, inconceivable voice, to attain a voice that is lovable and delightful, to attain a pure voice, to attain a voice that pervades every buddha-land, to attain a voice adorned with hundreds of thousands of nayutas of inexpressible merit, to attain a lofty, far-reaching voice, to attain a vast voice, to attain a voice that extinguishes every distraction, to attain a voice that fills the whole dharma-realm, to attain a voice that gathers in the language of every sentient being; to attain the wisdom of every sentient being's own boundless sound, to attain the wisdom of every pure language and sound, to attain the wisdom of immeasurable language and sound, to attain the wisdom of the most sovereign voice entering every sound; to attain a voice adorned with every purity, to attain a voice that no world ever tires of, to attain a voice ultimately unbound by any world, to attain a joyful voice, to attain a buddha's own pure, spoken voice, to attain a voice, universally heard, that expounds every buddha's own Dharma, far removed from the obscuration of ignorance, to attain a voice that causes every sentient being to attain the adornment of every dharma's own dhāraṇī, to attain a voice that expounds every immeasurable kind of dharma, to attain a voice that universally reaches every immeasurable assembly at the seat of enlightenment throughout the dharma-realm, to attain a voice that universally upholds the inconceivable vajra words of the Dharma, to attain a voice that opens up and reveals every dharma, to attain a voice that is a treasury of wisdom able to expound the distinct letters and phrases of the inexpressible, to attain a voice that expounds every dharma without attachment, unceasing, to attain a voice that illuminates every dharma with light, to attain a voice able to cause every world to become pure, ultimately reaching omniscience, to attain a voice that universally gathers in the meaning of every dharma's own words, to attain a voice of sovereign, unobstructed spiritual power and protection, and to attain the voice of wisdom that reaches the farther shore of every world. He further, through these wholesome roots, causes every sentient being to attain a voice that is never inferior, to attain a fearless voice, to attain a voice free of defilement and attachment, to attain a voice of joy throughout every assembly at the seat of enlightenment, to attain a voice that harmoniously accords, wondrous and beautiful, to attain a voice that skillfully expounds every buddha's own Dharma, to attain a voice that severs every sentient being's own doubt, causing them all to awaken, to attain a voice fully endowed with eloquence, and to attain a voice that universally awakens every sentient being sleeping through the long night.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Forty-four distinct qualities for a single faculty — voice — is a scale of enumeration this sūtra reserves for what it considers most consequential, and the choice is telling: not the body, not the mind alone, but the voice, the instrument of teaching itself. Everything else this dedication has vowed depends, in the end, on being spoken in a way that can actually reach someone still asleep.
