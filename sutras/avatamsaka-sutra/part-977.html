Fascicle Sixty-Four Closes Toward Loving-Conduct

Fierce-Heat closes his own teaching with a description of the great bodhisattvas' unshakeable resolve — a mind like Nārāyaṇa's own vajra-treasury, vows as steady as a wind-wheel — before confessing his limit and sending Good-Wealth south once more, to a maiden named Loving-Conduct in the city of Lion's Vigor.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 977 — fascicle sixty-four closes toward Loving-Conduct Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Mind Like Nārāyaṇa's Own Vajra-Treasury

Fierce-Heat's own confession of limitation opens with an image of unbreakable resolve — a flame of merit strong enough to burn away every trace of wrong view, a mind as steady as Nārāyaṇa's own vajra-treasury, and vows that turn like a wind-wheel without ever slowing.

Chinese Source

如諸菩薩摩訶薩大功德焰，能燒一切眾生見惑令無有餘，必不退轉無窮盡心、無懈怠心、無怯弱心，發如金剛藏那羅延心，疾修諸行無遲緩心，願如風輪普持一切精進大誓皆無退轉；而我云何能知能說彼功德行？

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the great bodhisattvas' own great flame of merit, able to burn away every being's own delusion of view without any remainder, certainly never regressing from an inexhaustible mind, a mind without laziness, a mind without timidity, giving rise to a mind like the vajra-treasury of Nārāyaṇa, swiftly cultivating every practice with a mind never sluggish, their vows like a wind-wheel universally upholding every great vow of diligence, none ever regressing — how could I possibly know or describe their own meritorious practice?

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

發如金剛藏那羅延心 — giving rise to a mind like the vajra-treasury of Nārāyaṇa. Fierce-Heat's own austerity has been a fire and a mountain of blades. His closing image reaches for a different kind of strength entirely — not heat or height, but the unbreakable firmness traditionally attributed to Nārāyaṇa, a strength that cannot be forced open at all.

South Once More, to Loving-Conduct

As with every teacher before him, Fierce-Heat's own confession closes not with silence but with directions — south to the city of Lion's Vigor, and a maiden whose very name, Loving-Conduct, promises something gentler than fire and blades.

Chinese Source

「善男子！於此南方，有城名：師子奮迅；中有童女，名曰：慈行。汝詣彼問：菩薩云何學菩薩行、修菩薩道？」時，善財童子頂禮其足，遶無數匝，辭退而去。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, to the south of here, there is a city named Lion's Vigor; within it there is a maiden named Loving-Conduct. Go to her and ask: 'How does a bodhisattva learn the bodhisattva practices, and cultivate the bodhisattva path?'" At that time, the boy Good-Wealth bowed his head at his feet, circumambulated him countless times, took his leave, and departed.
