Ten Paired Contemplations

Ten scenes, each with four roles fixed in place — Good-Wealth's own self, the good spiritual friend, the dharma spoken, and the practice cultivated — running from a sick person and a physician to a prince invested with the crown by the king's own edict.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1267 — fascicle 77 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Ten Paired Contemplations

Each of ten scenes assigns the same four roles — self, teacher, teaching, and practice — to a fresh set of images, so that the same relationship becomes, in turn, a sickbed, a journey, a river-crossing, a harvest, and a royal inheritance.

Chinese Source

「復次，善男子！汝應於自身生病苦想，於善知識生醫王想，於所說法生良藥想，於所修行生除病想；又應於自身生遠行想，於善知識生導師想，於所說法生正道想，於所修行生遠達想；又應於自身生求度想，於善知識生船師想，於所說法生舟檝想，於所修行生到岸想；又應於自身生苗稼想，於善知識生龍王想，於所說法生時雨想，於所修行生成熟想；又應於自身生貧窮想，於善知識生毘沙門王想，於所說法生財寶想，於所修行生富饒想；又應於自身生弟子想，於善知識生良工想，於所說法生技藝想，於所修行生了知想；又應於自身生恐怖想，於善知識生勇健想，於所說法生器仗想，於所修行生破怨想；又應於自身生商人想，於善知識生導師想，於所說法生珍寶想，於所修行生捃拾想；又應於自身生兒子想，於善知識生父母想，於所說法生家業想，於所修行生紹繼想；又應於自身生王子想，於善知識生大臣想，於所說法生王教想，於所修行生冠王冠想、服王服想、繫王繒想、坐王殿想。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Moreover, good son! You should give rise to the thought of a sick person toward your own self, the thought of the King of Physicians toward the good spiritual friend, the thought of good medicine toward the dharma spoken, and the thought of curing the sickness toward the practice cultivated; and, moreover, you should give rise to the thought of one on a distant journey toward your own self, the thought of a guide toward the good spiritual friend, the thought of the true road toward the dharma spoken, and the thought of arriving at the distant destination toward the practice cultivated; and, moreover, you should give rise to the thought of one seeking to cross over toward your own self, the thought of a boatman toward the good spiritual friend, the thought of the boat and oar toward the dharma spoken, and the thought of reaching the shore toward the practice cultivated; and, moreover, you should give rise to the thought of a young crop toward your own self, the thought of a nāga king toward the good spiritual friend, the thought of timely rain toward the dharma spoken, and the thought of ripening toward the practice cultivated; and, moreover, you should give rise to the thought of poverty toward your own self, the thought of King Vaiśravaṇa toward the good spiritual friend, the thought of treasure toward the dharma spoken, and the thought of abundant wealth toward the practice cultivated; and, moreover, you should give rise to the thought of a disciple toward your own self, the thought of a skilled craftsman toward the good spiritual friend, the thought of the craft itself toward the dharma spoken, and the thought of thorough understanding toward the practice cultivated; and, moreover, you should give rise to the thought of fear toward your own self, the thought of a valiant hero toward the good spiritual friend, the thought of weapons and armor toward the dharma spoken, and the thought of defeating the enemy toward the practice cultivated; and, moreover, you should give rise to the thought of a merchant toward your own self, the thought of a guide toward the good spiritual friend, the thought of rare treasure toward the dharma spoken, and the thought of gathering it in toward the practice cultivated; and, moreover, you should give rise to the thought of a child toward your own self, the thought of one's own parents toward the good spiritual friend, the thought of the family estate toward the dharma spoken, and the thought of carrying on its succession toward the practice cultivated; and, moreover, you should give rise to the thought of a prince toward your own self, the thought of a great minister toward the good spiritual friend, the thought of the king's own instruction toward the dharma spoken, and, toward the practice cultivated, the thought of wearing the king's own crown, the thought of wearing the king's own robes, the thought of binding on the king's own sash, and the thought of sitting upon the king's own throne."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

於自身生病苦想，於善知識生醫王想，於所說法生良藥想，於所修行生除病想 — the thought of a sick person toward your own self, the thought of the King of Physicians toward the good spiritual friend, the thought of good medicine toward the dharma spoken, and the thought of curing the sickness toward the practice cultivated. Ten sets in all, counted directly from the source text, each one fixing the same four roles onto a new scene — only the final set breaks the pattern, expanding its fourth role into four separate images at once: the crown, the robes, the sash, and the throne all belonging to a single royal inheritance.
