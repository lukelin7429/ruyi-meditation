Lights as Many as the Thoughts of Beings

Twenty-one stanzas of praise, and Good-Wealth begins by saying his own name. He counts the places her light comes out of — pores, brows, mouth, eyes — and arrives at the one that came into him.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1103 — verses of praise Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Body Like a Sky Full of Stars

He opens by naming himself, which he has not often done, and then reaches for the only comparison big enough — not one light, but a whole night sky of them.

Chinese Source

「我善財得見，如是大神力，
其心生歡喜，說偈而讚歎。

我見尊妙身，眾相以莊嚴；
譬如空中星，一切悉嚴淨。

所放殊勝光，無量剎塵數；
種種微妙色，普照於十方。

Ru-Yi Translation

"I, Good-Wealth, have been able to see
such great spiritual power as this;
my mind has given rise to joy,
and so I speak these verses in praise.

I see your wondrous body,
adorned with every mark;
it is like the stars in the sky,
every one of them pure and bright.

The surpassing light you send forth
is measureless as the motes of dust of the lands;
in every kind of subtle and wondrous colour
it shines universally through the ten directions.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

我善財得見 — I, Good-Wealth, have been able to see. The pronoun 我 runs through this verse seven times, which is a great deal for a hymn of praise. The other night-spirits' verses have mostly been about what they did; this one is about what happened to the person watching, and the grammar says so in the first three characters.

譬如空中星，一切悉嚴淨 — like the stars in the sky, every one of them pure and bright. The image is chosen for the teacher he is addressing. She is a night-spirit, a deity of the dark, and the only thing in her own element that could stand for the marks adorning her body is the night sky itself. He praises her with her own darkness.

One Light for Every Thought

Then the count begins, and the unit of counting is not space or time. It is how many thoughts sentient beings have had.

Chinese Source

一一毛孔放，眾生心數光；
一一光明端，皆出寶蓮華；

華中出化身，能滅眾生苦；
光中出妙香，普熏於眾生；

復雨種種華，供養一切佛。

Ru-Yi Translation

From each single pore you send forth
lights as many as the thoughts of beings;
and at the tip of each single light
a jewelled lotus flower comes out;

from within the flower a transformation body comes out,
able to extinguish the suffering of beings;
from within the light a wondrous fragrance comes out,
perfuming beings everywhere;

and it rains down every kind of flower besides,
making offerings to every buddha.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

眾生心數光 — lights as many as the thoughts of beings. Five characters, and they replace the sūtra's usual measure. Everywhere else the unit is 佛剎微塵數, the fine motes of dust in a buddha-land, which is a quantity of matter. Here the quantity is mental. If one light exists for every thought any being has ever had, then the light is not answering the world; it is answering the inside of heads, one by one, and the number of lights is exactly the number of times someone has needed one.

一一光明端，皆出寶蓮華；華中出化身 — at the tip of each single light a jewelled lotus flower comes out, and from within the flower a transformation body. The chain is three deep and each link is smaller and more concrete than the last: pore, light, flower, body. The vision does not expand outward into vastness. It telescopes down a single ray until it arrives at somebody able to end suffering — which is where, in this sūtra, the vast always ends up going.

Brows, Mouth, Eyes

Three more stanzas, three more openings in the same body, each with its own scale of comparison — a mountain, a sun, a moon.

Chinese Source

兩眉放妙光，量與須彌等，
普觸諸含識，令滅愚癡闇。

口放清淨光，譬如無量日，
普照於廣大，毘盧舍那境。

眼放清淨光，譬如無量月，
普照十方剎，悉滅世癡翳。

Ru-Yi Translation

From between your brows a wondrous light goes out,
its measure equal to Mount Sumeru;
it touches every being that has consciousness
and makes the dark of their folly cease.

From your mouth a pure light goes out,
like measureless suns,
shining universally upon the vast
domain of Vairocana.

From your eyes a pure light goes out,
like measureless moons,
shining universally on the lands of the ten directions
and dissolving the world's cataract of delusion.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

毘盧舍那境 — the domain of Vairocana. This is the single most striking philological detail in the fascicle. Across the whole eighty-fascicle T279, the buddha's name is written 毘盧遮那 one hundred and twenty-two times and 毘盧舍那 exactly once, and the once is here, in this line. Both spell the same Sanskrit name; 舍 is the older transliteration, familiar from Buddhabhadra's sixty-fascicle version. In a verse, where the line must come out at five characters and the ear governs, the older syllable slipped through the standardisation that had regularised every other occurrence in the sūtra.

The verb 放, to send forth, occurs six times in these twenty-one stanzas, and the sources it names are four: 毛孔, the pores; 兩眉, the brows; 口, the mouth; 眼, the eyes. The comparisons climb as the openings rise — a mountain for the brows, suns for the mouth, moons for the eyes — and the two great luminaries of the night-and-day cycle are handed to a spirit of the night.

A Shape to Match Whoever Is Looking

From light the verse turns to body, and the criterion for what body she takes is not her own dignity but the shape of the being in front of her.

Chinese Source

現化種種身，相狀等眾生，
充滿十方界，度脫三有海。

妙身遍十方，普現眾生前，
滅除水火賊，王等一切怖。

Ru-Yi Translation

You manifest every kind of transformed body,
its shape and appearance matching the beings themselves;
they fill the worlds of the ten directions
and ferry beings out of the ocean of the three realms of existence.

Your wondrous body pervades the ten directions,
appearing universally before beings,
doing away with every fear — of water, of fire,
of thieves, of kings, and the rest.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

滅除水火賊，王等一切怖 — doing away with every fear, of water, of fire, of thieves, of kings, and the rest. Four plain catastrophes, and they are the oldest list in the tradition of what can ruin a household: flood, fire, robbery, and the state. Part 1102 gave the same teacher a list of nineteen fears in which almost nothing was physical. The verse restores the other half. She is not only the remedy for spiritual dread; she is also what you call on when the river rises or the tax officer arrives.

The Light That Came Into Me

Now the verse stops describing her and reports what her light did, which is the reason there is a verse at all. It credits the previous teacher by name.

Chinese Source

我承喜目教，今得詣尊所，
見尊眉間相，放大清淨光，

普照十方海，悉滅一切闇，
顯現神通力，而來入我身。

我遇圓滿光，心生大歡喜，
得總持三昧，普見十方佛。

Ru-Yi Translation

Following the instruction of Joyful Eye,
I have now been able to come into your presence;
I see the mark between your brows
sending out a great pure light,

shining universally on the ocean of the ten directions,
extinguishing every darkness,
displaying its spiritual power,
and then coming to enter my own body.

Meeting that perfect and complete light,
my mind gave rise to great joy;
I attained dhāraṇī and samādhi
and saw everywhere the buddhas of the ten directions.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

我承喜目教，今得詣尊所 — following the instruction of Joyful Eye, I have now been able to come into your presence. He credits his previous teacher in his praise of the present one. The pilgrimage's whole architecture rests on this small courtesy: every teacher names the next, and the pilgrim keeps saying who sent him. It is also, quietly, the answer to the fear listed in Part 1102 as 離善知識怖 — being parted from a good spiritual friend. He has not been parted from her. Her instruction is what is standing here.

放大清淨光 … 而來入我身 — sending out a great pure light … and then coming to enter my own body. The brows have already been named once in this verse, two stanzas earlier, as one of the four places her light comes out of. The verse returns to them here for a different reason. The first time they were a feature to be admired from outside; the second time they are where the thing that is now inside him came from.

Three Kinds of Land in a Speck of Dust

The samādhi's content, now put into verse — and the taxonomy of worlds from Part 1101 comes back, shortened to three, with the mixed cases described closely enough that we can finally tell what they mean.

Chinese Source

我於所經處，悉見諸微塵，
一一微塵中，各見塵數剎。

或有無量剎，一切咸濁穢，
眾生受諸苦，常悲歎號泣。

或有染淨剎，少樂多憂苦；
示現三乘像，往彼而救度。

或有淨染剎，眾生所樂見，
菩薩常充滿，住持諸佛法。

Ru-Yi Translation

In every place I have passed through
I see all the fine motes of dust,
and within each single mote of dust
I see lands as numerous as those motes.

Some are measureless lands
wholly turbid and foul,
where beings undergo every suffering
and grieve and cry aloud without ceasing.

Some are lands defiled more than pure,
with little joy and much sorrow and pain;
you manifest the forms of the three vehicles
and go there to rescue and deliver them.

Some are lands pure more than defiled,
which beings delight to look upon,
where bodhisattvas are always thronging
and uphold and sustain the dharma of the buddhas.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

或有染淨剎，少樂多憂苦 … 或有淨染剎，眾生所樂見 — some are lands defiled more than pure, with little joy and much sorrow; some are lands pure more than defiled, which beings delight to look upon. This pair settles a question left open in Part 1101, where the prose listed 或有世界雜穢清淨，或有世界清淨雜穢 with nothing to indicate what the doubled compounds meant. Here the verse gives each of them a description, and the descriptions run in opposite directions. So the compounds are proportions, not sequences, and the leading character is the dominant one — a 染淨 land is mostly foul, a 淨染 land is mostly clean. The reading note on Part 1101 has been corrected accordingly.

示現三乘像，往彼而救度 — you manifest the forms of the three vehicles and go there to rescue and deliver them. It is the mixed and mostly-miserable land, not the pure one, that gets the three vehicles. Where conditions are bad, the full apparatus of graded teaching is deployed; where bodhisattvas already throng, no vehicles are mentioned at all. Skilful means is a function of how bad things are.

And Vairocana Purified Them Long Ago

The verse ends by pushing the credit backwards. Every pure land inside every speck of dust was somebody's past work — and then the last stanza finds her already there in all of them.

Chinese Source

一一微塵中，無量淨剎海；
毘盧遮那佛，往劫所嚴淨。

佛於一切剎，悉坐菩提樹，
成道轉法輪，度脫諸群生。

我見普救天，於彼無量剎，
一切諸佛所，普皆往供養。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Within each single mote of dust
are measureless oceans of pure lands,
which the Buddha Vairocana
adorned and purified in eons gone by.

In every one of those lands the Buddha
sits beneath the tree of awakening,
accomplishes the way and turns the dharma-wheel
and delivers the whole host of living beings.

I see you, the god Who Universally Rescues,
in all those measureless lands,
going everywhere to where every buddha is
to make your offerings there."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

毘盧遮那佛，往劫所嚴淨 — which the Buddha Vairocana adorned and purified in eons gone by. Two stanzas after the outlying spelling 毘盧舍那, the name returns in its standard form, and the difference between the two occurrences is not only orthographic. The first was a domain that light shone on; this one is an agent who did work. The pure lands in the dust are not a metaphysical given. They were made, and somebody made them, and the making is in the past tense.

我見普救天 — I see you, the god Who Universally Rescues. Her name is 普救眾生妙德 and her rank is 夜神, night-spirit; the five-character line has room for neither, so the verse abbreviates the name to 普救 and promotes the rank to 天, a god. It is not an error of status. In the prose that immediately follows, Good-Wealth opens his question to her with the vocative 天神, and it is worth noticing that a being the sūtra has been calling a spirit of the dark is addressed, the moment anyone speaks to her directly, as a deity.

Part 1103 Complete

This continues chapter thirty-nine, 入法界品, in fascicle seventy. Part 1104 drops out of verse into prose, where Good-Wealth asks the name of her liberation and how long she has had it, and she answers that the place is beyond gods, humans, and both of the lesser vehicles alike, listing nine kinds of person whose domain it actually is.
