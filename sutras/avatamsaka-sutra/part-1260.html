Not a Single Vow

A warning against ever thinking one virtue, one practice, or one vow could be enough opens onto a list that answers its own warning by sheer accumulation — forty-eight measureless things in this Part alone, with more still to come.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1260 — fascicle 77 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Not a Single Vow

Virtue-Born and Possessing Virtue name the very trap Good-Wealth might fall into — mistaking any one accomplishment for the whole path — and answer it not with an argument but with an enumeration that simply keeps going.

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son! You should not cultivate a single wholesome deed, illuminate a single dharma, perform a single practice, raise a single vow, attain a single prediction, or abide in a single forbearance, and thereby give rise to the thought that this is the ultimate; nor should you, with a mind of limited measure, practice the six pāramitās, abide in the ten grounds, purify buddha-lands, or serve good spiritual friends. Why is this? Good son! A bodhisattva-mahāsattva should plant measureless wholesome roots; should gather measureless requisites of bodhi; should cultivate measureless causes of bodhi; should learn measureless skillful dedications of merit; should transform the measureless realms of beings; should know the measureless minds of beings; should know the measureless faculties of beings; should discern the measureless understandings of beings; should contemplate the measureless conduct of beings; should tame measureless beings; should cut off measureless afflictions; should purify measureless habitual karma; should extinguish measureless wrong views; should remove the measureless defiled mind; should give rise to the measureless pure mind; should pull out the measureless poisoned arrows of suffering; should dry up the measureless ocean of craving and desire; should shatter the measureless darkness of ignorance; should topple the measureless mountain of self-conceit; should sever the measureless bonds of birth-and-death; should cross over the measureless streams of every state of existence; should exhaust the measureless ocean of taking birth; should cause measureless beings to emerge from the mire of the five desires; should cause measureless beings to leave the prison of the three realms; should place measureless beings upon the noble path; should dissolve measureless deeds of greed and desire; should purify and correct measureless deeds of anger and hatred; should crush and break measureless deeds of delusion; should transcend the measureless net of Māra; should leave behind the measureless deeds of Māra; should purify and correct a bodhisattva's own measureless desire and delight; should increase a bodhisattva's own measureless skillful means; should give rise to a bodhisattva's own measureless superior faculties; should clarify and purify a bodhisattva's own measureless resolute understanding; should enter into a bodhisattva's own measureless equality; should purify a bodhisattva's own measureless merit; should cultivate and perfect a bodhisattva's own measureless practices; should manifest a bodhisattva's own measureless practice of according with the world; should give rise to the measureless power of pure faith; should abide in the measureless power of diligence; should purify the measureless power of right mindfulness; should fulfill the measureless power of samādhi; should arouse the measureless power of pure wisdom; should make firm the measureless power of resolute understanding; should gather the measureless power of merit; should lengthen the measureless power of wisdom; should arouse the measureless power of a bodhisattva; should perfect and complete the measureless power of a tathāgata.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

汝不應修一善、照一法、行一行、發一願、得一記、住一忍，生究竟想 — you should not cultivate a single wholesome deed, illuminate a single dharma, perform a single practice, raise a single vow, attain a single prediction, or abide in a single forbearance, and thereby give rise to the thought that this is the ultimate. Six specific ways of mistaking "one" for "enough" are named before the list even begins — and the list itself, forty-eight items long in this Part alone, is the warning's own proof: no single item on it could ever have stood for the whole.
