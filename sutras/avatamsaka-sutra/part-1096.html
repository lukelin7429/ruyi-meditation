Buddhas Named Ten at a Time

Four eons, four worlds, forty buddhas named in order — and after each set of ten, the same admission. She served every one of them, and it had not yet worked. This is the most patient passage in the fascicle, and its subject is how long it takes.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1096 — buddhas named ten at a time Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Four Cycles, Each Ending the Same Way

The pattern is fixed: an eon and a world are named, then ten buddhas in numbered order, then one stanza saying it was not yet enough.

第三妙寶幢；第四虛空智；
第五蓮華藏；第六無礙慧；

如是等諸佛，我悉曾供養，
然未得慧眼，入於解脫海。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first buddha, Ocean of Merit; the second, Lamp of Merit; the third, Wondrous Jewel Banner; the fourth, Wisdom of Empty Space; the fifth, Lotus Treasury; the sixth, Unobstructed Wisdom; the seventh, Dharma-Moon King; the eighth, Wheel of the Lamp of Wisdom; the ninth, honored among the two-footed, Jewel-Flame Mountain Lamp King; the tenth, the Tamer, Flower-Light-Sound of the Three Times.

Buddhas such as these I served with offerings, every one; and yet I had not gained the eye of wisdom, nor entered the ocean of liberation.

After this there came in order the land All-Jewel Light, its eon named Heaven-Surpassing, in which five hundred buddhas arose in the world.

The first, Moonlight Wheel; the second, named Sun-Lamp; the third, named Light Banner; the fourth, Jewel Sumeru; the fifth, named Flower Flame; the sixth, called Ocean of Lamps; the seventh, Blazing Buddha; the eighth, Heavenly Treasury Buddha; the ninth, Radiance-King Banner; the tenth, Universal Wisdom Light King.

Buddhas such as these I served with offerings, every one; and still, among all dharmas, I reckoned as existent what does not exist.

After this there was again an eon, named Brahmā Radiance; its world was Lotus Lamp, most surpassing and wondrous in its adornment.

There were measureless buddhas there, each with a measureless assembly; I served them all with offerings, honoring them and hearing the dharma.

The first, Jewel Sumeru Buddha; the second, Ocean of Merit Buddha; the third, Dharma-Realm Sound Buddha; the fourth, Dharma Thunder-Peal Buddha; the fifth, named Dharma Banner Buddha; the sixth, named Earth Light Buddha; the seventh, named Light of Dharma-Power; the eighth, named Awakening of Empty Space; the ninth, Sumeru Light; the tenth, Cloud of Merit.

Tathāgatas such as these I served with offerings, every one; and yet I could not clearly understand the dharma so as to enter the ocean of the buddhas.

Next after this there was again an eon named Moon of Merit; and at that time there was a world whose name was Banner of Merit.

There were buddhas there, eighty nayutas of them; and with wondrous offerings, with a profound mind, I revered and served them all.

The first, Gandharva King; the second, named Great Tree King; the third, Merit Sumeru; the fourth, Jewel Eye Buddha; the fifth, Vairocana; the sixth, Light Adornment; the seventh, Dharma Ocean Buddha; the eighth, Light Victory Buddha; the ninth, named Worthy Victory Buddha; the tenth, Dharma King Buddha.

Buddhas such as these I served with offerings, every one; and yet I had not gained the profound wisdom that enters the ocean of all dharmas.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

如是等諸佛，我悉曾供養，然未得慧眼 — buddhas such as these I served with offerings, every one; and yet I had not gained the eye of wisdom. The refrain closes all four cycles, and each time it names a different shortfall: no eye of wisdom, no entry into the ocean of liberation; then a false reckoning of existence; then no clear understanding; then no profound wisdom. Forty buddhas served, and the verse's whole point is that service alone did not do it.

尚於諸法中，無而計為有 — and still, among all dharmas, I reckoned as existent what does not exist. This is the sharpest of the four admissions and the most technical: 無而計為有, taking the non-existent for existent, is the root error the Madhyamaka analysis addresses. She had been making offerings to buddhas for ten eons and was still making it.

第九兩足尊，寶焰山燈王；第十調御師，三世華光音 — the ninth, honored among the two-footed, Jewel-Flame Mountain Lamp King; the tenth, the Tamer, Flower-Light-Sound of the Three Times. The verse has to fit two names into five characters each, and for the last two of the first ten it borrows standard epithets — 兩足尊, honored among bipeds, and 調御師, the Tamer of beings — as line-fillers. The metre is doing work the prose lists never have to.

第五盧舍那 — the fifth, Vairocana. In the fourth cycle, among forty otherwise unfamiliar names, the buddha of this sūtra's own opening vision appears as one entry in a list — the fifth of ten, in an eon called Moon of Merit, served like the others and no differently.
