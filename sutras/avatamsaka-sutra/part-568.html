The Doubling Ladder Continues

Fifty more rungs of the Buddha's answer to the Bodhisattva Mind King, each one still the square of the name before it. A handful of these names briefly break from pure sound into plain meaning — the Tamed, the Prideless, the Unmoving, the Utmost — before the chain returns to sound alone for its final long stretch.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 568 — the doubling ladder, rungs 41 through 90 Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Fifty More Rungs

The ladder begun in Part 567 continues unbroken from Helipu. Partway through this stretch, four names step out of pure sound into ordinary meaning, before the chain returns to invented names for its remainder.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

調伏調伏為一離憍慢，離憍慢離憍慢為一不動，不動不動為一極量 — a Tamed of Tameds make one Prideless; a Prideless of Pridelesses make one Unmoving; an Unmoving of Unmovings make one Utmost. For four rungs in the middle of this stretch, the ladder briefly abandons invented sound for plain Chinese words — taming, pride, stillness, an upper limit — words that could just as easily describe stages of meditation as steps in a number sequence, before the text returns to pure transliteration for the remaining rungs.
