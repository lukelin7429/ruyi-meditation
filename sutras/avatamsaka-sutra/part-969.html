The Kings of Nirmāṇarati and Tuṣita Testify

Two further heavens add their own witness — Nirmāṇarati, whose kings confess the fire reached even their own palace-maidens, and Tuṣita, whose kings and gods rained down fragrance before admitting the same teaching left them wanting nothing more than the roots of goodness it planted in them.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 969 — the kings of Nirmāṇarati and Tuṣita testify Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Even the Palace-Maidens Were Touched

Nirmāṇarati's kings testify to something none of the previous groups mentioned — the fire's light did not stop at their own palaces, but reached their palace-maidens directly, leaving both kings and maidens alike no longer wanting the pleasures of desire at all.

Chinese Source

復有十千化樂天王，於虛空中，作天音樂，恭敬供養，作如是言：「善男子！此婆羅門五熱炙身時，其火光明照我宮殿諸莊嚴具及諸采女，能令我等不受欲樂、不求欲樂、身心柔軟，即與眾俱來詣其所。時，婆羅門為我說法，能令我等心得清淨、心得明潔、心得純善、心得柔軟、心生歡喜，乃至令得清淨十力清淨之身，生無量身，乃至令得佛身、佛語、佛聲、佛心，具足成就一切智智。」

Ru-Yi Translation

There were further ten thousand kings of the Nirmāṇarati heaven, in the sky above, making heavenly music, reverently making offerings, who spoke these words: "Good son, when this brahmin was scorching his own body with the five fires, the light of that fire illuminated our own palaces' every adornment and every one of our palace-maidens, causing us no longer to accept the pleasures of desire, no longer to seek the pleasures of desire, our bodies and minds becoming soft and pliant, so we, together with our whole assembly, came at once to his place. At that time, the brahmin taught the dharma for us, causing our minds to become pure, to become bright and clean, to become purely good, to become soft and pliant, and to give rise to joy, and even causing us to attain a pure body of the pure ten powers, giving rise to measureless bodies, and even causing us to attain a buddha's own body, a buddha's own speech, a buddha's own voice, a buddha's own mind, fully and completely accomplishing the wisdom of omniscience."

Fragrance from Tuṣita, Contentment as the Gift

Tuṣita's own contribution to this chorus of testimony is fragrance rained from the sky, and its own gift is the simplest one yet named — not power, not a buddha's body, but simply having few desires and knowing contentment.

Chinese Source

復有十千兜率天王、天子、天女、無量眷屬，於虛空中，雨眾妙香，恭敬頂禮，作如是言：「善男子！此婆羅門五熱炙身時，令我等諸天及其眷屬，於自宮殿無有樂著，共詣其所。聞其說法，能令我等不貪境界，少欲知足，心生歡喜，心得充滿，生諸善根，發菩提心，乃至圓滿一切佛法。」

Ru-Yi Translation

There were further ten thousand kings of Tuṣita, together with sons of gods, daughters of gods, and measureless retinues, in the sky above, raining down every kind of wondrous fragrance, reverently bowing their heads in homage, who spoke these words: "Good son, when this brahmin was scorching his own body with the five fires, it caused all of us gods and our own retinues to feel no delight or attachment for our own palaces, so we came together to his place. Hearing him teach the dharma, it caused us no longer to crave any domain, to have few desires and know contentment, our minds giving rise to joy, our minds becoming full, giving rise to every root of goodness, giving rise to the mind of bodhi, and even perfecting every buddha-dharma."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

少欲知足 — to have few desires and know contentment. Among every gift named across these testimonies — sovereignty, a buddha's own body, measureless forms — Tuṣita's own gift is the plainest, and perhaps for that reason the hardest: simply wanting less than before.
