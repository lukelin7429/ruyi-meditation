Walking Through Walls, Sitting in Mid-Air

The spiritual power that neither abides nor acts turns out to look, in practice, exactly like the miraculous feats this sūtra reserves for its most advanced figures — a body that passes through solid walls, enters earth as though it were water, and touches the sun and moon with an outstretched hand.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 915 — walking through walls, sitting in mid-air Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Body That Ignores Every Ordinary Limit

Every physical constraint a body normally obeys is reversed one by one: walls become passable as empty space, water becomes as solid as earth and earth as fluid as water, and Well-Abiding's own body rises to touch the sun and moon and reach the height of Brahmā's own palace.

Chinese Source

「善男子！我以得此神通力故，於虛空中或行、或住、或坐、或臥、或隱、或顯，或現一身，或現多身，穿度牆壁猶如虛空；於虛空中結跏趺坐，往來自在猶如飛鳥；入地如水，履水如地，遍身上下普出煙焰如大火聚。或時震動一切大地，或時以手摩觸日月，或現其身高至梵宮。或現燒香雲，或現寶焰雲，或現變化雲，或現光網雲，皆悉廣大彌覆十方。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son! Because I have attained this spiritual power, within empty space I sometimes walk, sometimes stand still, sometimes sit, sometimes lie down, sometimes hide myself, sometimes make myself manifest; I sometimes manifest a single body, sometimes manifest many bodies; I pass through walls just as though passing through empty space; I sit cross-legged within empty space, coming and going freely just like a bird in flight; I enter the earth as though entering water, and walk upon water as though walking upon earth; smoke and flame pour forth from every part of my own body, above and below, like a great mass of fire. Sometimes I shake the whole of the great earth; sometimes I reach out my hand and touch the sun and moon; sometimes I manifest my own body rising as high as the palace of Brahmā. Sometimes I manifest a cloud of burning incense, sometimes a cloud of jeweled flames, sometimes a cloud of transformation, sometimes a cloud of a net of light — every one of them vast, spreading to cover the ten directions entirely.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

入地如水，履水如地 — I enter the earth as though entering water, and walk upon water as though walking upon earth. This single reversal — the two most solid and most fluid things in the ordinary world, exchanging their own properties — captures the whole point of the previous Part's "spiritual power that neither abides nor acts": nothing here is fixed enough to obstruct him.

Unspeakably Many Worlds, Every Body an Offering

A single thought-moment carries Well-Abiding past worlds beyond counting, and at every buddha's own place he manifests bodies as numerous as motes of dust, each one raining down its own cloud of offerings — ten distinct kinds, from flowers to curtains, poured out at once.

Chinese Source

或一念中過於東方一世界、二世界、百世界、千世界、百千世界，乃至無量世界，乃至不可說不可說世界；或過閻浮提微塵數世界，或過不可說不可說佛剎微塵數世界。於彼一切諸佛國土佛世尊前聽聞說法，一一佛所現無量佛剎微塵數差別身，一一身雨無量佛剎微塵數供養雲，所謂：一切華雲、一切香雲、一切鬘雲、一切末香雲、一切塗香雲、一切蓋雲、一切衣雲、一切幢雲、一切幡雲、一切帳雲，以一切身雲而為供養。

Ru-Yi Translation

In a single thought-moment, I sometimes pass through, in the eastern direction, one world, two worlds, a hundred worlds, a thousand worlds, a hundred thousand worlds, and on up to measureless worlds, and on up to unspeakably unspeakable worlds; I sometimes pass through worlds as numerous as every mote of dust in Jambudvīpa, and I sometimes pass through worlds as numerous as every mote of dust in unspeakably unspeakable buddha-lands. In every one of those buddha-lands, before the World-Honored buddhas, I hear and listen to the dharma spoken; at every one of those buddhas' own places, I manifest bodies as numerous and varied as every mote of dust in measureless buddha-lands, and from every one of those bodies I rain down clouds of offerings as numerous as every mote of dust in measureless buddha-lands, namely: every kind of flower-cloud, every kind of incense-cloud, every kind of garland-cloud, every kind of powdered-incense-cloud, every kind of anointing-incense-cloud, every kind of canopy-cloud, every kind of garment-cloud, every kind of banner-cloud, every kind of pennant-cloud, and every kind of curtain-cloud, making offerings with every kind of body-cloud.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

一一身雨無量佛剎微塵數供養雲 — from every one of those bodies I rain down clouds of offerings as numerous as every mote of dust in measureless buddha-lands. The multiplication compounds twice over here: not one offering but a cloud of them, not one body making that offering but bodies as numerous as dust motes, each one raining its own cloud in turn.
