Seven Things It Has No End Of

Asked what her liberation is, she gives seven qualities — five of which are simply refusals of a limit — and then stops defining it and starts comparing it to things, beginning with the four elements.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1125 — what the liberation is like Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Seven Things It Has No End Of

Seven clauses, each a quality and a reason. Five of the seven qualities begin with the same character, and it is a negative.

Chinese Source

又，善男子！此解脫無邊，普入一切法界門故；此解脫無盡，等發一切智性心故；此解脫無際，入無際畔一切眾生心想中故；此解脫甚深，寂靜智慧所知境故；此解脫廣大，周遍一切如來境故；此解脫無壞，菩薩智眼之所知故；此解脫無底，盡於法界之源底故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Again, good son, this liberation is boundless, because it enters universally into every gate of the dharma-realm; this liberation is inexhaustible, because it gives rise equally to the mind whose nature is all-knowing wisdom; this liberation is without edge, because it enters into the thoughts of all beings, which have no edge or border; this liberation is profound, because it is the domain known by quiescent wisdom; this liberation is vast, because it pervades every tathāgata's domain; this liberation is indestructible, because it is what the bodhisattva's eye of wisdom knows; this liberation is bottomless, because it exhausts the very source and floor of the dharma-realm.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

無邊 … 無盡 … 無際 … 無壞 … 無底 — boundless, inexhaustible, without edge, indestructible, bottomless. Five of the seven qualities open with 無, without, and only two are stated positively: 甚深, profound, and 廣大, vast. Asked what a liberation is, this sūtra's first instinct is to say where it stops, and then to report that it does not.

此解脫無際，入無際畔一切眾生心想中故 — this liberation is without edge, because it enters into the thoughts of all beings, which have no edge or border. The word 無際 is used twice in one clause, once of the liberation and once of what it enters. The reason given for its limitlessness is not that it is large but that its object is. A gate that goes into people's thinking has to be as unbounded as thinking is, and the sūtra derives the property from the destination rather than from the traveller.

此解脫無底，盡於法界之源底故 — this liberation is bottomless, because it exhausts the very source and floor of the dharma-realm. The character 底, floor or bottom, appears twice, once denied and once affirmed. It is bottomless precisely because it has reached the bottom — which is not a contradiction so much as a statement that when you arrive at the floor of the dharma-realm there is nothing there to stand on.

A Gate, and Two Things It Is Not

At the eighth clause the grammar shifts. A particle appears that was not there before, and stays for the remaining twenty-five clauses.

Chinese Source

此解脫者即是普門，於一事中普見一切諸神變故；此解脫者終不可取，一切法身等無二故；此解脫者終無有生，以能了知如幻法故；此解脫者猶如影像，一切智願光所生故；此解脫者猶如變化，化生菩薩諸勝行故；

Ru-Yi Translation

This liberation is the universal gate itself, because within a single thing it sees universally every spiritual transformation there is; this liberation can never be grasped, because every dharma-body is equal and undivided; this liberation is never born at all, because it is able to know fully that dharmas are like an illusion; this liberation is like a reflected image, because it is produced by the light of the vow of all-knowing wisdom; this liberation is like a magical transformation, because it produces by transformation all the surpassing practices of a bodhisattva;

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The seven clauses before this one all read 此解脫, this liberation; every clause from here to the end of the run reads 此解脫者, with the topic particle 者 added. The count is exact — seven without it, twenty-five with — and the seam falls precisely where the sentences stop asserting properties and start asserting identities and likenesses. Chinese marks the change of what kind of statement is being made by adding one character, and does not explain.

此解脫者於一事中普見一切諸神變故 — because within a single thing it sees universally every spiritual transformation there is. 一事, a single thing or affair, is unusually plain vocabulary for this sūtra, which normally reaches for a mote of dust or a pore. The claim is the standard Huayan one at its most domestic: whatever is in front of you, all of it is in there.

此解脫者猶如影像 — this liberation is like a reflected image. This exact simile is used twice in the run, and the second occurrence, some way further on, gives a different reason: there, 隨眾生心而照現故, because it shines forth according to the minds of beings. Here the reason is that it is produced by the light of a vow. The same image serves once for where the liberation comes from and once for how it appears, and the sūtra does not flag the repetition.

Earth, Water, Fire, Wind

Then the four elements in order, one clause each — and two of them are about water, pulling in opposite directions.

Chinese Source

此解脫者猶如大地，為一切眾生所依處故；此解脫者猶如大水，能以大悲潤一切故；此解脫者猶如大火，乾竭眾生貪愛水故；此解脫者猶如大風，令諸眾生速疾趣於一切智故；

Ru-Yi Translation

this liberation is like the great earth, because it is the place on which all beings rest; this liberation is like great water, because it is able to moisten everything with great compassion; this liberation is like a great fire, because it dries up the water of beings' craving and love; this liberation is like a great wind, because it makes beings hasten swiftly toward all-knowing wisdom;

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

猶如大水，能以大悲潤一切故 … 猶如大火，乾竭眾生貪愛水故 — like great water, because it moistens everything with great compassion; like a great fire, because it dries up the water of beings' craving and love. Two adjacent clauses, and the second destroys what the first is made of. The resolution is in the adjectives: there are two waters here. 大悲, great compassion, is the water that should wet everything; 貪愛, craving and love, is the water that should be boiled off. The sūtra could have avoided the collision by choosing another image for fire. It chose not to, and the result is a reader who has to notice that the same element does opposite work depending on what it is.

猶如大地，為一切眾生所依處故 — like the great earth, because it is the place on which all beings rest. The first of the four, and the humblest claim in the whole run. In Part 1115 the previous night-spirit told Good-Wealth that she had once been 主地神, an earth-spirit named Bringing Forth the Meaning of Equality. That was a different teacher in a different fascicle, and no connection is drawn here. But the image of ground that holds everything up without preference keeps returning in these night-spirit chapters, and it returns now as what a liberation is.

Ocean, Mountain, City, Space, Cloud

The similes continue, moving from things that are large to things that are empty to things that fall out of the sky.

Chinese Source

此解脫者猶如大海，種種功德莊嚴一切諸眾生故；此解脫者如須彌山，出一切智法寶海故；此解脫者如大城廓，一切妙法所莊嚴故；此解脫者猶如虛空，普容三世佛神力故；此解脫者猶如大雲，普為眾生雨法雨故；

Ru-Yi Translation

this liberation is like the great ocean, because with every kind of merit it adorns all beings; this liberation is like Mount Sumeru, because from it comes the ocean of the dharma-jewels of all-knowing wisdom; this liberation is like a great walled city, because it is adorned by every wondrous dharma; this liberation is like empty space, because it universally contains the spiritual power of the buddhas of the three times; this liberation is like a great cloud, because it rains the rain of dharma universally for beings;

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

猶如虛空，普容三世佛神力故 — like empty space, because it universally contains the spiritual power of the buddhas of the three times. The verb is 容, to hold or make room for, and it is the only reason in the run that works by having nothing in it. Every other simile explains itself by what the thing does — earth supports, water moistens, fire dries, wind speeds, clouds rain. Space qualifies by being available.

如大城廓，一切妙法所莊嚴故 — like a great walled city, because it is adorned by every wondrous dharma. Part 1122 had this same teacher meeting people who 居住城廓, live in a walled city, and sending them to 法王城, the city of the dharma-king. The image has now moved from something she says to someone else about where they live, to something she says about what she has attained. She is describing her own liberation with the furniture of her students' lives.
