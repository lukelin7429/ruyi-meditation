A Tree Named Rootless

The long series of vajra similes begins: vajra that is born only where vajra or gold is found, a tree named Rootless whose branches and fruit flourish without any root, vajra that no inferior or broken vessel can hold, and a vajra that pierces every jewel, breaks every mountain, still outranks every jewel when broken, still ends all poverty when chipped, and even in the smallest piece breaks everything it meets.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1306 — fascicle 78 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

A Tree Named Rootless

Eight more paired similes continue the pattern — seven of them drawn from vajra, with a single tree named Rootless set between the first two.

Chinese Source

「善男子！譬如金剛，唯從金剛處及金處生，非餘寶處生。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心金剛亦復如是，唯從大悲救護眾生金剛處、一切智智殊勝境界金處而生，非餘眾生善根處生。「善男子！譬如有樹，名曰：無根，不從根生，而枝、葉、華、果悉皆繁茂。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心樹亦復如是，無根可得，而能長養一切智智神通大願；枝、葉、華、果，扶疎蔭映，普覆世間。「善男子！譬如金剛，非劣惡器及以破器所能容持，唯除全具上妙之器。菩提心金剛亦復如是，非下劣眾生慳、嫉、破戒、懈怠、妄念、無智器中所能容持，亦非退失殊勝志願、散亂、惡覺眾生器中所能容持，唯除菩薩深心寶器。「善男子！譬如金剛，能穿眾寶。菩提心金剛亦復如是，悉能穿徹一切法寶。「善男子！譬如金剛，能壞眾山。菩提心金剛亦復如是，悉能摧壞諸邪見山。「善男子！譬如金剛，雖破不全，一切眾寶猶不能及。菩提心金剛亦復如是，雖復志劣，少有虧損，猶勝一切二乘功德。「善男子！譬如金剛，雖有損缺，猶能除滅一切貧窮。菩提心金剛亦復如是，雖有損缺，不進諸行，猶能捨離一切生死。「善男子！如小金剛，悉能破壞一切諸物。菩提心金剛亦復如是，入少境界，即破一切無知諸惑。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Just as vajra is born only where vajra is found or where gold is found, and is not born where other jewels are found, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own vajra that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: it is born only from the vajra-place of great compassion that rescues and protects sentient beings and from the gold-place of the supreme domain of the wisdom of all-knowing wisdom, and is not born from the place of any other being's wholesome roots. Good son! Just as there is a tree named Rootless, which does not grow from a root and yet whose branches, leaves, flowers, and fruit all flourish in abundance, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own tree that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: no root of it can be found, and yet it is able to nurture the spiritual powers and great vows of the wisdom of all-knowing wisdom, its branches, leaves, flowers, and fruit spreading wide and casting their shade over the whole world. Good son! Just as vajra cannot be held by an inferior or base vessel or by a broken vessel, but only by a vessel that is whole, complete, and of the finest quality, the vajra that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: it cannot be held within the vessel of lowly beings of stinginess, envy, breaking the precepts, indolence, deluded thoughts, and lack of wisdom, nor within the vessel of beings who have fallen away from supreme resolve and aspiration, who are distracted, or whose thoughts are evil, but only within the jeweled vessel of a bodhisattva's profound mind. Good son! Just as vajra is able to pierce every jewel, the vajra that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: it is able to pierce through every jewel of the dharma. Good son! Just as vajra is able to break every mountain, the vajra that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: it is able to crush and break every mountain of wrong views. Good son! Just as vajra, even when broken and no longer whole, still cannot be equaled by any other jewel, the vajra that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: even when the resolve is weak and there is some slight deficiency, it still surpasses every merit of the two vehicles. Good son! Just as vajra, even when damaged and chipped, is still able to remove all poverty, the vajra that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: even when damaged and chipped, not advancing in the practices, it is still able to leave behind all of birth-and-death. Good son! Just as a small vajra is able to break apart every single thing, the vajra that is the mind of bodhi is likewise: entering even a small domain, it at once breaks every delusion of unknowing.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

金剛 — with this part the chain of paired similes settles on a single image, vajra, and stays with it until the close of the fascicle. Seven of the eight similes here are drawn from vajra; the one exception, the tree named 無根, Rootless, sits between the first and third and is itself a variation on the first, since vajra is born only from vajra and gold while the tree grows from no root at all. A small change in the formula also begins here: the first two similes name the bodhisattva-mahāsattva on the second side as usual, but from the third onward the passage says only 菩提心金剛亦復如是, the vajra that is the mind of bodhi is likewise, and the translation follows that shortening.

一切智智 — the doubled term is rendered the wisdom of all-knowing wisdom, as elsewhere on this site, so that it stays distinct from the simpler 一切智, all-knowing wisdom, which the chain of similes uses far more often. The phrase 無知諸惑 in the last simile is rendered every delusion of unknowing rather than of ignorance, to keep 無知 apart from 無明, the ignorance that heads the twelve links.
