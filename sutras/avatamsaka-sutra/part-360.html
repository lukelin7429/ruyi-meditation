Fourteen Faculties of Samantabhadra

The litany turns to the bodhisattva's own faculties — fourteen named in sequence, from the keen and sharp to the pure and unobstructed — before listing ten kinds of spiritual power, from filling every buddha-land with a single body to arranging inexpressible worlds within a single pore. The stretch closes with an image the sūtra will return to often: every land, without exception, contained within the measure of a single hair-tip.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 360 — fourteen faculties, ten spiritual powers, a hair-tip containing every land Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

From Sharp Faculties to a Single Hair-Tip

The list of fourteen faculties reads almost like a catalogue of capacities for practice itself — keenness, compliance, sovereignty, inexhaustibility — before the passage turns outward to spiritual power exercised at cosmic scale, and then, in its final image, inward again to the smallest unit imaginable: a single hair-tip, within which every inexpressible land throughout the whole of empty space is made entirely visible.

Ru-Yi Translation

With a mind of non-attachment, non-bondage, and liberation, he diligently cultivates Samantabhadra's own faculties, vows, and practices, attaining a keen and sharp faculty, a tamed and compliant faculty, a faculty sovereign over every dharma, an inexhaustible faculty, a faculty of diligently cultivating every wholesome root, a faculty equal to the domain of every buddha, a faculty of great diligence that confers upon every bodhisattva the prophecy of never regressing, a faculty of knowing the vajra realm of every buddha-dharma, a faculty of the vajra flame illuminated by the wisdom-light of every Tathāgata, a faculty sovereign in discerning every faculty, a faculty that establishes immeasurable sentient beings in all-wisdom, a boundless and vast faculty, an entirely perfected faculty, and a pure, unobstructed faculty. With a mind of non-attachment, non-bondage, and liberation, he cultivates Samantabhadra's own practice, attaining every bodhisattva's own spiritual power, namely: the spiritual power of immeasurable, vast strength; the spiritual power of immeasurable, sovereign wisdom; the spiritual power of manifesting himself throughout every buddha-land without moving his body; the spiritual power of unobstructed, unceasing sovereignty; the spiritual power of universally gathering every buddha-land and placing it within a single place; the spiritual power of a single body filling every buddha-land entirely; the spiritual power of unobstructed, liberated play; the spiritual power of sovereignty within a single thought that does nothing at all; the spiritual power of abiding without any nature and without any reliance; and the spiritual power, within a single pore, of arranging inexpressible worlds in sequence, journeying throughout the bodhimaṇḍa of every buddha in the Dharma-realm, manifesting to sentient beings so that they may all enter the gateway of great wisdom. With a mind of non-attachment, non-bondage, and liberation, he enters Samantabhadra's own gateway, giving rise to the practice of a bodhisattva, and with sovereign wisdom, within a single thought, universally enters immeasurable buddha-lands, a single body receiving and containing immeasurable buddha-lands, attaining the wisdom able to adorn and purify buddha-lands, forever beholding boundless buddha-lands with wisdom, never once giving rise to the mind of the two lesser vehicles. With a mind of non-attachment, non-bondage, and liberation, he cultivates Samantabhadra's own skillful practice, entering the domain of wisdom, being born into the household of the Tathāgata, abiding on the path of a bodhisattva, fully possessing inexpressible, inexpressible, immeasurable, inconceivable, and supreme minds, practicing immeasurable vows without ever resting, fully comprehending the entire Dharma-realm throughout the three times. With a mind of non-attachment, non-bondage, and liberation, he accomplishes Samantabhadra's own pure Dharma-gateway, within the measure of a single hair-tip entirely containing every inexpressible, inexpressible land throughout the whole of empty space and the entire Dharma-realm, causing them all to be seen clearly; and just as within the measure of that single hair-tip, so too is it within every single hair-tip's measure throughout the entire Dharma-realm and realm of empty space.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

"Never once giving rise to the mind of the two lesser vehicles" is a rare explicit contrast in this litany, naming what the bodhisattva's vast capacity is not aimed toward — the personal liberation sought by the śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha paths. Everything described here, from fourteen faculties to spiritual power spanning every land, serves a single aim the passage takes care to distinguish from lesser goals.
