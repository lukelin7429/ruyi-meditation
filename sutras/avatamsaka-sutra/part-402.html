The Buddha Answers with Light

Vimuktacandra has asked three times. Now the whole assembly of great bodhisattvas asks with one voice, in a verse that abandons argument for plain longing — the thirst for cool water, the hunger for good food, the sick remembering their medicine. The answer, when it comes, is not spoken. The World-Honored One emits a radiance from between his brows that reaches every world and quiets the sufferings of the evil destinies, and it is the platform of light itself that finally tells Vajragarbha to speak.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 402 — the assembly's one voice, the light from between the brows, and the verse from the platform of light Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

The Assembly Asks in One Voice

Vimuktacandra's three requests were arguments — about the audience's readiness, and then about the teaching's own necessity. The assembly's request is not an argument at all. It praises Vajragarbha's eloquence, states simply that no one here is in doubt, and then drops into four images of ordinary, undeniable need. This is the last of the requests; after it, no one asks again.

Chinese Source

爾時，諸大菩薩眾一時同聲向金剛藏菩薩而說頌言：
「上妙無垢智，無邊分別辯，宣暢深美言，第一義相應。
念持清淨行，十力集功德，辯才分別義，說此最勝地。
定戒集正心，離我慢邪見，此眾無疑念，唯願聞善說！
如渴思冷水，如飢念美食，如病憶良藥，如蜂貪好蜜；
我等亦如是，願聞甘露法！善哉廣大智，願說入諸地，
成十力無礙，善逝一切行！」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then the whole assembly of great bodhisattvas, with one voice together, spoke in verse to Vajragarbha Bodhisattva, saying:
"Supreme and wondrous undefiled wisdom, boundless in the eloquence that discriminates, proclaiming words both deep and beautiful, in accord with the highest meaning — mindful and upholding of pure practice, gathering in the merit of the ten powers, with an eloquence that discriminates the meaning — speak to us of these most supreme grounds! Settled in meditation and precepts, gathered in right intent, free of conceit and of false views, this assembly harbors not one doubting thought; we long only to hear it well taught! As the thirsty long for cool water, as the hungry think of fine food, as the sick call to mind good medicine, as bees crave excellent honey — so are we also; we long to hear the Dharma of sweet nectar! Excellent, O vast and far-reaching wisdom — we beg you to speak of the entering of all the grounds, of the ten powers accomplished without obstruction, of every practice of the Well-Gone One!"

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The four similes are not decoration standing in for a reason. Thirst, hunger, sickness, and the bee's instinct are all conditions in which the need is not chosen and cannot be talked out of. Vajragarbha's objection was that beings of weaker understanding would react badly to what they heard; the assembly answers, in effect, that they are not in a position to react at all — they are in a position to require. It is the one form of request his objection has no purchase on.

The Radiance from Between the Brows

The Buddha has been silent through this entire exchange — through Vimuktacandra's three requests and Vajragarbha's two refusals. He does not now settle the matter by instruction. He emits light. Note what the light does before it does anything else — it reaches every world without exception, and the suffering of the lowest destinies stops. The very beings Vajragarbha feared would be harmed are the first ones the answer touches.

Chinese Source

爾時，世尊從眉間出清淨光明，名：菩薩力焰明，百千阿僧祇光明以為眷屬，普照十方一切世界靡不周遍，三惡道苦皆得休息；又照一切如來眾會，顯現諸佛不思議力；又照十方一切世界，一切諸佛所加說法菩薩之身；作是事已，於上虛空中成大光明雲網臺而住。時，十方諸佛悉亦如是，從眉間出清淨光明，其光名號、眷屬、作業悉同於此，又亦照此娑婆世界佛及大眾，并金剛藏菩薩身、師子座已，於上虛空中成大光明雲網臺。

Ru-Yi Translation

Then the World-Honored One sent forth from between his brows a pure radiance, named the Blazing Light of a Bodhisattva's Power, having a hundred thousand asaṃkhyeyas of light-rays as its retinue. It universally illumined every world-system throughout the ten directions, leaving nothing unreached, and the sufferings of the three evil destinies were all set at rest. It illumined as well every assembly of every tathāgata, making manifest the inconceivable power of all buddhas; and it illumined as well, throughout every world-system of the ten directions, the very bodies of those Dharma-teaching bodhisattvas whom all the buddhas had empowered. Having accomplished all this, it formed in the space above a great canopy of clouds and light, woven like a net, and there abided. At that time the buddhas of the ten directions all did likewise: from between their brows they sent forth a pure radiance whose name, whose retinue, and whose activity were in every respect the same as this. It illumined as well the Buddha and the great assembly of this Sahā world, together with the body and the lion-throne of Vajragarbha Bodhisattva, and then formed in the space above a great canopy of clouds and light, woven like a net.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The light is named the Blazing Light of a Bodhisattva's Power (菩薩力焰明) — not the Buddha's power, the bodhisattva's. What it illumines, in every direction, is the bodies of those bodhisattvas whom the buddhas have empowered to teach. The Buddha's answer to "may I speak?" is to show, silently and everywhere at once, that the capacity to speak was never Vajragarbha's private possession to withhold or spend.

Note too that the buddhas of the ten directions do exactly the same thing, and that their light comes back to shine on this world — on Vajragarbha's own body and the seat he is sitting on. The request that began with one bodhisattva has become the concern of every buddha there is.

The Platform of Light Speaks

The light does not remain a sign to be interpreted. Gathered into its canopy above the assembly, it speaks — and what it says settles the question Vajragarbha raised. His worry was for those who would doubt. The verse concedes the point exactly, without softening it, and then reframes what follows from it.

Chinese Source

時，光臺中，以諸佛威神力故而說頌言：
「佛無等等如虛空，十力無量勝功德，人間最勝世中上，釋師子法加於彼。
佛子當承諸佛力，開此法王最勝藏，諸地廣智勝妙行，以佛威神分別說。
若為善逝力所加，當得法寶入其心，諸地無垢次第滿，亦具如來十種力。
雖住海水劫火中，堪受此法必得聞，其有生疑不信者，永不得聞如是義。
應說諸地勝智道，入住展轉次修習，從行境界法智生，利益一切眾生故。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then, from within the platform of light, by the majestic spiritual power of all buddhas, there came a verse, saying:
"The Buddha is peerless, like empty space, his ten powers a measureless and supreme merit; foremost among humankind and highest within the world — the Dharma of the Lion of the Śākyas is conferred upon him. Child of the Buddha, sustained now by the power of all buddhas, open this most supreme treasury of the Dharma-king! The vast wisdom of the grounds, their supreme and wondrous practice — by the majestic spiritual power of the Buddha, discriminate it and speak. Whoever is sustained by the strength of the Well-Gone One will find the treasure of the Dharma entering into his mind; the grounds, undefiled, will be fulfilled in their order, and he will be furnished as well with the Tathāgata's ten kinds of power. Though one should dwell in the waters of the ocean or within the kalpa-ending fire, whoever is fit to receive this Dharma will surely get to hear it; but whoever gives rise to doubt and does not believe will never hear this meaning at all. Speak, then, of the path of supreme wisdom that is the grounds — the entering, the abiding, and the unfolding in turn, cultivated one after another — born of the Dharma-wisdom that arises from the domain of practice, for the benefit of all sentient beings."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

"Though one should dwell in the waters of the ocean or within the kalpa-ending fire, whoever is fit to receive this Dharma will surely get to hear it" (雖住海水劫火中，堪受此法必得聞). The two images are the extremes of circumstance — drowned, or in the fire that ends a world-age — and the verse says circumstance is not what decides the matter. Fitness to receive is. And its converse is stated just as plainly, without any threat attached: one who doubts and does not believe simply will not hear this, ever. The doubter is not being punished. The verse is describing the condition that makes hearing possible, and doubt is its absence.

This is why the light's arrival changes Vajragarbha's situation rather than merely overruling it. He withheld the teaching to protect those who would doubt; the verse tells him that his silence protects no one, because those who would doubt were never going to hear it in any case, and those who are fit will hear it wherever they are.
