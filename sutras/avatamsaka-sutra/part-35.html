Names at the Scale of the Whole Sahā World

Mañjuśrī repeats the entire pattern from Part 34, but zoomed outward by an order of magnitude: not this one set of four continents, but the whole Sahā world of a hundred million such continents, itself bears a hundred thousand million different names for the Buddha — and the same is true of ten more worlds surrounding it, and, the chapter closes by insisting, of every world throughout the Dharma-realm without limit.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 35 — completes fascicle 12's opening chapter Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

The Whole Sahā World's Own Names

Chinese Source

「諸佛子！此娑婆世界有百億四天下，如來於中，有百億萬種種名號，令諸眾生各別知見。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Buddha's children! This Sahā world has a hundred million sets of four continents, and the Tathāgata, throughout them, has a hundred thousand million kinds of distinct names, causing every living being to know and see him each in their own distinct way.

Without End, Throughout the Dharma-Realm

Chinese Source

「諸佛子！如娑婆世界，如是東方百千億無數無量，無邊無等，不可數、不可稱、不可思、不可量、不可說，盡法界、虛空界、諸世界中，如來名號，種種不同；南、西、北方，四維、上、下，亦復如是。如世尊昔為菩薩時，以種種談論、種種語言、種種音聲，種種業，種種報，種種處，種種方便、種種根，種種信解、種種地位而得成熟，亦令眾生如是知見而為說法。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Buddha's children! Just as with this Sahā world, so too, to the east, throughout hundreds of thousands of millions, countless, immeasurable, boundless, unequaled, uncountable, inexpressible, inconceivable, immeasurable, ineffable worlds, throughout the whole extent of the Dharma-realm and the realm of empty space, the Tathāgata's own names are every kind of different; and it is likewise so to the south, west, and north, in the four intermediate directions, and above and below. Just as the World-Honored One, in ages past, when he was a bodhisattva, through every kind of discourse, every kind of language, every kind of voice, every kind of karma, every kind of recompense, every kind of place, every kind of skillful means, every kind of faculty, every kind of faith and understanding, and every kind of ground and station, attained ripening — so too he causes living beings to know and see in just this way, and teaches the Dharma for their sake."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The chapter's closing line quietly relocates the whole teaching from cosmology back onto the bodhisattva path: this proliferation of names is said to mirror how the Buddha himself, "in ages past, when he was a bodhisattva," ripened through every kind of language, karma, place, faculty, and station — the same variety just catalogued across worlds turns out to describe a single life's own trajectory as well. Chengguan reads this as the chapter's real point: infinite names are not merely a fact about how other beings perceive the Buddha from outside, but a record of the near-infinite diversity of conditions any bodhisattva's own path must pass through before arriving at buddhahood. This closes the sūtra's seventh chapter; the eighth, opened later in this same fascicle, applies the identical structure to the Four Noble Truths themselves.

Part 35 Complete

This closes Part 35 and the sūtra's seventh chapter, "The Names of the Tathāgata" (如來名號品第七): the same teaching from Part 34 repeated at the larger scale of the whole Sahā world and the ten worlds surrounding it, closing with the claim that this pattern extends, unbroken, throughout the entire Dharma-realm. Part 36 continues fascicle twelve with the sūtra's eighth chapter, "The Four Noble Truths" (四聖諦品第八), in which the very same structure is applied to the teaching of suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path.
