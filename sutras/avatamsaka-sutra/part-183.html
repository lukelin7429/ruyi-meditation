A Different Mind for Every Offering

Countless heavenly sons standing in empty space each give rise, before the Buddha, to a distinct mind — wisdom, joy, reverence, faith and understanding, the sense of a supreme field of merit, the sense of how rare this encounter is, exhilaration — and each mind produces its own particular offering, from incense-smoke and flower-clouds to nets of gold and palaces adorning the sky itself.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 183 — countless offerings, each arising from its own mind Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Nine Minds, Nine Offerings, in Empty Space

Where earlier catalogs multiplied the same offering a hundred million times, this passage pairs each offering with its own named mind — the offering is never generic gratitude but a specific mental state made visible: a mind of joy becomes a rain of flowers, a mind of reverence becomes a rain of canopies, a mind of ever-increasing resolve becomes garments spread along the road for the Buddha to walk upon.

Chinese Source

百千億那由他阿僧祇兜率陀天子，住虛空中，咸於佛所起智慧境界心，燒一切香，香氣成雲莊嚴虛空；又於佛所起歡喜心，雨一切天華雲莊嚴虛空；又於佛所起尊重心，雨一切天蓋雲莊嚴虛空；又於佛所起供養心，散一切天鬘雲莊嚴虛空；又於佛所生信解心，布阿僧祇金網彌覆虛空，一切寶鈴常出妙音；又於佛所生最勝福田心，以阿僧祇帳莊嚴虛空，雨一切瓔珞雲，無有斷絕；又於佛所生深信心，以阿僧祇諸天宮殿莊嚴虛空，一切天樂出微妙音；又於佛所生最勝難遇心，以阿僧祇種種色天衣雲莊嚴虛空，雨於無比種種妙衣；又於佛所生無量歡喜踊躍心，以阿僧祇諸天寶冠莊嚴虛空，雨無量天冠，廣大成雲；又於佛所起歡喜心，以阿僧祇種種色寶莊嚴虛空，雨一切瓔珞雲，無有斷絕。

Ru-Yi Translation

A hundred thousand million nayutas of asaṃkhyeyas of Tuṣita heavenly sons, abiding within empty space, all gave rise, before the Buddha, to a mind set upon the domain of wisdom, and burned every kind of incense, its fragrant smoke forming clouds that adorned empty space; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of joy, raining down clouds of every heavenly flower to adorn empty space; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of reverence, raining down clouds of every heavenly canopy to adorn empty space; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of offering, scattering clouds of every heavenly garland to adorn empty space; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of faith and understanding, spreading asaṃkhyeya golden nets to cover empty space entirely, every jeweled bell forever sending forth a wondrous sound; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of the most supreme field of merit, adorning empty space with asaṃkhyeya curtains, raining down clouds of every necklace without any interruption at all; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of deep faith, adorning empty space with asaṃkhyeya heavenly palaces, every heavenly instrument sending forth a subtle and wondrous sound; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of how most supremely rare this encounter is, adorning empty space with asaṃkhyeya clouds of heavenly garments of every color, raining down every kind of incomparable and wondrous garment; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of immeasurable joy and exhilaration, adorning empty space with asaṃkhyeya heavenly jeweled crowns, raining down immeasurable heavenly crowns, vast and expansive, forming clouds; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of joy, adorning empty space with asaṃkhyeya jewels of every color, raining down clouds of every necklace, without any interruption at all.

Ru-Yi Translation

A hundred thousand million nayutas of asaṃkhyeyas of heavenly sons all gave rise, before the Buddha, to a mind of pure faith, scattering countless heavenly flowers of every color, burning countless heavenly fragrances of every color, making offerings to the Tathāgata; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of vast adornment and transformation, holding countless heavenly sandalwood powders of every color, reverently scattering them upon the Tathāgata; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of joy and exhilaration, holding countless canopies of every color, following closely after the Tathāgata; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of ever-increasing resolve, holding countless heavenly jeweled garments of every color, spreading them out along the road, making offerings to the Tathāgata; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a pure mind, holding countless heavenly jeweled banner-poles of every color, reverently going forth to welcome the Tathāgata; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to a mind of ever-increasing joy, holding countless heavenly adorning implements of every color, making offerings to the Tathāgata; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to an unbreakable faith, holding countless heavenly jeweled garlands, making offerings to the Tathāgata; and, before the Buddha, gave rise to an incomparable mind of joy, holding countless heavenly jeweled banners of every color, making offerings to the Tathāgata. A hundred thousand million nayutas of asaṃkhyeyas of heavenly sons, with a mind gentle, tranquil, and free of heedlessness, held countless heavenly instruments of every color, sending forth wondrous sounds, making offerings to the Tathāgata.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The passage's final mind is the quietest of them all — not joy, not exhilaration, not even reverence, but a mind "gentle, tranquil, and free of heedlessness," expressed simply as music. After nine minds that each build outward into visible spectacle, ending on unheedless calm suggests that the offering the sūtra values most may not be the most dramatic one: a still, attentive mind, playing music without distraction, closes the list precisely where the loudest offering might have been expected instead.
