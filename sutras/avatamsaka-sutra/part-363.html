Knowing the Immeasurable Minds of Others

After the great enumeration of conceptions in the previous Part, the litany turns to a different kind of knowing: within each single mind, the bodhisattva comes to know immeasurable minds, distinguished by their reliance, their discrimination, their nature, their deeds, and their thought — before the same pattern repeats for place, for karmic act, for dharma, and finally for a single spoken sound, within which every spoken sound in the world becomes known.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 363 — one mind knows every mind, one sound knows every sound Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

One Instance, Every Instance

The pattern this Part establishes will recur through much of what follows: a single mind knows every mind, a single place knows every place, a single karmic act knows every karmic act, a single dharma knows every dharma, a single spoken sound knows every spoken sound — each clause closing with the same formula, "just as within that single one, so too is it within every one entirely," treating the particular and the universal as fully interchangeable rather than as different orders of scale.

Chinese Source

以無著無縛解脫心，為一切眾生修普賢行，生大智寶，於一一心中知無量心，隨其依止，隨其分別，隨其種性，隨其所作，隨其業用，隨其相狀，隨其思覺，種種不同靡不明見。以無著無縛解脫心，成就普賢大願智寶，於一處中知於無量不可說處；如於一處，於一切處悉亦如是。以無著無縛解脫心，修習普賢行業智地，於一業中能知無量不可說不可說業，其業各以種種緣造，明了知見；如於一業，於一切業悉亦如是。以無著無縛解脫心，修習普賢知諸法智，於一法中知不可說不可說法，於一切法中而知一法；如是諸法，各各差別，無有障礙，無違無著。以無著無縛解脫心，住菩薩行，得具普賢無礙耳根，於一言音中知不可說不可說言音無量無邊種種差別而無所著；如於一言音，於一切言音悉亦如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

With a mind of non-attachment, non-bondage, and liberation, he cultivates Samantabhadra's own practice for the sake of every sentient being, giving rise to the great treasury of wisdom, within each single mind knowing immeasurable minds, according to their reliance, according to their discrimination, according to their nature, according to their deeds, according to the function of their karma, according to their appearance, according to their thought and awareness — every kind of difference, without exception, clearly seen. With a mind of non-attachment, non-bondage, and liberation, he accomplishes Samantabhadra's own treasury of wisdom born of great vows, within a single place knowing immeasurable, inexpressible places; and just as within that single place, so too is it within every place entirely. With a mind of non-attachment, non-bondage, and liberation, he cultivates and practices the ground of wisdom regarding Samantabhadra's own karmic deeds, within a single karmic act able to know immeasurable, inexpressible, inexpressible karmic acts, each one created through various conditions, clearly known and seen; and just as within that single karmic act, so too is it within every karmic act entirely. With a mind of non-attachment, non-bondage, and liberation, he cultivates and practices Samantabhadra's own wisdom of knowing every dharma, within a single dharma knowing inexpressible, inexpressible dharmas, and within every dharma knowing that single dharma; thus every dharma, each one distinct, remains without any obstruction, without contradiction and without attachment. With a mind of non-attachment, non-bondage, and liberation, he abides in the practice of a bodhisattva, fully possessing Samantabhadra's own unobstructed faculty of hearing, within a single spoken sound knowing inexpressible, inexpressible spoken sounds, immeasurable, boundless, and of every kind of difference, and yet attached to none of them; and just as within that single spoken sound, so too is it within every spoken sound entirely.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The faculty described here — knowing every mind through a single mind — is qualified in a way easy to miss: it distinguishes each mind "according to their reliance, their discrimination, their nature, their deeds," seven distinct categories in all. Knowing everyone at once does not collapse individuals into sameness; the passage insists on preserving exactly what makes each mind distinct even while comprehending them all within a single instance.
