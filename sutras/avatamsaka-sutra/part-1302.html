Like a Heap of Ink

The paired similes turn to skills and their first steps: a master archer's disciples who already surpass everyone else, the archer who plants his feet before learning the method, the illusionist who must hold the method in mind before anything succeeds, an art that shows form while having none, a cat whose mere glance sends the mouse into its hole, Jambu-river gold beside which everything else looks like a heap of ink, and two lodestones with opposite effects on iron.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 1302 — fascicle 78 continues Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Like a Heap of Ink

Eight more paired similes continue the pattern — two from archery, two from illusion, a cat and a mouse, gold ornaments, and two lodestones.

Chinese Source

「善男子！譬如射師有諸弟子，雖未慣習其師技藝，然其智慧、方便、善巧，餘一切人所不能及。菩薩摩訶薩初始發心亦復如是，雖未慣習一切智行，然其所有願、智、解、欲，一切世間凡夫、二乘悉不能及。「善男子！如人學射，先安其足，後習其法。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，欲學如來一切智道，先當安住菩提之心，然後修行一切佛法。「善男子！譬如幻師，將作幻事，先當起意憶持幻法，然後所作悉得成就。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，將起一切諸佛菩薩神通幻事，先當起意發菩提心，然後一切悉得成就。「善男子！譬如幻術，無色現色。菩薩摩訶薩菩提心相亦復如是，雖無有色，不可覩見，然能普於十方法界示現種種功德莊嚴。「善男子！譬如貓貍，纔見於鼠，鼠即入穴不敢復出。菩薩摩訶薩發菩提心亦復如是，暫以慧眼觀諸惑業，皆即竄匿不復出生。「善男子！譬如有人，著閻浮金莊嚴之具，映蔽一切皆如聚墨。菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，著菩提心莊嚴之具，映蔽一切凡夫二乘功德莊嚴悉無光色。「善男子！如好磁石，少分之力，即能吸壞諸鐵鉤鎖。菩薩摩訶薩發菩提心亦復如是，若起一念，悉能壞滅一切見欲無明鉤鎖。「善男子！如有磁石，鐵若見之，即皆散去，無留住者。菩薩摩訶薩發菩提心亦復如是，諸業煩惱、二乘解脫，若暫見之，即皆散滅，亦無住者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Just as a master archer has disciples who, though they have not yet grown practiced in their master's skill, nonetheless possess a wisdom, skillful means, and ingenuity that no other person can equal, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva who has just first aroused the mind is likewise: though they have not yet grown practiced in the practices of all-knowing wisdom, nonetheless the vows, wisdom, understanding, and aspiration they possess cannot be equaled by any ordinary being of the world or any follower of the two vehicles. Good son! Just as a person learning to shoot first sets their feet firmly and only afterward trains in the method, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: wishing to learn the Tathāgata's path of all-knowing wisdom, they must first abide securely in the mind of bodhi and only afterward cultivate every buddha-dharma. Good son! Just as an illusionist about to perform a work of illusion must first bring to mind and hold firmly the method of illusion, and only afterward does everything they perform succeed, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: about to bring forth the illusion-like works of spiritual power of every buddha and bodhisattva, they must first bring to mind and arouse the mind of bodhi, and only afterward does everything succeed. Good son! Just as the art of illusion, having no form, manifests form, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's own mark of the mind of bodhi is likewise: though it has no form and cannot be seen, it is nonetheless able to display every kind of adornment of merit universally throughout the dharma-realms of the ten directions. Good son! Just as, when a cat has only just caught sight of a mouse, the mouse at once enters its hole and dares not come out again, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's arousing of the mind of bodhi is likewise: the moment they observe every delusion and karma with the eye of wisdom, all of these at once flee into hiding and never arise again. Good son! Just as a person wearing ornaments of Jambu-river gold outshines everything else so that all of it looks like a heap of ink, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva is likewise: wearing the ornaments that are the mind of bodhi, they outshine every adornment of merit of ordinary beings and the two vehicles so that all of it is left without light or color. Good son! Just as a fine lodestone, with only a small portion of its power, is at once able to draw apart and break every iron hook and chain, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's arousing of the mind of bodhi is likewise: should a single thought of it arise, it is able to break and destroy every hook and chain of views, desire, and ignorance. Good son! Just as, when there is a lodestone, any iron that comes before it at once scatters and departs, with none remaining, a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's arousing of the mind of bodhi is likewise: every karma and affliction and every liberation of the two vehicles, having once come before it even for a moment, at once scatters and is extinguished, with none that remains.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

磁石 — the lodestone supplies the last two similes of this part, and the two are set in deliberate contrast. In the seventh, the stone's small portion of power draws iron hooks and chains to it and breaks them apart; in the eighth, iron that comes before the stone scatters and departs, none of it remaining. The first describes the mind of bodhi breaking what binds, the second describes what cannot stay in its presence. Both are rendered lodestone, the stone itself, rather than the abstract magnet.

幻師 and 幻術 — the illusionist and the art of illusion are likewise a pair, the third and fourth similes: the first turns on the order of things, that the method must be held in mind before any work of illusion succeeds, and the second on the paradox that an art with no form of its own manifests form, which the passage applies to 菩提心相, the mark of the mind of bodhi, unseen yet displaying every adornment throughout the ten directions.
