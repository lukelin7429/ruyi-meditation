Where Does the Great Sage Come From

Having asked where the adornments came from, Good-Wealth asks where the Great Sage himself has come from, and Maitreya answers twice. First, in the manner of the dharma: bodhisattvas come without coming or going, without abiding, without attachment, neither arising nor ceasing, and they come from the places of great compassion, great loving-kindness, pure precepts, great vows, spiritual power, the unshakable, non-grasping, wisdom and skillful means, and manifested transformation. Then, in the plainest terms: he has come here from his birthplace, from a village named Houses, where an elder's son named Gopāla lives, and where he stayed to bring that man, the people of the place, and his own parents, kin, and the brāhmaṇas into the buddha-dharma.

Where Does the Great Sage Come From

Two answers to one question — the coming that is no coming, and the road from a named village.

Chinese Source

善財童子言：「大聖從何處來？」彌勒言：「善男子！諸菩薩無來無去，如是而來；無行無住，如是而來；無處無著，不沒不生，不住不遷，不動不起，無戀無著，無業無報，無起無滅，不斷不常，如是而來。善男子！菩薩從大悲處來，為欲調伏諸眾生故；從大慈處來，為欲救護諸眾生故；從淨戒處來，隨其所樂而受生故；從大願處來，往昔願力之所持故；從神通處來，於一切處隨樂現故；從無動搖處來，恒不捨離一切佛故；從無取捨處來，不役身心使往來故；從智慧方便處來，隨順一切諸眾生故；從示現變化處來，猶如影像而化現故。「然，善男子！汝問於我從何處來者。善男子！我從生處摩羅提國而來於此。善男子！彼有聚落，名為：房舍；有長者子，名：瞿波羅。為化其人，令入佛法，而住於彼；又為生處一切人民隨所應化而為說法，亦為父母及諸眷屬、婆羅門等演說大乘，令其趣入故住於彼。而從彼來。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good-Wealth the youth said, "Where has the Great Sage come from?" Maitreya said, "Good son! The bodhisattvas come neither coming nor going, and thus they come; they come without moving and without abiding, and thus they come; they come without place and without attachment, neither sinking nor arising, neither abiding nor shifting, neither stirring nor rising, without longing and without clinging, without karma and without retribution, neither arising nor ceasing, neither cut off nor permanent, and thus they come. Good son! A bodhisattva comes from the place of great compassion, because he wishes to tame sentient beings; he comes from the place of great loving-kindness, because he wishes to rescue and protect sentient beings; he comes from the place of pure precepts, because he takes birth according to his own wish; he comes from the place of great vows, because he is sustained by the power of vows made in ages past; he comes from the place of spiritual power, because he appears in every place as he wishes; he comes from the place of the unshakable, because he never forsakes any buddha; he comes from the place of neither grasping nor rejecting, because he does not make his body and mind labor in coming and going; he comes from the place of wisdom and skillful means, because he accords with every sentient being; and he comes from the place of manifested transformation, because he appears by transformation like a reflected image. And yet, good son, since you have asked me where I have come from: good son, I have come here from my birthplace, the land of Móluótí. Good son! There is a village there named Houses, and in it an elder's son named Gopāla. In order to transform that man and lead him into the buddha-dharma, I stayed there; and also to teach the dharma to all the people of my birthplace according to how each should be transformed, and to expound the Great Vehicle to my father and mother, my kin, and the brāhmaṇas, so as to lead them to enter it, I stayed there. And from there I have come."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

如是而來 — Maitreya's first answer is built on ten paired negations, 無來無去, 無行無住, and the rest, each pair denying both terms of a contrast, and the refrain 如是而來, and thus they come, returns three times to close the groups. It is then answered by a positive list of nine places from which a bodhisattva comes, each followed by its reason in 故. The translation keeps every pair and every because so that the two halves of the answer, the way of the dharma and the way of the vow, stand in the same balance as in the source.

摩羅提國 and 瞿波羅 — the second answer names a country, a village, and a man. 瞿波羅 is a straightforward transliteration and is restored as Gopāla, on the same pattern as 瞿波, Gopā, the name of the wife of the Buddha met earlier in this chapter. 摩羅提 is left in pinyin as Móluótí: the three characters are plainly a transliteration, but the Sanskrit form behind them is not securely recoverable from the Chinese alone, and the translation prefers a transparent placeholder to a guessed reconstruction. 房舍 is not a transliteration but an ordinary word for dwellings, and the village is accordingly named Houses.
