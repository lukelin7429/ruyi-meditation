Merit Subtle, Vast, and Profound

With the seventh dedication now formally complete, Vajradhvaja Bodhisattva again surveys the ten directions and, by the Buddha's own spiritual power, speaks a verse retelling everything the dedication has already said in prose — merit so vast that even a single moment of practice can dedicate it toward the boundless, gifts of the body given across every land, and a closing turn toward the emptiness of every dharma, where discrimination itself finally comes to an end.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 341 — the seventh dedication retold in verse, part one Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

From a Single Thought to the Boundless

The verse opens where the prose left off — merit "subtle, vast, and profound" — before tracing the same arc as the sixth dedication's own verse recap: gifts of possessions and of the body itself, given across every land, before rising into the more philosophical register that closes this stretch, where every dharma is known to be empty and yet no karmic fruit is ever lost.

Chinese Source

「菩薩所作諸功德，微妙廣大甚深遠，
乃至一念而修行，悉能迴向無邊際。
菩薩所有資生具，種種豐盈無限億，
香象寶馬以駕車，衣服珍財悉殊妙。
或以頭目并手足，或持身肉及骨髓，
悉遍十方無量剎，普施一切令充遍。
無量劫中所修習，一切功德盡迴向，
為欲救度諸群生，其心畢竟不退轉。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The merit that the bodhisattva creates is subtle and wondrous, vast and profound, reaching far and deep; even in the practice of a single thought, he is entirely able to dedicate it to the boundless and infinite. Every article for sustaining life that the bodhisattva possesses, abundant and overflowing in countless koṭis of every kind, fragrant elephants and jeweled horses yoked to chariots, clothing and precious wealth, all of it wondrous and rare — sometimes his head and eyes, together with his hands and feet, sometimes the flesh and marrow of his own body — entirely pervading immeasurable lands in the ten directions, he universally gives it all away, causing it to fill them completely. Throughout immeasurable kalpas of cultivation and practice, he dedicates every merit entirely, wishing to rescue and deliver every living being, his mind ultimately never regressing.

Chinese Source

菩薩為度眾生故，常修最勝迴向業，
普令三界得安樂，悉使當成無上果。
菩薩普興平等願，隨其所集清淨業，
悉以迴施諸群生，如是大誓終無捨。
菩薩願力無限礙，一切世間咸攝受，
如是迴向諸群生，未曾暫起分別心。
普願眾生智明了，布施持戒悉清淨，
精進修行不懈廢，如是大誓無休息。

Ru-Yi Translation

The bodhisattva, for the sake of delivering sentient beings, forever cultivates the most excellent karma of dedication, universally causing the three realms to attain peace and happiness, causing them all to accomplish the unsurpassed fruit. The bodhisattva universally arouses equal vows, and according to whatever pure karma he has gathered, gives and dedicates it all entirely to every living being; this great vow of his is never once abandoned. The power of the bodhisattva's vow is without limit or obstruction, gathering in every world entirely; dedicating in this way to every living being, he never once, even briefly, gives rise to a discriminating mind. He universally vows that sentient beings' wisdom become clear and bright, that their giving and upholding of precepts become entirely pure, that their diligent practice never slacken or be abandoned; this great vow of his is without any rest.

Chinese Source

菩薩迴向到彼岸，普開清淨妙法門，
智慧同於兩足尊，分別實義得究竟。
菩薩言辭已通達，種種智慧亦如是，
說法如理無障礙，而於其中心不著。
常於諸法不作二，亦復不作於不二，
於二不二並皆離，知其悉是語言道。
知諸世間悉平等，莫非心語一切業，
眾生幻化無有實，所有果報從茲起。

Ru-Yi Translation

The bodhisattva's dedication reaches the farther shore, universally opening the pure and wondrous gate of the Dharma; his wisdom equal to the Two-Footed Honored One, he distinguishes the true meaning and attains its ultimate end. The bodhisattva's own speech has already become fully mastered, and every kind of wisdom is likewise so; he expounds the Dharma in accordance with the true principle, without any obstruction, and yet within it his mind holds no attachment. He forever neither treats any dharma as dual, nor does he treat it as non-dual either; leaving behind both duality and non-duality alike, he knows that both are entirely the path of language. He knows that every world is entirely equal, none of it apart from mind, speech, and every karmic act; sentient beings are illusory transformations without any true reality, and yet every fruit of karma arises from just this.

Chinese Source

一切世間之所有，種種果報各不同，
莫不皆由業力成，若滅於業彼皆盡。
菩薩觀察諸世間，身口意業悉平等，
亦令眾生住平等，猶如無等大聖尊。
菩薩善業悉迴向，普令眾生色清淨，
福德方便皆具足，同於無上調御士。
菩薩利益諸群生，功德大海盡迴向，
願使威光特超世，得成雄猛大力身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Everything that exists within any world, every kind of differing karmic fruit, is without exception accomplished entirely through the power of karma; if karma itself were extinguished, all of it would come entirely to an end. The bodhisattva contemplates every world, the karma of body, speech, and mind entirely equal, and likewise causes sentient beings to abide in equality, just like the great sage honored one who has no equal. The bodhisattva dedicates every wholesome karma entirely, universally causing sentient beings' form to become pure, their merit and skillful means both fully complete, equal to the unsurpassed Tamer. The bodhisattva benefits every living being, dedicating the great ocean of his merit entirely, vowing that his majestic light may especially surpass the world, attaining a heroic body of great power.

Chinese Source

凡所修習諸功德，願使世間普清淨，
諸佛清淨無倫匹，眾生清淨亦如是。
菩薩於義得善巧，能知諸佛最勝法，
以眾善業等迴向，願令庶品同如來。
菩薩了知諸法空，一切世間無所有，
無有造作及作者，眾生業報亦不失。
諸法寂滅非寂滅，遠離此二分別心，
知諸分別是世見，入於正位分別盡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whatever merit he cultivates and practices, he vows that the world may become universally pure; the purity of every buddha is without peer or equal, and the purity of sentient beings is likewise so. The bodhisattva attains skillfulness regarding meaning, able to know the most excellent Dharma of every buddha; with every wholesome karma he dedicates equally, vowing that every kind of being become equal to the Tathāgata. The bodhisattva clearly knows that every dharma is empty, that nothing at all exists within any world; there is no doing and no doer, and yet the karmic fruit of sentient beings is never lost. Every dharma is quiescent, and yet not quiescent; forever leaving behind this discriminating mind between the two, he knows that every discrimination is merely a worldly view, and entering the true position, all discrimination comes entirely to an end.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

"There is no doing and no doer, and yet the karmic fruit of sentient beings is never lost" states plainly what the whole verse has been circling: emptiness and karmic consequence are not felt as a contradiction to be resolved here but as two truths held together without tension — precisely the same move the sixth dedication's closing verse made with dream, echo, and reflection.
