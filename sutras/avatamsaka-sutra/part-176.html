Clouds of Every Flower Raining Down

The description turns from the throne itself to the hall around it: a hundred million clouds each of kusuma, padma, utpala, kumuda, puṇḍarīka, and mandārava flowers rain down in turn, followed by jeweled banners, balustrades, tāla trees, pavilions, windows, and finally garments of blue, yellow, red, and every wondrous color, spread out so that whoever beholds them simply rejoices.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 176 — the palace's rain of offerings begins Source: Taishō T279 (Śikṣānanda, Tang, 695–699 CE)

Named Flower by Named Flower

Where the throne itself was built from a hundred million of every kind of treasure, the hall around it now fills with a hundred million of every kind of falling offering — flowers named one by one rather than gestured at collectively, followed by architecture, ornament, and finally garments of every color, each catalog closing the same way: whoever sees it rejoices.

Ru-Yi Translation

It hung a hundred million nets of jeweled bells, which, stirred by a gentle breeze, sent forth a wondrous sound; a hundred million jeweled balustrades, encircling it all around; a hundred million jeweled tāla trees, arrayed in ordered rows; a hundred million wondrous jeweled windows, resplendently adorned; a hundred million jeweled trees, casting their shade all around; a hundred million jeweled pavilions, extending far and wide, ornately adorned; a hundred million jeweled gates, draped with hanging necklaces; a hundred million golden bells, sending forth a wondrous sound; a hundred million necklaces of auspicious marks, pure and adorned, hanging down; a hundred million jeweled svastikas, able to dispel every evil; a hundred million treasuries of gold, woven entirely from threads of gold; a hundred million jeweled canopies, their poles made of every kind of treasure, held aloft in rows; a hundred million wheels, treasuries of the sun, and a hundred million wheels, treasuries of the moon, both formed entirely from immeasurable colored jewels gathered together; a hundred million fragrant flames, their light shining through with utter clarity; Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Where an ordinary description might simply say "every kind of flower," the sūtra insists on naming six kinds one after another — kusuma, padma, utpala, kumuda, puṇḍarīka, and mandārava — before finally adding "every heavenly flower" as if to close a list that could not otherwise be closed. This refusal to compress the particular into the general is the same habit of mind that built the throne item by item in the previous two Parts: totality, in this sūtra, is not a category substituted for its members but the sum of every member actually named.
