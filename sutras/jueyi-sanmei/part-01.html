The Marks of Dharmas, the Name, Expedient Practice, and the Marks of Mind

This short work, taught by Zhiyi and recorded by Guanding, sets out the practice that Calming and Contemplation calls the "neither-walking-nor-sitting samādhi," which Nanyue Huisi named "following one's own intent" and the Larger Prajñāpāramitā names the samādhi of awakening attention (覺意三昧): whatever the mind turns to, one awakens to it and sees it clearly. Part 1 covers the first four of the text's six sections. Section One opens with ten similes of the sun and empty space: the empty nature of mind is pure, yet delusion is removed only by the light of wisdom, which neither adds to nor takes from that nature. Section Two explains the name. "Attention" (意) is taken as the leading term because mind, attention, and consciousness are neither one nor three; the name is also linked to the seven factors of awakening (七覺意), which the text reads at six levels, from hearing the teaching to final awakening. Section Three gives the preparation: a great vow and the six perfections used as expedients to tame the false mind. Section Four introduces the fourfold moment — not-yet-thought, about-to-think, thinking, having-thought — as the object of contemplation, and defends against the objection that the first and last moments, or past and future minds, cannot be contemplated. Much of Sections Two and Four reappears, often word for word, in Calming and Contemplation, fascicle 2.

Section One: Distinguishing the Marks of Dharmas

Chinese Source

辯法相第一

Ru-Yi Translation

Distinguishing the Marks of Dharmas, Section One

Chinese Source

夫行人欲度生死大海、登涅槃彼岸者，必須了達妄惑之本，善知至道出要。妄惑之本，是即意之實際；至道出要，所謂反照心源。識之實際，即是正因佛性；反照心源，即了因也。而此二因，攝一切法，罄無不盡。譬如清淨虛空之中，圓滿日光湛然而照，然此空之與日非即非離、非住非不住。而日善作破暗良緣、顯空之要，雖復滅暗顯空，空無損益。理實無損，事以推之，暗蔽永除，性乃無增，空界所含，萬象皆現。而此虛空性雖清淨，若無日光則有暗起，非以虛空空故自能除暗。暗若除者，必假日光。日若無空，無光無照；空若無日，暗不自除。然此暗性無來無去，日之體相亦不生滅。但有日照空，則乾坤洞曉。以智慧日照心性空，亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, a practitioner who wishes to cross the great ocean of birth and death and climb to the far shore of nirvāṇa must thoroughly understand the root of false delusion and know well the essential way out of the ultimate path. The root of false delusion is the very reality-limit of attention; the essential way out of the ultimate path is what is called turning back to illuminate the source of mind. The reality-limit of consciousness is the correct cause, buddha-nature; turning back to illuminate the source of mind is the revealing cause. And these two causes include all dharmas, exhausting them without remainder. It is like the full light of the sun shining clear and still in pure empty space; yet this space and the sun are neither identical nor separate, neither abiding nor not abiding. The sun is a good condition for breaking darkness and the key to revealing space; yet, though it extinguishes darkness and reveals space, space suffers neither loss nor gain. In principle there is truly no loss; reckoned by phenomena, the darkness and obscuration are removed forever, while the nature is not increased, and the myriad images contained in the realm of space all appear. Though the nature of this empty space is pure, if there is no sunlight, darkness arises; it is not that empty space, because it is empty, can remove darkness by itself. If darkness is removed, it must rely on sunlight. If the sun had no space, there would be no light and no illumination; if space had no sun, darkness would not remove itself. Yet the nature of this darkness neither comes nor goes, and the essence and marks of the sun are also neither born nor perishing. Only when the sun illuminates space are heaven and earth fully bright. Illuminating the empty nature of mind with the sun of wisdom is just like this.

Chinese Source

如日非即空，亦不離虛空。若日即是空，虛空何能照？若日離於空，則不應依空而有照。慧日亦如是，非即心性空，非離心性空。若即心性空，則不因修而有照；若離心性空，修亦不能照。如日非住空，亦非不住空。以不住空故，能照一切空；非不住空故，終不墮於空。慧日亦如是，深觀心性空，不住心性空，能照一切空；非不住空故，雖照一切空，慧心無動退。

Ru-Yi Translation

Just as the sun is not identical with space, yet is not separate from empty space — if the sun were identical with space, how could empty space illuminate? If the sun were separate from space, it should not depend on space to have its illumination — the sun of wisdom is likewise: it is not identical with the empty nature of mind, and not separate from the empty nature of mind. If it were identical with the empty nature of mind, there would be illumination without depending on cultivation; if it were separate from the empty nature of mind, cultivation could not illuminate. Just as the sun does not abide in space, yet does not fail to abide in space — because it does not abide in space, it can illuminate all space; because it does not fail to abide in space, it never falls into space — the sun of wisdom is likewise: deeply contemplating the empty nature of mind, it does not abide in the empty nature of mind, and so can illuminate all emptiness; because it does not fail to abide in emptiness, though it illuminates all emptiness, the mind of wisdom neither wavers nor retreats.

Chinese Source

如日能破暗，顯出虛空相。慧日亦如是，能破無明暗，顯發心實相。如日雖滅暗，顯於虛空相，而空無損益；慧日亦如是，能除無明暗，顯發心實相，而於心性空，不增亦不減。如日不損空，亦復不益空，能除空中暗，顯空界萬象。慧日亦如是，雖於心性空無損亦無益，能斷諸煩惱，而成就萬行，顯現一切法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Just as the sun can break darkness and reveal the mark of empty space, the sun of wisdom is likewise: it can break the darkness of ignorance and reveal the true mark of mind. Just as the sun, though it extinguishes darkness and reveals the mark of empty space, brings space neither loss nor gain, the sun of wisdom is likewise: it can remove the darkness of ignorance and reveal the true mark of mind, yet to the empty nature of mind it neither adds nor takes away. Just as the sun neither diminishes space nor adds to space, yet can remove the darkness within space and reveal the myriad images of the realm of space, the sun of wisdom is likewise: though it neither diminishes nor adds to the empty nature of mind, it can cut off all afflictions, accomplish the myriad practices, and make all dharmas appear.

Chinese Source

如空雖清淨，無日故暗起。心性亦如是，本來雖清淨，以無智慧光，則有妄惑起。如空雖清淨，不能自除暗，而暗得除者，必假於日光。心性空亦爾，本來雖清淨，不能自除惑，而惑得滅者，必以智慧照。如日若無空，則無光亦無照；空若無日者，則暗終不除。慧日亦如是，若無心性空，則何能有所照！若心性空無慧，妄惑終不斷，如暗無去來，日亦不生滅。解惑亦如是，假名說破惑，惑性無所有，不來亦不去，實亦無所破。智慧雖普照，其性常寂然，不生亦不滅，畢竟無所照。如有日照空，則乾坤洞然曉，反觀心性空，則一切世間諸法及一切出世間法，朗然圓顯。以是義故，說智慧照於心性，如空中之日。若能尋空日十喻達諸法相，因此入覺意海，是則名為辯諸法相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Just as space, though pure, has darkness arise because there is no sun, the nature of mind is likewise: though originally pure, because it lacks the light of wisdom, false delusions arise. Just as space, though pure, cannot remove darkness by itself, and when darkness is removed it must rely on sunlight, the empty nature of mind is likewise: though originally pure, it cannot remove delusion by itself, and when delusion is extinguished it must be through the illumination of wisdom. Just as, if the sun had no space, there would be neither light nor illumination, and if space had no sun, darkness would never be removed, the sun of wisdom is likewise: if there were no empty nature of mind, how could there be anything for it to illuminate! If the empty nature of mind lacks wisdom, false delusion will never be cut off. As darkness has no going or coming and the sun is neither born nor perishing, so it is with resolving delusion: breaking delusion is spoken of only as a provisional name; the nature of delusion is nothing at all, neither coming nor going, and in reality there is nothing broken. Though wisdom illuminates everywhere, its nature is always quiescent, neither born nor perishing, and ultimately there is nothing it illuminates. Just as, when the sun illuminates space, heaven and earth become fully bright, so when one turns back to contemplate the empty nature of mind, all worldly dharmas and all world-transcending dharmas appear clearly and completely. For this reason it is said that wisdom illuminates the nature of mind like the sun in space. If one can follow these ten similes of space and the sun to penetrate the marks of all dharmas, and thereby enter the ocean of awakening attention, this is what is called distinguishing the marks of dharmas.

Section Two: The Name

Chinese Source

釋覺意三昧名第二

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Name "Samādhi of Awakening Attention," Section Two

Chinese Source

問曰：云何名為覺意三昧？何等是意？菩薩覺是意故，即得具足三摩提耶？且復諸法無量，何以但對意用覺以明三昧？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is called the samādhi of awakening attention? What is attention? Is it that a bodhisattva, by awakening to this attention, at once attains complete samādhi? Moreover, dharmas are immeasurable — why does one apply awakening only to attention to clarify samādhi?

Chinese Source

答曰：覺名照了，意名諸心、心數，三昧名調直定。行者諸心、心數起時，反照觀察不見動轉，以是義故，名為覺意三昧。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Awakening" means illuminating and understanding; "attention" means the various minds and mental factors; "samādhi" means the concentration that is regulated and direct. When the practitioner's minds and mental factors arise, one turns back and illuminates them, contemplating and examining, and does not see them move or shift. For this reason it is called the samādhi of awakening attention.

Chinese Source

如所問言，諸法無量，何以但對意用覺以明三昧，不論餘者？答：一切諸法雖復無量，然窮其本源，莫不皆從心意識造。所以然者，有人言：「若初對境覺知，異乎木石名為心；次籌量分別名曰意；了了識達名之識。是為心意識之別。」如是取者，即墮心顛倒、想顛倒、見顛倒中。若能了知心中非有意、亦非不有意，則心中非有識、亦非不有識；若意中非有心、亦非不有心，則意中非有識、亦非不有識；若識中不有意、亦非不有意，則識中非有心、亦非不有心。是心意識，非一故立三名，非三故說一性。若名非名，則性亦非性，非名故不三，非性故不一，非三故不合，非一故非散，非合故不有，非散故不空，非有故不常，非空故不斷，是故心意識不斷亦不常。若不見斷常，終不見一異，是故說意者，即攝於心、識義。一切法亦然，若能深心觀察，破意無明，則餘癡使亦皆隨滅，是諸法雖復眾多，但舉覺意以明三昧，其義苞含靡所不攝也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As to what you asked — dharmas are immeasurable, so why apply awakening only to attention to clarify samādhi and not discuss the others? Answer: Although all dharmas are immeasurable, if one traces them to their root source, none is not produced by mind, attention, and consciousness. Why is this so? Some say: "When first facing an object there is awakening and knowing, different from wood or stone — this is called mind; next, deliberating and discriminating is called attention; clearly discerning is called consciousness. This is the distinction among mind, attention, and consciousness." One who grasps it this way falls into the inversion of mind, the inversion of conception, and the inversion of view. If one can understand that within mind there is neither attention nor the absence of attention, then within mind there is neither consciousness nor the absence of consciousness; if within attention there is neither mind nor the absence of mind, then within attention there is neither consciousness nor the absence of consciousness; if within consciousness attention is not had, nor yet is it not had, then within consciousness there is neither mind nor the absence of mind. Mind, attention, and consciousness are not one, hence three names are established; they are not three, hence a single nature is spoken of. If the name is not a name, then the nature too is not a nature. Because it is not a name, it is not three; because it is not a nature, it is not one; because it is not three, it is not combined; because it is not one, it is not scattered; because it is not combined, it is not existent; because it is not scattered, it is not empty; because it is not existent, it is not permanent; because it is not empty, it is not annihilated. Therefore mind, attention, and consciousness are neither annihilated nor permanent. If one does not see annihilation or permanence, one will in the end not see oneness or difference. Therefore speaking of attention already includes the meanings of mind and consciousness. All dharmas are likewise: if one can contemplate and examine with a deep mind and break the ignorance of attention, the remaining envoys of folly will all perish along with it. Thus, though dharmas are many, merely raising awakening attention to clarify samādhi, its meaning embraces everything and leaves nothing out.

Chinese Source

復次，如經中說：「云何名覺意三昧？於諸三昧中得七覺意，故名覺意三昧。」所言諸三昧者，一切法皆是三摩提，以諸法本來常寂不動故。復次，三摩提略說者有三種：一者世間；二者出世間；三出世間上上。世間三摩提者，所謂欲界散心中，十大地定數，欲界定、未到地定、四禪、四無量心、四無色定；出世間三摩提者，謂背捨、勝處、十一切處、九次第定、師子奮迅超越等行行觀鍊熏修禪，乃至慧行三十七品、三解脫門、四諦、十二因緣等三昧；出世間上上三摩提者，所謂十力種性三昧，首楞嚴等百八三昧，乃至如十方界微塵等數三昧，是為三種三摩提。攝一切法即是一切法，故名諸三昧。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, as a sūtra says: "What is called the samādhi of awakening attention? Within all samādhis one attains the seven awakening attentions; hence it is called the samādhi of awakening attention." As for "all samādhis": all dharmas are samādhi, because all dharmas are originally always quiescent and unmoving. Further, samādhi, briefly stated, is of three kinds: first, worldly; second, world-transcending; third, the highest of the highest beyond the world. Worldly samādhi means the ten universal-ground factors of concentration within the scattered mind of the desire realm, the concentration of the desire realm, the not-yet-arrived concentration, the four dhyānas, the four immeasurable minds, and the four formless concentrations. World-transcending samādhi means the liberations, the bases of mastery, the ten all-pervading bases, the nine successive concentrations, the lion's-sprint and the transcending [samādhis], and the other practices — the contemplating, refining, perfuming, and cultivating dhyānas — up to the samādhis of the wisdom practices: the thirty-seven factors of the path, the three gates of liberation, the four truths, the twelve links of conditioned arising, and so on. The highest of the highest samādhi beyond the world means the samādhis of the lineage of the ten powers, the hundred and eight samādhis such as the Śūraṅgama, up to samādhis as numerous as the motes of dust in the realms of the ten directions. These are the three kinds of samādhi. They include all dharmas and are themselves all dharmas; hence they are called "all samādhis."

Chinese Source

云何得名七覺意？七覺意者，一擇覺；二精進覺；三喜覺；四除覺；五捨覺；六定覺；七念覺。是為七覺。七覺之義乃有多途，舉要略明不出六種。何等為六？一者因聞七覺、二者修行七覺、三者會理七覺、四者起方便七覺、五者入法門七覺、六者圓極七覺。

Ru-Yi Translation

How does one come to the name "seven awakening attentions"? The seven awakening attentions are: first, the awakening of discernment; second, the awakening of vigor; third, the awakening of joy; fourth, the awakening of removal; fifth, the awakening of relinquishment; sixth, the awakening of concentration; seventh, the awakening of mindfulness. These are the seven awakenings. The meaning of the seven awakenings runs along many lines; raising the essentials and explaining them briefly, they do not go beyond six kinds. What are the six? First, the seven awakenings through hearing; second, the seven awakenings of cultivation and practice; third, the seven awakenings of union with principle; fourth, the seven awakenings of giving rise to expedients; fifth, the seven awakenings of entering the dharma-gates; sixth, the seven awakenings of perfect culmination.

Chinese Source

第一因聞七覺者，一切諸法本性空寂，畢竟清淨，而諸眾生無能知者。若遇諸佛菩薩及善知識說一切諸法本來空寂，是人聞已，即大驚悟，因是了達心及諸法一切三摩提畢竟清淨、空無所有，得七覺意。是人因聞發故，故名因聞七覺。第二修行七覺者，若行人雖知心及諸法一切三摩提空無生滅，而倒想猶起。隨所起念，常以七覺調適修心反照觀察。以觀行調適故，即便豁然覺了心及諸法一切三摩提從本以來不生不滅，如大涅槃，是則名為修行七覺也。第三會理七覺者，若人藉此信法二行因緣，悟心及諸法一切三摩提理同一真如，而知真如亦非真如。若覺悟真如者，則於真如之理具七覺意，是以不住真如實際作證，是則名為會理七覺也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the seven awakenings through hearing: the original nature of all dharmas is empty and quiescent, ultimately pure, yet no sentient being is able to know it. If one meets the buddhas, bodhisattvas, and good teachers explaining that all dharmas are originally empty and quiescent, then, having heard it, this person is greatly startled into enlightenment, and through this thoroughly understands that mind, all dharmas, and all samādhis are ultimately pure, empty, and without anything at all, and attains the seven awakening attentions. Because this person is set off by hearing, it is called the seven awakenings through hearing. Second, the seven awakenings of cultivation and practice: although the practitioner knows that mind, all dharmas, and all samādhis are empty and without birth or perishing, inverted notions still arise. Whatever thought arises, one always uses the seven awakenings to adjust and cultivate the mind, turning back and illuminating, contemplating and examining. Because of adjusting through contemplative practice, one at once suddenly awakens clearly to the fact that mind, all dharmas, and all samādhis from the very beginning have been neither born nor perishing, like great nirvāṇa. This is called the seven awakenings of cultivation and practice. Third, the seven awakenings of union with principle: suppose a person, through the causes and conditions of these two practices of faith and dharma, is enlightened to the principle that mind, all dharmas, and all samādhis are the same single suchness, and knows that suchness too is not suchness. One who awakens and is enlightened to suchness possesses the seven awakening attentions within the principle of suchness; therefore one does not abide in the reality-limit of suchness and take realization there. This is called the seven awakenings of union with principle.

Chinese Source

第四起方便七覺者，若行人得理無證，憐愍眾生興心萬行，隨有所行悉知寂滅。雖知無住無行，而以七覺善巧修一切自利利他三摩提行，如空中種樹，是則名為起方便七覺也。第五入法門七覺者，菩薩若能如是不依心及諸法一切三摩提若真若俗，即是具足二空之觀，得入中道，雙照二諦，隨心所念，則自然出生一切十力種性諸三昧等，而亦不得諸三昧相。所以者何？諸陀羅尼諸陀羅尼相空，諸三昧諸三昧相空，故於一切陀羅尼、三昧功德智慧中，心無住著，是則菩薩七覺分分圓顯，故名入法門七覺，亦名開佛知見。若能開佛知見，則心心寂滅，自然流入十住、十行、十迴向、十地及等覺清淨禪中，是故得名入法門七覺也。第六圓極七覺者，若菩薩摩訶薩住金剛三昧清淨禪中，朗然大悟，得一念相應慧，寂然圓照一切了了分明，是名圓極七覺，亦名無上妙覺，亦名無學七覺。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the seven awakenings of giving rise to expedients: the practitioner, having attained the principle without taking realization, has pity on sentient beings and gives rise in mind to the myriad practices, and in whatever is practiced knows it all to be quiescent extinction. Though knowing there is no abiding and no practice, one uses the seven awakenings skillfully to cultivate all the samādhi practices that benefit oneself and benefit others, like planting trees in the sky. This is called the seven awakenings of giving rise to expedients. Fifth, the seven awakenings of entering the dharma-gates: if a bodhisattva can in this way not depend on mind, all dharmas, and all samādhis, whether real or conventional, this is the contemplation that fully possesses the two emptinesses; one enters the middle way and illuminates both of the two truths, and in accordance with whatever the mind thinks, all the samādhis of the lineage of the ten powers spontaneously come forth, and yet one does not obtain the mark of those samādhis. Why? Because the dhāraṇīs are empty of the mark of dhāraṇīs, and the samādhis are empty of the mark of samādhis; therefore, among all the merits and wisdom of dhāraṇī and samādhi, the mind has no abiding attachment. Then the bodhisattva's seven awakenings appear perfectly, part by part; hence it is called the seven awakenings of entering the dharma-gates, and also called opening the buddha's knowledge and vision. If one can open the buddha's knowledge and vision, then mind after mind is quiescent and extinguished, and one spontaneously flows into the ten abodes, the ten practices, the ten dedications, the ten grounds, and the pure dhyāna of equal awakening; therefore it is called the seven awakenings of entering the dharma-gates. Sixth, the seven awakenings of perfect culmination: if a bodhisattva-mahāsattva, abiding in the pure dhyāna of the vajra samādhi, is clearly and greatly enlightened and attains the wisdom that corresponds in a single thought, quiescently and perfectly illuminating everything clearly and distinctly, this is called the seven awakenings of perfect culmination; it is also called unsurpassed wondrous awakening, and also called the seven awakenings of no-more-learning.

Chinese Source

以如是等諸七覺義故，菩薩從初發心所有觀行法門，終至極果，通名七覺意，亦名觀心相，亦名反照識。如是等種種名字無量三昧者，秦言調直心，亦名常寂定，如明鏡不動、靜水無波，若對眾境，影像皆現。心亦如是，性雖明淨，以念動故，則無所照了。因上修習，即得念無動轉，普現法門。得此定已，心無邪曲，名為三昧，故云覺意三昧。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because of these and other meanings of the seven awakenings, all the dharma-gates of contemplative practice that a bodhisattva has, from the first arousing of the mind until finally reaching the ultimate fruit, are commonly called the seven awakening attentions, also called contemplating the marks of mind, also called turning back to illuminate consciousness. Samādhi, known by these various names without measure, means in the language of Qin "the regulated and direct mind"; it is also called "the concentration of constant quiescence." It is like a bright mirror unmoving, or still water without waves: facing all objects, every image appears. Mind is likewise: though its nature is bright and pure, because thought moves, there is nothing it illuminates and understands. Through the cultivation above, one attains thought without moving or shifting and everywhere manifests the dharma-gates. Having attained this concentration, the mind is without crookedness; this is called samādhi. Hence it is called the samādhi of awakening attention.

Section Three: Expedient Practice

Chinese Source

釋覺意三昧方便行第三

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Expedient Practice of the Samādhi of Awakening Attention, Section Three

Chinese Source

問曰：已知覺意三昧名義如是，行者行何方便得此三昧，於諸三昧得七覺意，入深法性，到大涅槃，獲常樂我淨，為一切眾生作無上洲渚？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Now that the name and meaning of the samādhi of awakening attention are known to be thus, by practicing what expedients does the practitioner attain this samādhi, attain the seven awakening attentions within all samādhis, enter the deep dharma-nature, reach great nirvāṇa, obtain permanence, bliss, self, and purity, and become an unsurpassed island for all sentient beings?

Chinese Source

答曰：行者為成就大悲度眾生，故求無上菩提至真之道，先當立大誓願，發志誠心以誓自要：「若我所學其事不成，終不中途有悔生退沒心。」爾時，心如金剛，決定信知諸法畢竟空寂，而不捨無邊眾生，故修諸行。云何為修？若行者了知心及一切諸法皆無所有、不生不滅、寂然清淨，而能善用六度方便以自調伏虛妄之心。妄心既息，三昧自發。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: In order to accomplish great compassion and deliver sentient beings, the practitioner seeks the supremely true path of unsurpassed bodhi. First he should establish a great vow, arouse a sincere mind, and bind himself with an oath: "If what I study does not succeed, I will never, midway, have regrets or give rise to a mind that retreats and sinks." At that time the mind is like vajra, firmly believing and knowing that all dharmas are ultimately empty and quiescent, yet not abandoning boundless sentient beings; therefore one cultivates all the practices. What is cultivation? It is when the practitioner understands that mind and all dharmas are without anything at all, neither born nor perishing, quiescent and pure, and yet can skillfully use the expedients of the six perfections to tame his own false mind. Once the false mind has ceased, samādhi arises of itself.

Chinese Source

何等為六？若行者知心及物如夢所見、皆無有實，是故於一切所有悉能捨離，常自覺識不令慳著想起，亦當迴此清淨捨心遍施眾生，是時名修淨施之心。因是心故，則能趣向檀波羅蜜。若行者知心如幻，外諸惡法皆不可得，雖對眾境常自覺了，不令惡念心生，是時名修淨戒之心。因此心故，則能趣向尸波羅蜜。若行者知心如焰、空無根本，外之八法亦皆無實，是故利、衰、毀、譽、稱、譏、苦、樂，常自覺了、不生愛恚，是名修堅固忍。因此心故，則能趣向羼提波羅蜜。

Ru-Yi Translation

What are the six? If the practitioner knows that mind and things are like what is seen in a dream, all without reality, he can therefore relinquish all that he has, always awakening and recognizing by himself so that no notion of stinginess or attachment arises; he should also dedicate this pure mind of relinquishment to all sentient beings everywhere. This is called cultivating the mind of pure giving. Because of this mind he can advance toward dāna-pāramitā. If the practitioner knows that the mind is like an illusion and all outer evil dharmas cannot be found, then, though facing all objects, he always awakens clearly by himself and does not let evil thoughts arise in the mind. This is called cultivating the mind of pure precepts. Because of this mind he can advance toward śīla-pāramitā. If the practitioner knows that the mind is like a mirage, empty and without root, and that the eight outer dharmas likewise are all unreal, then toward gain and loss, defamation and fame, praise and ridicule, suffering and pleasure, he always awakens clearly by himself and gives rise to neither love nor anger. This is called cultivating firm forbearance. Because of this mind he can advance toward kṣānti-pāramitā.

Chinese Source

若行者知心如化，常自覺了，觀行相續，不令懈怠、放逸心生，是時名修精進之心。因此心故，則能趣向毘梨耶波羅蜜。若行者知心如鏡中像，一切所緣諸法皆無所有，於行住坐臥四威儀中亂想不起，假令失念尋即覺知，故妄波不起心常寂然，是時名修清淨定心。因是心故，則能趣向禪波羅蜜。若行者了知心如虛空，六識所緣內外諸法皆無所有、畢竟空寂，善用無所得心破諸顛倒，不得一切法、不著一切法、了達一切法，是時名修正智慧心。因是心故，則能趣向般若波羅蜜。行者若不修如上六種向道清淨之心，則不堪修甚深三昧，是故欲修覺意三昧者，應須善學如上六度方便。此六方便攝一切方便，若能善用調伏六蔽麁心令意柔軟，然後審諦細心觀察入正慧門，是名習學甚深三昧初心方便。

Ru-Yi Translation

If the practitioner knows that the mind is like a magical transformation, always awakens clearly by himself, keeps contemplative practice continuous, and does not let a lazy or heedless mind arise, this is called cultivating the mind of vigor. Because of this mind he can advance toward vīrya-pāramitā. If the practitioner knows that the mind is like an image in a mirror and that all the dharmas taken as objects are without anything at all, then in the four postures of walking, standing, sitting, and lying down, confused notions do not arise; and should he lose mindfulness, he at once awakens to it and knows it. Therefore the waves of falsehood do not arise and the mind is always quiescent. This is called cultivating the pure mind of concentration. Because of this mind he can advance toward dhyāna-pāramitā. If the practitioner understands that the mind is like empty space, and that the inner and outer dharmas taken as objects by the six consciousnesses are all without anything at all, ultimately empty and quiescent, and skillfully uses the mind of non-attainment to break all inversions — not obtaining any dharma, not attached to any dharma, thoroughly understanding all dharmas — this is called cultivating the mind of right wisdom. Because of this mind he can advance toward prajñā-pāramitā. If the practitioner does not cultivate the six kinds of pure mind directed to the path described above, he is not fit to cultivate very deep samādhi. Therefore one who wishes to cultivate the samādhi of awakening attention must study well the expedients of the six perfections above. These six expedients include all expedients. If one can use them well to tame the coarse minds of the six obscurations and make attention supple, and then examine carefully with a subtle mind, contemplating, and enter the gate of right wisdom, this is called the expedient of the initial mind for training in very deep samādhi.

Section Four: The Marks of Mind

Chinese Source

釋覺意三昧明心相第四

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Marks of Mind in the Samādhi of Awakening Attention, Section Four

Chinese Source

問曰：行者欲入此三昧，當對幾心相而觀察之？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: When the practitioner wishes to enter this samādhi, with respect to how many marks of mind should he contemplate and examine?

Chinese Source

答曰：諸經論中辯心相各各不同，今不具述，是中略明四種心相以為觀境。何等為四？一者未念、二者欲念、三者念、四者念已。未念，名心未起緣境。欲念，名心欲起緣境。念，名緣境心滿住。念已，名緣境心滿足已，謝滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The sūtras and treatises distinguish the marks of mind each differently; here I do not set them all out, but briefly explain four kinds of marks of mind to serve as the object of contemplation. What are the four? First, not-yet-thought; second, about-to-think; third, thinking; fourth, having-thought. Not-yet-thought means the mind has not yet arisen to take an object. About-to-think means the mind is about to arise to take an object. Thinking means the mind taking the object abides in fullness. Having-thought means the mind taking the object, having been fulfilled, falls away and perishes.

Chinese Source

問曰：心相眾多，何以但舉此四運心相？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The marks of mind are many; why raise only these marks of mind in the fourfold moment?

Chinese Source

答曰：此四運心相攝一切心，如緣惡法，未念惡法、欲念惡法、念惡法、念惡法已；如緣善法，未念善、欲念善、念善、念善已；緣諸六塵及三毒等一切煩惱，乃至行住坐臥、言語飲食、所作施為一切諸事，皆有如上四相之心；及緣一切世間法，皆有如此四相之心。是故但說四種之相以為觀境，靡所不攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: These marks of mind in the fourfold moment include all minds. For example, in taking evil dharmas: not-yet-thinking of evil dharmas, about-to-think of evil dharmas, thinking of evil dharmas, having-thought of evil dharmas. In taking wholesome dharmas: not-yet-thinking of the wholesome, about-to-think of the wholesome, thinking of the wholesome, having-thought of the wholesome. In taking the six sense-objects and all afflictions such as the three poisons, up to walking, standing, sitting, and lying down, speaking, eating and drinking, and every undertaking and activity, all have minds of the four marks above; and in taking all worldly dharmas, all have minds of these four marks. Therefore only the four kinds of marks are spoken of as the object of contemplation, and nothing is left out.

Chinese Source

問曰：何謂為相？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is meant by "mark"?

Chinese Source

答曰：攬而可別名為相。心識之法既無形質，若不約此四運之念分別，則難可了知，若不可了知，則不可觀察，故須先以四相分別。若觀分明了達此相非相，即入一相平等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: What can be grasped and distinguished is called a mark. The dharmas of mind and consciousness have no form or substance; if they were not distinguished by way of the thoughts of these four moments, they would be hard to know; if they cannot be known, they cannot be contemplated and examined. Therefore one must first distinguish them by the four marks. If, contemplating clearly, one thoroughly understands that these marks are not marks, one at once enters the one mark of equality.

Chinese Source

問曰：觀欲念、念二運心相可爾。未念未起，則為無心，無心故，則無相可分別。念已已滅亦與無無異，無法即無相，云何可觀？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Contemplating the marks of mind of the two moments about-to-think and thinking may be possible. But not-yet-thought has not yet arisen, so it is no mind; because it is no mind, there is no mark to distinguish. Having-thought has already perished, which likewise is no different from nothing; where there is no dharma, there is no mark. How can these be contemplated?

Chinese Source

答曰：未念雖未起，而非畢竟無心。所以者何？譬如人未作，後有緣事即便作作。不可以未作故，即便無人，若定無人，後誰作作？以有未作人故，則後有作人。心相亦應如是，因未念故，得有欲念，若無未念之心，何得有欲念心耶？是故未念雖未起，不得言畢竟無也。汝言念已心已滅則不可觀者，是亦不然。念已雖滅，亦可觀察。譬如人作竟，不得言無人，若定無人者，前誰更作？念已心滅亦復如是，不得言永滅無心。若心滅已永滅者，則是斷見，說無因果。是故念已雖滅，亦可得觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Though not-yet-thought has not yet arisen, it is not ultimately no mind. Why? It is like a person who has not yet acted, and later, when there is some matter to attend to, acts. One cannot say there is no person because he has not yet acted; if there were definitely no person, who would act later? Because there is a person who has not yet acted, later there is a person who acts. The marks of mind should be likewise: because of not-yet-thought, there can be about-to-think; if there were no mind of not-yet-thought, how could there be a mind of about-to-think? Therefore, though not-yet-thought has not yet arisen, it cannot be said to be ultimately nothing. You say that having-thought, the mind having perished, cannot be contemplated; this too is not so. Though having-thought has perished, it can still be contemplated and examined. It is like a person who has finished acting: one cannot say there is no person; if there were definitely no person, who acted before? Having-thought, the mind having perished, is likewise: one cannot say it has perished forever and there is no mind. If the mind, having perished, perished forever, that would be the view of annihilation, which says there is no cause and effect. Therefore, though having-thought has perished, it can still be contemplated.

Chinese Source

問曰：汝云何觀心？若觀過去心，過去心已過；若觀未來心，未來心未至；若觀現在心，現在心不住。若離三世，則無有別心。更觀何等心？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: How do you contemplate mind? If you contemplate the past mind, the past mind has already passed; if you contemplate the future mind, the future mind has not yet arrived; if you contemplate the present mind, the present mind does not abide. Apart from the three times there is no other mind. What mind, then, do you contemplate?

Chinese Source

答曰：汝問非也。若過去永滅畢竟不可知者，云何諸聖人能知一切過去心？若未來心未至不有不可知，云何諸聖人能知一切未來心？若現在心無住不可知，云何諸聖人能知一切十方眾生現在念事？如世鬼神，尚自能知己三世心，亦能知他三世之心，何得佛法行人而起斷滅見，謂三世心，如龜毛兔角不可得知！當知三世之心雖無定實，亦可得知。故偈云：「諸佛之所說，雖空亦不斷，相續亦不常，罪福亦不失。」汝勿斷見住無所知，不修觀行猶如盲人，雖對眾色而無所見。汝亦如是，於佛法中無正觀眼，空無所獲。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Your question is wrong. If the past had perished forever and were ultimately unknowable, how could the sages know all past minds? If the future mind, not yet arrived, did not exist and were unknowable, how could the sages know all future minds? If the present mind, without abiding, were unknowable, how could the sages know the present thoughts and affairs of all sentient beings of the ten directions? Even the ghosts and spirits of the world can know their own minds of the three times, and can also know the minds of others in the three times; how can a practitioner of the Buddhadharma give rise to the view of annihilation, saying that the minds of the three times, like a turtle's hair or a rabbit's horn, cannot be known! You should know that the minds of the three times, though without fixed reality, can still be known. Hence the verse says: "What the buddhas teach is: / though empty, it is not annihilated; / though continuing, it is not permanent; / offense and merit are not lost." Do not hold the view of annihilation and dwell in knowing nothing, not cultivating contemplative practice, like a blind person who, though facing all forms, sees nothing. You are likewise: within the Buddhadharma you lack the eye of right contemplation, and you are left empty, gaining nothing.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Title. 釋摩訶般若波羅蜜經覺意三昧 (T1922). The samādhi is named in the Larger Prajñāpāramitā (T0223) list of samādhis. Calming and Contemplation fascicle 2 (site, Mohe Zhiguan Fascicle Two) identifies it with the neither-walking-nor-sitting samādhi and with Huisi's "following one's own intent" (隨自意).

Awakening attention (覺意). The site's Mohe Zhiguan renders 覺意三昧 "the samādhi of awakened intent" and 意 "intent." This draft uses the title fixed for this text, "awakening attention," and therefore renders 意 "attention" throughout, 心 "mind," 識 "consciousness," 心數 "mental factors," and 念 "thought" (in the fourfold moment) or "mindfulness" (念覺, 失念). 覺 is rendered "awaken / awakening" in every doctrinal use; 悟 is "be enlightened," 證 "realization."

Seven awakening attentions (七覺意). An older name for the seven factors of awakening (七覺支, bodhyaṅga). The text derives the samādhi's name from it (unit 8, quoting a sūtra), so the draft keeps the literal "seven awakening attentions" to preserve the link. The seven are rendered discernment / vigor / joy / removal (除, i.e., pliancy) / relinquishment / concentration / mindfulness.

Mind, attention, consciousness (unit 8). The definitions rejected here ("first facing an object… clearly discerning") and the tetralemma that follows are reproduced almost word for word in Calming and Contemplation fascicle 2; the site has the parallel in Mohe Zhiguan Fascicle Two. The order of 不合／不散 and 不有／不空 differs slightly between the two texts.

Reality-limit of attention / of consciousness (unit 4). The text first says 意之實際 and then 識之實際 for the same item; both are translated literally.

Space and emptiness (unit 4). In the ten similes 空 stands for 虛空 "empty space"; when the simile is applied to mind (心性空, 一切空) it is rendered "emptiness / the empty nature of mind." "Resolving delusion" (解惑): alternatively, "understanding and delusion."

Three kinds of samādhi (unit 8). The lists (ten universal-ground factors, not-yet-arrived concentration, liberations, bases of mastery, all-pervading bases, nine successive concentrations, lion's-sprint and transcending samādhis, the contemplating / refining / perfuming / cultivating dhyānas) follow the scheme of Zhiyi's Explanation of the Sequential Dharma-Gates of the Dhyāna Pāramitā (T1916). 十大地定數 is translated literally as "the ten universal-ground factors of concentration."

Open the buddha's knowledge and vision. From the Lotus, "Expedient Means" chapter; wording as in the site's Lotus Sūtra and the round-2 Fahua Sanmei Chanyi draft.

"Mind after mind is quiescent and extinguished … spontaneously flows into" (unit 8). Echoes the Yingluo Sūtra (T1485), which uses 心心寂滅，自然流入 for the bodhisattva stages.

Six perfections and six obscurations (unit 11). The similes dream / illusion / mirage / transformation / mirror image / space are matched to the six perfections. The "eight outer dharmas" are the eight worldly winds, listed in the text. 六蔽 "six obscurations" as in the site's Mohe Zhiguan.

Fourfold moment (units 14–20). 四運 and its four members follow the site's Mohe Zhiguan (Fascicle Two): the fourfold moment; not-yet-thought / about-to-think / thinking / having-thought. The argument from the person who has not yet acted is shared with that passage.

Verse (unit 22). Cf. Middle Treatise (T1564), chapter on action: 雖空亦不斷，雖有亦不常，業果報不失，是名佛所說. The text quotes a variant (相續亦不常，罪福亦不失). 罪福 "offense and merit" as in the round-2 Puxian Guan Jing draft.

Folly (癡). Because 惑 is "delusion" (glossary: 三惑), 癡 in 餘癡使 is rendered "folly."
