The Gates of Entering Contemplation and the Marks of Realization

Part 2 contains the last two of the text's six sections. Section Five, the longest, gives the method itself. It first works through the transition from not-yet-thought to about-to-think by four propositions — the earlier moment perishes, does not perish, both, or neither — and in each case tests whether the later moment arises identical with it or apart from it, so that its arising cannot be found; the same analysis is then run in reverse and extended to thinking and having-thought. Not finding the names of arising and perishing, or even their absence, the practitioner leaves the two extremes and the right wisdom of the middle way opens. The section then distinguishes general contemplation, done in seated meditation, from contemplation carried through the twelve objects of daily life: six outer doings (walking, standing, sitting, lying down, working, speaking) and six inner receivings (the six senses with their objects), each examined by the fourfold moment and each closed with a line from the Vimalakīrti. It ends with three contemplations — of the confused, the concentrated, and the contemplating mind — identified with the three samādhis, which overcome the four demons. Section Six describes the stages reached: the ten minds of faith at the outer ordinary (iron-wheel) stage, and entry into the first abode, the abode of arousing the mind, with its kinds of dhyāna. Calming and Contemplation, fascicle 2, gives a shorter version of much of this material.

Section Five: The Gates of Entering Contemplation

Chinese Source

釋覺意三昧入觀門第五

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Gates of Entering Contemplation in the Samādhi of Awakening Attention, Section Five

Chinese Source

問曰：已知四運心相攝一切心，行者云何觀察此心通達實相圓照，分明諸三昧具七覺意？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: It is now known that the marks of mind in the fourfold moment include all minds. How does the practitioner contemplate and examine this mind so as to penetrate the true mark with perfect illumination, so that all samādhis become clear and he possesses the seven awakening attentions?

Not-Yet-Thought and About-to-Think: Setting Out the Four Propositions

Chinese Source

答曰：行者先以大誓莊嚴，善修如上六度法門以調其心。信知諸法畢竟空寂，而我為無明所覆未能覺了，必須勤修正觀，行到乃知，豈可虛心妄解而自毀傷？既能善自調和，然後隨心所起，以無所住著之心，反照觀察未念、欲念、念、念已之相。爾時諦觀未念心為滅，欲念心生？未念心為不滅，欲念心生？未念心為亦滅亦不滅，欲念心生？未念心為非滅非不滅，欲念心生？如此於未念四句中，觀欲念心生皆不可得。若不得欲念心生，亦不得不生，即於心性而得解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The practitioner first adorns himself with the great vow and skillfully cultivates the dharma-gates of the six perfections above to adjust his mind. He believes and knows that all dharmas are ultimately empty and quiescent, but that he, covered by ignorance, has not yet been able to awaken clearly to this; he must diligently cultivate right contemplation, for only when practice arrives does one know — how could one, with an empty mind, understand falsely and so harm oneself? Having been able to adjust himself well, then, in accordance with whatever arises in the mind, with a mind that does not abide or attach, he turns back and illuminates, contemplating and examining the marks of not-yet-thought, about-to-think, thinking, and having-thought. At that time he carefully contemplates: Does the mind of not-yet-thought perish, and the mind of about-to-think arise? Does the mind of not-yet-thought not perish, and the mind of about-to-think arise? Does the mind of not-yet-thought both perish and not perish, and the mind of about-to-think arise? Does the mind of not-yet-thought neither perish nor not perish, and the mind of about-to-think arise? In this way, within the four propositions of not-yet-thought, he contemplates that the arising of the mind of about-to-think cannot be found in any of them. If he does not find the arising of the mind of about-to-think, and also does not find its not arising, he at once attains liberation in the nature of mind.

The First Proposition: Not-Yet-Thought Perishes

Chinese Source

云何名於未念四句中觀欲念心生不可得？一、先約未念初句觀欲念心生不可得。若謂未念心滅有欲念心生者，未念心已滅，欲念何處生？為即未念滅生？為離未念滅生？若即未念滅生欲念者，滅法不應生，以生滅性相違故。若謂即滅中有生，生滅不相違者，是事不然。若爾，應如熟果皮中有核，皮爛核出，皮非是核、核非是皮，何得皮即是核？心法亦如是，即滅不得有生，是故即未念滅欲念心生不可得。若謂離未念滅有欲念心生者，則為無因而有生，是事不然。以生無所從生，是則不名為生。如虛空無所從生，故虛空不名為生。當知離未念滅，欲念心生不可得。行者如是，若即若離中觀未念心滅欲念心生畢竟不可得。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is meant by contemplating, within the four propositions of not-yet-thought, that the arising of the mind of about-to-think cannot be found? 1. First, by the first proposition of not-yet-thought, one contemplates that the arising of the mind of about-to-think cannot be found. If it is said that the mind of not-yet-thought perishes and there is the arising of the mind of about-to-think: since the mind of not-yet-thought has already perished, where does about-to-think arise? Does it arise identical with the perishing of not-yet-thought? Or does it arise apart from the perishing of not-yet-thought? If about-to-think arises identical with the perishing of not-yet-thought, a perishing dharma should not arise, because the natures and marks of arising and perishing are contrary. If it is said that within perishing there is arising, and arising and perishing are not contrary, this is not so. If that were so, it should be like a ripe fruit with a pit within its skin: when the skin rots, the pit comes out; the skin is not the pit, and the pit is not the skin — how could the skin be the pit? The dharma of mind is likewise: identical with perishing there cannot be arising. Therefore the arising of the mind of about-to-think identical with the perishing of not-yet-thought cannot be found. If it is said that apart from the perishing of not-yet-thought there is the arising of the mind of about-to-think, then there would be arising without a cause; this is not so. For if the arising arises from nowhere, it is not called arising — just as empty space arises from nowhere, and therefore empty space is not called arising. You should know that apart from the perishing of not-yet-thought, the arising of the mind of about-to-think cannot be found. In this way the practitioner contemplates, whether identical or apart, that the mind of not-yet-thought perishing and the mind of about-to-think arising ultimately cannot be found.

The Second Proposition: Not-Yet-Thought Does Not Perish

Chinese Source

二、明約未念第二句觀欲念心生不可得。若謂未念心不滅，欲念心生者，為即不滅生？為離不滅生？若即不滅生，不滅已是生，是生何得生？若是生能生，此生復應有生生，則有無窮生。若此生生生，是事不然。若是一體生，一中不應有多生，如一指中則無多指。若是異體生，則不應名生生，以生體別不能相生故。如桃柰體別，桃不生柰，柰不生桃。是故，即未念不滅，欲念心生不可得。若謂離未念不滅有欲念心生者，欲念何處生？若生無處生，即是無因生。若是無因生，是則為非生。非生而說生者，是事不然，以墮無因果過，如說石女之子、黃門之兒。當知離未念不滅有欲念心生不可得。行者如是，若即若離中，觀未念不滅欲念心生畢竟不可得。

Ru-Yi Translation

2. Clarifying how, by the second proposition of not-yet-thought, one contemplates that the arising of the mind of about-to-think cannot be found. If it is said that the mind of not-yet-thought does not perish and the mind of about-to-think arises: does it arise identical with not perishing, or arise apart from not perishing? If it arises identical with not perishing, not perishing is already arising — how could this arising arise? If this arising can arise, this arising should in turn have an arising of arising, and then there would be endless arising. If [it is said that] this arising arises an arising, this is not so. If it is arising of one essence, within one there should not be many arisings, just as within one finger there are not many fingers. If it is arising of different essences, it should not be called the arising of arising, because the essences of the arisings are distinct and cannot give rise to one another — as the peach and the crab apple are distinct in essence: the peach does not produce the crab apple, and the crab apple does not produce the peach. Therefore the arising of the mind of about-to-think identical with not-yet-thought not perishing cannot be found. If it is said that apart from not-yet-thought not perishing there is the arising of the mind of about-to-think, where does about-to-think arise? If its arising arises from nowhere, it is arising without a cause. If it is arising without a cause, then it is non-arising. To speak of arising for what is non-arising is not so, because it falls into the error of [having] no cause and effect, like speaking of the child of a barren woman or the son of a eunuch. You should know that apart from not-yet-thought not perishing, the arising of the mind of about-to-think cannot be found. In this way the practitioner contemplates, whether identical or apart, that not-yet-thought not perishing and the mind of about-to-think arising ultimately cannot be found.

The Third Proposition: Both Perishing and Not Perishing

Chinese Source

三、明約未念第三句觀欲念心生不可得。若謂未念心亦滅亦不滅有欲念心生者，若是亦滅生，何須亦不滅？若是亦不滅生，何須亦滅？以不定因不能有定果故，如不定根人不能生定根之子。若謂亦滅亦不滅，體一無異故，有欲念心生者，是事不然。而今亦滅非亦不滅，亦不滅非亦滅，性相違故，不應體一不異能生於欲念，如不定根人二根體非一故，不能生一子。若謂亦滅亦不滅體異，二各能生欲念者，體異即還是定滅、定不滅，何名亦滅亦不滅？若是定滅定不滅各能生欲念者，即應二欲念生，今實不爾。若二各不生，則無欲念生。行者如是觀時，未念亦滅亦不滅欲念心生畢竟不可得。

Ru-Yi Translation

3. Clarifying how, by the third proposition of not-yet-thought, one contemplates that the arising of the mind of about-to-think cannot be found. If it is said that the mind of not-yet-thought both perishes and does not perish and there is the arising of the mind of about-to-think: if it is arising from "also perishing," what need is there of "also not perishing"? If it is arising from "also not perishing," what need is there of "also perishing"? For an indeterminate cause cannot have a determinate effect, just as a person whose [sexual] faculty is indeterminate cannot produce a child whose faculty is determinate. If it is said that "both perishing and not perishing" is one in essence, without difference, and therefore there is the arising of the mind of about-to-think, this is not so. For now "also perishing" is not "also not perishing," and "also not perishing" is not "also perishing"; since their natures are contrary, they should not be one in essence without difference and able to give rise to about-to-think — just as in a person whose faculty is indeterminate the two faculties are not one in essence, and so cannot produce a single child. If it is said that "both perishing and not perishing" differ in essence, and the two can each give rise to about-to-think, then being different in essence they revert to definite perishing and definite not perishing — how could they be called "both perishing and not perishing"? If definite perishing and definite not perishing could each give rise to about-to-think, there should be two arisings of about-to-think, which in fact is not so. If the two each do not give rise, then there is no arising of about-to-think. When the practitioner contemplates in this way, not-yet-thought both perishing and not perishing and the mind of about-to-think arising ultimately cannot be found.

The Fourth Proposition: Neither Perishing nor Not Perishing

Chinese Source

次明約未念第四句觀欲念心生不可得。若謂未念心非滅非不滅有欲念心生者，若因非滅生，不須非不滅；若因非不滅生，則不須非滅。以非滅非不滅所非各異，不應俱以為因，亦是相違之因不能共有一果故。如水火互非，終不於中而生果實。若謂俱因二非而有生者，是事不然。若二非之處各是有者，二有還應生二生，今實不爾。若二非之處各是無，則無能生，何能生所生？若無能生所生者，即所生不名為所生，以所生無所從生故。行者如是觀非滅非不滅欲念心生畢竟不可得。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, clarifying how, by the fourth proposition of not-yet-thought, one contemplates that the arising of the mind of about-to-think cannot be found. If it is said that the mind of not-yet-thought neither perishes nor does not perish and there is the arising of the mind of about-to-think: if it arises because of "not perishing," it does not need "not not perishing"; if it arises because of "not not perishing," then it does not need "not perishing." Since what "not perishing" and "not not perishing" negate are different, they should not both be taken as the cause; it is also a contrary cause, which cannot share a single effect — just as water and fire negate each other, and fruit never grows in between them. If it is said that because of both negations together there is arising, this is not so. If the places of the two negations each exist, the two existents should in turn give rise to two arisings, which in fact is not so. If the places of the two negations are each nonexistent, there is nothing that can give rise — how could it give rise to what is given rise to? If there is nothing that gives rise and nothing given rise to, then what is given rise to is not called "what is given rise to," because what is given rise to arises from nowhere. In this way the practitioner contemplates that, [with not-yet-thought] neither perishing nor not perishing, the arising of the mind of about-to-think ultimately cannot be found.

About-to-Think: Contemplating in Reverse

Chinese Source

復次，行者既能如是約初運未念四句中觀欲念心生不可得已，即當還約欲念心生四句轉觀，觀未念心滅不可得。云何為觀？若謂未念心是滅者，為欲念心生未念心滅？為欲念心不生未念心滅？為欲念心亦生亦不生未念心滅？為欲念心非生非不生未念心滅？如是還反約欲念中四句推求未念心滅畢竟不可得。（推檢之相還轉用上，約未念中四句觀欲念意言句一類，細比作自得具作云云）

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, once the practitioner has been able in this way, within the four propositions of the first moment, not-yet-thought, to contemplate that the arising of the mind of about-to-think cannot be found, he should then turn back and, by the four propositions of the arising of the mind of about-to-think, contemplate in turn that the perishing of the mind of not-yet-thought cannot be found. How does he contemplate? If it is said that the mind of not-yet-thought perishes: does the mind of not-yet-thought perish when the mind of about-to-think arises? Does the mind of not-yet-thought perish when the mind of about-to-think does not arise? Does the mind of not-yet-thought perish when the mind of about-to-think both arises and does not arise? Does the mind of not-yet-thought perish when the mind of about-to-think neither arises nor does not arise? In this way, turning back and by the four propositions within about-to-think, he searches and finds that the perishing of the mind of not-yet-thought ultimately cannot be found. (The marks of searching and examining are applied in turn as above: the meaning and wording are of one kind with contemplating about-to-think by the four propositions within not-yet-thought. Compare them in detail and work them out fully yourself, and so on.)

Thinking and Having-Thought: Names, the Two Extremes, and the Middle Way

Chinese Source

若行者不得未念欲念心生滅，則不得不生滅，亦生滅亦不生滅，非生滅非不生滅。但以凡夫顛倒妄，於未念欲念及一切法中，計有生滅乃至非生滅、非不生滅，虛誑無實，皆不可得，但有名字。名字之法，不在內、外、兩中間，亦不常自有，即是無名字。若不得生滅等四句名字，亦不得無名字。不得名字故非假，不得無名字故非空，不得假故非俗，不得空故非真，不得俗故非世間，不得真故非出世間，不得世間故非有漏，不得出世間故非無漏，不得有漏故非生死，不得無漏故非涅槃。行者如是觀未念欲念時，若不得二邊，則不取二邊；若不取二邊，則不執二邊起諸結業；若無二邊結業障覆，正觀之心猶如虛空湛然清淨。因是中道正慧朗然開發，雙照二諦，心心寂滅，自然流入大涅槃海。若觀未念、欲念如是，餘念、念已及一切心法類亦可知，是則略說正觀相。

Ru-Yi Translation

If the practitioner does not find the arising and perishing of the minds of not-yet-thought and about-to-think, then he does not find not arising and perishing, both arising and perishing and not arising and perishing, or neither arising and perishing nor not arising and perishing. It is only because ordinary beings are inverted and false that, within not-yet-thought, about-to-think, and all dharmas, they reckon there to be arising and perishing, up to neither arising and perishing nor not arising and perishing; this is deceptive and unreal, and none of it can be found; there are only names. The dharma of names is not inside, not outside, not in between, and does not always exist of itself; this is no-name. If one does not find the names of the four propositions such as arising and perishing, one also does not find no-name. Because one does not find names, it is not provisional; because one does not find no-name, it is not empty; because one does not find the provisional, it is not conventional; because one does not find the empty, it is not real; because one does not find the conventional, it is not worldly; because one does not find the real, it is not world-transcending; because one does not find the worldly, it is not with outflows; because one does not find the world-transcending, it is not without outflows; because one does not find what is with outflows, it is not birth and death; because one does not find what is without outflows, it is not nirvāṇa. When the practitioner contemplates not-yet-thought and about-to-think in this way, if he does not find the two extremes, then he does not grasp the two extremes; if he does not grasp the two extremes, then he does not cling to the two extremes and give rise to the karma of the fetters; if there is no obstruction or covering by the karma of the fetters of the two extremes, the mind of right contemplation is like empty space, clear, still, and pure. Through this the right wisdom of the middle way opens and unfolds clearly, illuminating both of the two truths; mind after mind is quiescent and extinguished, and one spontaneously flows into the ocean of great nirvāṇa. If not-yet-thought and about-to-think are contemplated in this way, the remaining thinking and having-thought, and all kinds of mental dharmas, can be known accordingly. This is a brief explanation of the marks of right contemplation.

General Contemplation in Sitting

Chinese Source

復次夫修正觀則有二種：一者總觀、二者歷別觀。第一、所言總觀者，若行人未有大方便力，不能一切處中觀察實相，故當先於坐中照了心意，是則名為總觀心意。第二、所以名為別觀者，若行人方便善巧，能一切處中常得用心，是歷別觀於心意。復次，行者欲入三昧，要先於坐中而觀心意，然後亦當一切處中悉觀心意。所以者何？四威儀中唯獨坐時身心安隱、不沈不浮。不異緣生故，則心審諦，事有觀法。故經云：「端坐念實相，是名第一懺。」是故行者當先於閑房靜室而修三昧。云何為修？行者應當善自調和身心等事。事如禪法中說，此中應廣明。行者既能善自調和，是時當於坐中正念觀察心意識等四運之義悉不可得，觀行破析悉如上說，是時名於坐禪中修行三昧。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the cultivation of right contemplation is of two kinds: first, the general contemplation; second, the contemplation that goes through [things] distinctly. First, the so-called general contemplation: if the practitioner does not yet have the power of great expedients and cannot contemplate and examine the true mark in all places, he should first, while sitting, illuminate and understand mind and attention; this is called the general contemplation of mind and attention. Second, the reason it is called the distinct contemplation: if the practitioner is skillful in expedients and can always apply the mind in all places, this is contemplating mind and attention by going through [things] distinctly. Further, the practitioner who wishes to enter samādhi must first contemplate mind and attention while sitting, and then he should also contemplate mind and attention in all places. Why? Among the four postures, only when sitting are body and mind at ease, neither sinking nor floating. Because no other objects arise, the mind is careful and exact, and in practice there is a method of contemplation. Hence the sūtra says: "Sit upright and be mindful of the true mark: this is called the foremost repentance." Therefore the practitioner should first cultivate samādhi in a secluded room or quiet chamber. How does he cultivate? The practitioner should adjust body, mind, and the like well. These matters are as explained in the dhyāna methods; here they should be explained at length. Once the practitioner has adjusted himself well, he should then, while sitting, with right mindfulness contemplate and examine that the meaning of the fourfold moment of mind, attention, consciousness, and so on cannot be found in any way; the contemplative practice of breaking and analyzing is all as described above. This is called cultivating samādhi in seated meditation.

The Twelve Objects: Six Doings and Six Receivings

Chinese Source

行者如是知心意識不見不得，復當隨有所作一一諦觀內外心。外心名作者，內心名受者，《大集經》中說「作者」「受者」。行人觀於作者凡有六事，觀於受者亦有六種，內外俱觀有十二種，是三昧境能生三昧，行者應當隨所起處而觀察之。外作六者，所謂：一行、二住、三坐、四臥、五作作、六言談。內受六者，所謂：一眼受色、二耳受聲、三鼻受香、四舌受味、五身受觸、六意緣法。是為十二觀境，是三昧門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having in this way known that mind, attention, and consciousness are not seen and not found, the practitioner should next, in accordance with whatever he does, carefully contemplate the inner and outer minds one by one. The outer mind is called the doer; the inner mind is called the receiver; the Great Collection Sūtra speaks of "the doer" and "the receiver." The practitioner contemplates the doer in six matters in all, and the receiver also in six kinds; contemplating both inner and outer, there are twelve kinds. These are the objects of samādhi and can give rise to samādhi; the practitioner should contemplate and examine them wherever they arise. The six outer doings are: first, walking; second, standing; third, sitting; fourth, lying down; fifth, working; sixth, speaking. The six inner receivings are: first, the eye receiving form; second, the ear receiving sound; third, the nose receiving scent; fourth, the tongue receiving taste; fifth, the body receiving touch; sixth, attention taking dharmas. These are the twelve objects of contemplation; they are the gates of samādhi.

Outer Doing 1: Walking

Chinese Source

第一若於行時，即應觀行中未行、欲行、行、行已心相，通達皆不可得，雙照分明如前所說。復作是念：「如是行動由心運役故有去來。」反觀行心不見住處，無有生滅一切相貌，當知行者畢竟空寂。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, when walking, one should contemplate, within walking, the marks of mind of not-yet-walking, about-to-walk, walking, and having-walked, and penetrate that none of them can be found, illuminating both clearly as described before. The Six Inner Receivings

Chinese Source

是為行者觀於外心六種事業，悉知空寂，不見作者有定實相，是故菩薩於一切事中修行三昧。故《般若經》中佛告須菩提：「若菩薩摩訶薩行時知行，乃至坐時知坐、臥時、言語、身服僧伽梨時，悉知已，不可得，故是為菩薩摩訶衍。」復次，行者觀於內心有六種受，知無受者。所以者何？諸受雖空，若不觀察，能作無量煩惱生死因緣，是故行者應當隨是諸根所受塵時一一觀察。云何觀察？

Ru-Yi Translation

This is the practitioner's contemplation of the six kinds of activity of the outer mind: he knows them all to be empty and quiescent and does not see that the doer has a fixed, true mark. Therefore the bodhisattva cultivates samādhi in all activities. Hence in the Prajñā Sūtra the Buddha tells Subhūti: "If a bodhisattva-mahāsattva, when walking, knows he is walking, up to, when sitting, knows he is sitting — when lying down, when speaking, when wearing the saṃghāṭī robe — having known it all, it cannot be found; therefore this is the bodhisattva's Mahāyāna." Further, the practitioner contemplates that the inner mind has six kinds of receiving, and knows that there is no receiver. Why? Though the receivings are empty, if they are not contemplated and examined, they can become immeasurable causes and conditions of affliction and birth and death; therefore the practitioner should contemplate and examine each one whenever these faculties receive sense-objects. How does he contemplate and examine?

Inner Receiving 1: The Eye and Form

Chinese Source

第一行者眼見色時，即應諦觀未見色、欲見色、見色、見色已四運之相皆不可得，雙照分明廣說如上。復作是念：「如是見者即無見相。所以者何？於彼根塵空明之中各各無見、亦無分別，和合因緣出生眼識，眼識因緣出生意識，意識出時即能分別種種諸色；亦依於意識則有眼識，眼識因緣能見於色而生貪著。」是故即當反觀念色之心，如是觀時不見此心從外來入而生領納，亦復不見心從內出而生分別。所以者何？外來於我無事，若自有不待因緣，當知受者畢竟空寂。故淨名菩薩云：「所見色與盲等。」

Ru-Yi Translation

First, when the practitioner's eye sees form, he should carefully contemplate that the marks of the fourfold moment — not-yet-seeing form, about-to-see form, seeing form, and having-seen form — cannot be found, illuminating both clearly, as explained at length above. Again he thinks: "This seeing is without a mark of seeing. Why? In faculty, object, space, and light, each has no seeing and no discrimination; through the coming together of causes and conditions, eye-consciousness arises; through eye-consciousness as cause and condition, mind-consciousness arises; when mind-consciousness arises, it can discriminate all kinds of forms; and depending on mind-consciousness there is eye-consciousness, and through eye-consciousness as cause and condition one can see forms and give rise to craving and attachment." Therefore he should at once turn back to contemplate the mind that thinks of form. When contemplating thus, he does not see this mind come in from outside and receive, nor does he see the mind come out from inside and discriminate. Why? What comes from outside has nothing to do with me; if it existed of itself, it would not depend on causes and conditions. You should know that the receiver is ultimately empty and quiescent. Hence the Bodhisattva Vimalakīrti says: "Regard what you see as no different from what the blind see."

Again he thinks: "This hearing of sound has no self-nature; it arises only from the coming together of faculty and object, and because of the conception and discrimination of mind-consciousness, toward what is heard one gives rise to afflictions and to evil karma." He should at once turn back to contemplate the mind-consciousness that takes sound, and he does not see its essential nature; you should know that the hearer is ultimately empty and quiescent. Hence the Bodhisattva Vimalakīrti says: "Regard what you hear as no different from an echo."

He should at once turn back to contemplate mind-consciousness, and he does not see its root source or its appearance; you should know that the one who receives is ultimately empty and quiescent. Hence the Bodhisattva Vimalakīrti says: "Regard what you smell as no different from the wind."

Inner Receiving 4: The Tongue and Taste

Chinese Source

第四行者舌受味時，即應諦觀未受味、欲受味、受味、受味已四運之相皆不可得，雙照分明廣說如上。復作是念：「如是受味實無自性。所以者何？外六味六味無分別，內舌根本無知故，但從和合因緣而生舌識，此識亦不定在內、外、兩中間故，是中心意強取味相，生著分別，故有一切諸使煩惱。」是時即當反觀著味心意識等，不見住處，況有生滅一切相貌，當知分別味者畢竟清淨。故淨名菩薩言：「所食味不分別。」

Fourth, when the practitioner's tongue receives taste, he should carefully contemplate that the marks of the fourfold moment — not-yet-receiving taste, about-to-receive taste, receiving taste, and having-received taste — cannot be found, illuminating both clearly, as explained at length above. Again he thinks: "This receiving of taste in reality has no self-nature. Why? The six outer tastes — the six tastes have no discrimination, and the inner tongue faculty is originally without knowing; tongue-consciousness arises only from the coming together of causes and conditions, and this consciousness is not fixed inside, outside, or in between. Here mind and attention forcibly grasp the mark of taste and give rise to attached discrimination; hence there are all the envoys and afflictions." At that time he should at once turn back to contemplate the mind, attention, consciousness, and so on that are attached to taste, and he does not see where they abide, much less any arising, perishing, or appearance at all; you should know that the one who discriminates taste is ultimately pure. Hence the Bodhisattva Vimalakīrti says: "Do not discriminate among the tastes of what you eat."

Inner Receiving 5: The Body and Touch

Chinese Source

第五行者身覺觸時，即應諦觀未覺觸、欲覺觸、覺觸、覺觸已四運之相皆不可得，雙照分明廣說如上。復作是念：「如是覺者不從內生，亦不從外來。所以者何？冷暖軟滑等悉非外來故。離冷暖等，無別來法故。身頭等六分非是生法，故，離身六分亦無生法故。二和合身識生時，即名為覺，而此識性不在內外，無所依倚，但以心意強作分別，謂證諸觸，生苦樂想，故有愛恚一切煩惱。」是時即當反觀緣觸心識，不見住處，況有生滅一切相貌，當知能覺觸者畢竟空寂。故淨名菩薩言：「受諸觸如智證。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, when the practitioner's body feels touch, he should carefully contemplate that the marks of the fourfold moment — not-yet-feeling touch, about-to-feel touch, feeling touch, and having-felt touch — cannot be found, illuminating both clearly, as explained at length above. Again he thinks: "This feeling does not arise from within, nor does it come from outside. Why? Because cold, warmth, softness, smoothness, and so on do not come from outside; and apart from cold, warmth, and so on, there is no other dharma that comes. The six parts of the body — head and so on — are not dharmas that give rise, and apart from the six parts of the body there is likewise no dharma that gives rise. When the two come together and body-consciousness arises, this is called feeling; yet the nature of this consciousness is neither inside nor outside and has nothing to rely on. Only because mind and attention forcibly make discriminations, claiming to realize the touches, do notions of suffering and pleasure arise, and hence there are love, anger, and all afflictions." At that time he should at once turn back to contemplate the mind-consciousness that takes touch, and he does not see where it abides, much less any arising, perishing, or appearance at all; you should know that the one who can feel touch is ultimately empty and quiescent. Hence the Bodhisattva Vimalakīrti says: "Receive every sensation the way a sage receives direct realization of the truth."

Inner Receiving 6: Attention and Dharmas

Chinese Source

第六行者意緣法時，即應諦觀未念法、欲念法、念法、念法已四運之心相皆不可得，雙照分明廣說如上。復作是念：「如是意識攀緣諸法悉是虛誑，無有實事。所以者何？法如幻化性無實故，心如陽炎無暫停故。法無定性不可緣故，心無住處誰是能緣？若離能緣、所緣更無別緣。當知但以虛妄憶想強起分別是法，而生諸見，一切煩惱生死業行相續不斷。」是故行者為破虛妄顛倒想及隨緣境時，即當反觀，反觀心意識根源。諦觀心時，不見住止及與生滅一切法相。若心無住處、生滅諸相，當知此心則不可得。尚不得心，況心數法。若無心數，一切諸法竟何所依？是故經言：「我心自空，罪福無主。一切法亦如是，無住無壞。」行者如是觀心意時，不得一切法，當知所攀緣法畢竟空寂。故淨名言：「知諸法如幻相，無自性、無他性，本自不然，今則無滅。」如是之言當何謂也？前破未念、欲念心正觀相應，以十二事中應當一一分別說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, when the practitioner's attention takes dharmas, he should carefully contemplate that the marks of mind of the fourfold moment — not-yet-thinking of dharmas, about-to-think of dharmas, thinking of dharmas, and having-thought of dharmas — cannot be found, illuminating both clearly, as explained at length above. Again he thinks: "This mind-consciousness grasping at dharmas is all deceptive, without any real thing. Why? Because dharmas are like illusions and transformations, their nature unreal; because mind is like a heat shimmer, without a moment's pause. Dharmas have no fixed nature and cannot be taken as objects; mind has no place of abiding — who is it that takes them? Apart from what takes and what is taken, there is no other taking. You should know that it is only through false recollection and conception that one forcibly discriminates these dharmas and gives rise to views, and all afflictions and the karmic activity of birth and death continue without interruption." Therefore, to break false, inverted notions, and whenever following objects, the practitioner should at once turn back to contemplate — turn back to contemplate the root source of mind, attention, and consciousness. When he carefully contemplates the mind, he does not see any abiding or stopping, nor any arising, perishing, or mark of any dharma. If mind has no place of abiding and no marks of arising and perishing, you should know that this mind cannot be found. If even mind is not found, how much less the mental factors? If there are no mental factors, on what, in the end, do all dharmas rely? Hence the sūtra says: "My mind is itself empty; offense and merit have no master. All dharmas are like this, without abiding and without decay." When the practitioner contemplates mind and attention in this way, he does not find any dharma; you should know that the dharmas grasped at are ultimately empty and quiescent. Hence Vimalakīrti says: "Know that every phenomenon is like an illusion, without a nature of its own and without a nature borrowed from anything else — never, in the first place, kindled, and so, now, with nothing that needs to be extinguished." What is meant by these words? The right contemplation that corresponds to breaking the minds of not-yet-thought and about-to-think above should be distinguished and explained one by one in the twelve matters.

Three Minds: Confused, Concentrated, and Contemplating

Chinese Source

行者如是觀察時，亦當應識有三種心：一者觀亂心、二者觀定心、三者觀於觀心。云何名為觀於亂心？如上所說種種事中，行者初學未了諸法，於是境界悉有亂起。一心諦觀不見心相，則無有亂，其心安隱，行住坐臥身心寂泊、澹然不動，即是定心。於是定心若不觀察，多生染著。如淨名菩薩言：「貪著禪味是菩薩縛。」是故，當觀定心不可得。尚無有心，定在何處？當知此定從顛倒生。如是觀時，不見於定及與非定，不生貪著，得脫定縛。故《淨名經》言：「以方便生是菩薩解。」是名觀相觀於定心。觀定心已，行者既未悟於理，或計：「我能觀心，是故不見有定亂相，當知如是妙慧最為殊勝。」著是觀慧即便自高，謂：「他不能解。」如是念時，是名智障，同彼外道。故釋論說：「是諸外道愛著觀空智慧，不得解脫。」行者既知計有觀者是大障礙、不會泥洹，即當反觀能觀之心，不見住處，亦復無起滅。當知畢竟無有觀者及非觀者。既無觀者，誰觀諸法？不得觀心，即離觀想。故《釋論》云：「念想觀已除，戲論心皆滅。無量眾罪除，清淨心常一。如是尊妙人，則能見般若。」是名為觀於心性。故《大集經》亦言：「觀於心心。」

Ru-Yi Translation

When the practitioner contemplates and examines in this way, he should also recognize that there are three kinds of mind: first, contemplating the confused mind; second, contemplating the concentrated mind; third, contemplating the contemplating mind. What is called contemplating the confused mind? In the various matters described above, the practitioner who is a beginner and has not yet understood dharmas has confusion arise in all these objects. Contemplating carefully with a single mind, he does not see the mark of mind, and so there is no confusion; his mind is at ease, and in walking, standing, sitting, and lying down, body and mind are quiet and calm, still and unmoving — this is the concentrated mind. If he does not contemplate and examine this concentrated mind, it often gives rise to defiled attachment. As the Bodhisattva Vimalakīrti says: "Attachment to the flavor of meditative absorption is a bodhisattva's bondage." Therefore he should contemplate that the concentrated mind cannot be found. If there is not even mind, where is concentration? You should know that this concentration arises from inversion. When contemplating thus, he sees neither concentration nor non-concentration, gives rise to no craving or attachment, and gains release from the bondage of concentration. Hence the Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "Being reborn through skillful means is a bodhisattva's liberation." This is called the contemplation that contemplates the concentrated mind. Having contemplated the concentrated mind, the practitioner, not yet enlightened to the principle, may reckon: "I am able to contemplate mind; therefore I do not see the marks of concentration and confusion. You should know that such wondrous wisdom is the most excellent." Attached to this contemplative wisdom, he exalts himself, saying: "Others cannot understand." When he thinks this way, it is called the obstruction of wisdom, the same as those of outside paths. Hence the Great Treatise says: "These followers of outside paths love and are attached to the wisdom that contemplates emptiness, and do not attain liberation." Since the practitioner knows that reckoning there to be a contemplator is a great obstruction and does not accord with nirvāṇa, he should at once turn back to contemplate the mind that contemplates, and he does not see where it abides, nor any arising or perishing. You should know that ultimately there is neither a contemplator nor a non-contemplator. Since there is no contemplator, who contemplates dharmas? Not finding the contemplating mind, he is free of the notion of contemplation. Hence the Great Treatise says: "Thought, conception, and contemplation are removed; / the mind of conceptual proliferation is wholly extinguished; / immeasurable offenses are removed; / the pure mind is always one. / Such a venerable and wondrous person / can see prajñā." This is called contemplating the nature of mind. Hence the Great Collection Sūtra also says: "Contemplate mind upon mind."

The Three Samādhis and the Four Demons

Chinese Source

是三觀者，即三三昧也。所以者何？於初觀中能破一切種種有相，不見內外，即空三昧也；第二觀中能壞空相，是則名為無相三昧；第三觀中不見作者，此即名曰無作三昧。菩薩行是三昧時，則能破壞三倒、三毒心意識相及三有流，亦能降伏四種魔怨。所以者何？夫煩惱者，悉是亂惑。如是觀空，能了煩惱。性無動轉，即是菩提。故《諸法無行經》云：「貪欲即是道，恚癡亦如是，如是三法中，具一切佛法。」若煩惱是菩提，何得復以菩提而惱菩提？若知煩惱相空即是菩提，度煩惱魔，餘三魔亦如是。所以然者，如《思益經》云：「愚於陰界入，而欲求菩提，陰界入即是，離是無菩提。」當知觀空即度陰魔。如《思益經》又云：「生死是涅槃，無退沒生故。」當知觀空即度死魔。《首楞嚴經》云：「魔界如即是佛界如，魔界如佛界如，一如無二。」如是故不出魔界而得佛界。當知觀空即度他化天子魔。菩薩行三空正觀，即時不復恐怖四魔，亦不得四魔，而能度四魔。故《釋論》云：「除諸法實相，其餘一切皆名魔事；若能善修實相，即無魔事。」是故行者善觀此意修行三昧，終無魔事。若離此觀分別憶想，必定墮魔網中。故釋論云：「若分別憶想，即是魔羅網。不動不分別，是則名法印。」

Ru-Yi Translation

These three contemplations are the three samādhis. Why? In the first contemplation one can break all kinds of marks of existence and does not see inside or outside — this is the samādhi of emptiness. In the second contemplation one can destroy the mark of emptiness — this is called the samādhi of marklessness. In the third contemplation one does not see a doer — this is called the samādhi of the unconditioned. When the bodhisattva practices these samādhis, he can break the three inversions, the three poisons, the marks of mind, attention, and consciousness, and the three streams of existence, and can also subdue the four kinds of demon-enemies. Why? Afflictions are all confusion and delusion. Contemplating emptiness in this way, one can understand afflictions; their nature does not move or shift — this is bodhi. Hence the Sūtra on the Practice of Non-Action says: "Craving is itself the path; anger and delusion are exactly the same; within these three dharmas, the whole of the Buddhadharma is complete." If afflictions are bodhi, how could bodhi in turn afflict bodhi? If one knows that the mark of afflictions is empty, this is bodhi; one crosses beyond the demon of afflictions, and the other three demons likewise. Why is this so? As the Viśeṣacintā Sūtra says: "Deluded about the aggregates, realms, and sense-fields, / yet wishing to seek bodhi — / the aggregates, realms, and sense-fields are it; / apart from these there is no bodhi." You should know that contemplating emptiness is crossing beyond the demon of the aggregates. As the Viśeṣacintā Sūtra also says: "Birth and death is nirvāṇa, because there is no retreating, sinking, or birth." You should know that contemplating emptiness is crossing beyond the demon of death. The Śūraṅgama Sūtra says: "The suchness of the demon realm is the suchness of the buddha realm; the suchness of the demon realm and the suchness of the buddha realm are one suchness, not two." For this reason, without leaving the demon realm one attains the buddha realm. You should know that contemplating emptiness is crossing beyond the demon who is a son of the gods of the heaven of [the gods who enjoy] others' transformations. When the bodhisattva practices the right contemplation of the three emptinesses, he at once no longer fears the four demons, nor does he find the four demons, and yet he can cross beyond the four demons. Hence the Great Treatise says: "Apart from the true mark of dharmas, everything else is called the work of demons; if one can cultivate the true mark well, there is no work of demons." Therefore, if the practitioner contemplates this meaning well and cultivates samādhi, there will never be any work of demons. If he departs from this contemplation and discriminates with recollection and conception, he will certainly fall into the net of demons. Hence the Great Treatise says: "If one discriminates with recollection and conception, / that is the net of Māra; / unmoving and not discriminating — / this is called the seal of the Dharma."

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Gate of the Marks of Realization in the Samādhi of Awakening Attention, Section Six

The Outer Ordinary Stage and the First Faith

Chinese Source

行者如是行時，必定當入外凡位中。因是位故，得入內凡初發心住。云何名為外凡位？外凡者，是鐵輪菩薩，具煩惱性，能知如來祕密之藏，亦名外凡十信。其名云何？一名信心、二念心、三曰精進心、四慧心、五定心、六不退心、七迴向心、八護心、九戒心、十願心。行者善修三種觀觀於諸法，若心安住念想心息時，或於入觀、或於住禪中、或出四威儀中，爾時自覺身心豁然空寂如影不實，外視諸法似如浮雲亦如幻化。必當於此生方便慧解，及知諸法不生不滅，生死涅槃無有二際。若聞十二部經，亦自開解。得此慧故，自知身中祕密之藏，一體三寶與佛無異，亦能巧說三乘法要，言語無盡。雖未證真，相似慧力了了無礙。得此證故，名曰信也。但初信心功德如是，況下九心而當可說。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the practitioner practices in this way, he will certainly enter the stage of the outer ordinary. Because of this stage, he can enter the first abode, the abode of arousing the mind, of the inner ordinary. What is called the stage of the outer ordinary? The outer ordinary is the iron-wheel bodhisattva, who possesses the nature of afflictions and can know the secret store of the Tathāgata; it is also called the ten faiths of the outer ordinary. What are their names? First, the mind of faith; second, the mind of mindfulness; third, the mind of vigor; fourth, the mind of wisdom; fifth, the mind of concentration; sixth, the mind of non-retreat; seventh, the mind of dedication; eighth, the mind of protection; ninth, the mind of precepts; tenth, the mind of vows. When the practitioner cultivates well the three kinds of contemplation and contemplates dharmas, if the mind abides at peace and the mind of thought and conception ceases — whether on entering contemplation, while abiding in dhyāna, or on coming out into the four postures — at that time he awakens by himself to the fact that body and mind are suddenly empty and quiescent, unreal like a shadow, and looking outward, dharmas seem like floating clouds, and also like illusions and transformations. He will certainly, in this life, give rise to expedient wisdom and understanding, and know that dharmas are neither born nor perishing, and that birth and death and nirvāṇa have no two boundaries. If he hears the twelve divisions of the scriptures, he opens to understanding by himself. Because he attains this wisdom, he knows by himself the secret store within his own body — the Three Jewels of one essence, no different from the Buddha — and he can also skillfully expound the essentials of the three-vehicle Dharma with inexhaustible words. Though he has not yet realized the real, the power of his resembling wisdom is clear and unobstructed. Because he attains this realization, it is called faith. The merit of just the first mind, of faith, is like this; how much more could be said of the nine minds that follow!

The Other Nine Minds

Chinese Source

信因緣故，知法實相是第一義，萬行之本、眾靈之源。是故，於一切時常念無生，破壞種種邪見妄執，成就正念。安心一相如如之理，無所取捨故，名念心成就。勤行三慧，進趣菩提無有懈怠，名精進心。精進勤行聞思修故，因是獲得正智慧眼，覺一切法，其心轉明，能入實相而無所著，故名慧心。智慧力故，破諸亂惑，安心理性入深三昧，故名定心。禪定因緣，扶同正慧，即得堅固，亦能長養大慈善根，名不退心。心力勇進，能遍了諸法，悉入無生，是時有所作事並趣菩提、莊嚴萬行者普施眾生，名迴向心。妙善開敷、勤心長養，不令諸過得入損於善根，故名護心。既能善遮內非，亦當嚴防外惡，為防惡故修二種戒，謂性重、息世譏嫌，微細不犯，故名戒心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Through the causes and conditions of faith, he knows that the true mark of dharmas is the ultimate meaning, the root of the myriad practices and the source of all spirits. Therefore at all times he always keeps non-arising in thought, breaks all kinds of wrong views and false clinging, and accomplishes right mindfulness. Settling the mind in the principle of the one mark, thus and thus, with nothing grasped or rejected — this is called the accomplishment of the mind of mindfulness. Diligently practicing the three wisdoms and advancing toward bodhi without laziness is called the mind of vigor. Because, with vigor, he diligently practices hearing, reflection, and cultivation, he thereby attains the eye of right wisdom and awakens to all dharmas; his mind becomes ever brighter, and he can enter the true mark without attachment; hence it is called the mind of wisdom. Through the power of wisdom he breaks all confusion and delusion, settles the mind in the principle-nature, and enters deep samādhi; hence it is called the mind of concentration. Through the causes and conditions of dhyāna-concentration, supporting right wisdom together with it, he at once attains firmness and can also nurture the wholesome roots of great kindness; this is called the mind of non-retreat. The power of mind advances bravely and can understand all dharmas everywhere, entering non-arising in all of them; at this time, whatever he does is directed toward bodhi, and the adornment of the myriad practices he gives universally to sentient beings; this is called the mind of dedication. Wondrous goodness opens and unfolds, and he diligently nurtures it, not letting faults enter and damage the wholesome roots; hence it is called the mind of protection. Having been able to restrain inner wrongs well, he should also guard strictly against outer evils; to guard against evil he cultivates two kinds of precepts, namely the precepts grave by nature and the precepts that stop the world's censure, not violating even the subtlest; hence it is called the mind of precepts.

Chinese Source

既能內防諸漏，外以戒自嚴，是時心無覆蓋，習理之慧踰成明顯。既解了無生，觀理之時，實不見眾生可度、煩惱可斷、法門可入、佛道可成，菩薩爾時恐失大悲墮二乘地，即作是念：「諸法空中當無眾生及與佛果，但世俗法中非無眾生乃至佛道。而一切眾生以不知空故，輪轉五道，其為可愍，我當為是虛妄眾生，起大誓願、增菩提心。」作是願言：「願得無生忍時，知眾生空及與不空，乃至菩提佛道亦復如是。」以知空故，發大誓願而成就之。住是地中，能知空故，過凡夫地；知不空故，過聲聞地。若不住空、不住不空，名為中道。行於中道真正願，故名曰願心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having been able to guard against all outflows within and to adorn himself with the precepts without, at this time the mind is without covering, and the wisdom that trains in the principle becomes ever more bright and evident. Having understood non-arising, when contemplating the principle he in reality does not see sentient beings that can be delivered, afflictions that can be cut off, dharma-gates that can be entered, or a buddha-path that can be accomplished. At that time the bodhisattva, fearing to lose great compassion and fall into the ground of the two vehicles, thinks: "Within the emptiness of dharmas there should be no sentient beings and no buddha-fruit; but within worldly conventional dharmas, it is not that there are no sentient beings, up to the buddha-path. Yet all sentient beings, because they do not know emptiness, revolve in the five paths; they are truly to be pitied. For the sake of these false sentient beings I shall make great vows and increase the bodhi-mind." He makes this vow: "When I attain the acceptance of non-arising, may I know the emptiness and the non-emptiness of sentient beings, and likewise of bodhi and the buddha-path." Because he knows emptiness, he makes great vows and accomplishes them. Abiding in this ground, because he can know emptiness, he passes beyond the ground of ordinary beings; because he knows non-emptiness, he passes beyond the ground of the śrāvakas. Not abiding in emptiness and not abiding in non-emptiness is called the middle way. Because he practices the true and right vow of the middle way, it is called the mind of vows.

The Inner Ordinary: The Abode of Arousing the Mind

Chinese Source

菩薩住是十心，名鐵輪位，名曰外凡，是人具煩惱性，能知如來祕密之藏，得相似中道智慧，住自性禪。善修如是十種心故，心得開發、豁然意解，見如來藏、悟一切法、獲無生忍。爾時，始得入發心住。住此位中，即入內凡，名銅輪位，亦名聞慧具足，亦名習種性，亦名伏忍，亦名十願，亦名發趣，亦名道慧，亦名不生生，亦名開佛知見，如是等異名無量。所以最初名發心住者，行人從初發心已來，雖有大慈、大悲、禪定、智慧、無量功德，而未得實相般若，但是發心不名為住，始於此位與理相應故得住名。故《仁王經》云：「入理般若名為住。」又解言：「發心住者，『發』謂開發，『住』名得安止處，是始得開發如來藏理，得無生安止之處。」具此二義，亦名發心住。

Ru-Yi Translation

The bodhisattva who abides in these ten minds is called [one at] the iron-wheel stage, called the outer ordinary. This person possesses the nature of afflictions, can know the secret store of the Tathāgata, attains the resembling wisdom of the middle way, and abides in the dhyāna of self-nature. Because he cultivates these ten kinds of mind well, the mind opens and unfolds, attention suddenly opens in understanding, he sees the tathāgata-store, is enlightened to all dharmas, and obtains the acceptance of non-arising. At that time he first enters the abode of arousing the mind. Abiding in this stage he enters the inner ordinary, called the copper-wheel stage; it is also called the completion of the wisdom of hearing, also called the lineage of habituation, also called subduing forbearance, also called the ten vows, also called setting out, also called path-wisdom, also called the birth of the unborn, also called opening the buddha's knowledge and vision; such different names are immeasurable. The reason it is first called the abode of arousing the mind is this: from the first arousing of the mind onward, though the practitioner has great kindness, great compassion, dhyāna-concentration, wisdom, and immeasurable merits, he has not yet attained the prajñā of the true mark; it is merely arousing the mind and is not called an abode. Only at this stage does he correspond to the principle, and so he obtains the name "abode." Hence the Humane Kings Sūtra says: "Prajñā that enters the principle is called an abode." It is also explained: "In 'the abode of arousing the mind,' 'arousing' means opening and unfolding, and 'abode' means obtaining a place of peaceful rest: here one first opens and unfolds the principle of the tathāgata-store and obtains the place of peaceful rest in non-arising." Because it has these two meanings, it is also called the abode of arousing the mind.

All the Dhyānas and the Difficult Dhyānas

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩住是位中，具一切禪及與難禪。所以者何？一切禪者，有三種：一、樂法樂住禪者，初位能斷一切三界煩惱，永盡無餘，故於諸法無愛著，所有禪定不生愛見，無為自在。二、出生三昧禪者，入初住位，能生無量十力種性諸三昧等。三、利益眾生禪者，入是位中，或面見十方三世諸佛，具大總持、辯才無礙，以利眾生，或得六通，同事度脫。是名初住具於三種一切義禪。得難禪時，亦有三種：一、入是位中捨此身時，雖無生死結業，而能起法性生身，遍現二十五有種種諸身。二、入是位中，必定越過三乘所證一切法門。三、入是位時，於念念中所有功德悉趣菩提。故《瓔珞經》云：「三賢菩薩，自然流入妙覺大海。」是名初發心住中具足三種難禪。菩薩具足自性禪、一切義禪者，是真初住入理賢人，名處在聖胎、得無生忍，亦復悉知上地法門，於一心中具足萬行，無量功德不可窮盡。其餘九住及十行、十金剛、十地、等覺、妙覺是諸佛境界，是菩薩所知，豈是凡識之所能量，是則略說修行覺意三昧最初境界，是中行者當善取其意勤而行之。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the bodhisattva abiding in this stage possesses all the dhyānas and the difficult dhyānas. Why? "All the dhyānas" are of three kinds. First, the dhyāna of abiding in the bliss of the Dharma: at the first stage one can cut off all the afflictions of the three realms, exhausted forever without remainder; therefore one has no love or attachment toward dharmas, no love or views arise in any dhyāna-concentration, and one is unconditioned and free. Second, the dhyāna that gives rise to samādhis: entering the stage of the first abode, one can give rise to immeasurable samādhis of the lineage of the ten powers and so on. Third, the dhyāna that benefits sentient beings: entering this stage, one may see face to face the buddhas of the ten directions and the three times and possess great total retention and unobstructed eloquence, to benefit sentient beings; or one may attain the six superknowledges and liberate beings by sharing their activities. These are called the three kinds of all-meaning dhyāna possessed in the first abode. On attaining the difficult dhyānas, there are also three kinds. First, entering this stage, when giving up this body, though without the karma of the fetters of birth and death, one can give rise to a body born of the dharma-nature and manifest everywhere all kinds of bodies in the twenty-five existences. Second, entering this stage, one certainly passes beyond all the dharma-gates realized by the three vehicles. Third, on entering this stage, in thought after thought all merits are directed toward bodhi. Hence the Yingluo Sūtra says: "The bodhisattvas of the three worthy [stages] spontaneously flow into the great ocean of wondrous awakening." These are called the three kinds of difficult dhyāna fully possessed in the first abode of arousing the mind. The bodhisattva who fully possesses the dhyāna of self-nature and the all-meaning dhyāna is a worthy who has truly entered the principle at the first abode, said to be in the womb of the sages and to have attained the acceptance of non-arising; he also knows fully the dharma-gates of the higher grounds, possesses the myriad practices within a single mind, and his immeasurable merits cannot be exhausted. The remaining nine abodes and the ten practices, the ten vajra [minds], the ten grounds, equal awakening, and wondrous awakening are the realm of the buddhas, what the bodhisattvas know — how could they be measured by ordinary consciousness! This is a brief explanation of the first realm in the practice of the samādhi of awakening attention; here the practitioner should grasp its meaning well and practice it diligently.

Translator's notes

Four propositions (unit 2). 滅／不滅／亦滅亦不滅／非滅非不滅 are rendered "perishes / does not perish / both perishes and does not perish / neither perishes nor does not perish." The site's Mohe Zhiguan (Fascicle Two) runs the same tetralemma more briefly for the wholesome, evil, and neutral.

Indeterminate faculty (unit 2). 不定根人 means a person of indeterminate sex; 根 is kept as "faculty" with "[sexual]" supplied. 石女 "barren woman" (lit. "stone woman"); 黃門 "eunuch."

Interlinear note (unit 2). The note 推檢之相還轉用上… is translated in parentheses at its place. Its last clause 細比作自得具作 is obscure; read as "compare in detail and work it out fully yourself."

"The one who walks" (unit 3). 行者畢竟空寂 matches the series 住者／坐者／眠者／作者／語者, so 行者 is read here as "the one who walks." Alternatively: "the practitioner is ultimately empty and quiescent." 住 here is "standing" (由心制御竪身安立), not "abiding."

"Foremost repentance" (unit 3). 端坐念實相，是名第一懺 also appears, with the same wording, in Calming and Contemplation. Cf. the Sūtra on Contemplating Samantabhadra, whose verse reads 端坐念實相，眾罪如霜露; the first line follows the round-2 Puxian Guan Jing draft ("sit upright and be mindful of the true mark").

Doer and receiver (unit 3). For Great Collection Sūtra (T0397) cf. 無有作者、無有受者. The six outer doings and six inner receivings correspond to the site's Mohe Zhiguan "six actions" and "six kinds of receiving" (the "twelve matters").

Vimalakīrti lines (unit 3). All six are from the "Disciples" chapter (Mahākāśyapa's alms round). They are quoted as in the site's Vimalakīrti Sūtra, as are 貪著禪味是菩薩縛 and 以方便生是菩薩解 (from the chapter on inquiring after the illness). "My mind is itself empty" (unit 3). From the Sūtra on Contemplating Samantabhadra; wording as in the round-2 Puxian Guan Jing draft.

Prajñā Sūtra quotation. Cf. Larger Prajñāpāramitā (T0223), the four foundations of mindfulness: 菩薩摩訶薩行時知行、住時知住、坐時知坐、臥時知臥. The text abbreviates and adds the saṃghāṭī; its punctuation (悉知已，不可得，故…) is translated as it stands.
