What in You Never Grows Old?

Fascicle 2 turns from refuting where the mind is to directly pointing at the unmoving, unborn nature of seeing itself — through King Prasenajit's aging body, the "eight things returned to their origins," and the teaching that the five aggregates are, each one, the tathāgatagarbha.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: fascicle 2 complete Source: Taishō T19, no. 945 Covers: the entire fascicle — King Prasenajit's dialogue through the five aggregates

About This Fascicle

Fascicle 1 ended with Ānanda unable to say what actually does the seeing, once the eye and the lamp have both been ruled out. Fascicle 2 answers that question — not by argument alone, but by turning the whole assembly's attention toward something none of them had thought to doubt: the nature of seeing itself, which, unlike the body, never ages, and, unlike any object, cannot itself be turned into an object of sight.

What Never Grows Old 波斯匿王請示不生滅性

Chinese Source

爾時，阿難及諸大眾聞佛示誨，身心泰然。念無始來失却本心，妄認緣塵分別影事，今日開悟，如失乳兒忽遇慈母。合掌禮佛，願聞如來顯出身心真妄虛實，現前生滅與不生滅二發明性。

波斯匿王起立白佛：「我昔未承諸佛誨勅，見迦旃延、毘羅胝子，咸言：『此身死後斷滅，名為涅槃。』我雖值佛，今猶狐疑。云何發揮，證知此心不生滅地？今此大眾諸有漏者，咸皆願聞。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Ānanda and the whole assembly, hearing the Buddha's instruction, felt their bodies and minds grow calm and at ease. Reflecting that, from beginningless time, they had lost their own original mind and mistakenly clung to the shadow-play of conditioned, discriminating thought, they now felt awakened — like an infant who has lost its milk and suddenly meets its loving mother again. Joining their palms, they bowed to the Buddha, wishing to hear the Tathāgata reveal, of body and mind alike, what is true and what is false, what is empty and what is real, and to make plain the nature of what before us arises and ceases and what does not.

King Prasenajit rose and said to the Buddha: "In the past, before I received the guidance of the buddhas, I heard Kātyāyana and Vairaṭīputra both claim that after this body dies it is utterly annihilated, and that this is what is called nirvāṇa. Though I have now met the Buddha, I still harbor this doubt. How can it be shown and proven that this mind reaches a state beyond birth and death? All those with outflows still remaining in this assembly wish equally to hear the answer."

佛告大王：「汝身現存，今復問汝：汝此肉身為同金剛常住不朽？為復變壞？」「世尊！我今此身終從變滅。」佛言：「大王！汝未曾滅，云何知滅？」「世尊！我此無常變壞之身雖未曾滅，我觀現前念念遷謝，新新不住；如火成灰，漸漸銷殞，殞亡不息，決知此身當從滅盡。」

佛言：「如是，大王！汝今生齡已從衰老，顏貌何如童子之時？」「世尊！我昔孩孺，膚腠潤澤，年至長成血氣充滿，而今頹齡迫於衰耄，形色枯悴，精神昏昧，髮白面皺，逮將不久，如何見比充盛之時！」

The Buddha said to the great king: "Your body still exists here before me — let me ask you: is this fleshly body of yours as everlasting and indestructible as vajra, or does it change and decay?" "World-Honored One, this body of mine will, in the end, change and perish." The Buddha said: "Great king, it has not yet perished — how do you know it will perish?" "World-Honored One, although this impermanent, decaying body of mine has not yet perished, I observe that, moment after moment, it is passing away, never for an instant staying the same — like a fire that gradually reduces itself to ash, extinguishing itself without rest. I know for certain that this body must, in the end, come to a complete end."

The Buddha said: "Just so, great king. Your years now bring you toward old age — how does your present countenance compare to when you were a child?" "World-Honored One, in my infancy my skin was moist and supple; when I reached my prime, my blood and vitality were full; but now, in this declining age, drawing near to senility, my form and complexion are withered, my spirit dull, my hair white, my face lined, and I have not much time left — how could this compare to the fullness of my prime!"

佛言：「大王！汝之形容應不頓朽？」王言：「世尊！變化密移，我誠不覺，寒暑遷流，漸至於此。何以故？我年二十雖號年少，顏貌已老初十年時；三十之年又衰二十；于今六十又過于二，觀五十時宛然強壯。世尊！我見密移，雖此殂落其間流易且限十年。若復令我微細思惟：『其變寧唯一紀二紀，實為年變；豈唯年變，亦兼月化；何直月化，兼又日遷。』沈思諦觀，剎那剎那、念念之間不得停住，故知我身終從變滅。」

佛言：「大王！汝見變化遷改不停，悟知汝滅；亦於滅時，知汝身中有不滅耶？」波斯匿王合掌白佛：「我實不知！」

The Buddha said: "Great king, surely your appearance did not decay all at once?" The king said: "World-Honored One, the change has crept along in secret; I truly never noticed it happening — the cold and the heat passing, little by little, until it came to this. Why do I say so? At twenty, though called young, my face was already older than at ten; at thirty it had declined further from twenty; and now, past sixty-two, looking back on fifty I seem to have still been vigorous then. World-Honored One, I see this secret change proceeding, marked off, roughly, in units of ten years. But if I reflect on it more minutely still: the change is not confined to a decade or two at a time — it is truly a change from year to year; and not only from year to year, but month to month; and not merely month to month, but day by day as well. Reflecting deeply and observing closely, moment after moment, instant after instant, it never once stays still — and so I know for certain that this body of mine must, in the end, undergo change and perish."

The Buddha said: "Great king, you see this unceasing change and transformation and thereby recognize that you will perish — but at the very time of that perishing, do you know whether there is, within your body, something that does not perish?" King Prasenajit, joining his palms, said to the Buddha: "I truly do not know."

佛言：「我今示汝不生滅性。大王！汝年幾時見恒河水？」王言：「我生三歲，慈母携我謁耆婆天，經過此流，爾時即知是恒河水。」佛言：「大王！如汝所說，二十之時衰於十歲，乃至六十，日月歲時念念遷變；則汝三歲見此河時，至年十三其水云何？」王言：「如三歲時宛然無異；乃至于今年六十二，亦無有異。」

佛言：「汝今自傷髮白面皺，其面必定皺於童年；則汝今時觀此恒河，與昔童時觀河之見，有童耄不？」王言：「不也，世尊！」

佛言：「大王！汝面雖皺，而此見精性未曾皺！皺者為變，不皺非變；變者受滅，彼不變者元無生滅，云何於中受汝生死？而猶引彼末伽梨等，都言此身死後全滅！」王聞是言，信知身後捨生趣生，與諸大眾踊躍歡喜，得未曾有。

The Buddha said: "I will now show you the nature that neither arises nor ceases. Great king, at what age did you first see the water of the Ganges?" The king said: "When I was three years old, my loving mother took me to pay homage at the shrine of the god Jīva, and on the way we passed this river — that was when I first knew it as the Ganges." The Buddha said: "Great king, as you have said, your body declined from twenty to a mere ten years' vigor, and on to sixty, changing moment by moment with the days, months, and years — so when you saw this river at age three, and again at age thirteen, how was the water then?" The king said: "It was exactly the same as when I was three — and even now, at sixty-two, it is no different at all."

The Buddha said: "You now grieve over your white hair and wrinkled face — your face is certainly more wrinkled now than in childhood. But when you look at this Ganges now, compared to how you looked at it as a child, is the seeing itself young in one case and old in the other?" The king said: "No, World-Honored One."

The Buddha said: "Great king, though your face is wrinkled, this essence of seeing has never once been wrinkled! What wrinkles is subject to change; what does not wrinkle is not subject to change. What changes is subject to perishing; what does not change is, from the very beginning, beyond birth and death — how could it undergo your birth and death? And yet you still cite the likes of Maskarin Gośālīputra, who claim that after this body dies everything is utterly annihilated!" Hearing this, the king came to believe and know that after this body is given up, one goes on to another rebirth, and he, together with the whole assembly, leapt for joy, having gained what they had never had before.

Reading note: The Buddha does not argue King Prasenajit out of his fear of annihilation with logic alone — he gets the king to notice something directly, in his own experience. The face in the mirror keeps changing; the river seen at three and the river seen at sixty-two look exactly the same, seen by exactly the same seeing. This "essence of seeing" (見精) that never wrinkles is fascicle 2's first concrete demonstration of something in Ānanda's own experience that is not subject to birth and death — and it is offered before any argument, as something the king can simply check for himself.

Upside-Down, or Right-Side Up? 垂手喻倒

Chinese Source

阿難即從座起，禮佛合掌，長跪白佛：「世尊！若此見聞必不生滅，云何世尊名我等輩遺失真性，顛倒行事？願興慈悲洗我塵垢！」

即時如來垂金色臂，輪手下指，示阿難言：「汝今見我母陀羅手為正為倒？」阿難言：「世間眾生以此為倒，而我不知誰正誰倒。」佛告阿難：「若世間人以此為倒，即世間人將何為正？」阿難言：「如來竪臂，兜羅綿手上指於空，則名為正。」

佛即竪臂，告阿難言：「若此顛倒首尾相換，諸世間人一倍瞻視，則知汝身與諸如來清淨法身比類發明；如來之身名正遍知，汝等之身號性顛倒。隨汝諦觀汝身、佛身，稱顛倒者，名字何處號為顛倒？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Ānanda then rose from his seat, bowed to the Buddha with palms joined, knelt at length, and said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, if this seeing and hearing are truly beyond birth and death, why does the World-Honored One say that we have lost our true nature and act in an upside-down way? I beg you, in your compassion, to wash away the dust that clouds us!"

At once the Tathāgata lowered his golden-hued arm, turned his hand palm-downward, and, pointing it toward the ground, said to Ānanda: "Do you see this hand of mine, marked with the wheel-sign, as right-side up or upside down?" Ānanda said: "Ordinary beings in the world would call this upside down; but I myself do not know which way is right and which is upside down." The Buddha said to Ānanda: "If people in the world call this upside down, what, then, would they call right-side up?" Ānanda said: "If the Tathāgata raised his arm and pointed his cotton-soft hand upward toward the sky, that would be called right-side up."

The Buddha then raised his arm and said to Ānanda: "If this is all it takes — head and tail simply switching places — for ordinary people in the world to see it doubly, one way and then the other, then you should understand, comparing your own body to the Tathāgatas' pure Dharma-body in just this way: the Tathāgata's body is called that of right and universal knowing, while your bodies are called, by their very nature, upside down. Look closely, then, at your own body and at the Buddha's body, and consider exactly where, in what you call 'upside down,' the name 'upside down' is actually to be found."

Reading note: The joke buried in this exchange is the point: a hand pointing down and a hand pointing up are, physically, the very same hand — "upside down" and "right-side up" are just two ways of describing an orientation, not two different kinds of hand. The Buddha uses this to needle at Ānanda's assumption that "true nature" and "upside-down, deluded existence" must be two different things happening to two different beings, rather than two ways of looking at the very same nature.

The Finger and the Moon, the Guest and the Host 法喻顯心：標指喻月 · 客塵喻

于時阿難與諸大眾，瞪矒瞻佛目精不瞬，不知身心顛倒所在。

佛興慈悲，哀愍阿難及諸大眾，發海潮音遍告同會：「諸善男子！我常說言：『色心諸緣及心所使、諸所緣法，唯心所現。』汝身、汝心，皆是妙明真精妙心中所現物。云何汝等遺失本妙圓妙明心寶明妙性、認悟中迷？晦昧為空；空晦暗中結暗為色；色雜妄想，想相為身；聚緣內搖，趣外奔逸，昏擾擾相以為心性。一迷為心，決定惑為色身之內，不知色身外洎山河虛空大地，咸是妙明真心中物。譬如澄清百千大海，棄之、唯認一浮漚體目為全潮，窮盡瀛渤。汝等即是迷中倍人！如我垂手等無差別，如來說為可憐愍者。」

阿難承佛悲救深誨，垂泣叉手而白佛言：「我雖承佛如是妙音，悟妙明心元所圓滿常住心地，而我悟佛現說法音，現以緣心，允所瞻仰；徒獲此心，未敢認為本元心地。願佛哀愍宣示圓音，拔我疑根歸無上道！」

佛告阿難：「汝等尚以緣心聽法，此法亦緣，非得法性。如人以手指月示人，彼人因指當應看月，若復觀指以為月體，此人豈唯亡失月輪，亦亡其指。何以故？以所標指為明月故。豈唯亡指，亦復不識明之與暗。何以故？即以指體為月明性，明暗二性無所了故。

汝亦如是，若以分別我說法音為汝心者，此心自應離分別音、有分別性。譬如有客寄宿旅亭，暫止便去，終不常住；而掌亭人都無所去，名為亭主。此亦如是，若真汝心，則無所去，云何離聲無分別性？斯則豈唯聲分別心，分別我容，離諸色相無分別性；如是乃至分別都無，非色非空，拘舍離等昧為冥諦，離諸法緣無分別性，則汝心性各有所還，云何為主？」

阿難言：「若我心性各有所還，則如來說妙明元心，云何無還？唯垂哀愍為我宣說！」

佛告阿難：「且汝見我見精明元，此見雖非妙精明心，如第二月；非是月影。汝應諦聽！今當示汝無所還地。

At this Ānanda and the whole assembly stared at the Buddha, their eyes unblinking, not knowing where this inversion of body and mind was to be found.

The Buddha, moved to kindness, pitied Ānanda and the whole assembly, and in a voice like the ocean's tide declared to all who were gathered: "Good men! I have always said that the conditions of form and mind, and what mind sets in motion, and all phenomena taken as objects, are nothing but manifestations of mind. Your body and your mind are alike things appearing within the wondrous, luminous, truly refined and wondrous mind. How is it that you have lost your originally wondrous, perfectly luminous mind, that jewel-bright wondrous nature, and taken delusion for awakening? Obscurity becomes space; and in that dark obscurity darkness congeals into form; form mixed with false thinking, and the aspect of that thinking, becomes the body; conditions gather and stir within and rush outward, and this murky agitation is taken for the nature of mind. Once this is mistaken for mind, one is settled in the delusion that it lies inside the physical body, not knowing that outside that body the mountains and rivers, empty space and the great earth, are all of them things within the wondrous luminous true mind. It is as though one threw away a hundred thousand clear great seas and recognized only a single floating bubble, calling it the whole tide and the sum of all the oceans. You are the doubly deluded among the deluded — no different from my hand hanging down; and of such a one the Tathāgata says that he is to be pitied."

Ānanda, receiving the Buddha's compassionate rescue and deep instruction, wept, folded his hands, and said to the Buddha: "Though I have received such wondrous words from the Buddha and have awakened to the wondrous luminous mind as the ground of mind, originally complete and ever abiding, still, in awakening to the Buddha's present words of Dharma, I am using a conditioned mind even in the reverence I offer. Having merely gained this much of mind, I dare not yet acknowledge it as the original ground of mind. I beg the Buddha to pity me, to declare the perfect sound, to pull out the root of my doubt and return me to the unsurpassed path!"

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "You are still listening to the Dharma with a conditioned mind, and so this Dharma too becomes a condition, and you do not gain the nature of Dharma. It is as when a man points at the moon to show it to another: that other ought to look at the moon by means of the finger; but if instead he looks at the finger and takes it for the moon, that man loses not only the moon but the finger as well. Why? Because he has taken the pointing finger for the bright moon. And he loses not only the finger but the very difference between light and darkness. Why? Because he has taken the substance of the finger for the moon's bright nature, and so has understood neither of the two natures, light and dark.

"It is just so with you. If you take the discriminating of the sound of my Dharma to be your mind, then that mind ought to have its discriminating nature apart from the sound it discriminates. It is like a traveling guest who lodges at an inn: he stops a while and goes, and never abides there; whereas the keeper of the inn has nowhere to go, and is therefore called the host. So it is here: if this were truly your mind, it would have nowhere to go — how is it, then, that apart from sound it has no discriminating nature? And this holds not only for the mind that discriminates sound: the mind that discriminates my face has no discriminating nature apart from the appearances of form; and so on until discriminating is altogether absent, and being neither form nor emptiness it is what Gośālīputra and his like obscurely called the dark principle — having no discriminating nature apart from phenomena as its conditions. Then the nature of your mind has, in each case, somewhere to return to; and how would it be the host?"

Ānanda said: "If the nature of my mind has, in each case, somewhere to return to, then what of the wondrous luminous original mind the Tathāgata speaks of — how has it nowhere to return to? I beg you to pity me and declare it!"

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "Consider your seeing of me, the luminous source of the essence of seeing: this seeing, though it is not the wondrous refined luminous mind, is like a second moon — it is not the moon's reflection. Listen closely now: I shall show you the ground that has nowhere to return to.

Reading note: Two images do a great deal of work here. "The finger and the moon" warns against mistaking the teaching itself — even the Buddha's own spoken words — for the reality it only points toward. "The guest and the host" introduces the criterion the rest of the fascicle will use to sort the false from the true: whatever comes and goes, arising with a condition and departing when that condition ends, is a "guest," not the "host." The discriminating mind that only ever arises in response to what it hears is shown to be exactly this kind of guest.

The Eight Things That Return, and the One That Does Not 八還辨見

阿難！此大講堂，洞開東方，日輪昇天則有明耀；中夜黑月，雲霧晦暝，則復昏暗；戶牖之隙則復見通；牆宇之間則復觀擁；分別之處則復見緣；頑虛之中遍是空性；欝[土*孛]之象則紆昏塵；澄霽斂氛又觀清淨。

阿難！汝咸看此諸變化相，吾今各還本所因處。云何本因？阿難！此諸變化，明還日輪；何以故？無日不明，明因屬日，是故還日。暗還黑月；通還戶牖；擁還牆宇；緣還分別；頑虛還空；欝[土*孛]還塵；清明還霽。則諸世間一切所有，不出斯類。

汝見八種見精明性，當欲誰還！何以故？若還於明，則不明時無復見暗。雖明暗等種種差別，見無差別，諸可還者自然非汝；不汝還者非汝而誰？則知汝心本妙明淨，汝自迷悶喪本受輪，於生死中常被漂溺，是故如來名可憐愍。」

"Ānanda, this great lecture hall opens wide to the east: when the sun's disc climbs the sky there is brightness; at midnight, with a dark moon and clouds and mist, there is darkness again; through the gaps of doors and windows one sees passage; between the walls one observes obstruction; where there is discriminating, one sees conditions; in blank vacancy there is nothing but the nature of space; in a dust-storm the aspect is murky with dust; and when the air clears after rain one observes purity again.

"Ānanda, look at all these changing aspects. I shall now return each to the place it came from. What are those origins? Ānanda, of these changes: brightness returns to the sun's disc — why? Because without the sun there is no brightness, and brightness has its cause in the sun, and so returns to the sun. Darkness returns to the dark moon; passage returns to doors and windows; obstruction returns to walls; conditions return to discriminating; blank vacancy returns to space; murkiness returns to dust; and clarity returns to the clearing sky. Everything there is in the world falls under one or another of these kinds.

"But this luminous nature of the essence of seeing, by which you see all eight — to what would you return it? Why? If it returned to brightness, then when there was no brightness you would no longer see darkness. Though brightness and darkness and the rest differ in all these ways, seeing does not differ. What can be returned is naturally not you; and what cannot be returned to you — if it is not you, what is it? So you may know that your mind is originally wondrous, luminous and pure, and that it is you yourself who, confused and blind, have lost your own root and undergone the wheel, forever adrift and drowning in birth and death. This is why the Tathāgata calls you one to be pitied."

Reading note: This is one of the sūtra's most quoted teaching devices. Every quality that shows up in seeing has an external cause it can be traced back to and "returned" to — brightness traces back to the sun, darkness to the absence of the moon's light, and so on. Once everything returnable has been returned, what is left over — the seeing itself, which is present whether the scene is bright or dark, open or blocked — has nowhere left to be sent back to. Yuanying's commentary calls this "the one thing that cannot be returned, because it is you."

Is Seeing a Thing? 辨見非物

阿難言：「我雖識此見性無還，云何得知是我真性？」

佛告阿難：「吾今問汝：今汝未得無漏清淨，承佛神力見於初禪，得無障礙；而阿那律見閻浮提，如觀掌中菴摩羅菓；諸菩薩等見百千界；十方如來窮盡微塵清淨國土無所不矚；眾生洞視不過分寸。阿難！且吾與汝觀四天王所住宮殿，中間遍覽水陸空行，雖有昏明種種形像，無非前塵分別留礙。汝應於此分別自他，今吾將汝擇於見中，誰是我體？誰為物象？

阿難！極汝見源，從日月宮是物非汝，至七金山周遍諦觀，雖種種光亦物非汝；漸漸更觀雲騰、鳥飛、風動、塵起、樹木、山川、草芥、人畜，咸物非汝。

阿難！是諸近遠諸有物性，雖復差殊，同汝見精清淨所矚。則諸物類自有差別，見性無殊。此精妙明，誠汝見性。若見是物，則汝亦可見吾之見。若同見者名為見吾，吾不見時，何不見吾不見之處？若見不見，自然非彼不見之相。若不見吾不見之地，自然非物，云何非汝？又則汝今見物之時，汝既見物，物亦見汝；體性紛雜，則汝與我并諸世間不成安立。阿難！若汝見時，是汝非我；見性周遍，非汝而誰？云何自疑汝之真性，性汝不真，取我求實！」

Ānanda said: "Though I now recognize that this nature of seeing has nowhere to return to, how am I to know that it is my own true nature?"

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "I now ask you this. You have not yet attained the purity that is free of outflows, yet by the Buddha's spiritual power you see as far as the first dhyāna without obstruction; Anuruddha sees this continent of Jambudvīpa as one looks at an āmalaka fruit in the palm; the bodhisattvas see a hundred thousand worlds; the Tathāgatas of the ten directions see through to the pure lands as many as dust-motes, and there is nothing they do not behold; while living beings see clearly no further than an inch or two. Ānanda, you and I are looking at the palaces where the four heavenly kings dwell, and taking in everything between that moves through water, over land, and through the air; and though there are all sorts of shapes and images, dim and bright, there is nothing there but objects in front of us, discriminated and held fast. Among all this you should distinguish self from other. I shall now have you pick out, within seeing, what is my own substance and what is a thing.

"Ānanda, press your seeing to its limit: from the palaces of sun and moon, these are things and not you; on to the seven golden mountains — examine them all closely, and though they have every kind of radiance, they too are things and not you; and look further, at the rising clouds, the flying birds, the stirring wind, the raised dust, the trees, the mountains and streams, the grasses, human beings and animals — all of them things, and none of them you.

"Ānanda, all these natures of things, near and far, however different they may be, are alike beheld by the pure essence of your seeing. The kinds of things have their own differences, but the nature of seeing has no difference at all: this refined and wondrous luminosity is truly your nature of seeing. If seeing were a thing, then you too could see my seeing. And if seeing the same thing were to be called seeing my seeing, then when I do not see, why do you not see the place where I do not see? And if you did see my not-seeing, that would naturally not be the aspect of what is not seen. And if you cannot see the ground of my not-seeing, then it is naturally not a thing — how, then, is it not you? Again, when you see a thing, since you see the thing, the thing also sees you; substance and nature are thrown into confusion, and then you and I and the whole world could not be established at all. Ānanda, when you see, it is you and not I; and seeing-nature being everywhere — if it is not you, what is it? Why do you doubt your own true nature, and, the nature that is yours not being taken as true, come to me to seek what is real?"

Reading note: The argument turns on a simple test: things can be seen; seeing itself cannot be. If seeing were just one more object among others, it would have to be visible the way objects are — and then Ānanda ought to be able to see the Buddha's seeing, or see the "gap" where the Buddha isn't seeing anything, both of which are absurd. Whatever necessarily escapes being turned into an object in this way is exactly what is doing the seeing — and that, the Buddha insists, simply is Ānanda, not some third thing to go looking for.

Does Seeing Shrink to Fit a Room? 辨見無舒縮

阿難白佛言：「世尊！若此見性必我非餘，我與如來觀四天王勝藏寶殿，居日月宮，此見周圓遍娑婆國；退歸精舍只見伽藍，清心戶堂但瞻簷廡。世尊！此見如是，其體本來周遍一界，今在室中唯滿一室，為復此見縮大為小？為當牆宇夾令斷絕？我今不知斯義所在，願垂弘慈為我敷演！」

佛告阿難：「一切世間大小、內外諸所事業，各屬前塵，不應說言見有舒縮。譬如方器中見方空，吾復問汝，此方器中所見方空，為復定方？為不定方？若定方者，別安圓器空應不圓；若不定者，在方器中應無方空。汝言不知斯義所在，義性如是，云何為在？阿難！若復欲令入無方圓，但除器方。空體無方，不應說言更除虛空方相所在。若如汝問『入室之時縮見令小』，仰觀日時，汝豈挽見齊於日面？若築牆宇能夾見斷，穿為小竇，寧無續迹？是義不然。一切眾生從無始來迷己為物，失於本心，為物所轉；故於是中觀大、觀小。若能轉物，則同如來身心圓明，不動道場，於一毛端遍能含受十方國土。」

Ānanda said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, if this nature of seeing must be mine and no one else's, then when the Tathāgata and I looked at the treasure palaces of the four heavenly kings and dwelt in the palaces of sun and moon, this seeing was full and round and reached throughout the Sahā world; and when we withdrew to the monastery I saw only the monastery grounds, and in the quiet of the hall I looked at nothing but the eaves. World-Honored One, this seeing is thus: its substance originally reached throughout a whole realm, and now, in a room, it fills only the room. Has this seeing been shrunk from great to small? Or have the walls squeezed it and cut it off? I do not know where the sense of this lies; I beg you in your great kindness to expound it for me!"

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "All the affairs of the world, great and small, inner and outer, belong to the objects in front of you; it will not do to say that seeing spreads and shrinks. It is like seeing square space within a square vessel. Let me ask you: is the square space seen within this square vessel fixed as square, or not fixed as square? If it is fixed as square, then when a round vessel is set there instead, the space in it should not be round; and if it is not fixed, then within the square vessel there should be no square space at all. You say you do not know where the sense of this lies — the nature of the sense is just this, so what is there to locate? Ānanda, if you wish to have it enter a state with neither square nor round, simply remove the vessel's squareness. The substance of space has no squareness, and it will not do to speak of further removing some square aspect belonging to space. And as for your question — 'on entering a room, seeing is shrunk small' — then when you look up at the sun, are you drawing your seeing out to be level with the sun's face? And if building walls could squeeze seeing and cut it off, then when a small hole is bored through, would there be no mark of its being joined again? The sense of it is not so. All living beings, from beginningless time, have mistaken themselves for things, lost their own original mind, and been turned about by things; and so, within this, they see great and see small. But if one can turn things about instead, then one is the same as the Tathāgata: body and mind perfect and luminous, an unmoving place of awakening in which the tip of a single hair can contain and receive the lands of the ten directions."

Reading note: The square-container analogy makes the point cleanly: the shape of space you see inside a square box is a fact about the box, not about space itself — space was never actually square, and removing the box doesn't require space to "expand" back to its true shape. In the same way, apparently seeing "more" outdoors and "less" indoors is a fact about the walls, not about seeing contracting and expanding. The closing line — "turn things, rather than be turned by them" — becomes a recurring standard the sūtra uses for what liberation actually looks like in practice.

Pointing to What, Exactly, Is Seeing 責令指陳非見

阿難白佛言：「世尊！若此見精必我妙性，今此妙性現在我前，見必我真，我今身心復是何物？而今身心分別有實，彼見無別分辨我身。若實我心，令我今見，見性實我而身非我，何殊如來先所難言『物能見我』？唯垂大慈，開發未悟！」

佛告阿難：「今汝所言『見在汝前』，是義非實。若實汝前，汝實見者，則此見精既有方所，非無指示。且今與汝坐祇陀林，遍觀林渠及與殿堂，上至日月，前對恒河。汝今於我師子座前，舉手指陳是種種相——陰者是林，明者是日，礙者是壁，通者是空，如是乃至草樹纖毫——大小雖殊，但可有形無不指著。若必有見現在汝前，汝應以手礭實指陳何者是見？阿難當知，若空是見，既已成見，何者是空？若物是見，既已是見，何者為物？汝可微細披剝萬象，析出精明淨妙見元，指陳示我，同彼諸物分明無惑。」

阿難言：「我今於此重閣講堂，遠洎恒河、上觀日月，舉手所指、縱目所觀，指皆是物，無是見者。世尊！如佛所說，況我有漏初學聲聞，乃至菩薩亦不能於萬物象前剖出精見離一切物別有自性！」佛言：「如是，如是！」

佛復告阿難：「如汝所言『無有精見離一切物別有自性』，則汝所指是物之中無是見者。今復告汝，汝與如來坐祇陀林，更觀林苑乃至日月種種象殊，必無見精受汝所指；汝又發明此諸物中何者非見？」

阿難言：「我實遍見此祇陀林，不知是中何者非見。何以故？若樹非見，云何見樹？若樹即見，復云何樹？如是乃至若空非見，云何為空？若空即見，復云何空？我又思惟：『是萬象中微細發明無非見者！』」佛言：「如是，如是。」

於是大眾非無學者，聞佛此言，茫然不知是義終始，一時惶悚，失其所守。如來知其魂慮變慴，心生憐愍，安慰阿難及諸大眾：「諸善男子！無上法王是真實語！如所如說不誑不妄，非末伽梨四種不死矯亂論議。汝諦思惟，無忝哀慕。」

Ānanda said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, if this essence of seeing must be my own wondrous nature, and this wondrous nature is now present in front of me, and the seeing is truly my own — then what are this body and mind of mine? Yet body and mind do have their real discriminating, while that seeing has no discriminating by which it makes out my body. And if it is really my mind, and makes me see as I now see, so that the nature of seeing is really me while the body is not me — how does that differ from what the Tathāgata challenged earlier, that 'a thing can see me'? I beg your great kindness: open this up for the unawakened!"

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "What you now say — 'seeing is in front of you' — is not really so. If it were really in front of you and you really saw it, then this essence of seeing, having a location, could not fail to be indicated. Now you and I are sitting in the Jeta Grove, looking all about at the groves and channels and the halls, up as far as the sun and moon, and facing the Ganges in front. Raise your hand here before my lion-seat and point out all these various aspects: what is shaded is the grove, what is bright is the sun, what obstructs is a wall, what lets through is space; and so on down to grasses and trees and the finest hair — great and small they differ, but whatever has a shape can be pointed at. If there must be a seeing present in front of you, then point out for me with your hand, firmly and definitely, which of them is the seeing. Ānanda, know this: if space is the seeing, then space having already become seeing, what is space? And if a thing is the seeing, then it having already become seeing, what is the thing? Peel the ten thousand appearances apart as finely as you can, and separate out the refined, luminous, pure and wondrous source of seeing; point it out and show it to me, as plain and free of confusion as those other things are."

Ānanda said: "Here in this two-storied lecture hall, out as far as the Ganges and up to the sun and moon, whatever I raise my hand to point at, and whatever my eye roves over, everything I point at is a thing, and none of it is the seeing. World-Honored One, as the Buddha has said — how much less could I, a beginning hearer with outflows, or even the bodhisattvas, cut the refined seeing out from in front of the ten thousand appearances, so that apart from all things it had a nature of its own!" The Buddha said: "So it is, so it is!"

The Buddha said further to Ānanda: "As you have said, 'there is no refined seeing which, apart from all things, has a nature of its own.' Then among the things you pointed at, none of them is the seeing. Now I tell you this as well: you and the Tathāgata are sitting in the Jeta Grove; look again at the groves and gardens, and on to the sun and moon and all their differing appearances. There will certainly be no essence of seeing there for you to point at — so now make this evident to me: among all these things, which is not the seeing?"

Ānanda said: "I really do see this whole Jeta Grove, and I do not know which thing in it is not the seeing. Why? If the tree is not the seeing, how is the tree seen? And if the tree is the seeing, then what is the tree? And so on: if space is not the seeing, how is space seen? And if space is the seeing, then what is space? I have considered it further: finely made evident among these ten thousand appearances, there is nothing that is not the seeing!" The Buddha said: "So it is, so it is."

At this those in the assembly who were not yet beyond study, hearing the Buddha say this, were blank, not knowing the beginning or end of his meaning, and all at once were alarmed and lost their footing. The Tathāgata, knowing that their spirits were shaken and afraid, felt pity arise, and comforted Ānanda and the whole assembly: "Good men! The unsurpassed King of Dharma speaks what is real. He says things as they are, without deceit and without falsehood — this is not Maskarī's four kinds of evasive argument about the deathless. Consider it closely, and do not shame the sorrow and longing with which you seek."

Mañjuśrī's Question, and the Second Moon 文殊致問 · 第二月喻

是時，文殊師利法王子愍諸四眾，在大眾中即從座起，頂禮佛足，合掌恭敬而白佛言：「世尊！此諸大眾，不悟如來發明二種精見色空、是非是義。世尊！若此前緣色空等象，若是見者，應有所指；若非見者，應無所矚。而今不知是義所歸，故有驚怖，非是疇昔善根輕尠。唯願如來大慈發明，此諸物象與此見精元是何物，於其中間無是非是。」

佛告文殊及諸大眾：「十方如來及大菩薩，於其自住三摩地中，見與見緣并所想相，如虛空花本無所有；此見及緣，元是菩提妙淨明體，云何於中有是非是？文殊！吾今問汝，如汝文殊，更有文殊是文殊者？為無文殊？」

「如是，世尊！我真文殊，無是文殊。何以故？若有是者，則二文殊。然我今日非無文殊，於中實無是非二相。」

佛言：「此見妙明與諸空塵，亦復如是。本是妙明無上菩提淨圓真心，妄為色空及與聞見，如第二月，誰為是月？又誰非月？文殊！但一月真，中間自無是月、非月。是以汝今觀見與塵種種發明，名為妄想，不能於中出是非是。由是精真妙覺明性，故能令汝出指非指！」

Then Mañjuśrī, prince of the Dharma, pitying the fourfold assembly, rose from his seat among them, bowed his head at the Buddha's feet, joined his palms in reverence, and said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, these assemblies have not awakened to the sense in which the Tathāgata has made evident the two — the refined seeing, and form-and-emptiness — as being and not being it. World-Honored One, if these appearances of form and emptiness before us are the seeing, there should be something to point at; and if they are not the seeing, there should be nothing to behold. Not knowing now where this sense comes to rest, they are startled and afraid; it is not that their roots of good from former times are slight. I beg the Tathāgata in his great kindness to make evident what these appearances of things and this essence of seeing originally are, and how, between them, there is neither being it nor not being it."

The Buddha said to Mañjuśrī and the whole assembly: "The Tathāgatas of the ten directions and the great bodhisattvas, abiding in their own samādhi, find that seeing, the conditions of seeing, and the aspects thought of, are like flowers in empty space: originally nothing at all. This seeing and its conditions are from the first the wondrous, pure, luminous substance of awakening — how could there be, within it, being it or not being it? Mañjuśrī, I ask you now: as for you, Mañjuśrī, is there a further Mañjuśrī who is Mañjuśrī? Or is there no Mañjuśrī?"

"Just so, World-Honored One. I am the real Mañjuśrī, and there is no 'being Mañjuśrī'. Why? Because if there were something that 'is' it, there would be two Mañjuśrīs. Yet it is not that I today am without Mañjuśrī; within it there are truly not the two aspects, being and not being."

The Buddha said: "This wondrous luminous seeing, together with space and the objects, is just so. It is originally the wondrous luminous, unsurpassed, pure, perfect true mind of awakening, falsely taken to be form and emptiness and hearing and seeing — like a second moon: which is the moon, and which is not the moon? Mañjuśrī, there is only one real moon, and in it there is of itself neither 'this is the moon' nor 'this is not the moon'. And so your looking now at seeing and objects and all that is made evident in them is called false thinking, and within it you cannot get free of 'is' and 'is not'. It is by this refined, real, wondrous awakened luminous nature that you are able to get free of pointing and not-pointing!"

Reading note: "The second moon" refers to the old image of pressing a finger against the eye and seeing one moon appear as two — the second moon is not simply nonexistent (something really does appear), yet it is not a second real moon either (there is, and always was, only one). The Buddha uses it, together with Mañjuśrī's own name, to dissolve the very question Ānanda kept trying to force an answer to: whether seeing "is" or "is not" its objects. The category itself, he suggests, does not apply here.

Not Cause and Condition, Not Spontaneity 辨非因緣非自然

阿難白佛言：「世尊！誠如法王所說，覺緣遍十方界湛然常住，性非生滅；與先梵志娑毘迦羅所談冥諦，及投灰等諸外道種說有真我遍滿十方，有何差別？世尊亦曾於楞伽山為大慧等敷演斯義——彼外道等常說自然，我說因緣非彼境界。——我今觀此覺性自然，非生非滅，遠離一切虛妄顛倒，似非因緣與彼自然；云何開示，不入群邪，獲真實心妙覺明性？」

佛告阿難：「我今如是開示方便，真實告汝，汝猶未悟，惑為自然。阿難！若必自然，自須甄明有自然體。汝且觀此妙明見中，以何為自？此見為復以明為自？以暗為自？以空為自？以塞為自？阿難！若明為自，應不見暗？若復以空為自體者，應不見塞？如是乃至諸暗等相以為自者，則於明時見性斷滅，云何見明？」

阿難言：「必此妙見性非自然，我今發明是因緣性。心猶未明，諮詢如來，是義云何合因緣性？」

佛言：「汝言因緣，吾復問汝，汝今因見，見性現前，此見為復因明有見？因暗有見？因空有見？因塞有見？阿難！若因明有，應不見暗，如因暗有，應不見明；如是乃至因空、因塞，同於明、暗。復次，阿難！此見又復緣明有見？緣暗有見？緣空有見？緣塞有見？阿難！若緣空有，應不見塞；若緣塞有，應不見空；如是乃至緣明、緣暗，同於空、塞。當知如是精覺妙明，非因、非緣，亦非自然、非不自然；無非、不非，無是、非是；離一切相，即一切法。汝今云何於中措心，以諸世間戲論名相而得分別！如以手掌撮摩虛空，只益自勞；虛空云何隨汝執捉？」

阿難白佛言：「世尊！必妙覺性非因非緣，世尊！云何常與比丘宣說見性具四種緣——所謂因空、因明、因心、因眼，是義云何？」

佛言：「阿難！我說世間諸因緣相，非第一義。阿難！吾復問汝，諸世間人說我能見，云何名見？云何不見？」

阿難言：「世人因於日、月、燈光見種種相，名之為見。若復無此三種光明，則不能見。」

「阿難！若無明時名不見者，應不見暗；若必見暗，此但無明云何無見？阿難！若在暗時，不見明故名為不見；今在明時，不見暗相還名不見；如是二相俱名不見？若復二相自相[夌*欠]奪，非汝見性於中暫無；如是則知二俱名見，云何不見？是故阿難！汝今當知，見明之時，見非是明；見暗之時，見非是暗；見空之時，見非是空；見塞之時，見非是塞，四義成就。汝復應知，見見之時，見非是見，見猶離見，見不能及，云何復說因緣自然及和合相？汝等聲聞狹劣無識，不能通達清淨實相。吾今誨汝，當善思惟，無得疲怠妙菩提路！」

阿難白佛言：「世尊！如佛世尊為我等輩宣說因緣及與自然，諸和合相與不和合，心猶未開；而今更聞見見非見，重增迷悶。伏願弘慈施大慧目，開示我等覺心明淨！」作是語已，悲淚頂禮，承受聖旨。

爾時，世尊憐愍阿難及諸大眾，將欲敷演大陀羅尼諸三摩提妙修行路，告阿難言：「汝雖強記，但益多聞，於奢摩他微密觀照，心猶未了。汝今諦聽！吾當為汝分別開示，亦令將來諸有漏者獲菩提果。

Ānanda said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, it is truly as the King of Dharma says: the conditions of awakening reach throughout the worlds of the ten directions, limpid and ever abiding, their nature neither arising nor ceasing. But how does this differ from the dark principle spoken of by the former brahmin Kapila, or from the claim of the ash-smearers and other such non-buddhists that there is a true self filling the ten directions? The World-Honored One also once expounded this meaning on Mount Laṅkā for Mahāmati and the others: that those non-buddhists constantly speak of spontaneity, while what I teach is causes and conditions, which is not their territory. Now, looking at this awakened nature as spontaneous, neither arising nor ceasing, far removed from every falsity and inversion, it seems to be neither causes-and-conditions nor their spontaneity. How are we to be instructed, so as not to fall in with that crowd of errors, and to gain the real mind, the wondrous awakened luminous nature?"

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "I have revealed it to you in this skillful way and told you what is real, and still you have not awakened, but are confused into taking it for spontaneity. Ānanda, if it must be spontaneous, then you must be able to make out a substance that is spontaneous. Look into this wondrous luminous seeing: what do you take as its own self? Does this seeing take brightness as its self? Darkness as its self? Space as its self? Obstruction as its self? Ānanda, if brightness is its self, it should not see darkness; and if space is taken as its own substance, it should not see obstruction. And so on: if aspects such as darkness are taken as its self, then in brightness the nature of seeing would be cut off — how then would it see brightness?"

Ānanda said: "This wondrous seeing must then have a nature that is not spontaneity, and I now make it out to be of the nature of causes and conditions. But my mind is still not clear, and I ask the Tathāgata: how does this sense accord with a nature of causes and conditions?"

The Buddha said: "You say causes and conditions. Let me ask you again: you now see because of seeing, and the nature of seeing is present before you. Does this seeing have seeing because of brightness? Because of darkness? Because of space? Because of obstruction? Ānanda, if it exists because of brightness, it should not see darkness; and if because of darkness, it should not see brightness; and so on with 'because of space' and 'because of obstruction', just as with brightness and darkness. Again, Ānanda: does this seeing have seeing conditioned by brightness? Conditioned by darkness? Conditioned by space? Conditioned by obstruction? Ānanda, if it exists conditioned by space, it should not see obstruction; and if conditioned by obstruction, it should not see space; and so on with 'conditioned by brightness' and 'conditioned by darkness', just as with space and obstruction. Know then that this refined awakened wondrous luminosity is neither cause nor condition, and neither spontaneous nor not spontaneous; there is no 'is not' and no 'is not not', no 'is' and no 'is not is'; apart from every aspect, it is every phenomenon. How is it that you now set your mind on it, and would discriminate it by the frivolous names and aspects of the world? That is like grasping and rubbing at empty space with the palm of the hand: you only tire yourself, and how would empty space submit to your grasping?"

Ānanda said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, if the wondrous awakened nature must be neither cause nor condition — World-Honored One, why do you constantly teach the monks that the nature of seeing is furnished with four conditions, namely because of space, because of brightness, because of mind, and because of the eye? How is this meaning to be taken?"

The Buddha said: "Ānanda, when I speak of the world's aspects of causes and conditions, that is not the first truth. Ānanda, let me ask you again: people in the world say 'I can see' — what is called seeing, and what is called not seeing?"

Ānanda said: "People of the world, by the light of sun, moon, and lamp, see all sorts of aspects, and that is called seeing; and if these three kinds of light were absent, they would not be able to see."

"Ānanda, if what is called not-seeing is when there is no brightness, then darkness should not be seen either; and if darkness certainly is seen, then this is merely the absence of brightness — how is it the absence of seeing? Ānanda, if in darkness one is said not to see because brightness is not seen, then now, in brightness, not seeing the aspect of darkness would likewise be called not seeing — so are both aspects to be called not-seeing? And if the two aspects displace one another, it is not that your nature of seeing is momentarily absent within them; and in that case you may know that both are to be called seeing — how could either be not-seeing? Therefore, Ānanda, you should know: when brightness is seen, the seeing is not the brightness; when darkness is seen, the seeing is not the darkness; when space is seen, the seeing is not the space; when obstruction is seen, the seeing is not the obstruction — the four senses are established. And you should know further: when seeing is seen, the seeing is not that seeing; seeing is still apart from seeing, and seeing cannot reach it. How, then, can you still speak of causes and conditions, of spontaneity, and of aspects of combination? You hearers are narrow and poor in understanding, and cannot penetrate the pure real aspect. I now instruct you: consider it well, and do not grow weary on the road of wondrous awakening!"

Ānanda said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, when the Buddha, the World-Honored One, expounded for us causes and conditions and spontaneity, and aspects of combination and non-combination, my mind was still not opened; and now, hearing further that when seeing is seen the seeing is not that seeing, my confusion is doubled. I humbly beg your great kindness to bestow the eye of great wisdom and reveal to us a mind of awakening, bright and pure!" Having said this, he wept, bowed his head, and received the holy intent.

Then the World-Honored One, pitying Ānanda and the whole assembly, and about to expound the great dhāraṇī and the road of wondrous practice in all the samādhis, said to Ānanda: "Though you have a strong memory, it has only added to your learning; in the subtle and secret contemplation of śamatha your mind is still not clear. Listen closely now: I shall distinguish and reveal it for you, and shall also let those with outflows in time to come gain the fruit of awakening.

Reading note: Ānanda's worry is a real one for any Buddhist audience: an unchanging, all-pervading awareness sounds uncomfortably close to the "self" (ātman) taught by rival schools like Sāṃkhya (Kapila's tradition). The Buddha's answer refuses both of the categories Ānanda offers — "arising from causes and conditions" and "existing spontaneously, on its own" — as equally beside the point. The closing line, "seeing seeing itself is not seeing" (見見之時，見非是見), is one of the sūtra's most compressed and most quoted formulations: awareness can never turn itself into one more object for itself to look at, which is exactly why no philosophical category built for objects — caused, uncaused, one, many, self, not-self — ever quite fits it.

Two Kinds of False Seeing 別業妄見 · 同分妄見

阿難！一切眾生輪迴世間，由二顛倒分別見妄，當處發生，當業輪轉。云何二見？一者、眾生別業妄見，二者、眾生同分妄見。

「云何名為別業妄見？阿難！如世間人目有赤眚，夜見燈光別有圓影五色重疊。於意云何？此夜燈明所現圓光，為是燈色？為當見色？阿難！此若燈色，則非眚人何不同見，而此圓影唯眚之觀。若是見色，見已成色，則彼眚人見圓影者名為何等？復次，阿難！若此圓影離燈別有，則合傍觀屏帳、几筵有圓影出。離見別有，應非眼矚，云何眚人目見圓影？是故當知色實在燈，見病為影；影、見俱眚，見眚非病。終不應言『是燈是見，於是中有非燈非見』，如第二月非體非影。何以故？第二之觀，揑所成故。諸有智者不應說言『此揑根元，是形非形，離見非見』，此亦如是，目眚所成，今欲名誰是燈、是見？何況分別非燈、非見！

「云何名為同分妄見？阿難！此閻浮提，除大海水，中間平陸有三千洲。正中大洲，東西括量，大國凡有二千三百；其餘小洲，在諸海中，其間或有三兩百國，或一或二，至于三十、四十、五十。阿難！若復此中有一小洲，只有兩國。唯一國人同感惡緣，則彼小洲當土眾生，覩諸一切不祥境界，或見二日，或見兩月，其中乃至暈蝕珮玦、彗勃飛流、負耳虹蜺。種種惡相，但此國見；彼國眾生，本所不見，亦復不聞。阿難！吾今為汝以此二事進退合明。

「阿難！如彼眾生別業妄見，矚燈光中所現圓影，雖現似境，終彼見者目眚所成。眚即見勞，非色所造；然見眚者終無見咎。例汝今日，以目觀見山河國土及諸眾生，皆是無始見病所成。見與見緣似現前境，元我覺明見所緣眚。覺見即眚，本覺明心覺緣非眚；覺所覺眚，覺非眚中，此實見見，云何復名覺、聞、知、見？是故汝今見我及汝并諸世間十類眾生，皆即見眚，非見眚者；彼見真精性非眚者，故不名見。

「阿難！如彼眾生同分妄見，例彼妄見別業一人，一病目人同彼一國。彼見圓影，眚妄所生。此眾同分所現不祥，同見業中瘴惡所起，俱是無始見妄所生。例閻浮提三千洲中，兼四大海娑婆世界，并洎十方諸有漏國及諸眾生，同是覺明無漏妙心，見、聞、覺、知虛妄病緣、和合妄生，和合妄死。若能遠離諸和合緣及不和合，則復滅除諸生死因；圓滿菩提不生滅性，清淨本心本覺常住。

"Ānanda, all living beings turn in the round of the world because of two inverted, discriminating falsities of seeing, which arise right where they are and turn in the round according to their karma. What are the two kinds of seeing? The first is a being's false seeing of individual karma; the second is the false seeing that beings share in common.

"What is called the false seeing of individual karma? Ānanda, it is as when a person in the world has a red film on the eye and at night sees, beside the lamplight, a separate round halo layered in five colors. What do you think: is this round light appearing in the lamp's brightness at night the lamp's color, or is it the color of seeing? Ānanda, if it is the lamp's color, then why do those without the film not see it as well, when in fact this round halo is seen only by the one with the film? And if it is the color of seeing, then seeing having already become color, what is one to call the man with the film who sees a round halo? Furthermore, Ānanda: if this round halo existed apart from the lamp, then on looking aside at the screens and curtains, the mats and couches, round halos ought to come out of those as well. And if it existed apart from seeing, it should not be beheld by the eye — how then does the man with the film see a round halo with his eye? So you should know that the color is really in the lamp, while the halo is the seeing's disease: halo and seeing are alike the film, but what sees the film is not itself diseased. And one should certainly never say, 'this is the lamp, this is the seeing, and within this there is what is neither lamp nor seeing' — as with the second moon, which is neither substance nor reflection. Why? Because the sight of a second moon is produced by pressing the eye. Those who have wisdom should not say of that pressing, 'this is at root a shape or not a shape, apart from seeing or not seeing.' It is the same here: with what the film on the eye produces, which of them would you now name the lamp, and which the seeing? How much less distinguish what is neither lamp nor seeing!

"What is called the false seeing shared in common? Ānanda, in this continent of Jambudvīpa, apart from the waters of the great sea, the level land between holds three thousand islands. On the great continent at the center, measured from east to west, there are in all two thousand three hundred large countries; and the smaller islands in the seas hold, some of them, two or three hundred countries, some one or two, and some as many as thirty, forty, or fifty. Ānanda, suppose among these there is a small island with only two countries, and the people of just one of them together meet with an evil condition: then the beings of that land on that small island will behold all sorts of inauspicious sights — some seeing two suns, some seeing two moons, and among them halos and eclipses, coronas and broken rings, comets and meteors, sun-ears and rainbows bright and dim. All these evil aspects are seen only in that country; the beings of the other country neither see them nor even hear of them. Ānanda, I shall now make these two matters clear for you by setting them side by side, forward and back.

"Ānanda, as for that being's false seeing of individual karma, who beholds a round halo appearing in the lamplight — though it appears to be an object, in the end it is produced by the film on the seer's eye. The film is the strain of seeing, and is not made by color; yet what sees the film is finally without fault of seeing. Take this as an instance for yourself today: your seeing, with your eyes, of mountains and rivers and lands and all living beings is entirely produced by a beginningless disease of seeing. Seeing and the conditions of seeing seem to be an object present before you, but they are, at root, the film upon what my awakened luminosity sees as its object. Awareness together with seeing is the film; the originally awakened luminous mind, aware of conditions, is not the film. What is aware of the film that is taken as an object, being aware, is not within the film — and this is truly 'seeing seeing'. How, then, could it further be called awareness, hearing, knowing, or seeing? Therefore your seeing now of me, and of yourself, and of the ten kinds of beings in all the worlds, is all of it the film upon seeing, and not what sees the film; and that seeing which is truly refined, being of a nature that is not the film, is therefore not called seeing at all.

"Ānanda, as for those beings' false seeing shared in common, take as its instance the false seeing of individual karma in one person: one person with a diseased eye corresponds to that one country. What he sees as a round halo is produced by the falsity of the film; and the inauspicious sights that appear to this multitude in common arise from the blight and evil within a karma of common seeing — both alike produced by a beginningless falsity of seeing. And taking as instances the three thousand islands of Jambudvīpa, together with the four great seas and the Sahā world, and on to all the lands with outflows in the ten directions and all their beings: they are alike this awakened luminous, outflow-free wondrous mind, in which seeing, hearing, awareness, and knowing are false, diseased conditions — falsely arising by combination, falsely dying by combination. If one can get far away from all conditions of combination and of non-combination alike, then the causes of birth and death are extinguished as well; and awakening is complete, of a nature that neither arises nor ceases — the pure original mind, the original awakening, ever abiding.

Reading note: The two kinds of false seeing scale the same diagnosis up from the individual to the whole shared world. A diseased eye sees a halo around a lamp that isn't really there — but the disease is in the eye, not the seeing itself, which remains untouched by what it mistakenly perceives. In just the same way, an entire population can share a "disease" of perception (their collective karma) that makes them see mountains, rivers, and each other as solid, external, and separate from mind — while the awareness underneath that shared affliction remains, in itself, no more diseased than the healthy seeing hidden beneath any one person's blurred vision.

Neither Combined nor Uncombined 非和合 · 非不和合

One more position remains to be closed off. Having granted that awakening is neither caused nor spontaneous, Ānanda might still suppose it is assembled — that seeing is awakening mixed with brightness. The Buddha takes combination and joining in turn, then takes their negations in turn, and closes all four.

「阿難！汝雖先悟本覺妙明性非因緣非自然性，而猶未明如是覺元非和合生及不和合。阿難！吾今復以前塵問汝，汝今猶以一切世間妄想和合諸因緣性，而自疑惑證菩提心和合起者。則汝今者妙淨見精，為與明和？為與闇和？為與通和？為與塞和？若明和者，且汝觀明，當明現前，何處雜見？見相可辨，雜何形像？若非見者，云何見明？若即見者，云何見見？必見圓滿，何處和明？若明圓滿不合見和，見必異明，雜則失彼性明名字；雜失明性，和明非義。彼暗與通及諸群塞，亦復如是。

「復次，阿難！又汝今者妙淨見精，為與明合？為與暗合？為與通合？為與塞合？若明合者，至於暗時明相已滅，此見即不與諸暗合，云何見暗？若見暗時不與暗合，與明合者應非見明？既不見明，云何明合了明非暗？彼暗與通及諸群塞，亦復如是。」

阿難白佛言：「世尊！如我思惟，此妙覺元與諸緣塵及心念慮非和合耶？」佛言：「汝今又言覺非和合，吾復問汝，此妙見精非和合者，為非明和？為非暗和？為非通和？為非塞和？若非明和，則見與明必有邊畔。汝且諦觀，何處是明？何處是見？在見在明，自何為畔？阿難！若明際中必無見者，則不相及；自不知其明相所在，畔云何成？彼暗與通及諸群塞，亦復如是。

「又妙見精非和合者，為非明合？為非暗合？為非通合？為非塞合？若非明合，則見與明性相乖角，如耳與明了不相觸；見且不知明相所在，云何甄明合非合理？彼暗與通及諸群塞，亦復如是。

「阿難！汝猶未明一切浮塵諸幻化相，當處出生，隨處滅盡；幻妄稱相，其性真為妙覺明體。如是乃至五陰、六入，從十二處至十八界，因緣和合虛妄有生，因緣別離虛妄名滅；殊不能知生滅去來，本如來藏常住妙明不動周圓妙真如性，性真常中求於去來、迷悟、死生了無所得。

"Ānanda, though you awakened earlier to the fact that the nature of original awakening, wondrous and luminous, is neither of causes-and-conditions nor of spontaneity, you are still not clear that this source of awakening is neither produced by combination nor uncombined. Ānanda, I shall question you once more by means of the objects before you. You still take up all the world's false thinking about combination and causes and conditions, and doubt on that account, supposing that the mind of realized awakening arises by combination. Then this wondrous pure essence of seeing of yours — is it combined with brightness? Combined with darkness? Combined with passage? Combined with obstruction? If it is combined with brightness, then look at brightness: when brightness is present before you, where is the seeing mixed into it? The aspect of seeing can be made out — what shape is it that is mixed in? If what is mixed in is not the seeing, how is brightness seen? And if it is the seeing, how is the seeing seen? And if seeing must be complete, where is there room for it to be combined with brightness? And if brightness is complete, it cannot be combined with seeing; and seeing being necessarily other than brightness, in mixing it would lose the very name of 'bright nature'; and mixing so as to lose the nature of brightness, 'combined with brightness' is not the right sense. And so likewise with darkness, with passage, and with all the kinds of obstruction.

"Furthermore, Ānanda: this wondrous pure essence of seeing of yours — does it join with brightness? Join with darkness? Join with passage? Join with obstruction? If it joins with brightness, then by the time of darkness the aspect of brightness has already vanished, and this seeing would then not be joined with any darkness — how would darkness be seen? And if in seeing darkness it is not joined with darkness, then in joining with brightness it should not be seeing brightness either; and not seeing brightness, how could 'joined with brightness' make out that brightness is not darkness? And so likewise with darkness, with passage, and with all the kinds of obstruction."

Ānanda said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, as I consider it, is this source of wondrous awakening then not combined with the conditioned objects and with the mind's thinking?" The Buddha said: "Now you say that awakening is uncombined. Let me question you again. This wondrous essence of seeing, being uncombined — is it uncombined with brightness? Uncombined with darkness? Uncombined with passage? Uncombined with obstruction? If it is uncombined with brightness, then seeing and brightness must have a boundary between them. Look closely: where is brightness? Where is seeing? Between seeing and brightness, what marks the boundary? Ānanda, if within the region of brightness there is certainly no seeing, then the two do not reach each other at all; and not knowing of itself where the aspect of brightness lies, how would a boundary be constituted? And so likewise with darkness, with passage, and with all the kinds of obstruction.

"And again, the wondrous essence of seeing being uncombined — is it unjoined with brightness? Unjoined with darkness? Unjoined with passage? Unjoined with obstruction? If it is unjoined with brightness, then the natures of seeing and brightness stand at odds, as the ear and brightness do not touch at all; and seeing not even knowing where the aspect of brightness lies, how would one make out the principle of its joining or not joining with brightness? And so likewise with darkness, with passage, and with all the kinds of obstruction.

"Ānanda, you are still not clear that all the floating objects and illusory transforming aspects arise right where they are and are extinguished right where they are; called aspects, they are illusory and false, and their nature is truly the wondrous awakened luminous substance. And so it is with the five aggregates and the six entrances, from the twelve fields to the eighteen elements: by the combining of causes and conditions there is a false arising, and by the parting of causes and conditions there is what is falsely named ceasing. What beings altogether fail to know is that arising and ceasing, coming and going, are originally the tathāgatagarbha — the ever-abiding, wondrously luminous, unmoving, all-pervading, wondrous suchness-nature; and that within this nature, truly constant, one may search for going and coming, delusion and awakening, death and birth, and find nothing at all.

Reading note: The four-cornered form of this passage — combined, joined, uncombined, unjoined — is a catuṣkoṭi run on a single question: can seeing be built out of parts? Each horn fails on the same point. If seeing were mixed into brightness you could say where the mixture is; if it were merely stuck to brightness it would fall off in the dark; if it were separate from brightness there would be a boundary to point at; and if it never touched brightness at all, it could not report on brightness any more than an ear can. The passage ends by naming what has been established since fascicle 1 and will carry the whole of fascicle 3: the aggregates, entrances, fields, and elements are not built, they are the tathāgatagarbha.

The Five Aggregates Are the Tathāgatagarbha 五陰本如來藏妙真如性

The fascicle closes with the Buddha taking each of the five aggregates (form, sensation, perception, formations, and consciousness) in turn, and showing, with a different everyday analogy for each, that none of them truly arises from any cause, or from itself, or from nowhere at all — each is, at root, nothing other than the tathāgatagarbha, described here for the first time by name.

Form: Flowers Conjured in Empty Space 色陰 · 空花喻

「阿難！云何五陰本如來藏妙真如性？

「阿難！譬如有人，以清淨目觀晴明空，唯一晴虛，逈無所有。其人無故不動目睛、瞪以發勞，則於虛空別見狂花，復有一切狂亂非相。色陰當知亦復如是。阿難！是諸狂花，非從空來，非從目出。如是阿難！若空來者，既從空來還從空入，若有出入即非虛空。空若非空，自不容其花相起滅，如阿難體不容阿難。若目出者，既從目出還從目入；即此花性從目出故，當合有見。若有見者，去既花空，旋合見眼。若無見者，出既翳空，旋當翳眼。又見花時目應無瞖，云何晴空號清明眼？是故當知色陰虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

"Ānanda, how are the five aggregates originally the tathāgatagarbha's wondrous suchness-nature?

"Ānanda, it is like a man who looks at a clear bright sky with clear eyes: there is nothing but clear emptiness, remote and with nothing in it at all. Then for no reason the man holds his eyes still and stares until they strain, and he sees in that empty space separate wild flowers, and all sorts of wild, disordered non-aspects besides. Know that the aggregate of form is just so. Ānanda, these wild flowers do not come from space and do not come out of the eye. So it is, Ānanda: if they came from space, then having come from space they would return into space; and if there were a coming out and a going in, that would not be empty space at all. And space, not being empty, could not of itself make room for those flower-aspects to arise and cease, just as Ānanda's own body makes no room for Ānanda. And if they came out of the eye, then having come out of the eye they would return into the eye; and this flower-nature, having come out of the eye, ought to have seeing. If it had seeing, then having gone out to become flowers in space, on turning back it ought to see the eye. And if it had no seeing, then having gone out to film the space, on turning back it ought to film the eye. Besides, when the flowers are seen the eye ought to have no film — why then is only a clear sky called a clear bright eye? You should therefore know that the aggregate of form is false — originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

「阿難！譬如有人，手足宴安，百骸調適，忽如忘生，性無違順。其人無故以二手掌於空相摩，於二手中妄生澁、滑、冷、熱諸相。受陰當知亦復如是。阿難！是諸幻觸，不從空來，不從掌出。如是阿難！若空來者，既能觸掌何不觸身？不應虛空選擇來觸。若從掌出，應非待合。又掌出故，合則掌知，離即觸入；臂腕骨髓，應亦覺知入時蹤跡。必有覺心知出知入，自有一物身中往來，何待合知要名為觸？是故當知受陰虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

"Ānanda, it is like a man whose hands and feet are at ease and whose whole frame is comfortable, so that he almost forgets he is alive, his nature neither averse nor drawn. Then for no reason he rubs his two palms together in the air, and in those two hands sensations of roughness and smoothness, cold and warmth, falsely arise. Know that the aggregate of sensation is just so. Ānanda, these illusory contacts do not come from space and do not come out of the palms. So it is, Ānanda: if they came from space, then being able to touch the palms, why do they not touch the body? It will not do for empty space to pick and choose what it comes and touches. And if they came out of the palms, they should not have to wait on the palms' meeting. Moreover, coming out of the palms, the palms would know it when they met and the contact would enter when they parted; and the arms, wrists, bones, and marrow ought to be aware of the track of its entering as well. There would have to be an aware mind that knows the going out and the coming in — that is, some one thing traveling to and fro inside the body; and then why wait on the palms' meeting to know it, and call that contact? You should therefore know that the aggregate of sensation is false — originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

「阿難！譬如有人，談說醋梅口中水出，思踏懸崖足心酸澁。想陰當知亦復如是。阿難！如是醋說不從梅生，非從口入。如是阿難！若梅生者，梅合自談何待人說？若從口入，自合口聞，何須待耳？若獨耳聞，此水何不耳中而出？想踏懸崖，與說相類。是故當知想陰虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

"Ānanda, it is like a man whose mouth waters when sour plums are spoken of, and the soles of whose feet go weak when he thinks of treading a cliff's edge. Know that the aggregate of perception is just so. Ānanda, this sourness in the speaking is not produced from the plum, and does not enter from the mouth. So it is, Ānanda: if it were produced from the plum, the plum should speak of itself — why wait on a person to speak of it? And if it entered from the mouth, then the mouth should do its own hearing — why wait on the ear? And if the ear alone hears it, why does this water not come out of the ear? Thinking of treading a cliff's edge is of a kind with the speaking. You should therefore know that the aggregate of perception is false — originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

「阿難！譬如暴流波浪相續，前際後際不相踰越。行陰當知亦復如是。阿難！如是流性，不因空生，不因水有；亦非水性，非離空水。如是阿難！若因空生，則諸十方無盡虛空成無盡流，世界自然俱受淪溺。若因水有，則此暴流性應非水，有所、有相，今應現在。若即水性，則澄清時應非水體；若離空水，空非有外，水外無流。是故當知行陰虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

"Ānanda, it is like a violent torrent whose waves follow one after another, the earlier and the later never overstepping each other. Know that the aggregate of formations is just so. Ānanda, this nature of flowing does not exist because of space and does not exist because of water; it is not the nature of water, and it is not apart from space and water. So it is, Ānanda: if it existed because of space, then all the endless empty space of the ten directions would become an endless torrent, and the world would of course be wholly drowned. If it existed because of water, then the nature of this torrent would not be water, and it would have a place and an aspect of its own, which ought to be present now. If it were the nature of water, then when the water is clear and still it would not be water's substance. And if it were apart from space and water — space has nothing outside it, and outside water there is no flowing. You should therefore know that the aggregate of formations is false — originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

「阿難！譬如有人，取頻伽瓶塞其兩孔，滿中擎空，千里遠行用餉他國。識陰當知亦復如是。阿難！如是虛空，非彼方來，非此方入。如是阿難，若彼方來，則本瓶中既貯空去，於本瓶地應少虛空。若此方入，開孔倒瓶，應見空出。是故當知識陰虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

"Ānanda, it is like a man who takes a kalaviṅka vase, stops up its two openings, fills it with air, and carries it a thousand li to make a present of it to another country. Know that the aggregate of consciousness is just so. Ānanda, this air did not come from that place and does not enter in this place. So it is, Ānanda: if it came from that place, then the vase having carried off air stored in it, there should be that much less space in the place it came from. And if it entered in this place, then on opening the holes and tipping the vase one ought to see the air come out. You should therefore know that the aggregate of consciousness is false — originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Reading note: Each analogy isolates the same structural point: the illusory flowers are neither in the sky nor in the eye; the false sensations are neither in space nor in either hand alone; the watering mouth is caused neither by the plum nor by the ear alone; the torrent is identical with neither empty space nor with water as such; the air in the sealed vase neither truly "comes from" its origin nor truly "enters" its destination. Each aggregate, examined this closely, turns out not to be an independently existing thing that was caused into being at all — which is exactly why it can be, without contradiction, identical with the tathāgatagarbha (如來藏, the "womb" or "matrix" of Buddhahood) rather than some obstruction standing in front of it. Fascicle 3 goes on to apply this same method to the twelve sense-fields and eighteen elements.
