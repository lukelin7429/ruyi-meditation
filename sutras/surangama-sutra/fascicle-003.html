Every Category, the Same Discovery

Fascicle 3 applies the method of fascicle 2 systematically: the six sense-entrances, the twelve sense-fields, the eighteen elements, and the seven great elements — earth, water, fire, wind, space, seeing, and consciousness — each shown, category by category, to be nothing other than the tathāgatagarbha. The fascicle ends with Ānanda's awakening and his verse of praise to the Buddha.

About This Fascicle

Fascicle 2 ended by showing that each of the five aggregates is, at root, the tathāgatagarbha. Fascicle 3 runs the same argument through three more classical categories of Buddhist analysis — the six sense-entrances (六入), the twelve sense-fields (十二處, each a faculty paired with its object), and the eighteen elements (十八界, each faculty-object pair together with the consciousness it gives rise to) — before turning to a final, more expansive analysis of seven "great elements" (七大): earth, water, fire, wind, space, seeing, and consciousness. Each item, in turn, is shown to have no independent origin — not from itself, not from its apparent cause, not from empty space, not spontaneously — and is therefore, in the sūtra's central refrain, "the nature of true emptiness, and true emptiness's own nature," pervading the whole Dharma-realm as the tathāgatagarbha.

The Six Sense-Entrances 六入本如來藏妙真如性

For each of the six sense-entrances, the Buddha applies the same test: this "entrance" is neither the sense-faculty itself, nor its object, nor empty space, nor does it appear from nowhere at all — it is, each time, the tathāgatagarbha.

Seeing 眼入

Chinese Source

「阿難！即彼目精瞪發勞者，兼目與勞，同是菩提瞪發勞相。因于明暗二種妄塵，發見居中，吸此塵象，名為見性。此見離彼明暗二塵，畢竟無體。如是阿難！當知是見非明暗來，非於根出，不於空生。何以故？若從明來，暗即隨滅，應非見暗。若從暗來，明即隨滅，應無見明。若從根生，必無明暗，如是見精本無自性。若於空出，前矚塵象，歸當見根；又空自觀，何關汝入？是故當知眼入虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ānanda, when the eye is stared fixed until it strains, both the eye and the strain alike are marks of bodhi itself straining into fatigue. Dependent on the two false objects of brightness and darkness, seeing arises in between them, taking hold of these objects — this is called the nature of seeing. This seeing, apart from those two objects of brightness and darkness, has, in the end, no substance of its own. Just so, Ānanda: you should know that this seeing does not come from brightness or darkness, does not come out of the faculty, and does not arise in empty space. Why? If it came from brightness, it should vanish along with darkness, and so should not be able to see darkness. If it came from darkness, it should vanish along with brightness, and so should have no seeing of brightness. If it arose from the faculty itself, then, brightness and darkness aside, this essence of seeing would have no nature of its own to begin with. If it emerged from empty space, then, having gazed outward at objects, it should turn and see the faculty itself — and besides, if space itself were doing the observing, what would that have to do with your own entrance of seeing? You should therefore know that the entrance of the eye is false — originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Hearing, Smelling, Tasting, Touching, Knowing 耳、鼻、舌、身、意入

「阿難！譬如有人，以兩手指急塞其耳，耳根勞故頭中作聲；兼耳與勞，同是菩提瞪發勞相。因于動靜二種妄塵，發聞居中，吸此塵象，名聽聞性。此聞離彼動靜二塵，畢竟無體。如是阿難！當知是聞，非動靜來，非於根出，不於空生。何以故？若從靜來，動即隨滅，應非聞動；若從動來，靜即隨滅，應無覺靜。若從根生，必無動靜，如是聞體本無自性。若於空出，有聞成性，即非虛空；又空自聞，何關汝入？是故當知耳入虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！譬如有人急畜其鼻，畜久成勞，則於鼻中聞有冷觸。因觸分別通塞、虛實，如是乃至諸香、臭氣；兼鼻與勞，同是菩提瞪發勞相。因于通塞二種妄塵，發聞居中，吸此塵象，名嗅聞性。此聞離彼通塞二塵，畢竟無體。當知是聞，非通塞來，非於根出，不於空生。何以故？若從通來，塞自隨滅，云何知塞？如因塞有，通則無聞，云何發明香、臭等觸？若從根生，必無通塞，如是聞體本無自性。若從空出，是聞自當迴嗅汝鼻；空自有聞，何關汝入？是故當知鼻入虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！譬如有人以舌舐吻，熟舐令勞，其人若病則有苦味，無病之人微有甜觸；由甜與苦顯此舌根，不動之時淡性常在。兼舌與勞，同是菩提瞪發勞相。因甜苦、淡二種妄塵，發知居中，吸此塵象，名知味性。此知味性，離彼甜、苦及淡二塵，畢竟無體。如是阿難！當知如是嘗苦、淡知，非甜、苦來，非因淡有，又非根出，不於空生。何以故？若甜、苦來，淡即知滅，云何知淡？若從淡出，甜即知亡，復云何知甜、苦二相？若從舌生，必無甜、淡及與苦塵，斯知味根本無自性。若於空出，虛空自味，非汝口知；又空自知，何關汝入？是故當知舌入虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！譬如有人，以一冷手觸於熱手，若冷勢多熱者從冷，若熱功勝冷者成熱。如是以此合覺之觸，顯於離知；涉勢若成，因于勞觸。兼身與勞，同是菩提瞪發勞相。因于離、合二種妄塵，發覺居中，吸此塵象，名知覺性。此知覺體，離彼離、合、違、順二塵，畢竟無體。如是阿難！當知是覺，非離、合來，非違、順有，不於根出，又非空生。何以故？若合時來，離當已滅，云何覺離？違、順二相，亦復如是。若從根出，必無離、合、違、順四相，則汝身知元無自性。必於空出，空自知覺，何關汝入？是故當知身入虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！譬如有人，勞倦則眠，睡熟便寤，覽塵斯憶，失憶為忘；是其顛倒生、住、異、滅，吸習中歸，不相踰越，稱意知根。兼意與勞，同是菩提瞪發勞相。因于生、滅二種妄塵，集知居中，吸撮內塵，見聞逆流，流不及地，名覺知性。此覺知性，離彼寤寐、生滅二塵，畢竟無體。如是阿難！當知如是覺知之根，非寤寐來，非生滅有，不於根出，亦非空生。何以故？若從寤來，寐即隨滅，將何為寐？必生時有，滅即同無，令誰受滅？若從滅有，生即滅無，孰知生者？若從根出，寤、寐二相隨身開合，離斯二體，此覺知者同於空花，畢竟無性。若從空生，自是空知，何關汝入？是故當知意入虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

Ānanda, it is like a person who presses two fingers firmly into his ears: the ear-faculty, strained, produces a ringing sound inside the head. Both the ear and the strain alike are marks of bodhi itself straining into fatigue. Dependent on the two false objects of motion and stillness, hearing arises in between them, taking hold of these objects — this is called the nature of hearing. This hearing, apart from those two objects of motion and stillness, has, in the end, no substance of its own. Just so, Ānanda: you should know that this hearing does not come from motion or stillness, does not come out of the faculty, and does not arise in empty space. Why? If it came from stillness, it should vanish along with motion, and so should not be able to hear motion; and if it came from motion, it should vanish along with stillness, and so should have no awareness of stillness. If it arose from the faculty itself, then, motion and stillness aside, the substance of hearing would have no nature of its own to begin with. And if it emerged from empty space, then, having hearing for its very nature, it would not be empty space at all — and besides, if space did its own hearing, what would that have to do with your own entrance of hearing? You should therefore know that the entrance of the ear is false — originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, it is like a person who inhales sharply through the nose until, over time, it becomes strained, so that he perceives a sensation of cold within the nose. Through that contact he distinguishes open from blocked, empty from solid, and so on to every scent, fragrant and foul. Both the nose and the strain alike are marks of bodhi itself straining into fatigue. Dependent on the two false objects of openness and blockage, smelling arises in between them, taking hold of these objects — this is called the nature of smelling. This smelling, apart from those two objects of openness and blockage, has, in the end, no substance of its own. You should know that this smelling does not come from openness or blockage, does not come out of the faculty, and does not arise in empty space. Why? If it came from openness, then blockage would vanish along with it — how then would blockage be known? And if it existed because of blockage, then in openness there would be no smelling — how then would the contacts of fragrance and foulness ever be made evident? If it arose from the faculty itself, then, openness and blockage aside, the substance of smelling would have no nature of its own to begin with. And if it emerged from empty space, this smelling ought to turn back and smell your own nose — and if space had its own smelling, what would that have to do with your own entrance of smelling? You should therefore know that the entrance of the nose is false — originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, it is like a person licking his own lips: licking them until worn and strained, if the person is ill he tastes bitterness, and if well, a faint sweetness. It is by sweetness and bitterness that this tongue-faculty is made evident, and when it is not moved, the bland is always there. Both the tongue and the strain alike are marks of bodhi itself straining into fatigue. Dependent on the two false objects of sweet-and-bitter and bland, knowing arises in between them, taking hold of these objects — this is called the nature that knows flavor. This nature that knows flavor, apart from those two objects — the sweet and bitter, and the bland — has, in the end, no substance of its own. Just so, Ānanda: you should know that this knowing that tastes bitterness and blandness does not come from sweetness or bitterness, does not exist because of blandness, does not come out of the faculty, and does not arise in empty space. Why? If it came from sweetness and bitterness, then with blandness that knowing would vanish — how then would blandness be known? And if it emerged from blandness, then with sweetness that knowing would be lost — how then would the two aspects, sweet and bitter, be known? If it arose from the tongue, then, sweetness and blandness and the object of bitterness necessarily aside, this faculty that knows flavor would have no nature of its own to begin with. And if it emerged from empty space, then space tastes of itself and it is not your mouth that knows — and if space did its own knowing, what would that have to do with your own entrance of tasting? You should therefore know that the entrance of the tongue is false — originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, it is like a person touching a cold hand to a hot one: if the cold is stronger, the hot hand turns cold; if the heat is stronger, the cold hand turns warm. In just this way, by the contact of this joining-awareness the knowing of separation is made evident; and if the interplay of forces is established, it is because of the contact of strain. Both the body and the strain alike are marks of bodhi itself straining into fatigue. Dependent on the two false objects of separation and joining, awareness arises in between them, taking hold of these objects — this is called the nature of sensing. This substance of sensing, apart from those objects — separation and joining, the disagreeable and the agreeable — has, in the end, no substance of its own. Just so, Ānanda: you should know that this sensing does not come from separation or joining, does not exist by the disagreeable or the agreeable, does not come out of the faculty, and does not arise in empty space. Why? If it came at the moment of joining, separation would already have vanished — how then would separation be sensed? And so likewise with the two aspects, disagreeable and agreeable. If it came out of the faculty, then, the four aspects of separation and joining, disagreeable and agreeable, necessarily aside, your body's knowing would have no nature of its own to begin with. And if it must emerge from empty space, then space does its own sensing, and what would that have to do with your own entrance of touch? You should therefore know that the entrance of the body is false — originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, it is like a person who, wearied, falls asleep, and, once well-rested, wakes; who, taking in an object, remembers it, and, losing that memory, forgets — this arising, abiding, changing, and ceasing, taken in through habituation, returns without ever overstepping its own bounds, and this is called the faculty of mind-cognition. Both the mind and the strain alike are marks of bodhi itself straining into fatigue. Dependent on the two false objects of arising and ceasing, knowing gathers in between them, drawing in the inner objects; seeing and hearing run counter to the current, and where the current cannot reach is called the nature of sensing and knowing. This nature of sensing and knowing, apart from those objects of waking and sleeping, arising and ceasing, has, in the end, no substance of its own. Just so, Ānanda: you should know that this faculty of sensing and knowing does not come from waking or sleeping, does not exist by arising or ceasing, does not come out of the faculty, and does not arise in empty space. Why? If it came from waking, it should vanish along with sleep — what then would do the sleeping? And if it must exist at the moment of arising, then at ceasing it is the same as nothing — who then would undergo the ceasing? If it existed from ceasing, then at arising ceasing is absent — who then would know the arising? If it came out of the faculty, then waking and sleeping, these two aspects, open and close along with the body; and apart from those two substances, this sensing and knowing is the same as a flower in the sky, in the end without any nature at all. And if it arose from empty space, then it is space's own knowing, and what would that have to do with your own entrance of mind? You should therefore know that the entrance of the mind is false — originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Reading note: Each of the six entrances gets a small, homely demonstration first — pressed fingers in the ears, rubbing the tongue against the lips, a cold hand against a warm one — engineering a sensation without any real outside object, precisely so that the "sense experience" it produces can be shown to have no independent origin: not the faculty, not the object, not space, not spontaneous. The method is identical to the five-aggregates analogies that closed fascicle 2, now run across all six senses in turn.

The Twelve Sense-Fields 十二處本如來藏妙真如性

The Buddha now takes each faculty together with its object — sight and form, hearing and sound, smell and fragrance, taste and flavor, touch and object touched, mind and mental phenomena — and asks, in each case, which one produces the other, or whether the sense travels to the object or the object travels to the sense.

Seeing and Form 色、眼

Chinese Source

「阿難！汝且觀此祇陀樹林及諸泉池，於意云何？此等為是色生眼見，眼生色相？阿難！若復眼根生色相者，見空非色，色性應銷；銷則顯發一切都無；色相既無，誰明空質？空亦如是。若復色塵生眼見者，觀空非色，見即銷亡；亡則都無，誰明空色？是故當知見與色、空俱無處所，即色與見二處虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ānanda, look now at this Jeta Grove with its trees and springs and pools — what do you think? Is it that the form gives rise to the eye's seeing, or that the eye gives rise to the appearance of form? Ānanda, if the eye-faculty gave rise to the appearance of form, then on seeing empty space, which is not form, the nature of form would be annulled; and being annulled, it would be revealed that there is nothing at all — and the appearance of form being absent, what would make the substance of space evident? The same holds for space as well. And if the object of form gave rise to the eye's seeing, then on looking at empty space, which is not form, the seeing itself would be annulled; and being annulled there would be nothing at all — what then would make space and form evident? You should therefore know that seeing and form, and empty space besides, have no location at all — the two fields of form and seeing are false, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Hearing and Sound, Smelling and Fragrance, Tasting and Flavor, Touching and Object, Mind and Mental Phenomena 聲、耳 · 香、鼻 · 味、舌 · 觸、身 · 法、意

「阿難！汝更聽此祇陀園中食辦擊鼓，眾集撞鐘，鐘鼓音聲前後相續。於意云何？此等為是聲來耳邊，耳往聲處？阿難！若復此聲來於耳邊，如我乞食室羅筏城，在祇陀林則無有我；此聲必來阿難耳處，目連、迦葉、應不俱聞？何況其中一千二百五十沙門，一聞鐘聲同來食處？若復汝耳往彼聲邊，如我歸住祇陀林中，在室羅城則無有我。汝聞鼓聲，其耳已往擊鼓之處；鐘聲齊出，應不俱聞？何況其中象、馬、牛、羊種種音響？若無來、往，亦復無聞？是故當知聽與音聲俱無處所，即聽與聲二處虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！汝又嗅此鑪中栴檀，此香若復然於一銖，室羅筏城四十里內同時聞氣。於意云何？此香為復生栴檀木，生於汝鼻，為生於空？阿難！若復此香生於汝鼻，稱鼻所生，當從鼻出；鼻非栴檀，云何鼻中有栴檀氣？稱汝聞香，當於鼻入，鼻中出香，說聞非義。若生於空，空性常恒，香應常在，何藉鑪中爇此枯木？若生於木，則此香質因爇成煙，若鼻得聞，合蒙煙氣？其煙騰空未及遙遠，四十里內云何已聞？是故當知香、鼻與聞俱無處所，即嗅與香二處虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！汝常二時眾中持鉢，其間或遇酥酪醍醐，名為上味。於意云何？此味為復生於空中？生於舌中？為生食中？阿難！若復此味生於汝舌，在汝口中秖有一舌，其舌爾時已成酥味，遇黑石蜜應不推移。若不變移，不名知味；若變移者，舌非多體，云何多味一舌之知？若生於食，食非有識，云何自知？又食自知，即同他食，何預於汝名味之知？若生於空，汝噉虛空，當作何味？必其虛空若作鹹味，既鹹汝舌亦鹹汝面，則此界人同於海魚。既常受鹹，了不知淡，若不識淡亦不覺鹹；必無所知，云何名味？是故當知味、舌與甞俱無處所，即甞與味二俱虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！汝常晨朝以手摩頭，於意云何？此摩所知，誰為能觸？能為在手、為復在頭？若在於手，頭則無知，云何成觸？若在於頭，手則無用，云何名觸？若各各有，則汝阿難應有二身。若頭與手，一觸所生，則手與頭當為一體；若一體者，觸則無成。若二體者，觸誰為在？在能非所，在所非能；不應虛空與汝成觸。是故當知覺、觸與身俱無處所，即身與觸二俱虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！汝常意中所緣善、惡、無記三性，生成法則；此法為復即心所生？為當離心別有方所？「阿難！若即心者，法則非塵，非心所緣，云何成處？若離於心別有方所，則法自性為知非知？知則名心，異汝非塵，同他心量。即汝即心，云何汝心更二於汝？若非知者，此塵既非色、聲、香、味、離、合、冷、煖及虛空相，當於何在？今於色、空都無表示，不應人間更有空外。心非所緣，處從誰立？是故當知法則與心俱無處所，則意與法二俱虛妄，本非因緣、非自然性。

Ānanda, listen again to the drum sounding in this Jeta Grove as the meal is prepared, and the bell struck to gather the assembly — the sounds of bell and drum following one another in succession. Does the sound come to your ear, or does your ear go out to where the sound is? Ānanda, if this sound comes to your ear — just as, when I beg my food in the city of Śrāvastī, there is no me in the Jeta Grove — then this sound must come to Ānanda's ear alone, and Maudgalyāyana and Kāśyapa should not hear it together with you. How much less the twelve hundred and fifty śramaṇas among them, who at one hearing of the bell all come together to the place of eating? And if your ear goes out to the side of that sound — just as, when I return and dwell in the Jeta Grove, there is no me in the city of Śrāvastī — then when you hear the drum, your ear has already gone to where the drum is struck, and when the bell sounds at the same moment you should not hear them both. How much less the many sounds among them of elephants and horses, oxen and sheep? And if there is neither a coming nor a going, then there would be no hearing at all. You should therefore know that hearing and sound have no location at all — the two fields of hearing and sound are false, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, smell again the sandalwood burning in the censer: if a single grain of it is lit, the scent is smelled throughout the forty li around Śrāvastī all at once. Does this fragrance arise from the sandalwood, from your nose, or from empty space? Ānanda, if this fragrance arose from your nose, then, being called what the nose produces, it should come out of the nose; but the nose is not sandalwood — how would there be a sandalwood scent inside the nose? And if you are said to smell the fragrance, it should enter at the nose; to have fragrance come out of the nose and call that smelling is not the right sense of the word. If it arose from empty space, space's nature is constant and the fragrance should be present always — what need then to burn this dry wood in the censer? And if it arose from the wood, then this fragrant substance becomes smoke by burning, and if the nose is to smell it, it should be by receiving the smoke; but the smoke rising into the air has not yet gone far — how is it already smelled forty li away? You should therefore know that fragrance, the nose, and the smelling of it have no location at all — the two fields of smelling and fragrance are false, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, at your two daily meal-times, holding your bowl among the assembly, you sometimes encounter ghee or clarified butter, called a superior flavor. Does this flavor arise in empty space, in the tongue, or in the food itself? Ānanda, if this flavor arose in your tongue, there is only one tongue in your mouth, and that tongue, having already become the flavor of butter, should not shift when it meets black rock-sugar. If it does not change and shift, it cannot be called knowing flavor; and if it does change and shift, the tongue is not many substances — how then does one tongue know many flavors? If the flavor arose in the food, the food has no consciousness — how would it know itself? And if the food knew of itself, that would be the same as someone else eating, and what would it have to do with what you call your knowing of flavor? If it arose in empty space, then chew on space and see what flavor it makes. And if space must have a salty flavor, then, having salted your tongue, it also salts your face, and the people of this world would be the same as the fish of the sea: always undergoing saltiness, they would never know the bland; and not recognizing the bland, they would not be aware of saltiness either — and knowing nothing at all, what would be called flavor? You should therefore know that flavor, the tongue, and the tasting of it have no location at all — the two fields of tasting and flavor are equally false, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, every morning you rub your head with your hand — what do you think? In this rubbing and its being felt, what is it that does the touching? Is the capacity to touch in the hand, or in the head? If it is in the hand, then the head has no knowing — how is a touch made? If it is in the head, then the hand is of no use — how is it called a touch? And if each of them has it, then you, Ānanda, should have two bodies. If head and hand arise from one single touch, then hand and head must be one substance; and if they are one substance, no touch can be made at all. And if they are two substances, in which of them does the touch reside? What is in the agent is not in the object, and what is in the object is not in the agent; and it will not do for empty space to make a touch together with you. You should therefore know that the sensation of touch, the object touched, and the body have no location at all — the two fields of body and touch are equally false, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, whatever your mind takes hold of among wholesome, unwholesome, and neutral phenomena, giving rise to their patterns as mental objects — do these mental objects arise from mind itself, or do they have some location apart from mind? Ānanda, if they are mind itself, then the mental object is not an object at all; and not being what mind takes hold of, how does it constitute a field? And if, apart from mind, it has some separate location, then is the mental object's own nature knowing, or not knowing? If it knows, it is called mind; and being other than you, and not an object, it is the same as another being's own measure of mind. And if it is you, and is your mind, how is it that your mind is a second thing beside you? But if it is not knowing, then, since this object is neither form nor sound, fragrance nor flavor, neither separation nor joining, neither cold nor warm, nor any aspect of empty space — where is it to be? Among form and space there is now nothing to point to, and it will not do to say that there is some further place among human beings outside of space itself. Mind having nothing it takes hold of, from what is the field established? You should therefore know that mental objects and mind have no location at all — the two fields of mind and mental phenomena are equally false, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

The Eighteen Elements 十八界本如來藏妙真如性

For each sense-field, the Buddha now asks after the consciousness that arises from it: does eye-consciousness, for instance, come from the eye, or from form? Each of the six is shown to be groundless in exactly the same way.

Eye-Consciousness, Ear-Consciousness 眼識界 · 耳識界

Chinese Source

「阿難！如汝所明眼、色為緣生於眼識，此識為復因眼所生、以眼為界？因色所生、以色為界？阿難！若因眼生，既無色、空，無可分別，縱有汝識，欲將何用？汝見又非青、黃、赤、白，無所表示，從何立界？若因色生，空無色時汝識應滅，云何識知是虛空性？若色變時，汝亦識其色相遷變；汝識不遷，界從何立？從變則變，界相自無；不變則恒。既從色生，應不識知虛空所在？若兼二種眼、色共生，合則中離、離則兩合，體性雜亂，云何成界？是故當知眼、色為緣生眼識界，三處都無，則眼與色及色界三，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！又汝所明耳、聲為緣生於耳識，此識為復因耳所生、以耳為界？因聲所生、以聲為界？「阿難！若因耳生，動、靜二相既不現前，根不成知，必無所知。知尚無成，識何形貌？若取耳聞，無動、靜故，聞無所成。云何耳形雜色、觸塵名為識界？則耳識界復從誰立！若生於聲，識因聲有、則不關聞，無聞則亡聲相所在。識從聲生，許聲因聞而有聲相，聞應聞識，不聞非界。聞則同聲，識已被聞，誰知聞識？若無知者，終如草木，不應聲、聞雜成中界。界無中位，則內、外相復從何成？是故當知耳、聲為緣生耳識界，三處都無，則耳與聲及聲界三，本非因緣、非自然性。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ānanda, as you understand it, eye and form, taken as conditions, give rise to eye-consciousness — but does this consciousness arise dependent on the eye, taking the eye as its basis, or dependent on form, taking form as its basis? Ānanda, if it arises because of the eye, then, form and space being absent, there is nothing at all to be distinguished, and even granting you a consciousness, what use would you put it to? And your seeing, besides, is neither blue nor yellow, red nor white, and has nothing by which it can be indicated — from what, then, would its basis be established? If it arises because of form, then when space is without form your consciousness should be extinguished — how then does it know that this is the nature of space? And if, when form alters, you know as well that the appearance of form has shifted and changed, while your consciousness does not shift — from what is its basis established? If it changes when form changes, then the aspect of a basis is of itself absent; and if it does not change, then it is constant — and arising from form as it does, it should not know where space is. And if it arises from both together, eye and form, then in their joining there is a gap in the middle, and in their parting there is a joining of the two, so that substance and nature are thrown into confusion — how does that make a basis? You should therefore know that eye-consciousness, said to arise from eye and form as conditions, has no location in any of the three — eye, form, and the resulting element of consciousness are, all three, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, as you further understand it, ear and sound, taken as conditions, give rise to ear-consciousness — but does this consciousness arise dependent on the ear, taking the ear as its basis, or dependent on sound, taking sound as its basis? Ānanda, if it arises because of the ear, then, the two aspects of motion and stillness not being present, the faculty cannot become a knowing, and there is necessarily nothing known. And knowing not yet being established, what shape or appearance would the consciousness have? If you take it as the ear's hearing, then, without motion and stillness, no hearing can be made at all — how then can the ear's own shape, which is a patch of color and an object of touch, be called an element of consciousness? From what, then, is the element of ear-consciousness set up? If it arises from sound, then the consciousness exists because of sound and has nothing to do with hearing; and with no hearing, the very place of sound's own aspect is lost as well. And if consciousness arises from sound, then, granting that sound has its aspect of sound because of hearing, hearing ought to hear the consciousness itself; and what is not heard is not the element. But if it is heard, it is the same as sound — and the consciousness having already been heard, who is it that knows this heard consciousness? And if there is no one who knows, then one is in the end like grass or wood. It will not do for sound and hearing to combine and make a middle element; and the element having no middle position, from what would the aspects of inner and outer be constituted? You should therefore know that ear-consciousness, said to arise from ear and sound as conditions, has no location in any of the three — ear, sound, and the resulting element of consciousness are, all three, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Nose-, Tongue-, Body-, and Mind-Consciousness 鼻識界 · 舌識界 · 身識界 · 意識界

「阿難！又汝所明鼻、香為緣生於鼻識，此識為復因鼻所生、以鼻為界？因香所生、以香為界？「阿難！若因鼻生，則汝心中以何為鼻？為取肉形雙爪之相？為取嗅知動搖之性？若取肉形，肉質乃身，身知即觸，名身非鼻，名觸即塵；鼻尚無名，云何立界？若取嗅知，又汝心中以何為知？以肉為知，則肉之知元觸非鼻。以空為知，空則自知，肉應非覺。如是則應虛空是汝，汝身非知，今日阿難應無所在。以香為知，知自屬香，何預於汝？若香、臭氣必生汝鼻，則彼香、臭二種流氣，不生伊蘭及栴檀木。二物不來，汝自嗅鼻為香為臭？臭則非香、香應非臭。若香、臭二俱能聞者，則汝一人應有兩鼻，對我問道有二阿難，誰為汝體？若鼻是一，香、臭無二，臭既為香，香復成臭，二性不有，界從誰立？若因香生，識因香有，如眼有見不能觀眼，因香有故應不知香。知則非生，不知非識，香非知有，香界不成。識不知香，因界則非從香建立。既無中間，不成內、外，彼諸聞性畢竟虛妄！是故當知鼻、香為緣生鼻識界，三處都無，則鼻與香及香界三，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！又汝所明舌、味為緣生於舌識，此識為復因舌所生、以舌為界？因味所生、以味為界？「阿難！若因舌生，則諸世間甘蔗、烏梅、黃連、石鹽、細辛、薑、桂、都無有味。汝自嘗舌為甜、為苦？若舌性苦，誰來嘗舌？舌不自嘗，孰為知覺？舌性非苦，味自不生，云何立界？若因味生，識自為味，同於舌根，應不自嘗，云何識知是味非味？又一切味非一物生，味既多生，識應多體！識體若一，體必味生，鹹、淡、甘、辛、和合俱生諸變異相，同為一味，應無分別！分別既無則不名識，云何復名舌味識界？不應虛空生汝心識！舌、味和合，即於是中元無自性，云何界生？是故當知舌、味為緣生舌識界，三處都無，則舌與味及舌界三，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！又汝所明身、觸為緣生於身識，此識為復因身所生、以身為界？因觸所生、以觸為界？「阿難！若因身生，必無合、離二覺觀緣，身何所識？若因觸生，必無汝身，誰有非身知合、離者？阿難！物不觸知，身知有觸；知身即觸，知觸即身；即觸非身，即身非觸，身、觸二相元無處所。合身即為身自體性，離身即是虛空等相，內、外不成，中云何立？中不復立，內、外性空，即汝識生從誰立界？是故當知身、觸為緣生身識界，三處都無，則身與觸及身界三，本非因緣、非自然性。

「阿難！又汝所明意、法為緣生於意識，此識為復因意所生、以意為界？因法所生、以法為界？「阿難！若因意生，於汝意中必有所思發明汝意，若無前法，意無所生，離緣無形，識將何用？又汝識心、與諸思量、兼了別性，為同為異？同意即意，云何所生？異意不同，應無所識！若無所識，云何意生？若有所識，云何識意？唯同與異二性無成，界云何立？若因法生，世間諸法不離五塵。汝觀色法及諸聲法、香法、味法、及與觸法，相狀分明以對五根，非意所攝。汝識決定依於法生，汝今諦觀法法何狀？若離色空、動靜、通塞、合離、生滅，越此諸相，終無所得。生則色、空諸法等生，滅則色、空諸法等滅；所因既無，因生有識，作何形相？相狀不有，界云何生？是故當知意、法為緣生意識界，三處都無，則意與法及意界三，本非因緣、非自然性。」

Ānanda, as you further understand it, nose and fragrance, taken as conditions, give rise to nose-consciousness — but does this consciousness arise dependent on the nose, taking the nose as its basis, or dependent on fragrance, taking fragrance as its basis? Ānanda, if it arises because of the nose, then in your own mind, what do you take the nose to be? Do you take the fleshly form with its pair of claws? Or do you take the nature that knows by smelling and stirs? If you take the fleshly form, then flesh is body-substance, and the body's knowing is touch: called body, it is not nose; called touch, it is an object — and the nose having no name at all, how is a basis set up? And if you take the knowing by smelling, then in your own mind, what do you take the knowing to be? If flesh is the knowing, then the knowing of flesh is from the first touch and not nose. If space is the knowing, then space knows of itself and flesh should have no awareness at all — and in that case space is you, and your body has no knowing, and Ānanda today should be nowhere at all. And if fragrance is the knowing, then the knowing belongs to fragrance, and what concern is it of yours? Moreover, if the airs of fragrance and foulness must be produced in your nose, then those two flowing airs, the fragrant and the foul, are not produced from the ilanda tree or from sandalwood — and with those two things absent, smell your own nose: is it fragrant, or foul? What is foul is not fragrant, and what is fragrant should not be foul. And if you are able to smell them both, then you alone should have two noses, and there would be two Ānandas asking me about the Way — which of them would be your own substance? And if the nose is one, and fragrance and foulness are not two, so that the foul has become fragrant and the fragrant has become foul in turn, then neither of the two natures exists, and from what is the basis set up? If it arises because of fragrance, then the consciousness exists because of fragrance; and just as the eye, having sight, cannot look at the eye, so, existing because of fragrance, it should not know fragrance. If it knows, it is not produced from it; and if it does not know, it is not consciousness at all. Fragrance does not exist by being known, and the element of fragrance is not constituted; and a consciousness that does not know fragrance is not an element established on the basis of fragrance. There being no middle, inner and outer are not constituted, and all those natures of smelling are, in the end, false. You should therefore know that nose-consciousness has no location in any of the three — nose, fragrance, and the resulting element, are, all three, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, as you further understand it, tongue and flavor, taken as conditions, give rise to tongue-consciousness — but does this consciousness arise dependent on the tongue, taking the tongue as its basis, or dependent on flavor, taking flavor as its basis? Ānanda, if it arises because of the tongue, then all the sugarcane and dark plums, the goldthread and rock salt, the wild ginger and ginger and cinnamon in the world would have no flavor at all. Taste your own tongue: is it sweet, or bitter? If the tongue's nature is bitter, who is it that comes to taste the tongue? And the tongue not tasting itself, what is it that has the knowing? But if the tongue's nature is not bitter, then flavor does not arise of itself — how is a basis set up? If it arises because of flavor, then the consciousness is itself a flavor; and being in that the same as the tongue-faculty, it should not taste itself — how then does it know what is a flavor and what is not? Moreover, all flavors are not produced from one single thing; and flavors being produced from many things, consciousness should have many substances. And if the substance of consciousness is one, that substance is necessarily produced from flavor, and then salty and bland, sweet and pungent, and all the altered aspects produced by their blending, would alike be one single flavor, and there should be no distinguishing at all. And distinguishing being absent, it is not called consciousness — how then is it further called the element of tongue-and-flavor-consciousness? It will not do for empty space to produce your mind's consciousness; and in the joining of tongue and flavor, right there in the middle there is from the first no nature of its own — how is a basis produced? You should therefore know that tongue-consciousness has no location in any of the three — tongue, flavor, and the resulting element, are, all three, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, as you further understand it, body and touch, taken as conditions, give rise to body-consciousness — but does this consciousness arise dependent on the body, taking the body as its basis, or dependent on touch, taking touch as its basis? Ānanda, if it arises because of the body, then, the two conditions of awareness and observation — joining and separation — being necessarily absent, what would the body be conscious of? And if it arises because of touch, then your body is necessarily absent, and who is there that, without being a body, knows joining and separation? Ānanda, a thing does not know by touch; it is the body's knowing that has touch. What knows the body is touch, and what knows touch is the body; yet what is touch is not body, and what is body is not touch, and the two aspects, body and touch, have from the first no location at all. Joined to the body, it is the body's own substance and nature; parted from the body, it is the same as the aspect of empty space. Inner and outer not being constituted, how is a middle established? And the middle not being established, and the natures of inner and outer being empty, then when your consciousness arises, from what is its basis established? You should therefore know that body-consciousness has no location in any of the three — body, touch, and the resulting element, are, all three, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Ānanda, as you further understand it, mind and mental phenomena, taken as conditions, give rise to mind-consciousness — but does this consciousness arise dependent on the mind, taking mind as its basis, or dependent on mental phenomena, taking those phenomena as its basis? Ānanda, if it arises because of mind, then in your mind there must be something thought that makes your mind evident; and if there is no phenomenon before it, mind has nothing from which to arise, and apart from its conditions it has no form at all — what use would the consciousness be? Moreover, your conscious mind, together with all your reckoning and your nature of discerning — are they the same, or different? If they are the same as mind, they are mind, and how are they what mind produces? And if they are different from mind and not the same, there should be nothing they are conscious of; and if there is nothing they are conscious of, how are they produced from mind at all? But if there is something they are conscious of, how is that a consciousness of mind? Neither of the two natures, the same and the different, being established, how is a basis set up? If it arises because of mental phenomena, then the phenomena of the world are not apart from the five objects. Look at the phenomena of form, and those of sound, of fragrance, of flavor, and of touch: their aspects are distinct and they stand over against the five faculties, and they are not what mind gathers in. Your consciousness is settled to arise on the basis of phenomena; so look closely now — what aspect does each of these phenomena have? Set aside form and space, motion and stillness, openness and blockage, joining and separation, arising and ceasing: go beyond all these aspects, and in the end there is nothing to be found. When they arise, form, space, and all such phenomena arise along with them; when they cease, form, space, and all such phenomena cease along with them. Its cause being absent, a consciousness existing because of such arising — what form and aspect would it make? And the form and aspect not existing, how is a basis produced? You should therefore know that mind-consciousness has no location in any of the three — mind, mental phenomena, and the resulting element, are, all three, originally neither a matter of causes and conditions, nor of any spontaneous nature.

Reading note: Together, the six entrances, twelve fields, and eighteen elements make up the three classical Abhidharma schemes for cataloguing the whole of experience — from the narrowest (just the six internal faculties) to the widest (faculty, object, and consciousness, eighteen items in all). Running the identical refutation across all three schemes, rather than just one, forecloses the objection that some other, uncovered category might still harbor a real, independent origin.

Ānanda's Objection: What About the Four Great Elements? 阿難執因緣和合以請

Chinese Source

阿難白佛言：「世尊！如來常說和合因緣，一切世間種種變化，皆因四大和合發明。云何如來因緣、自然二俱排擯？我今不知斯義所屬，唯垂哀愍，開示眾生中道了義無戲論法！」

Ānanda said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, the Tathāgata has always taught that all the world's various transformations come about through the combination of causes and conditions — through the combining of the four great elements. Why, then, does the Tathāgata now reject both causes-and-conditions and spontaneity alike? I do not know where this teaching belongs. I beg only that you, in compassion, reveal to sentient beings the middle way, the teaching of complete meaning, free of empty argument."

At that time the World-Honored One said to Ānanda: "You once turned away from the teachings of the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, resolving to seek unsurpassed bodhi — and so I now reveal to you the ultimate truth. Why, then, do you again entangle yourself in the world's empty arguments and deluded talk of causes and conditions? Though you are learned, you are like a person who can recite the names of medicines but cannot recognize the real medicine when it is right in front of him — the Tathāgata truly calls this pitiable! Listen closely now: I shall distinguish and reveal it for you, and shall let those who cultivate the Great Vehicle in time to come penetrate the real aspect as well." The Seven Great Elements 七大本如來藏妙真如性

The Buddha now answers Ānanda's challenge directly, taking each of the four classical "great elements" — earth, fire, water, wind — together with space, seeing, and consciousness, and showing, through a vivid analogy for each, that none of them is produced by "combination" in the way Ānanda assumes. Each is, instead, the tathāgatagarbha itself.

Earth: Splitting a Dust Mote Down to Emptiness 地大

Ānanda, if this near-void mote, split, becomes empty space, then you must hold that space gives birth to the aspect of form. You have just asked whether it is by combination that the world's transforming aspects are produced: then look at this single near-void mote — how much space is combined to make it exist? It will not do for near-void motes to combine and make a near-void mote. And for a near-void mote to be split and enter into space, how many aspects of form are combined to make that space? If it is form that joins, then the joined form is not space; and if it is space that joins, then the joined space is not form. Form can still be split — but how is space joined at all? You do not know from the first that, in the tathāgatagarbha, the nature of form is true emptiness, and the nature of emptiness is true form — pure and unmoved from the start, pervading the whole Dharma-realm, appearing according to each sentient being's mind and capacity to know. It manifests according to karma; the unknowing world mistakes this for a matter of causes and conditions or of spontaneity — all of it merely the discriminating, calculating mind; there is only talk, with no real substance in it at all.

Fire: The Drill and the Burning-Glass 火大

「阿難！火性無我，寄於諸緣。汝觀城中未食之家，欲炊爨時，手執陽燧日前求火。阿難！名和合者，如我與汝一千二百五十比丘今為一眾；眾雖為一，詰其根本各各有身，皆有所生氏族名字。如舍利弗、婆羅門種，優盧頻螺、迦葉波種，乃至阿難、瞿曇種姓。阿難！若此火性因和合有，彼手執鏡於日求火，此火為從鏡中而出？為從艾出？為於日來？阿難！若日來者，自能燒汝手中之艾，來處林木皆應受焚？若鏡中出，自能於鏡出然于艾，鏡何不鎔？紆汝手執尚無熱相，云何融泮？若生於艾，何藉日、鏡、光明相接，然後火生？汝又諦觀，鏡因手執，日從天來，艾本地生，火從何方遊歷於此？日、鏡相遠，非和非合，不應火光無從自有。汝猶不知如來藏中性火真空、性空真火，清淨本然周遍法界，隨眾生心應所知量。阿難！當知世人一處執鏡一處火生，遍法界執滿世間起，起遍世間，寧有方所？循業發現，世間無知，惑為因緣及自然性；皆是識心分別計度，但有言說，都無實義！

Ānanda, fire has no self of its own; it depends on various conditions to appear. Observe a household in the city that has not yet cooked its meal, wishing to light the stove, holding a burning-glass up to the sun to kindle fire. Ānanda, as for what is called combination: it is as I and you and the twelve hundred and fifty bhikṣus are now one assembly; and though the assembly is one, question its root and each has a body of his own, and each has the clan and family name into which he was born — Śāriputra of a brahmin clan, Uruvilvā of the Kāśyapa clan, and Ānanda too of the Gautama line. Ānanda, if this nature of fire exists by combination, then when that hand holds the mirror to the sun to seek fire, does this fire come out of the mirror, out of the tinder, or from the sun itself? Ānanda, if it comes from the sun, then it is able of itself to burn the tinder in your hand — and every tree and thicket it passed through on the way should be set alight as well. If it comes out of the mirror, then it is able of itself to come out of the mirror and kindle the tinder — why, then, does the mirror not melt? Even the hand holding it shows no sign of heat — how, then, would the mirror liquefy? And if it is produced from the tinder, why must it wait on sun and mirror and the meeting of their light before fire arises at all? Look closely once more: the mirror is held in the hand, the sun comes from the sky, the tinder is produced from the ground — from what quarter did the fire travel to arrive here? The sun and the mirror are far apart from one another; there is neither a true mixing nor a true combining between them, and yet it would be wrong to say the fire's light simply has no source at all. You still do not know that, in the tathāgatagarbha, the nature of fire is true emptiness, and the nature of emptiness is true fire — pure and unmoved from the start, pervading the whole Dharma-realm, appearing according to each sentient being's mind and capacity to know. Ānanda, know that where a person in the world holds up a mirror in one place, fire arises in that one place; and were it held up throughout the Dharma-realm, it would arise filling the whole world — and arising throughout the world, how could it have any fixed location? It manifests according to karma; the unknowing world mistakes this for a matter of causes and conditions or of spontaneity — all of it merely the discriminating, calculating mind; there is only talk, with no real substance in it at all.

Water: The Moon-Pearl That Gathers Dew 水大

「阿難！水性不定，流息無恒。如室羅城迦毘羅仙、斫迦羅仙、及鉢頭摩訶薩多等諸大幻師，求太陰精用和幻藥。是諸師等，於白月晝，手執方諸，承月中水。此水為復從珠中出？空中自有？為從月來？阿難！若從月來，尚能遠方令珠出水，所經林木皆應吐流。流則何待方諸所出？不流明水非從月降。若從珠出，則此珠中常應流水；何待中宵承白月晝？若從空生，空性無邊，水當無際；從人洎天，皆同滔溺，云何復有水、陸、空行？汝更諦觀，月從天陟，珠因手持，承珠水盤本人敷設，水從何方流注於此？月珠相遠，非和非合，不應水精無從自有。汝尚不知如來藏中性水真空、性空真水，清淨本然周遍法界，隨眾生心應所知量。一處執珠一處水出，遍法界執滿法界生，生滿世間，寧有方所？循業發現，世間無知，惑為因緣及自然性；皆是識心分別計度，但有言說，都無實義！

Ānanda, the nature of water is unfixed: it flows or rests without constancy. The great sorcerers of Śrāvastī — the sage Kapila, the sage Cakra, Padma-mahāsattva and the rest — seeking the essence of the moon to mix into their magical potions, hold up a moon-crystal in the hand on the days of the waxing moon, catching the water that gathers from the moon. Does this water come out of the pearl, exist already in empty space, or come from the moon itself? Ānanda, if it comes from the moon, then, being able to make the pearl give out water from so great a distance, every tree and thicket it passed through should spit out a flow as well; and if they do flow, what need is there to wait on the moon-crystal for the water to come out? And if they do not flow, it is clear the water did not come down from the moon. If it comes out of the pearl, then water should always be flowing from this pearl — what need to catch it at midnight on the days of the waxing moon? And if it arises from empty space, space's nature is boundless and the water should have no limit; from human beings up to the gods, all alike would be drowned, and how would there still be travel by water, by land, and through the air? Look closely once more: the moon rises in the sky, the pearl is held in the hand, and the basin that catches the pearl's water was set out by a person — from what quarter did the water pour to arrive here? The moon and the pearl are far apart from one another; there is neither a true mixing nor a true combining between them, and yet it would be wrong to say the water's essence simply has no source at all. You still do not know that, in the tathāgatagarbha, the nature of water is true emptiness, and the nature of emptiness is true water — pure and unmoved from the start, pervading the whole Dharma-realm, appearing according to each sentient being's mind and capacity to know. Where a pearl is held up in one place, water comes out in that one place; and were it held up throughout the Dharma-realm, water would be produced filling the whole Dharma-realm — and being produced filling the world, how could it have any fixed location? It manifests according to karma; the unknowing world mistakes this for a matter of causes and conditions or of spontaneity — all of it merely the discriminating, calculating mind; there is only talk, with no real substance in it at all.

Wind: A Corner of a Monastic Robe 風大

「阿難！風性無體，動靜不常。汝常整衣入於大眾，僧伽梨角動及傍人，則有微風拂彼人面。此風為復出袈裟角？發於虛空？生彼人面？阿難！此風若復出袈裟角，汝乃披風；其衣飛搖，應離汝體！我今說法會中垂衣，汝看我衣風何所在？不應衣中有藏風地。若生虛空，汝衣不動，何因無拂？空性常住，風應常生；若無風時虛空當滅。滅風可見，滅空何狀！若有生滅，不名虛空；名為虛空，云何風出？若風自生彼拂之面，從彼面生，當應拂汝！自汝整衣，云何倒拂？汝審諦觀，整衣在汝，面屬彼人，虛空寂然不參流動，風自誰方鼓動來此？風、空性隔，非和非合；不應風性無從自有。汝宛不知如來藏中性風真空、性空真風，清淨本然周遍法界，隨眾生心應所知量。阿難！如汝一人微動服衣有微風出，遍法界拂滿國土生，周遍世間，寧有方所？循業發現，世間無知，惑為因緣及自然性；皆是識心分別計度，但有言說，都無實義！

Ānanda, wind has no fixed substance; its moving and stillness are not constant. When you straighten your robe to enter the assembly, the corner of your outer robe brushes against the person beside you, and a faint breeze touches that person's face. Does this wind come out of the corner of the robe, arise from empty space, or come from that person's own face? Ānanda, if this wind comes out of the corner of the robe, then you are wearing wind, and the robe would fly and shake and should leave your body altogether. I am now letting my own robe hang here in this Dharma-assembly: look at my robe — where in it is the wind? It will not do for the robe to have a place inside it where wind is stored. If the wind arises from empty space, then when your robe does not move, why is there no brushing? Space's nature abides constantly, and so wind should be produced constantly; and when there is no wind, space itself ought to be extinguished. The extinction of wind can be seen — but what would the extinction of space look like? And if space had arising and ceasing, it would not be called empty space; while if it is called empty space, how does wind come out of it? And if the wind is produced of itself from that face it brushed, then, being produced from that face, it ought to brush you instead — why, when it is you who straightened the robe, does it brush the other way? Examine it closely: the straightening of the robe is yours, the face belongs to that person, and empty space is still and takes no part in the flowing motion — from what quarter, then, did the wind stir and come here? Wind and space are, by nature, separate from each other; there is neither a true mixing nor a true combining between them, and yet it would be wrong to say this nature of wind simply has no source at all. You truly do not know that, in the tathāgatagarbha, the nature of wind is true emptiness, and the nature of emptiness is true wind — pure and unmoved from the start, pervading the whole Dharma-realm, appearing according to each sentient being's mind and capacity to know. Ānanda, as from your one person slightly stirring the robe a slight wind comes out, so were it stirred throughout the Dharma-realm, wind would be produced brushing the whole land — and pervading the world, how could it have any fixed location? It manifests according to karma; the unknowing world mistakes this for a matter of causes and conditions or of spontaneity — all of it merely the discriminating, calculating mind; there is only talk, with no real substance in it at all.

Space: Digging a Well 空大

「阿難！空性無形，因色顯發。如室羅城去河遙處，諸剎利種及婆羅門、毘舍、首陀，兼頗羅墮、旃陀羅等，新立安居，鑿井求水。出土一尺，於中則有一尺虛空；如是乃至出土一丈，中間還得一丈虛空。虛空淺深，隨出多少。此空為當因土所出？因鑿所有？無因自生？阿難！若復此空無因自生，未鑿土前何不無礙？唯見大地逈無通達。若因土出，則土出時應見空入。若土先出無空入者，云何虛空因土而出？若無出入，則應空、土元無異因；無異則同，則土出時空何不出？若因鑿出，則鑿出空，應非出土；不因鑿出，鑿自出土，云何見空？汝更審諦、諦審、諦觀，鑿從人手隨方運轉，土因地移，如是虛空因何所出？鑿、空虛實不相為用，非和非合，不應虛空無從自出。若此虛空性圓周遍本不動搖，當知現前地、水、火、風均名五大，性真圓融，皆如來藏，本無生滅。阿難！汝心昏迷，不悟四大元如來藏，當觀虛空為出為入？為非出入？汝全不知如來藏中性覺真空、性空真覺，清淨本然周遍法界，隨眾生心應所知量。「阿難！如一井空、空生一井，十方虛空亦復如是，圓滿十方，寧有方所？循業發現，世間無知，惑為因緣及自然性；皆是識心分別計度，但有言說，都無實義！

Ānanda, space has no form of its own; it is revealed only through form. Consider, in a place far from the river in Śrāvastī, the kṣatriyas and brahmins, the vaiśyas and śūdras, together with the Bhāradvājas and the caṇḍālas and the rest, newly settled and digging a well in search of water. For every foot of earth removed, a foot of empty space appears in its place; and so on, up to removing a full fathom of earth, revealing a full fathom of empty space in turn — the depth of the space keeping exact pace with how much earth is removed. Does this space come from the earth, from the digging, or does it arise spontaneously, with no cause at all? Ānanda, if this space arose spontaneously with no cause, why was it not unobstructed before the earth was dug? One saw only solid ground, remote and with nothing passing through. If it comes out because of the earth, then as the earth came out one should see space go in. And if the earth came out first and no space went in, how is space said to come out because of earth? But if there is no going out and coming in, then space and earth would from the first have no different cause; and having no difference they are the same — so why, when the earth came out, did space not come out along with it? If it comes out because of the digging, then the digging brings out space and should not bring out earth; and if it does not come out because of the digging, then the digging brings out earth of itself — how, then, is space seen? Examine again, and closely examine, and closely look: the digging follows a person's hand and is turned this way and that, and the earth is moved because of the ground — from what, then, does this space come out? Digging and space, one solid and one empty, cannot act upon each other; there is neither a true mixing nor a true combining between them, and yet it would be wrong to say this space simply has no source at all. If this nature of space is round and pervading and originally does not stir, then know that the present earth, water, fire, and wind are alike called the five great elements, their natures truly round and fused, all of them the tathāgatagarbha, originally without arising or ceasing. Ānanda, your mind is in a daze and does not awaken to the four great elements being, from the first, the tathāgatagarbha. You should consider: does space come out, or go in, or neither come out nor go in? You do not know at all that, in the tathāgatagarbha, the nature of awareness is true emptiness, and the nature of emptiness is true awareness — pure and unmoved from the start, pervading the whole Dharma-realm, appearing according to each sentient being's mind and capacity to know. Ānanda, as one well's space, and space giving rise to one well, so it is with the space of the ten directions: filling the ten directions completely, how could it have any fixed location? It manifests according to karma; the unknowing world mistakes this for a matter of causes and conditions or of spontaneity — all of it merely the discriminating, calculating mind; there is only talk, with no real substance in it at all.

Seeing: Neither the Same as Light and Space, Nor Different 見大

「阿難！見覺無知，因色空有。如汝今者在祇陀林，朝明、夕昏，設居中宵，白月則光、黑月便暗；則明暗等因見分析。此見為復與明、暗相并太虛空為同一體？為非一體？或同非同？或異非異？阿難！此見若復與明與暗及與虛空元一體者，則明與暗二體相亡，暗時無明，明時非暗。若與暗一，明則見亡；必一於明，暗時當滅，滅則云何見明見暗？若暗明殊，見無生滅，一云何成？若此見精與暗與明非一體者，汝離明暗及與虛空，分析見元作何形相？離明、離暗及離虛空，是見元同龜毛、兔角。明、暗、虛空三事俱異，從何立見？明、暗相背，云何或同？離三元無，云何或異？分空、分見本無邊畔，云何非同？見暗、見明性非遷改，云何非異？汝更細審、微細審、詳審、諦審觀，明從太陽，暗隨黑月，通屬虛空，壅歸大地，如是見精因何所出？見覺、空頑，非和非合，不應見精無從自出。若見、聞、知性圓周遍，本不動搖，當知無邊不動虛空并其動搖地、水、火，風，均名六大。性真圓融，皆如來藏，本無生滅。阿難！汝性沈淪，不悟汝之見、聞、覺，知本如來藏。汝當觀此見、聞、覺、知，為生、為滅？為同、為異？為非生滅？為非同異？汝曾不知如來藏中性見覺明、覺精明見，清淨本然周遍法界，隨眾生心應所知量。如一見根見周法界，聽、嗅、嘗、觸、覺觸覺知，妙德瑩然，遍周法界，圓滿十虛，寧有方所？循業發現，世間無知，惑為因緣及自然性。皆是識心分別計度，但有言說，都無實義！

Ānanda, the awareness of seeing has, in itself, no content of its own — it exists because of form and space. As you are now here in the Jeta Grove: bright in the morning and dim at evening, and should you stay till midnight, with a white moon there is light and with a dark moon there is darkness — and brightness and darkness and the rest are analyzed by means of seeing. Is this seeing one and the same substance as brightness, darkness, and the great void of space together, or not the same substance? Is it, perhaps, both the same and not the same, or both different and not different? Ānanda, if this seeing were from the first one substance with brightness, with darkness, and with empty space, then brightness and darkness, as two substances, annul each other: in darkness there is no brightness, and in brightness there is no darkness. If it were one with darkness, then in brightness the seeing would be lost; and if it must be one with brightness, then in darkness it would be extinguished — and being extinguished, how would it see brightness and see darkness at all? And if darkness and brightness differ while seeing has no arising or ceasing, how is "one substance" constituted? But if this essence of seeing is not one substance with darkness and with brightness, then set aside brightness and darkness and empty space, and analyze the source of seeing: what form and aspect does it make? Apart from brightness, apart from darkness, and apart from space, this seeing is from the first the same as a tortoise's hair or a rabbit's horn. Brightness, darkness, and space, these three things, being wholly different — from which of them is seeing set up? Brightness and darkness stand opposed to each other — how then could it be "both the same"? And the three being from the first absent — how then could it be "both different"? Divide space, divide seeing, and there is from the first no dividing line between them — how could it be called "not the same"? Seeing darkness and seeing brightness — this seeing-nature itself never changes — so how could it be called "not different"? Examine still more finely, and finely examine, and examine in detail, and closely examine and look: brightness comes from the sun, darkness follows the dark of the moon, what passes through belongs to space, and what blocks goes back to the great earth — from what, then, does this essence of seeing come out? Seeing-awareness and dull space are neither truly mixed nor truly combined, and yet it would be wrong to say this essence of seeing simply comes out of nowhere at all. If the natures of seeing, hearing, and knowing are round and pervading and originally do not stir, then know that boundless unmoving space, together with the stirring earth, water, fire, and wind, are alike called the six great elements, their natures truly round and fused, all of them the tathāgatagarbha, originally without arising or ceasing. Ānanda, your nature is sunk under, and you do not awaken to your seeing, hearing, sensing, and knowing being, from the first, the tathāgatagarbha. You should consider this seeing, hearing, sensing, and knowing: does it arise, or cease? Is it the same, or different? Is it neither arising nor ceasing? Is it neither the same nor different? You have never known that, in the tathāgatagarbha, the nature of seeing is true awareness and bright, the essence of awareness is true, bright seeing — pure and unmoved from the start, pervading the whole Dharma-realm, appearing according to each sentient being's mind and capacity to know. As one faculty of seeing sees throughout the Dharma-realm, so it is with hearing, smelling, tasting, and touching, with the sensing of touch and the knowing of awareness: their wondrous virtue is lustrous, pervading the whole Dharma-realm, filling the ten directions of emptiness completely — how could they have any fixed location? It manifests according to karma; the unknowing world mistakes this for a matter of causes and conditions or of spontaneity — all of it merely the discriminating, calculating mind; there is only talk, with no real substance in it at all.

Consciousness: Recognizing the Assembly 識大

「阿難！識性無源，因於六種根塵妄出。汝今遍觀此會聖眾，用目循歷，其目周視，但如鏡中無別分析；汝識於中次第標指，此是文殊、此富樓那、此目犍連、此須菩提、此舍利弗。此識了知，為生於見？為生於相？為生虛空？為無所因突然而出？阿難！若汝識性生於見中，如無明、暗及與色、空，四種必無，元無汝見；見性尚無，從何發識？若汝識性生於相中、不從見生，既不見明、亦不見暗，明暗不矚，即無色、空；彼相尚無，識從何發？若生於空，非相、非見，非見無辯，自不能知明、暗、色、空；非相滅緣，見、聞、覺、知無處安立。處此二非，空非同無，有非同物；縱發汝識，欲何分別？若無所因突然而出，何不日中別識明月？汝更細詳、微細詳審，見託汝睛，相推前境，可狀成有，不相成無，如是識緣因何所出？識動、見澄，非和非合；聞、聽、覺、知亦復如是，不應識緣無從自出。若此識心本無所從，當知了別見、聞、覺、知，圓滿湛然，性非從所；兼彼虛空、地、水、火、風，均名七大。性真圓融，皆如來藏，本無生滅。阿難！汝心麁浮，不悟見、聞發明了知，本如來藏。汝應觀此六處識心，為同、為異？為空、為有？為非同異？為非空有？汝元不知如來藏中性識明知、覺明真識，妙覺湛然，遍周法界，含吐十虛，寧有方所？循業發現，世間無知，惑為因緣及自然性；皆是識心分別計度，但有言說，都無實義。」

Ānanda, consciousness has no single source; it arises falsely, dependent on the six pairs of sense-faculties and their objects. Look now across this whole assembly of noble ones — as your eye passes over them, roving freely, it sees them at first exactly as a mirror does, without distinguishing anything at all. Then your consciousness, one by one, picks out and names each one in turn: this is Mañjuśrī, this is Pūrṇa, this is Maudgalyāyana, this is Subhūti, this is Śāriputra. Does this act of recognizing consciousness arise from the seeing, from the appearances themselves, from empty space, or does it emerge suddenly, with no cause at all? Ānanda, if your consciousness-nature arose within seeing, then, without brightness and darkness and form and space — these four being necessarily absent — there is from the first no seeing of yours at all; and the nature of seeing not existing, from what would consciousness arise? And if your consciousness-nature arose within the appearances and not from seeing, then, since it neither sees brightness nor sees darkness, and brightness and darkness not being looked upon there is no form and no space, that appearance not existing, from what would consciousness arise? If it arose in empty space, then, being neither the appearance nor the seeing, and not being seeing there is nothing to distinguish with, so of itself it could not know brightness, darkness, form, or space; and not being the appearance, its conditions are extinguished, and seeing, hearing, sensing, and knowing have no place to be established. Standing between these two negations, space is not the same as nothing, and existence is not the same as a thing — and even granting that your consciousness arose, what would it distinguish? And if it emerges suddenly with no cause at all, why do you not pick out the bright moon in the middle of the day? Examine yet more in detail, and finely and in detail examine: seeing is lodged in your eye, the appearance is pushed forward as the scene before you, what can be given a shape becomes existence and what has no aspect becomes nothing — from what, then, does this condition of consciousness come out? Consciousness stirs and seeing is clear; they are neither truly mixed nor truly combined — and so likewise with hearing and listening, sensing and knowing — and yet it would be wrong to say the condition of consciousness simply comes out of nowhere at all. If this conscious mind is from the first from nowhere, then know that discerning, and seeing, hearing, sensing, and knowing, are full and utterly still, and their nature is not from anywhere; and together with empty space and earth, water, fire, and wind they are alike called the seven great elements, their natures truly round and fused, all of them the tathāgatagarbha, originally without arising or ceasing. Ānanda, your mind is coarse and unsettled, and you do not awaken to seeing and hearing making knowing evident as being, from the first, the tathāgatagarbha. You should consider these six places of conscious mind: are they the same, or different? Are they empty, or existent? Are they neither the same nor different? Are they neither empty nor existent? You have simply never known that, in the tathāgatagarbha, the nature of consciousness is bright knowing, and true consciousness is the brightness of awareness itself — wondrously aware and utterly still, pervading the whole Dharma-realm, containing and giving forth the ten directions of emptiness — how could it have any fixed location? The unknowing world mistakes this for a matter of causes and conditions or of spontaneity — all of it merely the discriminating, calculating mind; there is only talk, with no real substance in it at all.

Reading note: The refrain "the nature of X is true emptiness, and the nature of emptiness is true X" is the fascicle's structural key. It is not saying that earth, fire, water, and the rest simply dissolve into nothingness — that would be the "denying" extreme fascicle 1's terminology already ruled out. It says that each element's true nature already is empty in the specific sense of having no fixed, independent origin of its own — and it is exactly this absence of any fixed origin that lets each element appear, without contradiction, "wherever karma calls it forth," pervading the whole Dharma-realm rather than being locked into one particular place. Seeing and consciousness are added to the traditional four great elements (plus space) precisely to make the point unmistakable: mind itself is not somehow exempt from this analysis, standing outside it as a special case — it is one more of the seven, subject to exactly the same discovery.

At that time Ānanda and the whole assembly, receiving the Tathāgata's subtle and wondrous instruction, felt body and mind grow entirely open, free of every obstruction. Each one of them came to know, for themselves, that mind pervades the ten directions, and saw the empty space of the ten directions the way one sees a leaf held in the palm of the hand. Everything that exists in the whole world is nothing but the wondrous, bright, original mind of bodhi; the essence of mind, perfectly full and complete, encloses and contains the ten directions within itself. Looking back at the body born of their own parents, it seemed like a single speck of dust blown about somewhere within the boundless space of the ten directions, now appearing, now vanishing; like a single bubble floating on a vast, calm ocean, arising and ceasing with no origin to be found. Clearly he knew for himself that he had gained the original wondrous mind, ever abiding and unceasing. He bowed to the Buddha, joined his palms, gained what he had never had before, and before the Tathāgata spoke this verse in praise of the Buddha:

"Wondrously still, all-holding, unmoving Honored One — Śūraṅgama King, rare in this world! You have dissolved away my inverted thinking of a hundred million kalpas, so that without passing through an asaṃkhya of ages I gain the Dharma-body. May I now win the fruit and become a Jewel-King, and return to deliver beings numberless as the Ganges' sands; this deep mind I offer up to lands numberless as dust — and that is what is called repaying the Buddha's kindness. Reading note: This verse — often chanted on its own in Chinese monasteries independently of the sūtra — marks the turning point of the whole work. Fascicles 1–3 have been almost entirely dialectical: refuting false locations for the mind, and then showing category after category to be groundless in the same way. Ānanda's vow here ("if a single sentient being has not yet become Buddha, I will not take final nirvāṇa") is the bodhisattva vow proper, and signals that the sūtra is about to turn from analysis to practice — fascicle 4 takes up Pūrṇa's questions about how the world of delusion arises in the first place, and the path toward the Buddha's answer in the famous teaching on the twenty-five methods of perfect penetration.
