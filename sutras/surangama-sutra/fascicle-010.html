Fascicle 10 · 大佛頂如來密因修證了義諸菩薩萬行首楞嚴經卷第十

The Final Twenty States, and the Five Layers of False Thought

The sūtra's last fascicle completes the fifty states of demonic interference — ten deviant philosophies arising in the aggregate of formations, and ten deviant final attainments arising in the aggregate of consciousness — before the Buddha explains the five layers of false thought underlying all five aggregates, compares the merit of teaching this sūtra to any imaginable act of material generosity, and closes with the assembly's rejoicing.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: fascicle 10 complete — sūtra translation complete Source: Taishō T19, no. 945 Covers: the entire fascicle — the aggregates of formations and consciousness through the sūtra's close

About This Fascicle

Fascicle 9 covered the first twenty of the fifty states of demonic interference — the aggregates of form and sensation in full, plus the ten heavenly-demon states opening the aggregate of perception. Fascicle 10 completes the teaching: ten deviant philosophical positions that can crystallize out of genuine insight into the aggregate of formations, and ten deviant "final attainments" that can arise at the very threshold of the aggregate of consciousness — each one a genuine partial realization mistaken for the whole path's completion. The Buddha then steps back to explain the single mechanism behind all fifty states across all five aggregates: five layers of false thought, one nested inside the next, and closes the entire sūtra with a direct comparison between two kinds of merit and the assembly's departure. This is the tenth and final fascicle of the Śūraṅgama Sūtra, completing Ru-Yi's translation of the bare sūtra text in full.

This page is Ru-Yi's own translation, made directly from the Chinese text preserved by CBETA (public domain). It does not reproduce or paraphrase the Buddhist Text Translation Society's existing English edition; see the translation notes for terminology, and the companion Jiǎngyì project for Master Yuanying's commentary, which continues in far greater detail across 24 fascicles.

Ru-Yi Translation

Translated in the first year of the Shénlóng era of the great Tang dynasty [705 CE], the year in the cycle being yǐsì, on the twenty-third day of the fifth month, by the central-Indian monk Paramiti, at the Zhìzhǐ place of practice in Guangzhou. Reading note: This formal colophon, preserved only at the head of the sūtra's final fascicle, is the historical record behind the translation account given in fascicle 1's introduction: Paramiti's oral rendering into Chinese, Meghaśikha's role as interpreter, and Fang Rong — an exiled statesman at the time — polishing the resulting prose into the literary Chinese this sūtra is known for.

The Aggregate of Formations: Its Boundary 行陰區宇

Chinese Source

阿難！彼善男子修三摩提想陰盡者，是人平常夢想銷滅，寤寐恒一。覺明虛靜猶如晴空，無復麁重前塵影事，觀諸世間大地山河，如鏡鑑明，來無所粘，過無蹤跡。虛受照應，了罔陳習，唯一精真；生滅根元，從此披露。……猶如野馬熠熠清擾，為浮根塵究竟樞穴；此則名為行陰區宇。若此清擾熠熠元性，性入元澄，一澄元習，如波瀾滅化為澄水，名行陰盡，是人則能超眾生濁。觀其所由，幽隱妄想以為其本。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Ānanda, once this good person, cultivating samādhi, has exhausted the aggregate of perception, their ordinary dreaming thoughts dissolve away, so that waking and sleeping become exactly the same state. Awakened brightness becomes empty and still, like a clear sky, with no more coarse shadow-images of past objects remaining; observing the great earth, mountains, and rivers of the world becomes like a bright mirror reflecting things — what comes leaves no trace clinging to it, what passes leaves no residue behind. Receiving and responding entirely empty of content, every trace of old habitual tendency dissolved, only a single, essential truth remains — and it is from this point that the very root of arising and ceasing itself is finally laid bare. ... [Every kind of being throughout the ten directions is seen at once, though not yet each one's individual thread of destiny —] like heat-shimmer flickering over open country, the finest, most restless hinge underlying every coarse sense-faculty and sense-object. This is called the boundary of the aggregate of formations. Once this flickering, restless original nature settles into its own original stillness, and that stillness absorbs its own original habitual tendency, like waves subsiding into still water, this is called the exhaustion of the aggregate of formations, and such a person can transcend the turbidity of sentient existence itself. Examining its cause, this aggregate is rooted in hidden, deeply concealed false thought."

Ten Wild Interpretations in the Aggregate of Formations 行陰十種禪那狂解

The Buddha warns that a practitioner whose concentrated mind stays clear will not be troubled by the ten kinds of heavenly demon at this stage — but may still fall into one of ten deviant philosophical conclusions, each one reasoning correctly from a genuine but partial observation of the aggregate of formations to a false, absolutized theory about the nature of existence itself. Each is traced to a different school of non-Buddhist thought.

1. Two theories of no cause 二無因論 — seeing eighty thousand kalpas of beings cycling through birth and death but nothing beyond that span, one concludes the world arose from no cause at all; or, seeing that crows have always been black and swans always white, that humans are born upright and animals born sideways, one concludes nothing was ever caused to become what it now is, and nothing else — including one's own future buddhahood — could be caused either.
2. Four theories of universal permanence 四遍常論 — from observing, through deepening concentration, that mind and its objects cycle without loss across twenty, forty, or eighty thousand kalpas, or that the four elements' nature endures unchanged, or that the root of mind, intellect, and consciousness never truly disperses, one concludes that mind, the elements, or consciousness itself must be universally permanent.
3. One part permanent, one part impermanent 一分無常一分常論 — taking one's own unmoving, all-pervading awareness as a permanent, universal self while regarding everything arising and ceasing within that awareness as impermanent; or regarding only those world-systems currently undergoing destruction as impermanent while others remain permanent; or taking the subtlest thread of one's own mind, imperceptibly present throughout the ten directions, as the permanent "self" while the body's birth and death remain impermanent; or, having exhausted perception yet still observing formations flowing on, concluding that the flowing itself is permanent while form, sensation, and perception — already extinguished — were impermanent.
4. Four theories of finitude 四有邊論 — concluding that past and future are finite while the continuously flowing present mind is infinite; or that the eighty thousand kalpas one can observe are finite while what lies beyond them, unseen, is infinite; or that one's own boundless knowing extends everywhere while others' knowing — never directly experienced — must be finite by comparison; or, examining the aggregate of formations as empty, concluding that every being is exactly half arising and half ceasing at any moment, so that the world itself is half finite and half infinite.
5. Endless evasive equivocation 四種顛倒不死矯亂遍計虛論 — having observed change so closely that arising, ceasing, continuity, and increase or decrease all blur into one another, one begins answering every question with a strategic non-answer: "both arising and ceasing, both existing and not existing, both increasing and decreasing" to any question at all; or a single fixed answer, always "no," regardless of the question; or always "yes," regardless of the question; or a shifting mixture of both, designed specifically to be unfalsifiable and impossible to pin down through further questioning.
6. Sixteen forms of "existence after death" 死後有相 — from observing the aggregate of formations' endless flow without yet exhausting it, one concludes the self continues to exist, in some specific form, after death — reasoning variously that form itself is the self, that the self contains all lands within it, that external conditions belong to the self, or that the self persists within ongoing formations — sixteen permutations built from these four positions crossed with the four remaining aggregates, each concluding that afflictions and bodhi can coexist forever, side by side, neither ever touching the other.
7. Eight forms of "no existence after death" 死後無相 — having already seen form, sensation, and perception each individually dissolve into nothing, one concludes that death itself must mean the utter absence of any form whatsoever afterward — eight permutations across the remaining aggregates, concluding that even nirvāṇa and cause and effect are ultimately nothing more than empty names, ending finally in total extinction.
8. Eight forms of "neither existence nor non-existence" 死後俱非 — since formations are still observed flowing while form, sensation, and perception have already ceased, both "existence" and "non-existence" seem to undercut each other at once; taking whichever aggregate one examines as neither straightforwardly existent nor straightforwardly nonexistent, one concludes that "after death" is neither one thing nor the other — a position the sūtra says leaves its holder simply confused about the future, with nothing further to say.
9. Seven theories of annihilation 七斷滅論 — concluding that the body, desire itself, suffering itself, the bliss of the higher heavens, or the equanimity of the highest formless states, each in turn, simply ceases entirely and is not followed by anything further — seven variations across seven possible destinies, each concluding in total annihilation with nothing left over.
10. Five theories of "present" nirvāṇa 五現涅槃論 — mistaking the desire realm itself for the true "basis of return" out of longing for its vivid clarity, or mistaking each of the four dhyāna heavens in turn — free of worry, free of suffering, full of joy, or free of both joy and suffering — for the true, unconditioned nirvāṇa, simply because each level feels, by comparison, more settled and secure than the one below it.
Chinese Source (closing warning)

阿難！如是十種禪那狂解，皆是行陰用心交互，故現斯悟。眾生頑迷，不自忖量，逢此現前，以迷為解，自言登聖，大妄語成，墮無間獄。汝等必須將如來語，於我滅後傳示末法，遍令眾生覺了斯義。無令心魔自起深孽，保持覆護，消息邪見，教其身心，開覺真義，於無上道不遭枝岐。勿令心祈得少為足，作大覺王清淨標指。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Ānanda, these ten wild interpretations in meditation are all produced by the interaction of effort and the aggregate of formations, and so these particular realizations appear. Beings, stubborn and confused, fail to measure themselves honestly; meeting these states, they mistake confusion for genuine understanding, declaring themselves to have ascended to sagehood — committing the offense of great false speech, and falling into the unrelenting hell for it. You must take these very words of the Tathāgata and, after my passing, transmit them throughout the age of the Dharma's decline, so that every being everywhere may awaken to this teaching. Do not let a self-generated demon of the mind take root and grow — instead protect and guide practitioners, dissolve wrong views at their source, and teach them, in both body and mind, to awaken to the true meaning, so that they meet no forked path on the unsurpassed way. Do not let their own hearts settle for some small attainment as though it were complete — hold up instead the pure standard of the great, awakened king himself."

Reading note: Unlike the aggregates of form and sensation, where the danger is mistaking a genuine experience for sagehood, and unlike perception, where the danger is an external demon exploiting a craving, the danger here is purely intellectual: each of these ten positions is a coherent, carefully reasoned philosophical system, built from real meditative observation, that goes wrong only in the final step of over-generalizing a partial, temporary view into an absolute metaphysical claim. Yuanying's companion commentary, when it reaches this fascicle, identifies several of these ten with specific historical schools of Indian philosophy the Buddha's own contemporaries would have recognized by name.

The Aggregate of Consciousness: Its Boundary 識陰區宇

Chinese Source

阿難！彼善男子修三摩提，行陰盡者，諸世間性幽清擾動同分生機倏然墮裂，沈細綱紐補特伽羅酬業深脈感應懸絕。於涅槃天將大明悟，如鷄後鳴；瞻顧東方已有精色。六根虛靜，無復馳逸，內外湛明，入無所入，深達十方十二種類受命元由。……此則名為識陰區宇。若於群召已獲同中，銷磨六門合開成就，見聞通鄰，互用清淨；十方世界及與身心如吠瑠璃，內外明徹，名識陰盡，是人則能超越命濁。觀其所由，罔象虛無顛倒妄想以為其本。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Ānanda, once this good person, cultivating samādhi, has exhausted the aggregate of formations, the hidden, subtle, restless hinge underlying every being's shared basis of life suddenly splits apart and falls away — the deep, fine web of thread by which each individual being responds to and receives the fruit of its own karma is abruptly severed. At this point, on the very verge of nirvāṇa's own dawning, it is like the moment just before daybreak: looking toward the east, one already glimpses the first traces of color. The six sense-faculties become empty and still, no longer racing outward; both within and without, everything becomes translucent, entering a state where there is nothing further left to enter, and one comes to understand deeply, throughout the ten directions, the root cause behind all twelve categories of birth receiving their particular span of life. ... This is called the boundary of the aggregate of consciousness. Once, amid this shared origin common to every summoned kind of being, the six sense-gates are ground down until opening and closing become one and the same, seeing and hearing interchange and interpenetrate in perfect purity, and the worlds of the ten directions, together with one's own body and mind, become like clear glass, transparent within and without — this is called the exhaustion of the aggregate of consciousness, and such a person can transcend the turbidity of life-span itself. Examining its cause, this aggregate is rooted in formless, empty, inverted false thought."

Ten Deviant Final Attainments in the Aggregate of Consciousness 識陰十種

At this final threshold, the Buddha warns, the danger is no longer wild philosophical theory but something subtler still: a genuine, advanced practitioner mistaking one specific insight — real as far as it goes — for the completed path itself, and stopping there. Each of the ten is traced to a specific school of non-Buddhist practice, or, in the last two cases, to a permanently fixed śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha who never turns toward the greater vehicle at all.

1. Taking a "cause" as the source 因所所執 — concluding that some single, dark, causeless principle underlies and produces everything, and taking that "returned-to" cause itself as ultimate reality. Falls in with Kapila's own followers and their "dark principle" (冥諦).
2. Taking the "capable" self as universal 能非能執 — regarding what one has "returned to" as one's own body, and concluding that every being throughout all twelve categories flows outward from oneself. Falls in with Maheśvara and his followers, who manifest a boundless body.
3. Taking dependence as permanence 常非常執 — suspecting that body and mind flow out from whatever one has "returned to," and that all of space arises from it as well, then taking this generative ground itself as a permanent, unborn, unceasing body — permanence mistakenly discovered in the very midst of arising and ceasing. Falls in with the Freely-Abiding Heaven's own devas.
4. Taking universal knowing as absolute 知無知執 — concluding, from the sense that knowing pervades everything without exception, that even grass and trees possess sentience no different from human beings, and that humans, upon death, become grass and trees in turn — knowledge applied indiscriminately, without any real distinction at all. Falls in with the followers of Vasiṣṭha and Śaṇḍilya, who hold that everything is sentient.
5. Taking worship of the elements as the path 生無生執 — having gained free interchange among the sense-faculties, one turns to reverence the elements themselves — seeking fire's brightness, delighting in water's purity, loving wind's constant motion, contemplating earth's stability — and takes devotion to these elements as the very cause of permanence. Falls in with the various fire-worshipping Kāśyapas and water-worshipping brahmins, who labor in body and mind to serve fire and honor water, seeking escape from birth and death through these austerities.
6. Taking emptiness itself as the final refuge 歸無歸執 — mistaking a merely conceptual, empty voidness — a bare absence within understanding rather than a positive extinguishing of every process — as the true, ultimate refuge to be returned to. Falls in with the Śūnyapuṣpas of the No-Conception Heaven.
7. Clinging to bodily permanence 貪非貪執 — fixating on preserving this very body forever, taking its present refined, subtle state as proof it need never decline or pass away. Falls in with Asita and other seekers of extended life.
8. Indulging worldly pleasure out of fear of its loss 真無真執 — observing that life is mutually interconnected with worldly entanglement, and, fearing that entanglement's complete exhaustion, deliberately sitting amid a "lotus palace" of one's own conjuring, surrounding oneself with treasures and consorts, indulging the mind without restraint. Falls in with beings like Vajra-Piercing King, and gives rise to the very seed of heavenly demons.
9. Stopping at the śrāvaka's own cessation 圓精應心，成趣寂果 — correctly discerning refined from coarse within this understanding of life itself, correctly resolving cause and effect, yet seeking only direct correspondence with the four noble truths — seeing suffering, cutting off its origin, realizing cessation, cultivating the path — and, upon reaching cessation, simply stopping there rather than advancing further. Falls in among fixed-nature śrāvakas, monks of little learning caught in overreaching pride.
10. Stopping at the pratyekabuddha's own tranquil clarity 圓覺㳷心，成湛明果 — refining this same interfused, pure, awakened brightness to its subtlest, most wondrous point, and, upon establishing this state as "nirvāṇa," simply stopping there rather than advancing further. Falls in among fixed-nature pratyekabuddhas, solitary realizers who never turn their minds toward the greater vehicle.
Chinese Source (closing warning)

阿難！如是十種禪那，中途成狂，因依迷惑，於未足中生滿足證，皆是識陰用心交互，故生斯位。眾生頑迷，不自忖量，逢此現前，各以所愛先習迷心而自休息；將為畢竟所歸寧地，自言滿足無上菩提。大妄語成，外道邪魔所感業終，墮無間獄。聲聞、緣覺，不成增進。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Ānanda, in each of these ten states, meditation goes astray midway through, deluded by relying on some particular condition, giving rise to a false sense of completion at a point that is not yet complete at all — all of it produced by the interaction of effort and the aggregate of consciousness, and so these particular positions arise. Beings, stubborn and confused, fail to measure themselves honestly; meeting these states, each settles into whatever confused mind their own prior inclination favors, taking it as the ultimate resting place, and declaring themselves to have fulfilled the unsurpassed bodhi — committing the offense of great false speech. Whether drawn by non-Buddhist teachings or deviant demonic influence, once their allotted karma is exhausted, they fall into the unrelenting hell; śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas caught here likewise fail to advance any further."

Consciousness Exhausted: The Path to Wondrous Enlightenment 識陰盡 · 圓滿菩提

Chinese Source

汝等存心秉如來道，將此法門，於我滅後傳示末世，普令眾生覺了斯義。無令見魔自作沈孽，保綏哀救，消息邪緣，令其身心入佛知見，從始成就不遭歧路。如是法門，先過去世恒沙劫中微塵如來乘此心開，得無上道。識陰若盡，則汝現前諸根互用。從互用中，能入菩薩金剛乾慧。圓明精心於中發化，如淨瑠璃內含寶月。如是乃超十信、十住、十行、十迴向、四加行心、菩薩所行金剛十地、等覺圓明，入於如來妙莊嚴海；圓滿菩提，歸無所得！

Ru-Yi Translation

"You must hold fast to the Tathāgata's own path, and, after my passing, transmit this teaching throughout the age of the Dharma's decline, so that every being everywhere awakens to this meaning. Do not let a view-generated demon take root in one's own mind — instead protect, comfort, and rescue practitioners, dissolving deviant conditions at their source, and lead them, in both body and mind, into the Buddha's own knowledge and vision, so that they reach completion from the very start without meeting a single forked path. This very teaching is the one by which countless buddhas, numerous as dust motes across kalpas as many as the sands of the Ganges in ages past, opened their own minds and attained the unsurpassed path. Once the aggregate of consciousness is exhausted, all your sense-faculties presently before you interchange freely with one another. From this free interchange, you become able to enter the bodhisattvas' own vajra-like dry wisdom. This perfect, bright, essential mind then unfolds its own transformation from within, like a precious moon held within pure, clear lapis lazuli. In just this way you pass beyond the ten faiths, the ten abidings, the ten practices, the ten transferences, the four aids to penetration, the bodhisattvas' own vajra-like ten grounds, and equal enlightenment's own perfect brightness, entering at last the Tathāgata's own ocean of wondrous adornment — perfect, complete bodhi, arriving finally at a place where there is nothing further to attain at all!"

Reading note: "Arriving at a place where there is nothing further to attain" closes the whole fifty-state teaching on exactly the note it opened on: every single one of the fifty states, however genuine or however deviant, was still something "gained" and clung to. The completed path, by contrast, is described here in purely negative terms — not a fifty-first attainment stacked on top of the rest, but the exhaustion of the very impulse to attain anything at all.

Closing Warning, and the Protection of the Mantra 結勸持咒

Chinese Source

此是過去先佛世尊奢摩他中、毘婆舍那覺明分析微細魔事。魔境現前，汝能諳識，心垢洗除，不落邪見。陰魔銷滅，天魔摧碎，大力鬼神褫魄逃逝，魑魅、魍魎、無復出生。直至菩提，無諸少乏。下劣增進，於大涅槃，心不迷悶。若諸末世愚鈍眾生，未識禪那，不知說法，樂修三昧；汝恐同邪，一心勸令持我佛頂陀羅尼呪。若未能誦，寫於禪堂、或帶身上，一切諸魔所不能動。汝當恭欽十方如來究竟修進最後垂範！

Ru-Yi Translation

"This is the detailed analysis, made by every past buddha and World-Honored One within śamatha and vipaśyanā, of these subtle demonic affairs. When a demonic state appears, if you are able to recognize it clearly, the defilements of your mind are washed away, and you do not fall into wrong views. The demons rooted in your own aggregates dissolve; heavenly demons are shattered; powerful ghosts and spirits lose their nerve and flee; mountain goblins and nature-spirits find no further opening to arise at all. You proceed straight on to bodhi, lacking nothing whatsoever. Even those of lesser capacity continue to advance, their minds never confused or troubled regarding the great nirvāṇa.

"But if, in the final age, there are beings dull and slow of understanding, who do not yet recognize true meditation or know how to explain the Dharma, yet still love to cultivate samādhi — fearing they may fall into deviant paths without realizing it, urge them with your whole heart to hold the dhāraṇī of the Buddha's own crown. Even if they cannot yet recite it, let them write it out and keep it in the meditation hall, or wear it on the body — and no demon whatsoever will be able to move them. You should hold in the deepest reverence this final teaching, the ultimate guidance for cultivation and advancement handed down by every Tathāgata of the ten directions."

Ānanda's Question, and the Five Layers of False Thought 五重妄想

Chinese Source

阿難即從坐起，聞佛示誨，頂禮欽奉，憶持無失。於大眾中重復白佛：「如佛所言『五陰相中，五種虛妄為本想心』，我等平常未蒙如來微細開示。又此五陰為併銷除？為次第盡？如是五重詣何為界？唯願如來發宣大慈，為此大眾清明心目，以為末世一切眾生作將來眼！」

Ru-Yi Translation

Ānanda rose at once from his seat, and, having heard the Buddha's teaching, bowed reverently, taking it fully to heart without the slightest loss. In the midst of the assembly he addressed the Buddha once more: "As the Buddha has said, within the marks of the five aggregates, there are five kinds of false thought serving as their root — but we have never before received the Tathāgata's own detailed explanation of this. Are these five aggregates removed all at once, or exhausted one after another in sequence? And where, exactly, does the boundary lie between each of these five layers? I beg only that the Tathāgata, in great compassion, open our minds and eyes clearly on this point, to serve as a guiding light for every being of the age to come."

Chinese Source

佛告阿難：「精真妙明本覺圓淨，非留死生及諸塵垢；乃至虛空皆因妄想之所生起，斯元本覺妙明真精，妄以發生諸器世間。如演若多迷頭認影。妄元無因，於妄想中立因緣性；迷因緣者，稱為自然。彼虛空性猶實幻生，因緣、自然，皆是眾生妄心計度。……是故如來與汝發明，五陰本因同是妄想。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "The essential, true, wondrously bright original awakening is perfectly pure of itself, retaining nothing of birth, death, or any worldly defilement at all — even empty space itself arises only because of false thought. It is this same original awakening, wondrously bright, essentially true, that falsely gives rise to every world of physical containers, exactly as Yajñadatta, in his confusion, mistook his own reflection for his lost head. Falseness has no cause of its own to begin with; it is only within false thought that the very principle of causation is established. And one who, in turn, remains confused about causation itself calls whatever remains simply 'spontaneous.' Yet even that quality of emptiness is itself already something falsely conjured — both 'caused' and 'spontaneous' are nothing but calculations made by the deluded minds of sentient beings. ... This is exactly why the Tathāgata reveals to you that the root cause of all five aggregates alike is false thought."

First: The Aggregate of Form — Solid False Thought 堅固第一妄想

The body itself first arose from the false thought of one's parents; had one's own mind not itself been a kind of thought, it could never have come to receive life within that thought in the first place. Just as thinking of something sour brings saliva to the mouth, and thinking of standing at a height makes the sole of the foot ache, even though no cliff-edge and no actual sour fruit are anywhere present — if one's own body were not itself made of exactly this same insubstantial stuff, how could mere talk of something sour produce real saliva in the mouth? This present physical body is therefore called the solid, first false thought.

Second: The Aggregate of Sensation — Empty, Bright False Thought 虛明第二妄想

The very thought of standing at a height, just described, can make this body genuinely feel a sour ache — sensation arising because of thought, in turn moving the physical body itself. The way one is presently driven, moment to moment, by what feels beneficial and what feels harmful, is called the empty, bright, second false thought.

Third: The Aggregate of Perception — Interfusing False Thought 融通第三妄想

Thought and reflection drive this physical body to act — yet the body itself is not made of the same stuff as thought; how, then, does it come to be moved by thought into taking on so many different images? Mind gives rise to an idea, and the body takes on its corresponding form; awake, this is called thought; asleep, it becomes dreams. This churning of thought and idea, stirring false feeling into motion, is called the interfusing, third false thought.

Fourth: The Aggregate of Formations — Hidden False Thought 幽隱第四妄想

The process of transformation never rests; it operates continuously, secretly shifting things along — nails grow, hair lengthens, vital energy is spent, the face wrinkles, day replacing night without one's ever quite noticing it happen. Ānanda, if this were not truly you, how could your own body be changing this way? And if it truly is you, why are you not aware of it happening? This ceaseless flow of your own formations, moment after moment never stopping, is called the hidden, fourth false thought.

Fifth: The Aggregate of Consciousness — Formless, Empty, Inverted False Thought 罔象虛無第五顛倒細微精想

That essential, bright, seemingly unmoving stillness one takes to be one's own constant nature never actually goes beyond the functions of seeing, hearing, sensing, and knowing. If it were truly, essentially real, it should admit no trace of habituated falseness at all — yet why is it that some object glimpsed once, years ago, can be entirely forgotten, and then, on suddenly seeing it again, be recognized at once, the memory perfectly intact, with nothing ever actually lost? Within this seemingly still, unmoving essence, then, every single moment is quietly being conditioned and stored — who could possibly count them all? Ānanda, you should know: this apparent stillness is not truly real at all — it is like a swiftly flowing river that looks, from a distance, perfectly calm, simply because the current moves too fast to see, not because there is no current at all. If this were not itself the very root of thought, how could it still be susceptible to habituation by thought at all? Unless your six sense-faculties open and close, interchanging freely with one another, this false thought will never, at any point, be extinguished. This is why what churns, unnoticed, within your present seeing, hearing, sensing, and knowing is called the formless, empty, fifth, inverted, most subtle and essential false thought.

Chinese Source

阿難！是五受陰，五妄想成。汝今欲知因界淺深，唯色與空，是色邊際；唯觸及離，是受邊際；唯記與忘，是想邊際；唯滅與生，是行邊際；湛入合湛，歸識邊際。此五陰元重疊生起，生因識有，滅從色除。理則頓悟，乘悟併銷；事非頓除，因次第盡。我已示汝劫波巾結，何所不明再此詢問？汝應將此妄想根元心得開通，傳示將來末法之中諸修行者，令識虛妄深厭自生，知有涅槃不戀三界。

"Ānanda, these five aggregates one clings to are all formed from these five false thoughts. If you now wish to know the relative depth of each one's own domain: form and emptiness alone mark the outer edge of the aggregate of form; contact and its absence alone mark the edge of sensation; remembering and forgetting alone mark the edge of perception; ceasing and arising alone mark the edge of formations; and returning into stillness, merging fully with that stillness, marks the edge of consciousness. These five aggregates arise layered, one upon another — their arising is rooted in consciousness, and their cessation must proceed by removing form first. In principle, this can be awakened to all at once, and, riding that single awakening, all five may dissolve together; but in actual practice, they are not removed all at once — they are exhausted one by one, in sequence. I have already shown you, in the parable of the knotted cloth, exactly this same point — what remains unclear, that you should ask again? You should take this understanding of the very root of false thought and open it up fully in your own mind, then transmit it to every practitioner of the age of the Dharma's decline yet to come, so that they recognize falseness for what it is, grow deeply weary of its arising within themselves, and, knowing that nirvāṇa exists, no longer cling with longing to the three realms."

Reading note: This closing teaching resolves the whole fascicle's structure into a single image: five layers of false thought, each nested inside the one before it — solid, then empty-bright, then interfusing, then hidden, then formless-empty — precisely matching the five aggregates the fifty demonic states were sorted by. The "understand all at once, but exhaust it one by one" principle directly recalls the six-knotted-cloth demonstration from fascicle 5–6 (the sūtra's own cross-reference here, "劫波巾結"): sudden insight into the nature of the knot does not, by itself, physically untie it — each knot still has to be worked loose in the order it was tied.

The Sūtra's Close: A Comparison of Merit, and the Assembly's Departure 校量功德 · 流通分

Chinese Source

阿難！若復有人遍滿十方所有虛空盈滿七寶，持以奉上微塵諸佛，承事供養，心無虛度。於意云何？是人以此施佛因緣得福多不？」阿難答言：「虛空無盡，珍寶無邊，……是福云何更有邊際！」佛告阿難：「諸佛如來語無虛妄，若復有人，身具四重、十波羅夷，瞬息即經此方他方阿鼻地獄，乃至窮盡十方無間靡不經歷。能以一念將此法門於末劫中開示未學，是人罪障應念銷滅。變其所受地獄苦因，成安樂國，得福超越前之施人，百倍、千倍、千萬億倍，如是乃至算數譬喻所不能及。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Ānanda, suppose someone were to fill the whole of empty space throughout the ten directions with the seven treasures, and offer it all up to buddhas as numerous as dust motes, honoring and making offerings to each one without a single moment wasted — what do you think? Would such a person, through this cause of giving to buddhas, gain great merit?" Ānanda answered: "Empty space itself is endless, and its treasures beyond number ... how, then, could such merit have any limit at all?"

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "The words of every buddha and Tathāgata are never false. Suppose, instead, that someone guilty of the four gravest offenses and the ten pārājika transgressions were, in a single instant, to pass through the Avīci hells of this world and of every other world, exhausting every unrelenting hell throughout the ten directions without exception — yet were able, with but a single thought, to explain this very teaching to someone not yet trained in it, sometime in the final kalpa. That person's own karmic obstructions would dissolve the very instant of that thought, transforming the causes of hellish suffering they were to undergo into a land of peace and joy, and the merit gained would surpass that of the person who gave all those treasures by a hundred times, a thousand times, a thousand million times over — beyond anything that number or comparison could ever measure."

阿難！若有眾生能誦此經，能持此呪，如我廣說窮劫不盡。依我教言，如教行道，直成菩提，無復魔業。」佛說此經已，比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷，一切世間、天、人、阿修羅，及諸他方菩薩、二乘、聖仙童子，并初發心大力鬼神，皆大歡喜，作禮而去。

"Ānanda, if there are beings able to recite this sūtra and hold this mantra, were I to explain their merit in full, I could speak for a whole kalpa and never exhaust it. Rely on my teaching, and practice the path exactly as taught, and you will proceed straight to bodhi, with no further demonic hindrance at all."

When the Buddha had finished speaking this sūtra, the monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen; every being of the world — devas, humans, and asuras; bodhisattvas and śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas from other worlds; sages, immortals, and youths; and the powerful ghosts and spirits newly resolved on bodhi — all rejoiced greatly, bowed in reverence, and departed.

Reading note: The sūtra closes on the same practical register that has run through it from fascicle 1's opening rescue onward: not a final metaphysical pronouncement, but a comparison of merit designed to make one thing unmistakably clear — teaching this understanding to even a single other person outweighs any quantity of material offering, because it addresses the root cause of suffering directly rather than any of its symptoms.
