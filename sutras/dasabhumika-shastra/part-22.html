The Four Immeasurables, and the Mastery of Spiritual Power

Within the division of the fruit of world-weariness, the bodhisattva-mahāsattva cultivates loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity — vast, immeasurable, without duality, without enmity or opposition, pervading every corner of the world — and then displays immeasurable spiritual power: shaking the great earth, transforming one body into many and many into one, passing through walls of stone and mountain-barriers as through empty space, walking on water as on solid ground, flying through the sky like a bird upon the wing, his body ablaze like fire and pouring forth water like a cloud, touching the very sun and moon with his hand. The treatise then turns to its own extensive exposition of what such mastery of spiritual power actually consists of — mastery over worlds, over the body, and over an eightfold karmic function, together with the workings of the deva-ear, the knowledge of others' minds, the knowledge of past lives, and the deva-eye.

The Four Immeasurables, and the Display of Immeasurable Spiritual Power

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩慈心，隨順廣大無量不二無瞋恨、無對無障、無惱害，遍至一切世間處，法界世間最、究竟虛空界，遍覆一切世間行。如是菩薩悲心隨順喜心、隨順捨心、隨順廣大無量不二無瞋恨無對無障無惱害，遍至一切世間處，法界世間最究竟虛空界，遍覆一切世間行。

論曰：無量有三種：一、眾生念；二、法念；三、無念。眾生念者，有四種相差別：一、與樂；二、障對治；三、清淨；四、攝果。云何與樂？與三種樂：一、與欲界樂；二、與色界同喜樂；三、與不同喜樂，彼離苦離喜故。不二者，亦是廣大無量，如經「是菩薩慈心隨順廣大無量不二」故。云何障對治？與不愛者與愛，此障對治，如經「無瞋恨無對」故。云何清淨？正斷身心不調，睡眠掉悔諸蓋等，如經「無障」故。云何攝果？欲色界中受正果習果，無苦事故，如經「無惱害」故。云何法念？遍一切處所有欲色無色界凡夫，有學無學眾生等法，及眾生所有分別作者皆能念知，如經「遍至一切世間處」故。無念者有二種：一、自相無念，觀法無我，世間中最，如經「法界世間最」故。二、遍至無盡觀，如經「究竟虛空界」故。一切世間者，一切世界普行故，如經「遍覆一切世間行」故。諸神通者，四通明智，第五明見。初一神通，身業清淨。天耳、他心智二通，口業清淨。宿命、生死智二通，意業清淨。一身通，能到眾生所。天耳、他心二通，能知說法音聲義故，以知他心故，隨種種言音皆能盡知。依於此義，種種異名說，隨眾生用故。去來二通，盡知眾生過去未來所應受化故。云何身通？

經曰：是菩薩現無量神通力能動大地，一身為多身、多身為一身，現沒還出，石壁山障皆能徹過如行虛空，於虛空中加趺而去猶如飛鳥，入出於地如水無異，履水如地，身出煙焰如大火聚，身中出水猶如大雲，日月有大神德、有大威力而能以手捫摸摩之，身力自在乃至梵世。

是菩薩以天耳界清淨過人，悉聞人天二種音聲若近若遠，乃至蚊虻蠅等悉聞其聲。

是菩薩以他心智如實知他眾生心及心數法，有貪心如實知有貪心、離貪心如實知離貪心。如實知有瞋心離瞋心、有癡心離癡心，如實知有染心離染心、小心廣心、大心無量心、攝心不攝心、住定心不住定心、解脫心不解脫心、求心不求心，上心如實知上心、非上心如實知非上心，如是以他心智如實知他眾生心及心數法。

是菩薩如實念知無量宿命諸所生處，亦能念知一生二三四五乃至十二十三十四十五十，亦能念知百生，念知無量百生無量千生無量百千生，念知成劫壞劫及成壞劫無量成壞劫，乃至念知百劫、千劫、百千劫、億劫、百億劫、千億劫、百千億劫，乃至念知無量百千萬億那由他劫，我本在某處，如是名、如是姓、如是生、如是色、如是飲食、如是壽命、如是久住、如是受苦樂。我於彼死生於此間，於此間死生於彼間。如是過去世種種相貌，說姓相等皆能念知。

是菩薩天眼界清淨過人，見諸眾生若生若死形色好醜、善行不善行、貧賤富貴，是諸眾生隨所造業皆如實知。是諸眾生成就身惡業、成就口惡業、成就意惡業，謗諸賢聖成就邪見，及邪見業因緣故，身壞命終必墮惡道生地獄中。是諸眾生成就身善業、成就口善業、成就意善業，不謗賢聖成就正見，及正見善業因緣故，身壞命終必生善道及諸天中。如是菩薩天眼界清淨過人，相貌姓名，見諸眾生若生若死、形色好醜、善行不善行、貧賤富貴，是諸眾生隨業受報皆如實知。

是菩薩於禪解脫三昧三摩跋提能入能出，而不隨禪解脫力生，隨見能滿菩提分法處，以願力故而生其中。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva's mind of loving-kindness accords with what is vast, immeasurable, without duality, without anger or resentment, without opposition or obstruction, without affliction or harm, reaching everywhere throughout the world, foremost within the dharma-realm and the world, ultimate as the realm of space itself, pervading and covering every course of conduct in the world. So too this bodhisattva's mind of compassion, mind of sympathetic joy, and mind of equanimity all accord with what is vast, immeasurable, without duality, without anger or resentment, without opposition or obstruction, without affliction or harm, reaching everywhere throughout the world, foremost within the dharma-realm and the world, ultimate as the realm of space itself, pervading and covering every course of conduct in the world.

Commentary: The immeasurables are of three kinds: first, the mindfulness that takes sentient beings as its object; second, the mindfulness that takes dharmas as its object; third, the mindfulness that takes no object at all. The mindfulness that takes sentient beings as its object has four distinguishing aspects: first, bestowing joy; second, the antidote to obstruction; third, purity; fourth, the gathering of fruit. What is bestowing joy? Bestowing three kinds of joy: first, the joy of the desire realm; second, the joy shared with delight, as in the first and second dhyānas; third, the joy not shared with delight, since it is free of both suffering and delight. "Without duality" is likewise "vast" and "immeasurable" — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva's mind of loving-kindness accords with what is vast, immeasurable, without duality." What is the antidote to obstruction? Giving love to one who is unloved — this is the antidote to obstruction, as the sūtra says, "without anger or resentment, without opposition." What is purity? The correct severing of disharmony of body and mind — sleepiness and torpor, restlessness and remorse, and the other hindrances — as the sūtra says, "without obstruction." What is the gathering of fruit? Within the desire realm and the form realm, receiving both the direct fruit and the habituated fruit, there being no matter of suffering — as the sūtra says, "without affliction or harm." What is the mindfulness that takes dharmas as its object? Everywhere, in every place, the dharmas of all ordinary beings within the desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm, of those in training and beyond training, and of sentient beings in general, together with whatever discriminating deeds sentient beings perform, are all borne in mind and known — as the sūtra says, "reaching everywhere throughout the world." The mindfulness that takes no object is of two kinds: first, the no-mindfulness of own-characteristics, contemplating dharmas as without self, foremost within the world — as the sūtra says, "foremost within the dharma-realm and the world." Second, the contemplation reaching everywhere without exhaustion — as the sūtra says, "ultimate as the realm of space itself." "Every course of conduct in the world" means universally practiced throughout every world — as the sūtra says, "pervading and covering every course of conduct in the world." As for the spiritual powers: four of them are of the nature of knowledge, and the fifth is of the nature of seeing. The first spiritual power purifies the karma of the body. The two powers of the deva-ear and of the knowledge of others' minds purify the karma of speech. The two powers of the knowledge of past lives and of the knowledge of death and rebirth purify the karma of mind. The power of the body is able to reach wherever sentient beings are. The two powers of the deva-ear and of the knowledge of others' minds are able to know the sound and meaning of the Dharma being taught — since one knows others' minds, one is able to fully understand every kind of speech and sound, relying on this same meaning yet expressed through words of every different kind, according to what is of use to sentient beings. The two powers reaching to what has gone and what is to come fully know what sentient beings, in the past and in the future, ought to receive in the way of teaching and transformation. What is the mastery of spiritual power?

Sūtra: This bodhisattva displays immeasurable spiritual power, able to shake the great earth; he transforms one body into many bodies, and many bodies back into one body; he appears and vanishes, then emerges again; walls of stone and mountain-barriers he is able to pass through entirely, as though walking through empty space; seated cross-legged, he travels through the sky just like a bird upon the wing; he enters and emerges from the earth as though it were water, no different; he walks upon water as upon solid ground; from his body he emits smoke and flame like a great mass of fire; from his body he pours forth water like a great cloud; the sun and moon, possessed of such great spiritual virtue and such great majestic power, he is able to touch and stroke with his very hand; the power of his body extends, self-mastering, all the way to the Brahmā world.

This bodhisattva, through the purity of the deva-ear realm surpassing that of ordinary people, hears fully both kinds of sound, human and divine, whether near or far, hearing even the sound of gnats, mosquitoes, and flies.

This bodhisattva, through the wisdom that knows others' minds, knows as it really is the minds and mental factors of other sentient beings: a mind possessed of craving he knows truly as a mind possessed of craving, a mind free of craving he knows truly as a mind free of craving. He knows truly a mind possessed of hatred and a mind free of hatred, a mind possessed of delusion and a mind free of delusion; he knows truly a mind possessed of defilement and a mind free of defilement, a small mind and a vast mind, a great mind and an immeasurable mind, a gathered mind and an ungathered mind, a mind abiding in concentration and a mind not abiding in concentration, a liberated mind and an unliberated mind, a seeking mind and a mind not seeking, a superior mind he knows truly as a superior mind and a mind not superior he knows truly as a mind not superior — thus, through the wisdom that knows others' minds, he knows as it really is the minds and mental factors of other sentient beings.

This bodhisattva truly recollects and knows the boundless places of his own past lives; he is able to recollect and know one life, two, three, four, five lives, all the way to ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty lives; he is able to recollect and know a hundred lives, recollecting and knowing boundless hundreds of lives, boundless thousands of lives, boundless hundreds of thousands of lives; he recollects and knows eons of formation, eons of dissolution, eons of formation-and-dissolution, and boundless eons of formation-and-dissolution, all the way to recollecting and knowing a hundred eons, a thousand eons, a hundred thousand eons, a koṭi of eons, a hundred koṭis of eons, a thousand koṭis of eons, a hundred thousand koṭis of eons, all the way to recollecting and knowing boundless hundreds of thousands of myriad koṭis of niyutas of eons: "I myself once dwelt in such-and-such a place, with such-and-such a name, such-and-such a clan, born in such-and-such a manner, of such-and-such an appearance, eating such-and-such food, of such-and-such a lifespan, abiding so long, and experiencing such-and-such suffering and joy. I died there and was born here; I died here and was born there." Thus every manner of characteristic belonging to past lives — their telling, their clan, their features, and the rest — he is fully able to recollect and know.

This bodhisattva, through the purity of the deva-eye realm surpassing that of ordinary people, sees sentient beings being born and dying, their bodily form fine or ugly, their conduct wholesome or unwholesome, their condition poor and lowly or wealthy and noble; whatever karma these sentient beings have created, he knows it all truly. These sentient beings, having accomplished evil karma of body, evil karma of speech, and evil karma of mind, slandering the sages and the worthy, accomplishing wrong view — because of the causes and conditions of wrong view and its karma, when the body perishes and the life ends, they must fall into an evil destiny and be born within the hells. These sentient beings, having accomplished wholesome karma of body, wholesome karma of speech, and wholesome karma of mind, not slandering the sages and the worthy, accomplishing right view — because of the causes and conditions of right view and its wholesome karma, when the body perishes and the life ends, they must be born within a wholesome destiny and among the various heavens. Thus this bodhisattva, through the purity of the deva-eye realm surpassing that of ordinary people, knowing their characteristics, clan, and name, sees sentient beings being born and dying, their bodily form fine or ugly, their conduct wholesome or unwholesome, their condition poor and lowly or wealthy and noble; according to their karma, whatever retribution these sentient beings receive, he knows it all truly.

This bodhisattva, with respect to dhyāna, liberation, samādhi, and samāpatti, is able to enter and able to emerge; yet he is not born through the power of dhyāna and liberation, but, according to what he sees as able to fulfill the factors of enlightenment, through the power of his vow, he is born within it.

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Where Part 21 draws on two of Huiyuan's five reasons for why the bodhisattva enters the dhyānas and the formless absorptions (subduing pride taken in worldly dhyāna, and pride taken in the formless attainments), the four immeasurables and the display of spiritual power answer to the remaining three. Huiyuan states plainly why loving-kindness and compassion are undertaken at all: 為苦生者，總明慈悲二行所為為拔苦也 — "as for those born amid suffering, this states in general that the two practices of loving-kindness and compassion are undertaken in order to pull beings out of suffering." Sympathetic joy and equanimity answer to a different pair of beings, those already glimpsing release: 以彼脫苦慶之以憙，心放捨也 — "rejoicing, through delight, at their release from suffering, the mind is set at ease through equanimity." And the display of spiritual power itself answers to a fifth kind of being entirely, neither suffering nor near release, but sheltering in a false teaching: 言為邪歸入勝通者，有諸眾生歸邪外道，故入勝通令信正法 — "as for those who take a wrongful refuge, he enters into supreme spiritual powers because there are sentient beings who take refuge in false, non-Buddhist paths, and so he enters into supreme spiritual powers in order to cause them to have faith in the true Dharma."

On the treatise's own remark that four of the five spiritual powers are "of the nature of knowledge" while only the fifth, the deva-eye, is named "seeing," Huiyuan explains why the fifth alone earns a different verb, even though all five are strictly the same kind of thing: 理實五通體皆是智，但彼天眼照矚分明，廢體從根，故說為見 — "in reality, the essence of all five powers is alike wisdom; but because the deva-eye illuminates and discerns with such clarity, the treatise sets aside the essence and speaks instead from the faculty, and so calls it seeing." He then heads off the obvious objection — if the eye-faculty earns its own verb, why not the ear as well? — 若爾，天耳何不從根說之為聞…耳聞不順，故不名聞 — "if this is so, why does the deva-ear not likewise take its name from its faculty and be called hearing?… but hearing does not sit well with the meaning of wisdom, and so it is not named hearing" — leaving sight alone, among the five, as the power named for its organ rather than its object.

The Detailed Exposition of Mastery — World, Body, and Karmic Function

Chinese Source

論曰：身通者，得勝自在應知。自在有三種：一、世界自在，能動世界大地，如經「是菩薩現無量神通力能動大地」故。二、身自在，彼能散合隱顯，如經「一身為多身、多身為一身，現沒還出」故。三、作業自在，作業有八種：一者傍行無礙，如經「石壁山障皆能徹過如行虛空」故。二者上行，如經「於虛空中加趺而去猶如飛鳥」故。三者上下行，如經「入出於地如水無異」故。四者涉水不沒，如經「履水如地」故。五者其身熾然，如經「身出煙焰如大火聚」故。六者身能注水，如經「身中出水猶如大雲」故。七者身能摩捫，如經「日月有大神德有大威力而能以手捫摸摩之」故。八者自在，乃至梵世間、器世間隨意轉變得自在故，如經「身力自在乃至梵世」故。

天耳通者，隨能聞所聞，如實示現清淨諦聞故。過人者，遠聞故過人。聲者，下乃至阿鼻地獄等聲悉能聞故。如經「是菩薩以天耳界清淨過人」故。隨人天等所作音聲現聞明了，乃至蚊虻蠅等微細音聲亦能聞故，如經「悉聞人天二種音聲，若近若遠，乃至蚊虻蠅等悉聞其聲」故。

他心通者，他心差別有八種：一、隨煩惱；二、使；三、生；四、學三昧行；五、得三昧；六、得解脫；七、妄行正行；八、餘凡夫增上慢。隨煩惱者，與貪瞋等和合，如經「是菩薩以他心智如實知他眾生心及心數法，有貪心如實知有貪心乃至離癡心」故。使者，有煩惱離煩惱等，如經「如實知有染心離染心」故。生者，人中小、欲天中廣、色天中大、無色二解脫中無量，如經「小心廣心大心無量心」故。學三昧行者，散心不散心，如經「攝心不攝心」故。得三昧者，入定不入定時及在定起時，如經「住定心不住定心」故。得解脫者，有縛無縛，如經「解脫心不解脫心」故。妄行正行者，於名聞中現起悕望順不順故，如經「求心不求心」故。餘凡夫增上慢者，麁細習行故，如經「上心如實知上心、非上心如實知非上心」故。

宿命智通者，誰能念？智能念故，如經「是菩薩如實念知無量宿命諸所生處」故。念何等事？如經「亦能念知一生乃至念知無量百千萬億那由他劫」故。云何念？因名字差別，如經「我本在某處如是名」故。家差別，如經「如是姓」故。貴賤差別，如經「如是生」故。好醜差別，如經「如是色」故。供饍差別，如經「如是飲食」故。業行差別，如經「如是壽命如是久住」故。衰利成壞差別，如經「如是受苦樂，我於彼死生於此間，於此間死生於彼間」故。是中家差別者，謂父母差別。貴賤差別者，剎利、婆羅門等差別。業行差別者，命差別，時非時死故。是中種種相貌者，一切諸相差別，如經「種種相貌」故。說者名稱，如經「說」故。姓相者，家姓等故，如經「姓相等皆能念知」故。復有異義，亦能念知一生二生。如是等，是名相貌。說者，名等故。相者，我於彼死生於此間。如是等，皆能念知故。

生死智通者，誰能見？以天眼見故。清淨者，審見故。過人者，遠見故，如經「是菩薩以天眼界清淨過人」故。見何等事？謂眾生生死等，如經「見諸眾生若生若死乃至隨所造業皆如實知」故。云何見？如經「是諸眾生成就身惡業乃至隨業受報皆如實知」故。餘者如前二地中說。善惡業報此亦如是應知。

是中禪者四禪，解脫者四無色定，三昧者四無量，三摩跋提者五神通。能入能出者，即生心時隨心用現在前故。而不隨禪解脫力生者，彼淳厚深念心，此成就示現。隨見能滿菩提分法處者，與諸佛大菩薩共生一處故，如經「是菩薩於禪解脫乃至以願力故而生其中」故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: "Mastery of spiritual power" should be understood as the attainment of supreme self-mastery. This self-mastery is of three kinds: first, mastery over worlds, being able to shake the great earth of the world — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva displays immeasurable spiritual power, able to shake the great earth." Second, mastery over the body, being able to disperse and unite it, to conceal and reveal it — as the sūtra says, "he transforms one body into many bodies, and many bodies back into one body; he appears and vanishes, then emerges again." Third, mastery over karmic function. This karmic function is of eight kinds. First, moving crosswise without obstruction — as the sūtra says, "walls of stone and mountain-barriers he is able to pass through entirely, as though walking through empty space." Second, moving upward — as the sūtra says, "seated cross-legged, he travels through the sky just like a bird upon the wing." Third, moving both upward and downward — as the sūtra says, "he enters and emerges from the earth as though it were water, no different." Fourth, treading upon water without sinking — as the sūtra says, "he walks upon water as upon solid ground." Fifth, his body itself ablaze — as the sūtra says, "from his body he emits smoke and flame like a great mass of fire." Sixth, his body able to pour forth water — as the sūtra says, "from his body he pours forth water like a great cloud." Seventh, his body able to touch and stroke — as the sūtra says, "the sun and moon, possessed of such great spiritual virtue and such great majestic power, he is able to touch and stroke with his very hand." Eighth, self-mastery itself, extending all the way to the Brahmā world — attaining self-mastery in freely transforming both the sentient world and the receptacle world according to his will — as the sūtra says, "the power of his body extends, self-mastering, all the way to the Brahmā world."

As for the power of the deva-ear: according to what is able to be heard and what is heard, it truly and manifestly shows forth pure and attentive hearing. "Surpassing that of ordinary people" means surpassing ordinary people through hearing what is far off. As for sound: extending down all the way to the sounds of the Avīci hell, he is able to hear it all — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, through the purity of the deva-ear realm, surpasses that of ordinary people." Whatever sound is made by human beings, devas, and the rest, he manifestly hears it clearly, extending even to the faint, fine sound of gnats, mosquitoes, and flies, which he is likewise able to hear — as the sūtra says, "he hears fully both kinds of sound, human and divine, whether near or far, hearing even the sound of gnats, mosquitoes, and flies."

As for the power of knowing others' minds: the differences among others' minds are of eight kinds: first, that which accords with affliction; second, the latent tendency; third, the mind as born into a given realm; fourth, the practice of training in samādhi; fifth, the attainment of samādhi; sixth, the attainment of liberation; seventh, false conduct and correct conduct; eighth, the remaining ordinary person's overweening conceit. "That which accords with affliction" means being conjoined with craving, hatred, and the rest — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, through the wisdom that knows others' minds, knows as it really is the minds and mental factors of other sentient beings: a mind possessed of craving he knows truly as a mind possessed of craving," all the way to "a mind free of delusion." "The latent tendency" means having affliction and being free of affliction, and the rest — as the sūtra says, "he knows truly a mind possessed of defilement and a mind free of defilement." "The mind as born" means small within the human realm, vast within the heavens of desire, great within the heavens of form, and immeasurable within the two liberations of the formless realm — as the sūtra says, "a small mind and a vast mind, a great mind and an immeasurable mind." "The practice of training in samādhi" means a scattered mind and an unscattered mind — as the sūtra says, "a gathered mind and an ungathered mind." "The attainment of samādhi" means the time of entering concentration and the time of not entering concentration, and the time of abiding in concentration and rising from it — as the sūtra says, "a mind abiding in concentration and a mind not abiding in concentration." "The attainment of liberation" means having bondage and being without bondage — as the sūtra says, "a liberated mind and an unliberated mind." "False conduct and correct conduct" means, with regard to fame and renown, giving rise manifestly to longing, whether in accordance with it or not — as the sūtra says, "a seeking mind and a mind not seeking." "The remaining ordinary person's overweening conceit" means the coarse and the fine habituated conduct — as the sūtra says, "a superior mind he knows truly as a superior mind and a mind not superior he knows truly as a mind not superior."

As for the power of the wisdom of past lives: who is able to recollect? It is wisdom that is able to recollect — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly recollects and knows the boundless places of his own past lives." What matters does he recollect? As the sūtra says, "he is able to recollect and know one life," all the way to "recollecting and knowing boundless hundreds of thousands of myriad koṭis of niyutas of eons." How does he recollect? Through the difference in name — as the sūtra says, "I myself once dwelt in such-and-such a place, with such-and-such a name." The difference in family — as the sūtra says, "such-and-such a clan." The difference in noble or lowly station — as the sūtra says, "born in such-and-such a manner." The difference in fine or ugly appearance — as the sūtra says, "of such-and-such an appearance." The difference in food provided — as the sūtra says, "eating such-and-such food." The difference in the conduct of karma — as the sūtra says, "of such-and-such a lifespan, abiding so long." The difference of decline and gain, formation and dissolution — as the sūtra says, "experiencing such-and-such suffering and joy — I died there and was born here; I died here and was born there." Here, the difference in family means the difference of father and mother. The difference in noble or lowly station means the difference between kṣatriya, brāhmaṇa, and the rest. The difference in the conduct of karma means the difference of lifespan — dying at the proper time or dying before the proper time. Here, "every manner of characteristic" means the difference of every characteristic — as the sūtra says, "every manner of characteristic." "Telling" means naming — as the sūtra says, "telling." "Clan characteristics" means family and clan and the rest — as the sūtra says, "their telling, their clan, their features, and the rest, he is fully able to recollect and know." There is, further, another interpretation: "is able to recollect and know one life, two lives," and so on — this is called "characteristics." "Telling" means the name and the rest. "Features" means "I died there and was born here," and so on — all of which he is fully able to recollect and know.

As for the power of the wisdom of death and rebirth: who is able to see? It is through the deva-eye that one sees. "Pure" means seeing with certainty. "Surpassing that of ordinary people" means seeing what is far off — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, through the purity of the deva-eye realm, surpasses that of ordinary people." What matters does he see? Namely, the birth and death of sentient beings, and the rest — as the sūtra says, "he sees sentient beings being born and dying," all the way to "whatever karma these sentient beings have created, he knows it all truly." How does he see? As the sūtra says, "these sentient beings, having accomplished evil karma of body," all the way to "according to their karma, whatever retribution these sentient beings receive, he knows it all truly." The rest is as explained within the preceding two grounds; the retribution of wholesome and unwholesome karma should likewise be understood here just as there.

Here, "dhyāna" refers to the four dhyānas; "liberation" refers to the four formless concentrations; "samādhi" refers to the four immeasurables; "samāpatti" refers to the five spiritual powers. "Able to enter and able to emerge" means that, at the very moment the mind arises, its function manifests immediately before him, in accordance with the mind. "Yet he is not born through the power of dhyāna and liberation" means that this manifests the accomplishment of that mind of deep and heartfelt mindfulness, sincere and full. "According to what he sees as able to fulfill the factors of enlightenment" means being born together, in one and the same place, with the buddhas and the great bodhisattvas — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, with respect to dhyāna, liberation," all the way to "through the power of his vow, he is born within it."

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Closing his commentary on this stretch of the treatise, Huiyuan pauses to disambiguate four terms the sūtra text sets side by side — dhyāna, liberation, samādhi, and samāpatti — which might otherwise read as near-synonyms for a single concentrative state: 四禪能生一切功德，故名為禪，此翻名為功德藂林 — "the four dhyānas are able to give rise to every merit, and so are named dhyāna, which translates as 'the thicket-grove of merit.'" 四無色定是八解中四解脫攝，故名解脫 — "the four formless concentrations are gathered among the four liberations within the eight liberations, and so are named liberation." 四無量心，菩薩正定，故名三昧，此云正定 — "the four immeasurable minds are the bodhisattva's own correct concentration, and so are named samādhi, which translates as 'correct concentration.'" 五通現用，名三摩提，此翻名為定用現前 — "the five spiritual powers manifesting their function are named samāpatti, which translates as 'the present manifestation of concentration's function.'" Four words that, read only in the sūtra's own prose, could pass for interchangeable turn out, in Huiyuan's accounting, to each name a distinct kind of attainment.

On the closing line explaining why the bodhisattva, though he enters and leaves dhyāna and liberation at will, is "not born through the power of dhyāna and liberation" but instead comes to dwell wherever will fulfill the factors of enlightenment, Huiyuan records two distinct readings of what this companionship with the buddhas and great bodhisattvas actually accomplishes. Read as a matter of his own practice: 諸佛菩薩是己所見，生道法處，與彼同生，自增道也 — "the buddhas and bodhisattvas are what he himself has seen; being born in a place of the Dharma of the path, and born there together with them, he thereby increases his own path." The Bodhisattvabhūmi (地持論), he notes, instead reads the same passage as a matter of benefiting others: 隨他見已，生道法處，往與同生，增彼道也 — "according to what others have seen, he goes and is born together with them in a place of the Dharma of the path, in order to increase their path." The same rebirth, on Huiyuan's reading, serves either the bodhisattva's own advancement or the advancement of those he accompanies, depending on which text's lens is applied.

Part 22 Complete

This closes Part 22: the bodhisattva-mahāsattva's cultivation of loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity — vast, immeasurable, free of duality and of enmity, pervading every corner of the world — followed by his display of immeasurable spiritual power: shaking the great earth, dividing one body into many and gathering many into one, passing through walls of stone and mountain-barriers as through empty space, walking on water as on solid ground, flying through the sky like a bird upon the wing, his body ablaze like fire and pouring forth water like a cloud, touching the sun and moon with his own hand, his mastery of body extending all the way to the Brahmā world — and, closing this part, the treatise's own extensive exposition of what such mastery of spiritual power actually consists of: mastery over worlds, over the body, and over an eightfold karmic function, together with the detailed workings of the deva-ear, the knowledge of others' minds, the knowledge of past lives, the deva-eye, and the four named aspects — dhyāna, liberation, samādhi, and samāpatti — of concentrative attainment itself.
