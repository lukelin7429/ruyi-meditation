The Ninth Ground Complete

A bodhisattva who well knows the four unhindered wisdoms, dwelling securely in this Ninth Ground, is called one who has attained the buddhas' own Dharma-treasury and become a great Dharma teacher — and the treatise names his accomplishment of retention through ten distinct kinds of dhāraṇī, from the dhāraṇī of manifold meanings through the dhāraṇī of joyful exposition. The sūtra then unfolds this bodhisattva's boundless eloquence at the Dharma-seat — a single sound understood by every hearer at once, every pore emitting the sound of the Dharma, every question raised across worlds without number answered through that same single sound — before the ground's closing fruit shows a bodhisattva who never departs from the sight of countless buddhas, whose roots of goodness the treatise likens to genuine gold fastened upon a wheel-turning sage king's own crown that no lesser king's ornament can equal, and to a Great Brahmā King's radiance filling two thousand worlds at once — closing, at last, the entire Ninth Ground, Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom.

Accomplishing the Status of a Great Dharma Teacher: The Ten Kinds of Dhāraṇī

Chinese Source

經曰：佛子！菩薩如是善知無礙智，安住第九菩薩地，名為得諸佛法藏能作大法師。得眾義陀羅尼、眾法陀羅尼、起智陀羅尼、光明陀羅尼、善意陀羅尼、眾財陀羅尼、威德陀羅尼、無障礙門陀羅尼、無量陀羅尼，得種種義陀羅尼。得如是等陀羅尼門，滿足十阿僧祇百千陀羅尼門。如是十阿僧祇百千音聲方便，如是十阿僧祇百千無量信樂門差別說法。是菩薩得如是十阿僧祇百千無量陀羅尼門，能於無量諸佛所聽法聞已不忘，如所聞法能以無量差別門為人演說。

論曰：持成就者，有十種陀羅尼：一、義陀羅尼，如經「得眾義陀羅尼」故。二、聞陀羅尼，如經「得眾法陀羅尼」故。三、智陀羅尼，如經「起智陀羅尼」故。四、放光陀羅尼，如經「光明陀羅尼」故。五、降伏他陀羅尼，如經「善意陀羅尼」故。六、供養如來布施攝取貧窮眾生陀羅尼，如經「得眾財陀羅尼」故。七、於大乘中狹劣眾生示教利益陀羅尼，如經「威德陀羅尼」故。八、不斷辯才陀羅尼，如經「得無障礙門陀羅尼」故。九、無盡樂說陀羅尼，如經「得無量陀羅尼」故。十、種種義樂說陀羅尼，如經「得種種義陀羅尼」故。乃至隨所聞無量差別說如是等餘經文，說成就、問答成就、受持成就，如經說應知，易解故不釋。餘如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva who thus well knows the unhindered wisdoms, dwelling securely in this Ninth Bodhisattva Ground, is called one who has attained the buddhas' own Dharma-treasury, able to become a great Dharma teacher. He attains the dhāraṇī of manifold meanings, the dhāraṇī of manifold dharmas, the dhāraṇī that gives rise to wisdom, the dhāraṇī of light, the dhāraṇī of good intention, the dhāraṇī of manifold wealth, the dhāraṇī of majestic virtue, the dhāraṇī of the unobstructed gate, the dhāraṇī of boundlessness, and attains the dhāraṇī of manifold meaning. Having attained dhāraṇī-gateways such as these, he fulfills ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand dhāraṇī-gateways. Just so, ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand skillful means of voice; just so, ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand boundless gateways of faith and delight, through which he expounds the Dharma, each with its own distinction. This bodhisattva, having attained ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand boundless dhāraṇī-gateways such as these, is able, in the presence of boundless buddhas, to listen to the Dharma and, having heard it, never forget it; and, according with the Dharma he has heard, he is able to proclaim it to others through boundless distinct gateways.

Commentary: Accomplishing retention is of ten kinds of dhāraṇī. First, the dhāraṇī of meaning — as the sūtra says, "he attains the dhāraṇī of manifold meanings." Second, the dhāraṇī of hearing — as the sūtra says, "he attains the dhāraṇī of manifold dharmas." Third, the dhāraṇī of wisdom — as the sūtra says, "the dhāraṇī that gives rise to wisdom." Fourth, the dhāraṇī that emits light — as the sūtra says, "the dhāraṇī of light." Fifth, the dhāraṇī that subdues others — as the sūtra says, "the dhāraṇī of good intention." Sixth, the dhāraṇī of making offerings to the tathāgatas, of giving, and of gathering in impoverished sentient beings — as the sūtra says, "he attains the dhāraṇī of manifold wealth." Seventh, the dhāraṇī that instructs and benefits sentient beings of narrow, inferior capacity within the great vehicle — as the sūtra says, "the dhāraṇī of majestic virtue." Eighth, the dhāraṇī of unbroken eloquence — as the sūtra says, "he attains the dhāraṇī of the unobstructed gate." Ninth, the dhāraṇī of inexhaustible, joyful exposition — as the sūtra says, "he attains the dhāraṇī of boundlessness." Tenth, the dhāraṇī of the joyful exposition of manifold meanings — as the sūtra says, "he attains the dhāraṇī of manifold meaning." And so on, according with whatever he has heard, expounding it through boundless distinctions — the remaining sūtra text of this kind, concerning the accomplishment of teaching, the accomplishment of question-and-answer, and the accomplishment of further reception, should be understood just as the sūtra itself states; since it is easy to understand, it is not further explained here. The rest is as already explained before.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang reads this whole fourth major section of the ground under the heading of "accomplishing the status of a Dharma teacher" (法師成就), dividing it into four: 一持成就，蘊法在心；二說成就，依持起說；三問答成就，他惑疑問，菩薩善答；四受持成就，能更受勝法，為他宣說 — "first, accomplishing retention, the Dharma stored within the mind; second, accomplishing teaching, exposition arising in dependence on that retention; third, accomplishing question-and-answer, skillfully resolving others' confusions and doubts; fourth, accomplishing further reception, able to receive still more supreme teachings and proclaim them for others." Only the first of these four — retention — receives the treatise's own line-by-line commentary; the rest, he notes, the treatise's author leaves unglossed: 自下經文，論主不釋 — "from this point on in the sūtra text, the treatise's author does not comment" — which is exactly why the section that follows in this translation runs as sūtra text alone, without any interposed commentary.

On the ten dhāraṇī themselves, Fazang groups them by which of the three karmic actions each arises from: 謂初三起意業...次三起身...下四起口業 — "the first three arise from mental action... the next three arise from bodily action... the last four arise from verbal action." The first three — meaning, hearing, and the wisdom that gives rise to pervasive transformation — are purely cognitive. Of the bodily three, he glosses the fourth and fifth as two complementary modes of gathering beings in: 善耎眾生慈光攝取 for the soft-hearted, gathered in through a light of loving-kindness, and 剛強眾生威力伏取 for the obstinate, subdued through majestic power — adding that 示違伏物是善巧之慧, "showing that even subduing what resists is itself an expression of skillful wisdom." Of the final four, verbal dhāraṇī, he reads 不斷 as constant teaching, 無盡 as profound teaching, and 種種 as extensive teaching — three registers of the same unbroken exposition.

Boundless Eloquence at the Dharma-Seat, and the Closing Fruit of Sādhumatī

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩於一佛所，以十阿僧祇百千陀羅尼門聽受法：如從一佛聽法，餘無量無邊諸佛亦復如是。是菩薩於禮敬佛時，所聞法明門能受，非多學聲聞，得大陀羅尼力，於十萬劫所能受持。是菩薩得如是陀羅尼力及無礙智樂說力說法。說法時在於法座，遍一切三千大千世界，隨眾生心差別說法。是菩薩法座，唯除諸佛及受職菩薩，於一切中最為殊勝，得無量法明。是菩薩處於法座，或以一音說，令一切大眾悉得解了、即得解了。或以種種音說，令一切大眾各得開解、即得開解。或但放光明說，令一切大眾各得解法、即得解法。或以一切毛孔皆出法音，或以三千大千世界所有色物皆出法音，或以一音周遍一切法界皆令得解，或以一切音聲法聲住持，或於一切世界歌詠樂音一切音聲皆出法音，或於一字聲中一切法字句聲皆差別說，或於不可說世界無量地水火風聚細微塵差別一一微塵中不可說法門皆悉能說。是菩薩，三千大千世界所有眾生於一念間一時問難，彼一一眾生以無量音聲差別問難，如一人所問餘者異問，是菩薩於一念間悉受如是問難，但以一音皆令開解。如是二三千大千世界，若三四五，若十二十三十四十五十，若百三千大千世界，若千三千大千世界，若萬十萬百萬若億三千大千世界，若十億百千萬億那由他，乃至無量無邊不可說不可說三千大千世界，滿中眾生於一念間一時問難，彼一一眾生以無量音聲差別問難，如一人所問餘者異問，是菩薩於一念間悉受如是問難，但以一音皆令開解。

是菩薩於不可說不可說世界遍滿其中，隨心、隨根、隨信為眾生說法，得法明故。求如來力滿足佛事，與一切眾生而作依止。

是菩薩轉倍精進，攝取如是智明。若於一一毛頭處，有不可說不可說世界微塵數如來大會，佛在其中而為說法，一一如來為不可說不可說世界微塵數眾生說法，一一眾生心中有不可說不可說世界微塵數心生，如來如是隨眾生心而與法門。如一佛，一切佛在一一毛頭處亦如是。如是一切法界中，於是中生大憶念力，於一念間從一切佛所受一切法明而不失一句，何況所說一切世界中眾生。

是菩薩住此菩薩善慧地中轉勝，晝夜更無餘念入佛境界，常得親近一切諸佛，通達甚深菩薩解脫。是菩薩隨順如是智，常入三昧不離親近諸佛，而於一一劫中見無量佛、無量百佛、無量千佛、無量百千佛、無量百千那由他佛、無量億佛、無量百億佛、無量千億佛、無量百千億佛、無量百千億那由他佛，以上妙供具供養恭敬尊重讚歎親近諸佛，於諸佛所種種問難通達說法陀羅尼。是菩薩彼諸善根轉勝明淨。佛子！譬如本真金，作莊嚴具已，繫在轉輪聖王若頸若頂，一切小王四天下人所有一切諸莊嚴具無能及者。如是佛子！菩薩住此菩薩善慧地中，彼諸善根轉勝明淨，一切聲聞辟支佛及下地菩薩所不能壞。是菩薩善根轉明，能照眾生煩惱心稠林處，照已還攝。佛子！譬如大梵王，二千世界中所有一切深稠林處皆悉能照。

如是佛子！菩薩住此菩薩善慧地中，彼諸善根光明照諸眾生煩惱心稠林處，照已還攝。是菩薩十波羅蜜中力波羅蜜增上，餘波羅蜜非不修習，隨力隨分。佛子！是名略說菩薩第九菩薩善慧地。若廣說者，於無量劫說不可盡。菩薩住此地中，多作大梵天王，得大勢力主二千世界，於自在中而得自在，如實正解最為殊勝，善能宣說聲聞辟支佛菩薩波羅蜜行，眾生問難無能窮盡。所作善業布施愛語利益同事，是諸福德皆不離念佛、念法、念僧、念菩薩、念菩薩行、念波羅蜜、念十地、念不壞力、念無畏、念不共佛法，乃至不離念一切種一切智智。常生是心：我當於一切眾生中，為首為勝為大、為妙為微妙、為上為無上、為導為將、為師為尊，乃至為一切智智依止者。復從是念發精進行，以精進力故，於一念間得十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數三昧，見十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數佛、知十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數佛神力、能動十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數世界、能入十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數世界、能照十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數世界、能化十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數世界眾生、能住壽十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數劫、能知過去未來世各十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數劫事、能善入十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數法門、能變身為十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數，於一一身能示十阿僧祇百千佛國土微塵數菩薩以為眷屬。若以願力自在勝上，菩薩願力過於此數。示種種神通，或身、或光明、或神通、或眼、或境界、或音聲、或行、或莊嚴、或加、或信、或業。是諸神通，乃至無量百千萬億那由他劫不可數知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, in the presence of a single buddha, receives the Dharma through ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand dhāraṇī-gateways; and just as he listens to the Dharma from that one buddha, so too is it with every one of the boundless, limitless other buddhas. This bodhisattva, at the moment of paying reverent homage to the buddhas, is able to receive the illuminating gateway of the Dharma he has heard — unlike a śrāvaka of much learning, for, having attained the power of great dhāraṇī, he is able to retain what he has received for a hundred thousand eons. This bodhisattva, having attained a power of dhāraṇī and a power of unhindered, joyful exposition such as this, expounds the Dharma. When he expounds the Dharma, seated at the Dharma-seat, he pervades every trichiliocosm, expounding the Dharma according to each distinction of sentient beings' own minds. This bodhisattva's Dharma-seat — save only for the buddhas and the bodhisattvas who have received their consecration — is, among all seats, the most excellent, attaining boundless illumination of the Dharma. This bodhisattva, seated upon the Dharma-seat, at times expounds through a single sound, causing the entire great assembly to attain understanding entirely, and understanding is attained at once. At times he expounds through manifold sounds, causing the entire great assembly to attain, each their own, understanding, and understanding is attained at once. At times he simply emits light and expounds, causing the entire great assembly to attain, each their own, understanding of the Dharma, and understanding of the Dharma is attained at once. At times every one of his pores emits the sound of the Dharma; at times every material thing within the trichiliocosm emits the sound of the Dharma; at times a single sound pervades the entire dharma-realm, causing understanding everywhere; at times every sound, every voice, sustains and upholds the Dharma; at times, throughout every world, the songs and music and every sound all emit the sound of the Dharma; at times, within the sound of a single syllable, every distinction of the Dharma's own words, phrases, and sounds is expounded, each with its own difference; at times, within each and every one of the fine motes of dust composing the boundless earth, water, fire, and wind of inexpressible worlds, he is able to expound entirely the inexpressible Dharma-gateways contained within it. This bodhisattva — should every sentient being within a trichiliocosm, within a single thought-moment, all at once raise questions and objections, each and every one of these sentient beings raising questions and objections through boundless, distinct sounds, each person's question differing from every other's — this bodhisattva, within a single thought-moment, receives every one of these questions and objections entirely, and, through a single sound alone, causes every one of them to attain understanding. And so it is with two trichiliocosms, or three, four, five; or ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty; or a hundred trichiliocosms; or a thousand trichiliocosms; or ten thousand, a hundred thousand, a million, or a koṭi of trichiliocosms; or ten koṭis, a hundred thousand koṭis of niyutas; all the way to boundless, limitless, inexpressible, inexpressible trichiliocosms — should every sentient being filling them, within a single thought-moment, all at once raise questions and objections, each and every one of these sentient beings raising questions and objections through boundless, distinct sounds, each person's question differing from every other's — this bodhisattva, within a single thought-moment, receives every one of these questions and objections entirely, and, through a single sound alone, causes every one of them to attain understanding.

This bodhisattva, pervading and filling inexpressible, inexpressible worlds, expounds the Dharma for sentient beings according to their minds, according to their faculties, and according to their faith, since he has attained the illumination of the Dharma. Seeking the tathāgatas' own power, he fulfills the buddhas' own work, and becomes the very support relied upon by every sentient being.

This bodhisattva, redoubling his diligent exertion again and again, gathers in this illumination of wisdom. Should there be, within each and every hair-tip, a number of great assemblies of tathāgatas equal to the fine motes of dust in inexpressible, inexpressible worlds, with a buddha present within each, expounding the Dharma; and should each and every one of these tathāgatas expound the Dharma for a number of sentient beings equal to the fine motes of dust in inexpressible, inexpressible worlds; and should there arise, within the mind of each and every one of these sentient beings, a number of minds equal to the fine motes of dust in inexpressible, inexpressible worlds — the tathāgatas, in just this way, bestow the Dharma-gateway according to each sentient being's own mind. And just as with a single buddha, so it is with every buddha present within each and every hair-tip. Throughout the entire dharma-realm in just this way, this bodhisattva gives rise, within it, to the power of great mindfulness, so that, within a single thought-moment, he receives from every buddha every illumination of the Dharma without losing a single phrase — how much more, then, the Dharma spoken to the sentient beings within every world.

This bodhisattva, dwelling within this bodhisattva's Sādhumatī Ground, becomes ever more excellent, day and night having no other thought but to enter the buddhas' own realm, constantly drawing near to every buddha, thoroughly penetrating the bodhisattva's own profound liberation. This bodhisattva, according with wisdom such as this, constantly enters samādhi, never departing from drawing near to the buddhas — and, within every single eon, sees boundless buddhas, boundless hundreds of buddhas, boundless thousands of buddhas, boundless hundreds of thousands of buddhas, boundless hundreds of thousands of niyutas of buddhas, boundless koṭis of buddhas, boundless hundreds of koṭis of buddhas, boundless thousands of koṭis of buddhas, boundless hundreds of thousands of koṭis of buddhas, and boundless hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of buddhas, drawing near to these buddhas with supreme, sublime offerings, revering, honoring, and praising them, and, in the presence of these buddhas, through manifold questions and objections, thoroughly penetrating the dhāraṇī of expounding the Dharma. This bodhisattva — these roots of goodness of his become ever more excellent, radiant, and pure. Son of the Buddha! It is like genuine gold that has been fashioned into an ornament and then fastened upon the neck or the crown of a wheel-turning sage king — no ornament belonging to any lesser king or to the people of the four continents is able to equal it. Just so, son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, dwelling in this bodhisattva's Sādhumatī Ground, these roots of goodness of his become ever more excellent, radiant, and pure — no śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, or bodhisattva of a lower ground is able to destroy them. This bodhisattva's roots of goodness, becoming ever more radiant, are able to illumine the dense thicket-places of sentient beings' own afflicted minds, and, having illumined them, withdraw once more. Son of the Buddha! It is like a Great Brahmā King who is able to illumine every deep, dense thicket-place within two thousand worlds entirely.

Just so, son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, dwelling in this bodhisattva's Sādhumatī Ground, the light of these roots of goodness of his illumines the dense thicket-places of every sentient being's own afflicted mind, and, having illumined them, withdraws once more. Among this bodhisattva's ten perfections, the perfection of power now excels — the other perfections he does not fail to cultivate, but in accordance with his own strength and portion. Son of the Buddha! This is what is briefly called a bodhisattva's Ninth Ground, the bodhisattva's Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom; spoken of at length, throughout boundless eons, it could not be exhausted. Should a bodhisattva dwell in this ground, he often becomes a Great Brahmā Heaven King, attaining great power and sovereignty over two thousand worlds, self-mastering among the self-mastering, his true and correct understanding most excellent of all, well able to proclaim the practice of the perfections belonging to śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and bodhisattvas — and, questioned and challenged by sentient beings, there is none able to exhaust him. The wholesome deeds he performs — giving, kind speech, beneficial action, and cooperative work — every one of these merits is never apart from mindfulness of the buddhas, mindfulness of the Dharma, mindfulness of the monastic assembly, mindfulness of every bodhisattva, mindfulness of the bodhisattva's own practice, mindfulness of every perfection, mindfulness of the ten grounds, mindfulness of unbreakable power, mindfulness of fearlessness, mindfulness of the unshared buddha-dharmas, all the way to never apart from mindfulness of being complete in the wisdom of all modes, the wisdom of omniscience. He constantly gives rise to this thought: among all sentient beings, I shall be foremost, I shall be supreme, I shall be great, I shall be sublime, I shall be exceedingly sublime, I shall be highest, I shall be unsurpassed, I shall be a guide, I shall be a leader, I shall be a teacher, I shall be one held in honor, all the way to being the very support of the wisdom of omniscience. Further, from this thought he sets forth diligent exertion. Through the power of this diligence, within a single thought-moment he attains a number of samādhis equal to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, sees a number of buddhas equal to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, knows the spiritual power of buddhas equal in number to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, is able to shake worlds equal in number to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, is able to enter worlds equal in number to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, is able to illumine worlds equal in number to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, is able to transform the sentient beings of worlds equal in number to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, is able to dwell in life for a number of eons equal to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, is able to know the affairs of each past and future era equal in number to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, is able to enter skillfully into a number of Dharma-gateways equal to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, and is able to transform his own body into a number equal to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, manifesting, within each and every one of those bodies, a number of bodhisattvas equal to the fine motes of dust in ten asaṃkhyeya hundred-thousand buddha-lands, as its own retinue. Should he, through the power of vow, be self-mastering and supreme, a bodhisattva's power of vow manifests beyond even this number, manifesting all manner of spiritual power — whether of body, or of radiance, or of spiritual power itself, or of the eye, or of the object-realm, or of voice, or of activity, or of adornment, or of empowerment, or of faith, or of karmic function — every one of these spiritual powers, throughout boundless hundreds of thousands of myriad koṭis of niyutas of eons, beyond all counting or knowing.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang maps this entire, unglossed sūtra passage onto the three remaining parts of his fourfold "Dharma-teacher" scheme. 二「是菩薩於一佛」下明說成就。於中三：初明能受多法；二能廣說法；三能起說自在 — "second, from 'this bodhisattva, in the presence of a single buddha' onward, explains accomplishing teaching, which has three parts: first, showing his ability to receive manifold teachings; second, his ability to expound the Dharma at length; third, his ability to give rise to eloquence at his own ease" — this last being the passage of one voice heard as many, light alone teaching, and pores and dust motes each emitting the Dharma. He then reads the multiplying-trichiliocosm passage as 問答成就, accomplishing question-and-answer, structured in two stages: 初一世界中答難、二明一切世界 — "first, answering questions within a single world; second, explaining every world" — the single world standing as the base case for what then scales without limit. The hair-tip and dust-mote passage he reads as 受持成就, accomplishing further reception, likewise in two stages, 先於一塵處、後類一切塵 — "first at a single mote of dust, and then extended, by analogy, to every mote of dust."

On the ground's closing fruit, Fazang notes only that it follows the same pattern already established: 下明地果，三果同前 — "below, this explains the fruit of the ground; the threefold fruit is the same as before." He closes his commentary on the whole Ninth Ground by tallying its verse-summary — forty-two and a half verses divided into six groups, the ninefold-and-a-half breakdown of the fourth group tracking persistence, teaching, questioning, and reception in turn — before a single final verse: 六末後一頌歎深結說 — "sixth, the very last verse praises its profundity and closes the exposition." He ends the passage with the four characters that close out the entire ground: 善慧地竟 — "the explanation of the Ninth Ground is complete."

Part 45 Complete — Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom, Now Entirely Translated

This closes Part 45, and with it the entire Ninth Ground: Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom. Parts 41 through 45 — the extensive "thicket" analysis of sentient beings, the threefold grouping of beings, this bodhisattva's becoming a great Dharma teacher, the long, systematic exposition of the four unhindered wisdoms across many paired topics, and now his accomplishment of retention through ten named kinds of dhāraṇī, his boundless eloquence at the Dharma-seat answering every question of every world at once, and the ground's closing fruit of countless buddhas seen without interruption, genuine gold fastened to a wheel-turning king's own crown, and a Great Brahmā King's radiance filling two thousand worlds at once — have now translated the whole of fascicle eleven of this twelve-fascicle treatise. Part 46 turns to Dharmameghā, the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud — the Tenth and final Ground — where Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 continues as this collection's reading-note source, closing the entire treatise.
