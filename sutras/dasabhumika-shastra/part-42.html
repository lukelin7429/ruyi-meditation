Faculties, Latent Tendencies, Birth-Destinies, and Habitual Tendencies

The treatise continues Sādhumatī's systematic "thicket" analysis of sentient beings, turning now to four further thickets beyond mind, affliction, and karma already treated. The ninefold thicket of faculties analyzes sentient beings' capacities as gentle, middling, or superior, and much more — the very "vessel" to which the Dharma is entrusted. The thicket of latent tendencies (使) takes up the treatise's own etymology: named, like a courier who pursues and binds a fugitive, for its own function of "following and binding" the sentient beings within whom it lies dormant. The eightfold thicket of birth-destinies traces the manifold ways beings are born across hell-beings, animals, hungry ghosts, asuras, humans, and gods — karma as field, craving as water, ignorance as darkness, consciousness as seed. And the tenfold thicket of habitual tendencies closes this part, tracking the residual imprints that karma and affliction leave behind even once the affliction itself has already ceased.

The Thicket of Faculties, and the Thicket of Latent Tendencies

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩如實知諸根濡中上差別相，先際後際別異不別異相、上中下相，煩惱共生不離相，乘非乘定不定相、淳熟定相，隨根網輕轉壞取相相、根增上不壞相、轉不轉根差別相，深入共生種種差別相，略說乃至如實知八萬四千諸根差別相。是菩薩如實知眾生信濡中利相，略說乃至如實知八萬四千信差別相。是菩薩如實知諸性濡中上相，略說乃至如實知八萬四千諸性差別相。是菩薩如實知心濡中上相，略說乃至如實知八萬四千心差別相。

論曰：根行稠林差別有九種：一、說器差別，如經「是菩薩如實知諸根濡中上差別相」故。二、根轉差別，如經「先際後際別異不別異相」故。三、性差別，如經「上中下相」故。四、煩惱染差別，如經「煩惱共生不離相」故。五、定不定差別，如經「乘非乘定不定相」故、「淳熟定相」故。六、順行差別，如經「隨根網輕轉壞取相相」故。七、聲聞淨差別，如經「根增上不壞相」故。八、菩薩淨差別，如經「轉不轉根差別相」故。九、示一切根攝差別，如經「深入共生種種差別相」故，「略說乃至如實知八萬四千諸根差別相」故。是中根轉差別者，前後根前根下增平故。性差別者，於三乘中性差別故。煩惱染差別者，喜樂等諸根隨煩惱習使染故。定不定差別者，於三乘中於世間中定不定、熟不熟故。是中小乘不定根眾生，菩薩令轉向大乘故；定根者菩薩令度，二乘中解脫報定者捨。順行差別者，有三種順行：一、身依順行，迭共相縛六入展轉故。二、生滅順行，輕壞故。三、觀行取相故。聲聞淨差別者，行增上障滅，能成義故。菩薩淨差別者，轉不轉地差別故。一切根攝差別者，始行方便報熟根差別故。信性心濡中上等無量差別相皆如實知，如經「是菩薩如實知眾生信濡中上相，乃至如實知八萬四千心差別相」故。如是性入應知。云何使行稠林差別？

經曰：是菩薩如實知諸使深共生心共生相、心相應不相應不離相、遠入相、無始來不恐怖相、一切禪定解脫三昧三摩跋提神通正修相違相、堅繫縛三界繫相、無始來心相續集相、開諸入門集相、得對治實相、地入隨順不隨順相、不異聖道滅動相，略說乃至如實知八萬四千種種使差別相。

論曰：是中使者，隨逐縛義故。此使行稠林差別者，何處隨逐？以何隨逐？此事差別示現。何處隨逐者，報非報心，如經「是菩薩如實知使深共生心共生相」故，心不離現事故。欲色無色上中下差別，如經「心相應不相應不離相」故。隨順乃至有頂，如經「遠入相」故。無邊世界唯智，怖畏如怨賊，未曾有聞思修智，是故不滅，如經「無始來不恐怖相」故。世間禪定等不能滅心隨順行，如經「一切禪定解脫三昧三摩跋提神通正修相違相」故。以何隨逐者，有六種隨逐。六種隨逐者六句說：一者有不斷隨逐，以有不斷相似使作縛故，如經「堅繫縛三界繫相」故。二、遠時隨逐故，如經「無始來心相續集相」故。三、一身生隨逐故，眼等諸入門六種生集識同生隨逐故，及阿黎耶熏故隨逐，如經「開諸入門集相」故。四、不實隨逐，對治實義故，如經「得對治實相」故。五、微細隨逐，於九地中六入處煩惱身隨逐故，如經「地入隨順不隨順相」故。六、離苦隨逐，出世間行餘行不能離故，如經「不異聖道滅動相故，略說乃至如實知八萬四千種種使差別相」故。云何生行稠林差別？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva truly knows sentient beings' faculties in their distinguishing marks of gentle, middling, and superior; the mark of whether they are distinct or not distinct with respect to the prior limit and the later limit; the mark of superior, middling, and inferior; the mark of being born together with affliction, inseparable from it; the mark of the vehicle or the non-vehicle, determined or undetermined; the mark of ripened determination; the mark of following the net of the faculties, lightly turning, breakable, grasping at marks; the mark of the faculties' own excellence, indestructible; the mark of the distinction between the faculty that turns and the faculty that does not turn; and the mark of the manifold distinctions born together through deep penetration — in brief, all the way to truly knowing the eighty-four thousand distinguishing marks of the faculties. This bodhisattva truly knows sentient beings' faith in its marks of gentle, middling, and sharp — in brief, all the way to truly knowing the eighty-four thousand distinguishing marks of faith. This bodhisattva truly knows sentient beings' natures in their marks of gentle, middling, and superior — in brief, all the way to truly knowing the eighty-four thousand distinguishing marks of nature. This bodhisattva truly knows sentient beings' minds in their marks of gentle, middling, and superior — in brief, all the way to truly knowing the eighty-four thousand distinguishing marks of mind.

Commentary: The distinctions of the thicket of faculties are of nine kinds: first, the distinction of the vessel spoken of — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows sentient beings' faculties in their distinguishing marks of gentle, middling, and superior." Second, the distinction of the turning of the faculties — as the sūtra says, "the mark of whether they are distinct or not distinct with respect to the prior limit and the later limit." Third, the distinction of nature — as the sūtra says, "the mark of superior, middling, and inferior." Fourth, the distinction of affliction's defilement — as the sūtra says, "the mark of being born together with affliction, inseparable from it." Fifth, the distinction of determined and undetermined — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the vehicle or the non-vehicle, determined or undetermined," and "the mark of ripened determination." Sixth, the distinction of accordant practice — as the sūtra says, "the mark of following the net of the faculties, lightly turning, breakable, grasping at marks." Seventh, the distinction of the śrāvaka's own purity — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the faculties' own excellence, indestructible." Eighth, the distinction of the bodhisattva's own purity — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the distinction between the faculty that turns and the faculty that does not turn." Ninth, showing the distinction that gathers in every faculty — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the manifold distinctions born together through deep penetration," and "in brief, all the way to truly knowing the eighty-four thousand distinguishing marks of the faculties." Here, "the distinction of the turning of the faculties" means that the prior faculty and the subsequent faculty increase or diminish relative to one another, level with one another. "The distinction of nature" means the distinction of nature within the three vehicles. "The distinction of affliction's defilement" means that the faculties of joy, pleasure, and the rest are defiled in accordance with the latent tendency of affliction. "The distinction of determined and undetermined" means being determined or undetermined, ripened or unripened, within the three vehicles, within the world. Here, among sentient beings of undetermined faculty within the small vehicle, the bodhisattva causes them to turn toward the great vehicle; among those of determined faculty, the bodhisattva causes them to cross over; but among the two vehicles, those determined in the recompense of liberation, he relinquishes. "The distinction of accordant practice" has three kinds of accordant practice: first, accordant practice dependent on the body, since the six sense-fields, bound mutually together, turn on one another in succession; second, accordant practice of arising and ceasing, since it is light and easily broken; third, contemplative practice grasping at marks. "The distinction of the śrāvaka's own purity" means that, his practice having become excellent, the hindrance is extinguished, since he is able to accomplish this meaning. "The distinction of the bodhisattva's own purity" means the distinction between the ground that turns and the ground that does not turn. "The distinction gathering in every faculty" means the distinction of the faculty that, from the outset of practice through skillful means, ripens into recompense. Faith, nature, and mind, in their marks of gentle, middling, and superior and the rest, boundless in their distinctions, are all truly known — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows sentient beings' faith in its marks of gentle, middling, and superior," all the way to "truly knowing the eighty-four thousand distinguishing marks of mind." This should be understood as the entry into nature. What, then, is the distinction of the thicket of latent tendencies?

Sūtra: This bodhisattva truly knows the latent tendencies in their mark of being born together with the deep mind and born together with the mind; the mark of according or not according with the mind, yet not separate from it; the mark of penetrating far within; the mark of being, from beginningless time, not to be feared; the mark of standing in contradiction to the correct cultivation of every meditative concentration, liberation, samādhi, samāpatti, and spiritual power; the mark of firmly binding, bound within the three realms; the mark of the mind's continuous gathering from beginningless time; the mark of the gathering that opens every sense-gate; the mark of the reality attained through the antidote; the mark of according or not according with entry into the grounds; and the mark of extinguishing the movement not different from the noble path — in brief, all the way to truly knowing the eighty-four thousand manifold distinguishing marks of the latent tendencies.

Commentary: Here, "latent tendency" means the meaning of following after and binding. As for the distinctions of this thicket of latent tendencies — where do they follow after? By what do they follow after? This matter is shown through its own distinctions. As for "where they follow after": it is the mind of retribution and the mind not of retribution — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows the latent tendencies in their mark of being born together with the deep mind and born together with the mind," since the mind is not separate from the manifest matter. It is the distinction of superior, middling, and inferior within the realm of desire, the realm of form, and the formless realm — as the sūtra says, "the mark of according or not according with the mind, yet not separate from it." It accords all the way up to the peak of existence — as the sūtra says, "the mark of penetrating far within." Throughout boundless worlds, only wisdom — feared as one fears an enemy or a bandit — since he has never yet attained the wisdom born of hearing, reflection, and cultivation, it is for this reason not extinguished — as the sūtra says, "the mark of being, from beginningless time, not to be feared." Worldly meditative concentration and the rest are unable to extinguish the mind's accordant activity — as the sūtra says, "the mark of standing in contradiction to the correct cultivation of every meditative concentration, liberation, samādhi, samāpatti, and spiritual power." As for "by what they follow after": there are six kinds of following-after, set out in six clauses. First, following-after through what is unbroken, since it is through a latent tendency unbroken and continuous in kind that binding is done — as the sūtra says, "the mark of firmly binding, bound within the three realms." Second, following-after across distant time — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the mind's continuous gathering from beginningless time." Third, following-after born together within a single body, since the six kinds of consciousness gathered and born through the sense-gates such as the eye and the rest are born together with it, following after it, and it follows after through the perfuming of the ālaya-consciousness as well — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the gathering that opens every sense-gate." Fourth, following-after that is not real, since it is the true meaning of the antidote — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the reality attained through the antidote." Fifth, subtle following-after, since, throughout the six sense-fields within the nine grounds, affliction follows after the body — as the sūtra says, "the mark of according or not according with entry into the grounds." Sixth, following-after that departs from suffering, since no practice other than the supramundane practice is able to depart from it — as the sūtra says, "the mark of extinguishing the movement not different from the noble path — in brief, all the way to truly knowing the eighty-four thousand manifold distinguishing marks of the latent tendencies." What, then, is the distinction of the thicket of birth-destinies?

Reading note — after Fazang

On the thicket of faculties, Fazang glosses its opening term concretely: 一軟中上者是說器故，謂是說法所授之器 — "the first, 'gentle, middling, and superior,' means the vessel spoken of — that is, the very vessel to which the Dharma taught is entrusted," also called the dull, middling, and sharp faculty, distinguished by how quickly or slowly, how deeply or shallowly, how broadly or narrowly a hearer receives it. On the fifth item, the determined-and-undetermined thicket, he draws directly on the Abhidharmakośa for its account of how affliction attaches itself to feeling: 於樂受貪隨增，於苦受瞋隨增，於不苦不樂受無明隨增 — "craving increases in accordance with pleasant feeling, aversion increases in accordance with painful feeling, and ignorance increases in accordance with feeling neither painful nor pleasant." He adds a candid aside on doctrinal framing: 此約初教大乘，許五性別故作是說 — "this is stated from the standpoint of the initial teaching of the great vehicle, which permits the distinction of the five natures" — flagging that the treatise's own talk of some faculties as permanently fixed reflects one particular doctrinal position, not an uncontested one.

On the thicket of latent tendencies, Fazang gives the treatise's own etymology in full: 使者，隨逐縛義，從喻名使。如公使逐賊，得便繫縛；煩惱亦爾，隨逐眾生，得便繫縛 — "'latent tendency' [使] means the sense of following after and binding; it is named after a simile — just as a government courier pursuing a fugitive binds him the moment he is caught, so too does affliction follow after sentient beings, binding them the moment it catches hold." He also maps the passage's own architecture: 十句中，初四句明何處隨逐，後六句以何隨逐 — "of the ten clauses, the first four make clear where it follows after, and the last six make clear by what means it follows after" — noting that the latent tendency's subtlety is such that it cannot be seen, "like oil within a sesame seed" (如麻中油不可現見), present yet undetectable until pressed out.

The Thicket of Birth-Destinies, and the Thicket of Habitual Tendencies

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩如實知諸生差別相、隨業生相、地獄畜生餓鬼阿修羅人天差別相、有色無色生差別相、有想無想生差別相。業是田、愛是水、無明是黑闇、識是種子，後身是生芽相、名色共生而不離相、有癡求愛相續相、欲受欲生樂眾生相續無際相、貪著三界相出相，皆如實知。

論曰：生行稠林差別有八種：一、身種種，如經「是菩薩如實知諸生差別相」故。二、業種種，如經「隨業生相」故。三、住處種種，如經「地獄畜生餓鬼阿修羅人天差別相」故。四、色相上下種種，如經「有色無色生差別相」故、「有想無想生差別相」故。五、同外色因種種，如經「業是田、愛是水、無明是黑闇、識是種子，後身是生芽相」故。六、自相種種，如經「名色共生而不離相」故。七、本順生因種種，如經「有癡求愛相續相」故。八、集苦諦種種差別示現，如經「欲受欲生樂眾生相續無際相」故、「貪著三界相出相皆如實知」故。是中欲受者，樂貪共取，處處求故。欲生者，復有樂有眾生，愛自身、他身，心著相往來上下界取著故。小大無量無想相，出有輪展轉苦諦差別示現。云何習氣行稠林差別？

經曰：是菩薩如實知習氣行不行差別相，隨道生處熏有習氣、隨共眾生行有習氣、隨業煩惱有習氣、隨善不善無記法有習氣、後有有習氣、次第隨逐有習氣、深入不斷煩惱牽有習氣、有實不實有習氣、聲聞辟支佛菩薩如來見聞親近熏有習氣，皆如實知。

論曰：習氣行稠林差別有十種：一、與果現在非現在差別，如經「是菩薩如實知習氣行不行相」故。二、道熏差別，如經「隨道生處熏有習氣」故。三親近眾生熏差別，如經「隨共眾生行行有習氣」故。四、功業煩惱熏差別，如經「隨業煩惱有習氣」故。五、善業等熏差別，如經「隨善不善無記法有習氣」故。六、中陰熏差別，如經「後有有習氣」故。七、與果次第熏差別，如經「次第隨逐有習氣」故。八、離世間禪因熏差別，遠入熏不斷煩惱煩惱牽故，如經「深入不斷煩惱牽有習氣」故。九、同法異外道行解脫熏差別，如經「有實不實有習氣」故。十、乘熏差別示現，如經「聲聞辟支佛菩薩如來見聞親近熏有習氣皆如實知」故。云何三聚行稠林差別？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva truly knows the mark of the manifold distinctions among births; the mark of birth in accordance with karma; the mark of the distinctions among hell-beings, animals, hungry ghosts, asuras, humans, and gods; the mark of the distinctions of birth with form and without form; the mark of the distinctions of birth with conception and without conception. Karma is the field, craving is the water, ignorance is the darkness, and consciousness is the seed; the subsequent body is the mark of the sprout of birth; name-and-form is born together, inseparable in mark; there is the mark of the continuity of ignorant craving and seeking; there is the mark of the boundless continuity of sentient beings who desire to receive and desire the pleasure of birth; and there is the mark of the manifestation of attachment to the three realms — all of these he truly knows.

Commentary: The distinctions of the thicket of birth-destinies are of eight kinds: first, the manifold varieties of body — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows the mark of the manifold distinctions among births." Second, the manifold varieties of karma — as the sūtra says, "the mark of birth in accordance with karma." Third, the manifold varieties of dwelling-place — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the distinctions among hell-beings, animals, hungry ghosts, asuras, humans, and gods." Fourth, the manifold varieties of higher and lower material mark — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the distinctions of birth with form and without form," and "the mark of the distinctions of birth with conception and without conception." Fifth, the manifold varieties of the external cause resembling matter — as the sūtra says, "karma is the field, craving is the water, ignorance is the darkness, and consciousness is the seed; the subsequent body is the mark of the sprout of birth." Sixth, the manifold varieties of one's own mark — as the sūtra says, "name-and-form is born together, inseparable in mark." Seventh, the manifold varieties of the fundamental, accordant cause of birth — as the sūtra says, "there is the mark of the continuity of ignorant craving and seeking." Eighth, showing forth the manifold distinctions of the truth of the origin of suffering — as the sūtra says, "there is the mark of the boundless continuity of sentient beings who desire to receive and desire the pleasure of birth," and "there is the mark of the manifestation of attachment to the three realms — all of these he truly knows." Here, "desiring to receive" means grasping together with pleasure and craving, seeking everywhere. "Desiring birth" means, further, that there are sentient beings who take pleasure in craving their own body and the body of others, their mind attached to marks, coming and going, grasping and attached within the higher and lower realms. Small, great, and boundless, and the mark of the absence of conception — these show forth, in manifold distinctions, the truth of the origin of suffering, turning about within the wheel of existence. What, then, is the distinction of the thicket of habitual tendencies?

Sūtra: This bodhisattva truly knows the distinguishing marks of habitual tendencies, active and inactive: there is the habitual tendency perfumed in accordance with the destiny of birth on the path; there is the habitual tendency in accordance with practice alongside sentient beings; there is the habitual tendency in accordance with karma and affliction; there is the habitual tendency in accordance with wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate dharmas; there is the habitual tendency of subsequent existence; there is the habitual tendency of successive following-after; there is the habitual tendency drawn onward by affliction, deeply entered and unbroken; there is the habitual tendency, whether real or unreal; and there is the habitual tendency perfumed through seeing, hearing, and drawing near to śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, bodhisattvas, and tathāgatas — all of these he truly knows.

Commentary: The distinctions of the thicket of habitual tendencies are of ten kinds: first, the distinction of whether the habitual tendency is present or not present together with its fruit — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows the distinguishing marks of habitual tendencies, active and inactive." Second, the distinction of the perfuming of the path — as the sūtra says, "there is the habitual tendency perfumed in accordance with the destiny of birth on the path." Third, the distinction of the perfuming of drawing near to sentient beings — as the sūtra says, "there is the habitual tendency in accordance with practice alongside sentient beings." Fourth, the distinction of the perfuming of karmic action and affliction — as the sūtra says, "there is the habitual tendency in accordance with karma and affliction." Fifth, the distinction of the perfuming of wholesome karma and the rest — as the sūtra says, "there is the habitual tendency in accordance with wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate dharmas." Sixth, the distinction of the perfuming of the intermediate state — as the sūtra says, "there is the habitual tendency of subsequent existence." Seventh, the distinction of the perfuming that is in sequence together with its fruit — as the sūtra says, "there is the habitual tendency of successive following-after." Eighth, the distinction of the perfuming of the cause that departs from worldly meditative concentration, since, penetrating far within, the perfuming of unbroken affliction is drawn onward by affliction — as the sūtra says, "there is the habitual tendency drawn onward by affliction, deeply entered and unbroken." Ninth, the distinction of the perfuming of the practice of liberation, whether the same in dharma or different, as with the followers of other paths — as the sūtra says, "there is the habitual tendency, whether real or unreal." Tenth, showing forth the distinction of the perfuming of the vehicle — as the sūtra says, "there is the habitual tendency perfumed through seeing, hearing, and drawing near to śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, bodhisattvas, and tathāgatas — all of these he truly knows." What, then, is the distinction of the thicket differentiated by the threefold grouping?

Reading note — after Fazang

On the fourth item of the birth-destiny thicket — birth "with form and without form" — Fazang anticipates an obvious objection and answers it from within Yogācāra cosmology: 既是無色，何故言色上下？答：依大乘，無色界中有微細色，是色中上也 — "since it is formless, why does the text speak of 'form,' higher and lower? Answer: according to the great vehicle, even within the formless realm there exists a subtle form — this is what 'form' means here, superior among the superior." He makes a matching point about the "no-conception" heaven, noting that non-Buddhist paths mistake it for liberation itself: 外道取彼為涅槃故 — "because the followers of other paths take that very state to be nirvāṇa."

On the ninth item of the habitual-tendency thicket — the mark of habitual tendency "whether real or unreal" — Fazang traces the distinction to who holds it: 同法是二乘解脫，以諸外道不同佛法故名為異，彼取無想以為解脫...故云有實不實，實是小乘、不實是外 — "what accords with the Dharma is the liberation of the two lesser vehicles; because the followers of other paths differ from the Buddhadharma, they are called 'different' — they take the state of no-conception to be liberation...hence it is said, 'whether real or unreal': the real belongs to the lesser vehicles, the unreal belongs to the outsiders." The same habitual residue, in other words, can look identical from the outside while marking two entirely different attainments beneath it.

Part 42 Complete

This closes Part 42, continuing Sādhumatī, the Ninth Ground's, systematic thicket analysis of sentient beings. Building on Part 41's opening thickets of mind, affliction, and karma, this part has translated four further thickets in full: the ninefold thicket of faculties, distinguishing sentient beings' capacities as gentle, middling, or superior, and identifying the very vessel to which the Dharma is entrusted; the thicket of latent tendencies, the dispositions that, as the treatise's own etymology has it, "follow and bind" like a courier pursuing a fugitive; the eightfold thicket of birth-destinies, tracing how beings are born across hell-beings, animals, hungry ghosts, asuras, humans, and gods, karma as field and craving as water; and the tenfold thicket of habitual tendencies, the residue that karma and affliction leave behind even once the affliction itself has already ceased. Part 43 completes this thicket analysis with its final member — the threefold grouping of sentient beings as correctly settled, wrongly settled, or undetermined — before opening this ground's next division, the "accomplishing teaching" division, in which the bodhisattva becomes a great Dharma teacher and the treatise begins its exposition of the four unhindered wisdoms.
