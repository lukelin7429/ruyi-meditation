The Second Ground Complete

The bodhisattva's contemplation of suffering beings closes with those shut up in the prison-house of the triple world and those who cling to a self among the non-Buddhist paths, followed by those who timidly settle for the Śrāvaka or Pratyekabuddha vehicle; and, closing the entire Second Ground, the threefold fruit — gentle taming, gathered retribution as a Wheel-Turning King, and the wisdom of vow, gold now refined not by fire alone but by fire and alum together — before the treatise turns, at last, to Prabhākarī, the Luminous Ground.

Realm-Difference, Self-Clinging, and the Timid Lesser Vehicle — The Nine Contemplations Complete

Chinese Source

經曰：是諸眾生閉在世間牢獄之處，眾多患苦多惱妄想，愛憎繫縛、憂悲共行，愛鎖所繫，入於三界無明稠林所覆。我應令彼眾生遠離一切三界所著，令住離相無礙涅槃。

論曰：有求眾生界差別者，閉在牢獄過，如經「是諸眾生閉在世間牢獄之處」故。此牢獄過有五種隨逐應知：一、苦事；二、財盡；三、愛離；四者縛；五、障礙。此示五種難差別：一者無病難，多種病苦妄想愁惱，如經「眾多患苦多惱妄想」故。二者資生難，於愛不愛事中憎愛所縛，如經「愛憎繫縛」故。三者親難，親愛離壞憂悲增長，如經「憂悲共行」故。四者戒難，雖生色無色中暫離犯戒，不免戒行相違愛欲使縛，如經「愛鎖所繫」故。五者見難，得世間智，彼相違無明使之所覆蔽，如經「入於三界無明稠林所覆」故。彼諸眾生隨順對治，以如實法令住離相無礙涅槃，如經「我應令彼眾生遠離一切三界所著，令住離相無礙涅槃」故。云何邪見諸外道等？

經曰：是諸眾生深著我相，於五陰巢窟不能自出，行四顛倒、依六入空聚，常為四大毒蛇之所侵惱，為五陰怨賊之所殺害，受此一切無量苦惱。我應令彼眾生離一切障礙，令住空無我智道，所謂涅槃，滅一切障礙。

論曰：邪梵行諸外道等者，執取我相過，此餘見根本，如經「是諸眾生深著我相」故。彼諸眾生欲趣涅槃城，以有我故於五陰舍不能動發，如經「於五陰巢窟不能自出」故。欲行正道，以顛倒故行彼邪道，如經「行四顛倒」故。依恃我想欲趣涅槃，虛妄我見住六入聚，如經「依六入空聚」故。受老病死等諸苦，意欲遠離而不得離，恒隨己身，如經「常為四大毒蛇之所侵惱」故。陰怨隨逐而不放捨，如經「為五陰怨賊之所殺害」故。常為種種諸苦隨逐，如經「受此一切無量苦惱」故。彼諸眾生隨順對治，以如實法離一切障，令住涅槃，如經「我應令彼眾生離一切障礙，令住空無我智道，所謂涅槃，滅一切障礙」故。云何正梵行？同法小乘等。

經曰：是諸眾生小心狹劣不求大乘，其心遠離無上一切智智，等有出行而樂聲聞辟支佛乘。我應令彼眾生安住微妙無上佛法深廣大意。諸佛子！菩薩如是隨順持戒力，善能廣起方便行故。

論曰：行正梵行求小乘過。此小乘意有二種：一者小心，佛法微妙廣大無量，其心退沒而不能證故；二者狹心，於無量眾生作利益懈怠故。如經「是諸眾生小心狹劣，不求大乘法」故。復依小乘心願過，願小乘故，如經「其心遠離無上一切智智」故。修行過，不定聚眾生實有大乘出法而修行小乘，如經「等有出行而樂聲聞辟支佛乘」故。彼諸眾生隨順對治，以如實法令住微妙無上佛法廣大心故，如經「我應令彼眾生安住微妙無上佛法深廣大意」故。依持戒行故，得此戒力能作善法，善巧起諸善行故，如經「諸佛子！菩薩如是隨順持戒力，善能廣起方便行故」。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: These sentient beings are shut up within the prison-house of the world, beset by manifold afflictions and suffering, tormented by false thought, bound by the fetters of love and hate, ever accompanied by grief and sorrow, fettered by the chain of love, covered by the dense thicket of ignorance within the triple world. I must cause these sentient beings to depart from every attachment to the triple world, and cause them to abide in the unobstructed nirvāṇa free of characteristics.

Commentary: With respect to the difference among the realms of sentient beings for those who seek the holy life, there is the fault of being shut up in a prison-house — as the sūtra says, "these sentient beings are shut up within the prison-house of the world." This fault of the prison-house is accompanied, it should be understood, by five kinds of affliction that follow it everywhere: first, the matter of suffering; second, the exhaustion of wealth; third, separation from what one loves; fourth, bondage; and fifth, obstruction. This shows a difference among five kinds of difficulty. First, the difficulty of having no health — the manifold suffering of illness, false thought, and sorrow — as the sūtra says, "beset by manifold afflictions and suffering, tormented by false thought." Second, the difficulty of the necessities of life, bound by hatred and love amid what is loved and what is not loved — as the sūtra says, "bound by the fetters of love and hate." Third, the difficulty of kinship, grief and sorrow increasing as those dear to one are separated and destroyed — as the sūtra says, "ever accompanied by grief and sorrow." Fourth, the difficulty of moral discipline: though born within the realm of form or the formless realm, one is only temporarily free of transgressing the precepts, and cannot escape the affliction-fetter of craving that runs contrary to the conduct of moral discipline — as the sūtra says, "fettered by the chain of love." Fifth, the difficulty of views: attaining worldly wisdom, one remains covered over by the contrary affliction-fetter of ignorance — as the sūtra says, "covered by the dense thicket of ignorance within the triple world." Toward these sentient beings, in accordance with the antidote, by means of the dharma of suchness he causes them to abide in the unobstructed nirvāṇa free of characteristics — as the sūtra says, "I must cause these sentient beings to depart from every attachment to the triple world, and cause them to abide in the unobstructed nirvāṇa free of characteristics." What is the wrong view of the various non-Buddhist paths?

Sūtra: These sentient beings, deeply attached to the mark of self, are unable to leave the lair of the five aggregates of their own accord; they practice the four inversions, and dwell within the empty village of the six sense-fields; they are constantly afflicted and tormented by the four great elements as though by venomous serpents, and are slain by the five-aggregate thieves, undergoing every manner of boundless suffering and affliction as a result. I must cause these sentient beings to depart from every obstruction, and cause them to abide in the path of the wisdom of emptiness and no-self, namely nirvāṇa, which extinguishes every obstruction.

Commentary: "The various non-Buddhist seekers of a wrongful holy life" have the fault of grasping at the mark of self, which is the root of every other view — as the sūtra says, "these sentient beings are deeply attached to the mark of self." These sentient beings wish to advance toward the city of nirvāṇa, but, because they hold to a self, they are unable to bestir themselves to leave the dwelling of the five aggregates — as the sūtra says, "unable to leave the lair of the five aggregates of their own accord." Wishing to practice the correct path, because of their inverted thinking they instead practice a wrongful path — as the sūtra says, "they practice the four inversions." Relying on and clinging to the notion of self, wishing to advance toward nirvāṇa, their false view of self dwells within the six sense-fields — as the sūtra says, "dwell within the empty village of the six sense-fields." Undergoing every suffering of old age, sickness, and death, they wish to depart from it but are unable to, for it constantly follows their own person — as the sūtra says, "constantly afflicted and tormented by the four great elements as though by venomous serpents." The aggregate-thieves pursue them without ever releasing them — as the sūtra says, "slain by the five-aggregate thieves." They are constantly pursued by every manner of suffering — as the sūtra says, "undergoing every manner of boundless suffering and affliction as a result." Toward these sentient beings, in accordance with the antidote, by means of the dharma of suchness he causes them to depart from every obstruction and abide in nirvāṇa — as the sūtra says, "I must cause these sentient beings to depart from every obstruction, and cause them to abide in the path of the wisdom of emptiness and no-self, namely nirvāṇa, which extinguishes every obstruction." What is the correct holy life? Those who share the same Dharma, yet incline toward the Lesser Vehicle, and the rest.

Sūtra: These sentient beings, their minds small and narrow, do not seek the Great Vehicle; their minds are far removed from the unsurpassed wisdom of omniscience; though they equally possess the path of going forth, they delight instead in the vehicle of the Śrāvaka or the Pratyekabuddha. I must cause these sentient beings to abide securely in the sublime, unsurpassed Buddha-dharma, its profound and vast great meaning. Sons of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, by thus according with the power of upholding moral discipline, is well able to broadly give rise to expedient practice.

Commentary: Those who practice the correct holy life yet seek the Lesser Vehicle have this fault. This intention toward the Lesser Vehicle is of two kinds: first, a small mind, since the Buddha-dharma is sublime, vast, and boundless, yet their mind retreats and shrinks back, unable to realize it; second, a narrow mind, since they are slothful in bringing benefit to boundless sentient beings — as the sūtra says, "these sentient beings, their minds small and narrow, do not seek the dharma of the Great Vehicle." There is, further, the fault of their vow and aspiration relying on the Lesser Vehicle, since they vow for the Lesser Vehicle — as the sūtra says, "their minds are far removed from the unsurpassed wisdom of omniscience." There is the fault in their practice: sentient beings of indeterminate lineage, who in truth possess the dharma of going forth by the Great Vehicle, instead practice the Lesser Vehicle — as the sūtra says, "though they equally possess the path of going forth, they delight instead in the vehicle of the Śrāvaka or the Pratyekabuddha." Toward these sentient beings, in accordance with the antidote, by means of the dharma of suchness he causes them to abide in the vast mind of the sublime, unsurpassed Buddha-dharma — as the sūtra says, "I must cause these sentient beings to abide securely in the sublime, unsurpassed Buddha-dharma, its profound and vast great meaning." In dependence on the practice of upholding moral discipline, he attains this power of moral discipline, able to perform wholesome deeds, skillfully giving rise to every wholesome practice — as the sūtra says, "Sons of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, by thus according with the power of upholding moral discipline, is well able to broadly give rise to expedient practice."

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Huiyuan reads the prison-house of the "realm-difference" contemplation as a five-part structure, not a single undifferentiated image of confinement: 三界難出，事如牢獄，執相堅故，名之為閉 — "the triple world is hard to escape; the matter is like a prison, and because the grasping at characteristics is so firm, this is called 'being shut in.'" He then sorts the five afflictions that follow this confinement into two kinds — the first three (illness, want, and the loss of kin) naming actual karmic suffering-retribution, the last two (the fetter of craving even in the higher realms, and the covering of ignorance even where worldly wisdom has been attained) naming affliction itself, still at work beneath a surface where gross suffering has temporarily subsided.

On the non-Buddhist self-clinging contemplation, Huiyuan explains why the treatise calls the five aggregates specifically "enemies" rather than simply "suffering": 人皆樂住，而五陰中，識、受、想、行，前後集起，後起催前，念念盡滅，違返人情，故說為怨 — "everyone wishes to abide permanently, yet within the five aggregates, consciousness, feeling, perception, and volition arise one after another, the latter arising displacing the former, ceasing utterly moment by moment — running contrary to human sentiment, and this is why they are called 'enemies.'" And on the timid lesser-vehicle contemplation, he maps the treatise's own graded vocabulary of "small," "narrow," and "boundless" directly onto the ten grounds themselves: 廣者，初地乃至六地；大者，七地；無量，是其八、九、十地 — "'vast' spans the First Ground through the Sixth; 'great' is the Seventh; 'boundless' belongs to the Eighth, Ninth, and Tenth" — so that timidly settling for the lesser vehicle is, on this reading, a failure to trust that the path ahead scales all the way up through exactly this sequence.

The Threefold Fruit: Gentle Taming, Gathered Retribution, and Vow and Wisdom — The Second Ground Complete

Chinese Source

經曰：諸佛子！是菩薩住此菩薩離垢地已，多見諸佛，以大神通力、大願力故；見多百佛、多千佛、多百千佛、多百千那由他佛、多億佛、多百億佛、多千億佛、多百千億佛、多百千億那由他佛，以大神通力、大願力故。是菩薩見諸佛時，以上心深心供養恭敬尊重讚歎，衣服飲食臥具湯藥一切供具悉以奉施。以諸菩薩上妙樂具供養眾僧，以此善根皆願迴向阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。於諸佛所生上恭敬心，復受十善法。受善法已，乃至得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提終不中失。是菩薩於無量劫、無量百劫、無量千劫、無量百千劫、無量億劫、無量百億劫、無量千億劫、無量百千億劫、無量百千億那由他劫，遠離慳嫉破戒垢心，習行布施持戒清淨。諸佛子！譬如成鍊真金，置礬石中煮已，離一切垢轉復明淨。諸佛子！菩薩住此離垢地中亦復如是，於無量劫乃至無量百千億那由他劫，遠離慳嫉破戒垢心，成就布施持戒清淨。菩薩爾時於四攝法中愛語偏多，十波羅蜜中戒波羅蜜增上，餘波羅蜜非不修集，隨力隨分。諸佛子！是名略說菩薩摩訶薩第二菩薩離垢地。

菩薩住是地中，多作轉輪聖王，得法自在七寶具足，有自在力能除一切眾生破戒等垢，以善方便令諸眾生修行十善業道。所作善業布施愛語利益同事，是諸福德皆不離念佛、念法、念僧、念菩薩、念菩薩行、念波羅蜜、念十地、念不壞力、念無畏、念不共佛法，乃至不離念具足一切種一切智智。

常生是心：我當於一切眾生中為首為勝為大、為妙為微妙、為上為無上、為導為將、為師為尊，乃至為一切智智依止者。諸佛子！是菩薩摩訶薩若欲捨家勤行精進，於佛法中便能捨家妻子五欲。得出家已勤行精進，於一念間得千三昧，得見千佛、知千佛神力，能動千佛世界、能入千佛世界、能照千佛世界、能教化千佛世界眾生、能住壽千劫、能知過去未來世各千劫事、能善入千法門、能變身為千，於一一身能示千菩薩以為眷屬。

若以願力自在勝上，菩薩願力過於此數，示種種神通，或身、或光明、或神通、或眼、或境界、或音聲、或行、或莊嚴、或加、或信、或業，是諸神通乃至無量百千萬億那由他劫不可數知。

論曰：此中果利益挍量勝事者，如初地說，此地亦如是，有者同，無者應知。此中勝事者，於無量劫遠離慳嫉破戒垢心，成就布施持戒清淨等諸事勝故，於初地中戒未淨故施亦未淨。若爾，何故初地中說檀波羅蜜增上，餘者不如？然彼檀波羅蜜等，此地中轉勝清淨故。以離慳嫉破戒等垢，是故此地釋名離垢。初地中金但以火鍊，除外貪等麁垢故，說鍊金清淨。今於此地，復置礬石中煮，除自體明垢故，自性真淨故，說性戒清淨義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Sons of the Buddha! This bodhisattva, having dwelt in this bodhisattva's Ground Free of Defilement, sees many buddhas. Through great spiritual power and the power of his great vows, he sees hundreds of buddhas, thousands of buddhas, hundreds of thousands of buddhas, hundreds of thousands of niyutas of buddhas, koṭis of buddhas, hundreds of koṭis of buddhas, thousands of koṭis of buddhas, hundreds of thousands of koṭis of buddhas, and hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of buddhas — through great spiritual power and the power of his great vows. When this bodhisattva sees these buddhas, with a supreme, deep mind he makes offerings to them, reveres, honors, and praises them, presenting robes, food and drink, bedding, medicine, and every requisite entirely as offerings. And, with the supreme and sublime objects of delight belonging to the bodhisattvas, he makes offerings to the entire monastic assembly, dedicating this root of goodness, wishing for unsurpassed, perfect, complete enlightenment. Toward the buddhas he gives rise to a supreme mind of reverence, and further receives the ten wholesome dharmas. Having received these wholesome dharmas, all the way until he attains unsurpassed, perfect, complete enlightenment, he never loses them. This bodhisattva, throughout boundless eons, boundless hundreds of eons, boundless thousands of eons, boundless hundreds of thousands of eons, boundless koṭis of eons, boundless hundreds of koṭis of eons, boundless thousands of koṭis of eons, boundless hundreds of thousands of koṭis of eons, and boundless hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of eons, departs from the taint of stinginess, envy, and broken moral discipline, cultivating and practicing giving and moral discipline until they are pure. Sons of the Buddha! It is like refined, true gold that, once placed within alum-water and boiled, departs from every impurity and becomes ever more radiant and pure. Sons of the Buddha! A bodhisattva dwelling in this Ground Free of Defilement is just the same: throughout boundless eons, all the way to boundless hundreds of thousands of koṭis of niyutas of eons, he departs from the taint of stinginess, envy, and broken moral discipline, accomplishing giving and moral discipline until they are pure. At that time, among the four means of attraction, this bodhisattva's kind speech now predominates; and, among the ten perfections, the perfection of moral discipline now excels — the other perfections he does not fail to cultivate and gather, but in accordance with his own strength and portion. Sons of the Buddha! This is what is briefly called a bodhisattva-mahāsattva's Second Ground, the bodhisattva's Ground Free of Defilement.

A bodhisattva dwelling in this ground often becomes a Wheel-Turning King, attaining mastery of the Dharma, complete with the seven treasures, possessing the power of self-mastery to remove every sentient being's taint of broken moral discipline and the rest, and, through skillful means, causing sentient beings to practice the ten wholesome courses of action. The wholesome deeds he performs — giving, kind speech, beneficial action, and cooperative work — every one of these merits is never apart from mindfulness of the buddhas, mindfulness of the Dharma, mindfulness of the monastic assembly, mindfulness of every bodhisattva, mindfulness of the bodhisattva's own practice, mindfulness of every perfection, mindfulness of the ten grounds, mindfulness of unbreakable power, mindfulness of fearlessness, mindfulness of the unshared buddha-dharmas, all the way to never apart from mindfulness of being complete in the wisdom of all modes, the wisdom of omniscience.

He constantly gives rise to this thought: among all sentient beings, I shall be foremost, I shall be supreme, I shall be great, I shall be sublime, I shall be exceedingly sublime, I shall be highest, I shall be unsurpassed, I shall be a guide, I shall be a leader, I shall be a teacher, I shall be one held in honor, all the way to being the very support of the wisdom of omniscience. Sons of the Buddha! Should this bodhisattva-mahāsattva then wish to leave home and diligently exert himself, within the buddha-dharma he is thereupon able to leave home, his wife and children, and the five desires behind. Having gone forth into homelessness, diligently exerting himself, within a single thought-moment he attains a thousand samādhis, is able to see a thousand buddhas, comes to know the spiritual power of a thousand buddhas, is able to shake a thousand buddha-worlds, is able to enter a thousand buddha-worlds, is able to illumine a thousand buddha-worlds, is able to teach and transform the sentient beings of a thousand buddha-worlds, is able to dwell in life for a thousand eons, is able to know the affairs of a thousand eons each in the past and the future, is able to enter skillfully into a thousand Dharma-gates, and is able to transform his own body into a thousand bodies, manifesting, within each and every one of those thousand bodies, a thousand bodhisattvas as its own retinue.

Should he, through the power of vow, be self-mastering and supreme, a bodhisattva's power of vow manifests beyond even this number, manifesting all manner of spiritual power — whether of body, or of radiance, or of spiritual power itself, or of the eye, or of the object-realm, or of voice, or of activity, or of adornment, or of empowerment, or of faith, or of karmic function — every one of these spiritual powers, throughout boundless hundreds of thousands of myriad koṭis of niyutas of eons, beyond all counting or knowing.

Commentary: Here, the excellence of the fruit and benefit, comparatively measured, is just as explained for the First Ground; this ground is likewise so — where the two share the same matter, it is the same, and where one is absent, this should be understood accordingly. The excellence here lies in this: throughout boundless eons, departing from the taint of stinginess, envy, and broken moral discipline, he accomplishes giving and moral discipline until they are pure, and every such matter excels — for in the First Ground, moral discipline was not yet pure, and so giving, too, was not yet pure. If this is so, why then does the First Ground say that the perfection of giving excels, the rest not being its equal? It is because that very perfection of giving, and the rest, become more supremely pure within this ground. Because he departs from the taint of stinginess, envy, and broken moral discipline, this ground is therefore explained by the name Free of Defilement. In the First Ground, the gold was only refined by fire, removing outer taints such as greed — and so it was said that the refined gold became pure. Now, within this ground, it is further placed within alum-water and boiled, removing the taint obscuring the gold's own substance — since its own nature becomes genuinely pure, this is said to be the meaning of the purity of the nature of moral discipline.

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

On the tension between this ground naming moral discipline as its predominant perfection and the First Ground's own earlier claim that giving predominates there, Huiyuan states the resolution directly rather than treating it as a demotion of one virtue in favor of another: 前地雖說檀度增上，然彼檀度此轉勝也 — "though the First Ground speaks of the perfection of giving as predominant, that very perfection of giving becomes more supremely excellent within this very ground." Giving is not superseded or judged inferior in hindsight; it is the same practice, now standing on genuinely purified moral discipline rather than moral discipline still mixed with worldly imperfection — so that it is giving itself, not some new and unrelated virtue, that reaches its fuller excellence here. He adds that every single ground in truth possesses all ten perfections complete, and that naming one perfection "predominant" within a given ground is a way of measuring relative excellence, not a claim that the other nine are somehow missing.

On the closing etymology of the ground's own name, Huiyuan reads the shift from a single firing to firing followed by an alum-water boiling as marking two distinct kinds of impurity, not one impurity removed twice over. The First Ground's gold "is only refined by fire" — 除外貪等麁垢故，說鍊金清淨 — "removing outer, coarse taints such as greed, and so the refined gold is said to become pure." This ground places that same gold into alum-water afterward: 除體垢…謂除金中障體明垢，自性真淨 — "removing the taint of the substance itself… namely, removing, within the gold, the taint obscuring the substance's own luster, so that its own nature becomes genuinely pure." Fire removes only what clings to the gold from without; the alum-bath afterward reaches into the gold's own body and clears the dullness native to the metal itself — which Huiyuan maps directly onto the difference between the First Ground's moral discipline, still mixed with worldly imperfection, and this ground's moral discipline, its own essence now genuinely pure: 自性真淨. This is Huiyuan's last surviving word on the Second Ground; his commentary continues, in his own colophon, "十地義記卷第四本."

Part 19 Complete — Vimalā, the Ground Free of Defilement, Now Entirely Translated

This closes Part 19, and with it the entire Second Ground: Vimalā, the Ground Free of Defilement. Parts 15 through 19 — the ten straightforward minds, the ten wholesome courses of action, the three vehicles, the nine contemplations on categories of suffering beings, and the threefold fruit of gentle taming, gathered retribution, and vow and wisdom — have now translated the whole of fascicle four of this twelve-fascicle treatise.
