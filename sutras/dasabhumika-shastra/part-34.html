Why the Seventh Ground Alone Is Called Excellent

Vimuktacandra Bodhisattva asks Vajragarbha a searching question: are the factors that assist enlightenment perfected only within this Seventh Ground, or within every one of the ten bodhisattva grounds alike? Vajragarbha answers that all ten grounds perfect them equally — yet the Seventh alone is named excellent, since its own surpassing practice has reached completion, opening onto the great path of penetrating wisdom. He traces what each of the first seven grounds itself contributes, then the path of wisdom traversed continuously from the First Ground through the Seventh, enabling the effortless practice of the three grounds still to come, and the simile of two worlds — one defiled-and-pure, one wholly pure — hard to cross without great vow-power, skillful wisdom, and spiritual might. A second question follows: is the Seventh Ground's own practice itself defiled or pure? Vajragarbha answers through the simile of a Wheel-Turning King who, upon becoming a Brahmā, is no longer touched by the taint he once moved among as a man — and so this bodhisattva is called neither one who has affliction, nor one who is without it. This part closes with this ground's own threefold fruit: the ultimate purification of body, speech, and mind karma; the attainment of ten named samādhis under the sign of well-choosing wisdom; and, through this samādhi joined with great compassion, the bodhisattva's surpassing of the very stage of the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha.

Vimuktacandra's Question: Why Not Simply Cease? — Lower-Ground and Higher-Ground Practice Distinguished

Chinese Source

經曰：爾時解脫月菩薩問金剛藏菩薩言：佛子！菩薩但於第七菩薩地中具足一切助菩提分法？為當一切菩薩諸地中亦皆具足？金剛藏菩薩言：佛子！菩薩於十菩薩地中悉具足一切助菩提分法，但第七地勝故得名。何以故？佛子！是菩薩此菩薩地中方便行具足，得入智慧神通行故。佛子！菩薩於初地中發願觀一切佛法故，具足助菩提分法。第二地中除心惡垢故，具足助菩提分法。第三地中願轉增長得法明故，具足助菩提分法。第四地中入道故，具足助菩提分法。第五地中隨順行世間法故，具足助菩提分法。第六地中入甚深法門故，具足助菩提分法。此第七菩薩地中起一切佛法故，具足一切助菩提分法。

論曰：云何下地增上方便行滿足？以滿足故入大智通行，如經「金剛藏菩薩言：佛子！菩薩於十菩薩地中悉具足一切菩提分法，但第七地勝故得名。何以故？佛子？是菩薩此菩薩地中方便行具足，得入智慧神通行故」。是中通者，五神通。智者，如前說。云何此地中方便行滿足？彼餘世間出世間行中更起殊勝行，是故此七地中起一切佛法故，能具足助菩提分法。如經「佛子！菩薩於初地中發願觀一切佛法故，具足助菩提分法。乃至此第七菩薩地中起一切佛法故，具足一切助菩提分法」故。如是前上地勝差別，下地增上方便行滿足已說。云何上地增上修行智慧方便菩提分功用行滿足故？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: At that time, the bodhisattva Vimuktacandra asked the bodhisattva Vajragarbha, saying: Son of the Buddha! Does a bodhisattva perfect every dharma that assists enlightenment only within the Seventh Ground, the bodhisattva ground — or does he perfect it, just the same, within all of the bodhisattva's ten grounds? The bodhisattva Vajragarbha said: Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, within all ten of the bodhisattva grounds, perfects every one of these dharmas that assist enlightenment without exception; it is only that the Seventh Ground is called by this name because it excels. Why is this so? Son of the Buddha! Because this bodhisattva, within this bodhisattva ground, perfects the practice of skillful means, and thereby attains entry into the practice of wisdom and spiritual power. Son of the Buddha! Within the First Ground, a bodhisattva perfects the dharmas that assist enlightenment because he sets forth his vow and contemplates every buddha-dharma. Within the Second Ground, he perfects the dharmas that assist enlightenment because he removes the defiling taint from his mind. Within the Third Ground, he perfects the dharmas that assist enlightenment because his vow turns ever more increasing and he attains the light of the Dharma. Within the Fourth Ground, he perfects the dharmas that assist enlightenment because he enters the path. Within the Fifth Ground, he perfects the dharmas that assist enlightenment because he practices in accordance with the dharmas of the world. Within the Sixth Ground, he perfects the dharmas that assist enlightenment because he enters a profound Dharma-gate. Within this Seventh Ground, the bodhisattva ground, he perfects every dharma that assists enlightenment because he gives rise to every buddha-dharma.

Commentary: What is the fulfillment of the lower grounds' surpassing practice of skillful means? Because it is fulfilled, one thereby enters the practice of great wisdom and spiritual penetration — as the sūtra says, "the bodhisattva Vajragarbha said: Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, within all ten of the bodhisattva grounds, perfects every one of these factors of enlightenment without exception; it is only that the Seventh Ground is called by this name because it excels. Why is this so? Son of the Buddha! Because this bodhisattva, within this bodhisattva ground, perfects the practice of skillful means, and thereby attains entry into the practice of wisdom and spiritual power." Here, "spiritual power" means the five spiritual powers; "wisdom" is as already explained before. What, then, is the fulfillment of the practice of skillful means within this ground? Within the practices of the world and beyond the world that came before, this ground further gives rise to a still more excellent practice — and this is why, within this Seventh Ground, giving rise to every buddha-dharma, one is able to perfect the dharmas that assist enlightenment, as the sūtra says, "Son of the Buddha! Within the First Ground, a bodhisattva perfects the dharmas that assist enlightenment because he sets forth his vow and contemplates every buddha-dharma," all the way to, "within this Seventh Ground, the bodhisattva ground, he perfects every dharma that assists enlightenment because he gives rise to every buddha-dharma." Thus the prior distinction of excellence relative to the higher grounds — the fulfillment of the lower grounds' surpassing practice of skillful means — has already been explained. What, then, is the fulfillment of the higher grounds' surpassing practice, the exertion belonging to the wisdom and skillful means of the factors of enlightenment?

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang frames this entire dialogue as answering one deceptively simple question — since every ground alike perfects this same array of dharmas, why is the Seventh Ground alone singled out and named "excellent"? His own answer turns on a difference of scope, not of kind: 上六各是一行中具一切，今此地中一切中具一切，故過前也 — "the prior six grounds each, within a single practice, possess the whole; but this ground, within the whole, possesses the whole — and this is why it surpasses what came before." Each earlier ground perfects the full array of enlightenment-dharmas by way of one governing practice; only the Seventh perfects them by way of every practice at once.

Fazang also glosses why this fulfillment matters structurally, not merely honorifically: 此地中功用行滿，故云功行具足也，能入第八無功用道也 — "within this ground, the practice of exertion reaches its very fulfillment — hence it is said that the practice of exertion is perfected — and this is what enables entry into the Eighth Ground's effortless path." The excellence named here is not a static rank among equals but the threshold condition for everything the Eighth Ground will make effortless.

The Path of Wisdom Through Seven Grounds, and the Simile of Two Worlds

Chinese Source

經曰：何以故？佛子！菩薩從初地來乃至七地，得諸智慧所行道。以是力故，從第八菩薩地乃至第十地無功用行自然滿足。佛子！譬如二世界：一、染淨世界；二、純淨世界。是二中間，難可得過。欲過此界，當以大神通力。佛子！菩薩如是行於染淨菩薩道難可得過，當以大願力、大方便智力、大神通力故乃可得過。

解脫月菩薩言：佛子！七菩薩地為是染行、為是淨行？金剛藏菩薩言：佛子！從初地來菩薩所行皆離煩惱染業。何以故？迴向阿耨多羅三藐三菩提故。隨道所行如分平等故，不名為過七地煩惱行。佛子！譬如轉輪聖王乘上寶象遊四天下，知有貧窮困苦染過之人，而不為彼過所染。然王未免人身，若捨人身生於梵世住梵天宮，見行千世界，示梵王光明威力。爾時不名為人。佛子！菩薩亦如是，從初地來乘諸波羅蜜乘行一切世間，亦知世間煩惱染過，而不為煩惱過之所染。以乘正道故，而不名為過七地煩惱染。若菩薩捨一切功用行，從七地入第八地，爾時名為乘菩薩清淨乘行一切世間。如實知一切煩惱染過，而不為煩惱過之所染，以得過故。佛子！菩薩住是第七菩薩地，過多貪欲等諸煩惱眾。是菩薩住此菩薩遠行地中，不名有煩惱者、不名無煩惱者。何以故？一切煩惱不行故，不名有煩惱者；貪求如來智慧未滿足故，不名無煩惱者。

論曰：云何上地增上智慧方便行菩提分功用滿足故？如經「何以故？佛子！菩薩從初地來乃至七地得諸智慧所行道，乃至當以大願力、大方便智力、大神通力乃可得過」故。從初地來遠離一切煩惱，示現如是。此地名為染淨，非染行，如分行平等道故。彼菩薩此地中，隨力分捨功用道，如轉輪王譬。第八地中，自然得報行過煩惱染過，示現如生梵世，捨轉輪王人身。如經「諸佛子！譬如轉輪聖王乃至不為煩惱過之所染，以得過故。佛子！菩薩住是第七菩薩地，過多貪欲等諸煩惱眾者，未至報地故。是故此地不名離煩惱行，有功用故。菩薩住此遠行地中，不名有煩惱者乃至未滿足故」。不名無煩惱者，如是前上地勝差別分已說。次說雙行果差別。此有四種相：一、業清淨；二、得勝三昧；三、過地；四、得勝行。云何業清淨？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Why is this so? Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, coming from the First Ground all the way to the Seventh, attains the path traversed by every kind of wisdom. Through the power of this attainment, from the Eighth Ground, the bodhisattva ground, all the way to the Tenth Ground, the effortless practice is spontaneously fulfilled. Son of the Buddha! It is like two worlds: one, a world both defiled and pure; the other, a world wholly pure. Between these two there lies a passage hard to cross. To cross beyond this boundary, one must rely on great spiritual power. Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, practicing thus along the bodhisattva path that is both defiled and pure, likewise finds it hard to cross beyond — he must rely on the power of a great vow, the power of great skillful wisdom, and the power of great spiritual might before he is able to cross beyond it.

The bodhisattva Vimuktacandra said: Son of the Buddha! Is the practice of the seventh bodhisattva ground a defiled practice, or is it a pure practice? The bodhisattva Vajragarbha said: Son of the Buddha! From the First Ground onward, every practice a bodhisattva undertakes is already free from the defiled karma of affliction. Why is this so? Because he dedicates it toward unsurpassed, perfect, complete enlightenment. Since what he practices, in according with the path, is equal according to its portion, this is not called "passing beyond" the afflicted practice of the Seventh Ground. Son of the Buddha! It is like a Wheel-Turning King who, mounted upon a supreme, jeweled elephant, journeys throughout the four continents: he knows there are people who are poor, destitute, afflicted, and stained by defilement, and yet he is not himself stained by their defilement. And yet this king has not thereby escaped the human body; only were he to relinquish the human body and be born into the Brahmā world, abiding within the palace of the Brahmā heaven, beholding a thousand world-systems at once, manifesting the Brahmā king's own radiance and majestic power — only then would he no longer be called a human being. Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva is just the same: from the First Ground onward, mounted upon the vehicle of the perfections, he practices throughout every world, and he too knows the world's own defilement by affliction, and yet he is not himself stained by this defilement of affliction. Because he is mounted upon the correct path, this is not called "passing beyond" the afflicted defilement of the Seventh Ground. Should a bodhisattva relinquish every practice of exertion and pass from the Seventh Ground into the Eighth Ground, then, at that time, he is called one mounted upon the bodhisattva's own pure vehicle, practicing throughout every world — knowing truly every defilement of affliction, and yet not being stained by this defilement of affliction, because he has thereby attained passing beyond it. Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, dwelling in this Seventh Ground, the bodhisattva ground, passes beyond the great host of afflictions such as craving and desire. This bodhisattva, dwelling within this bodhisattva's Dūraṅgamā, the Far-Reaching Ground, is called neither one who has affliction, nor one who is without affliction. Why is this so? Because no affliction whatsoever is active within him, he is not called one who has affliction; because his craving in pursuit of the Tathāgata's own wisdom is not yet fulfilled, he is not called one who is without affliction.

Commentary: What is the fulfillment of the higher grounds' surpassing practice, the exertion belonging to the wisdom and skillful means of the factors of enlightenment? As the sūtra says, "Why is this so? Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, coming from the First Ground all the way to the Seventh, attains the path traversed by every kind of wisdom," all the way to, "he must rely on the power of a great vow, the power of great skillful wisdom, and the power of great spiritual might before he is able to cross beyond it." That, from the First Ground onward, he is far removed from every affliction — this is what is thereby made manifest. This ground is named "defiled-and-pure," not "defiled practice," since what he practices, according to its portion, is an equal path. This bodhisattva, within this ground, relinquishes the path of exertion according to his own strength and portion, as in the simile of the Wheel-Turning King. Within the Eighth Ground, he spontaneously attains the practice of retribution, passing beyond the defilement of affliction — this is what is made manifest through the simile of being born into the Brahmā world, relinquishing the Wheel-Turning King's own human body, as the sūtra says, "Sons of the Buddha! It is like a Wheel-Turning King," all the way to, "and yet he is not stained by this defilement of affliction, because he has thereby attained passing beyond it. Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, dwelling in this Seventh Ground, the bodhisattva ground, passes beyond the great host of afflictions such as craving and desire" — this is because he has not yet arrived at the ground of retribution. This is why this ground is not called a practice free of affliction, since it still involves exertion. A bodhisattva dwelling within this Far-Reaching Ground is called neither one who has affliction," all the way to, "because it is not yet fulfilled." "Not called one who is without affliction" — thus the prior distinction of excellence relative to the higher grounds has already been explained. Next is explained the distinction belonging to the fruit of dual practice. This has four aspects: first, the purification of karma; second, the attainment of a supreme samādhi; third, surpassing the ground; and fourth, the attainment of a supreme practice. What, then, is the purification of karma?

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang reads this whole stretch as Vajragarbha's demonstration of the Seventh Ground's excellence relative to the three grounds still ahead, and his gloss on the two-worlds simile gives it two layered readings at once: 無功用位如淨世界，有功用位如染世界，隔位難超，故以為喻。又釋：前六地喻之以染，後之三地喻之以淨，唯第七地名染淨位，以能越染到此淨處 — "the stage of effortless practice is likened to the pure world, and the stage of effortful practice to the defiled world, the gap between the two being hard to cross; or, on another reading, the prior six grounds are likened to the defiled world and the latter three to the pure world, with the Seventh Ground alone named the 'defiled-and-pure' stage, since it alone is able to cross beyond the defiled and arrive at the pure." He draws the conclusion directly: 既染由此越、淨由此到，是故此地極為要勝 — "since the defiled is crossed by way of this ground, and the pure is arrived at by way of this ground, this ground is therefore exceedingly essential and supreme." The Seventh Ground's excellence, on this reading, is not a property it holds in itself but its position as the very hinge between two otherwise incommensurable worlds.

On the closing line that this bodhisattva is called neither one who has affliction nor one who is without it, Fazang locates the reason in something closer to intermittency than to degree: 以不能常在純無流觀，是故將出觀求佛以為煩惱也 — "since he is not yet able to remain constantly within pure, uncontaminated contemplation, the very act of emerging from meditative absorption to seek the Buddha's own wisdom is itself counted as a kind of affliction." What looks, on first reading, like a hedge between two categories turns out to name a specific instability: even the reaching-toward-buddhahood that draws him out of stillness still counts against him here.

Purifying Body, Speech, and Mind: The Samādhi of Well-Choosing Wisdom, and Surpassing the Śrāvaka and Pratyekabuddha

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩住此第七菩薩遠行地中，畢竟成就深淨身業、畢竟成就深淨口業、畢竟成就深淨意業。是菩薩所有不善業道，諸佛所呵，皆已捨離。所有善業道，諸佛所歎，是則常行。世間所有經書技術，如五地中說自然而行。是菩薩於三千大千世界中得為大師，唯除諸佛及八地菩薩，無有眾生深心妙行能與等者。是菩薩所有禪定三昧三摩跋提神通解脫一切現前。修行門中非善成就報力，如第八菩薩地。是菩薩住此第七菩薩遠行地，於念念中具足修集方便智力及一切助菩提分法，得轉勝具足。

論曰：業清淨者有四種相：一、戒淨勝，如經「是菩薩住此第七菩薩遠行地中，畢竟成就深淨身業乃至是則常行」故。二、世間智清淨勝，如經「世間所有經書技術乃至自然而行」故。三、得自身勝心行二平等無與等者，如經「是菩薩於三千大千世界中乃至深心妙行無與等者」故。四、得勝力禪等現前勝，如經「是菩薩所有禪定三昧乃至得轉勝具足」故。是中依禪起三昧三摩跋提神通解脫，為教化眾生故、寂滅樂行故。滅定三摩拔提，如是次第、如是雙行果。業清淨四種相已說。次說得勝三昧。

經曰：是菩薩住此第七菩薩遠行地中，入名善擇智菩薩三昧、善思義三昧、益意三昧、分別義藏三昧、擇一切義三昧、善住堅根三昧、智神通門三昧、法界業三昧、如來利益三昧，入名種種義藏世間涅槃門菩薩三昧。菩薩如是大智通門，滿足上首十三昧，能入百千菩薩三昧門，淨治此地。

論曰：得勝三昧者有十種相：一、依未觀義，二、依已觀義，如經「是菩薩住此第七菩薩遠行地中入名善擇智菩薩三昧故、善思義三昧」故。三、依一句無量義勝，四、依一義說無量名，如經「益意三昧故、分別義藏三昧」故。五、依通一切五明處智，如經「擇一切義三昧」故。六、依煩惱障淨，真如觀堅根故，如經「善住堅根三昧」故。七、依智障淨，有四種障淨故。一、勝功德障此對治，如經「智神通門三昧」故。二、無障礙智障此對治，如經「法界業三昧」故。三、於深上佛法性弱障此對治，如經「如來利益三昧」故。四、不住行障此對治，如經「入名種種義藏世間涅槃門菩薩三昧」故。種種義藏者，種種善根故。如是大智通門滿足上首十三昧，能入百千萬菩薩三昧門，淨治此地故。已說得勝三昧。次說過地。

經曰：是菩薩得是三昧智慧方便善清淨故、得大悲力故，過聲聞辟支佛地，現前思量趣智慧地。

論曰：過聲聞辟支佛地者，有二種相：一、修行方便智力；二、大悲力故。現前者，能入法流水。思量智慧地者，八地智慧應知但觀奢摩他、毘婆舍那道，彼處成就故。復次過者，業勝故示現。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, dwelling within this Seventh Ground, the bodhisattva's Dūraṅgamā, the Far-Reaching Ground, ultimately accomplishes a deep and pure bodily karma, ultimately accomplishes a deep and pure verbal karma, and ultimately accomplishes a deep and pure mental karma. This bodhisattva, whatever unwholesome course of karma the buddhas condemn, has already relinquished and departed from entirely; whatever wholesome course of karma the buddhas praise, this he constantly practices. Whatever scriptures, treatises, and skilled arts exist in the world, he masters spontaneously, just as was already explained within the Fifth Ground. This bodhisattva becomes a great teacher throughout the great trichiliocosm; apart only from the buddhas and the bodhisattvas of the Eighth Ground, there is no sentient being whose deep mind and sublime practice can equal his own. This bodhisattva's every dhyāna, samādhi, samāpatti, spiritual power, and liberation all stand directly present before him. Within the gate of cultivated practice, this is not yet the excellent accomplishment of the power of retribution, as it will be within the Eighth Ground, the bodhisattva ground. This bodhisattva, dwelling within this Seventh Ground, the bodhisattva's Dūraṅgamā, the Far-Reaching Ground, in thought after thought, perfects and gathers the power of skillful wisdom together with every dharma that assists enlightenment, attaining a fulfillment ever more excellent still.

Commentary: The purification of karma has four aspects. First, the excellence of purified precepts, as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, dwelling within this Seventh Ground, the bodhisattva's Dūraṅgamā, ultimately accomplishes a deep and pure bodily karma," all the way to, "this he constantly practices." Second, the excellence of purified worldly wisdom, as the sūtra says, "whatever scriptures, treatises, and skilled arts exist in the world," all the way to, "he masters spontaneously." Third, attaining, in his own person, an excellent mind and conduct, the two being equal, with no one able to equal him, as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva becomes a great teacher throughout the great trichiliocosm," all the way to, "there is no sentient being whose deep mind and sublime practice can equal his own." Fourth, attaining the excellence of supreme power — dhyāna and the rest — standing directly present, as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva's every dhyāna, samādhi," all the way to, "attaining a fulfillment ever more excellent still." Herein, in dependence on dhyāna, he gives rise to samādhi, samāpatti, spiritual power, and liberation, for the sake of teaching and transforming sentient beings, and for the sake of the practice of quiescent bliss. The samāpatti of cessation, in this same sequence, likewise belongs to this fruit of dual practice. The four aspects of the purification of karma have now been explained. Next is explained the attainment of a supreme samādhi.

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, dwelling within this Seventh Ground, the bodhisattva's Dūraṅgamā, the Far-Reaching Ground, enters the bodhisattva's samādhi named well-choosing wisdom, the samādhi of well-considered meaning, the samādhi of beneficial intent, the samādhi of the treasury of discriminated meaning, the samādhi that chooses every meaning, the samādhi of well-abiding, firm roots, the samādhi of the gateway of wisdom and spiritual power, the samādhi of the karmic function of the Dharma-realm, the samādhi of the Tathāgata's own benefit, and enters the bodhisattva's samādhi named the gateway of the manifold treasury of meaning and of nirvāṇa within the world. A bodhisattva, through this gateway of great wisdom and spiritual power, having thus perfected these ten foremost samādhis, is able to enter a hundred thousand of the bodhisattva's own gateways of samādhi, purifying and cultivating this ground.

Commentary: The attainment of a supreme samādhi has ten aspects. First, in dependence on meaning not yet contemplated; second, in dependence on meaning already contemplated — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, dwelling within this Seventh Ground, the bodhisattva's Dūraṅgamā, enters the bodhisattva's samādhi named well-choosing wisdom," and "the samādhi of well-considered meaning." Third, in dependence on the excellence of boundless meaning within a single phrase; fourth, in dependence on speaking boundless names for a single meaning — as the sūtra says, "the samādhi of beneficial intent," and "the samādhi of the treasury of discriminated meaning." Fifth, in dependence on the wisdom that penetrates every one of the five sciences — as the sūtra says, "the samādhi that chooses every meaning." Sixth, in dependence on the purification of the hindrance of affliction, since the contemplation of suchness is firmly rooted — as the sūtra says, "the samādhi of well-abiding, firm roots." Seventh, in dependence on the purification of the hindrance to wisdom, which has four aspects of purification. First, the antidote to the hindrance that obstructs supreme merit — as the sūtra says, "the samādhi of the gateway of wisdom and spiritual power." Second, the antidote to the hindrance to unobstructed wisdom — as the sūtra says, "the samādhi of the karmic function of the Dharma-realm." Third, the antidote to the hindrance of weakness regarding the deep and supreme buddha-dharma — as the sūtra says, "the samādhi of the Tathāgata's own benefit." Fourth, the antidote to the hindrance of not abiding in practice — as the sūtra says, "entering the bodhisattva's samādhi named the gateway of the manifold treasury of meaning and of nirvāṇa within the world." "The manifold treasury of meaning" means manifold roots of goodness. Through this gateway of great wisdom and spiritual power, having thus perfected these ten foremost samādhis, one is able to enter a hundred thousand myriad of the bodhisattva's own gateways of samādhi, purifying and cultivating this ground. The attainment of a supreme samādhi has now been explained. Next is explained surpassing the ground.

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, because this samādhi's wisdom and skillful means are excellently purified, and because he attains the power of great compassion, surpasses the ground of the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha, and stands directly present, turning his thought toward the ground of wisdom.

Commentary: "Surpassing the ground of the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha" has two aspects: first, the power of skillful wisdom cultivated in practice; and second, the power of great compassion. "Standing directly present" means being able to enter the stream of the Dharma. "Turning his thought toward the ground of wisdom" should be understood as the wisdom of the Eighth Ground, which contemplates only the path of śamatha and vipaśyanā, since it is accomplished within that very place. Furthermore, "surpassing" is what is made manifest through the excellence of karma.

Reading note — after Fazang

On the ten named samādhis, Fazang supplies a structural key before glossing each one: 初五起解分別、後五成行分別 — "the first five give rise to understanding through discrimination; the latter five bring practice itself to accomplishment through discrimination." Of the first five, he further pairs them: the first two "know the principle" — one stilling the mind toward contemplation not yet undertaken, the other reviewing what has already been contemplated — while the next two "know the teaching," and the fifth, resting 依通一切五明處智 on "the wisdom that penetrates every one of the five sciences," is knowledge of factual matters, 故云如實分別 — "hence called discriminating them just as they are." Of the latter five, he adds: 初一煩惱障淨，顯其行深；後四智障淨，顯其行廣 — "the first purifies the hindrance of affliction, displaying this practice's depth; the remaining four purify the hindrance to wisdom, displaying this practice's breadth." Ten names that might otherwise read as a loose list turn out, on his account, to move in a deliberate order — from stilling the mind, through mastering both principle and teaching, to a final widening from depth into breadth.

On why this samādhi enables the bodhisattva to surpass the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha, Fazang locates the reason in where it actually leads: 此能入八地已上法流水中，止觀俱現，任運流注入佛果海故名也 — "this is because he is thereby able to enter the very stream of the Dharma belonging to the Eighth Ground and beyond, calm-abiding and insight both manifesting together, flowing spontaneously onward into the ocean of the buddha-fruit — and this is why it is so named." The surpassing named here is not yet an arrival but the moment the current itself is joined.

Part 34 Complete

This closes Part 34: Vimuktacandra's pointed question as to why a bodhisattva who has attained a wisdom equal to a śrāvaka's or a pratyekabuddha's own liberating insight does not simply realize final cessation, and Vajragarbha's answer distinguishing the lower grounds' surpassing practice of skillful means, already fulfilled, from the higher grounds' surpassing exertion of wisdom, not yet fulfilled; the path of wisdom traversed continuously from the First Ground through the Seventh, and the simile of two worlds — one defiled-and-pure, one wholly pure — that only great vow-power, skillful wisdom, and spiritual might can cross, together with the wheel-king simile explaining why this ground's practice, though not yet free of affliction, is likewise not named a defiled practice; and this ground's own threefold fruit — the ultimate purification of body, speech, and mind karma; the attainment of ten named samādhis under the sign of well-choosing wisdom; and, through this samādhi joined with the power of great compassion, the bodhisattva's surpassing of the very stage of the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha.
