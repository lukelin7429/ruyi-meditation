The Dharma Teacher's Skillful Means and the First Three Thickets

The treatise turns to Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom — the Ninth Ground — opening with the first three of its four structural divisions. The commentary contrasts this ground directly with the one just completed: within the Eighth Ground, the bodhisattva merely purified buddha-lands and taught sentient beings; within this Ninth Ground, through the power of eloquence, he teaches and transforms sentient beings to accomplish every characteristic — being able to teach, this surpasses that. Vajragarbha then names the skillful means of a Dharma teacher: the bodhisattva's immeasurable wisdom and well-considering wisdom seeking ever more excellent liberation, and the tathāgatas' own ultimate wisdom, unfolded through clauses benefiting both others and himself. Accomplishing wisdom follows: the bodhisattva's true knowledge of wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate dharmas, defiled and undefiled, worldly and supramundane, conceivable and inconceivable, determinate and indeterminate, across the three vehicles. The treatise then opens accomplishing entry into practice with this ground's most distinctive feature: a systematic analysis of eleven "thickets" — dense, tangled proliferations of mental and karmic activity that a bodhisattva must thoroughly know in order to teach skillfully — of which this part translates the first three: the thicket of the mind's own activity, unfolded through eight distinct marks; the thicket of afflictions' own activity; and the thicket of karma's own activity.

Opening the Ninth Ground: The Skillful Means of a Dharma Teacher and Accomplishing Wisdom

Chinese Source

論曰：第九地中有四分差別：一、法師方便成就；二、智成就；三、入行成就；四、說成就。第八地中但淨佛國土教化眾生，此第九地中辯才力故，教化眾生成就一切相，能教化故、此勝彼故。云何法師方便成就？

經曰：爾時金剛藏菩薩言：佛子！菩薩以如是無量智、善思量智，更求轉勝深寂滅解脫。復轉求如來究竟智慧，入如來深密法中，思惟選擇不思議大智慧，選擇諸陀羅尼三昧及智令清淨故。現諸神通廣大行，通達世界差別行，修如來力、無畏、不共佛法無障調柔，通達如來轉法輪莊嚴事，不捨大悲大願力，得入第九菩薩地。

論曰：是中法師方便成就者，依他利益、自利益，一一五三句示現。依無色得解脫想，可化眾生作利益故。如經「爾時金剛藏菩薩言：佛子！菩薩以如是無量智、善思量智，更求轉勝深寂滅解脫」故。依未得究竟佛智自利益，如經「復轉求如來究竟智」故。依根熟菩薩、依邪念修行可化眾生、依未知法眾生，轉法輪令得知。依邪歸依眾生、依信生天眾生，如是次第五句示利益他行。如經「入如來深密法中故、思惟選擇不思議大智慧故、選擇諸陀羅尼三昧及智令清淨故、現諸神通廣大行故、通達世界差別行故、示清淨國土轉信生天眾生令入佛法」故。依正覺、依轉法輪、依涅槃，此如是次第三句示自利益行。如經「修如來力無畏不共佛法無障調柔故、通達如來轉法輪莊嚴事故、不捨大悲大願力得入第九菩薩地」故。

不捨利益眾生，大涅槃示現，以得不捨大悲大願力故應知。如是九地法師方便成就分已說。云何智成就？

經曰：是菩薩住此菩薩善慧地中，如實知善不善無記法行，有漏無漏法行，世間出世間法行，思議不思議法行，定不定法行，聲聞辟支佛法行，菩薩行法行，如來地法行，有為法行，如實知無為法行。

論曰：是中智成就者，依何等法說法？應知彼法淨染不二，如經「是菩薩住此菩薩善慧地中，如實知善不善無記法行」故。於淨法中有有漏無漏，如經「有漏無漏法行」故。復無漏法中，有世間出世間法行，如經「世間出世間法行」故。復彼法有思議不思議，如經「思議不思議法行」故。彼思議復有定不定，如經「定不定法行」故。彼復於三乘中，如經「聲聞辟支佛法行故、菩薩行法行、如來地法行」故。彼復三乘法中示有為無為依順行，如經「有為法行，如實知無為法行」故。如是九地智成就分已說。云何入行成就？

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: Within the Ninth Ground there are four distinct divisions: first, accomplishing the skillful means of a Dharma teacher; second, accomplishing wisdom; third, accomplishing entry into practice; and fourth, accomplishing teaching. Within the Eighth Ground, he merely purified buddha-lands and taught sentient beings; within this Ninth Ground, through the power of eloquence, he teaches and transforms sentient beings to accomplish every characteristic — being able to teach, this surpasses that. What, then, is accomplishing the skillful means of a Dharma teacher?

Sūtra: At that time the bodhisattva Vajragarbha said: Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, through wisdom such as this — immeasurable wisdom, well-considering wisdom — seeks ever more excellent, profound liberation of quiescent cessation. Turning further, he seeks the tathāgatas' own ultimate wisdom, entering into the tathāgatas' own profound and secret Dharma, reflecting upon and selecting inconceivable great wisdom, selecting every dhāraṇī, samādhi, and wisdom, causing them to become pure. He manifests every spiritual power in vast, expansive practice, thoroughly penetrating the practice of the world's own distinctions, cultivating the tathāgatas' own strength, fearlessness, and unshared buddha-dharmas, unobstructed and well-tempered, thoroughly penetrating the tathāgatas' own adornment of turning the wheel of the Dharma, never abandoning the power of great compassion and great vow — and thereby attains entry into the Ninth Ground of the bodhisattva.

Commentary: Here, accomplishing the skillful means of a Dharma teacher is manifest through benefiting others and benefiting himself, in the pattern of one clause, one clause, five clauses, and three clauses. In dependence on the formless attainment of the thought of liberation, he benefits the sentient beings who can be transformed — as the sūtra says, "at that time the bodhisattva Vajragarbha said: Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, through wisdom such as this — immeasurable wisdom, well-considering wisdom — seeks ever more excellent, profound liberation of quiescent cessation." In dependence on not yet having attained the buddhas' own ultimate wisdom, he benefits himself — as the sūtra says, "turning further, he seeks the tathāgatas' own ultimate wisdom." In dependence on bodhisattvas whose faculties are ripened, in dependence on sentient beings who can be transformed who cultivate practice through mistaken mindfulness, and in dependence on sentient beings who do not yet know the Dharma, he turns the wheel of the Dharma, causing them to come to know it. In dependence on sentient beings with mistaken refuge, and in dependence on sentient beings who have faith and are born in the heavens — in this way, five clauses in turn show the practice of benefiting others. As the sūtra says, "entering into the tathāgatas' own profound and secret Dharma," "reflecting upon and selecting inconceivable great wisdom," "selecting every dhāraṇī, samādhi, and wisdom, causing them to become pure," "manifesting every spiritual power in vast, expansive practice," "thoroughly penetrating the practice of the world's own distinctions," and "manifesting a pure buddha-land, turning sentient beings of faith who are born in the heavens so that they enter the buddha-dharma." In dependence on correct enlightenment, in dependence on turning the wheel of the Dharma, and in dependence on nirvāṇa — these, in this way, three clauses in turn show the practice of benefiting himself. As the sūtra says, "cultivating the tathāgatas' own strength, fearlessness, and unshared buddha-dharmas, unobstructed and well-tempered," "thoroughly penetrating the tathāgatas' own adornment of turning the wheel of the Dharma," and "never abandoning the power of great compassion and great vow, he attains entry into the Ninth Ground of the bodhisattva."

"Never abandoning the benefiting of sentient beings" is manifest as the great nirvāṇa — this should be understood through his attaining the power of never abandoning great compassion and great vow. In this way, the division of accomplishing the skillful means of a Dharma teacher within the Ninth Ground has now been explained. What, then, is accomplishing wisdom?

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, dwelling within this bodhisattva's Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom, truly knows the activity of wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate dharmas; the activity of defiled and undefiled dharmas; the activity of worldly and supramundane dharmas; the activity of conceivable and inconceivable dharmas; the activity of determinate and indeterminate dharmas; the activity of the dharmas of śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, the activity of the dharma of bodhisattva practice, and the activity of the dharma of the tathāgata-ground; the activity of conditioned dharmas; and truly knows the activity of unconditioned dharmas.

Commentary: Here, accomplishing wisdom — in dependence on what sort of dharmas is the Dharma taught? It should be understood that these dharmas are not dual, whether pure or defiled — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, dwelling within this bodhisattva's Ground of Excellent Wisdom, truly knows the activity of wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate dharmas." Within pure dharmas there are the defiled and the undefiled — as the sūtra says, "the activity of defiled and undefiled dharmas." Further, within undefiled dharmas, there is the activity of worldly and supramundane dharmas — as the sūtra says, "the activity of worldly and supramundane dharmas." Further, these dharmas have the conceivable and the inconceivable — as the sūtra says, "the activity of conceivable and inconceivable dharmas." Within the conceivable, there is further the determinate and the indeterminate — as the sūtra says, "the activity of determinate and indeterminate dharmas." This, further, is within the three vehicles — as the sūtra says, "the activity of the dharmas of śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas," "the activity of the dharma of bodhisattva practice," and "the activity of the dharma of the tathāgata-ground." This, further, within the dharmas of the three vehicles, shows the conditioned and the unconditioned, in dependence on the practice he accords with — as the sūtra says, "the activity of conditioned dharmas" and "truly knows the activity of unconditioned dharmas." In this way, the division of accomplishing wisdom within the Ninth Ground has now been explained. What, then, is accomplishing entry into practice?

Reading note — after Fazang

Opening the Ninth Ground, Fazang stacks up authorities on why it is called Sādhumatī, excellent wisdom. The Mahāyānasaṃgraha: 由得最勝無礙智故 — "because he attains the most excellent unhindered wisdom." Vasubandhu's own commentary: 由無礙解智說名為慧，此慧妙善故名善慧 — "through the unhindered wisdom of understanding it is called wisdom; and since this wisdom is sublime and good, it is named excellent wisdom." Asvabhāva glosses it further: 謂得最勝四無礙解⋯智即是慧，故名善慧 — "namely, attaining the most excellent four kinds of unhindered understanding⋯wisdom is itself this very wisdom, hence it is named excellent wisdom." Fazang then defines each of the four unhindered wisdoms in turn: 由法無礙，自在了知一切名句 — "through the unhindered wisdom of dharmas, he freely knows every name and phrase"; 由義無礙，自在通達一切義理 — "through the unhindered wisdom of meaning, he freely penetrates every principle of meaning"; 由詞無礙，自在分別一切言詞 — "through the unhindered wisdom of language, he freely discriminates every word and phrase"; 由辯無礙，遍於十方隨其所宜自在辯說 — "through the unhindered wisdom of eloquence, throughout the ten directions, according to what is fitting, he freely expounds." He stresses that this ground is where these four are attained 最初⋯先未曾得 — "for the very first time, never before attained."

Citing the Vijñaptimātratāsiddhi, Fazang names the two subtle kinds of ignorance severed only upon entering this ground: 一於無量所說法無量名句字，後後慧辯陀羅尼自在愚 — "first, the delusion obstructing mastery of the dhāraṇī of boundless taught dharmas, boundless names, phrases, and syllables, and of ever-further wisdom and eloquence"; 二辯才自在愚 — "second, the delusion obstructing mastery of eloquence itself" — both 便能永斷, cut off forever the moment one enters the Ninth Ground. He also glosses the treatise's own four-part outline directly: 法師方便成就者，此地能起辯才說法，名法師地⋯智成就者，知法智成故。入行成就者，知所化器故。說成就者，稱根正說故 — "accomplishing the skillful means of a Dharma teacher: since this ground is able to arouse eloquence and teach the Dharma, it is named the ground of the Dharma teacher⋯accomplishing wisdom: because the wisdom that knows the Dharma is accomplished. Accomplishing entry into practice: because he knows the vessel to be transformed. Accomplishing teaching: because he teaches correctly, matching the faculties [of his listeners]."

The Thicket of the Mind's Own Activity

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩隨順如是智慧，如實知眾生心行稠林、煩惱行稠林、業行稠林、根行稠林、信行稠林、性行稠林、深心行稠林、使行稠林、生行稠林、習氣行稠林，如實知三聚差別行稠林。

論曰：是中入行成就者，依共依煩惱業生，共染煩惱染淨等，依定不定時。如經「是菩薩隨順如是智慧，如實知眾生心行稠林故，乃至三聚差別行稠林」故。彼復定不定時根等、次第根等、相似信等，如經「根行稠林故、信行稠林故、性行稠林故、深心行稠林故、使行稠林故、生行稠林故、習氣行稠林故，如實知三聚差別行稠林」故。稠林者，眾多義故、難知義故。行者，不正信義故。云何心行稠林差別？

經曰：是菩薩如實知眾生諸心種種相，心雜相、心輕轉生不生相、心無形相、心無邊一切處眾多相、心清淨相、心染不染相、心縛解相、心幻起相、心隨道生相，乃至無量百千種種心差別相，皆如實知。

論曰：是中心行稠林差別者，心種種差別異故，如經「是菩薩如實知眾生心種種相」故。彼心種種相，有八種：一、差別相，心意識六種差別故，如經「心雜相」故。二、行相，住異生滅行故，如經「心輕轉生不生相」故。三、第一義相，觀彼心離心，心身不可得故，如經「心無形相」故。四、自相順行無量境界取故，如經「心無邊一切處眾多相」故。五、自性不染相，如經「心清淨相」故。

六、同煩惱不同煩惱相，如經「心染不染相」故。七、同使不同使相，如經「心縛解相」故。八、因相，諸菩薩以願力生，餘眾生自業力生故，如經「心幻起相故、心隨道生相故，乃至無量百千種種心差別相皆如實知」故，以自性清淨心故。第六第七心染不染故、心縛解故，此二句煩惱染示現。第八句心隨道故，生染示現。云何煩惱行稠林差別？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, according with wisdom such as this, truly knows the thicket of sentient beings' own mental activity, the thicket of afflictions' own activity, the thicket of karma's own activity, the thicket of faculties' own activity, the thicket of faith's own activity, the thicket of nature's own activity, the thicket of deep mind's own activity, the thicket of latent tendencies' own activity, the thicket of birth's own activity, and the thicket of habitual tendencies' own activity; and truly knows the thicket of the activity of the distinctions among the three groups.

Commentary: Here, accomplishing entry into practice is in dependence on what is shared, in dependence on affliction, karma, and birth, in dependence on shared defilement — affliction, defilement and purity, and the rest — and in dependence on determinate and indeterminate time. As the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, according with wisdom such as this, truly knows the thicket of sentient beings' own mental activity," all the way to "the thicket of the activity of the distinctions among the three groups." This, further, concerns determinate and indeterminate time, the faculties and the rest, the sequence of the faculties and the rest, and the resemblance of faith and the rest — as the sūtra says, "the thicket of faculties' own activity," "the thicket of faith's own activity," "the thicket of nature's own activity," "the thicket of deep mind's own activity," "the thicket of latent tendencies' own activity," "the thicket of birth's own activity," "the thicket of habitual tendencies' own activity," and "truly knows the thicket of the activity of the distinctions among the three groups." "Thicket" means the meaning of being many, and the meaning of being difficult to know. "Activity" means the meaning of faith not being correct. What, then, are the distinctions of the thicket of the mind's own activity?

Sūtra: This bodhisattva truly knows the manifold marks of sentient beings' every mind: the mark of the mind's admixture, the mark of the mind's swift turning, arising and not arising, the mark of the mind's formlessness, the mark of the mind's boundless multiplicity throughout every place, the mark of the mind's purity, the mark of the mind's defilement and non-defilement, the mark of the mind's bondage and liberation, the mark of the mind's illusory arising, and the mark of the mind's arising in accordance with the path — all the way to boundless hundreds of thousands of manifold distinct marks of mind, he truly knows every one.

Commentary: Here, the distinctions of the thicket of the mind's own activity mean that the mind has manifold, distinct differences — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows the manifold marks of sentient beings' every mind." These manifold marks of mind are of eight kinds: first, the mark of distinction, since mind, mentation, and consciousness have six distinct kinds — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the mind's admixture." Second, the mark of activity, since abiding, change, arising, and ceasing are its activity — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the mind's swift turning, arising and not arising." Third, the mark of ultimate meaning: contemplating that this mind is free of mind, since mind and its embodiment cannot be apprehended — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the mind's formlessness." Fourth, its own mark, since it accords in practice with the apprehension of boundless object-realms — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the mind's boundless multiplicity throughout every place." Fifth, the mark of its own nature being undefiled — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the mind's purity."

Sixth, the mark of being the same as affliction and not the same as affliction — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the mind's defilement and non-defilement." Seventh, the mark of being the same as the latent tendencies and not the same as the latent tendencies — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the mind's bondage and liberation." Eighth, the mark of cause, since bodhisattvas are born through the power of vow while other sentient beings are born through the power of their own karma — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the mind's illusory arising," "the mark of the mind's arising in accordance with the path," all the way to "boundless hundreds of thousands of manifold distinct marks of mind, he truly knows every one" — since the mind, in its own nature, is pure. The sixth and seventh — "the mind's defilement and non-defilement" and "the mind's bondage and liberation" — these two clauses manifest the defilement of affliction. The eighth clause, "the mind's arising in accordance with the path," manifests the defilement of birth. What, then, are the distinctions of the thicket of afflictions' own activity?

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang groups the eight marks of mind into two clusters of four, attributing the division to an earlier master: 遠云「八中初四妄心、後四真心」— "Yuan[gong] says: among the eight, the first four concern the false mind, the last four concern the true mind." On the third mark, formlessness, he anticipates an objection — if this mark belongs to the ultimate meaning, how can it still be called mind at all? — and answers by locating the whole analysis within a single paradox: 以真性隨緣起此心故，如云自性清淨心，不染而染，成就染心等 — "because the true nature, according with conditions, gives rise to this very mind — as it is said, the mind pure in its own nature, though undefiled, becomes defiled, thereby accomplishing the defiled mind." The false mind and the true mind, on this reading, are not two separate minds but the same mind read from two directions.

On the eighth mark, the mark of cause, Fazang resolves an apparent tension in the treatise's own language: a bodhisattva's birth through the power of vow is called 幻起, "illusory arising" — language that might suggest deception, 似同諂曲, "resembling flattery and crookedness." But he insists 然實成益，故云質直 — "yet in reality it accomplishes benefit, and is therefore called upright and direct" — the appearance of illusion belongs only to the manner of the birth, not to the bodhisattva's own sincerity in undertaking it. He also notes why the sixth and seventh marks can speak of the mind's bondage at all: 明在纏不染 — they show the mind "within the bonds of affliction, yet undefiled" — a claim that only holds together because the fifth mark's pure own-nature has already been established.

The Thickets of Afflictions' and Karma's Own Activity

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩如實知諸煩惱深入相、行無邊相、共生不離相、煩惱使一義相、心相應不相應相、隨道生處得報相、三界中差別相、愛無明見箭大過相、三種業因不斷相，略說乃至如實知八萬四千煩惱行差別相。

論曰：煩惱行稠林差別者，三種事示現：一、遠入，乃至有頂故，如經「是菩薩如實知煩惱深入相」故。二、難知，無量善根等修集行故，如經「行無邊相」故。三、染業，煩惱生染故。是中隨所縛、以何縛及所縛事，此事說煩惱染染事示現，如經「共生不離相故、煩惱使一義相故、心相應不相應相」故。是中隨所縛者，迭共同事、迭共相依，共生不離故。以何縛者，謂使以有使故不得解脫，煩惱使一義故。所縛事者，謂心心相應、不相應故。不相應者，示可得解脫故。身事生道界因故，生煩惱染示現。如經「隨道生處得報相故、三界中差別相故、於三分中業因障解脫故、隨順世間身口意業故、不斷起因」故。業煩惱妄想染示現，如經「愛無明見箭大過相故、三種業根本不斷相故、乃至如實知八萬四千煩惱行差別相」故。三分者，一、愛行欲眾生；二、無戒眾生；三、外道眾生。云何業行稠林差別？

經曰：是菩薩如實知諸業善不善無記相、有作未作相、心共生不離相、因自性盡集果不失次第相、有報無報相、黑業白業黑白業不黑不白業正受業差別相、業因無量相、聖世間差別相、現報生報後報相、乘非乘定不定相，乃至如實知八萬四千諸業差別相。

論曰：業行稠林差別者，道因差別示現，如經「是菩薩如實知諸業善不善無記相」故。自性差別，如經「有作未作相」故。方便差別，如經「心共生不離相」故。盡集果差別，如經「因自性盡集果不失次第相」故。已受果未受果差別，如經「有報無報相」故。對差別，如經「黑業白業黑白業不黑不白業正受業差別相」故。因緣差別，如經「業因無量相」故。未集已集差別，如經「聖世間差別相」故。定不定報差別，如經「現報生報後報相故、乘非乘定不定相故，乃至如實知八萬四千諸業差別相」故。是中自性差別有二種業：一、籌量時；二、作業時。方便差別者，心共生熏心，不別生果故。盡集果差別者，無始時業自然念念滅壞，集不失故，有為作業因盡集故。已受果未受果差別者，生報後報受不受應知。對差別者，黑業對白業、白業對黑業、不黑不白業對二業、二業對不黑不白業，業集成就差別應知。定不定差別者，三種時定不定故、三種乘定不定故。非乘者，世間定不定應知。云何根行稠林差別？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva truly knows every affliction's mark of penetrating deeply, the mark of its boundless activity, the mark of its arising together, inseparable, the mark of afflictions and latent tendencies being of a single meaning, the mark of the mind's association and non-association, the mark of receiving retribution according to the place of birth on the path, the mark of distinctions among the three realms, the mark of the great fault of the arrows of craving, ignorance, and views, and the mark of the threefold cause of karma remaining unbroken — briefly stated, all the way to truly knowing the eighty-four thousand distinct marks of the activity of afflictions.

Commentary: The distinctions of the thicket of afflictions' own activity are manifest through three matters: first, penetrating far, reaching even to the Peak of Existence — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows every affliction's mark of penetrating deeply." Second, being difficult to know, since it is the practice gathered through boundless roots of goodness and the rest — as the sūtra says, "the mark of its boundless activity." Third, the defilement of karma, since affliction gives rise to defilement. Here, what is bound according to, by what it is bound, and the matter that is bound — this matter is spoken of to manifest the defiled matter of affliction's own defilement — as the sūtra says, "the mark of arising together, inseparable," "the mark of afflictions and latent tendencies being of a single meaning," and "the mark of the mind's association and non-association." Here, "what is bound according to" means that mind and affliction mutually share the same function and mutually depend on each other, arising together, inseparable. "By what it is bound" means that, because there is the latent tendency, one cannot attain liberation — since affliction and the latent tendency are of a single meaning. "The matter that is bound" means the mind's association and non-association. "Non-association" shows that liberation can be attained. Since the matter of the body is the cause of the realm of birth on the path, the defilement of birth's own affliction is manifest. As the sūtra says, "the mark of receiving retribution according to the place of birth on the path," "the mark of distinctions among the three realms," "within the three portions, the cause of karma hinders liberation," "according with the world's own action of body, speech, and mind," and "the cause that ceaselessly arises." The defilement of karma, affliction, and false conceptualization is manifest — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the great fault of the arrows of craving, ignorance, and views," "the mark of the threefold root of karma remaining unbroken," all the way to "truly knowing the eighty-four thousand distinct marks of the activity of afflictions." The three portions are: first, sentient beings whose activity is craving desire; second, sentient beings without precepts; third, sentient beings of other paths. What, then, are the distinctions of the thicket of karma's own activity?

Sūtra: This bodhisattva truly knows the mark of every karma's wholesomeness, unwholesomeness, and indeterminacy, the mark of having been done and not yet done, the mark of the mind's arising together, inseparable, the mark of the cause's own nature exhausting, gathering, and its fruit not being lost in sequence, the mark of having retribution and having no retribution, the mark of the distinctions of black karma, white karma, black-and-white karma, neither-black-nor-white karma, and karma directly experienced, the mark of the boundlessness of the cause of karma, the mark of the distinction between the noble and the worldly, the mark of retribution in this very life, retribution in the next birth, and retribution thereafter, and the mark of the determinacy and indeterminacy of vehicle and non-vehicle — all the way to truly knowing the eighty-four thousand distinct marks of every karma.

Commentary: The distinctions of the thicket of karma's own activity are manifest through the distinction of the cause of the path — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows the mark of every karma's wholesomeness, unwholesomeness, and indeterminacy." The distinction of its own nature — as the sūtra says, "the mark of having been done and not yet done." The distinction of skillful means — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the mind's arising together, inseparable." The distinction of the exhausting and gathering of the fruit — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the cause's own nature exhausting, gathering, and its fruit not being lost in sequence." The distinction between the fruit already received and the fruit not yet received — as the sūtra says, "the mark of having retribution and having no retribution." The distinction of opposition — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the distinctions of black karma, white karma, black-and-white karma, neither-black-nor-white karma, and karma directly experienced." The distinction of cause and condition — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the boundlessness of the cause of karma." The distinction between not yet gathered and already gathered — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the distinction between the noble and the worldly." The distinction of determinate and indeterminate retribution — as the sūtra says, "the mark of retribution in this very life, retribution in the next birth, and retribution thereafter," "the mark of the determinacy and indeterminacy of vehicle and non-vehicle," all the way to "truly knowing the eighty-four thousand distinct marks of every karma." Here, the distinction of its own nature has two kinds of karma: first, the time of deliberation; second, the time of performing the action. The distinction of skillful means means that the mind, arising together with the intention, perfumes the mind, without giving rise to any separate fruit. The distinction of the exhausting and gathering of the fruit means that karma, from beginningless time, naturally ceases and perishes moment by moment, yet what is gathered is not lost, since the conditioned cause of the action performed is exhausted and gathered. The distinction between the fruit already received and the fruit not yet received should be understood as retribution in the next birth and retribution thereafter, whether received or not yet received. The distinction of opposition should be understood as: black karma opposed to white karma, white karma opposed to black karma, neither-black-nor-white karma opposed to the two karmas, and the two karmas opposed to neither-black-nor-white karma — the distinctions of karma's own gathering and accomplishment. The distinction of determinacy and indeterminacy means the determinacy and indeterminacy of the three times, and the determinacy and indeterminacy of the three vehicles. "Non-vehicle" should be understood as the determinacy and indeterminacy of the world. What, then, are the distinctions of the thicket of faculties' own activity?

Reading note — after Fazang

On the treatise's recurring figure of "eighty-four thousand" — used here for both the varieties of affliction and, just below, of karma — Fazang supplies a full numerological derivation from the *Bhadrakalpika-sūtra: 佛一代有三百五十度功德法門⋯一一各以六度為因，便有二千一百 — "across a buddha's teaching career there are three hundred fifty gates of meritorious dharma⋯each multiplied by the six perfections as its cause yields two thousand one hundred." Multiplied again against the four great elements and the six kinds of decay, 即成二萬一千 — "this comes to twenty-one thousand" — and then against four types of sentient being differing in their besetting fault, 一多貪、二多嗔、三多癡、四等分 — "first, much craving; second, much hatred; third, much delusion; fourth, equally distributed" — 即成八萬四千諸度法門 — "this comes to eighty-four thousand gates of the perfections," whose obstructing counterparts are precisely the eighty-four thousand afflictions the sūtra names; and, he adds, 業等亦爾, the same holds for karma.

On "by what it is bound," Fazang identifies the binding substance precisely: 謂使也，以有使故、不得解脫故⋯謂十使、十纏，故云差別 — "namely, the latent tendency: because there is the latent tendency, one does not attain liberation⋯that is, the ten latent tendencies and the ten fetters — hence 'distinction.'" He notes further that the treatise elsewhere separates these two, 使、纏，俱是能縛 — "the latent tendency and the fetter, both being what is capable of binding" — one by way of underlying pursuit, the other by way of open entanglement.

On the fourfold classification of black and white karma, Fazang reaches directly for the *Abhidharmakośa: 諸不善業一向名黑，染污性故⋯色界善一向名白，不雜惡故⋯欲界善業名黑白，惡所雜故⋯諸無流業名非黑白 — "every unwholesome karma is uniformly called black, being defiled in nature⋯wholesome karma of the form realm is uniformly called white, unmixed with evil⋯wholesome karma of the desire realm is called black-and-white, mixed with evil⋯every undefiled karma is called neither black nor white."

Part 41 Complete

This closes Part 41, opening Sādhumatī, the Ninth Ground, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom. The treatise's own fourfold division of this ground has now been set out — accomplishing the skillful means of a Dharma teacher, accomplishing wisdom, accomplishing entry into practice, and accomplishing teaching — together with the commentary's opening contrast to the Eighth Ground's mere purifying of buddha-lands. Vajragarbha's account of the bodhisattva's immeasurable and well-considering wisdom as the skillful means of a Dharma teacher has been translated in full, together with his true knowledge of wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate dharmas, defiled and undefiled, worldly and supramundane, conceivable and inconceivable, determinate and indeterminate, across the three vehicles. Opening the third division, accomplishing entry into practice, this part has translated the first three of the treatise's eleven-part analysis of the "thickets" of sentient beings' own activity: the thicket of the mind's own activity, unfolded by the commentary into eight distinct marks spanning the false mind and the true; the thicket of afflictions' own activity, unfolded into three matters of penetrating deeply, being difficult to know, and defiled karma; and the thicket of karma's own activity, unfolded into ten further distinctions of its own nature, skillful means, and retribution. Part 42 continues this same systematic analysis with the thicket of faculties, the thicket of latent tendencies, the thicket of birth-destinies, and the thicket of habitual tendencies.
