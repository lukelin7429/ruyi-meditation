The Orphaned Without Refuge, and the Titles of Mastery

Continuing within the excellence of the practice of the non-abiding path, the bodhisattva reflects that ordinary, unenlightened beings are truly strange and pitiable — foolish and without wisdom, their countless bodies extinguished again and again, yet never once giving rise to any thought of revulsion toward this suffering, which only ever increases. He sees these beings as orphaned, solitary, without one to depend upon, without a dwelling, without an island, without any ultimate resolution, and blind — and from this very compassion arises his vow to rescue and deliver every sentient being, in nine kinds, both now and in every future. The treatise then names the titles this Fifth Ground confers upon him: ten titles of mastery in what is heard, thought, and cultivated — "one who is mindful, since he never forgets any dharma" foremost among them — and ten further titles of a practice that is insatiable, unremitting, and never wearied.

Astonished Compassion, and Beings Orphaned Without Refuge

Chinese Source

經曰：菩薩爾時作如是念：此諸凡夫甚為可怪，愚癡無智，有無量無邊阿僧祇身，已滅、今滅、當滅。如是盡滅已，不能於身生厭離想，轉更增長機關苦身，常隨世間漂流不能得返，歸五陰舍不能捨離，不畏四大毒蛇，不能拔出我慢見箭，不能滅除貪恚癡火，不能破壞無明黑暗，不能乾竭愛著大河，不求十力大聖導師，常入魔意稠林，於生死海中常為諸惡覺觀所轉。

論曰：大悲正觀深重苦者，無量世隨逐種種苦故。有無量無邊阿僧祇身滅者，無量世隨逐故。云何種種苦觀？生苦故、彼集故、離彼滅道故。如是觀老病死，苦故、彼集故、離彼滅道故。是中此身依因緣有機關苦身者，生苦示現。常隨世間漂流不能得返者，集愛示現。歸五陰舍不能捨離者，離彼滅道示現。不畏四大毒蛇者，謂病苦，彼增損生故。妄梵行求眾生、欲求眾生受欲者，行惡行者。有求眾生者，不能拔出我慢見箭示現。不能滅除貪恚癡火，不能破壞無明黑暗，不能乾竭愛著大河。如是次第彼集示現。趣無畏處，不求十力大聖導師，遠離彼滅示現。常入魔意稠林，隨順怨道，遠離彼道示現。於生死海中常為諸惡覺觀所轉者，一切三界心心數相虛妄分別應知。次說上大慈光明觀行。

經曰：我見彼諸眾生受如是苦惱，孤獨無救，無有依者、無有舍者、無有洲者、無究竟者、盲無目者，無明翳藏所纏、愚癡所覆。為此眾生發如是心：唯我一人獨無等侶，修集功德智慧助道，以是功德智慧助道資糧令一切眾生得住畢竟清淨，乃至使得佛十力無障礙智盡。

論曰：彼受如是苦惱者，如前所說。孤獨者，於彼苦中無救拔故。復次孤獨者，於已受苦未受苦中故。言無救無依者，離善知識故。言無有舍者，離聞正法故。言無有洲者，離寂靜思惟故。言無究竟者，離於正見故。言盲無目者，彼障諸舊煩惱及客塵煩惱常起邪念，不聞正法等故。言無明翳藏所纏愚癡所覆，唯我一人獨無等侶者，顯示勇猛勝事。修集功德智慧助道者，顯示修行增長，依彼教化眾生作人天因乃至涅槃因故。畢竟清淨者，勝世間清淨故。得佛十力者，降伏諸魔怨故。無障礙智盡者，勝聲聞辟支佛淨智故。

經曰：菩薩如是善觀起智慧力，所修諸善發願為救一切眾生故、為一切眾生作利益故、為一切眾生得安樂故、愍念一切眾生故、為令一切眾生無苦惱故、為令一切眾生得解脫故、為攝一切眾生故、為一切眾生心清淨故、為調伏一切眾生故，發願為一切眾生令入大涅槃故。

論曰：是中善觀起智慧力者，以正觀智調伏眾生故。皆為救度一切眾生者，拔一切苦惱故。救度有九種：一、住不善眾生令住善法，如經「為一切眾生作利益故」。二、住善法眾生令得樂果，如經「為一切眾生得安樂故」。三、住貧乏眾生與一切資生之具，如經「愍念一切眾生故」。四、住病苦及諸外緣所惱眾生皆令除斷，如經「為令一切眾生無苦惱故」。五、世間繫閉眾生令得出離，如經「為令一切眾生得解脫故」。有四種相令諸外道信解正法，如經「為攝一切眾生故」。疑惑眾生善決定斷疑，如經「為一切眾生心清淨故」。已住決定眾生勸修三學，如經「為調伏一切眾生故」。已住三學眾生令得涅槃，如經「發願為一切眾生令入大涅槃故」。此九種救苦中，初二句為救未來，餘句為救現在亦救未來。是名不住道修行勝。次說不住道修行勝果。有四種相：一、攝功德勝；二、修行勝；三、教化眾生勝；四、起隨順世間智勝。云何攝功德勝？攝聞戒智勝故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: At that time the bodhisattva gives rise to this reflection: these ordinary, unenlightened beings are truly strange and pitiable — foolish and without wisdom, possessed of bodies boundless, limitless, and beyond number, already extinguished, now being extinguished, and yet to be extinguished. Even so, utterly extinguished again and again, they are unable to give rise, with regard to the body, to any thought of revulsion and weariness, but instead ever more increase this mechanism-body of suffering; they ever follow the world's drifting current, unable to find their way back; they return to the dwelling-house of the five aggregates, unable to abandon it; they do not fear the four great poisonous serpents; they are unable to draw out the arrow of the view that is the conceit of self; they are unable to extinguish the fire of craving, hatred, and delusion; they are unable to break apart the darkness of ignorance; they are unable to dry up the great river of attachment and craving; they do not seek out the great sagely guide of the ten powers; they ever enter the thicket of Māra's intent; and, upon the sea of birth-and-death, they are ever turned about by every evil thought and deliberation.

Commentary: "The right contemplation of great compassion upon this deep and heavy suffering" means that it is followed and pursued through boundless ages by manifold kinds of suffering. "Possessed of bodies boundless, limitless, and beyond number" that are extinguished means that it is followed and pursued through boundless ages. What, then, is the contemplation of manifold suffering? It is the suffering of birth, its arising, and the path that departs from its cessation. Old age, sickness, and death are contemplated in just the same way — as suffering, as its arising, and as the path that departs from its cessation. Here, that this body, in dependence on causes and conditions, is this mechanism-body of suffering, makes manifest the suffering of birth. That they ever follow the world's drifting current, unable to find their way back, makes manifest the arising that is craving. That they return to the dwelling-house of the five aggregates, unable to abandon it, makes manifest the path that departs from its cessation. That they do not fear the four great poisonous serpents refers to the suffering of sickness, since it is these elements' own waxing and waning that gives rise to it. Those who, in false brahmacarya, seek out beings, and those who, in desire, seek out beings that receive desire, are those who practice evil conduct. That those who seek out beings of existence are unable to draw out the arrow of the view that is the conceit of self makes this manifest. That they are unable to extinguish the fire of craving, hatred, and delusion, unable to break apart the darkness of ignorance, and unable to dry up the great river of attachment and craving — these, in this order, make manifest that very arising. Their heading toward the place of fearlessness, and their failing to seek out the great sagely guide of the ten powers, makes manifest their departing far from that cessation. Their ever entering the thicket of Māra's intent, according with the path of an enemy, makes manifest their departing far from that path. That, upon the sea of birth-and-death, they are ever turned about by every evil thought and deliberation should be understood as the false discrimination belonging to the mind and mental factors throughout the three realms entire. Next is explained the contemplation of great loving-kindness's radiant light, above.

Sūtra: I see these sentient beings undergoing suffering and affliction such as this — orphaned and without rescue, having no one to depend upon, no dwelling, no island, no ultimate resolution, blind and without eyes, wrapped about by the cataract-veil of ignorance, and covered over by folly. For the sake of these beings I give rise to this mind: I alone, singular, without any equal companion, shall gather and cultivate the merit and wisdom that assist the path, and, by means of these provisions of merit and wisdom that assist the path, cause all sentient beings to abide in ultimate purity, even unto attaining the Buddha's ten powers and the exhaustion of unobstructed wisdom.

Commentary: "Undergoing suffering and affliction such as this" is as explained before. "Orphaned" means that, in the midst of that suffering, there is no one to come to the rescue. Furthermore, "orphaned" refers to being so both in suffering already undergone and suffering not yet undergone. Speaking of "without rescue" and "without one to depend upon" means being apart from a good spiritual friend. Speaking of "without a dwelling" means being apart from hearing the correct Dharma. Speaking of "without an island" means being apart from tranquil reflection. Speaking of "without ultimate resolution" means being apart from right view. Speaking of "blind and without eyes" means that, obstructed by afflictions of long standing and by the adventitious afflictions of the guest-dust, one constantly gives rise to erroneous thought, and does not hear the correct Dharma, and so forth. Speaking of "wrapped about by the cataract-veil of ignorance and covered over by folly," and "I alone, singular, without any equal companion," makes manifest a matter of courageous excellence. "Gathering and cultivating the merit and wisdom that assist the path" makes manifest the increase of cultivation, since, in dependence on this, one teaches and transforms sentient beings, making it the cause of birth among gods and humans, even unto the cause of nirvāṇa. "Ultimate purity" means a purity that surpasses the world's own. "Attaining the Buddha's ten powers" means subduing every host of Māra's enmity. "The exhaustion of unobstructed wisdom" means a purity of wisdom surpassing that of the śrāvakas and the pratyekabuddhas.

Sūtra: The bodhisattva, having thus well contemplated and given rise to the power of wisdom, in all the wholesome practices he cultivates, sets forth the vow: for the sake of rescuing all sentient beings; for the sake of bringing benefit to all sentient beings; for the sake of enabling all sentient beings to attain ease and joy; out of compassion and pity for all sentient beings; so that all sentient beings may be free of suffering and affliction; so that all sentient beings may attain liberation; for the sake of gathering in all sentient beings; so that the minds of all sentient beings may become pure; for the sake of taming and subduing all sentient beings; and he sets forth the vow that all sentient beings may enter great nirvāṇa.

Commentary: Here, "having well contemplated and given rise to the power of wisdom" means taming and subduing sentient beings through the wisdom of right contemplation. "All for the sake of rescuing and delivering every sentient being" means drawing out every suffering and affliction. This rescuing and delivering is of nine kinds. First, causing beings who abide in what is unwholesome to abide in wholesome dharmas — as the sūtra says, "for the sake of bringing benefit to all sentient beings." Second, causing beings who abide in wholesome dharmas to attain the fruit of joy — as the sūtra says, "for the sake of enabling all sentient beings to attain ease and joy." Third, giving to beings who abide in poverty and want every implement of livelihood — as the sūtra says, "out of compassion and pity for all sentient beings." Fourth, causing every being afflicted by the suffering of sickness and by every external condition to have it entirely removed and cut off — as the sūtra says, "so that all sentient beings may be free of suffering and affliction." Fifth, causing beings bound and confined within the world to attain release from it — as the sūtra says, "so that all sentient beings may attain liberation." Causing every non-Buddhist, through four kinds of marks, to gain faith and understanding in the correct Dharma — as the sūtra says, "for the sake of gathering in all sentient beings." Causing beings caught in doubt and confusion to reach a sound resolve and cut off their doubt — as the sūtra says, "so that the minds of all sentient beings may become pure." Urging beings already abiding in resolve to cultivate the three trainings — as the sūtra says, "for the sake of taming and subduing all sentient beings." Causing beings already abiding in the three trainings to attain nirvāṇa — as the sūtra says, "he sets forth the vow that all sentient beings may enter great nirvāṇa." Among these nine kinds of rescuing from suffering, the first two clauses rescue beings in the future, while the remaining clauses rescue beings in the present and also in the future. This is called the excellence of the practice of the non-abiding path. Next is explained the fruit of the excellence of the practice of the non-abiding path, which has four kinds of mark: first, the excellence of gathering in merit; second, the excellence of practice; third, the excellence of teaching and transforming sentient beings; and fourth, the excellence of giving rise to wisdom that accords with the world. What, then, is the excellence of gathering in merit? It is the excellence of gathering in what is heard, in moral discipline, and in wisdom.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang is precise about why this suffering is called "strange" (可怪) rather than merely pitiable, and he gives two distinct reasons rather than one. The first is the beings' own failure to react: 初觀眾生受苦不厭離，故云可怪 — "first, contemplating that sentient beings undergo suffering without feeling revulsion or weariness toward it — this is why it is called 'strange.'" The second is their clinging to what was never there to begin with: 又於無所有中而生貪著，故云可怪 — "further, giving rise to craving and attachment amid what has no real existence at all — this too is why it is called 'strange.'" He traces both failures to a single root: 謂無明癡故, "namely, ignorance and folly." The astonishment, in other words, is not at the suffering itself but at beings' proximity to it without recoil — and worse, their reaching for more of what caused it.

On the fourfold taxonomy of "orphaned, without refuge," Fazang maps each of the sūtra's four terms onto a distinct practice a good student would otherwise rely on — nearness to a friend, hearing the Dharma, reflection, and cultivation — and glosses the second with a memorable image: 聞法如洲，依之即免四流水所漂；不能聽聞，故云無洲 — "hearing the Dharma is like an island: relying on it, one is at once spared being swept away by the four floods; being unable to hear it, this is why it is called 'without an island.'" He also resolves an apparent tension in "I alone, without any equal companion" — surely the Dharma-realm is full of buddhas and bodhisattvas — by locating the claim not in fact but in resolve: 諸佛菩薩充滿法界，何故言孤獨？不欲復待彼助 — "since the buddhas and bodhisattvas fill the entire Dharma-realm, why does he speak of being 'orphaned'? It is because he does not wish to wait upon their help any further."

The Titles of Mastery This Ground Confers

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩住此第五菩薩難勝地已，名為念者，不忘諸法故。名為意者，善決定智慧故。名為去者，知經書意次第故。名為有慚愧者，自護護彼故。名為堅心者，不捨持戒行故。名為覺者，善思惟是處非處故。名為隨智者，不隨他故。名為隨慧者，善分別諸法章句是義非義故。名為得神通者，善修禪定故。名為善方便者，隨世間法行故。

論曰：攝聞勝者，攝聞思修慧勝故。云何慧勝？如經「名為念者，不忘諸法故。名為意者，善決定智慧故。名為去者，知經書意次第故」。是中念者，聞慧勝故。意者，思慧勝故。去者，修慧勝故。此諸句次第復有異釋：聞持勝故名為念者，法智甚深勝故名為意者，意甚深智勝故名為去者，此略說成就二種善巧法善巧義善巧故。戒攝勝者有二種：忍辱柔和勝，及戒無缺勝。如經「名為有慚愧者，自護護彼故。名為堅心者，不捨持戒行故」。攝智勝者有五種：一者因緣集智，無因顛倒因邪見對治，如經「名為覺者，善思惟是處非處故」。二者證智，魔事對治，如經「名為隨智者，不隨他故」。三者知妄說智，異說對治，善知是句義非句義雜句義，如經「名為隨慧者，善分別諸法章句是義非義故」。四者神力智，邪歸依行對治，如經「名為得神通者，善修禪定故」。五者化眾生智，方便攝取故，如經「名為善方便者，隨世間法行故」。是名攝功德勝。云何修行勝？

經曰：名為無厭足者，善集功德行助道故。名為不休息精進者，常求智慧行助道故。名為無疲惓者，集大慈悲行助道故。名為常念佛法者，求佛十力、四無所畏、十八不共佛法故。名為善念修行者，起莊嚴佛國故。名為具足修行種種善業者，集三十二相八十種好故。名為常行精進者，求莊嚴佛身口意故。名為樂大恭敬法者，親近供養一切菩薩及法師故。名為善起願自在者，大方便善入世間故。名為日夜遠離餘心者，常樂教化一切眾生故。

論曰：是中修行勝有十種：一、增長因行，如經「名為無厭足者，善集功德行助道故」。二、依止因行，如經「名為不休息精進者，常求智慧行助道故」。三、化眾生不疲惓行，如經「名為無疲惓者，集大慈悲行助道故」。四、起佛法行，如經「名為常念佛法者，求佛十力、四無所畏、十八不共佛法故」。五、起淨佛國土行，如經「名為善念修行者，起莊嚴佛國故」。云何莊嚴？無煩惱染得堅固智慧，眾生住在其中及佛法莊嚴故。六、依佛法身起行，如經「名為具足修行種種善業者，集三十二相八十種好故」。七、依佛所作起行，如經「名為常行精進者，求莊嚴佛身口意故」。八、敬重法行，如經「名為樂大恭敬法者，親近供養一切菩薩及法師故」。九、願取有行，如經「名為善起願自在者，大方便善入世間故」。十、離小乘心行，如經「名為日夜遠離餘心者，常樂教化一切眾生故」。是名修行勝。云何教化眾生勝？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, having dwelt in this Fifth Ground, the bodhisattva ground Sudurjayā, is named "one who is mindful," since he never forgets any dharma. He is named "one of understanding," since his wisdom is well and resolutely settled. He is named "one who has gone forth," since he knows the sequence of the meaning of the scriptures. He is named "one who has a sense of shame," since he guards himself and guards others. He is named "one of firm resolve," since he never abandons the practice of upholding moral discipline. He is named "one who is awakened," since he skillfully reflects upon what is the case and what is not the case. He is named "one who follows wisdom," since he does not follow after others. He is named "one who follows insight," since he skillfully discerns, in the chapter and verse of every dharma, what accords with the meaning and what does not. He is named "one who attains spiritual powers," since he skillfully cultivates meditative concentration. He is named "one of skillful means," since he acts in accordance with the dharmas of the world.

Commentary: "The excellence of gathering in what is heard" means the excellence of gathering in the wisdom of hearing, of thought, and of cultivation. What, then, is the excellence of wisdom? As the sūtra says: "he is named 'one who is mindful,' since he never forgets any dharma; he is named 'one of understanding,' since his wisdom is well and resolutely settled; he is named 'one who has gone forth,' since he knows the sequence of the meaning of the scriptures." Here, "one who is mindful" is the excellence of the wisdom of hearing; "one of understanding" is the excellence of the wisdom of thought; "one who has gone forth" is the excellence of the wisdom of cultivation. These clauses, moreover, admit, in their sequence, of a further interpretation: he is named "one who is mindful" because his retention of what is heard is excellent; he is named "one of understanding" because his wisdom of the dharma is excellent in its very profundity; he is named "one who has gone forth" because his wisdom, profound in its very understanding, is excellent — this, in brief, is to say that he accomplishes two kinds of skillful means: skillfulness with regard to the dharma, and skillfulness with regard to its meaning. "The excellence of the gathering-in that is moral discipline" is of two kinds: the excellence of patience and gentleness, and the excellence of moral discipline without flaw — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one who has a sense of shame,' since he guards himself and guards others; he is named 'one of firm resolve,' since he never abandons the practice of upholding moral discipline." "The excellence of the gathering-in that is wisdom" is of five kinds. First, the wisdom of the arising of causes and conditions, the antidote to the erroneous view of no cause and of a false cause — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one who is awakened,' since he skillfully reflects upon what is the case and what is not the case." Second, the wisdom of realization, the antidote to the affairs of Māra — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one who follows wisdom,' since he does not follow after others." Third, the wisdom that knows false teachings, the antidote to divergent teachings, skillfully knowing what accords with the meaning of a phrase, what does not accord with it, and what is a mixture of meanings — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one who follows insight,' since he skillfully discerns, in the chapter and verse of every dharma, what accords with the meaning and what does not." Fourth, the wisdom of spiritual power, the antidote to taking a false refuge — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one who attains spiritual powers,' since he skillfully cultivates meditative concentration." Fifth, the wisdom that transforms sentient beings, since it gathers them in through skillful means — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one of skillful means,' since he acts in accordance with the dharmas of the world." This is called the excellence of gathering in merit. What, then, is the excellence of practice?

Sūtra: He is named "one who is insatiable," since he well gathers in the practice of merit that assists the path. He is named "one of unremitting vigor," since he constantly seeks the practice of wisdom that assists the path. He is named "one without weariness," since he gathers in the practice of great loving-kindness and compassion that assists the path. He is named "one who ever is mindful of the buddha-dharma," since he seeks the Buddha's ten powers, four kinds of fearlessness, and eighteen unshared buddha-dharmas. He is named "one whose mindful practice is excellent," since he gives rise to the adornment of a buddha-realm. He is named "one complete in the cultivation and practice of manifold wholesome karmic action," since he gathers in the thirty-two marks and eighty excellent characteristics. He is named "one who ever practices vigor," since he seeks the adornment of the Buddha's body, speech, and mind. He is named "one who delights in the dharma of great reverence," since he draws near to and makes offerings to every bodhisattva and every Dharma-teacher. He is named "one who well gives rise to a vow of self-mastery," since through great skillful means he well enters the world. He is named "one who day and night departs far from every other mind," since he ever delights in teaching and transforming all sentient beings.

Commentary: Here, "the excellence of practice" is of ten kinds. First, the practice of the increasing cause — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one who is insatiable,' since he well gathers in the practice of merit that assists the path." Second, the practice of the supporting cause — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one of unremitting vigor,' since he constantly seeks the practice of wisdom that assists the path." Third, the practice of transforming sentient beings without weariness — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one without weariness,' since he gathers in the practice of great loving-kindness and compassion that assists the path." Fourth, the practice of giving rise to the buddha-dharma — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one who ever is mindful of the buddha-dharma,' since he seeks the Buddha's ten powers, four kinds of fearlessness, and eighteen unshared buddha-dharmas." Fifth, the practice of giving rise to a pure buddha-realm — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one whose mindful practice is excellent,' since he gives rise to the adornment of a buddha-realm." What, then, is this adornment? It is that, undefiled by any affliction, one attains firm and solid wisdom, and that the sentient beings who dwell within it, together with the buddha-dharma itself, are thereby adorned. Sixth, the practice that arises in dependence on the Buddha's dharma-body — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one complete in the cultivation and practice of manifold wholesome karmic action,' since he gathers in the thirty-two marks and eighty excellent characteristics." Seventh, the practice that arises in dependence on what the Buddha himself has done — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one who ever practices vigor,' since he seeks the adornment of the Buddha's body, speech, and mind." Eighth, the practice of revering the Dharma — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one who delights in the dharma of great reverence,' since he draws near to and makes offerings to every bodhisattva and every Dharma-teacher." Ninth, the practice of the vow to take on existence — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one who well gives rise to a vow of self-mastery,' since through great skillful means he well enters the world." Tenth, the practice that departs from the mind of the Lesser Vehicle — as the sūtra says, "he is named 'one who day and night departs far from every other mind,' since he ever delights in teaching and transforming all sentient beings." This is called the excellence of practice. What, then, is the excellence of teaching and transforming sentient beings?

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang frames these titles as belonging to a larger fourfold structure the treatise only completes across two parts, and he is explicit that the division falls along a self/other axis: 行有四種：一攝功德勝、二修行勝，此二自利……三教化眾生勝、四起隨順世間智勝，此二利他 — "the practice is of four kinds: first, the excellence of gathering in merit, and second, the excellence of practice — these two benefit oneself … third, the excellence of teaching and transforming sentient beings, and fourth, the excellence of giving rise to wisdom that accords with the world — these two benefit others." The twenty titles translated here, in other words, are only the self-benefiting half of this ground's mastery; the other-benefiting half, built on the four means of attraction, belongs to what follows.

On the second set of ten titles, Fazang actually reconstructs eleven distinct items rather than ten, and he says so plainly: after listing the treatise's own titles he adds a further clause on not giving rise to false thought so that moral discipline stays unblemished, noting of it 論經欠此句 — "the treatise's own text lacks this clause." His fuller count also regroups the material: the first four items he treats as this ground's "own division" of practice, while the remaining items — beginning with the vow to adorn a buddha's realm — he calls "surpassing-advancement practice," the reach toward what a bodhisattva has not yet attained rather than the consolidation of what he already has.

Part 27 Complete

This closes Part 27, continuing within Sudurjayā, the Ground Difficult to Conquer. Here the bodhisattva's astonished compassion at how strange and pitiable ordinary, deluded beings truly are — foolish, without wisdom, their innumerable bodies extinguished again and again without ever giving rise to a single thought of revulsion — opens onto his contemplation of these same beings as orphaned, solitary, and utterly without refuge: without one to depend upon, without a dwelling, without an island, without any ultimate resolution, and blind. From this compassionate contemplation arises his vow, in nine kinds, to rescue and deliver every sentient being, both now and in every future to come. The treatise then names the titles this Fifth Ground confers upon him — ten titles of mastery in what is heard, thought, and cultivated, and ten further titles of a practice insatiable, unremitting, and never wearied. Part 28 continues within this same ground, turning to the other-benefiting half of this mastery: the bodhisattva's teaching of sentient beings through the four means of attraction, his command of the world's crafts and sciences for the sake of beings' benefit, and the threefold fruit that closes the entire Fifth Ground.
