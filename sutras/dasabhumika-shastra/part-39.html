Ten Kinds of Mastery, the Threefold Great Excellence, and the Ground's Ten Names

This bodhisattva's well-knowing how to give rise to bodies of every kind culminates in his own mastery over life itself, and in nine further kinds of self-mastery — over mind, over material things, over karmic activity, over birth, over vow, over faith and understanding, over as-he-wishes, over the Dharma, and over wisdom — each the antidote to one of ten named fears, from the fear of death to the fear of a great assembly's own awesome virtue. Having attained these, he is at once named "one of inconceivable wisdom" and three further titles, and the treatise sets out a threefold "great excellence" belonging to this ground alone: the greatness of wisdom, the greatness of karmic activity — his three pure actions said to arise as a single event together with the very wisdom that arouses them — and the greatness of a sevenfold merit on which both of these depend. The treatise then opens its own explanation of this ground's name, tracing all ten of its titles, from Acalā, the Immovable, to the Ground of No Effortful Application, back to six distinct aspects of a single ground newly free of every fault — before turning, at the very end, to ask by what meaning a bodhisattva himself, and not only this ground, comes to be called "immovable."

Mastery Over Life, and the Ten Kinds of Self-Mastery

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩善知起如是諸身，則得命自在，不可說不可說劫命住持故。得心自在，無量阿僧祇三昧入智故。得物自在，一切世界無量莊嚴嚴飾住持示現故。得業自在，如現生後時業報住持示現故。得生自在，一切世界生示現故。得願自在，隨心所欲佛國土時示成三菩提故。得信解自在，一切世界中佛滿示現故。得如意自在，一切佛國土中如意作變事示現故。得法自在，無邊無中法門明示現故。得智自在，如來力、無畏、不共法、相好莊嚴、三菩提示現故。

論曰：得自在者，是菩薩如是修行，器世間、眾生世間智正覺世間三種自在行故，得十自在。如經「是菩薩善知起如是諸身則得命自在乃至得智自在」故。此十自在，對治十種怖畏，如是次第應知。

何者是十種怖畏？一、死怖畏；二、煩惱垢怖畏；三、貧窮怖畏；四、惡業怖畏；五、惡道怖畏；六、求不得怖畏；七、謗法罪業怖畏；八、追求時縛不活怖畏；九、云何云何疑怖畏；十、大眾威德怖畏。如是八地得十自在分已說。次說大勝分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, well knowing how to give rise to bodies of this kind, thereby attains mastery over life, sustaining and upholding his own life-span across unspeakable, unspeakable numbers of eons. He attains mastery over mind, through entering the wisdom of boundless, incalculable samādhis. He attains mastery over material things, through sustaining, upholding, and manifesting boundless adornments throughout every world-system. He attains mastery over karmic activity, through sustaining, upholding, and manifesting the ripened fruit of karma as it appears in this present life and in lives to come. He attains mastery over birth, through manifesting birth throughout every world-system. He attains mastery over vow, through manifesting the accomplishment of complete enlightenment within a buddha-land at whatever time his mind may wish. He attains mastery over faith and understanding, through manifesting buddhas filling every world-system. He attains mastery over as-he-wishes, through manifesting wondrous transformations, as he wishes, throughout every buddha-land. He attains mastery over the Dharma, through manifesting, with clarity, the boundless, centerless gateways of the Dharma. He attains mastery over wisdom, through manifesting the tathāgatas' own powers, fearlessnesses, unshared buddha-dharmas, the adornment of excellent marks, and complete enlightenment.

Commentary: "Attaining mastery" means that this bodhisattva, cultivating practice in this way, through the threefold practice of mastery over the receptacle-world, the world of sentient beings, and the world of the wisdom of correct enlightenment, attains these ten kinds of mastery — as the sūtra says, from "this bodhisattva, well knowing how to give rise to bodies of this kind, thereby attains mastery over life" all the way to "he attains mastery over wisdom." These ten kinds of mastery serve as the antidote to ten kinds of fear, and should be understood in just this sequence.

What are these ten kinds of fear? First, the fear of death; second, the fear of the defilement of affliction; third, the fear of poverty; fourth, the fear of evil karma; fifth, the fear of evil destinies; sixth, the fear of not obtaining what is sought; seventh, the fear of the karmic offense of slandering the Dharma; eighth, the fear, in the pursuit of a livelihood, of being bound by time and unable to sustain life; ninth, the fear of doubt, wondering "how is this, how is this?"; and tenth, the fear of the awesome virtue of a great assembly. In this way, the Eighth Ground's division of attaining the ten kinds of mastery has now been explained. Next, the division of great excellence will be explained.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang organizes this passage under four headings — 辨相、治障、出因、得位 — "distinguishing its mark, [naming] the hindrance it counteracts, tracing its cause, and [locating] the position attained." Tracing the cause of each mastery, he draws on the Mahāyānasaṃgraha's own sixfold analysis by perfection: 依《攝論》第十殊勝中以六度為因，初三施為因，以一切時施得命自在、一切處施得心自在、一切物施得財自在 — "following the Mahāyānasaṃgraha's tenth chapter on surpassing qualities, [these masteries] take the six perfections as their cause: the first three take giving as their cause — through giving at all times, one attains mastery over life; through giving in every place, one attains mastery over mind; through giving every kind of thing, one attains mastery over material wealth." Discipline grounds the next two, patience the mastery of faith and understanding, diligence the mastery of vow, meditative concentration the mastery of as-one-wishes, and wisdom the final two.

On where this same tenfold list is attained, Fazang draws a comparison the base treatise itself never makes: 《攝論》約果明佛地所得...今此就因八地所得...下〈離世間品〉十自在，約普賢位貫通諸位 — "the Mahāyānasaṃgraha discusses this in terms of its fruit, as what is attained at the buddha-stage ... whereas here [the treatise] discusses it in terms of its cause, as what is attained at the Eighth Ground ... below, in the 'Leaving the World' chapter [of the Avataṃsaka], the same ten masteries are discussed in terms of the stage of Samantabhadra, running through every stage of the path alike." The same ten names, on this reading, name a single achievement seen from three different vantage points — as buddha-fruit, as eighth-ground cause, and as a thread running the whole path through.

The Threefold Great Excellence: Wisdom, Karmic Activity, and Their Supporting Merit

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩得是菩薩十自在已，即時名為不可思議智者、無量智者、廣智者、不可壞智者。菩薩如是至已，如是智成就，常集起清淨身業、常集起清淨口業、常集起清淨意業，智慧為首，智隨順轉般若波羅蜜增上；大悲為首，方便善巧善能分別。自起願力，善加諸佛所加，常不捨利益眾生行，遍知無邊世界差別事。佛子！略說菩薩得此菩薩不動地，身口意業所作皆能集起一切佛法。

是菩薩得此菩薩不動地，善住淨心力中，離一切煩惱集故。善住深心力中，常不離道故。善住大悲力中，不捨利益眾生故。善住大慈力中，救一切世間故。善住陀羅尼力中，不忘法故。善住辯才力中，智慧善巧分別一切佛法故。善住神通力中，行無邊世界差別故。善住願力中，不捨一切菩薩所行故。善住波羅蜜力中，修集一切佛法故。善住如來加力中，一切種一切智智現前故。是菩薩得如是智力，示一切所作，一切事中無有過咎故。

論曰：大勝者，有三種大：一、智大；二、業大；三、彼二住功德大。云何智大？不可思議智者不住世間、不住涅槃故。如經「是菩薩得是菩薩十自在已，即時名為不可思議智者」故。此不可思議有三種應知：一、修行盡至不可思議；二、所知不可思議；三、除障智不可思議。如經「無量智者故、廣智者故、不可壞智者故」，是名智大。

菩薩如是至已，乃至集起一切佛法故。菩薩如是至已者，如上說。如是智成就者，亦如上說。常集起清淨三業者，此是業大。彼淨業有四種相：一、起能起起同時，如經「智慧為首故、智隨順轉故」。二、智攝不染作利益眾生行等，如經「般若波羅蜜增上故，大悲為首，方便善巧善能分別」故。三因攝自行他行因等，如經「善起願力故，善加諸佛所加」故。四、作業所起利益眾生，淨佛國土成就一切佛法，如經「常不捨利益眾生行故、遍知無邊世界差別事故」。佛子！略說菩薩得此菩薩不動地，身口意業所作，皆能集起一切佛法故。是名業大。

彼二住功德大者，善住淨心力等示現，依七種功德故：一、善住道功德，如經「是菩薩得此菩薩不動地，善住淨心力中，離一切煩惱集故。善住深心力中，常不離道故。善住大悲力中，不捨利益眾生故。善住大慈力中，救一切世間故」。又善住道功德，初二遠離障故，對治堅固故。次二不捨眾生故。二不忘功德。如經「善住陀羅尼力中，不忘法故」。三、成就口業功德，如經「善住辯才力中，智慧善巧分別一切佛法故」。

四、心自在成就功德，如經「善住神通力中，行無邊世界差別故」。五、願力成就功德，如經「善住願力中，不捨一切菩薩所行故」。六、修行成就功德，如經「善住波羅蜜力中，集一切佛法故」。七、與智功德，如經「善住如來加力中，一切種一切智智現前故」。是菩薩得如是智力示一切所作者，得無憎愛，不分別眾生有煩惱、無煩惱，平等作業故。一切事中無有過咎者，以得此七種功德故。如是八地大勝分已說。次說釋名分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, having attained these ten kinds of mastery belonging to the bodhisattva, is at once named "one of inconceivable wisdom," "one of immeasurable wisdom," "one of vast wisdom," and "one of indestructible wisdom." The bodhisattva, having arrived in this way, this wisdom now accomplished, constantly gathers and gives rise to pure bodily action, constantly gathers and gives rise to pure verbal action, and constantly gathers and gives rise to pure mental action — wisdom foremost, this wisdom according with and turning the perfection of wisdom to ever greater excellence; great compassion foremost, skillful and expedient, well able to discern. Of himself he gives rise to the power of vow, well empowered by the empowerment of the buddhas, never abandoning the practice of benefiting sentient beings, and thoroughly knowing the distinct affairs of boundless world-systems. Son of the Buddha! In brief, a bodhisattva who attains this bodhisattva's Acalā, the Immovable Ground, in whatever he performs through bodily, verbal, and mental action, is able to gather and give rise to every buddha-dharma.

This bodhisattva, having attained this bodhisattva's Acalā, the Immovable Ground, well abides in the power of a pure mind, since he is departed from the accumulation of every affliction. He well abides in the power of a deep mind, since he is never apart from the path. He well abides in the power of great compassion, since he does not abandon the practice of benefiting sentient beings. He well abides in the power of great loving-kindness, since he rescues every world. He well abides in the power of dhāraṇī, since he does not forget the Dharma. He well abides in the power of eloquence, since his wisdom skillfully and expediently discerns every buddha-dharma. He well abides in the power of spiritual penetration, since he practices throughout the distinctions of boundless world-systems. He well abides in the power of vow, since he does not abandon whatever every bodhisattva practices. He well abides in the power of the perfections, since he cultivates and gathers every buddha-dharma. He well abides in the power of the tathāgatas' own empowerment, since the wisdom of all modes, the wisdom of omniscience, stands directly present. This bodhisattva, having attained wisdom-power such as this, manifests it in whatever he performs, since in every matter there is nothing amiss.

Commentary: "Great excellence" has three kinds of greatness: first, the greatness of wisdom; second, the greatness of karmic activity; and third, the greatness of the merit on which both of these depend. What, then, is the greatness of wisdom? "One of inconceivable wisdom" means that he abides neither in the world nor in nirvāṇa — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, having attained these ten kinds of mastery belonging to the bodhisattva, is at once named 'one of inconceivable wisdom.'" This "inconceivable" should be understood as threefold: first, the inconceivability of the ultimate arrival of his cultivated practice; second, the inconceivability of what is known; and third, the inconceivability of the wisdom that removes every hindrance — as the sūtra says, "'one of immeasurable wisdom,' 'one of vast wisdom,' 'one of indestructible wisdom.'" This is called the greatness of wisdom.

"The bodhisattva, having arrived in this way," all the way to "gathers and gives rise to every buddha-dharma" — "the bodhisattva, having arrived in this way" is as explained above, and "this wisdom now accomplished" is likewise as explained above. "Constantly gathering and giving rise to the three pure actions" — this is the greatness of karmic activity. This pure karmic activity has four aspects. First, the arising itself, that which is able to arouse it, and its arising, all being simultaneous — as the sūtra says, "wisdom foremost" and "this wisdom according with and turning." Second, wisdom taking up, unstained, the performance of the practice of benefiting sentient beings and the rest — as the sūtra says, "the perfection of wisdom to ever greater excellence; great compassion foremost, skillful and expedient, well able to discern." Third, the cause that takes up his own practice and the cause of others' practice — as the sūtra says, "of himself he gives rise to the power of vow, well empowered by the empowerment of the buddhas." Fourth, the karmic activity performed that benefits sentient beings, purifies buddha-lands, and accomplishes every buddha-dharma — as the sūtra says, "never abandoning the practice of benefiting sentient beings, and thoroughly knowing the distinct affairs of boundless world-systems." "Son of the Buddha! In brief, a bodhisattva who attains this bodhisattva's Acalā, the Immovable Ground, in whatever he performs through bodily, verbal, and mental action, is able to gather and give rise to every buddha-dharma." This is called the greatness of karmic activity.

"The greatness of the merit on which both of these depend" is manifested through "well abiding in the power of a pure mind" and the rest, in dependence on seven kinds of merit. First, the merit of well abiding in the path — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, having attained this bodhisattva's Acalā, the Immovable Ground, well abides in the power of a pure mind, since he is departed from the accumulation of every affliction; well abides in the power of a deep mind, since he is never apart from the path; well abides in the power of great compassion, since he does not abandon the practice of benefiting sentient beings; well abides in the power of great loving-kindness, since he rescues every world." Further, regarding the merit of well abiding in the path: the first two are far removed from hindrance, since the antidote is firm; the next two do not abandon sentient beings. Second, the merit of not forgetting — as the sūtra says, "well abides in the power of dhāraṇī, since he does not forget the Dharma." Third, the merit of accomplishing verbal action — as the sūtra says, "well abides in the power of eloquence, since his wisdom skillfully and expediently discerns every buddha-dharma."

Fourth, the merit of accomplishing mastery of mind — as the sūtra says, "well abides in the power of spiritual penetration, since he practices throughout the distinctions of boundless world-systems." Fifth, the merit of accomplishing the power of vow — as the sūtra says, "well abides in the power of vow, since he does not abandon whatever every bodhisattva practices." Sixth, the merit of accomplishing cultivated practice — as the sūtra says, "well abides in the power of the perfections, since he gathers every buddha-dharma." Seventh, the merit of bestowing wisdom — as the sūtra says, "well abides in the power of the tathāgatas' own empowerment, since the wisdom of all modes, the wisdom of omniscience, stands directly present." "This bodhisattva, having attained wisdom-power such as this, manifests it in whatever he performs" means that he attains a mind free of hatred and attachment, not discriminating whether a sentient being has affliction or is without affliction, performing his karmic activity equally. "In every matter there is nothing amiss" is because he has attained these seven kinds of merit. In this way, the Eighth Ground's division of great excellence has now been explained. Next, the division explaining the name will be explained.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang's own analysis of the greatness of karmic activity identifies a subtlety the treatise states but does not linger on: the arising of pure karmic action, the wisdom that arouses it, and that arising itself are said to occur together, not one after another — 起能起起同時，謂前三業是起，智慧起彼是名能起，彼業起竟共慧相隨名曰同時 — "the arising, that which is able to arouse the arising, and the arising itself are simultaneous: the three kinds of karmic action just named are 'the arising'; the wisdom that arouses them is called 'that which is able to arouse'; and, once that karmic activity has arisen, its constant accompaniment by wisdom is called 'simultaneous.'" Karmic action here is never a wisdom-informed deed happening after the fact of wisdom — the two occur as a single event.

On the sevenfold merit passage, Fazang further sorts its first four clauses — pure mind, deep mind, great compassion, great loving-kindness — into a self-and-other pairing the sūtra's own wording leaves implicit: 前中四句：初二自行...後二利他，悲慈不捨救也 — "of these first four clauses, the first two are self-directed practice ... the latter two are practice benefiting others: compassion and loving-kindness, not abandoning beings, rescuing them." Purity of mind is the ground the bodhisattva stands on; compassion and loving-kindness are what he does once standing there.

Explaining the Ground's Name: Ten Titles, from Acalā to No Effortful Application

Chinese Source

經曰：佛子！此菩薩智地名為不動地，不可壞故。名為不轉地，智慧不退故。名為難得地，一切世間難知故。名為王子地，無家過故。名為生地，隨意自在故。名為成地，更不作故。名為究竟地，智慧善分別故。名為涅槃地，善起大願力故。名為加地，他不能動故。名為無功用地，善起先道故。

論曰：釋名有二種：一、地釋名；二、智者釋名。地釋名者，有六種相：一、染對治，此染有二種：一、下地功用行小乘願諸魔業；二、煩惱習行此對治。如經「佛子！此菩薩智地名為不動地，不可壞故。名為不轉地，智慧不退故」。二、得甚深，如經「名為難得地，一切世間難知故」。

三、發行清淨，如經「名為王子地，無家過故。名為生地，隨意自在故」。是中發淨者，如王子，一切所作無過故。行淨者，住生地，所欲事自在成就故。

四、世間、出世間有作淨勝，如經「名為成地，更不作故。名為究竟地，智慧善分別故」。是中出世間有作淨勝者，以智慧善分別智障淨故。五、彼二無作淨勝，如經「名為涅槃地，善起大願力故」。無作淨勝者，以本願力，不捨利益一切眾生故。六、菩薩地勝，如經「名為加地，他不能動故。名為無功用地，善起先道故」。又菩薩地勝者，六地七地勝故。六地勝者，發起殊勝行，他事念動故。七地有功用，此地中善起先道無功用自然行故。

智者釋名者，以何義故菩薩名為得不動菩薩？今說此事應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Son of the Buddha! This bodhisattva's wisdom-ground is named Acalā, the Immovable Ground, since it cannot be destroyed. It is named the Unturning Ground, since his wisdom does not regress. It is named the Ground Hard to Attain, since the whole world finds it hard to know. It is named the Prince's Ground, since it is without the fault of a household. It is named the Ground of Birth, since he is self-mastering, as he wishes. It is named the Ground of Accomplishment, since there is nothing further to be done. It is named the Ultimate Ground, since his wisdom skillfully discerns. It is named the Nirvāṇa Ground, since he well gives rise to the power of his great vow. It is named the Ground of Empowerment, since no other is able to move it. It is named the Ground of No Effortful Application, since he well gives rise to the path that came before.

Commentary: The explanation of the name is of two kinds: first, explaining the name of the ground; second, explaining the name of the wise one. As for explaining the name of the ground, it has six distinct aspects. First, the antidote to defilement — this defilement is of two kinds: first, the effortful application of the lower grounds, the vow-work of the two vehicles, and the karmic activity of every māra; second, the habituated conduct of affliction — this is what is counteracted — as the sūtra says, "Son of the Buddha! This bodhisattva's wisdom-ground is named Acalā, the Immovable Ground, since it cannot be destroyed. It is named the Unturning Ground, since his wisdom does not regress." Second, attaining profundity — as the sūtra says, "it is named the Ground Hard to Attain, since the whole world finds it hard to know."

Third, the purity of arousing practice — as the sūtra says, "it is named the Prince's Ground, since it is without the fault of a household. It is named the Ground of Birth, since he is self-mastering, as he wishes." Here, "the purity of arousing" means that, like a prince, there is nothing amiss in whatever he does; "the purity of practice" means that, abiding in the Ground of Birth, whatever he wishes accomplishes itself, self-mastering.

Fourth, the mundane and supramundane excellence of purity-through-action — as the sūtra says, "it is named the Ground of Accomplishment, since there is nothing further to be done. It is named the Ultimate Ground, since his wisdom skillfully discerns." Here, the supramundane excellence of purity-through-action means that, through wisdom skillfully discerning, the hindrance to wisdom is purified. Fifth, the excellence of purity without action, belonging to both of these — as the sūtra says, "it is named the Nirvāṇa Ground, since he well gives rise to the power of his great vow." "The excellence of purity without action" means that, through the power of his original vow, he does not abandon the benefiting of every sentient being. Sixth, the excellence of the bodhisattva-ground — as the sūtra says, "it is named the Ground of Empowerment, since no other is able to move it. It is named the Ground of No Effortful Application, since he well gives rise to the path that came before." Further, "the excellence of the bodhisattva-ground" means that it surpasses the Sixth and the Seventh Grounds. As for surpassing the Sixth Ground: that ground arouses surpassing practice, yet his mindfulness there is still stirred by the affairs of another. The Seventh Ground has effortful application; within this ground, he well gives rise to the path that came before, without effortful application, of itself.

As for explaining the name of the wise one: for what reason is a bodhisattva named "one who has attained the immovable bodhisattva-ground"? This matter will now be explained, and should be understood.

Reading note — after Fazang

On why the Prince's Ground is named for freedom "from the fault of a household," Fazang reaches for the image of a boy who has never had to labor for what he already owns: 如王子不憂居家農務之過。菩薩亦爾，生在此家無功用之失...此中如童子無欲，譬純無流，無惑染之過 — "like a prince, who need not worry over the toil of tending his own household's farmwork; the bodhisattva is just the same, born into this [buddha-]family, free from the fault of effortful application ... here, like a boy free of desire, this stands as a metaphor for what is utterly undefiled, free of the fault of affliction's own taint." The prince does not labor to earn his birthright; the bodhisattva, likewise, no longer labors to sustain his own purity.

On why this ground surpasses the Sixth specifically, Fazang locates the difference in what still moves the earlier ground's own contemplation of emptiness: 六地觀空以有為他動其空觀，今則不爾故勝也 — "at the Sixth Ground, his contemplation of emptiness is still stirred, through encountering existence, as though by something else disturbing that very contemplation; now, at this ground, this is no longer so — hence it surpasses the Sixth." What the Sixth Ground reaches through contemplation, and can still be jostled by the world in reaching, the Eighth Ground no longer needs to reach for at all.

Part 39 Complete

This closes Part 39, within the Eighth Ground, Acalā, the Immovable Ground: the treatise's division of attaining mastery, in which this bodhisattva's well-knowing how to give rise to bodies of every kind culminates in mastery over his own life-span and nine further kinds of self-mastery, each the antidote to one of ten named fears; the division of great excellence, a threefold greatness of wisdom — earning this bodhisattva four named titles — of karmic activity, whose three pure actions and the wisdom that arouses them are said to arise as a single, simultaneous event, and of a sevenfold merit supporting both; and the opening of the division explaining this ground's own name, tracing all ten of its titles, from Acalā, the Immovable, and the Unturning Ground, through the Prince's Ground and the Ground of Birth, to the Ground of Empowerment and the Ground of No Effortful Application, back to six distinct aspects of a single ground now entirely free of fault. Part 40 turns to the treatise's own further explanation of why not only this ground, but the bodhisattva himself, is named "immovable" and "unturning," a further account of his attainment of entry into buddha-nature, and the closing threefold fruit of the entire Eighth Ground.
