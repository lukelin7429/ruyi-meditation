The Fifth Ground Complete

This bodhisattva teaches and transforms sentient beings through the four means of attraction — giving, kind speech, beneficial action, and cooperative work — and masters, for the sake of benefiting them alone, every manner of worldly treatise and craft: writing, seals, arithmetic, the properties of stones and gems, the sun, moon, and stars, and the medicine that cures every disease and poison. Closing the entire Fifth Ground, its fruit shows a bodhisattva who sees countless buddhas and becomes a master of the Dharma endowed with a dhāraṇī of ever more supreme, vast learning, and the treatise itself explains why this ground is named Sudurjayā, the Ground Difficult to Conquer, through the simile of refined gold and, new to this ground, the light of the sun, moon, and stars that no wind can move — before the treatise turns, at last, to Abhimukhī, the Ground of Direct Presence.

Teaching Through the Four Means of Attraction, and Mastery of Every Worldly Craft

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩成就如是行時，以布施教化眾生，又以愛語、利益、同事教化眾生，又以色身示現教化眾生，又以說法教化眾生，又廣示菩薩行神通事教化眾生，又說諸佛大事教化眾生，又示世間過惡教化眾生，又說諸佛智慧利益教化眾生，又現大神通莊嚴相亦說種種行教化眾生。是菩薩如是教化眾生方便成就，身心常趣佛智而不退失，諸善根行常勤修行轉勝道故。

論曰：是中教化眾生勝者，以四攝法教化，如經「是菩薩成就如是行時，以布施教化眾生，又以愛語、利益、同事教化眾生」故。為同事隨順眾生應化自眾，如經「又以色身示現教化眾生」故。為疑惑眾生可說法成就，如經「又以說法教化眾生」故。為於菩提無方便眾生，如經「又廣示菩薩行神通事教化眾生」故。為於大乘疲惓眾生，如經「又說諸佛大事教化眾生」故。為樂世間眾生，如經「又示世間過惡教化眾生」故。為不信大乘眾生，如經「又說諸佛智慧利益教化眾生」故。為無智眾生現神通莊嚴，示種種行，以一身示無量身，如是等令生決定信，如經「又現大神通莊嚴相，亦說種種行教化眾生」故。是菩薩如是教化眾生方便成就者，如前說。身心常趣佛智者，為教化眾生求勝力故。而不退失諸善根行者，隨所得功德智慧皆不退失故。常勤修行轉勝道者，彼所修諸行欲令增勝故。是名教化眾生勝。云何起隨順世間智勝染障對治？

經曰：是菩薩為利益眾生故，善知世間所有書論、印、算數、石性等論、治諸病方，所謂治乾枯病、治顛狂病、治鬼著病、治蠱毒病等，損害眾生者皆悉能治，謂呪藥等。作論經書伎樂戲笑歡娛等事，國土城邑、聚落室宅、河泉池流、園觀花果、藥草林樹等，金銀琉璃、摩尼真珠、珊瑚琥珀、車璩馬瑙諸寶性等，日月星宿地動夢想吉凶入等遍身諸相，持戒行處、禪定神通、四無量心、四無色定，凡諸不惱眾生事、能利益安樂眾生事，憐愍眾生故出，漸令信入無上佛法故。

論曰：隨順世間智者，染障對治，如經「是菩薩為利益眾生故，善知世間所有一切書論」等。是中書等有四種障對治。四種障者：一、所用事中忘障，取與寄付聞法思義，作不作事、已作未作事、應作不應作事，此對治故書。二、邪見軟智障，以因論、聲論對治此二故論。三、所取物不守護障，此對治故印。四、取與生疑障，此對治故算數。數者，一一為二、二二為四如是等。算者，一縱十橫如是等。石性等論者，貧事障對治故。治諸病方者，四大不調眾生毒相，病障對治故。乾枯顛狂病者，四大不調相故。鬼著病等，是眾生相。蠱毒病者，亦四大不調，亦眾生相病因及死因，此對治謂呪藥等。日月星宿地動夢想吉凶入等遍身諸相者，是中唯有日月等相見故，日等曜等攝故。入者，入八業果故。遍身諸相者，愛不愛果行故。持戒行處等者，是中持戒行處、禪定神通、四無量心、四無色定等，如是次第破戒染、貪欲染、邪歸依染、妄行功德染、妄修解脫染，如是次第說。如經「石性等論乃至呪藥等」故。作論經書伎樂戲笑歡娛等者，憂惱障對治故。國土城邑乃至藥草林樹等者，此不喜樂障對治故。金銀琉璃乃至諸寶性等者，繫閉等障，此對治故。日月星宿乃至遍身諸相等者，所得報分過作惡因障，此對治故。持戒行處乃至無色定者，五種染對治。何者五種染？破戒染乃至妄修解脫染故。此起世間智，具四種相：一、異障中無障故，如經「凡諸不惱眾生事」故。二、與無過樂，如經「能利益安樂眾生事」故。三、發起清淨，如經「憐愍眾生故出」。四、所用清淨，如經「漸令信入無上佛法故」。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, having accomplished practice in this way, teaches and transforms sentient beings through giving; he also teaches and transforms sentient beings through kind speech, beneficial action, and cooperative work; he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by manifesting his own physical body; he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by expounding the Dharma; he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by widely displaying the spiritual matters of the bodhisattva's own practice; he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by proclaiming the great matters of the buddhas; he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by showing forth the faults and evils of the world; he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by proclaiming the benefit of the buddhas' own wisdom; and he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by manifesting the adorned marks of great spiritual power and likewise expounding all manner of practice. This bodhisattva, having thus accomplished the skillful means of teaching and transforming sentient beings, his body and mind constantly advance toward the wisdom of the buddhas without regression or loss, and the practice of every root of goodness is constantly and diligently cultivated, turning ever more toward the supreme path.

Commentary: Here, the excellence of teaching and transforming sentient beings means teaching through the four means of attraction — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, having accomplished practice in this way, teaches and transforms sentient beings through giving; he also teaches and transforms sentient beings through kind speech, beneficial action, and cooperative work." For the sake of cooperative work, according with sentient beings, he responds by transforming them as his own following — as the sūtra says, "he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by manifesting his own physical body." For sentient beings caught in doubt and confusion, the Dharma can be expounded to bring them to accomplishment — as the sūtra says, "he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by expounding the Dharma." For sentient beings who lack skillful means toward enlightenment — as the sūtra says, "he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by widely displaying the spiritual matters of the bodhisattva's own practice." For sentient beings grown weary of the Great Vehicle — as the sūtra says, "he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by proclaiming the great matters of the buddhas." For sentient beings who delight in the world — as the sūtra says, "he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by showing forth the faults and evils of the world." For sentient beings who do not believe in the Great Vehicle — as the sūtra says, "he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by proclaiming the benefit of the buddhas' own wisdom." For sentient beings without wisdom, he manifests spiritual power and adornment, displays all manner of practice, and with a single body shows boundless bodies, causing them, in this way, to give rise to resolute faith — as the sūtra says, "he also teaches and transforms sentient beings by manifesting the adorned marks of great spiritual power and likewise expounding all manner of practice." "This bodhisattva, having thus accomplished the skillful means of teaching and transforming sentient beings" is as already explained before. "His body and mind constantly advance toward the wisdom of the buddhas" means that, for the sake of teaching and transforming sentient beings, he seeks this supreme power. "Without regression or loss of the practice of every root of goodness" means that whatever merit and wisdom he attains is never lost through regression. "Constantly and diligently cultivated, turning ever more toward the supreme path" means that he wishes every practice he cultivates to increase and excel. This is called the excellence of teaching and transforming sentient beings. What, then, is the arising of a worldly wisdom, according with the world, that excels in the antidote to the hindrance of defilement?

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, for the benefit of sentient beings, well masters every worldly writing and treatise, seals, arithmetic and computation, treatises on the nature of stones and the like, and every medical formula for curing disease — namely, curing the disease of wasting and desiccation, curing the disease of madness, curing the disease of possession by ghosts, curing the disease of venomous curses, and the rest — whatever harms sentient beings, he is entirely able to cure, through spells, medicines, and the like. He masters the composing of treatises, scriptures, and writings, music, jest and laughter, amusement and entertainment, and the like; lands and cities, villages and dwellings, rivers, springs, ponds and streams, gardens, groves, flowers and fruit, medicinal herbs and forest trees, and the like; the properties of gold, silver, lapis lazuli, wish-fulfilling pearls, coral, amber, tridacna-shell, and agate, and every kind of jewel; the sun, the moon, the constellations, earthquakes, dreams, and every auspicious and inauspicious sign entering the body's every mark; the practice-ground of upholding the precepts, meditative concentration and spiritual power, the four immeasurable minds, and the four formless absorptions; every matter that does not trouble sentient beings, and every matter able to benefit sentient beings and bring them ease and joy. He brings all this forth out of compassion for sentient beings, gradually causing them to gain faith and enter the unsurpassed buddha-dharma.

Commentary: "A worldly wisdom according with the world" means the antidote to the hindrance of defilement — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, for the benefit of sentient beings, well masters every worldly writing and treatise," and the rest. Here, writing and the rest serve as the antidote to four kinds of hindrance. The four kinds of hindrance are these: first, the hindrance of forgetting amid the matters one puts to use — taking and giving, entrusting and dispatching, hearing the Dharma and reflecting on its meaning, matters done and undone, matters already done and not yet done, matters that should be done and should not be done — the antidote to this is writing. Second, the hindrance of a false view and a feeble wisdom — through the treatise on causes and the treatise on sound, this twofold hindrance is countered — the antidote to this is treatises. Third, the hindrance of failing to safeguard what one has received — the antidote to this is the seal. Fourth, the hindrance of doubt arising in taking and giving — the antidote to this is arithmetic and computation. "Number" means one and one make two, two and two make four, and so forth; "computation" means one placed vertically and ten horizontally, and so forth. "Treatises on the nature of stones and the like" serve as the antidote to the hindrance of poverty. "Medical formulas for curing every disease" serve as the antidote to the hindrance of disease, the poisonous marks of sentient beings whose four elements are out of balance. "The disease of wasting and desiccation" and "the disease of madness" are marks of the four elements out of balance; "the disease of possession by ghosts" and the like are marks belonging to other sentient beings; "the disease of venomous curses" is likewise a matter of the four elements out of balance and likewise a mark belonging to other sentient beings, both the cause of disease and the cause of death — the antidote to this is spells, medicines, and the like. As for "the sun, the moon, the constellations, earthquakes, dreams, and every auspicious and inauspicious sign entering the body's every mark" — here there are only the marks seen in the sun and the like, since the sun and the rest are subsumed under the luminaries. "Entering" means entering the fruit of the eight karmic actions. "Every mark entering the body" means the conduct of desirable and undesirable fruit. As for "the practice-ground of upholding the precepts" and the rest — here, the practice-ground of upholding the precepts, meditative concentration and spiritual power, the four immeasurable minds, and the four formless absorptions, in this same order, counter the defilement of breaking the precepts, the defilement of greed and desire, the defilement of taking wrong refuge, the defilement of falsely practicing for merit, and the defilement of falsely cultivating liberation — explained in just this order, as the sūtra says, "treatises on the nature of stones and the like... spells, medicines, and the like." "Composing treatises, scriptures, and writings, music, jest and laughter, amusement and entertainment, and the like" serves as the antidote to the hindrance of worry and vexation. "Lands and cities... medicinal herbs and forest trees, and the like" serves as the antidote to the hindrance of finding no joy. "Gold, silver, lapis lazuli... every kind of jewel" counters the hindrance of being bound and confined, and the like. "The sun, the moon... every mark entering the body" counters the hindrance of the fault attached to the portion of retribution one has obtained, the cause of committing evil. "The practice-ground of upholding the precepts... the formless absorptions" are the antidote to five kinds of defilement. What are these five kinds of defilement? The defilement of breaking the precepts, and so forth, up to the defilement of falsely cultivating liberation. This arising of worldly wisdom is complete with four marks: first, being free of hindrance amid every other hindrance — as the sūtra says, "every matter that does not trouble sentient beings"; second, granting a joy free of fault — as the sūtra says, "every matter able to benefit sentient beings and bring them ease and joy"; third, the purity of what gives rise to it — as the sūtra says, "he brings this forth out of compassion for sentient beings"; fourth, the purity of what it is put to use for — as the sūtra says, "gradually causing them to gain faith and enter the unsurpassed buddha-dharma."

Reading note — after Fazang

On the mastery of worldly crafts, Fazang is precise about what problem each skill solves rather than treating the list as ornamental erudition: 金石等者，以工巧論能治貧也 — "gold, minerals, and the like: through the craft-treatise, one is able to cure poverty." Medicine, in particular, he reads as resting on a threefold diagnosis rather than one undifferentiated notion of illness: 論病有三種，謂乾消等內四大不調相，鬼著等是外眾生相，蠱毒等通內外 — "the treatise holds that disease is of three kinds: wasting and desiccation and the like are marks of the internal four elements out of balance; possession by ghosts and the like are marks belonging to other, external beings; and venomous curses belong to both, internal and external." A bodhisattva's medical mastery, on this reading, is not a generic compassion toward the sick but a discriminating clinical grasp of three distinct causes.

Fazang also draws the whole enumeration back together under a single classical rubric before moving on: 以此五門即五明所攝，能除闇障，俱能生智 — "these five approaches are exactly what is gathered under the five sciences [the five vidyāsthāna]; all are able to dispel the darkness of hindrance, and together they are able to give rise to wisdom." What reads, in the sūtra's own list, as a miscellany of secular skills — writing, healing, arithmetic, craft, and the rest — is, for Fazang, already the classical Buddhist taxonomy of the five sciences: each one at once a worldly competence and a wisdom-generating discipline in its own right.

Seeing Countless Buddhas, a Dhāraṇī of Vast Learning, and Gold, Sun, Moon, and Stars Explaining "Difficult to Conquer"

Chinese Source

經曰：諸佛子！是菩薩住此菩薩難勝地已，多見諸佛，以大神通力、大願力故。見多百佛、多千佛、多百千佛、多百千那由他佛、多億佛、多百億佛、多千億佛、多百千億佛、多百千萬億那由他佛，以大神通力、大願力故。是菩薩見諸佛時，以上心深心供養恭敬尊重讚歎，衣服飲食臥具湯藥一切供具悉以奉施，以諸菩薩上妙樂具供養眾僧，以此善根皆願迴向阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。於諸佛所生上恭敬專心聽法，聞已受持隨力修行，於佛法中出家，得出家已於諸佛所聽受經法而為法師，說法利益，得轉勝多聞陀羅尼成就法師。

是菩薩爾時住此菩薩難勝地中，無量百劫彼諸善根轉勝明淨，無量千劫、無量百千劫、無量百千那由他劫無量億劫、無量百億劫、無量千億劫、無量百千億劫、無量百千萬億那由他劫，彼諸善根轉勝明淨，是菩薩成就如是教化眾生法。諸佛子！譬如本真金，以車璩摩瑩，光色轉勝明淨。諸佛子！菩薩住此菩薩難勝地中，彼諸善根以方便智思量力故轉勝明淨，彼智慧善根成就不退思量轉勝，下地善根所不能及。

諸佛子！譬如日月星宿諸天宮殿光輪圓滿成就不壞，風不能動。如是諸佛子！菩薩住此菩薩難勝地中，彼諸善根以方便智思量力故成就不退，一切聲聞辟支佛世間善根所不能及。是菩薩十波羅蜜中禪波羅蜜增上，餘波羅蜜非不修習，隨力隨分。諸佛子！是名略說菩薩第五菩薩難勝地。

若菩薩住此地中，多作兜率陀天王，所作自在摧伏一切外道邪見，能令眾生住實諦中。所作善業布施愛語利益同事，是諸福德皆不離念佛、念法、念僧、念菩薩、念菩薩行、念波羅蜜、念十地、念不壞力、念無畏、念不共佛法，乃至不離念一切種一切智智。常生是心：我當於一切眾生中，為首為勝為大、為妙為微妙、為上為無上、為導為將、為師為尊，乃至為一切智智依止者。

復從是念發精進行，以精進力故，於一念間得千億三昧，見千億佛、知千億佛神力、能動千億佛世界、能入千億佛世界、能照千億佛世界、能化千億佛世界眾生、能住壽千億劫、能知過去未來世各千億劫事、能善入千億法門、能變身為千億，於一一身能示千億菩薩以為眷屬。若以願力自在勝上，菩薩願力過於此數。示種種神通，或身、或光明、或神通、或眼、或境界、或音聲、或行、或莊嚴、或加、或信、或業。是諸神通，乃至無量百千萬億那由他劫不可數知。

論曰：得轉勝多聞陀羅尼成就法師者，非得義陀羅尼，以平等清淨心甚難得故。又樂出世間智現世間智最難故，得聞持陀羅尼。此地智光明真如事示現，如經「諸佛子！譬如本真金乃至下地善根所不能及」故。日月光輪者，依阿含增長智慧光明，勝於前地智故，如經「諸佛子！譬如日月星宿乃至世間善根所不能及」故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Sons of the Buddha! This bodhisattva, having dwelt in this bodhisattva's Sudurjayā, the Ground Difficult to Conquer, sees many buddhas. Through great spiritual power and the power of his great vows, he sees hundreds of buddhas, thousands of buddhas, hundreds of thousands of buddhas, hundreds of thousands of niyutas of buddhas, koṭis of buddhas, hundreds of koṭis of buddhas, thousands of koṭis of buddhas, hundreds of thousands of koṭis of buddhas, and hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of niyutas of buddhas — through great spiritual power and the power of his great vows. When this bodhisattva sees these buddhas, with a supreme, deep mind he makes offerings to them, reveres, honors, and praises them, presenting robes, food and drink, bedding, and medicine, every requisite entirely as offerings; and, with the supreme and sublime objects of delight belonging to the bodhisattvas, he makes offerings to the entire monastic assembly, dedicating this root of goodness, wishing for unsurpassed, perfect, complete enlightenment. Toward the buddhas he gives rise to a supreme mind of reverence, single-mindedly listening to the Dharma; having heard it, he upholds it and, in accordance with his own strength, practices it; within the buddha-dharma he goes forth from the household life; and, having gone forth, he listens to and receives the sūtra-teachings from the buddhas themselves and becomes a master of the Dharma, expounding the Dharma for others' benefit, attaining a dhāraṇī of ever more supreme, vast learning, and becoming an accomplished master of the Dharma.

This bodhisattva, at that time dwelling within this bodhisattva's Sudurjayā, throughout boundless hundreds of eons, these roots of goodness of his become ever more excellent, radiant, and pure; throughout boundless thousands of eons, boundless hundreds of thousands of eons, boundless hundreds of thousands of niyutas of eons, boundless koṭis of eons, boundless hundreds of koṭis of eons, boundless thousands of koṭis of eons, boundless hundreds of thousands of koṭis of eons, and boundless hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of niyutas of eons, these roots of goodness of his become ever more excellent, radiant, and pure, and this bodhisattva accomplishes this Dharma of teaching and transforming sentient beings. Sons of the Buddha! It is like genuine gold that, polished with tridacna-shell, becomes ever more excellent in its radiant color, and pure. Sons of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, dwelling within this bodhisattva's Sudurjayā, these roots of goodness of his, through the power of reflection belonging to skillful, expedient wisdom, become ever more excellent, radiant, and pure; these roots of goodness of his wisdom are accomplished without regression, their reflection ever more excellent, and the roots of goodness of the lower grounds cannot equal them.

Sons of the Buddha! It is like the sun, the moon, the constellations, and the palaces of the heavenly beings, whose full and perfect halo of light is accomplished, indestructible, and cannot be moved by any wind. Just so, Sons of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, dwelling within this bodhisattva's Sudurjayā, these roots of goodness of his, through the power of reflection belonging to skillful, expedient wisdom, are accomplished without regression, and the worldly roots of goodness of every śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha cannot equal them. Among this bodhisattva's ten perfections, the perfection of meditative concentration now excels — the other perfections he does not fail to cultivate, but in accordance with his own strength and portion. Sons of the Buddha! This is what is briefly called a bodhisattva's Fifth Ground, the bodhisattva's Sudurjayā.

Should a bodhisattva dwell in this ground, he often becomes a king of Tuṣita Heaven, self-mastering in all that he does, subduing every heterodox teacher's false view, and causing sentient beings to abide in the truth of reality. The wholesome deeds he performs — giving, kind speech, beneficial action, and cooperative work — every one of these merits is never apart from mindfulness of the buddhas, mindfulness of the Dharma, mindfulness of the monastic assembly, mindfulness of every bodhisattva, mindfulness of the bodhisattva's own practice, mindfulness of every perfection, mindfulness of the ten grounds, mindfulness of unbreakable power, mindfulness of fearlessness, mindfulness of the unshared buddha-dharmas, all the way to never apart from mindfulness of being complete in the wisdom of all modes, the wisdom of omniscience. He constantly gives rise to this thought: among all sentient beings, I shall be foremost, I shall be supreme, I shall be great, I shall be sublime, I shall be exceedingly sublime, I shall be highest, I shall be unsurpassed, I shall be a guide, I shall be a leader, I shall be a teacher, I shall be one held in honor, all the way to being the very support of the wisdom of omniscience.

Further, from this thought he sets forth diligent exertion. Through the power of this diligence, within a single thought-moment he attains a thousand koṭis of samādhis, sees a thousand koṭis of buddhas, knows the spiritual power of a thousand koṭis of buddhas, is able to shake a thousand koṭis of buddha-worlds, is able to enter a thousand koṭis of buddha-worlds, is able to illumine a thousand koṭis of buddha-worlds, is able to transform the sentient beings of a thousand koṭis of buddha-worlds, is able to dwell in life for a thousand koṭis of eons, is able to know the affairs of a thousand koṭis of eons each in the past and the future, is able to enter skillfully into a thousand koṭis of Dharma-gates, and is able to transform his own body into a thousand koṭis of bodies, manifesting, within each and every one of those bodies, a thousand koṭis of bodhisattvas as its own retinue. Should he, through the power of vow, be self-mastering and supreme, a bodhisattva's power of vow manifests beyond even this number, manifesting all manner of spiritual power — whether of body, or of radiance, or of spiritual power itself, or of the eye, or of the object-realm, or of voice, or of activity, or of adornment, or of empowerment, or of faith, or of karmic function — every one of these spiritual powers, throughout boundless hundreds of thousands of myriad koṭis of niyutas of eons, beyond all counting or knowing.

Commentary: "Attaining a dhāraṇī of ever more supreme, vast learning and becoming an accomplished master of the Dharma" — this is not the attainment of the dhāraṇī of meaning, since a mind of equanimity and purity is exceedingly difficult to attain. And, since delighting in supramundane wisdom while manifesting worldly wisdom is most difficult of all, he attains this dhāraṇī of vast learning and retention. This ground's light of wisdom makes manifest the matter of suchness itself realized — as the sūtra says, "Sons of the Buddha! It is like genuine gold... the roots of goodness of the lower grounds cannot equal them." As for the halo of the sun and moon: in dependence on scriptural transmission, the light of wisdom grows and increases, surpassing the wisdom of the previous ground — as the sūtra says, "Sons of the Buddha! It is like the sun, the moon... the worldly roots of goodness cannot equal them."

Reading note — after Fazang

On the ground's closing dhāraṇī of vast learning, Fazang first insists on what it is not: 非得義陀羅尼，謂對勝彰劣也 — "this is not the attainment of the dhāraṇī of meaning; it is spoken of relative to something superior, so as to disclose what is, by comparison, lesser." Even the ground's own culminating attainment, he is careful to note, still stands beneath a higher dhāraṇī not yet reached. What is reached here is nonetheless doubly hard-won: 以能具二難，故得聞持：一地初平等心難得，二樂出世間現世間智難 — "because he is able to possess two things difficult to attain, he attains this retention of vast learning: first, the mind of equanimity at the very start of the ground, which is difficult to attain; second, delighting in supramundane wisdom while manifesting worldly wisdom, which is most difficult of all." On the new sun-moon-and-star simile explaining the ground's own name, Fazang is explicit that it is pitched, deliberately, above the wish-fulfilling jewel of the ground before it: 日月等，喻教智淨，依阿含增長智慧光明，勝於前地智故，謂勝前地珠光故也 — "the sun, moon, and the rest liken the purity of teaching and wisdom; in dependence on scriptural transmission, the light of wisdom grows and increases, surpassing the wisdom of the previous ground — that is, surpassing the previous ground's jewel-light."

Fazang's own condensed verse-summary of the entire Fifth Ground divides its closing "ground-fruit" (地果) into three parts by weight rather than by topic alone: 地果，初三調柔、次三半頌攝報、次半願智 — "as for the ground's fruit: the first three verses concern the tempering and taming of the mind; the next three and a half verses gather in the fruit of retribution; the final half-verse concerns vow and wisdom." His commentary on the entire Fifth Ground then closes in four characters, exactly as his commentary on the Fourth Ground had: 難勝地竟, "Sudurjayā, the Ground Difficult to Conquer, is complete."

Part 28 Complete — Sudurjayā, the Ground Difficult to Conquer, Now Entirely Translated

This closes Part 28, and with it the entire Fifth Ground: Sudurjayā, the Ground Difficult to Conquer. Parts 26 through 28 — the ten kinds of equal, pure mind, the bodhisattva's contemplation of the Four Noble Truths through the lens of deceptive, illusory appearance, his great compassion for suffering sentient beings, his mastery of the ten titles of supreme wisdom and an unremitting practice, his teaching and transformation of sentient beings through the four means of attraction, his mastery, for their benefit alone, of every worldly treatise and craft, and now the seeing of countless buddhas, a dhāraṇī of ever more supreme learning, and the gold-and-sunlight etymology of this ground's own name — have now translated the whole of fascicle seven of this twelve-fascicle treatise.
