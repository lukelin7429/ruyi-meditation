The Second Refusal, and the Assembly's Joint Request

Vimuktacandra praises the assembly's purity through a full ninefold framework, only for Vajragarbha to decline again — not because of this assembly, but out of compassion for others elsewhere who delight in lesser teachings. Vimuktacandra presses a third time with the simile of the initial syllable that underlies all writing, and finally the whole assembly speaks together in verse — and even this is still not enough.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 4 of 52 complete Source: Taishō T1522, 十地經論 (Vasubandhu, tr. Bodhiruci et al., Northern Wei) Reading notes after Jingying Huiyuan's 十地經論義記 (X0753)

Vimuktacandra's Second Request Explained, and Vajragarbha's Second Refusal

Chinese Source

論曰：聖者解脫月何故復歎此眾？上言世間證信者難得，示現此眾有堪能故。善淨深心者是總，此善淨深心有二種：一、阿含淨；二、證淨。是阿含淨有五種：一者欲淨，隨所念阿含得方便念覺淨，如經「善清淨諸念」故。二者求淨，得隨順身口敬行，如經「善集諸行」故。三者受持淨，於無量世多聞憶持不謬故，如經「多親近諸佛」故。四者生得淨，願得上上生勝念勝，如經「善集助道法」故。五者行淨，求善證法習少欲頭陀等成就多功德，如經「具足無量功德」故。證淨者有四種：一者得淨，現智善決定故，如經「離癡疑悔」故。二者不行淨，修道中一切煩惱不行故，如經「無有染污」故。三者無厭足淨，不樂小乘得上勝悕望心，如經「善住深心信」故。深心者，悕欲故。信者，決定故，復念持彼功德故。四者不隨他教淨，趣盡道中自正行故，如經「於佛法中不隨他教」故。

經曰：爾時金剛藏菩薩言：佛子！雖此菩薩眾善清淨深心、善清淨諸念、善集諸行、多親近諸佛、善集助道法、具足無量功德、離癡疑悔、無有染污。善住深心信，於佛法中不隨他教。其餘樂小法者，聞是甚深難思議事多生疑惑，是人長夜受諸無利衰惱。我愍此等，是故默然。

論曰：是聖者金剛藏領彼解脫月菩薩所歎眾清淨功德已，於所說法中不見法器，聞增疑惑，是故不說。於一法中有二種過：疑者正行相違猶豫義故；惑者心迷義故，能壞善法遠離善法故。如是顯示不受行因、受行退因。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: Why does the noble Vimuktacandra praise this assembly once again? Because it was just said that, for the world, realization and belief are hard to attain — and this makes manifest that this assembly is indeed capable of them. "Their deep minds are well purified" is the universal aspect; this well-purified deep mind is of two kinds: purity through scriptural transmission, and purity through realization. Purity through scriptural transmission is of five kinds. First, purity of aspiration: in accordance with whatever scriptural teaching is held in mind, one attains the purity of skillful mindfulness and awareness — as the sūtra says, "their every thought well purified." Second, purity of seeking: one attains reverent conduct of body and speech in accordance with the teaching — as the sūtra says, "their every practice well gathered." Third, purity of upholding: over immeasurable ages, having heard much, one holds it in mind without error — as the sūtra says, "having drawn near to many buddhas." Fourth, purity of favorable rebirth: vowing to attain an ever more excellent birth with excellent mindfulness — as the sūtra says, "having well gathered the dharmas that assist the path." Fifth, purity of practice: seeking to realize the good Dharma, cultivating fewness of desire, the ascetic practices, and the like, accomplishing manifold merit — as the sūtra says, "complete in boundless merit." Purity through realization is of four kinds. First, purity of attainment: present wisdom is well resolved — as the sūtra says, "free of delusion, doubt, and regret." Second, purity of non-activity: within the cultivated path, no affliction is active — as the sūtra says, "without any defilement." Third, purity of being insatiable: not delighting in the lesser vehicle, one holds a mind that aspires ever higher — as the sūtra says, "well abiding in the faith of a deep mind." "Deep mind" means aspiration; "faith" means resolve, and further, holding that merit in mind. Fourth, purity of not following another's teaching: advancing toward the ultimate path through one's own correct practice — as the sūtra says, "they do not follow another's teaching within the Buddha-dharma."

Sūtra: At that time, Vajragarbha Bodhisattva said: "Son of the Buddha! Although this assembly of bodhisattvas has deep minds well purified, every thought well purified, every practice well gathered, has drawn near to many buddhas, has well gathered the dharmas that assist the path, is complete in boundless merit, is free of delusion, doubt, and regret, and is without any defilement — well abiding in the faith of a deep mind, not following another's teaching within the Buddha-dharma — there are others still, who delight in the lesser Dharma; on hearing this profound and inconceivable matter, they would give rise to much doubt and confusion, and such people would, through the long night, undergo every manner of unprofitable affliction. It is out of compassion for them that I remain silent."

Commentary: Once the noble Vajragarbha had acknowledged the pure merits of the assembly that Vimuktacandra Bodhisattva had just praised, he still did not see, within the Dharma to be taught, a fit vessel — for hearing it would only increase doubt and confusion, and this is why he does not teach. Within any single dharma there are two kinds of fault: doubt, meaning vacillation contrary to correct practice; and confusion, meaning a bewildered mind, able to destroy wholesome dharmas and cause one to depart from them. This makes manifest, respectively, the cause of not receiving and practicing the teaching, and the cause of receiving it and then falling back.

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Huiyuan notes a deliberate change of method between Vimuktacandra's first and second requests: the first request praised the "assembly born alongside" Vajragarbha and the "assembly born otherwise" separately, in different verses, because Vajragarbha's initial silence seemed to single out one group as unfit to listen; but here, since Vajragarbha's own reply located the difficulty in the world's capacity generally rather than in either group specifically, Vimuktacandra now praises both groups together in a single, unified ninefold account: 前者懼金剛藏偏畏異生不堪聽受，故嘿不說，故須別嘆異生眾淨。上金剛藏酬答之中，但言世間證信難得，不言異生不堪聽受，故此總嘆 — "before, fearing that Vajragarbha's silence specifically doubted whether the differently-born assembly could listen, [Vimuktacandra] had to praise that assembly's purity separately; but in Vajragarbha's reply above, he said only that realization and belief are hard for the world to attain, without saying that the differently-born assembly specifically could not listen — and this is why the praise here is unified." The ninefold breakdown that follows — five kinds of purity through scriptural transmission (aspiration, seeking, upholding, favorable rebirth, practice) paired with four kinds of purity through realization (attainment, non-activity, insatiability, independence from others' teaching) — maps this same assembly's qualities onto the identical two-fold aḥama/realization structure the treatise has already used to analyze Vajragarbha's own qualifications to teach, treating teacher and audience as needing to satisfy parallel, not merely comparable, standards.

On Vajragarbha's second refusal, which risks reading as a contradiction — he has just been told, at length, how pure this very assembly is, and now declines again — Huiyuan is careful to show that the refusal targets someone else entirely: 其餘樂小，舉其人也…謂此會中小心眾生處處有故，亦可指彼餘處後時樂小眾生…今說地法，為益此眾，餘處有小，何勞簡乎？良以聖心廣被故爾 — "'others who delight in lesser teachings' names a different group of people… meaning either that beings of lesser inclination are scattered everywhere within this very assembly, or that this points to those elsewhere, in later times, who delight in lesser teachings… since the teaching of the grounds is meant to benefit this present assembly, why trouble to exclude those elsewhere at all? It is only because a sage's mind extends everywhere alike that this concern is even raised." Huiyuan then supplies the reason the exclusion is voiced anyway, given that Vajragarbha's compassion in fact excludes no one: 此為簡取，不欲簡去，欲使如來以力加被，又欲令彼勵心聽法，是故簡之 — "this naming is meant to draw something in, not to shut something out: it is meant to cause the tathāgatas to add their own empowering power, and further to cause the assembly to redouble its own resolve to listen — this is why the exclusion is named at all." The second refusal is not a retraction of the praise just given; it is a further piece of the same narrative engineering that has been driving the whole section of request, this time aimed at pulling in one more layer of empowerment before Vajragarbha will finally speak.

The Third Request: The Simile of the Initial Syllable

Chinese Source

經曰：爾時解脫月菩薩請金剛藏菩薩言：善哉佛子！重請此事，願承佛神力，善分別此不可思議法佛所護念事，令人易信解。所以者何？善說十地義，十方諸佛法應護念，一切菩薩護是智地勤行方便。何以故？此是菩薩最初所行成就一切諸佛法故。佛子！譬如一切書字數說皆初章所攝、初章為本，無有書字數說不入初章者。如是佛子！十地者是一切佛法之根本，菩薩具足行是十地能得一切智慧。是故佛子！願說此義，諸佛護念加以神力，令人信受不可破壞。

論曰：聖者解脫月何故復重請？示彼疑惑此不可避，若不說者有多過咎，不得成就一切佛法故，以是義故重請金剛藏菩薩。若諸佛有力能令生信，何故眾生於彼法中猶起謗意？有二種定：一、感報定；二、作業定。此二種定，諸佛威力所不能轉。最初所行者，依阿含行故。成就一切佛法者，謂是證智。書者，是字相，如嘶字師子形相等。字者，噁阿等音。數者，名句。此二是數義，說者是語。一切書、字、數、說等，皆初章為本。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: At that time, Vimuktacandra Bodhisattva requested of Vajragarbha Bodhisattva, saying: "Excellent, son of the Buddha! I request this matter once again: may you, relying on the Buddha's spiritual power, skillfully discriminate this inconceivable Dharma that the buddhas hold ever in mind, so that people may easily believe and understand it. And why is this? Because in well teaching the meaning of the ten grounds, the buddhas of the ten directions must surely hold it in mind, and every bodhisattva guards this ground of wisdom, diligently practicing its skillful means. Why is this? Because this is the very first practice of a bodhisattva, and accomplishes every buddha-dharma. Son of the Buddha! It is like how every act of writing, every letter, every numeral, every word is subsumed within the initial syllable, taking the initial syllable as its root — there is no act of writing, no letter, numeral, or word that does not enter into the initial syllable. Just so, son of the Buddha, the ten grounds are the very root of every buddha-dharma; a bodhisattva who fully practices these ten grounds is able to attain the wisdom of omniscience. Therefore, son of the Buddha, I beg you to teach this meaning — may the buddhas hold it in mind and add to it their spiritual power, so that people may believe and receive it, unbreakably."

Commentary: Why does the noble Vimuktacandra request yet again? To show that this doubt and confusion cannot be avoided, and that, were he not to teach, there would be many faults — for one would then fail to accomplish every buddha-dharma — and it is for this reason that he requests Vajragarbha once more. If the buddhas have the power to cause faith to arise, why is it that sentient beings still give rise to slanderous thoughts regarding this Dharma? There are two kinds of fixed karma: fixed karma determining what is experienced as retribution, and fixed karma determining the deed itself. Neither of these two kinds of fixed karma can be altered even by the majestic power of the buddhas. "The very first practice" means practice grounded in scriptural transmission. "Accomplishing every buddha-dharma" refers to the wisdom of realization. "Writing" refers to the shape of a letter, such as the lion-like form of the syllable "sa." "Letters" refers to sounds such as "a" and so on. "Numerals" refers to names and phrases; these two constitute the meaning of "numeral." "Word" is speech. Every act of writing, every letter, numeral, and word takes the initial syllable as its root.

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Huiyuan reads Vimuktacandra's three requests as escalating in a specific, deliberate order — first he alone speaks, then the whole assembly speaks together, then the buddhas themselves are drawn in — and treats this passage as the hinge where the second stage prepares for the third: 一人之感未若多人…一人之請顯法不勝，故次第二、大眾同請 — "the force of one person's plea is not as great as that of many… a request from a single person does not sufficiently display the Dharma's weight, and this is why a second stage, the assembly's joint request, follows." The initial-syllable simile that closes this passage is doing exactly this preparatory work: by comparing the ten grounds to the single syllable that every possible act of writing depends on, Vimuktacandra is not merely praising the teaching's importance in the abstract, but building the case for why an appeal from one bodhisattva, however senior, cannot on its own carry weight proportionate to what is being requested — only the combined voice of the full assembly, and beyond that the buddhas' own added power, will match the claim just made about how foundational this teaching is.

The Assembly's Joint Verse — Still Not Enough

Chinese Source

經曰：爾時諸菩薩眾，一時同聲以偈頌請金剛藏菩薩言：

「上妙無垢智，　堪無量義辯，
演說美妙言，　真實義相應。

念堅清淨慧，　為十力淨心，
無礙分別義，　說此十地法。

定戒深正意，　離我慢妄見，
此眾無疑心，　唯願聞善說。

如渴思冷水，　如飢思美食，
如病思良藥，　如眾蜂依蜜，
我等亦如是，　願聞甘露法。

善哉清淨智，　說勝地無垢，
具十力無礙，　盡說善逝道。」

論曰：初偈歎證力辯才成就。第二偈上句歎阿含力辯才成就，以證力阿含力故能有所說，是故讚歎。上者是總，又復上者顯證力辯才勝故。歎辯才有三種：一、真實智；二、體性；三者果。真實智者是無漏智，勝聲聞緣覺智等。偈言「妙無垢智」故。體性者成就無量義辯才，偈言「堪無量義辯」故。果者字義成就，復是滑利勝上字義成就，偈言「演說美妙言，真實義相應」故。第二偈上句歎阿含力，偈言：

「念堅清淨慧，　為十力淨心，
無礙分別義，　說此十地法。」

念堅者，受持顯說故，是菩薩於阿含中淨慧無疑故。如是歎證力阿含力已，次令聽者入證入阿含，是故請說云何入證？已入地者令得佛力故，未入地者令得入地故，偈言「為十力」故、「為淨心」故。云何為入阿含無礙分別義？令受持十地法故。如是歎說者成就證力阿含力已，次復歎聽眾堪受阿含及證力故，偈言：

「定戒深正意，　離我慢妄見，
是眾無疑心，　唯願聞善說。」

此偈中唯願者是總。唯願有二種：一、求阿含；二、求正證。有二種妄想不堪聞阿含：一、我；二、慢。以我慢故，於法法師不生恭敬。復有二種妄想不堪得證：一、見；二、疑。見者顛倒見故，疑者於不思議處不生信故。妄者謂妄想見中同使故。有二種對治堪聞阿含：一、定；二、戒。定者心調伏故，戒者善住威儀故。次有二種對治堪能得證：一、正見；二、正意。正見者善思義故，正意者得歡喜故。深者，細意善思惟故。復以諸喻顯示大眾求法轉深，偈言：

「如渴思冷水，　如飢思美食，
如病思良藥，　如眾蜂依蜜，
我等亦如是，　願聞甘露法。」

此四喻者喻四種義門，示現正受彼所說義。何等為四？一、受持；二、助力；三、遠離；四、安樂行。此義云何？如水不嚼隨得而飲，如是聞慧初聞即受隨聞受持，如食咀嚼身力助成。如是思慧嚼所聞法智力助成，如服良藥藥行除病。如是具聞思慧，隨順正義如法修行，遠離一切煩惱習患。如蜜眾蜂所依樂行住處；如是聞思修慧果聖所依處，現法愛味受樂行故。如是讚歎說者聽者請說已，次歎所說法利益咸皆共請。偈言：

「善哉清淨智，　說勝地無垢，
具十力無礙，　盡說善逝道。」

善哉者，所說法中善具足故。善哉有三種：一、所依；二、體性；三、果。所依者謂淨慧，體性者謂說諸地未曾說法，勝地者地挍量勝。無垢者，說不違義。違義說者有三種垢：一者倒說；二謗如來；三誑聞者。果者，謂具十力無障礙佛菩提故。如是請已猶故不說。何故不說？請不滿故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: At that time, the whole assembly of bodhisattvas, with one voice together, addressed Vajragarbha Bodhisattva in verse, saying:

"Sublime, undefiled wisdom, capable of boundless eloquence in meaning, speaking words both fine and wondrous, in accord with their true significance —

firm in mindfulness, of pure wisdom, with a mind purified for the ten powers, discriminating their meaning without obstruction — please speak this Dharma of the ten grounds.

Settled in meditation and precepts, deep and of right intent, free of conceit and false views — this assembly, free of doubt, longs only to hear it well taught.

As the thirsty long for cool water, as the hungry long for fine food, as the sick long for good medicine, as a swarm of bees depends upon honey — so too are we; we long to hear this Dharma of sweet nectar.

Excellent, pure wisdom — speak of the supreme, undefiled grounds; complete in the ten powers, without obstruction, expound in full the path of the Well-Gone One."

Commentary: The first verse praises his accomplishment in the eloquence of the power of realization. The upper lines of the second verse praise his accomplishment in the eloquence of the power of scriptural transmission; because he has both the power of realization and the power of scriptural transmission, he is able to teach — and it is for this that he is praised. "Sublime" is the universal aspect, and further, "sublime" makes manifest the superiority of the eloquence of the power of realization. Praising eloquence is of three kinds: true wisdom, its essential nature, and its fruit. True wisdom is undefiled wisdom, surpassing the wisdom of śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas — as the verse says, "sublime, undefiled wisdom." Its essential nature is the accomplishment of boundless eloquence in meaning — as the verse says, "capable of boundless eloquence in meaning." Its fruit is the accomplishment of letters and their meaning, further, the accomplishment of letters and meaning both fluent and supreme — as the verse says, "speaking words both fine and wondrous, in accord with their true significance." The upper lines of the second verse praise the power of scriptural transmission; the verse says:

"Firm in mindfulness, of pure wisdom, with a mind purified for the ten powers, discriminating their meaning without obstruction — please speak this Dharma of the ten grounds."

"Firm in mindfulness" means upholding and setting forth the teaching, since this bodhisattva has, within scriptural transmission, pure wisdom free of doubt. Having thus praised his accomplishment in both the power of realization and the power of scriptural transmission, the verse next causes the listeners to enter both realization and scriptural transmission, and this is why it requests him to speak. How does one enter realization? So that those who have already entered a ground may attain a buddha's power, and those who have not yet entered a ground may attain entry into a ground — as the verse says, "for the ten powers," and "purified mind." How does one enter scriptural transmission and discriminate its meaning without obstruction? By causing them to uphold the Dharma of the ten grounds. Having thus praised the teacher's accomplishment in both the power of realization and the power of scriptural transmission, the verse next praises the listening assembly for being capable of receiving both the power of scriptural transmission and the power of realization; the verse says:

"Settled in meditation and precepts, deep and of right intent, free of conceit and false views — this assembly, free of doubt, longs only to hear it well taught."

In this verse, "longs only" is the universal aspect. "Longs only" is of two kinds: seeking scriptural transmission, and seeking correct realization. There are two kinds of false thought that make one incapable of hearing scriptural transmission: self, and conceit; through self and conceit, one fails to give rise to reverence toward the Dharma and its teacher. There are, further, two kinds of false thought that make one incapable of attaining realization: wrong views, and doubt; views mean inverted views, and doubt means failing to give rise to faith regarding what is inconceivable. "False" means being of one and the same habituated tendency as false views. There are two kinds of antidote that make one capable of hearing scriptural transmission: meditative concentration, and precepts; concentration means the mind is tamed, and precepts mean one abides well in proper deportment. Next, there are two kinds of antidote that make one capable of attaining realization: right view, and right intent; right view means skillfully reflecting on the meaning, and right intent means attaining joy. "Deep" means reflecting well with a subtle mind. The verse further illustrates, through various similes, how the assembly's seeking of the Dharma grows ever deeper; the verse says:

"As the thirsty long for cool water, as the hungry long for fine food, as the sick long for good medicine, as a swarm of bees depends upon honey — so too are we; we long to hear this Dharma of sweet nectar."

These four similes illustrate four kinds of meaning, showing how one properly receives the meaning to be taught. What are the four? Upholding; assisting power; being freed from affliction; and the practice of ease. What does this mean? Just as water need not be chewed but may simply be drunk as it is obtained, so too, at the first hearing of the wisdom born of hearing, one receives it and upholds it in accordance with what is heard. Just as food is chewed and thereby assists and completes the body's strength, so too the wisdom born of reflection chews over what has been heard, and the power of wisdom assists and completes it. Just as taking good medicine, through the medicine's own action, removes illness, so too, being complete in the wisdom born of hearing and reflection, one cultivates in accordance with correct meaning, according to the Dharma, and is thereby freed from every habituated affliction. Just as honey is the place bees depend upon and delight in dwelling, so too the fruit of the wisdom born of hearing, reflecting, and cultivating is the place the noble ones depend upon, savoring the taste of the Dharma present before them and undertaking the practice of ease. Having thus praised both teacher and listeners and requested the teaching, the verse next praises the benefit of the Dharma to be taught, which all together they now request as one; the verse says:

"Excellent, pure wisdom — speak of the supreme, undefiled grounds; complete in the ten powers, without obstruction, expound in full the path of the Well-Gone One."

"Excellent" means that, within the Dharma to be taught, goodness is complete. "Excellent" is of three kinds: what it depends on, its essential nature, and its fruit. What it depends on is pure wisdom; its essential nature is the teaching of the grounds as a Dharma never before taught; "supreme grounds" means the grounds, compared and measured, are supreme. "Undefiled" means the teaching does not contradict the meaning. A teaching that contradicts the meaning has three kinds of defilement: first, an inverted teaching; second, slandering the Tathāgata; third, deceiving the listener. Its fruit is being complete in the ten powers, unobstructed, in a buddha's enlightenment. Even after such a request, he still does not teach. Why does he not teach? Because the request is not yet complete.

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Huiyuan reads the assembly's joint verse as a structural mirror of Vimuktacandra's very first request, now sung with one voice rather than spoken by a single representative: the opening verses again separate praise of the teacher's "power of realization" from praise of his "power of scriptural transmission," and the closing verses again turn to praising the listeners' own fitness — but where the first request needed two full verses to establish that fitness through the six turbidities and their antidotes, here it compresses to a single verse built on two paired obstacles (self and conceit blocking scriptural transmission; wrong view and doubt blocking realization) and their two paired remedies (concentration and precepts; right view and right intent). Even the four similes of thirst, hunger, sickness, and bees are not decorative repetition of Vimuktacandra's earlier bee-and-honey image but a distinct fourfold structure of their own — upholding, assisting power, freedom from affliction, and the practice of ease — each keyed to a specific stage of how the wisdom born of hearing, reflecting, and cultivating actually functions, water needing no chewing standing for the immediacy of first hearing, food needing chewing standing for reflection's active digestion of what was heard.

That even this combined appeal — teacher praised, assembly praised, the Dharma's own benefit praised, voiced by the entire assembly as one — still does not move Vajragarbha to speak is, on Huiyuan's account, exactly the point rather than an anticlimax: the section of request has been engineered from its very first line to require the buddhas' own request before the teaching can properly begin, and no purely human plea, however complete or however many voices join it, can substitute for that final stage. The single closing clause 請不滿故 — "because the request is not yet complete" — names a structural fact about how many parties still need to speak, not a judgment on the quality of what the assembly has just offered.

Part 4 Complete

This closes Part 4: Vimuktacandra's ninefold praise of the assembly's purity, Vajragarbha's second refusal out of compassion for those elsewhere who delight in lesser teachings, the third request built on the simile of the single syllable underlying all writing, and the whole assembly's own joint verse of appeal — still, by the treatise's own reckoning, an incomplete request. Part 5 turns to what completes it: Śākyamuni Buddha's light, radiating from between his brows across the ten directions, and the buddhas of every world-system responding with light of their own.
