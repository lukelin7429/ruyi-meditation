The Threefold Grouping, the Great Dharma Teacher, and the Four Unhindered Wisdoms Begin

This bodhisattva, dwelling within the Ninth Ground, brings the ground's long thicket-analysis to its close with a final thicket: sentient beings sorted, in five distinct ways, into the mark of the correctly settled group bound for liberation, the mark of the wrongly settled group cut off from it, and, apart from these two, the mark of the undetermined group — by spiritual lineage, by correct or mistaken view, by karmic destiny, by the distinction of outsider and śrāvaka, and by a bodhisattva's own distinct fall into envy, stinginess, and unrelenting evil conduct. The treatise then opens its third division, "accomplishing teaching": the bodhisattva, thoroughly knowing sentient beings' differing dispositions, becomes, in brief, a great Dharma teacher, dwelling within the great Dharma teacher's own profound and subtle meaning through twenty named aspects of teaching well — right timing, correct intention, immediacy, continuity, and the rest. Finally, the treatise turns to the four unhindered wisdoms of dharmas, meaning, language, and eloquence that give this Ninth Ground its very name, Sādhumatī, and begins their detailed exposition with the unhindered wisdom of dharmas' own fourfold particular characteristic and the unhindered wisdom of meaning's fourfold shared characteristic.

The Final Thicket: The Threefold Grouping of Correctly Settled, Wrongly Settled, and Undetermined

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩如實知眾生三聚正定相、邪定相、離此二不定相，正見正定相、邪見邪定相、離此二不定相，五逆邪定相、五根正定相、離此二不定相，八邪邪定相、正位正定相、更不作故離此二不定相，妬悋惡行不轉邪定相、修行無上聖道正定相、離此二不定相，皆如實知。佛子！菩薩隨順如是智，名為安住菩薩善慧地。

論曰：眾生三聚行稠林差別有五種：一、有涅槃法無涅槃法三乘中一向定差別，如經「是菩薩如實知眾生三聚正定相、邪定相、離此二不定相」故。二、善行惡行因差別，如經「正見正定相、邪見邪定相、離此二不定相」故。三、惡道善道因差別，如經「五逆邪定相、五根正定相、離此二不定相」故。四、外道聲聞因差別，如經「八邪邪定相、正位正定相、更不作故離此二不定相」故。五、菩薩差別示現，如經「妬悋惡行不轉邪定相、修行無上聖道正定相、離此二不定相皆如實知」故。捨可化眾生名妬，不喜施他財名悋過，能生他苦惡行不轉。菩薩波羅蜜相違邪定菩薩，是名法師方便成就、智成就、入行成就。三種事成就，此地中善住，如經「佛子！菩薩隨順如是智，名為安住菩薩善慧地」故。云何說成就？與眾生解脫方便故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva truly knows sentient beings sorted into the mark of the threefold grouping: the mark of the correctly settled group, the mark of the wrongly settled group, and, apart from these two, the mark of the undetermined group; the mark of the correctly settled group through correct view, the mark of the wrongly settled group through wrong view, and, apart from these two, the mark of the undetermined group; the mark of the wrongly settled group through the five heinous deeds, the mark of the correctly settled group through the five faculties, and, apart from these two, the mark of the undetermined group; the mark of the wrongly settled group through the eight wrongnesses, the mark of the correctly settled group through the correct station — since he will never act wrongly again — and, apart from these two, the mark of the undetermined group; the mark of the wrongly settled group through envy, stinginess, and unrelenting evil conduct, the mark of the correctly settled group through cultivating the unsurpassed noble path, and, apart from these two, the mark of the undetermined group — all of these he truly knows. Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva who accords with wisdom such as this is called one who dwells securely in the bodhisattva's Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom.

Commentary: The distinctions within the thicket of sentient beings' threefold grouping are of five kinds. First, the distinction, among the three vehicles, of those who constantly and decisively possess the dharma of nirvāṇa and those who lack the dharma of nirvāṇa — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows sentient beings sorted into the mark of the threefold grouping: the mark of the correctly settled group, the mark of the wrongly settled group, and, apart from these two, the mark of the undetermined group." Second, the distinction of the causes of wholesome and unwholesome conduct — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the correctly settled group through correct view, the mark of the wrongly settled group through wrong view, and, apart from these two, the mark of the undetermined group." Third, the distinction of the causes of evil destinies and wholesome destinies — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the wrongly settled group through the five heinous deeds, the mark of the correctly settled group through the five faculties, and, apart from these two, the mark of the undetermined group." Fourth, the distinction of the causes belonging to non-Buddhists and to śrāvakas — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the wrongly settled group through the eight wrongnesses, the mark of the correctly settled group through the correct station — since he will never act wrongly again — and, apart from these two, the mark of the undetermined group." Fifth, the distinct manifestation belonging to bodhisattvas — as the sūtra says, "the mark of the wrongly settled group through envy, stinginess, and unrelenting evil conduct, the mark of the correctly settled group through cultivating the unsurpassed noble path, and, apart from these two, the mark of the undetermined group — all of these he truly knows." Abandoning sentient beings who could be converted is called envy; not delighting in giving one's own wealth to others is called the fault of stinginess; conduct able to give rise to suffering in others is unrelenting evil conduct. A bodhisattva contrary to the perfections is a wrongly settled bodhisattva. This is called the skillful means of the Dharma teacher accomplished, wisdom accomplished, and entry into practice accomplished. These three matters accomplished, he abides securely within this ground — as the sūtra says, "Son of the Buddha! A bodhisattva who accords with wisdom such as this is called one who dwells securely in the bodhisattva's Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom." What, then, is accomplishing teaching? It is giving sentient beings the very means of liberation.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang reads the treatise's fivefold analysis of the threefold grouping as approaching the very same three-way division from several different angles at once. The first analysis works 約種姓以分三聚 — "by way of spiritual lineage, dividing the threefold grouping" — sorting beings simply by whether they possess or lack the very capacity for nirvāṇa. The second works 約解惑以分三聚 — "by way of correct or mistaken understanding" — where correct view alone reliably produces wholesome action, and wrong view alone reliably produces unwholesome action. The third works 約行業以分三聚 — "by way of karmic activity" — where the five heinous deeds reliably lead to evil destinies and the five faculties reliably lead to wholesome ones. Three separate lenses, he notes, converging on the very same threefold shape.

On the treatise's own fifth analysis — the distinctly bodhisattva version of this grouping — Fazang identifies exactly which perfections envy, stinginess, and unrelenting evil conduct each obstruct: 波羅蜜相違者，此中六蔽障六度行⋯謂妬障後三慧定進也，悋障施行，惡行障於戒忍二行 — "as for being contrary to the perfections: here, the six defilements obstruct the practice of the six perfections... namely, envy obstructs the latter three of wisdom, meditation, and diligence; stinginess obstructs the practice of giving; and evil conduct obstructs the two practices of morality and patience." A bodhisattva blocked in this specific way, he concludes, is thereby reckoned among the wrongly settled.

Becoming a Great Dharma Teacher, and the Four Unhindered Wisdoms Giving Rise to Speech

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩住此菩薩善慧地已，如實知眾生如是諸行差別相，隨其解脫而與因緣。是菩薩如實知化眾生法，如實知度眾生法，說聲聞乘法、說辟支佛乘法、說菩薩乘法，如實知說如來地法。是菩薩如是知已，如實為眾生說法令得解脫。隨心差別、隨使差別、隨根差別、隨信差別、隨境界差別、種種行習氣、隨順一切境界智、隨順性行稠林、隨生煩惱業習氣轉、隨聚差別、隨乘信，令得解脫而為說法。

論曰：說成就者，隨其解脫而與因緣，如經「是菩薩住此菩薩善慧地已，如實知眾生如是諸行差別相，隨其解脫而與因緣」故。彼說成就，復三種相示現：一、智成就；二、口業成就；三、法師成就。智成就者，隨所知、隨所依，此事說應知。何者隨所知說？解脫器得熟故、解脫體正度故、解脫差別以三乘差別故，如經「是菩薩如實知化眾生法乃至如實知說如來地法」故。何者隨所依說？所說法對器故。隨應度者，授對治法故。是義二句說。所說法器成，隨根隨信而為說法，此義二句說。如經「是菩薩如是知已，如實為眾生說法，令得解脫，隨心差別、隨使差別、隨根差別、隨信差別」故。隨譬喻解器，如經「隨境界差別」，種種行習氣故。隨種種異行器，如經「隨順一切境界智」故。乃至得成就器，如經「隨順性行稠林」故。隨辭辯器，彼生煩惱業熏同行故，如經「隨生煩惱業習氣轉」故。定不定根轉器，如經「隨聚差別」故。隨乘因能乘出器，如經「隨乘信令得解脫而為說法」故。云何智業成就？

經曰：是菩薩住此菩薩善慧地中，略說作大法師，住在大法師深妙義中，守護諸佛法藏。

論曰：是中說者、持者二句示現。住在大法師深妙義中者，有二十種能作法師事。云何能作法師事？一者時；二者正意；三者頓；四者相續；五者漸；六者次；七者句義漸次；八者示；九者喜；十者勸；十一者具德；十二者不毀；十三者不亂；十四者如法；十五者隨眾；十六者慈心；十七者安隱心；十八者憐愍心；十九者不著利養名聞；二十者不自讚毀他。是中時者，無八難故。如偈說：

「如王懷憂惱，　病恚著諸欲，
險處無侍衛，　讒佞無忠臣。

如是八難時，　智臣不應語，
心王亦如是，　非時不應說。」

正意者，正威儀住，非不正住。此義云何？自立他坐不應為說法，如是等如戒經中廣說。何以故？諸佛菩薩敬重法故。以敬法故，令他生尊重心，聞法恭敬攝心聽故。頓者，是菩薩正意，為一切眾說一切法，離慳法垢故。相續者，說無休息，捨諸法中嫉妬意故。漸者，如字句次第說故。次者，如字句次第義，亦如是說故。句義漸次者，說同義法不說不同義法故。示者，示所應示等故。喜者，喜所應喜故。勸者，怯弱眾生助令勇猛故。具德者，現智、比智阿含所證具說故。不毀者，隨順善道說故。不亂者，不動不雜，正入非稠林故。如法者，具說四聖諦故。隨眾者，於四眾八部隨所應聞而為說法故。如是十五種相，菩薩隨順利益他說一切法故。慈心者，於怨眾生中起慈心說法故。安隱心者，於惡行眾生中起利益心說法故。憐愍心者，於受苦樂放逸眾生中起憐愍利樂心說法故。不著利養名聞者，心不悕望，常行遠離故。不自讚毀他者，離我慢嫉妬隨煩惱為眾生說法故。如是五種相，菩薩自心清淨故。具此二十事能作法師，是名住大法師深妙義中故。如是說成就中智成就已說。云何口業成就？

經曰：通達無量智方便四無礙智，起菩薩言辭說法，是菩薩日夜常不壞四無礙智。何等為四？所謂法無礙、義無礙、辭無礙、樂說無礙。

論曰：口業成就者，菩薩以四無礙言音說法，如經「通達無量智方便乃至樂說無礙」故。不壞者，不動故。是中四無礙境界者，一、法體；二、法境界體；三、正得與眾生；四、正求與無量門。是中法體者，遠離二邊生法所攝，如色礙相如是等。法境界體者，彼遠離二邊生法所攝中，如實智境界。菩薩如彼生法所攝智境界中住如色。何者是色？眼色等虛妄分別如是等。正得與眾生者，於彼如實智境界中隨他所喜言說正知隨他言說正知而與故。正求與無量門者，於彼隨他所喜言語正知無量種種義語隨知而與故。是四無礙智十種差別：一、依自相；二、依同相；三、行相；四、說相；五、智相；六、無我慢相；七、小乘大乘相；八、菩薩地相；九、如來地相；十、作住持相。後五是淨相。云何自相？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, having already come to dwell in this bodhisattva's Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom, truly knows sentient beings' differing marks of conduct such as these, and bestows upon them the very causes and conditions in accordance with their liberation. This bodhisattva truly knows the Dharma of converting sentient beings, truly knows the Dharma of ferrying sentient beings across, teaches the Dharma of the śrāvaka vehicle, teaches the Dharma of the pratyekabuddha vehicle, teaches the Dharma of the bodhisattva vehicle, and truly knows how to teach the Dharma of the Tathāgata's own ground. This bodhisattva, having known in this way, truly teaches the Dharma to sentient beings so that they attain liberation — in accordance with the distinctions of their minds, the distinctions of their latent tendencies, the distinctions of their faculties, the distinctions of their faith, the distinctions of their objects of experience, their various habitual tendencies of conduct, the wisdom that accords with every object of experience, according with the thicket of their inherent conduct, according with the turning of the habitual tendencies of the karma born of affliction, according with the distinctions of their grouping, and according with the faith proper to their vehicle — teaching them the Dharma so that they attain liberation.

Commentary: "Accomplishing teaching" means bestowing the very causes and conditions in accordance with sentient beings' liberation — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, having already come to dwell in this bodhisattva's Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom, truly knows sentient beings' differing marks of conduct such as these, and bestows upon them the very causes and conditions in accordance with their liberation." This "accomplishing teaching," moreover, is manifest through three further marks: first, wisdom accomplished; second, verbal action accomplished; third, the Dharma teacher accomplished. As for wisdom accomplished, this matter should be understood as spoken of according to what is known and according to what is relied upon. What is spoken of according to what is known? Since the vessel for liberation attains ripeness, since the very essence of liberation is correctly ferried across, and since the distinctions of liberation are the distinctions of the three vehicles — as the sūtra says, from "this bodhisattva truly knows the Dharma of converting sentient beings" all the way to "truly knows how to teach the Dharma of the Tathāgata's own ground." What is spoken of according to what is relied upon? It is because the Dharma taught corresponds to the vessel. As for "according with those who should be ferried across," this is bestowing the antidote-dharma — this meaning is spoken of in two phrases. As for the vessel for the Dharma taught being brought to completion, teaching the Dharma according to the faculties and according to faith — this meaning, too, is spoken of in two phrases — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, having known in this way, truly teaches the Dharma to sentient beings so that they attain liberation, in accordance with the distinctions of their minds, the distinctions of their latent tendencies, the distinctions of their faculties, the distinctions of their faith." As for according with the vessel understood through simile — as the sūtra says, "the distinctions of their objects of experience" — this refers to their various habitual tendencies of conduct. As for according with the vessel of their various differing conduct — as the sūtra says, "the wisdom that accords with every object of experience." As for the vessel that attains accomplishment — as the sūtra says, "according with the thicket of their inherent conduct." As for according with the vessel of eloquent speech, since this refers to the arising of the affliction-born karma that habituates the very conduct he shares with them — as the sūtra says, "according with the turning of the habitual tendencies of the karma born of affliction." As for the vessel that turns upon settled and undetermined faculties — as the sūtra says, "according with the distinctions of their grouping." As for according with the cause of the vehicle, able to bring the vessel forth — as the sūtra says, "according with the faith proper to their vehicle, teaching them the Dharma so that they attain liberation." What, then, is the accomplishment of wisdom-activity?

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, dwelling within this bodhisattva's Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom, is, in brief, said to become a great Dharma teacher, dwelling within the great Dharma teacher's own profound and subtle meaning, guarding and protecting the treasury of every buddha's Dharma.

Commentary: Here, two phrases are manifest: "teaching" and "upholding." As for "dwelling within the great Dharma teacher's own profound and subtle meaning," there are twenty aspects able to perform the work of a Dharma teacher. What are these twenty aspects able to perform the work of a Dharma teacher? First, timing; second, correct intention; third, immediacy; fourth, continuity; fifth, gradualness; sixth, sequence; seventh, the gradual sequence of phrase and meaning; eighth, demonstration; ninth, joy; tenth, encouragement; eleventh, being complete in merit; twelfth, not disparaging; thirteenth, not being disordered; fourteenth, according with the Dharma; fifteenth, according with the assembly; sixteenth, a mind of loving-kindness; seventeenth, a mind of peace and security; eighteenth, a mind of compassion; nineteenth, not attaching to profit, support, or renown; twentieth, not praising oneself while disparaging others. Here, "timing" means being free of the eight difficulties. As a verse says:

"Like a king harboring worry and vexation, sick, wrathful, or attached to every desire,
in a place of danger, without attendants; among flatterers, without a loyal minister —

at such times as these eight difficulties, a wise minister ought not to speak;
the mind-king, too, is just the same — at the wrong time, one ought not teach."

"Correct intention" means abiding in correct dignified deportment, not abiding incorrectly. What is the meaning of this? If he himself stands while another sits, he should not teach the Dharma — matters such as this are set forth at length within the scriptures on precepts. Why is this? Because every buddha and bodhisattva reveres and honors the Dharma; and because, through revering the Dharma, he causes others to give rise to a mind of reverence, hearing the Dharma with reverence, gathering in their minds to listen. "Immediacy" means that this bodhisattva, with correct intention, teaches every dharma to every assembly, being free of the defilement of stinginess with the Dharma. "Continuity" means teaching without rest, abandoning any mind of envy within every dharma. "Gradualness" means teaching according to the sequence of letter and phrase. "Sequence" means that the meaning, too, follows the sequence of letter and phrase, and is likewise taught in this way. "The gradual sequence of phrase and meaning" means teaching dharmas of shared meaning without teaching dharmas of differing meaning. "Demonstration" means demonstrating what should be demonstrated, and so forth. "Joy" means delighting in what should be delighted in. "Encouragement" means helping timid sentient beings so that they become courageous. "Being complete in merit" means fully teaching what is realized through manifest wisdom and inferential wisdom, and through the scriptural tradition. "Not disparaging" means teaching in accordance with the wholesome path. "Not being disordered" means being unmoved and unmixed, correctly entering what is not the thicket. "According with the Dharma" means fully teaching the four noble truths. "According with the assembly" means teaching the Dharma, among the four assemblies and the eight kinds of beings, according to what each is fit to hear. These fifteen kinds of mark are because the bodhisattva, according with benefiting others, teaches every dharma. "A mind of loving-kindness" means giving rise to a mind of loving-kindness and teaching the Dharma even among sentient beings who are his enemies. "A mind of peace and security" means giving rise to a mind that benefits, and teaching the Dharma, even among sentient beings of evil conduct. "A mind of compassion" means giving rise to a mind of compassion that brings benefit and joy, and teaching the Dharma, even among sentient beings who, experiencing suffering and pleasure, are given to heedlessness. "Not attaching to profit, support, or renown" means that his mind has no craving for them, and he constantly practices remaining far removed from them. "Not praising oneself while disparaging others" means teaching the Dharma to sentient beings free of pride, envy, and every secondary affliction. These five kinds of mark are because the bodhisattva's own mind is pure. Being complete in these twenty matters, he is able to perform the work of a Dharma teacher — this is what is called dwelling within the great Dharma teacher's own profound and subtle meaning. In this way, the wisdom accomplished within "accomplishing teaching" has already been explained. What, then, is verbal action accomplished?

Sūtra: Penetrating the skillful means of boundless wisdom, the four unhindered wisdoms give rise to the bodhisattva's own eloquence in teaching the Dharma; this bodhisattva, day and night, constantly remains unbroken in the four unhindered wisdoms. What are these four? They are the unhindered wisdom of dharmas, the unhindered wisdom of meaning, the unhindered wisdom of language, and the unhindered wisdom of eloquence.

Commentary: "Verbal action accomplished" means that the bodhisattva teaches the Dharma through the eloquent speech of the four unhindered wisdoms — as the sūtra says, from "penetrating the skillful means of boundless wisdom" all the way to "the unhindered wisdom of eloquence." "Unbroken" means unmoved. Here, the object-realm of the four unhindered wisdoms is of four kinds: first, the very substance of dharmas; second, the very substance of dharmas' object-realm; third, correctly obtaining and bestowing upon sentient beings; fourth, correctly seeking and bestowing through boundless gateways. Here, "the very substance of dharmas" means what is gathered within dharmas that are born, far removed from the two extremes — as with the mark of form's own resistant quality, and the rest, of this kind. "The very substance of dharmas' object-realm" means the object-realm of true wisdom within what is gathered within dharmas that are born, far removed from the two extremes. The bodhisattva abides within the object-realm of wisdom gathered within dharmas that are born, just as with form. What is this "form"? The eye, form, and the rest, according to their false discrimination, and so forth, of this kind. "Correctly obtaining and bestowing upon sentient beings" means that, within that object-realm of true wisdom, he correctly knows the speech that others delight in, and, correctly knowing, speaks according to others, and bestows it accordingly. "Correctly seeking and bestowing through boundless gateways" means that, according with the speech others delight in and correctly knowing it, he correctly knows boundless and various words of meaning, and, according with this knowing, bestows them. These four unhindered wisdoms have ten distinctions: first, in dependence on the particular characteristic; second, in dependence on the shared characteristic; third, the characteristic of activity; fourth, the characteristic of teaching; fifth, the characteristic of wisdom; sixth, the characteristic of being free of pride; seventh, the characteristic of the lesser vehicle and the great vehicle; eighth, the characteristic of the bodhisattva-ground; ninth, the characteristic of the Tathāgata-ground; tenth, the characteristic of sustaining activity. The latter five are the characteristics of purity. What, then, is the particular characteristic?

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang's own division of "accomplishing teaching" cuts across the treatise's threefold structure with a finer four-segment map: 文有四段，義有三重：一盡此初段是智成就；二「為大法師」下一段亦智成亦口業成；三「用無量慧」下唯口業成；四「得陀羅尼」下是法師成就 — "the text has four segments, of three layers of meaning: the first segment, up through here, is wisdom-accomplishment; the second, from 'becomes a great Dharma teacher' onward, is both wisdom-accomplishment and verbal-accomplishment together; the third, from 'using boundless wisdom' onward, is verbal-accomplishment alone; the fourth, from 'attains dhāraṇī' onward, is Dharma-teacher-accomplishment." By this count, Part 43 closes partway through his own third segment — the treatise's fourth segment, Dharma-teacher-accomplishment proper, still lies ahead.

On the twenty aspects of the great Dharma teacher's own work, Fazang glosses the sūtra's opening pair of terms precisely: 論中，說者是其口業，明入深義也，持者是智成顯守護法也 — "in the treatise, 'teaching' is his own verbal action, making manifest his entry into profound meaning; 'upholding' is wisdom accomplished, displaying his guarding of the Dharma." Two verbs, two of the three accomplishments already folded together in a single phrase, before the twenty named aspects unfold what "teaching" alone actually requires.

The Particular Characteristic and the Shared Characteristic of All Dharmas

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩用法無礙智知諸法自相，以義無礙智知諸法差別相，以辭無礙智知不壞說諸法，以樂說無礙智知諸法次第不斷說。

論曰：是中自相者，有四種：一、生法自相；二、差別自相；三、想堅固自相；四、彼想差別自相。如經「是菩薩用法無礙智知諸法自相故，以義無礙智知諸法差別相故，以辭無礙智知不壞說諸法故，以樂說無礙智知諸法次第不斷」故。是中不壞說者，隨所覺諸相，隨彼彼眾生種種說法故。次第不斷說者，次第不息無量眾多異名，為堅固彼義故。云何同相？

經曰：復次以法無礙智知諸法無體性，以義無礙智知諸法生滅相，以辭無礙智知諸法假名而不斷假名法說，以樂說無礙智隨假名不壞無邊法說。

論曰：是中同相有四種：一者一切法同相；二者一切有為法同相；三者一切法假名同相；四者假名假名同相。如經「復次以法無礙智知諸法無體性故，以義無礙智知諸法生滅相故，以辭無礙智知諸法假名而不斷假名法說故，以樂說無礙智隨假名不壞無邊法說」故。是中無常門入無我義中。第二同相初智境界成，是中知諸法假名而不斷假名法者，假名法以餘假名法說。隨假名不壞無邊法者，不壞前假名而能異假名說。云何行相？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, using the unhindered wisdom of dharmas, knows dharmas' own particular characteristic; through the unhindered wisdom of meaning, knows dharmas' distinguishing characteristic; through the unhindered wisdom of language, knows how to teach every dharma without breaking it apart; through the unhindered wisdom of eloquence, knows how to teach every dharma in sequence, without interruption.

Commentary: Here, the particular characteristic is of four kinds: first, the particular characteristic of dharmas as they arise; second, the particular characteristic of their distinctions; third, the particular characteristic of conceptions made firm; fourth, the particular characteristic of the distinctions among those conceptions — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, using the unhindered wisdom of dharmas, knows dharmas' own particular characteristic; through the unhindered wisdom of meaning, knows dharmas' distinguishing characteristic; through the unhindered wisdom of language, knows how to teach every dharma without breaking it apart; through the unhindered wisdom of eloquence, knows how to teach every dharma in sequence, without interruption." Here, "teaching without breaking apart" means teaching the Dharma in various ways to sentient being after sentient being, according with each mark he has awakened to. "Teaching in sequence, without interruption" means, in sequence and without ceasing, using boundless and manifold differing names, in order to make that very meaning firm. What, then, is the shared characteristic?

Sūtra: Furthermore, through the unhindered wisdom of dharmas, he knows that dharmas are without any essential nature; through the unhindered wisdom of meaning, he knows dharmas' characteristic of arising and ceasing; through the unhindered wisdom of language, he knows dharmas as provisional names, and, without breaking apart the provisional name, teaches the Dharma; through the unhindered wisdom of eloquence, according with the provisional name, without breaking it apart, he teaches boundless dharmas.

Commentary: Here, the shared characteristic is of four kinds: first, the shared characteristic of all dharmas; second, the shared characteristic of all conditioned dharmas; third, the shared characteristic of all dharmas as provisional names; fourth, the shared characteristic of provisional name upon provisional name — as the sūtra says, "furthermore, through the unhindered wisdom of dharmas, he knows that dharmas are without any essential nature; through the unhindered wisdom of meaning, he knows dharmas' characteristic of arising and ceasing; through the unhindered wisdom of language, he knows dharmas as provisional names, and, without breaking apart the provisional name, teaches the Dharma; through the unhindered wisdom of eloquence, according with the provisional name, without breaking it apart, teaches boundless dharmas." Here, it is through the gateway of impermanence that he enters the meaning of no-self. The object-realm of wisdom at the very outset of this second, the shared characteristic, is thereby accomplished. Here, "knowing dharmas as provisional names, and, without breaking apart the provisional name, teaching the Dharma" means that a dharma under a provisional name is taught by means of a further provisional name. "According with the provisional name, without breaking it apart, teaching boundless dharmas" means that, without breaking apart the prior provisional name, he is nonetheless able to teach through a different provisional name. What, then, is the characteristic of activity?

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang frames the treatise's own tenfold breakdown of the four unhindered wisdoms as splitting five and five: 初五汎知理教、後五知三乘淨德 — "the first five broadly know principle and teaching; the latter five know the pure virtues of the three vehicles." Within the first five, he glosses exactly why particular characteristic and shared characteristic pair together as the opening two: 一約知事法，事法緣別，各住不雜，名為自相；二知理法，理法遍通，名為同相 — "the first is knowing phenomenal dharmas — since phenomenal dharmas differ by their own condition, each abiding distinct and unmixed, this is called the particular characteristic; the second is knowing principial dharmas — since principial dharmas pervade universally, this is called the shared characteristic." Particular characteristic tracks each thing in its own distinctness; shared characteristic tracks the single principle running through everything at once.

He adds a short closing gloss on the pair, 此二當相明法 — "these two, corresponding to the characteristic, make the dharma manifest" — before noting where the sequence goes next: 三約時辨法，謂三世遷流，故名行相 — "the third distinguishes dharmas by way of time, namely, the flowing transformation of the three time-periods, and so is called the characteristic of activity" — the very topic this part leaves for Part 44 to take up.

Part 43 Complete

This closes Part 43, continuing further into Sādhumatī, the Ninth Ground, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom. This part completed the ground's final thicket — the threefold grouping of sentient beings into the correctly settled, the wrongly settled, and, apart from these two, the undetermined, analyzed fivefold by the commentary through spiritual lineage, correct and mistaken view, karmic destiny, the distinction of outsider and śrāvaka, and a bodhisattva's own distinct fall into envy, stinginess, and unrelenting evil conduct that obstructs the perfections — and opened the ground's third division, "accomplishing teaching": the bodhisattva becoming, in brief, a great Dharma teacher, dwelling within the great Dharma teacher's own profound and subtle meaning through twenty named aspects of teaching well, from right timing through not praising himself while disparaging others. The treatise then turned to the four unhindered wisdoms of dharmas, meaning, language, and eloquence that give this Ninth Ground its very name, and began their detailed tenfold exposition with dharmas' own fourfold particular characteristic and fourfold shared characteristic. Part 44 continues this same long sequence of paired topics — through the unhindered wisdom of dharmas, know one thing; through the unhindered wisdom of meaning, know another — turning next to the characteristic of activity tracked through the three time-periods, the teachings' own textual forms, unbreakable skillful means, freedom from pride toward ultimate or worldly truth, the distinction between the lesser vehicle and the great vehicle, the bodhisattva-ground's own characteristic, the Tathāgata-ground's own characteristic, and the buddhas' own powers, fearlessness, and unshared qualities that sustain every activity.
