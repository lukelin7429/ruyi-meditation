The Ten Inexhaustible Clauses, and Why Great Compassion Arises

Ten realms sustain the great vows for as long as sentient beings themselves exist — and, once faith itself is shown to be superior in six distinct ways, the section of comparative excellence turns to the excellence of practice, tracing great compassion back to three contemplations: how profound the Dharma actually is, how utterly filled with suffering ordinary life actually is, and the double error that keeps most beings from ever noticing either.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 12 of 52 complete Source: Taishō T1522, 十地經論 (Vasubandhu, tr. Bodhiruci et al., Northern Wei) Reading notes after Jingying Huiyuan's 十地經論義記 (X0753)

The Ten Inexhaustible Clauses, and the Excellence of Practice Begins

Chinese Source

經曰：以十盡句成諸大願。何等為十？所謂一、眾生界盡；二、世界盡；三、虛空界盡；四、法界盡；五、涅槃界盡；六、佛出世界盡；七、如來智界盡；八、心所緣界盡；九、佛境界智入界盡；十、世間轉法轉智轉界盡。如眾生界盡，我願乃盡。如世界盡、如虛空界盡、如法界盡、如涅槃界盡、如佛出世界盡、如佛智界盡、如心所緣界盡、如佛境界智入界盡、如世間轉法轉智轉界盡，若彼界盡，我願乃盡。如是眾生界盡不盡，我此善根亦不可盡。世界盡不盡、虛空界盡不盡、法界盡不盡、涅槃界盡不盡、佛出世界盡不盡、如來智界盡不盡、心所緣界盡不盡、佛境界智入界盡不盡、世間轉法轉智轉界盡不盡，我此諸願善根亦不可盡。

論曰：於中眾生界盡是總，世界盡乃至智轉界盡是別。何等是眾生界？眾生界盡故。何處住？世界盡故。所有虛空界虛空界盡故，說何法教化？法界盡故，隨化眾生置何處？涅槃界盡故，佛出世界盡故，以何方便善巧？如來智界盡故，復隨所緣心緣界盡故，復隨以何界？佛境界智入界盡故。此事已說盡者，示現不斷盡，非念念盡故。此九種盡，略說三種三轉，示現此十盡句增上力故，諸佛以此力常為眾生作利益故。如是已說願挍量勝。云何行挍量勝？

經曰：諸佛子！菩薩決定發如是諸大願已，則得調順心、柔軟心，如是則成信者。信諸佛如來本所行入，集諸波羅蜜而得增長，善成就諸地，具足無畏、力、不共佛法不壞故，不可思議佛法，無中無邊如來境界起，無量行門諸如來境界入，信成就果。舉要言之，信一切菩薩行，乃至得如來智地說加故。

論曰：發如是諸大願已則得調順心者，彼諸善根中得自在勝故。柔軟心者，得勝樂行故。如是則成信者，於中本行入者從菩薩行入乃至成菩提覺故。於中信菩薩行所攝本行入，有二種相：一、云何菩薩行；二、云何次第成。如經「集諸波羅蜜而得增長」故，善成就諸地故。此菩提所攝本行入有六種勝，是故信勝。一者外道魔怨、聲聞緣覺對治等勝，如經「具足無畏、力、不共佛法不壞」故。二者不思議神通力上勝，如經「不可思議佛法」故。三者不雜染勝，如經「無中無邊如來境界起」故。四者一切種智勝，如經「無量行門諸如來境界入」故。五者離勝，一切煩惱習常遠離故，如經「信成就果」故。復略說彼菩薩本行入示現，如經「舉要言之，信一切菩薩行乃至得如來智地說加」故。說者所說，加者得證故。此菩薩三種觀，於諸眾生起大慈悲：一、遠離最上第一義樂；二、具足諸苦；三、於彼二顛倒。云何遠離最上第一義樂？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Through ten inexhaustible clauses, these great vows are accomplished. What are the ten? First, the exhaustion of the realm of sentient beings; second, the exhaustion of the realm of world-systems; third, the exhaustion of the realm of space; fourth, the exhaustion of the dharma-realm; fifth, the exhaustion of the realm of nirvāṇa; sixth, the exhaustion of the realm of buddhas appearing in the world; seventh, the exhaustion of the realm of the tathāgatas' wisdom; eighth, the exhaustion of the realm of what the mind takes as its object; ninth, the exhaustion of the realm entered by the buddhas' own wisdom; and tenth, the exhaustion of the realm of the world's turning, the turning of the Dharma, and the turning of wisdom. Should the realm of sentient beings ever be exhausted, only then would my vow itself be exhausted. Should the realm of world-systems, the realm of space, the dharma-realm, the realm of nirvāṇa, the realm of buddhas appearing in the world, the realm of the buddhas' wisdom, the realm of what the mind takes as its object, the realm entered by the buddhas' own wisdom, or the realm of the world's turning, the turning of the Dharma, and the turning of wisdom ever be exhausted — should any of those realms be exhausted, only then would my vow itself be exhausted. But since the realm of sentient beings can never be exhausted, this root of goodness of mine can likewise never be exhausted. Since the realm of world-systems, the realm of space, the dharma-realm, the realm of nirvāṇa, the realm of buddhas appearing in the world, the realm of the tathāgatas' wisdom, the realm of what the mind takes as its object, the realm entered by the buddhas' own wisdom, and the realm of the world's turning, the turning of the Dharma, and the turning of wisdom can never be exhausted, this root of goodness of my every vow can likewise never be exhausted.

Commentary: Here, the exhaustion of the realm of sentient beings is the universal aspect, and the exhaustion of the realm of world-systems through the exhaustion of the realm of the turning of wisdom are the particular aspects. What is the realm of sentient beings? It is the exhaustion of the realm of sentient beings. Where do they dwell? It is the exhaustion of the realm of world-systems. Whatever realm of space there may be — it is the exhaustion of the realm of space. What dharma is taught to transform them? It is the exhaustion of the dharma-realm. Wherever the transformed sentient beings are placed — it is the exhaustion of the realm of nirvāṇa, the exhaustion of the realm of buddhas appearing in the world. By what skillful means? It is the exhaustion of the realm of the tathāgatas' wisdom, and further, in accordance with whatever the mind takes as its object, the exhaustion of the realm of what the mind takes as its object. And further, in accordance with which realm? It is the exhaustion of the realm entered by the buddhas' own wisdom. Having now explained this matter of "exhaustion," it makes manifest that this is an unbroken exhaustion, not an exhaustion moment by moment. These nine kinds of exhaustion, briefly stated, are three kinds of turning, making manifest the increasing power of these ten inexhaustible clauses — and it is through this power that the buddhas constantly bring benefit to sentient beings. This concludes the explanation of the excellence of vow. What is the excellence of practice?

Sūtra: Sons of the Buddha! Once a bodhisattva has resolutely aroused these great vows, he attains a tamed mind and a gentle mind, and thereby becomes one of faith. He has faith in the buddhas' and tathāgatas' own original practice and entry, gathering the perfections and thereby increasing them, well accomplishing every ground, complete in fearlessness, power, and the unshared buddha-dharmas, unbreakable, the inconceivable buddha-dharma, arising from the tathāgatas' own domain, without center and without limit, entering the tathāgatas' own domain through boundless gateways of practice, and thus his faith accomplishes its fruit. In brief: he has faith in every bodhisattva's practice, all the way to attaining the tathāgatas' ground of wisdom — which is spoken of as an empowerment.

Commentary: "Once he has aroused these great vows, he attains a tamed mind" means that, among his roots of goodness, he attains supreme self-mastery. "A gentle mind" means he attains a supreme, joyful practice. "Thereby becomes one of faith" — here, "entry into the original practice" means entering from a bodhisattva's practice all the way to accomplishing the awakening of enlightenment. Within this, having faith in the entry into the original practice encompassed by a bodhisattva's practice has two aspects: first, what is a bodhisattva's practice; and second, in what sequence is it accomplished — as the sūtra says, "gathering the perfections and thereby increasing them," and "well accomplishing every ground." This entry into the original practice encompassed by enlightenment has six kinds of superiority, and this is why the faith in it is superior. First, superiority in counteracting the outsiders, Māra's hosts, and the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas — as the sūtra says, "complete in fearlessness, power, and the unshared buddha-dharmas, unbreakable." Second, the supreme superiority of inconceivable spiritual power — as the sūtra says, "the inconceivable buddha-dharma." Third, superiority in being free of mixed defilement — as the sūtra says, "arising from the tathāgatas' own domain, without center and without limit." Fourth, the superiority of the wisdom of all modes — as the sūtra says, "entering the tathāgatas' own domain through boundless gateways of practice." Fifth, the superiority of freedom — every habituated tendency of affliction being forever left behind — as the sūtra says, "his faith accomplishes its fruit." Further, this briefly summarizes and makes manifest that bodhisattva's entry into the original practice — as the sūtra says, "in brief: he has faith in every bodhisattva's practice, all the way to attaining the tathāgatas' ground of wisdom, which is spoken of as an empowerment." "Spoken of" means what is taught; "empowerment" means what is realized. This bodhisattva has three contemplations, giving rise to great loving-kindness and compassion toward every sentient being: first, that they are far removed from the supreme bliss of ultimate meaning; second, that they are utterly filled with every suffering; and third, that they are caught within these two errors. What is meant by being far removed from the supreme bliss of ultimate meaning?

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Huiyuan reads the ten inexhaustible clauses not as ten independent items loosely gathered under one label but as an interlocking chain of questions, each answer generating the very question the next clause answers: 何等是眾生界？眾生界盡故。何處住？世界盡故。所有虛空界虛空界盡故，說何法教化？法界盡故，隨化眾生置何處？涅槃界盡故，佛出世界盡故，以何方便善巧？如來智界盡故 — "What is the realm of sentient beings? It is the exhaustion of the realm of sentient beings. Where do they dwell? It is the exhaustion of the realm of world-systems. Whatever realm of space there may be — it is the exhaustion of the realm of space. What dharma is taught to transform them? It is the exhaustion of the dharma-realm. Wherever the transformed sentient beings are placed — it is the exhaustion of the realm of nirvāṇa, the exhaustion of the realm of buddhas appearing in the world. By what skillful means? It is the exhaustion of the realm of the tathāgatas' wisdom." Asking after sentient beings raises the question of where they live; asking where they live raises the question of what space contains that; asking what teaching reaches them raises the question of where the taught are placed; and asking how they are placed raises the question of the skillful means needed to place them there. What looks, on first reading, like an arbitrary list of ten cosmic categories turns out to be the complete logical unfolding of a single practical question — how does a bodhisattva actually reach and transform another being — asked all the way down to its final presuppositions.

On the opening of the excellence of practice, Huiyuan resolves a small counting puzzle in the text's own language: the passage names only five specific kinds of superiority in its detailed run-through, yet calls the whole set "six kinds of superiority." His explanation is that the sixth is folded into the passage's own closing summary rather than given a separate numbered clause: 前四句是其智德，智者是道，道諦望滅義分為因；離者是滅，滅諦望道義說為果，故獨名此以為果矣 — "the first four clauses concern the virtue of wisdom, wisdom being the path — and relative to cessation, the truth of the path counts as the cause; freedom is cessation, and relative to the path, the truth of cessation counts as the fruit — and this is why this fifth item alone is named the fruit." The five detailed clauses split into four describing wisdom (the cause) and one describing freedom from affliction (the fruit that wisdom produces), and it is this cause-and-fruit pairing, taken as a further, sixth unit on top of the five, that yields the "six kinds of superiority" the opening line promises. Faith, on this reading, is not simply agreement with a doctrine — it is confidence specifically in a demonstrated causal structure: this wisdom, cultivated this way, produces this exact freedom.

Three Contemplations: The Dharma's Profundity, and Suffering's Fullness

Chinese Source

經曰：諸佛子！彼菩薩作是念：諸佛正法如是甚深、如是寂靜、如是寂滅、如是空、如是無相、如是無願、如是無染、如是無量、如是上，此諸佛法如是難得。

論曰：諸佛正法如是甚深者，有九種：一、寂靜甚深；二、寂滅甚深；三、空甚深；四、無相甚深；五、無願甚深；六、無染甚深；七、無量甚深；八、上甚深；九、難得甚深。寂靜者，離妄計實有故，妄計正取故。寂滅者，法義定故。空無相無願者，三障對治解脫門觀故。何者三障？一分別、二相、三取，捨願故。無染者，離雜染法觀故。無量者，不可算數不可思量，生善根觀故。上者，依自利利他增上智觀故。難得者，三阿僧祇劫證智觀故。云何具足諸苦？

經曰：而諸凡夫心墮邪見，為無明癡闇蔽其意識，常立憍慢幢，墮在念欲渴愛網中，隨順諂曲林，常懷嫉妬而作後身生處因緣。多集貪欲、瞋、癡，起諸業行，嫌恨猛風吹罪心火常令熾燃，有所作業皆與顛倒相應，隨順欲漏、有漏、無明漏，相續起心意識種子。

論曰：而諸凡夫心墮邪見者，邪見有九種：一者蔽意邪見，如經「為無明癡闇蔽其意識」故。二者憍慢邪見，如經「常立憍慢幢」故。三者愛念邪見，如經「墮在念欲渴愛網中」故。四者諂曲心邪見，如經「隨順諂曲林」故。五者嫉妬行邪見，如經「常懷嫉妬而作後身生處因緣」故。六者集業邪見，如經「多集貪欲瞋癡起諸業行」故。七者吹心熾燃邪見，如經「嫌恨猛風吹罪心火常令熾燃」故。八者起業邪見，如經「有所作業皆與顛倒相應」故。九者心意識種子邪見，如經「隨順欲漏有漏無明漏相續起心意識種子」故。

是中蔽意邪見、憍慢邪見、愛念邪見，此三邪見依法義妄計如是次第。諂曲心邪見、嫉妬行邪見，此二邪見於追求時心行過故。嫉者於身起邪行故，妬者於資財等，是故生生之處墮卑賤中，形貌鄙陋資生不足故。第六集業邪見，受諸受時憎愛彼二顛倒境界故。第七吹心熾燃邪見，於怨恨時互相追念欲起報惡業故。第八起業邪見，於作惡時迭相加害故。第九心意識種子邪見，於作善業時所有布施持戒修行善根等業皆是有漏故。

經曰：於三界地復有芽生，所謂名色共生不離，此名色增長已成六入聚，成六入已內外相對生觸，觸因緣故生受，深樂受故生渴愛，渴愛增長故生取，取增長故復起後有，有因緣故有生老死憂悲苦惱，如是眾生生長苦聚。是中皆空離我我所，無知無覺如草木石壁又亦如響；然諸眾生不知不覺而受苦惱。

論曰：是中因緣有三種：一、自相，從復有芽生乃至於有；二、同相，謂生老病死等過；三、顛倒相，離我我所等。自相者有三種：一者報相，名色共阿黎耶識生，如經「於三界地復有芽生，所謂名色共生」故。名色共生者，名色共彼生故。二者彼因相，是名色不離彼、依彼共生故，如經「不離」故。三者彼果次第相，從六入乃至於有，如經「此名色增長已成六入聚，成六入已內外相對生觸，觸因緣故生受，深樂受故生渴愛，渴愛增長故生取，取增長故復起後有，有因緣故有生老死憂悲苦惱，如是眾生生長苦聚」故。是中離我我所者，此二示現空。無知無覺者，自體無我故。彼無知無覺，示非眾生數動不動事。如經「如草木石壁又亦如響」故，因緣相似相類法故。云何於彼二顛倒？如經「然諸眾生不知不覺而受苦惱」故。

經曰：菩薩如是見諸眾生不離苦聚，是故即生大悲智慧；是諸眾生我應教化令住涅槃畢竟之樂，是故即生大慈智慧。

論曰：云何具諸苦聚？云何遠離？最上第一義樂，此先已說，示現大悲慈等故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Sons of the Buddha! That bodhisattva reflects thus: the true Dharma of every buddha is thus profound, thus still, thus quiescent, thus empty, thus signless, thus free of aspiration, thus undefiled, thus boundless, thus supreme — this Dharma of every buddha is thus hard to attain.

Commentary: "The true Dharma of every buddha is thus profound" is of nine kinds: the profundity of stillness; the profundity of quiescence; the profundity of emptiness; the profundity of signlessness; the profundity of freedom from aspiration; the profundity of being undefiled; the profundity of boundlessness; the profundity of supremacy; and the profundity of being hard to attain. "Stillness" means being free of the false conception of real existence, since false conception grasps directly at it as real. "Quiescence" means the meaning of the Dharma is settled. "Emptiness," "signlessness," and "freedom from aspiration" mean the contemplation of the three gates of liberation that counteract the three hindrances. What are the three hindrances? Discrimination, marks, and grasping — these three gates are their respective antidotes, since the third involves relinquishing aspiration. "Undefiled" means the contemplation of being free of mixed, defiling dharmas. "Boundless" means the contemplation of a root of goodness that cannot be counted or measured. "Supreme" means the contemplation of the supreme wisdom that rests on both self-benefit and benefiting others. "Hard to attain" means the contemplation of the wisdom realized only after three incalculable eons. What is meant by being utterly filled with every suffering?

Sūtra: Yet ordinary people's minds fall into wrong views, their discriminating consciousness obscured by the darkness of ignorance and delusion, constantly raising the banner of conceit, sunk within the net of craving desire, following the forest of flattery and deceit, ever harboring envy that becomes the very cause of their next rebirth. Gathering greed, hatred, and delusion in abundance, they give rise to all manner of karmic action; the fierce wind of resentment fans the flame of their sinful mind, ever keeping it ablaze; whatever deeds they undertake all accord with inverted views; and, following the outflows of desire, of existence, and of ignorance, they continuously give rise to the very seeds of mind, thought, and consciousness.

Commentary: "Yet ordinary people's minds fall into wrong views" — wrong views are of nine kinds. First, the wrong view of an obscured mind — as the sūtra says, "their discriminating consciousness obscured by the darkness of ignorance and delusion." Second, the wrong view of conceit — as the sūtra says, "constantly raising the banner of conceit." Third, the wrong view of craving — as the sūtra says, "sunk within the net of craving desire." Fourth, the wrong view of a flattering, deceitful mind — as the sūtra says, "following the forest of flattery and deceit." Fifth, the wrong view of envious conduct — as the sūtra says, "ever harboring envy that becomes the very cause of their next rebirth." Sixth, the wrong view of gathering karma — as the sūtra says, "gathering greed, hatred, and delusion in abundance, they give rise to all manner of karmic action." Seventh, the wrong view of a fanned, blazing mind — as the sūtra says, "the fierce wind of resentment fans the flame of their sinful mind, ever keeping it ablaze." Eighth, the wrong view of initiating karma — as the sūtra says, "whatever deeds they undertake all accord with inverted views." Ninth, the wrong view of the seeds of mind, thought, and consciousness — as the sūtra says, "following the outflows of desire, of existence, and of ignorance, they continuously give rise to the very seeds of mind, thought, and consciousness."

Here, the wrong view of an obscured mind, the wrong view of conceit, and the wrong view of craving — these three wrong views proceed in this order through false conception based on the meaning of the Dharma. The wrong view of a flattering, deceitful mind and the wrong view of envious conduct — these two wrong views concern faults of mental conduct at the time of seeking something. Envy gives rise to wrongful conduct toward one's own person; jealousy concerns wealth and possessions — and this is why, in life after life, one falls into a lowly and base condition, one's form and appearance repugnant, one's means of livelihood insufficient. The sixth, the wrong view of gathering karma, concerns hating and desiring the two inverted object-realms at the time of experiencing any feeling. The seventh, the wrong view of a fanned, blazing mind, concerns dwelling again and again on resentment, wishing to initiate retaliatory, evil karma. The eighth, the wrong view of initiating karma, concerns injuring one another in turn at the time of committing evil. The ninth, the wrong view of the seeds of mind, thought, and consciousness, concerns the fact that, even at the time of performing wholesome karma — giving, upholding precepts, cultivating roots of goodness, and the like — all such karma is still tainted with outflow.

Sūtra: Within the ground of the three realms, a sprout further arises, namely name-and-form arising together, never apart from one another; once this name-and-form has grown, it becomes the cluster of the six sense-bases; once the six sense-bases have formed, contact arises from the meeting of the inner and outer; from the condition of contact, feeling arises; from delighting deeply in feeling, craving arises; from the increase of craving, grasping arises; from the increase of grasping, further existence arises again; from the condition of existence, there is birth, old age, death, sorrow, grief, suffering, and affliction — and thus sentient beings give rise to and increase this very cluster of suffering. Within this, all is empty, free of a self and of what belongs to a self, without knowing and without awareness, like grass, trees, stone, or a wall, and also like an echo; and yet sentient beings, unknowing and unaware, undergo suffering and affliction because of it.

Commentary: Within this, causes and conditions are of three kinds: first, their own mark, from "a sprout further arises" all the way to "existence"; second, the shared mark, namely the faults of birth, old age, sickness, and death; and third, the mark of inversion, being free of a self and of what belongs to a self, and the rest. Their own mark is of three kinds. First, the mark of retribution: name-and-form arising together with the ālayavijñāna — as the sūtra says, "within the ground of the three realms, a sprout further arises, namely name-and-form arising together." "Name-and-form arising together" means that name and form arise together with that consciousness. Second, the mark of their cause: this name-and-form is never apart from that consciousness, arising together in dependence on it — as the sūtra says, "never apart from one another." Third, the mark of the sequence of their fruit: from the six sense-bases all the way to existence — as the sūtra says, "once this name-and-form has grown, it becomes the cluster of the six sense-bases; once the six sense-bases have formed, contact arises from the meeting of the inner and outer; from the condition of contact, feeling arises; from delighting deeply in feeling, craving arises; from the increase of craving, grasping arises; from the increase of grasping, further existence arises again; from the condition of existence, there is birth, old age, death, sorrow, grief, suffering, and affliction — and thus sentient beings give rise to and increase this very cluster of suffering." Within this, "free of a self and of what belongs to a self" — these two make manifest emptiness. "Without knowing and without awareness" means that its own essence is without a self. That it is without knowing and without awareness shows that this is not a matter belonging to sentient beings' own moving or unmoving activity. As the sūtra says, "like grass, trees, stone, or a wall, and also like an echo," since it is a dharma resembling and analogous to causes and conditions themselves. What is meant by being caught within these two errors? As the sūtra says, "and yet sentient beings, unknowing and unaware, undergo suffering and affliction because of it."

Sūtra: Seeing sentient beings in this way, never apart from this cluster of suffering, the bodhisattva thereupon gives rise to the wisdom of great compassion; and, reflecting that he himself should teach and transform these very sentient beings, causing them to abide in the ultimate bliss of nirvāṇa, he thereupon gives rise to the wisdom of great loving-kindness.

Commentary: What is meant by being utterly filled with the cluster of suffering, and by being far removed from the supreme bliss of ultimate meaning — this has already been explained above, making manifest great compassion and loving-kindness respectively.

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Huiyuan maps the middle three of the Dharma's nine profundities — emptiness, signlessness, and freedom from aspiration, the classical "three gates of liberation" — onto three specific hindrances they each counteract, rather than treating them as three roughly similar synonyms for the same insight: 三障對治解脫門觀…分別、二相、三取 — "the contemplation of the three gates of liberation, which counteract the three hindrances: discrimination, marks, and grasping." Emptiness counters discrimination itself, the mind's habit of dividing experience into fixed categories; signlessness counters attachment to the false marks discrimination produces; and freedom from aspiration counters the grasping and rejecting that follow once those false marks have been taken for real — 願通兩處：好則願取，惡則願捨, "aspiration reaches toward two things: toward what is pleasing, it aspires to grasp; toward what is displeasing, it aspires to reject." The three gates form a sequence, not three parallel doors to the same room: discrimination generates marks, marks generate grasping and rejecting, and each gate is aimed precisely at severing one specific link in that chain.

On the twelve links of dependent origination compressed into this passage's account of suffering, Huiyuan explains a classification choice the bare text leaves unstated: the first three links (ignorance, karmic formations, and consciousness as their cause) are grouped together with the passage's own opening — "ordinary people's minds fall into wrong views" — into a single unit of cause, 以前三支同是其因，因是倒惑，邪見義同，一處解之 — "because these first three factors are alike in being the cause, and this cause is itself delusion and inversion, sharing the same meaning as 'wrong view,' they are explained together in one place" — while the remaining nine links, from name-and-form onward, are grouped together as fruit, the growth of the suffering already set in motion. He flags a genuine subtlety in how consciousness, specifically, gets classified: depending on which of three ways a source text defines "the link of consciousness," it can count as fruit (the consciousness of the very moment of conception), as both cause and fruit at once (consciousness in the intermediate state, fruit relative to what precedes it and cause relative to what follows), or purely as cause (the seed-consciousness that gives rise to everything after it) — and this treatise, Huiyuan notes, is working from the last of these three readings, treating consciousness as belonging with cause rather than with the nine links of fruit that follow name-and-form.
