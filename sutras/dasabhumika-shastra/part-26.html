The Ten Equal Minds and the Four Noble Truths

The treatise turns to Sudurjayā, the Ground Difficult to Conquer — the Fifth Ground, whose three divisions are named at the outset: the antidote to the pride of excellence, the excellence of the practice of the non-abiding path, and the excellence of its fruit. Vajragarbha names the ten kinds of equal, pure mind that bring the bodhisattva into this ground, who then cultivates the factors of enlightenment yet further through eight modes of according with the practice that accords with suchness. He contemplates the Four Noble Truths — suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path — first as the real dharmas themselves and then as ten truths distinguished by the very sentient beings they teach, understood in both a worldly and a supramundane register. The treatise closes this part with the bodhisattva's realization that all conditioned formations are entirely deceptive and illusory, the great compassion and the light of great loving-kindness this realization brings, and his resolve, through the power of this wisdom, never to abandon any sentient being while ceaselessly seeking the Buddha's own wisdom.

The Three Divisions of the Fifth Ground, and the Ten Kinds of Equal, Pure Mind

Chinese Source

論曰：第五地中分別有三：一、勝慢對治；二、不住道行勝；三、彼果勝。云何勝慢對治？

經曰：爾時金剛藏菩薩言：諸佛子！若菩薩得第四菩薩地善滿諸行已，欲入第五菩薩地，當以十平等深淨心得入第五地。何等為十？一、過去佛法平等深淨心故；二、未來佛法平等深淨心故；三、現在佛法平等深淨心故；四、戒淨平等深淨心故；五、心淨平等深淨心故；六、除見疑悔淨平等深淨心故；七、道非道智淨平等深淨心故；八、行斷智淨平等深淨心故；九、思量一切菩提分法上上淨平等深淨心故；十、化度一切眾生淨平等深淨心故。諸佛子！菩薩以是十種平等深淨心得入第五菩薩地。

論曰：勝慢對治者，謂十平等深淨心，同念不退轉心故。前已說解法慢對治，今此地中說身淨分別慢對治。是中平等深淨心者，於平等中心得清淨。此深淨心分別有十種。十種深淨心者，是諸佛法及隨順諸佛法，彼分別應知。何者是諸佛法？謂三世力等，如經「過去佛法平等深淨心故、未來佛法平等深淨心故、現在佛法平等深淨心故」。如是三世佛法力等已說，次說隨順諸佛法。彼諸佛法云何得成？因戒定智及化眾生故。是中依戒淨，如經「戒淨平等深淨心故」。依定淨，如經「心淨平等深淨心故」。依智淨，如經「除見疑悔淨平等深淨心故、道非道智淨平等深淨心故、行斷智淨平等深淨心故、思量一切菩提分法上上淨平等深淨心故」。是中行斷智者，思量一切菩提分法上上轉勝故，依教化眾生，如經「化度一切眾生淨平等深淨心故」。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: Within the Fifth Ground, the distinctions are three: first, the antidote to the pride of excellence; second, the excellence of the practice of the non-abiding path; and third, the excellence of its fruit. What, then, is the antidote to the pride of excellence?

Sūtra: At that time the bodhisattva Vajragarbha said: Sons of the Buddha! When a bodhisattva, having brought every practice of the Fourth Ground, the bodhisattva ground, to its full completion, wishes to enter the Fifth Ground, the bodhisattva ground, he should, through ten kinds of equal, pure mind, enter into the Fifth Ground. What are the ten? First, the equal, pure mind regarding the buddha-dharma of the past; second, the equal, pure mind regarding the buddha-dharma of the future; third, the equal, pure mind regarding the buddha-dharma of the present; fourth, the equal, pure mind of the purity of moral discipline; fifth, the equal, pure mind of the purity of mind; sixth, the equal, pure mind of the purity that removes wrong views, doubt, and regret; seventh, the equal, pure mind of the purity of the wisdom that discerns the path from what is not the path; eighth, the equal, pure mind of the purity of the wisdom of practice and its cutting off; ninth, the equal, pure mind of the purity, higher and higher still, of the consideration of all the dharmas of the factors of enlightenment; and tenth, the equal, pure mind of the purity of transforming and delivering every sentient being. Sons of the Buddha! A bodhisattva, through these ten kinds of equal, pure mind, is thereby able to enter the Fifth Ground, the bodhisattva ground.

Commentary: "The antidote to the pride of excellence" refers to these ten kinds of equal, pure mind, since they are of a single mindfulness with the mind of non-regression. Earlier, the antidote to the pride of understanding the Dharma was already explained; now, within this ground, the antidote to the pride that discriminates the purity of one's own person is explained. Here, "equal, pure mind" means that, within equality, the mind attains purity. This pure mind is distinguished into ten kinds. As for these ten kinds of pure mind: they concern the buddha-dharmas themselves, and the dharmas that accord with the buddha-dharmas — their distinctions should be understood accordingly. What are the buddha-dharmas themselves? They refer to the powers, and the like, of the three times — as the sūtra says, "the equal, pure mind regarding the buddha-dharma of the past," "the equal, pure mind regarding the buddha-dharma of the future," and "the equal, pure mind regarding the buddha-dharma of the present." Having thus explained the powers and the like of the buddha-dharmas of the three times, next the dharmas that accord with the buddha-dharmas are explained. How are these buddha-dharmas themselves brought to accomplishment? Through the cause of moral discipline, concentration, and wisdom, and through transforming sentient beings. Here, in dependence on the purity of moral discipline — as the sūtra says, "the equal, pure mind of the purity of moral discipline." In dependence on the purity of concentration — as the sūtra says, "the equal, pure mind of the purity of mind." In dependence on the purity of wisdom — as the sūtra says, "the equal, pure mind of the purity that removes wrong views, doubt, and regret," "the equal, pure mind of the purity of the wisdom that discerns the path from what is not the path," "the equal, pure mind of the purity of the wisdom of practice and its cutting off," and "the equal, pure mind of the purity, higher and higher still, of the consideration of all the dharmas of the factors of enlightenment." Here, "the wisdom of practice and its cutting off" means that the consideration of all the dharmas of the factors of enlightenment turns ever more excellent, higher and higher still; and, in dependence on transforming and teaching sentient beings — as the sūtra says, "the equal, pure mind of the purity of transforming and delivering every sentient being."

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang opens his treatment of this ground with an unusually crowded name-etymology section, stacking authority after authority on why 難勝(地) means "Difficult to Conquer." The Vijñaptimātratāsiddhi locates the difficulty in reconciling two kinds of wisdom: 真俗兩智行相互違，合令相應極難勝故 — "the practice-aspects of the wisdom of the ultimate and the wisdom of the conventional oppose one another, and to unite them, bringing them into mutual accord, is extremely difficult to conquer — hence the name." Vasubandhu's own commentary on the Mahāyānasaṃgraha sharpens this into a precise technical claim: 知真諦智是無分別，知世間工巧智是有分別，此二相違應修令合，能合難合令相應故，故名極難勝 — "the wisdom that knows the truth of the ultimate is non-discriminating, while the wisdom that knows the world's own skillful arts is discriminating; these two oppose one another, and yet must be cultivated into union — being able to unite what is difficult to unite, bringing it into accord, is why it is named the Ground Extremely Difficult to Conquer." The *Sūtrālaṃkāra names a different pair of difficulties entirely, social rather than epistemic: 於五地有二種難：一勸化眾生心無惱難、二生不從化心無惱難，此地菩薩能退二難，於難得勝，故名難勝 — "in the Fifth Ground there are two difficulties: first, the difficulty of a mind untroubled while exhorting sentient beings toward transformation; second, the difficulty of a mind untroubled when they arise and yet do not follow that transformation. The bodhisattva of this ground is able to overcome both difficulties, gaining victory over what is difficult — hence it is named Difficult to Conquer."

Further Cultivation Toward the Non-Abiding Path, and the Four Noble Truths in Two Registers

Chinese Source

經曰：諸佛子！是菩薩住第五菩薩地已，善修行菩提分法故、善深淨心故、轉求上勝行故、隨順如道行故、得大願力故、以慈悲心不捨一切眾生故、修習功德智慧行故、不休息諸行故、起方便善巧故、照見上上地故、正受如來加故、得念意去智力故、成就不退轉心故。

論曰：是中善修行菩提分法，乃至隨順如道行者，皆是正修諸行。善修行菩提分法者，第四菩薩地中修菩提分法善清淨深心故，彼深淨心等悕求勝行，如是不住道行勝，破彼慢故。隨順如道行者，彼平等中深淨心不退轉心現成就故，隨彼平等清淨法住，如是菩薩深心安住名為隨順如道行故。是中隨順如道行有八種：一、修習菩提心，如經「得大願力故」。二、不疲惓，如經「以慈悲心不捨一切眾生故」。三、得善根力，如經「修習功德智慧行故」。四、不捨眾行，如經「不休息諸行故」。五、正修行，如經「起方便善巧故」。六、無厭足，如經「照見上上地故」。七、得他勝力，如經「正受如來加故」。八、自身得勝力故、勝念等三慧故，如經「得念意去智力故，成就不退轉心故」。去者，修慧觀，無障礙義故。

論曰：云何不住道行勝？不住道行勝有二種觀：一、所知法中智清淨勝；二、利益眾生勤方便勝。以是二法故，不住世間不住涅槃。云何所知法中智清淨勝？

經曰：是菩薩如實知，是苦聖諦、是苦集諦、是苦滅諦，如實知是至滅苦道聖諦。是菩薩善知世諦、善知第一義諦、善知相諦、善知差別諦、善知說成諦、善知事諦、善知生諦、善知盡無生智諦、善知令入道智諦、善知一切菩薩地次第成就諦，及善知集如來智諦。是菩薩隨眾生意令歡喜故，善知世諦，通達一切法一相故。善知第一義諦，覺法自相同相故。善知相諦，覺法差別故。善知差別諦，覺分別陰界入故。善知說成諦，覺身心苦惱故。善知事諦，覺諸道生相續故。善知生諦，畢竟滅一切熱惱故。善知盡無生智諦，起不二行故。善知令入道智諦，正覺一切法相故。善知一切菩薩地次第成就諦，及善知集如來智諦，以信解力故。知非得一切究竟智知。

論曰：彼所知法中智清淨勝有二種：一、實法分別，如實知四諦，如經「是菩薩如實知苦聖諦乃至如實知至滅苦道聖諦」故。二、化眾生方便差別，十諦差別方便智，如經「是菩薩善知世諦乃至及善知集如來智諦」故。是中實法分別者，有佛無佛苦集二諦果因差別體是妄想染故，滅道二諦果因差別體是淨法故。化眾生方便差別者，所化眾生差別故，方便差別應知。所化眾生有七種，小乘可化有六種：一、為根未熟眾生故，知世諦方便。二、為根熟眾生故，知第一義諦方便。三、為疑惑深法眾生故，知相諦方便。四、為謬解迷惑深法眾生故，知差別諦方便。五、為離正念眾生故，知說成諦方便。六、為正見眾生義故，知事諦方便、知生諦方便、知盡無生智諦方便、知令入道智諦方便。事諦等四諦，苦諦等所攝。七、為大乘可化眾生故，善知一切菩薩地次第成就諦方便及善知集如來智諦方便。如經「是菩薩隨眾生意令歡喜故，善知世諦乃至非得一切究竟智知」故。是中菩薩地次第者，地地中間如自地次第入應知，一切生處身心受苦惱故。知事諦苦者，所有受者皆是苦事故。起不二行者，一行故。正覺一切相者，五明論處善巧知故、信解力故。知者，鏡像觀智力，非成就觀智力故。如是所知法中智清淨勝已說，云何利益眾生勤方便勝？以知一切有為法虛妄相故，起憐愍眾生念。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Sons of the Buddha! This bodhisattva, having dwelt in the Fifth Ground, the bodhisattva ground — because he well cultivates the dharmas of the factors of enlightenment; because his deep, pure mind is well accomplished; because he turns to seek an ever more excellent, supreme practice; because he accords with the practice that accords with suchness; because he attains the power of great vows; because, through a mind of compassion and loving-kindness, he never abandons any sentient being; because he cultivates the practice of merit and wisdom; because he never rests in any of his practices; because he gives rise to skillful and expedient means; because he illumines and beholds the higher grounds still to come; because he rightly receives the empowerment of the Tathāgata; because he attains the power of mindfulness, intention, and the wisdom that goes forth without hindrance; and because he accomplishes the mind of non-regression.

Commentary: Here, from "well cultivates the dharmas of the factors of enlightenment" all the way to "accords with the practice that accords with suchness" — every one of these is the correct cultivation of practice. "Well cultivates the dharmas of the factors of enlightenment" means that, because the factors of enlightenment were cultivated in the Fourth Ground, the bodhisattva ground, the deep mind was well purified; and, because this deep, pure mind and the like aspire to and seek an excellent practice, in just this way the excellence of the practice of the non-abiding path breaks apart that pride. "Accords with the practice that accords with suchness" means that, because the deep, pure mind within equality and the mind of non-regression are manifestly accomplished, and because one abides in accordance with that equal, pure dharma, the bodhisattva's profound mind, thus abiding, is called "according with the practice that accords with suchness." Here, according with the practice that accords with suchness is of eight kinds: first, the cultivation of the mind of enlightenment — as the sūtra says, "because he attains the power of great vows." Second, never growing weary — as the sūtra says, "because, through a mind of compassion and loving-kindness, he never abandons any sentient being." Third, attaining the power of roots of goodness — as the sūtra says, "because he cultivates the practice of merit and wisdom." Fourth, never abandoning any practice — as the sūtra says, "because he never rests in any of his practices." Fifth, correct cultivation and practice — as the sūtra says, "because he gives rise to skillful and expedient means." Sixth, never growing satisfied or weary — as the sūtra says, "because he illumines and beholds the higher grounds still to come." Seventh, attaining the supreme power of another — as the sūtra says, "because he rightly receives the empowerment of the Tathāgata." Eighth, because his own person attains supreme power, and because of the three kinds of supreme wisdom — mindfulness and the rest — as the sūtra says, "because he attains the power of mindfulness, intention, and the wisdom that goes forth without hindrance, and because he accomplishes the mind of non-regression." "Goes forth" means the meaning of the contemplation of cultivated wisdom being without hindrance.

Commentary: What, then, is the excellence of the practice of the non-abiding path? The excellence of the practice of the non-abiding path has two kinds of contemplation: first, the excellence of the purity of wisdom regarding the dharmas that are known; and second, the excellence of the diligent, skillful means that benefits sentient beings. Through these two dharmas, one abides neither in the world nor in nirvāṇa. What, then, is the excellence of the purity of wisdom regarding the dharmas that are known?

Sūtra: This bodhisattva truly and really knows: this is the noble truth of suffering, this is the noble truth of the origin of suffering, this is the noble truth of the cessation of suffering; and he truly and really knows this is the noble truth of the path that leads to the cessation of suffering. This bodhisattva well knows the worldly truth, well knows the truth of ultimate meaning, well knows the truth of characteristics, well knows the truth of distinctions, well knows the truth of what is established through explanation, well knows the truth of matters, well knows the truth of arising, well knows the truth of the wisdom of exhaustion and non-arising, well knows the truth of the wisdom that causes entry into the path, well knows the truth of the sequential accomplishment of every bodhisattva ground, and well knows the truth of the accumulation of the Tathāgata's wisdom. This bodhisattva, so as to gladden the intentions of sentient beings, well knows the worldly truth, since he penetrates the single characteristic of all dharmas. He well knows the truth of ultimate meaning, since he awakens to the self-characteristic and the common characteristic of dharmas. He well knows the truth of characteristics, since he awakens to the distinctions among dharmas. He well knows the truth of distinctions, since he awakens to the discernment of the aggregates, elements, and sense-fields. He well knows the truth of what is established through explanation, since he awakens to the suffering and affliction of body and mind. He well knows the truth of matters, since he awakens to the arising and continuity of the various paths of rebirth. He well knows the truth of arising, since he ultimately extinguishes every feverish affliction. He well knows the truth of the wisdom of exhaustion and non-arising, since he gives rise to the practice of non-duality. He well knows the truth of the wisdom that causes entry into the path, since he correctly awakens to the characteristics of all dharmas. He well knows the truth of the sequential accomplishment of every bodhisattva ground, and well knows the truth of the accumulation of the Tathāgata's wisdom, through the power of faith and understanding. This knowing is not yet the knowing of the ultimate, complete wisdom itself.

Commentary: This excellence of the purity of wisdom regarding the dharmas that are known is of two kinds: first, the discernment of the real dharmas, truly and really knowing the Four Truths — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly and really knows the noble truth of suffering... all the way to truly and really knows the noble truth of the path that leads to the cessation of suffering." Second, the distinctions of the skillful means that transform sentient beings, the wisdom of skillful means distinguished into ten truths — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva well knows the worldly truth... all the way to well knows the truth of the accumulation of the Tathāgata's wisdom." Here, "the discernment of the real dharmas" means that, whether or not a buddha appears in the world, the very substance of the distinct fruit and cause of the two truths of suffering and its origin is the defilement of false conception, while the very substance of the distinct fruit and cause of the two truths of cessation and the path is the pure dharma. "The distinctions of the skillful means that transform sentient beings" means that, because the sentient beings to be transformed differ, the distinctions of skillful means should accordingly be understood. The sentient beings to be transformed are of seven kinds; among them, those who can be transformed by the Lesser Vehicle are of six kinds: first, for the sake of sentient beings whose faculties are not yet ripe, he knows the skillful means of the worldly truth. Second, for the sake of sentient beings whose faculties are ripe, he knows the skillful means of the truth of ultimate meaning. Third, for the sake of sentient beings who doubt the profound Dharma, he knows the skillful means of the truth of characteristics. Fourth, for the sake of sentient beings who mistakenly understand and are confused about the profound Dharma, he knows the skillful means of the truth of distinctions. Fifth, for the sake of sentient beings who have departed from right mindfulness, he knows the skillful means of the truth of what is established through explanation. Sixth, for the sake of the meaning belonging to sentient beings of right view, he knows the skillful means of the truth of matters, the skillful means of the truth of arising, the skillful means of the truth of the wisdom of exhaustion and non-arising, and the skillful means of the truth of the wisdom that causes entry into the path — the truth of matters and these four truths are subsumed under the truth of suffering and the rest. Seventh, for the sake of sentient beings who can be transformed by the Great Vehicle, he well knows the skillful means of the truth of the sequential accomplishment of every bodhisattva ground, and well knows the skillful means of the truth of the accumulation of the Tathāgata's wisdom. As the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, so as to gladden the intentions of sentient beings, well knows the worldly truth... all the way to this knowing is not yet the knowing of the ultimate, complete wisdom itself." Here, "the sequential accomplishment of the bodhisattva grounds" means that, between each ground and the next, one should understand entry as proceeding in the sequence proper to one's own ground, since in every place of birth body and mind receive suffering and affliction. "Knowing the suffering of the truth of matters" means that whatever is experienced is entirely a matter of suffering. "Gives rise to the practice of non-duality" means that it is a single practice. "Correctly awakens to every characteristic" means that he skillfully knows the domain of the five sciences, through the power of faith and understanding. "Knows" refers to the power of the wisdom that contemplates as a mirror-image, not yet the power of the wisdom of contemplation fully accomplished. The excellence of the purity of wisdom regarding the dharmas that are known has thus been explained. What, then, is the excellence of the diligent, skillful means that benefits sentient beings? It is that, through knowing the deceptive characteristic of all conditioned dharmas, one gives rise to a thought of compassion for sentient beings.

Reading note — after Fazang

On why the Fourth Ground's factors of enlightenment must be settled before the Fifth Ground's Four Noble Truths can even begin, Fazang's reason-for-arising section gives a clean answer: 前地雖得出世，而未能隨順世間五明攝化，今此地中修證彼法，是故次來 — "though the earlier ground already attained the transcendent, it was not yet able to accord with the world's five sciences in order to gather and transform beings; this ground now cultivates and realizes that very dharma, and this is why it follows next in sequence." He also distinguishes this ground's own tenfold equal mind from the equal mind of the sixth ground and beyond: 以前地中取淨法為慢故，此地中偏觀淨法平等、後地通觀染淨平等 — "because the previous ground grasped the pure dharma as a basis for pride, this ground specifically contemplates the equality of the pure dharma alone, while the later ground contemplates, universally, the equality of both defilement and purity together."

On the sevenfold purities that make up the ten equal minds, Fazang notes a genuine parting of ways between the two vehicles over the last two items: 小乘中六名行淨、七名行斷淨，以彼宗趣盡滅故；大乘中六名行斷、七名思量菩提分法上上淨，以此宗依其行斷起勝求故 — "in the Lesser Vehicle, the sixth is named the purity of practice and the seventh the purity of practice's cutting off, since that tradition's own aim is utter extinction; in the Great Vehicle, the sixth is named practice's cutting off and the seventh the purity, higher and higher still, of the consideration of the dharmas of the factors of enlightenment, since this tradition, in dependence on that very cutting off, gives rise to an ever more excellent seeking." The same ten items, in other words, name a terminus for one vehicle and a launching point for the other.

All Conditioned Formations as Deceptive and Illusory, and the Resolve Never to Abandon Any Being

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩如是善起諸諦智已，如實知一切有為行皆是虛妄誑詐誑惑凡夫。菩薩爾時復於眾生中大悲轉勝而現在前，及生大慈光明。

論曰：是菩薩如是善起諸諦智已，乃至及生大慈光明者，是中妄想常等不相似無故虛，常作我想慢事故妄、世法盡壞故誑、世法牽取愚癡凡夫故詐、常等相無非有似有故虛、事中意正取我想慢事正取故妄、事是患世法利盡故誑、事牽心世法愚癡凡夫牽取故詐。事相現故言誑惑凡夫，此諸句義應知。凡夫者，依止彼正取我慢身。大悲大慈者，憐愍彼眾生。勝利益示現勝彼前地悲故，言大悲不住，道行勝故。救眾生方便智成就故，言大慈光明。

經曰：是菩薩得如是智慧力，不捨一切眾生，常求佛智慧，如實觀一切有為行先際後際，知諸眾生從先際無明有愛故生，流轉世間歸五陰家，不能動發，增長苦陰聚，是中無我、無壽命、無眾生，離我我所，皆如實知。後際亦如是。此無所有虛妄貪著分段盡出有無，皆如實知。

論曰：是菩薩得如是智慧力者，如前說。不捨一切眾生者，大悲大慈光明亦如前說。求佛智慧者，救一切眾生義故。如實觀一切有為行先際後際者，隨彼大悲觀示二種相：一、如實觀苦因緣集故，如經「知諸眾生從先際無明有愛故生，乃至有無皆如實知」故。是中從先際無明有愛故生者，此顯凡夫生，非菩薩生，菩薩以善巧方便生。何故不言餘因緣分？無明有愛是有分根本故。彼生者，說有三種眾生：欲求眾生、妄梵行求眾生、有求眾生，乃至依有頂五陰苦聚。是無我事，是中自身無我。及彼無我事，第一義故無。然後無我依命根力住數數受生，眾生身心相續非常非斷故，說有命有眾生；破彼慢取意故，說無命無眾生；遠離能取所取我慢意故，說離我我所前際。以何因隨所有眾生、隨所有苦行？彼正觀已，後際亦如是，隨彼苦因。無明愛事盡者名滅，勝世間滅故。出者名道。無亦如實知，有亦如實知，是名一種大悲正觀因緣集念如實苦故。次說第二種大悲正觀深重苦無量世隨逐及種種苦事。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, having thus well given rise to the wisdom of these truths, truly and really knows that all conditioned formations are entirely deceptive, illusory, and false, deceiving and confusing ordinary beings. The bodhisattva, at that time, moreover gives rise, before all sentient beings, to a great compassion turned ever more excellent, manifest before him, and gives rise as well to the light of great loving-kindness.

Commentary: "This bodhisattva, having thus well given rise to the wisdom of these truths... all the way to gives rise as well to the light of great loving-kindness" — here, the false conceptions of permanence and the like, being dissimilar to what has no real existence, are hence "empty"; the constant generation of the notion of self, and the matter of pride, are hence "deluded"; because worldly dharmas are utterly destroyed, they are hence "deceptive"; because worldly dharmas drag along and ensnare foolish, ignorant, ordinary beings, they are hence "fraudulent." The characteristic of permanence and the like, though without real existence, resembles existence as if it were there, and is hence "empty"; within these matters, the mind rightly grasps at the notion of self and the matter of pride, taking hold of it directly, and this is hence "deluded"; these matters are themselves an affliction, since worldly benefit is exhausted, and this is hence "deceptive"; these matters drag the mind, and worldly dharmas draw in and ensnare foolish, ignorant, ordinary beings, and this is hence "fraudulent." Because the characteristic of these matters is manifest, it is said, "deceiving and confusing ordinary beings" — the meaning of all these clauses should thus be understood. "Ordinary beings" means those who rely upon and directly grasp at a body conceived through pride in self. "Great compassion" and "great loving-kindness" mean having pity and compassion for these sentient beings. This manifests a supreme benefit surpassing the compassion of the earlier grounds — hence it is called "great compassion," because of the excellence of the non-abiding practice of the path. Because the skillful, expedient wisdom that rescues sentient beings is accomplished, it is called "the light of great loving-kindness."

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, having attained this power of wisdom, never abandons any sentient being, and constantly seeks the Buddha's own wisdom, truly and really contemplating all conditioned formations across the prior limit and the later limit. He knows that sentient beings, from the prior limit onward, are born on account of ignorance and craving, and, turning and flowing on within the world, return again and again to the household of the five aggregates, unable to be shaken loose from it, ever increasing the mass of suffering aggregates; and, within this, there is no self, no life-span, and no sentient being — he truly and really knows the departure from self and from what belongs to self. The later limit is likewise so. This — having nothing whatsoever, deceptive, illusory, and clung to in craving, the exhaustion of these segments of birth and death, and their going forth into existence and non-existence — he truly and really knows all of it.

Commentary: "This bodhisattva, having attained this power of wisdom" is as already explained before. "Never abandons any sentient being" — the great compassion and the light of great loving-kindness are likewise as already explained before. "Seeks the Buddha's own wisdom" refers to the meaning of rescuing every sentient being. "Truly and really contemplates all conditioned formations across the prior limit and the later limit" — in accordance with that great compassion, the contemplation displays two aspects: first, the true and real contemplation of the arising of suffering through causes and conditions — as the sūtra says, "he knows that sentient beings, from the prior limit onward, are born on account of ignorance and craving... all the way to he truly and really knows all of it, both existence and non-existence." Here, "born, from the prior limit onward, on account of ignorance and craving" makes manifest the birth of ordinary beings, not the birth of a bodhisattva — a bodhisattva is born through skillful and expedient means. Why does it not speak of the other portions among the causes and conditions? Because ignorance and craving are the very root of the portion of continued existence. As for that birth: it speaks of three kinds of sentient beings — sentient beings who seek desire, sentient beings who falsely seek the brahma-conduct, and sentient beings who seek continued existence, extending all the way to the mass of suffering aggregates that depend upon the very summit of existence. This is the matter of there being no self: here, one's own person has no self. And, as for that matter of no-self, it is, by the meaning of the ultimate, truly without self. Yet, after this no-self, in dependence on the power of the life-faculty, one abides and repeatedly receives birth; and, because the body and mind of sentient beings continue in succession, being neither permanent nor cut off, it is said that there is a life-span and there is a sentient being; in order to break apart that intention of prideful grasping, it is said that there is no life-span and no sentient being; in order to depart far from the intention of the pride of self involved in the grasper and the grasped, it is said, regarding the prior limit, "the departure from self and from what belongs to self." By what cause does one accord with whatever sentient beings there are, and accord with whatever practice of suffering there is? Once that right contemplation is complete, the later limit is likewise so, according with that cause of suffering. The exhaustion of the matter of ignorance and craving is called "cessation," because it surpasses the cessation of the world; "going forth" is called "the path." Non-existence he truly and really knows, and existence he truly and really knows as well — this is called the one kind of great compassion's right contemplation, mindful of the arising of causes and conditions, in accordance with the truth of suffering. Next will be explained the second kind of great compassion's right contemplation — the deep and heavy suffering that pursues one across boundless lifetimes, and its manifold matters of suffering.

Reading note — after Fazang

The treatise's own eightfold litany of "empty, deluded, deceptive, fraudulent" reads as a single undifferentiated list, but Fazang shows it is actually two parallel fourfold analyses, one turned inward and one turned outward: 虛偽者，觀內五蘊，謂妄想常等，不相似無故虛，此明所取非實也；常作我想，慢事故偽，此顯能取不真 — "'empty' and 'deluded' concern the contemplation of the five aggregates within: false conceptions of permanence and the like, being dissimilar to what has no real existence, are 'empty' — this makes manifest that what is grasped is not real; the constant generation of the notion of self, the matter of pride, is 'deluded' — this reveals that the grasper itself is not genuine." The outward half then mirrors it exactly: 誑詐者，觀外六塵，謂世法盡壞故誑，此明所取不實，但是誑也 — "'deceptive' and 'fraudulent' concern the contemplation of the six external dusts: because worldly dharmas are utterly destroyed, they are 'deceptive' — this makes manifest that what is grasped is not substantial, but merely a deception."

On why the sūtra can say, in the same breath, both that there is a sentient being and that there is none, Fazang traces the term itself back to its Sanskrit root: 數數受生，釋眾生也，謂眾多生死是眾生義……以梵名補特伽羅，此云數取趣，即數數取諸趣受生故也 — "'repeatedly receiving birth' glosses 'sentient being,' since the meaning of 'sentient being' is precisely one who undergoes birth-and-death again and again... in Sanskrit this is called pudgala, rendered here as 'one who repeatedly takes hold of the destinies' — that is, one who again and again takes hold of the various destinies and receives birth within them." The conventional person, on this reading, is named for nothing more than the repetition of the taking itself — which is exactly why the sūtra can deny any self within it while still, in the very same passage, affirming a sentient being who is born.

Part 26 Complete

This closes Part 26: the three divisions of Sudurjayā, the Ground Difficult to Conquer — the antidote to the pride of excellence, the excellence of the practice of the non-abiding path, and the excellence of its fruit — named at the outset; Vajragarbha's ten kinds of equal, pure mind that bring the bodhisattva into this ground; his further cultivation of the factors of enlightenment through eight modes of according with the practice that accords with suchness; his deep contemplation of the Four Noble Truths, first as the real dharmas themselves and then as ten truths distinguished by the sentient beings they teach, understood in both a worldly and a supramundane register; and, closing this part, his realization that all conditioned formations are deceptive and illusory, the great compassion and the light of great loving-kindness this realization brings, and his resolve, through the power of this wisdom, never to abandon any sentient being while ceaselessly seeking the Buddha's own wisdom. Part 27 continues within the excellence of the practice of the non-abiding path, turning to the bodhisattva's astonished compassion at the suffering of ordinary beings, a taxonomy of beings orphaned and without any refuge, his vow to rescue every one of them, and his mastery of ten kinds of unforgetting wisdom joined to a tireless, unremitting practice.
