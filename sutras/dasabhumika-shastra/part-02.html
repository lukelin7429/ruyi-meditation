Empowerment of Speech, Mind, and Body — and the Ten Grounds Named

The buddhas complete Vajragarbha's empowerment through speech, mind, and body, touching his crown so that he rises from samādhi — and, before anyone has asked him to, briefly names and glosses all ten bodhisattva grounds for the first time, from Pramuditā, the Ground of Joy, to Dharmameghā, the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud, comparing them to the ten periods a child passes through before birth.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 2 of 52 complete Source: Taishō T1522, 十地經論 (Vasubandhu, tr. Bodhiruci et al., Northern Wei) Reading notes after Jingying Huiyuan's 十地經論義記 (X0753)

The Section of Empowerment (II): Speech, Mind, and Body

Chinese Source

經曰：復次善男子！汝當辯說此諸法門差別方便法故、承諸佛神力如來智明加故、自善根清淨故、法界淨故、饒益眾生界故、法身智身故、正受一切佛位故、得一切世間最高大身故、過一切世間道故、出世間法道清淨故、得一切智人智滿足故。

論曰：此十句中，辯才者，隨所得法義憶持不忘說故。諸法門者，謂十地法。差別者，種種名相故。此法善巧成，是故名方便。依根本辯才有二種辯才：一者他力辯才；二者自力辯才。他力辯才者，承佛神力故。云何承佛神力？如來智力不闇加故，如經「承諸佛神力如來智明加」故。自力辯才者有四種：一者有作善法淨辯才，如經「自善根清淨」故；二者無作法淨辯才，如經「法界淨」故；三者化眾生淨辯才，如經「饒益眾生界」故；四者身淨辯才，是身淨中顯三種盡：一者菩薩盡有二種利益、二者聲聞辟支佛不同盡、三者佛盡。菩薩盡者，法身離心意識，唯智依止，如經「法身智身」故。二種利益者，現報利益，受佛位故；後報利益，摩醯首羅智處生故。如經「正受一切佛位」故，「得一切世間最高大身」故。二乘不同盡者，度五道復涅槃道淨故，如經「過一切世間道」故，「出世間法道清淨」故。佛盡者，入一切智智滿足故，如經「得一切智人智滿足」故，「自力辯才校量轉勝上上」故。已說口加，云何意加？

經曰：爾時諸佛與金剛藏菩薩真實無畏身、與無障礙樂說辯才、與善淨智差別入、與善憶念不忘加、與善決定意方便、與遍至一切智處、與諸佛不壞力、與如來無所畏不怯弱、與一切智人智無礙分別法正見、與一切如來善分別身口意莊嚴起故。

論曰：此十句意加。無畏身者有二種：一者與無上勝威德身，如王處眾自在無畏；二者與辯才無畏身，前色身勝、後名身勝。是名身有九種：一者不著辯才，說法不斷無滯礙故，如經「與無障礙樂說辯才」故。二者堪辯才，善淨堪智有四種：一者緣、二者法、三者作、四者成，善知此義成不成相故，如經「與善淨智差別入」故。

三者任放辯才，說不待次、言辭不斷、處處隨意、不忘名義故，如經「與善憶念不忘加」故，「是不忘加意力加」故。四者能說辯才，隨所應度，種種譬喻能斷疑故，如經「與善決定意方便」故。

五者不雜辯才，三種同相智常現前故，如經「與遍至一切智處」故。六者教出辯才，得佛十力，不壞於可度者令斷煩惱故，如經「與諸佛不壞力」故。七者不畏辯才，得佛決定，無畏於他，言說不怯弱故，如經「與如來無所畏不怯弱」故。八者無量辯才，於一切智隨順宣說修多羅等法六種正見故，如經「與一切智人智無礙分別法正見」故。九者同化辯才，得一切佛無畏身等三種教化，隨所度者顯示殊勝三業神化故，如經「與一切如來善分別身口意莊嚴起」故。又諸佛有力有慈悲，何故以十種無畏身唯加金剛藏而不加餘者？

經曰：何以故？以得菩薩大乘光明三昧法故，亦是菩薩本願起故、善淨深心故、善淨智圓滿故、善集助道法故、善修本業故、念持無量法故、信解清淨光明法故、善得陀羅尼門不壞故、法界智印善印故。

論曰：以是菩薩得大乘光明三昧法，餘者不得故。得三昧法有二種：一者本願成就現前故，如經「亦是菩薩本願起」故；二者三昧身攝功德故。此三昧身攝功德有八種，依自利利他故。一者因淨，深心趣菩薩地，盡清淨故，如經「善淨深心」故。深心者，信樂等，復是一切善法根本故。二者智淨，趣菩薩地，盡修道真如觀智故，如經「善淨智圓滿」故。此真如觀內智圓滿普照法界，猶如日輪光遍世界故。三者身轉淨，生生轉勝善行成滿故，如經「善集助道法」故。四者心調伏淨，善斷煩惱習故，如經「善修本業」故。

五者聞攝淨，堪能受持一切如來所說祕密法故，如經「念持無量法」故。六者通淨，得勝通自在故，如經「信解清淨光明法」故，「以決定信力攝取通」故。七者辯才淨，善知陀羅尼門不相違故，如經「善得陀羅尼門不壞」故。於中所有初章字者是陀羅尼門，一一字門攝持無量名句字身故，不壞者前後不相違故。八者離慢淨，謂真實智教授不異故，如經「法界智印善印」故。於中三昧身攝功德有四種依自利因：善淨深心故、善淨智圓滿故、善集助道法故、善修本業故。此修多羅中四句次第說：精進因、不忘因、勢力因、彼不染因。復依利他因有四種：念持無量法故，斷疑因；信解清淨光明法故，敬重因；以神通力示現不思議處，令諸見者決定信入故，善得陀羅尼門不壞故，轉法理因；法若壞時假餘尊法誦持故，法界智印善印故，教授出離因。如是化者，得自利不忘故。已說意加，云何身加？摩頂覺故。

經曰：爾時十方諸佛，不離本處以神通力皆申右手，善摩金剛藏菩薩摩訶薩頂。

論曰：不離本處而摩此者，顯示殊勝神力，若來此處則非奇異。是如意通力，非餘通等。已說加分，云何起分？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: "Furthermore, good son — we grant you this, so that you may expound the distinctions and skillful means of this Dharma-gate; so that you may be empowered by the sustaining spiritual power of the buddhas and the clarity of the Tathāgatas' wisdom; so that your own root of goodness may be purified; so that the dharma-realm may be purified within you; so that you may bring benefit to the realm of sentient beings; so that you may have the dharma-body and the wisdom-body; so that you may properly receive every buddha's station; so that you may attain the highest, greatest body in every world; so that you may transcend every path of the world; so that the path of the supramundane dharma may be purified within you; and so that you may fulfill the wisdom of the one who knows all."

Commentary: Among these ten clauses, "eloquence" means that, in accordance with whatever meaning of the Dharma one has attained, one holds it in mind without forgetting it and is able to speak of it. "This Dharma-gate" means the dharma of the ten grounds. "Distinctions" means its various names and characteristics. Because this Dharma has been skillfully brought to completion, it is therefore called a skillful means. Based on the fundamental eloquence, there are two kinds of eloquence: first, eloquence through another's power; second, eloquence through one's own power. Eloquence through another's power means being sustained by the Buddha's spiritual power. What is meant by being sustained by the Buddha's spiritual power? That the power of the Tathāgatas' wisdom empowers one without any obscurity — as the sūtra says, "so that you may be empowered by the sustaining spiritual power of the buddhas and the clarity of the Tathāgatas' wisdom." Eloquence through one's own power is of four kinds. First, the eloquence of the purity of contrived wholesome dharmas — as the sūtra says, "so that your own root of goodness may be purified." Second, the eloquence of the purity of the uncontrived dharma — as the sūtra says, "so that the dharma-realm may be purified within you." Third, the eloquence of purity in transforming sentient beings — as the sūtra says, "so that you may bring benefit to the realm of sentient beings." Fourth, the eloquence of the purity of the body — and within this purity of the body, three kinds of exhaustive attainment are made manifest: first, a bodhisattva's exhaustive attainment, which has two kinds of benefit; second, the exhaustive attainment not shared with śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas; and third, a buddha's exhaustive attainment. As for a bodhisattva's exhaustive attainment: the dharma-body is free of mind, thought, and consciousness, resting only on wisdom — as the sūtra says, "the dharma-body and the wisdom-body." As for the two kinds of benefit: the benefit of present retribution is receiving a buddha's station; the benefit of future retribution is being born in the domain of Maheśvara's wisdom — as the sūtra says, "so that you may properly receive every buddha's station," and "so that you may attain the highest, greatest body in every world." As for the exhaustive attainment not shared with the two vehicles: it is crossing beyond the five destinies and further purifying the path of nirvāṇa — as the sūtra says, "so that you may transcend every path of the world," and "so that the path of the supramundane dharma may be purified within you." As for a buddha's exhaustive attainment: it is entering the fulfillment of the wisdom of omniscience — as the sūtra says, "so that you may fulfill the wisdom of the one who knows all," and "so that eloquence through one's own power, thus compared and measured, becomes ever more excellent, higher and higher." This concludes the empowerment of speech; what is the empowerment of mind?

Sūtra: At that time, the buddhas granted Vajragarbha Bodhisattva a body of true fearlessness; unobstructed and joyous eloquence; the entering of skillful discernment through pure wisdom; skillful mindfulness that never forgets; skillful means of resolute mind; the reaching of the domain of omniscience everywhere; the buddhas' unbreakable power; the Tathāgatas' fearlessness and lack of timidity; the correct view that discerns the Dharma without obstruction, belonging to the wisdom of the one who knows all; and the arising of every Tathāgata's skillful discernment, adorning body, speech, and mind alike.

Commentary: These ten clauses are the empowerment of mind. There are two kinds of fearless body: first, being granted a body of unsurpassed, supreme majestic virtue, like a king dwelling among his subjects, self-mastering and fearless; second, being granted a fearless body of eloquence — the former is the superiority of the physical body, the latter the superiority of the body of names. This latter, the body of names, is of nine kinds. First, unattached eloquence, because one speaks the Dharma without break and without any obstruction — as the sūtra says, "unobstructed and joyous eloquence." Second, capable eloquence: skillful, pure, capable wisdom is of four kinds — condition, dharma, action, and accomplishment — knowing well whether each of these is accomplished or not — as the sūtra says, "the entering of skillful discernment through pure wisdom."

Third, unrestrained eloquence, because one speaks without waiting for any fixed order, one's words never break off, one acts freely everywhere, and one never forgets the names and meanings — as the sūtra says, "skillful mindfulness that never forgets," this being "the empowerment of never forgetting, the empowerment of mental power." Fourth, capable-of-explaining eloquence, because, in accordance with whomever is to be brought across, one is able to cut off doubt through all manner of illustrations — as the sūtra says, "skillful means of resolute mind."

Fifth, unmixed eloquence, because the wisdom of the three kinds of sameness-marks is forever present before one — as the sūtra says, "the reaching of the domain of omniscience everywhere." Sixth, teaching-forth eloquence, because, having attained the buddhas' ten powers, one is unbreakable with respect to those who can be brought across, causing them to cut off affliction — as the sūtra says, "the buddhas' unbreakable power." Seventh, fearless eloquence, because, having attained the buddhas' resoluteness, one is fearless before others, one's speech never timid — as the sūtra says, "the Tathāgatas' fearlessness and lack of timidity." Eighth, boundless eloquence, because, in accordance with omniscience, one expounds the sūtras and other teachings with six kinds of correct view — as the sūtra says, "the correct view that discerns the Dharma without obstruction, belonging to the wisdom of the one who knows all." Ninth, eloquence of shared transformation, because, having attained every buddha's fearless body and the three kinds of teaching and transformation, one displays, for whomever is to be brought across, the supreme, wondrous transformation of the three karmic activities — as the sūtra says, "the arising of every Tathāgata's skillful discernment, adorning body, speech, and mind alike." And yet, since every buddha has power and has compassion, why is it that these ten kinds of fearless body are granted only to Vajragarbha, and to no other?

Sūtra: Why is this? Because it is he who has attained the bodhisattva samādhi of the Light of the Great Vehicle — also because it arises from a bodhisattva's original vow; because his deep mind is well purified; because his wisdom is well purified and perfected; because he has well gathered the dharmas that assist the path; because he has well cultivated the fundamental practice; because he holds boundless dharmas in mind; because he has faith and understanding in the pure, radiant Dharma; because he has well attained the unbreakable gate of dhāraṇī; and because the seal of the wisdom of the dharma-realm is well imprinted in him.

Commentary: It is because this bodhisattva alone has attained the dharma of the samādhi of the Light of the Great Vehicle, and no other has attained it. Attaining the dharma of this samādhi is of two kinds: first, because the original vow has been accomplished and now stands manifest before him — as the sūtra says, "it arises from a bodhisattva's original vow"; second, because of the merit gathered within the body of this samādhi. This merit gathered within the samādhi-body is of eight kinds, based on both self-benefit and benefiting others. First, purity of cause: because, advancing toward the bodhisattva ground, his deep mind is purified to completion — as the sūtra says, "his deep mind is well purified." "Deep mind" means faith and delight and the like, which are, further, the root of every wholesome dharma. Second, purity of wisdom: because, advancing toward the bodhisattva ground, the wisdom of the contemplation of suchness through the cultivated path is perfected — as the sūtra says, "his wisdom is well purified and perfected." This inner wisdom of the contemplation of suchness, perfected, universally illumines the dharma-realm, just as the light of the sun's disk pervades the whole world. Third, purity in the turning of the body: because, from lifetime to lifetime, ever more excellent wholesome practice is brought to completion — as the sūtra says, "he has well gathered the dharmas that assist the path." Fourth, purity in the taming of the mind: because he has well cut off the habituated tendencies of affliction — as the sūtra says, "he has well cultivated the fundamental practice."

Fifth, purity in gathering through hearing: because he is capable of upholding every secret dharma spoken by the Tathāgatas — as the sūtra says, "he holds boundless dharmas in mind." Sixth, purity of spiritual power: because he has attained mastery of supreme spiritual powers — as the sūtra says, "he has faith and understanding in the pure, radiant Dharma," and "through the power of resolute faith, he gathers in spiritual power." Seventh, purity of eloquence: because he well knows that the gates of dhāraṇī are never in mutual contradiction — as the sūtra says, "he has well attained the unbreakable gate of dhāraṇī." Here, whatever constitutes an initial syllable is a gate of dhāraṇī; each and every syllable-gate holds within it boundless names, phrases, and bodies of letters — and "unbreakable" means that what comes before and after is never in mutual contradiction. Eighth, purity of freedom from conceit: because the teaching of true wisdom is never inconsistent — as the sūtra says, "the seal of the wisdom of the dharma-realm is well imprinted." Here, of the merit gathered within the samādhi-body, four kinds depend on the cause of self-benefit: his deep mind is well purified; his wisdom is well purified and perfected; he has well gathered the dharmas that assist the path; he has well cultivated the fundamental practice. This sūtra speaks of these four clauses in order as: the cause of diligence, the cause of never forgetting, the cause of gathered power, and the cause of being unstained by them. Further, depending on the cause of benefiting others, there are four kinds: holding boundless dharmas in mind is the cause of cutting off doubt; having faith and understanding in the pure, radiant Dharma is the cause of reverence; through spiritual power manifesting inconceivable realms, causing all who see them to enter with resolute faith, and having well attained the unbreakable gate of dhāraṇī, is the cause of turning the principle of the Dharma; because, should the Dharma ever be on the verge of being lost, one is able to rely on other venerable dharmas to recite and uphold it, the seal of the wisdom of the dharma-realm being well imprinted is the cause of teaching liberation. Through transformations such as these, he attains self-benefit that is never forgotten. This concludes the empowerment of mind; what is the empowerment of body? It is the touching of the crown of his head so that he awakens.

Sūtra: At that time, the buddhas of the ten directions, without leaving their own abodes, extended their right hands through spiritual power and gently touched the crown of Vajragarbha Bodhisattva-mahāsattva's head.

Commentary: That they touch him thus without leaving their own abodes makes manifest their supreme spiritual power; had they come to this place in person, it would be nothing extraordinary. This is the power of the spiritual ability of wish-fulfillment, not any other kind of spiritual ability. This concludes the section of empowerment; what is the section of arising?

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Huiyuan notes that the three modes of empowerment are not interchangeable, and reads their fixed order — speech, then mind, then body — off what each concretely accomplishes: 口則勸說以增力，意則冥被以加威，身則摩頂而令覺 — "empowerment of speech exhorts him to speak, thereby increasing his strength; empowerment of mind covers him invisibly, thereby adding to his majesty; empowerment of body touches his crown, thereby causing him to awaken and rise from samādhi." Because the buddhas have just finished explaining why they empower him at all, speech comes first as the direct continuation of that exhortation; because touching the crown is what will make him rise from samādhi, body comes last as the natural close; mind, having no comparably obvious narrative anchor, simply falls in the middle. Huiyuan also carries the "eloquence through another's power / eloquence through one's own power" division he uses to structure the ten clauses of speech-empowerment straight over into the twenty clauses of mind-empowerment — the first ten of each set showing power received from without, the second showing what that received power actually enables Vajragarbha himself to do — a parallel the bare sūtra text leaves the reader to notice unaided.

On why the buddhas touch Vajragarbha's crown "without leaving their own abodes" rather than manifesting bodily in the assembly to do so, Huiyuan treats the detail as the entire point rather than incidental staging: 顯示殊勝神力，若來此處則非奇異 — "it makes manifest their supreme spiritual power; had they come to this place in person, it would be nothing extraordinary." He reasons that an ordinary manifestation, however impressive, would still be a familiar kind of miracle; touching a bodhisattva's crown from world-systems away, without moving at all, is a different order of demonstration entirely — precisely because it could not be mistaken for mere presence. He is careful to specify which of the four kinds of spiritual power this is — 是如意通力，非餘通等 — "this is the power of the spiritual ability of wish-fulfillment, not any other kind" — distinguishing it from the ability to transform external things, the ability to reason without obstruction through the Dharma, or the ability to transmute one's own experience of pleasure and pain, none of which this particular gesture is meant to illustrate.

The Section of Arising, and the Section of the Outline

Chinese Source

經曰：諸佛摩金剛藏菩薩頂已，爾時金剛藏菩薩即從三昧起。

論曰：即從三昧起者，以三昧事訖故。又得勝力說時，復至定無言說故。已說起分，云何本分？

經曰：起三昧已告諸菩薩言：諸佛子！是諸菩薩願善決定無雜不可見，廣大如法界，究竟如虛空，盡未來際覆護一切眾生界。佛子！是諸菩薩乃能入過去諸佛智地，乃能入未來諸佛智地，乃能入現在諸佛智地。諸佛子！此菩薩十地是過去未來現在諸佛已說今說當說，我因是事故如是說。何等為十？一名歡喜地；二名離垢地；三名明地；四名焰地；五名難勝地；六名現前地；七名遠行地；八名不動地；九名善慧地；十名法雲地。諸佛子！此菩薩十地，過去未來現在諸佛已說今說當說。佛子！我不見有諸佛世界是諸如來不歎說此菩薩十地者。何以故？此是菩薩摩訶薩增上勝妙法故，亦是菩薩光明法門，所謂分別十地事。諸佛子！是事不可思議，所謂菩薩摩訶薩諸地智慧。

論曰：何故不請而說？若不自說，眾則不知為說不說，又復不知欲說何法。願善決定者，如初地中說發菩提心，即此本分中願應知。

善決定者，真實智攝故善；決定者即是善決定。此已入初地，非信地所攝。此善決定有六種：一者觀相善決定，真如觀一味相故，如經「無雜」故。二者真實善決定，非一切世間境界出世間故，如經「不可見」故。三者勝善決定，大法界故、一切佛根本故，如經「廣大如法界」故。大、勝、高、廣，一體異名，法相義故，一切法法爾故。復法界大真如觀，勝諸凡夫二乘智等，淨法法爾故。復法界大方便集地，謂說大乘法法爾故。復法界大白法界，善法法爾故。四者因善決定有二種：一成無常愛果因善決定。是因如虛空，依是生諸色，色不盡故。如經「究竟如虛空」故。二常果因善決定，得涅槃道，如經「盡未來際」故。五者大善決定，隨順作利益他行，如經「覆護一切眾生界」故。次前善決定，此願世間涅槃中非一向住故。六者不怯弱善決定，入一切諸佛智地不怯弱故，如經「佛子是諸菩薩乃至入現在諸佛智地」故。復此十地生成佛智住持故，如經「諸佛子此菩薩十地是過去未來現在諸佛已說今說當說」故。於中善決定者是總相，餘者是別相。同相者善決定，異相者別相故。成相者是略說，壞相者廣說故，如世界成壞。何故定說菩薩十地？對治十種障故。何者十障？一者凡夫我相障；二者邪行於眾生身等障；三者闇相於聞思修等諸法忘障；四者解法慢障；五者身淨我慢障；六者微煩惱習障；七者細相習障；八者於無相有行障；九者不能善利益眾生障；十者於諸法中不得自在障。何故十地初名歡喜乃至十名法雲？成就無上自利利他行，初證聖處多生歡喜，故名歡喜地。離能起誤心犯戒煩惱垢等，清淨戒具足，故名離垢地。

隨聞思修等照法顯現，故名明地。不忘煩惱薪智火能燒，故名焰地。得出世間智方便善巧，能度難度，故名難勝地。般若波羅蜜行有間大智現前，故名現前地。善修無相行功用，究竟能過世間二乘出世間道，故名遠行地。報行純熟無相無間，故名不動地。無礙力說法，成就利他行，故名善慧地。得大法身具足自在，故名法雲地。如是受法王位，猶如太子於諸王子而得自在，是處有微智障故不自在，對治此障故說佛地。又如懷孕在藏，菩薩十地亦復如是，以諸地有障故。如子生時，佛時亦爾，事究竟故。又如生時諸根覺了，佛亦如是，於一切境界智明了故。藏有十時：一者陀羅婆身時；二者捭羅婆身時；三者尸羅他身時；四者堅身時；五者形相似色身時；六者性相似身時；七者業動身時；八者滿足身時，於中有三種：根滿足時、男女相別滿足時、廣長諸相滿足時。如是十時諸地相似故。佛子我不見有諸佛世界是諸如來不歎說此菩薩十地者，顯此勝法，為令時眾增渴仰故。佛世界者，於中成佛，喻如稻田。往作佛事者，亦名佛世界。歎說者，於中有二種：一者為說阿含義；二者為證入義。摩訶薩者，有三種大：一願大；二行大；三利益眾生大。勝妙法者，諸法門中最殊勝故。光明者，此大乘法顯照一切餘法門故。法門者，名為法故。分別十地事者，顯示世間智所知法故。是事不可思議，所謂菩薩摩訶薩諸地智慧者，顯示出世間智故，此非世間分別地智能成菩薩清淨道故。已說本分，云何請分？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Once the buddhas had touched Vajragarbha Bodhisattva's crown, at that time Vajragarbha Bodhisattva then arose from samādhi.

Commentary: His arising at once from samādhi is because the purpose of the samādhi had been accomplished; and further, because, having received supreme power, the time to speak had come, while yet within samādhi itself there can be no speech. This concludes the section of arising; what is the section of the outline?

Sūtra: Having arisen from samādhi, he addressed the bodhisattvas, saying: "Sons of the Buddha! This vow of the bodhisattvas is well resolved, unmixed, and cannot be seen; vast as the dharma-realm, ultimate as space itself, it protects and shelters the whole realm of sentient beings unto the very end of future time. Sons of the Buddha! These bodhisattvas are thereby able to enter the ground of wisdom of the buddhas of the past, are thereby able to enter the ground of wisdom of the buddhas of the future, and are thereby able to enter the ground of wisdom of the buddhas of the present. Sons of the Buddha! These ten bodhisattva grounds have been taught, are being taught, and will be taught by the buddhas of the past, future, and present — and it is on account of this matter that I now speak of them thus. What are the ten? The first is called Pramuditā, the Ground of Joy; the second, Vimalā, the Ground Free of Defilement; the third, Prabhākarī, the Luminous Ground; the fourth, Arciṣmatī, the Blazing Ground; the fifth, Sudurjayā, the Ground Difficult to Conquer; the sixth, Abhimukhī, the Ground of Direct Presence; the seventh, Dūraṅgamā, the Far-Reaching Ground; the eighth, Acalā, the Immovable Ground; the ninth, Sādhumatī, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom; and the tenth, Dharmameghā, the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud. Sons of the Buddha! These ten bodhisattva grounds have been taught, are being taught, and will be taught by the buddhas of the past, future, and present. Sons of the Buddha! I have never seen any world-system of any buddha in which the tathāgatas do not praise and teach these ten bodhisattva grounds. Why is this? Because this is the bodhisattva-mahāsattvas' supreme and sublime Dharma, and is also the bodhisattvas' Dharma-gate of radiant light — that is to say, the distinguishing of the matter of the ten grounds. Sons of the Buddha! This matter is inconceivable — namely, the wisdom of the bodhisattva-mahāsattvas at every ground."

Commentary: Why does he speak without being asked? If he did not speak of his own accord, the assembly would have no way of knowing whether he intended to speak at all, and further would not know what Dharma he intended to speak of. "The vow is well resolved" should be understood in the same way as the arousing of the thought of enlightenment spoken of in the First Ground — this is that same arousing, now within this section of the outline.

"Well resolved" means: because it is held within true wisdom, it is "well"; "resolved" is precisely this well-resolvedness. This has already entered the First Ground, and does not belong to the ground of faith that precedes it. This well-resolvedness is of six kinds. First, well-resolvedness in the mark of contemplation, because the contemplation of suchness has the single mark of one taste — as the sūtra says, "unmixed." Second, well-resolvedness in truth, because it is not any worldly object-realm, but a supramundane one — as the sūtra says, "cannot be seen." Third, supreme well-resolvedness, because it is the great dharma-realm, the root of every buddha — as the sūtra says, "vast as the dharma-realm." Great, supreme, lofty, vast: these are one in substance though different in name, since it is a matter of the meaning of a dharma's own nature — true of every dharma as a matter of course. Furthermore, the dharma-realm's greatness in the contemplation of suchness surpasses every ordinary being's and every follower of the two vehicles' wisdom and the like — true of the pure dharma as a matter of course. Furthermore, the dharma-realm's greatness as the skillful-means-gathering ground refers to the teaching of the Great Vehicle's dharma — true as a matter of course. Furthermore, the dharma-realm's greatness as the white dharma-realm is true of wholesome dharmas as a matter of course. Fourth, well-resolvedness in cause, of two kinds: first, well-resolvedness in the cause that accomplishes the fruit of impermanence and delight. This cause is like space, since it is in dependence on it that every form is generated, and forms are never exhausted — as the sūtra says, "ultimate as space itself." Second, well-resolvedness in the cause of the constant fruit, which attains the path of nirvāṇa — as the sūtra says, "unto the very end of future time." Fifth, great well-resolvedness, according with and carrying out the practice of benefiting others — as the sūtra says, "protects and shelters the whole realm of sentient beings." Following directly upon the previous well-resolvedness, this vow does not abide exclusively in either the world or in nirvāṇa. Sixth, well-resolvedness free of timidity, because one enters the ground of wisdom of every buddha without timidity — as the sūtra says, "Sons of the Buddha, these bodhisattvas... are thereby able to enter the ground of wisdom of the buddhas of the present." Further, because these ten grounds generate and bring to completion the buddhas' wisdom, sustaining it — as the sūtra says, "Sons of the Buddha, these ten bodhisattva grounds have been taught, are being taught, and will be taught by the buddhas of the past, future, and present." Here, well-resolvedness is the universal aspect, and the rest are the particular aspects. The aspect of sameness is well-resolvedness itself; the aspect of difference is the particular aspects. The aspect of formation is what is spoken of concisely; the aspect of disintegration is what is spoken of at length — just as with a world-system's formation and disintegration. Why does he specifically teach ten bodhisattva grounds? In order to counteract ten kinds of hindrance. What are the ten hindrances? First, the hindrance of the ordinary person's mark of self. Second, the hindrance of wrongful conduct toward the bodies of sentient beings and the like. Third, the hindrance of the darkened mark that forgets the dharmas of hearing, reflection, cultivation, and the rest. Fourth, the hindrance of conceit regarding the understanding of the Dharma. Fifth, the hindrance of conceit regarding the purity of the body. Sixth, the hindrance of the habituated tendencies of subtle affliction. Seventh, the hindrance of habituated tendencies toward subtle marks. Eighth, the hindrance of activity with respect to the signless. Ninth, the hindrance of being unable to benefit sentient beings well. Tenth, the hindrance of not attaining mastery within all dharmas. Why is the first of the ten grounds named Joy, and so on down to the tenth, named the Dharma-Cloud? Accomplishing the unsurpassed practice of self-benefit and benefiting others, one first realizes the sacred ground and gives rise to great joy — and this is why it is called the Ground of Joy. Leaving behind the defilement of the erring mind that transgresses the precepts and the afflictions and the like, one's pure precepts become complete — and this is why it is called the Ground Free of Defilement.

In accordance with hearing, reflection, cultivation, and the rest, the Dharma is illumined and made manifest — and this is why it is called the Luminous Ground. The fire of wisdom, never forgetting, is able to burn away the fuel of affliction — and this is why it is called the Blazing Ground. Attaining the skillful means of supramundane wisdom, one is able to cross what is difficult to cross — and this is why it is called the Ground Difficult to Conquer. Through the practice of the perfection of wisdom, without interruption, great wisdom stands directly present — and this is why it is called the Ground of Direct Presence. Well cultivating the exertion of the signless practice, one is ultimately able to surpass the supramundane paths of the world and of the two vehicles alike — and this is why it is called the Far-Reaching Ground. The fruit of one's karmic practice having ripened fully, signless and uninterrupted — and this is why it is called the Immovable Ground. Teaching the Dharma with unobstructed power, accomplishing the practice of benefiting others — and this is why it is called the Ground of Excellent Wisdom. Attaining the great dharma-body, complete and self-mastering — and this is why it is called the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud. Receiving in this way the position of the Dharma-king is like a crown prince who, among the other princes, has attained self-mastery: in this position there remains only a subtle hindrance of wisdom, for which reason he is not yet fully self-mastering — and it is to counteract this hindrance that the buddha-ground is spoken of. Again, it is like a child carried within the womb: the bodhisattva's ten grounds are just the same, since every ground still has its hindrance. Just as when the child is born, so too is it when the buddha-ground is attained, since the matter is then brought to its ultimate completion. And again, just as when a child is born its faculties become fully aware, so too is it with a buddha, whose wisdom becomes clear and fully aware with respect to every object-realm. The womb has ten periods: first, the period of the kalala body; second, the period of the arbuda body; third, the period of the peśī body; fourth, the period of the ghana, or solid, body; fifth, the period of the body resembling a form; sixth, the period of the body resembling its eventual nature; seventh, the period of the body with karmic movement; eighth, the period of the fully-formed body — within which there are three further stages: the period when the faculties are fully formed, the period when the marks distinguishing male and female are fully formed, and the period when the extended, major marks are all fully formed. These ten periods are analogous to the ten grounds in just this way.

"Sons of the Buddha, I have never seen any world-system of any buddha in which the tathāgatas do not praise and teach these ten bodhisattva grounds" — this makes manifest this supreme Dharma, so as to cause the assembly gathered at this time to long for it all the more. "The world-system of a buddha" is that within which a buddha is accomplished, comparable to a rice field; going forth to perform a buddha's deeds is also called "a buddha's world-system." "Praise and teach" is of two kinds: first, teaching for the sake of scriptural transmission; second, teaching for the sake of realization. "Mahāsattva" has three kinds of greatness: first, greatness of vow; second, greatness of practice; third, greatness of benefiting sentient beings. "Supreme and sublime Dharma" means that, among every Dharma-gate, it is the most excellent. "Radiant light" means that this Mahāyāna dharma illumines and outshines every other Dharma-gate. "Dharma-gate" means that it is named as a dharma. "The distinguishing of the matter of the ten grounds" makes manifest the dharma known by worldly wisdom. "This matter is inconceivable — namely, the wisdom of the bodhisattva-mahāsattvas at every ground" makes manifest supramundane wisdom, since it is not the wisdom that discriminates the grounds by worldly means that is able to accomplish a bodhisattva's pure path. This concludes the section of the outline; what is the section of request?

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

On why Vajragarbha speaks before anyone has asked him to — a genuine oddity, since the sūtra's own much longer request section is still to come — Huiyuan gives two distinct reasons: 若不自說，眾則不知為說不說…又復不知欲說何法 — "if he did not speak of his own accord, the assembly would have no way of knowing whether he intended to speak at all… and further would not know what Dharma he intended to speak of." Without this brief outline first, the assembly would have nothing concrete to request — they could not even phrase a question, since they would not yet know there was a doctrine of ten named grounds to ask about. The section of the outline exists, on this reading, purely to manufacture the conditions under which a request becomes possible at all — a small but exact piece of narrative engineering the bare sūtra text leaves entirely implicit.

Huiyuan reads the sūtra's naming of the ten hindrances as a precise, one-to-one correspondence with the ten grounds rather than a loose thematic pairing — 此障與地更得相顯 — "hindrance and ground mutually manifest each other," each ground defined exactly by what it overcomes. He flags, without fully resolving it in this passage, a further question he treats at greater length elsewhere in his commentary: whether these ten hindrances are real or merely provisional — 此障為虗為實 — since a bodhisattva who has actually reached a given ground can, in one sense, be shown never to have truly possessed the hindrance that ground is said to counteract. Rather than settle the question here, he defers it — 十障之義，廣如別章，此應具論，今且釋名 — "the meaning of the ten hindrances is treated at length elsewhere; here it should suffice merely to explain the names" — and moves directly into the ground-by-ground etymologies the treatise itself supplies in full, letting Vasubandhu's own gloss of each name carry the weight rather than adding a second, independent explanation on top of it.

Part 2 Complete

This closes Part 2: the empowerment of speech and mind completing Vajragarbha's preparation, the buddhas touching his crown from afar so that he rises from samādhi, and — before any request has been made — the treatise's first full naming of all ten grounds, each with its own etymology, set alongside the ten periods of gestation before a child is born. Part 3 turns to the section of request: the assembly's response to hearing these ten names for the first time, and their appeal to Vajragarbha to expound the grounds in full.
