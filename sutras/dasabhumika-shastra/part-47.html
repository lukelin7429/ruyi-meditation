The Coronation, and the Sevenfold Greatness of Wisdom

At the heart of the Tenth Ground's third division, the division of attaining the reception of investiture, the buddhas of the ten directions release, from between their brows, the radiance of the mark of the white curl-tuft — named "empowering all wisdom" — together with a boundless retinue of further radiances, and converge this light into the crown of the bodhisattva's own head as he sits enthroned upon his lotus-seat. The treatise's own simile makes the scene explicit: just as the eldest son of a Wheel-Turning Sage King, ascending to the throne, is anointed with water drawn from the four great oceans and poured upon his own crown, so too does the bodhisattva, receiving this light, attain investiture, enter the buddhas' own state, and become numbered among the buddhas — dwelling securely, at last, in Dharmameghā, the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud. The treatise then opens its fivefold "division of entering the great consummation" and unfolds, in full, the first of its five members: the sevenfold "greatness of wisdom" this investiture confers, beginning with true knowledge of the accumulated causes of birth within the desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm, and continuing through the greatness of responsive transformation, empowerment, subtle entry, secrecy, entering eons, and entering the path.

The Buddhas' Light Empowers the Bodhisattva: The Coronation

Chinese Source

經曰：如是佛子！爾時諸佛放眉間白毫相光，名益一切智通，有阿僧祇光明眷屬，照於十方一切世界無有遺餘，十匝圍遶一切世界，示於諸佛大神通力，勸發無量百千萬億諸佛。一切十方諸佛國土六種震動，滅除一切惡道苦惱，一切魔宮隱蔽不現，示一切諸佛得菩提處、示一切諸佛大會神通莊嚴之事，照明一切法界際、一切虛空界，盡一切世界已還來集，在一切菩薩大會之上周匝圍遶，示大神通光明莊嚴之事。是光明入彼大菩薩頂上，其諸眷屬光明入諸眷屬蓮花座上。菩薩頂上光明入是菩薩身時，彼諸菩薩各得先所未得十十百千三昧。彼諸光明一時入彼菩薩頂時，彼菩薩名為得位，入諸佛境界、具佛十力、墮在佛數。佛子！譬如轉輪聖王長子，玉女寶所生，具足王相。轉輪聖王令子在白象寶閻浮檀金座上，取四大海水，上張羅網寶蓋幡華寶幢種種莊嚴，手執金鍾香水灌子頂上，即名灌頂剎利王數，具足轉十善道故，得名轉輪聖王。如是佛子！彼菩薩從諸如來得受位已，名得智位，具足十力墮在佛數。佛子？是名菩薩大乘位地。菩薩為是位故，受無量百千萬億苦難行事。是菩薩得是位已，無量功德智慧轉增，名為安住菩薩法雲地。

論曰：隨所得位者，諸如來光明，彼菩薩迭互知平等攝受故。如經「如是佛子！爾時諸佛放眉間白毫相光名益一切智通」如是等。云何得位如轉輪聖王長子？如經「譬如轉輪聖王長子」如是等。此菩薩同得位時，名為善住此地中，如經「是菩薩得是位已，無量功德智慧轉增，名為安住菩薩法雲地」。如是得受位分已說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Thus, son of the Buddha! At that time the buddhas released, from between their brows, the radiance of the mark of the white curl-tuft, named "empowering all wisdom," together with a retinue of asaṃkhyeya further radiances, illuminating every world throughout the ten directions without exception, circling around every world ten times over, manifesting the buddhas' own great spiritual power, and calling forth boundless hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of buddhas. Every buddha-land throughout the ten directions shook in six ways; every suffering and affliction of the evil destinies was extinguished; every palace of every māra was hidden from view and appeared no more; the places where every buddha attained enlightenment were displayed, and the spiritually empowered, adorned events of every buddha's great assembly were displayed; the farthest reaches of the entire dharma-realm and every realm of space were illumined; and, having passed through every world, the light returned and converged, circling entirely around above the great assembly of every bodhisattva, displaying its own great spiritual power and adorned radiance. This radiance entered the crown of that great bodhisattva's own head, and every radiance of its retinue entered the lotus-thrones of the retinue's own bodhisattvas. When the radiance upon the bodhisattva's own crown entered into this bodhisattva's own body, each and every one of these bodhisattvas attained ten times a hundred thousand samādhis such as he had never before attained. When every one of these radiances, all at once, entered that bodhisattva's own crown, that bodhisattva was called one who has attained investiture, entering the buddhas' own state, endowed with the buddhas' own ten powers, numbered among the buddhas. Son of the Buddha! It is like the eldest son of a Wheel-Turning Sage King, born of the queen-jewel, endowed with every mark of kingship. The Wheel-Turning Sage King seats his son upon a throne of Jambūnada gold, set atop the white elephant-jewel; above him a jeweled net, a jeweled canopy, banners, flowers, and a jeweled standard are raised, adorned in every manner; and, with his own hand holding a golden vessel of fragrant water drawn from the four great oceans, he pours it upon his son's own crown, which is called the ceremony of anointment, thereby entering the number of anointed kṣatriya kings — and, being thereby endowed with the turning of the ten wholesome paths, he attains the name of a Wheel-Turning Sage King. Just so, son of the Buddha! That bodhisattva, having received investiture from every tathāgata, is called one who has attained the investiture of wisdom, endowed with the ten powers, numbered among the buddhas. Son of the Buddha! This is called the ground of a bodhisattva's own Mahāyāna investiture. For the sake of this investiture, the bodhisattva undergoes boundless hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of difficult and painful practices. This bodhisattva, having attained this investiture, his boundless merit and wisdom become ever more increased, and he is called one who dwells securely in the bodhisattva's own Dharmameghā, the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud.

Commentary: As for according with the investiture attained, the radiance of every tathāgata and that bodhisattva mutually come to know each other, gathering him in equally — as the sūtra says, "thus, son of the Buddha! At that time the buddhas released, from between their brows, the radiance of the mark of the white curl-tuft, named empowering all wisdom," and so on. How is the investiture attained like that of the eldest son of a Wheel-Turning Sage King? As the sūtra says, "it is like the eldest son of a Wheel-Turning Sage King," and so on. When this bodhisattva attains investiture in this way, he is called one who dwells well within this ground — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, having attained this investiture, his boundless merit and wisdom become ever more increased, and he is called one who dwells securely in the bodhisattva's own Dharmameghā, the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud." In this way, the division of attaining the reception of investiture has now been explained.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang divides the entire division of receiving investiture — spanning what this collection's Part 46 and Part 47 together translate — into six matching elements, each keyed to the same royal analogy: 一隨何等座、二隨何等身、三隨何等眷屬、四隨何等相、五隨何等出處、六隨所得位 — throne, body, retinue, mark, point of emission, and, sixth, the investiture itself received. Of these six he treats the first three together, the next two together, and reserves the sixth — the coronation proper — for separate treatment on its own: precisely the passage translated here. His own gloss states the parallel outright: 六隨所得位者，如世王子受位之時，父王手執金鍾盛四海水灌太子頂，即名灌頂輪王。菩薩如是，十方諸佛白毫所放一切智光入菩薩頂，名受佛位 — "sixth, according with the investiture attained: just as, when a worldly prince receives investiture, his father the king, with his own hand holding a golden vessel filled with the water of the four seas, pours it upon the crown prince's own head, which is called the anointed Wheel-Turning King — just so with the bodhisattva: the light of all wisdom, released from the white curl-tuft of the buddhas of the ten directions, enters the bodhisattva's own crown, which is called receiving the buddhas' own investiture."

On what this coronation doctrinally accomplishes, Fazang glosses the treatise's own phrase 迭互知平等攝受, "mutually coming to know each other, gathering him in equally," as a bidirectional exchange of light: 謂菩薩頂光入佛足，是上進故也。佛光入菩薩頂，下攝故也。因果迭合，故云平等。身光即智，故云智也 — "the bodhisattva's own crown-light entering the buddhas' feet is his advancing upward; the buddhas' light entering the bodhisattva's own crown is their gathering him in from above. Cause and fruit thus meet each other in turn, and this is why it is called equal; the light of the body is itself wisdom, and this is why it is called wisdom." On the fourfold closing of the investiture — entering the buddhas' realm, being endowed with the ten powers, and being numbered among the buddhas — he offers a homely but exact analogy for why full buddhahood can still lie ahead even as the bodhisattva is already, in a real sense, counted among the buddhas: 以成佛德墮在佛數，如始出家雖未受戒即墮僧數 — "because he has accomplished the virtue of buddhahood, he is numbered among the buddhas — just as one newly gone forth into homelessness, though not yet having received full ordination, is immediately numbered among the saṅgha."

The Division of Entering the Great Consummation: The Sevenfold Greatness of Wisdom

Chinese Source

云何入大盡分？入大盡分者，有五種：一、智大；二、解脫大；三、三昧大；四、陀羅尼大；五、神通大。此事依五種義分別應知。一、依正覺實智義；二、依心自在義；三、依發心即成就一切事義；四、依一切世間隨利益眾生義；五、依堪能度眾生義。云何智成就？

經曰：佛子！是菩薩住此菩薩法雲地，如實知欲界集、色界集、無色界集。如實知眾生界集、識界集、有為界集、無為界集、虛空界集、法界集，如實知涅槃界集。如實知邪見諸煩惱界集、世界成壞集、聲聞行集、辟支佛行集、菩薩行集、諸佛力無畏不共佛法色身法身集、一切種一切智智集、得菩提轉法輪示滅度集，略說乃至如實知入一切法成智差別集。是菩薩以如是智通達勝慧，如實知眾生業化、煩惱化、見作化、世界化、法界化、聲聞化、辟支佛化、菩薩化、如來化，如實知一切分別無分別化。是菩薩如實知佛力持、法持、僧持、業持、煩惱持、時持、願持、供養持、行持、劫持，如實知智持。是菩薩如實知諸佛所有細微入智，所謂行細微入智、退細微入智、入胎細微入智、生細微入智、奮迅細微入智、出家細微入智、得菩提細微入智、轉法輪細微入智、持壽命細微入智、示涅槃細微入智，如實知法久住細微入智。是菩薩如實知諸佛所有密處，所謂身密口密意密、籌量時非時密、與菩薩授記密、攝伏眾生密、乘種種密、一切根行差別密、一切信如實所作密，如實知行得菩提密。是菩薩如實知諸佛所有入劫智，所謂一劫入阿僧祇劫、阿僧祇劫入一劫，有數劫入無數劫、無數劫入有數劫，一念劫入無量劫、無量劫入一念劫，劫入非劫、非劫入劫，有佛劫入無佛劫、無佛劫入有佛劫、有佛劫入有佛劫、無佛劫入無佛劫，過去未來劫入現在劫、現在劫入過去未來劫、未來過去劫入現在劫、現在劫入未來過去劫，長劫入短劫、短劫入長劫、短劫入短劫、長劫入長劫，如實知一切劫相相入。是菩薩如實知諸佛所有入智，所謂入凡夫道智、入微塵智、入國土身菩提智、入眾生身心菩提智、入一切處隨菩提智、入亂行示現智、入順行示現智、入逆行示現智、入思議不思議智、入世間出世間智行示現智、入聲聞智辟支佛智菩薩智，如實知如來智行智。佛子！諸佛智慧如是廣大無量無邊，菩薩住此地即能得入如是智慧。

論曰：是中智大復有七種應知：一、集智大；二、應化智大；三、加持智大；四、入細微智大；五、密處智大；六、入劫智大；七、入道智大。是中初依能斷疑力應知，第二依彼身起力，第三依彼如是如是轉行力，第四依彼應化加持善集不二智作故，第五依護根未熟眾生不令驚怖，第六依命行加持捨自在意故，第七依對治意說。是中集智者，因緣集智應知。彼復隨所有分染或淨或滅，隨所有三界處、隨所有眾生、隨染淨等心、隨所有有為法無為法無知知故。隨所有處虛空等，隨所說正不正法，隨所證不證，謂於涅槃隨所邪見過餘外道等彼不能證。隨所有器世間壞成、隨所有三乘，彼集差別應知。如經「佛子！是菩薩住此菩薩法雲地，如實知欲界集，乃至如實知入一切法成智差別集」故。是中應化智者，眾生應化等差別應知，如經「是菩薩以如是智通達勝慧乃至如實知一切分別無分別化」故。是中煩惱見作化者，應化示煩惱染見作故。法界化者，所說法行故，彼應化一切分別無分別如實知故。是中加持智者，如經「是菩薩如實知佛力持乃至如實知智持」故，如經應知。是中智持者，一切智智故，此智能作一切事故。

入細微智者，如經「是菩薩如實知諸佛所有細微入智乃至如實知法久住細微入智」故，如經應知。是中奮迅者，現行七步等應知。是中密處智者，如經「是菩薩如實知諸佛所有密處乃至如實知行得菩提密」故，如經應知。是中入劫智者，所謂入劫，如經「是菩薩如實知諸佛所有入劫智，所謂一劫入阿僧祇劫乃至如實知一切劫相相入」故，如經應知。是中入者，平等解脫一切劫迭相入故。是中入道智者，依凡夫地依我慢行者、依信求生天者、依覺觀者，如經「是菩薩如實知諸佛所有入智，所謂入凡夫道智乃至即能得入如是智慧」故。如是七種智大已說。云何解脫大？

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: What, then, is the division of entering the great consummation? The division of entering the great consummation is of five kinds: first, the greatness of wisdom; second, the greatness of liberation; third, the greatness of samādhi; fourth, the greatness of dhāraṇī; and fifth, the greatness of spiritual power. This matter should be understood as distinguished in dependence on five kinds of meaning: first, in dependence on the meaning of the true wisdom of correct enlightenment; second, in dependence on the meaning of the mind's own mastery; third, in dependence on the meaning that, the moment the thought is set forth, every matter is accomplished; fourth, in dependence on the meaning of according, throughout every world, with benefiting sentient beings; and fifth, in dependence on the meaning of being capable of delivering sentient beings. What, then, is accomplishing wisdom?

Sūtra: Son of the Buddha! This bodhisattva, dwelling within this bodhisattva's Dharmameghā, the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud, truly knows the accumulation of the desire realm, the accumulation of the form realm, and the accumulation of the formless realm. He truly knows the accumulation of the realm of sentient beings, the accumulation of the realm of consciousness, the accumulation of the realm of the conditioned, the accumulation of the realm of the unconditioned, the accumulation of the realm of space, and the accumulation of the dharma-realm; he truly knows the accumulation of the realm of nirvāṇa. He truly knows the accumulation of the realm of every affliction born of wrong views, the accumulation of the formation and dissolution of worlds, the accumulation of the practice of śrāvakas, the accumulation of the practice of pratyekabuddhas, the accumulation of the practice of bodhisattvas, the accumulation of every buddha's powers, fearlessnesses, and unshared buddha-dharmas, form-body, and dharma-body, the accumulation of the wisdom of all modes, the wisdom of omniscience, and the accumulation of attaining enlightenment, turning the wheel of the Dharma, and manifesting final nirvāṇa — briefly stated, all the way to truly knowing the distinct accumulation of the wisdom that enters every dharma and accomplishes it. This bodhisattva, through wisdom such as this, thoroughly penetrates supreme wisdom, and truly knows the transformation of sentient beings' own karma, the transformation of affliction, the transformation of views and their action, the transformation of worlds, the transformation of the dharma-realm, the transformation of śrāvakas, the transformation of pratyekabuddhas, the transformation of bodhisattvas, and the transformation of tathāgatas; he truly knows every transformation, whether accompanied by discrimination or free of discrimination. This bodhisattva truly knows the sustaining power of the buddhas, the sustaining power of the Dharma, the sustaining power of the saṅgha, the sustaining power of karma, the sustaining power of affliction, the sustaining power of time, the sustaining power of vow, the sustaining power of offering, the sustaining power of practice, and the sustaining power of eons; he truly knows the sustaining power of wisdom. This bodhisattva truly knows every subtle wisdom of entry possessed by the buddhas — namely, the subtle wisdom of entry into conduct, the subtle wisdom of entry into descent, the subtle wisdom of entry into the womb, the subtle wisdom of entry into birth, the subtle wisdom of entry into vigorous exertion, the subtle wisdom of entry into renunciation, the subtle wisdom of entry into attaining enlightenment, the subtle wisdom of entry into turning the wheel of the Dharma, the subtle wisdom of entry into sustaining the lifespan, and the subtle wisdom of entry into manifesting final nirvāṇa; he truly knows the subtle wisdom of entry into the Dharma's own long abiding. This bodhisattva truly knows every secret possessed by the buddhas — namely, the secret of bodily secrecy, verbal secrecy, and mental secrecy; the secret of deliberating over the timely and the untimely; the secret of bestowing prophecy upon bodhisattvas; the secret of gathering in and subduing sentient beings; the secret of the manifold vehicles; the secret of the distinctions among every faculty and practice; and the secret of every true action performed in faith; he truly knows the secret of practice attaining enlightenment. This bodhisattva truly knows every wisdom of entering eons possessed by the buddhas — namely, one eon entering an asaṃkhyeya of eons, and an asaṃkhyeya of eons entering one eon; a countable number of eons entering an uncountable number of eons, and an uncountable number of eons entering a countable number of eons; an eon of a single thought-moment entering boundless eons, and boundless eons entering an eon of a single thought-moment; an eon entering what is not an eon, and what is not an eon entering an eon; an eon with a buddha entering an eon without a buddha, an eon without a buddha entering an eon with a buddha, an eon with a buddha entering an eon with a buddha, and an eon without a buddha entering an eon without a buddha; a past or future eon entering the present eon, the present eon entering a past or future eon, a future or past eon entering the present eon, and the present eon entering a future or past eon; a long eon entering a short eon, a short eon entering a long eon, a short eon entering a short eon, and a long eon entering a long eon; he truly knows every mark of every eon mutually entering every other. This bodhisattva truly knows every wisdom of entry possessed by the buddhas — namely, the wisdom of entering the path of ordinary beings, the wisdom of entering the fine motes of dust, the wisdom of entering the enlightenment of the body of buddha-lands, the wisdom of entering the enlightenment of sentient beings' own body and mind, the wisdom of entering enlightenment according with every place, the wisdom of entering the manifestation of disordered practice, the wisdom of entering the manifestation of practice in accordance, the wisdom of entering the manifestation of practice in contrariety, the wisdom of entering the conceivable and the inconceivable, the wisdom of entering the manifestation of the practice of the worldly and the supramundane, and the wisdom of entering the wisdom of śrāvakas, the wisdom of pratyekabuddhas, and the wisdom of bodhisattvas; he truly knows the wisdom of the tathāgatas' own wisdom-practice. Son of the Buddha! The buddhas' own wisdom is, in this way, vast, boundless, and limitless — and a bodhisattva, dwelling in this ground, is thereby able to attain entry into wisdom such as this.

Commentary: Here, the greatness of wisdom should further be understood as of seven kinds: first, the greatness of the wisdom of accumulation; second, the greatness of the wisdom of responsive transformation; third, the greatness of the wisdom of empowerment; fourth, the greatness of the wisdom of subtle entry; fifth, the greatness of the wisdom of secrecy; sixth, the greatness of the wisdom of entering eons; and seventh, the greatness of the wisdom of entering the path. Here, the first should be understood in dependence on the power capable of severing doubt; the second, in dependence on the power that arises from that very body; the third, in dependence on the power that, in just this way and just that way, turns and continues; the fourth, since it is made through the undivided wisdom that well accumulates that responsive transformation and empowerment; the fifth, in dependence on protecting sentient beings whose faculties are not yet ripened, so that they are not startled; the sixth, in dependence on the empowerment of the sustaining of the lifespan, releasing the mind's own mastery; and the seventh is spoken in dependence on the intention of the antidote. Here, the wisdom of accumulation should be understood as the wisdom of the accumulation of cause and condition. This, further, according with whatever portion is defiled, or pure, or ceased; according with whatever place among the three realms; according with whatever sentient beings; according with whatever defiled or pure mind and the rest; and according with whatever conditioned dharma or unconditioned dharma, whether unknown or known. According with whatever place, such as space and the rest; according with whatever dharma is spoken, whether correct or incorrect; according with whatever is realized or not realized — namely, as for nirvāṇa, according with whatever wrong view causes the fault whereby the remaining outsiders and the rest are unable to realize it. According with whatever dissolution or formation of the vessel-world, and according with whatever of the three vehicles — its accumulation and distinctions should be understood accordingly. As the sūtra says, "Son of the Buddha! This bodhisattva, dwelling within this bodhisattva's Dharmameghā, the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud, truly knows the accumulation of the desire realm," all the way to "truly knowing the distinct accumulation of the wisdom that enters every dharma and accomplishes it." Here, the wisdom of responsive transformation should be understood as the distinctions of sentient beings' own responsive transformation and the rest — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, through wisdom such as this, thoroughly penetrates supreme wisdom," all the way to "he truly knows every transformation, whether accompanied by discrimination or free of discrimination." Here, the transformation of afflictions' own views and their action means that responsive transformation manifests the defiled action of afflictions' own views. The transformation of the dharma-realm means the practice of the Dharma spoken — since that responsive transformation, whether accompanied by discrimination or free of discrimination, is truly known in every case. Here, the wisdom of empowerment is as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows the sustaining power of the buddhas," all the way to "he truly knows the sustaining power of wisdom" — it should be understood just as the sūtra states. Here, the sustaining power of wisdom means the wisdom of omniscience, since this wisdom is able to accomplish every task.

The wisdom of subtle entry is as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows every subtle wisdom of entry possessed by the buddhas," all the way to "he truly knows the subtle wisdom of entry into the Dharma's own long abiding" — it should be understood just as the sūtra states. Here, "vigorous exertion" should be understood as manifesting the taking of seven steps and the like. Here, the wisdom of secrecy is as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows every secret possessed by the buddhas," all the way to "he truly knows the secret of practice attaining enlightenment" — it should be understood just as the sūtra states. Here, the wisdom of entering eons means entering eons — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows every wisdom of entering eons possessed by the buddhas — namely, one eon entering an asaṃkhyeya of eons," all the way to "he truly knows every mark of every eon mutually entering every other" — it should be understood just as the sūtra states. Here, "entering" means that, through equal liberation, every eon mutually enters every other, one after another. Here, the wisdom of entering the path is in dependence on the ground of ordinary beings, in dependence on one whose conduct is bound by conceit, in dependence on one who, through faith, seeks birth in the heavens, and in dependence on one given to discursive reflection — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva truly knows every wisdom of entry possessed by the buddhas — namely, the wisdom of entering the path of ordinary beings," all the way to "he is thereby able to attain entry into wisdom such as this." In this way, the sevenfold greatness of wisdom has now been explained. What, then, is the greatness of liberation?

Reading note — after Fazang

On the sevenfold greatness of wisdom this investiture confers, Fazang glosses the treatise's own list of seven — 一集智大、二應化智大、三加持智大、四入微細智、五密處智、六入劫智、七入道智 — the greatness of accumulation, responsive transformation, empowerment, subtle entry, secrecy, entering eons, and entering the path — and unpacks the treatise's terse opening line, 彼復隨所有分染或淨或滅, into a sixfold classification of exactly what is being "accumulated": 一或唯染⋯二或唯淨⋯三或唯滅⋯四染淨合⋯五淨滅合⋯六通於染淨及滅 — "first, sometimes purely defiled⋯second, sometimes purely pure⋯third, sometimes purely cessation⋯fourth, defilement and purity combined⋯fifth, purity and cessation combined⋯sixth, common to defilement, purity, and cessation" — with the three realms and the wrong views of heterodox teachers falling under the first, nirvāṇa and the practice of śrāvakas under the second, space alone under the third, and the dharma-realm itself, spanning all three, under the sixth.

On the fourth greatness, subtle entry, Fazang ties it directly to the traditional eight aspects of a buddha's career, reading the treatise's own term almost literally: 八相之中，一現一切，故稱微細 — "within the eight aspects [of a buddha's life], each single one manifests every other, and this is why it is called subtle" — with 奮迅, "vigorous exertion," glossed simply as 現行七步等, "manifesting the taking of the seven steps and the like," at a newborn buddha's birth. On the fifth greatness, secrecy, he reads the buddhas' motive as frankly pastoral rather than merely mysterious: 謂將護初心根未熟者，現麁隱細，令彼不怖 — "namely, in order to protect and shelter those of newly arisen aspiration whose faculties are not yet ripened, the buddhas display the coarse and conceal the subtle, so that these beings are not frightened." And on the sixth, entering eons, he explains the mutual interpenetration of eons as itself an expression of liberation, not a cosmological curiosity: 謂不思議解脫之法，平等齊均遍諸劫海故，令諸劫迭互相入 — "namely, the inconceivable dharma of liberation, equally and uniformly pervading the entire ocean of eons, is what causes every eon to mutually enter every other, one after another."

Part 47 Complete

This closes Part 47. The buddhas of the ten directions, releasing the radiance of the mark of the white curl-tuft from between their brows — named "empowering all wisdom" — have converged this light into the crown of the bodhisattva's own head as he sits enthroned upon his lotus-throne, exactly as the treatise's own famous simile describes: water drawn from the four great oceans, poured at his coronation upon the crowned head of a Wheel-Turning Sage King's own son. Through this investiture the bodhisattva enters the buddhas' own state, is endowed with their ten powers, and is numbered among the buddhas, dwelling securely, at last, in Dharmameghā, the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud. This part has then translated the opening of the treatise's fivefold division of entering the great consummation, and, in full, its first member: the sevenfold greatness of wisdom this investiture confers — the greatness of accumulation, responsive transformation, empowerment, subtle entry, secrecy, entering eons, and entering the path — beginning from true knowledge of the accumulated causes of the desire, form, and formless realms and closing with the wisdom that enters the path of ordinary beings itself. Part 48 continues with the remaining four greatnesses of this same division, beginning with the inconceivable greatness of liberation, through the bodhisattva's attainment of boundless wisdom according with enlightenment, and the treatise's own threefold explanation of why this ground is named Dharmameghā, the Dharma-Cloud.
