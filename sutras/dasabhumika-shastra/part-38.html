The Three Masteries and the Ten Bodies

The treatise's fourth structural division within Acalā, the Immovable Ground — 淨佛國土分, the division of purifying buddha-lands — opens as this bodhisattva, having attained the Eighth Ground, arises, from great skillful means, a mind of effortless action, and, dwelling within the bodhisattva's own path, contemplates the very power of the wisdom of omniscience: the formation and destruction of world-systems, the four elements down to their last particle of dust, and the three realms themselves, constituting mastery over the receptacle-world in five described aspects. He then attains mastery over the world of sentient beings, manifesting his own body according with the differing faith and resolve of sentient beings across countless buddha-lands, from the śramaṇa and the brāhmaṇa up through Śakra and Brahmā, and from the śrāvaka up through the buddha's own body. Finally, departing from every discrimination of bodily characteristics and realizing the equality of bodies, he attains the profound wisdom of ultimate meaning — expounded through a tenfold taxonomy of bodies, from the bodies of sentient beings, lands, and karmic retribution, through the śrāvaka, the pratyekabuddha, and the bodhisattva, to the Tathāgata's own body, the body of wisdom, the body of the Dharma, and the body of space, this last topic receiving the treatise's own unusually long and detailed commentary.

The Mind of Effortless Action, and Mastery over the Receptacle-World

Chinese Source

經曰：佛子！是菩薩得菩薩第八地，從大方便慧起無功用心，在菩薩道觀一切智智力，所謂觀世界成、觀世界壞。是菩薩隨世間成，彼如實知；隨世間壞，彼如實知。隨業因緣集故，世間成彼亦知；隨業因緣盡故，世間壞彼亦知。隨世間幾時成，彼亦知；隨世間幾時壞，彼亦知。隨世間幾時成住，彼亦知；隨世間幾時壞住，彼亦知。是菩薩知地界小相、知地界大相、知地界無量相、知地界差別相。是菩薩知水界小相、知水界大相、知水界無量相、知水界差別相。是菩薩知火界小相、知火界大相、知火界無量相、知火界差別相。是菩薩知風界小相、知風界大相、知風界無量相、知風界差別相。是菩薩知微塵細相、麁相、無量相，知差別相。隨何世界中所有微塵集散，微塵差別皆悉能知。隨何世界中，所有地界若干微塵皆悉能知，所有水界若干微塵皆悉能知，所有火界若干微塵皆悉能知，所有風界若干微塵皆悉能知，所有眾生身若干微塵皆悉能知，所有國土身若干微塵皆悉能知。是菩薩知諸眾生麁身細身差別若干微塵成，知地獄身依若干微塵成，知畜生身依若干微塵成，知餓鬼身依若干微塵成，知阿修羅身依若干微塵成，知天身依若干微塵成，知人身依若干微塵成。是菩薩通達入如是分別微塵智已，知欲界成、知色界成、知無色界成，知欲界壞、知色界壞、知無色界壞，知欲界小相、知欲界大相、知欲界無量相、知欲界差別相，知色界小相、知色界大相、知色界無量相、知色界差別相，知無色界小相、知無色界大相、知無色界無量相、知無色界差別相，如是入思量三界智中。

是菩薩復善起智明，善知眾生身差別、善知分別眾生身、善觀所應生處。是菩薩隨眾生生處、隨眾生身集業，而為受身，教化眾生故。是菩薩現身遍滿三千大千世界，隨眾生身各各差別，如是隨順生處起現前光明，若二三千大千世界、若三四五、若十二十三十四十五十、若百三千大千世界、若千若萬若百萬若千萬若百千萬、若億萬若百千萬億那由他，乃至無量無邊不可說不可說三千大千世界，身遍其中，隨眾生自身差別信如是生處，起現前光明，智隨順故。是菩薩成就如是智慧，於一佛國土身不動搖，乃至不可說諸佛國土，於眾會中起現前光明故。

論曰：器世間自在行者，有五種自在：一、隨心所欲彼能現及不現；二、隨何欲彼能現；三、隨時欲彼即時現；四、隨廣狹欲彼能現；五、隨心幾許欲彼能現。如經「佛子！是菩薩得菩薩第八地，從大方便慧起無功用心，在菩薩道觀一切智智力乃至隨世間壞彼如實知」故。此世界成壞等，初器世間自在行中，隨心所欲彼能現及不現故。隨業因緣集故，世間成彼亦知；隨業因緣盡故，世間壞彼亦知，業集盡智，第二隨何欲彼能現故。隨世間幾時成乃至第四句隨世間幾時壞住彼亦知。隨世間幾時成等智，第三隨幾時欲彼即時現故。是菩薩知地界小相乃至知人身依若干微塵成。地等相差別智，第四隨廣狹欲彼能現故。是菩薩入如是分別微塵智中，乃至現前光明成壞智，乃至於一佛國土身不動搖，第五隨心幾許欲彼能現故。

是中地界次第，境界智、相智。云何境界智？非定地報識境界是名小相，定地識境界是名大相，如來境界是名無量相。云何相智？自相、同相是名差別相。身麁細者，色無色等諸眾生，如是次第欲界等境界智、相智。欲界人境界是名小相，天境界大相；色界覺觀境界小相，無覺無觀境界大相；無色界佛法中凡夫境界小相，聲聞菩薩大相，一切如來境界無量相。善知眾生身差別、善知分別眾生身者，善知身不同，方便異生、同生差別應知。諸佛國土於眾會中起現前光明者，彼處處法身體示現。如是淨佛國土器世間自在行已說。云何眾生世間自在行？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: Son of the Buddha! This bodhisattva, having attained the bodhisattva's Eighth Ground, from great skillful, expedient wisdom gives rise to a mind of effortless action, and, dwelling within the bodhisattva's own path, contemplates the power of the wisdom of omniscience — namely, contemplating the formation of world-systems, and contemplating the destruction of world-systems. This bodhisattva, according with the world's own formation, knows it just as it is; according with the world's own destruction, knows it just as it is. According with the gathering together of karmic causes and conditions, he likewise knows that the world is thereby formed; according with the exhaustion of karmic causes and conditions, he likewise knows that the world is thereby destroyed. According with how long the world takes to be formed, he likewise knows it; according with how long the world takes to be destroyed, he likewise knows it. According with how long the world, once formed, abides, he likewise knows it; according with how long the world, once destroyed, abides in its destruction, he likewise knows it. This bodhisattva knows the small mark of the realm of earth, knows the great mark of the realm of earth, knows the boundless mark of the realm of earth, and knows the distinct mark of the realm of earth. This bodhisattva knows the small mark of the realm of water, knows the great mark of the realm of water, knows the boundless mark of the realm of water, and knows the distinct mark of the realm of water. This bodhisattva knows the small mark of the realm of fire, knows the great mark of the realm of fire, knows the boundless mark of the realm of fire, and knows the distinct mark of the realm of fire. This bodhisattva knows the small mark of the realm of wind, knows the great mark of the realm of wind, knows the boundless mark of the realm of wind, and knows the distinct mark of the realm of wind. This bodhisattva knows the subtle mark of every particle of dust, its coarse mark, its boundless mark, and knows its distinct mark. Whatever world-system it may be, the gathering and scattering of every particle of dust within it, and every distinction among these particles, he is entirely able to know. Whatever world-system it may be, however many particles of dust make up the realm of earth there, he is entirely able to know; however many particles of dust make up the realm of water there, he is entirely able to know; however many particles of dust make up the realm of fire there, he is entirely able to know; however many particles of dust make up the realm of wind there, he is entirely able to know; however many particles of dust make up the bodies of sentient beings there, he is entirely able to know; and however many particles of dust make up the body of that land itself, he is entirely able to know. This bodhisattva knows how many particles of dust the coarse bodies and subtle bodies of every sentient being, in their distinctions, are made of; knows how many particles of dust the body of a hell-being depends upon and is made of; knows how many particles of dust the body of an animal depends upon and is made of; knows how many particles of dust the body of a hungry ghost depends upon and is made of; knows how many particles of dust the body of an asura depends upon and is made of; knows how many particles of dust the body of a god depends upon and is made of; and knows how many particles of dust the body of a human being depends upon and is made of. This bodhisattva, having thoroughly penetrated and entered wisdom that discriminates particles of dust in this way, knows the formation of the realm of desire, knows the formation of the realm of form, and knows the formation of the formless realm; knows the destruction of the realm of desire, knows the destruction of the realm of form, and knows the destruction of the formless realm; knows the small mark of the realm of desire, knows the great mark of the realm of desire, knows the boundless mark of the realm of desire, and knows the distinct mark of the realm of desire; knows the small mark of the realm of form, knows the great mark of the realm of form, knows the boundless mark of the realm of form, and knows the distinct mark of the realm of form; knows the small mark of the formless realm, knows the great mark of the formless realm, knows the boundless mark of the formless realm, and knows the distinct mark of the formless realm — and in this way enters into the wisdom that reflects upon the three realms.

This bodhisattva, further well arousing the light of wisdom, well knows the distinctions among the bodies of sentient beings, well knows how to discriminate the bodies of sentient beings, and well contemplates the place where each ought to be born. This bodhisattva, according with the place of birth of sentient beings and according with the karma gathered by the bodies of sentient beings, takes on a body accordingly, for the sake of teaching and transforming sentient beings. This bodhisattva manifests his own body filling an entire great trichiliocosm, and, according with the various distinctions among the bodies of sentient beings, in this way, according with their place of birth, gives rise to radiance directly present — whether within two great trichiliocosms, or three, four, or five, or ten, twenty, thirty, forty, or fifty, or a hundred great trichiliocosms, or a thousand, or a myriad, or a hundred myriads, or a thousand myriads, or a hundred thousand myriads, or a koṭi of myriads, or a hundred thousand myriads of koṭis of niyutas, all the way to boundless, limitless, ineffable, and inconceivable great trichiliocosms — his body filling every one of them, and, according with the differing confidence of sentient beings themselves in such a place of birth, giving rise to radiance directly present, according with wisdom. This bodhisattva, having accomplished wisdom such as this, within a single buddha-land, his body unmoved and unshaken, and all the way to ineffable buddha-lands, gives rise, within every assembly, to radiance directly present.

Commentary: "Mastery over the receptacle-world" has five kinds of mastery: first, according with whatever the mind desires, he is able to manifest it, and able not to manifest it; second, according with whatever he desires to know, he is able to manifest it; third, according with whenever he desires to know, he manifests it at that very time; fourth, according with however broad or narrow he desires his knowledge to be, he is able to manifest it; and fifth, according with however much the mind desires to know, he is able to manifest it. As the sūtra says, from "Son of the Buddha! This bodhisattva, having attained the bodhisattva's Eighth Ground, from great skillful, expedient wisdom gives rise to a mind of effortless action, and, dwelling within the bodhisattva's own path, contemplates the power of the wisdom of omniscience," all the way to "according with the world's own destruction, knows it just as it is." This formation and destruction of world-systems and the rest belongs to the first kind of mastery within mastery over the receptacle-world: according with whatever the mind desires, he is able to manifest it, and able not to manifest it. "According with the gathering together of karmic causes and conditions, he likewise knows that the world is thereby formed; according with the exhaustion of karmic causes and conditions, he likewise knows that the world is thereby destroyed" — this wisdom of the gathering and exhaustion of karma is the second kind of mastery: according with whatever he desires to know, he is able to manifest it. From "according with how long the world takes to be formed" all the way to the fourth clause, "according with how long the world, once destroyed, abides in its destruction, he likewise knows it" — this wisdom of how long the world takes to be formed and the rest is the third kind of mastery: according with whenever he desires to know, he manifests it at that very time. From "this bodhisattva knows the small mark of the realm of earth" all the way to "knows how many particles of dust the body of a human being depends upon and is made of" — this wisdom of the distinct marks of earth and the rest is the fourth kind of mastery: according with however broad or narrow he desires his knowledge to be, he is able to manifest it. From "this bodhisattva enters into wisdom that discriminates particles of dust in this way" all the way to the wisdom of radiance directly present and of formation and destruction, and all the way to "within a single buddha-land, his body unmoved and unshaken" — this is the fifth kind of mastery: according with however much the mind desires to know, he is able to manifest it.

Here, within the sequence of the realm of earth and the rest, there is the wisdom of the object-realm and the wisdom of characteristic-marks. What is the wisdom of the object-realm? The object-realm of the retribution-consciousness belonging to a stage lacking concentration is called the small mark; the object-realm of the consciousness belonging to a stage of concentration is called the great mark; and the object-realm of the Tathāgata is called the boundless mark. What is the wisdom of characteristic-marks? Own-characteristic and shared characteristic are called the distinct mark. "Coarse body and subtle body" refers to sentient beings of the realm of form and of the formless realm and the rest; in just this sequence, there is the wisdom of the object-realm and the wisdom of characteristic-marks belonging to the realm of desire and the rest. Within the realm of desire, the object-realm of a human being is called the small mark, and that of a god is the great mark; within the realm of form, the object-realm accompanied by initial thought and sustained thought is the small mark, and that free of initial thought and sustained thought is the great mark; within the formless realm, in the buddha-dharma, the object-realm of an ordinary being is the small mark, that of a śrāvaka or a bodhisattva is the great mark, and the object-realm of every Tathāgata is the boundless mark. "Well knowing the distinctions among the bodies of sentient beings, well knowing how to discriminate the bodies of sentient beings" means well knowing that bodies are not the same, and should be understood as the skillful means of manifesting a body different from or the same as that of the beings being born. "Within every buddha-land, giving rise, within every assembly, to radiance directly present" means that the very body of the Dharma-body is manifest in place after place. In this way, mastery over the receptacle-world, within the purifying of buddha-lands, has now been explained. What, then, is mastery over the world of sentient beings?

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang splits the very first of the five masteries into two distinct powers folded together in the sūtra's own single clause: 一隨心所欲者，於此世界隨於己心欲知即知，又亦隨他眾生之心欲知即知，此是智自在。彼能現不現者，是通自在。能現是成，不現是壞 — "as for 'according with whatever the mind desires': within this world, according with his own mind's desire to know, he knows it at once, and, moreover, according with other sentient beings' own mind's desire to know, he likewise knows it at once — this is the mastery of wisdom. As for 'he is able to manifest it, and able not to manifest it' — this is the mastery of spiritual power. To manifest is formation; not to manifest is destruction." Knowing a world and bringing a world about, on this reading, are two separate masteries the sūtra's own compressed phrasing quietly holds together.

On why the formless realm can still be said to have "particles of dust" at all, despite being defined by the very absence of form, Fazang notes a distinctly Mahāyāna claim standing behind the treatise's silence on the point: 大乘宗，無色界中亦有微塵，即定自在所生色 — "in the Mahāyāna's own tenet, even within the formless realm there are particles of dust as well — namely, form generated by mastery in concentration." What looks like formlessness from the outside is, on this account, simply a subtlety of form generated through the very mastery this ground is describing.

Mastery over the World of Sentient Beings, According with Faith and Resolve

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩隨眾生身差別信、隨決定信差別，彼彼佛國土中、彼彼大會中，如是如是自身示現。是菩薩若於沙門眾中示沙門形色，婆羅門眾中示婆羅門形色，剎利眾中示剎利形色，毘舍眾中示毘舍形色，首陀眾中示首陀形色，居士眾中示居士形色，長者眾中示長者形色，四天王眾中示四天王形色，帝釋眾中示帝釋形色。如是焰摩眾中、兜率眾中、化樂眾中、他化自在眾中、魔眾中、梵天眾中，示梵天形色，乃至阿迦尼吒天眾中示阿迦尼吒天形色。

是菩薩應以聲聞身度者示聲聞形色，應以辟支佛身度者示辟支佛形色，應以菩薩身度者示菩薩形色，應以佛身度者示佛身形色。佛子！如是所有不可說諸佛國土中，隨眾生身信樂差別，彼彼佛國土中，如是自身差別示現。

論曰：眾生世間自在行者，彼調伏自在故，彼行化眾生身心自同事自身心等分示現。如經「是菩薩隨眾生身差別信乃至彼彼佛國土中如是自身差別示現」故。如是眾生世間自在行已說。云何智正覺自在行？第一義諦智、世諦智等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, according with the distinctions in the confidence of the bodies of sentient beings, and according with the distinctions in their settled confidence, within buddha-land after buddha-land, and within great assembly after great assembly, manifests his own body accordingly, in just this way. This bodhisattva, when among an assembly of śramaṇas, manifests the form and appearance of a śramaṇa; when among an assembly of brāhmaṇas, manifests the form and appearance of a brāhmaṇa; when among an assembly of kṣatriyas, manifests the form and appearance of a kṣatriya; when among an assembly of vaiśyas, manifests the form and appearance of a vaiśya; when among an assembly of śūdras, manifests the form and appearance of a śūdra; when among an assembly of householders, manifests the form and appearance of a householder; when among an assembly of elders, manifests the form and appearance of an elder; when among an assembly of the Four Heavenly Kings, manifests the form and appearance of the Four Heavenly Kings; when among an assembly of Śakra, manifests the form and appearance of Śakra. Likewise, when among an assembly of Yāma's heaven, when among an assembly of Tuṣita, when among an assembly of Nirmāṇarati, when among an assembly of Paranirmita-vaśavartin, when among an assembly of māras, and when among an assembly of Brahmā, manifests the form and appearance of Brahmā, all the way to, when among an assembly of the Akaniṣṭha heaven, manifesting the form and appearance of the Akaniṣṭha heaven.

This bodhisattva, toward whoever ought to be liberated through the body of a śrāvaka, manifests the form and appearance of a śrāvaka; toward whoever ought to be liberated through the body of a pratyekabuddha, manifests the form and appearance of a pratyekabuddha; toward whoever ought to be liberated through the body of a bodhisattva, manifests the form and appearance of a bodhisattva; toward whoever ought to be liberated through the body of a buddha, manifests the form and appearance of a buddha's own body. Son of the Buddha! In this way, within all the ineffable buddha-lands there are, according with the distinctions in the confidence and joy of the bodies of sentient beings, within buddha-land after buddha-land, he manifests his own body accordingly, in these very distinctions.

Commentary: "Mastery over the world of sentient beings" means that, through his own mastery in taming sentient beings, in his practice of teaching and transforming sentient beings he takes on the same task in body and mind as they do, manifesting his own body and mind in equal measure with theirs. As the sūtra says, from "this bodhisattva, according with the distinctions in the confidence of the bodies of sentient beings" all the way to "within buddha-land after buddha-land, manifests his own body accordingly, in these very distinctions." In this way, mastery over the world of sentient beings has now been explained. What, then, is the mastery of wisdom and realization? It is the wisdom of ultimate meaning and the wisdom of worldly truth, and the rest.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang separates two distinct registers of accommodation folded into the commentary's own single description: 言身心自同事者，釋別顯文內化同物身，謂菩薩自身隨眾生心，以自己身同形事，故言身心自同事也。言身心等分示者，此釋中化應物心，菩薩自身隨眾生必量宜而現，名等分示，謂應以聲聞身等 — "'body and mind taking on the same task' glosses the passage's own detailed account of transforming himself into a body the same as that of the being taught: the bodhisattva's own body, according with the sentient being's own mind, takes on the very same form and task as that being — hence it is called 'body and mind taking on the same task.' 'Manifesting body and mind in equal measure' glosses the transformation that responds to the object's own mind: the bodhisattva's own body, according with whatever is truly fitting for that sentient being, manifests accordingly — this is called 'manifesting in equal measure,' meaning manifesting the body of a śrāvaka and the rest, as is appropriate." Mimicking a sentient being's own social form and calibrating which of the four vehicle-bodies best suits their capacity are, on this reading, two separate operations, not one.

On why "mastery" is the right word for a bodhisattva able to move so freely between śramaṇa and brāhmaṇa, human court and heavenly assembly, śrāvaka and buddha, Fazang locates the whole exhaustive list under a single named power: 彼調伏眾生自在，是總釋其相，以此菩薩調伏眾生中得自在故，能種種現 — "'mastery in taming sentient beings' is what generally explains this mark: it is precisely because this bodhisattva has attained mastery amid the very taming of sentient beings that he is able to manifest in such manifold ways." The list's sheer range, from the lowest caste to Brahmā himself and from the śrāvaka to the buddha, is not incidental variety but the deliberate demonstration that this one mastery admits no exception of register.

The Equality of Bodies: The Wisdom of Ultimate Meaning and the Tenfold Body

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩遠離一切身相分別得身平等。是菩薩知眾生身、知國土身、知業報身、知聲聞身、知辟支佛身、知菩薩身、知如來身、知智身、知法身、知虛空身。

是菩薩如是知眾生深心起樂，若以眾生身作自身，如是國土身、業報身、聲聞身、辟支佛身、菩薩身、如來身、智身、法身、虛空身作自身。是菩薩如是知眾生深心起樂，若以自身作眾生身，如是國土身、業報身、聲聞身、辟支佛身、菩薩身、如來身、智身、法身、虛空身作眾生身。是菩薩如是知眾生深心起樂，何等何等身、何等何等身中能自在作。

是菩薩知眾生身、集業身、報身、煩惱身、色身，知無色身。

是菩薩知國土身小相、大相、無量相、垢相、淨相、廣相、亂住相、倒住相、平正相，知方網差別相，知業報身假名差別、聲聞身假名差別、辟支佛身假名差別、知菩薩身假名差別。

是菩薩知如來身、菩提身、願身、化身、受神力身、相好莊嚴身、光明身、意生身、功德身、法身，知智身。

是菩薩善知智身善思量相、善如實觀相、果行所攝相、世間出世間差別相、三乘差別相、共不共相、乘不乘相、善知學無學相。

是菩薩知法身平等相、知不壞相、知轉時假名差別相、眾生非眾生法差別相、知佛法聖僧法差別相。

是菩薩知虛空身無量相、周遍相、無形相、不異相、無邊相，知顯色身別異相。

論曰：第一義諦智者，遠離一切身相分別，示得身平等，自身他身不分別故。如經「是菩薩遠離一切身相分別得身平等」故。此是不同聲聞辟支佛第一義智示現。

世諦智者，善知眾生身等染分、淨分、不二分，善分別知故。如經「是菩薩知眾生身乃至知虛空身」故。是中眾生世間、器世間彼二生因，業煩惱是染分，三乘是淨分。此三乘隨何智、隨何法？彼淨顯示虛空是不二分故。

是菩薩如是知眾生深心起樂，若以眾生身作自身，乃至以虛空身作自身等。是菩薩如是知眾生深心起樂，若以自身作眾生身，乃至以自身作虛空身等。是中以眾生身作自身者，彼自在中所作攝取行種種示現。

是中眾生身者，業生煩惱妄想染差別、色無色界差別皆如實知。如經「是菩薩知眾生身、集業身、報身、煩惱身、色身，知無色身」故。

國土身者，千等世界差別應知，淨不淨世界差別皆善分別知，廣等世界差別皆善分別知。如經「是菩薩知國土身乃至知方網差別相」故。廣相等諸句義，如初地說。業報、聲聞、辟支佛、菩薩身假名差別者，自相同相差別假名分別，無我人故。如經「是菩薩知業報身假名差別乃至知菩薩身假名差別」故。

是菩薩知如來身者，示成正覺為菩提故、願生兜率天故、所有佛應化故、自身舍利住持故、所有實報身故、所有光明攝伏眾生故、所有同不同世間出世間心得自在解脫故、所有不共能作廣大利益因故、所有如來無漏界故、所有無障礙智故。是故此智能作一切事，彼事差別皆悉能知。如經「是菩薩知如來身、菩提身乃至知智身」故。

是菩薩知智身者，聞思智差別、修智差別、果行智差別、世間出世間智差別，皆如實知。如經「是菩薩善知智身乃至知學無學相」故。

是菩薩知法身平等相者，無量法門明等一法身故、如聞取故、隨所化眾生根性相應時說差別故、有根無根差別相故，知第一相差別，皆悉能知。如經「是菩薩知法身平等相乃至知佛法聖僧法差別相」故。

是菩薩知虛空身者，知無盡相、遍相、不可見相、無障礙相、無為相，能通受色相因色，彼分別皆悉能知。如經「是菩薩知虛空身無量相乃至知顯色身別異相」故。如是八地淨佛國土三自在行已說。次說得十自在。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, departed far from every discrimination of bodily characteristics, attains the equality of bodies. This bodhisattva knows the body of sentient beings, knows the body of lands, knows the body of karmic retribution, knows the body of the śrāvaka, knows the body of the pratyekabuddha, knows the body of the bodhisattva, knows the body of the Tathāgata, knows the body of wisdom, knows the body of the Dharma, and knows the body of space.

This bodhisattva, knowing in this way the joy that arises in the deep mind of sentient beings, is able, whether by taking the body of sentient beings as his own body, or, likewise, the body of lands, the body of karmic retribution, the body of the śrāvaka, the body of the pratyekabuddha, the body of the bodhisattva, the body of the Tathāgata, the body of wisdom, the body of the Dharma, or the body of space, in each case to take it as his own body. This bodhisattva, knowing in this way the joy that arises in the deep mind of sentient beings, is able, whether by taking his own body and making it the body of sentient beings, or, likewise, making it the body of lands, the body of karmic retribution, the body of the śrāvaka, the body of the pratyekabuddha, the body of the bodhisattva, the body of the Tathāgata, the body of wisdom, the body of the Dharma, or the body of space, in each case to make it the body of sentient beings. This bodhisattva, knowing in this way the joy that arises in the deep mind of sentient beings, is able, within whatever body and whatever body, to act with mastery.

This bodhisattva knows the body of sentient beings as the body of gathered karma, the body of retribution, the body of affliction, the body of form, and knows the formless body.

This bodhisattva knows the small mark, the great mark, the boundless mark, the defiled mark, the pure mark, the vast mark, the mark of dwelling in disorder, the mark of dwelling inverted, and the mark of dwelling level and upright belonging to the body of lands, knows the distinct mark of their directional network, knows the distinctions of provisional designation belonging to the body of karmic retribution, the distinctions of provisional designation belonging to the body of the śrāvaka, the distinctions of provisional designation belonging to the body of the pratyekabuddha, and knows the distinctions of provisional designation belonging to the body of the bodhisattva.

This bodhisattva knows the body of the Tathāgata as the body of enlightenment, the body of vow, the body of transformation, the body that receives spiritual power, the body adorned with excellent marks, the body of radiance, the mind-made body, the body of merit, and the body of the Dharma, and knows the body of wisdom.

This bodhisattva well knows the body of wisdom in its mark of skillful reflection, its mark of well contemplating things just as they are, its mark of being gathered within fruit and practice, its mark of the distinction between the mundane and supramundane, its mark of the distinction among the three vehicles, its mark of the shared and the unshared, its mark of the vehicle and what is not a vehicle, and well knows its mark of the trainee and the one beyond training.

This bodhisattva knows the body of the Dharma in its mark of equality, knows its mark of the indestructible, knows its mark of the distinctions of provisional designation across changing times, its mark of the distinction between the dharmas of sentient beings and of what is not a sentient being, and knows its mark of the distinctions among the dharmas of the Buddha, the Dharma, and the noble Saṅgha.

This bodhisattva knows the body of space in its boundless mark, its all-pervading mark, its formless mark, its mark of being without difference, and its limitless mark, and knows the mark of the distinct differences of manifest, colored bodies.

Commentary: "The wisdom of ultimate meaning" means departing far from every discrimination of bodily characteristics, manifesting the attainment of the equality of bodies, since he does not discriminate between his own body and the bodies of others. As the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, departed far from every discrimination of bodily characteristics, attains the equality of bodies." This is what makes manifest a wisdom of ultimate meaning not shared with the śrāvaka or the pratyekabuddha.

"The wisdom of worldly truth" means well knowing the defiled portion, the pure portion, and the non-dual portion of the body of sentient beings and the rest, well discriminating and knowing them. As the sūtra says, from "this bodhisattva knows the body of sentient beings" all the way to "knows the body of space." Here, the cause that gives rise to both the world of sentient beings and the receptacle-world is karma and affliction, which is the defiled portion; the three vehicles are the pure portion. As for these three vehicles, according with what wisdom, according with what dharma are they known? That purity is manifested through wisdom and through the Dharma — and space itself is the non-dual portion.

"This bodhisattva, knowing in this way the joy that arises in the deep mind of sentient beings, is able, whether by taking the body of sentient beings as his own body," all the way to "taking the body of space as his own body," and the rest; "this bodhisattva, knowing in this way the joy that arises in the deep mind of sentient beings, is able, whether by taking his own body and making it the body of sentient beings," all the way to "making his own body the body of space," and the rest. Here, "taking the body of sentient beings as his own body" means that, within this very mastery, the practice of gathering-in that he performs is manifest in manifold ways.

Here, "the body of sentient beings" means that the distinctions of the defilement of karma, birth, affliction, and false discrimination, and the distinctions between the realm of form and the formless realm, are all known just as they are. As the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva knows the body of sentient beings as the body of gathered karma, the body of retribution, the body of affliction, the body of form, and knows the formless body."

"The body of lands" should be understood as the distinctions among a thousand and so forth of world-systems; the distinctions between pure and impure world-systems are all well discriminated and known, and the distinctions among vast world-systems and the rest are all well discriminated and known. As the sūtra says, from "this bodhisattva knows the body of lands" all the way to "knows the distinct mark of their directional network." The meaning of "the vast mark" and the other clauses is as already explained within the First Ground. "The distinctions of provisional designation belonging to the body of karmic retribution, the śrāvaka, the pratyekabuddha, and the bodhisattva" means that own-characteristic and shared characteristic, in their distinctions, are discriminated through provisional designation, since there is no self and no person in any of them. As the sūtra says, from "this bodhisattva knows the distinctions of provisional designation belonging to the body of karmic retribution" all the way to "knows the distinctions of provisional designation belonging to the body of the bodhisattva."

"This bodhisattva knows the body of the Tathāgata" means: manifesting the accomplishment of true enlightenment — this is the body of enlightenment; vowing to be born in Tuṣita Heaven — this is the body of vow; every buddha's own responsive transformation — this is the body of transformation; his own relics upholding the Dharma — this is the body that upholds them; every one of his genuine, retributive bodies — this is the body adorned with excellent marks; every radiance that gathers in and subdues sentient beings — this is the body of radiance; every mastery and liberation of mind, whether the same as or different from the mundane and the supramundane — this is the mind-made body; every unshared cause able to accomplish vast and great benefit — this is the body of merit; every unconditioned realm belonging to the Tathāgata — this is the body of the Dharma; and every unobstructed wisdom — this is the body of wisdom. It is for this reason that this wisdom is able to accomplish every task, and every distinction among these tasks it is entirely able to know. As the sūtra says, from "this bodhisattva knows the body of the Tathāgata, the body of enlightenment" all the way to "knows the body of wisdom."

"This bodhisattva knows the body of wisdom" means that the distinctions among the wisdom of hearing and reflection, the wisdom of cultivation, the wisdom of fruit and practice, and the wisdom of the mundane and the supramundane, are all known just as they are. As the sūtra says, from "this bodhisattva well knows the body of wisdom" all the way to "knows the mark of the trainee and the one beyond training."

"This bodhisattva knows the body of the Dharma in its mark of equality" means that boundless Dharma-gates make manifest a single, equal Dharma-body; that it is grasped just as it is heard; that, according with the faculties and nature of the sentient beings being transformed, it is taught with distinctions suited to the time; that there is the distinct mark of what has faculties and what has none; and that the distinctions of its own supreme mark are known — all of this he is entirely able to know. As the sūtra says, from "this bodhisattva knows the body of the Dharma in its mark of equality" all the way to "knows its mark of the distinctions among the dharmas of the Buddha, the Dharma, and the noble Saṅgha."

"This bodhisattva knows the body of space" means knowing its inexhaustible mark, its all-pervading mark, its invisible mark, its unobstructed mark, and its unconditioned mark, and being able to receive throughout every colored mark on account of color, discriminating and entirely knowing all of this. As the sūtra says, from "this bodhisattva knows the body of space in its boundless mark" all the way to "knows the mark of the distinct differences of manifest, colored bodies." In this way, the three kinds of mastery belonging to the purifying of buddha-lands, within the Eighth Ground, have now been explained. Next will be explained the attainment of the ten kinds of mastery.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang's richest move in this passage is mapping the ten bodies onto the Yogācāra's own three-nature doctrine: 初中十身內，初三是染分、次六是淨分、後一是不二分，各言分者，此是同一大緣起法義分出故。如《攝論》中於依他起上離染分名遍計，清淨分名圓成，不二分為依他，是故若見一分，餘分性不異，皆融通全攝此中 — "among these ten bodies, the first three are the defiled portion, the next six are the pure portion, and the last is the non-dual portion. Each is called a 'portion' because they are all divided out from one and the same great matter of dependent origination. As in the [Mahāyāna]saṃgraha, where, upon the very ground of the dependent nature, the portion free of defilement is called the imagined nature, the pure portion is called the fully accomplished nature, and the non-dual portion is the dependent nature itself — for this reason, if one sees any single portion, the nature of every remaining portion is no different from it, all fused and interpenetrating, wholly gathered within this one." The body of sentient beings, the body of lands, and the karmic-retribution body stand in for the imagined nature; the six bodies from the śrāvaka through the Dharma-body stand in for the fully accomplished; and the body of space, precisely because it is spoken of purely in terms of the exhaustion of every characteristic-mark, is made to represent the dependent nature that underlies both.

On the sūtra's own claim that each body can be taken up as any other, Fazang reads a startlingly large number out of the passage's own compressed phrasing, together with a threefold rationale for how it can possibly be so: 通自身有十，一身亦十，一度相作成一百一十身，皆無礙自在顯現...略論三種：一約法性融通門...二約緣起相由門...三約菩薩自在智力，以此位中遠前二門無礙道理，是故能作 — "body altogether has ten, and each single body also has ten within it; taken together in one reckoning, this makes up a hundred and ten bodies, every one of them manifest without obstruction, in mastery... treated briefly under three headings: first, the gateway of the fusion and interpenetration of the nature of dharmas; second, the gateway of dependent origination's own mutual reliance; and third, the bodhisattva's own power of mastery and wisdom, since, at this stage, he has gone beyond the first two gateways into the very principle of the unobstructed path, and is for this reason able to act in this way." The bodhisattva does not merely resemble a śrāvaka or a Tathāgata when the occasion calls for it — on Fazang's reading, each of the ten bodies genuinely contains all ten within it, and it is only at this ground that the power to actualize that latent interpenetration is finally his.

Part 38 Complete

This closes Part 38, within Acalā, the Immovable Ground: the treatise's fourth structural division, 淨佛國土分, the division of purifying buddha-lands, opening as this bodhisattva arises, from great skillful means, a mind of effortless action, and attains mastery over the receptacle-world in five described aspects — knowing a world-system's formation and destruction, the four elements and every particle of dust that composes them, and the three realms themselves, all according with however the mind desires to know or to manifest. He then attains mastery over the world of sentient beings, manifesting his own body according with the differing faith and resolve of sentient beings across countless buddha-lands, from the śramaṇa and the brāhmaṇa up through the very assembly of Brahmā, and from the śrāvaka's own body up through the buddha's own body. The part closes with the profound wisdom of ultimate meaning, attained through departing from every discrimination of bodily characteristics and realizing the equality of bodies — expounded at unusual length through a tenfold taxonomy of the bodies of sentient beings, lands, karmic retribution, the śrāvaka, the pratyekabuddha, the bodhisattva, the Tathāgata, wisdom, the Dharma, and space, a taxonomy Fazang reads as mapped onto the three-nature doctrine and as ten bodies each containing all ten within it. Part 39 turns to the bodhisattva's mastery over his own life-span, attaining ten named kinds of mastery and becoming one of inconceivable and immeasurable wisdom, before the treatise closes this ground with the threefold "great excellence" of wisdom, of karmic activity, and of the merit that supports them both.
