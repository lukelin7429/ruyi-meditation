Seeking the Dharma, Renouncing All Wealth, and Entering the Eight Absorptions

The bodhisattva's world-weariness continues: deep mindfulness of the entire realm of sentient beings, aimed toward the wisdom of omniscience; his resolve never to depart from the unobstructed, liberating wisdom, traced back through a chain of five dependent supports to the wisdom born of hearing; his day-and-night seeking of the true Dharma; his willingness to hold back no wealth whatsoever, even his own body, for the Dharma's sake; and, at last, his withdrawal into a quiet and secluded place to enter, in unbroken sequence, the four dhyānas and the four formless absorptions — followed by the treatise's own account of why he enters them, for the sake of five kinds of sentient being.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 21 of 52 complete Source: Taishō T1522, 十地經論 (Vasubandhu, tr. Bodhiruci et al., Northern Wei) Reading notes after Jingying Huiyuan's 十地經論義記 (X0753)

Mindfulness Toward Omniscience and the Unobstructed, Liberating Wisdom

Chinese Source

經曰：菩薩如是善厭離一切有為行已，深念一切眾生界，趣一切智智無量利益。即時依如來智慧救度眾生，作是思惟：此諸眾生墮在大苦煩惱業中，以何方便行而拔濟之，令住涅槃畢竟之樂？

論曰：是中厭離深念利益，示現三種因：一者遠離妄想因，善厭離一切有為行故；二者不捨世間因，深念一切眾生故；三者發精進因，趣一切智智無量利益故。於中趣利益處者，能修行彼道故。深念者，能善化眾生故。依如來智慧救度眾生，此言示現發起方便攝行，如經「作是思惟：此諸眾生墮在大苦煩惱業中，以何方便行而拔濟之，令住涅槃畢竟之樂」故。墮在大苦煩惱業中者，苦者生妄想，煩惱者煩惱妄想，業者業妄想，涅槃畢竟樂者無上涅槃故。是中方便攝行有三種：一、證畢竟盡；二、起上上證畢竟盡；三、彼起依止行。

經曰：是菩薩作如是念：不離無障礙解脫智處。彼無障礙解脫智處，不離一切法如實覺。彼一切法如實覺，不離無行無生行慧。如是智慧光明，不離禪方便決定智慧觀。彼禪方便決定智慧觀，不離聞慧方便。

論曰：證畢竟盡者，住無障礙解脫智中，如經「是菩薩作如是念：不離無障礙解脫智處」故。彼盡以如來所說，一切法隨順如實覺起，以此如實覺起彼無障礙解脫智，如經「彼無障礙解脫智處，不離一切法如實覺」故。此自相同相無分別行慧，如經「彼一切法如實覺，不離無行無生行慧」故。如是智慧光明不離禪方便決定智慧觀者，彼慧此中名光明，依是光明故名明地。彼菩薩於禪定中方便決定智慧觀，如經「彼禪方便決定智慧觀，不離聞慧方便」故。彼禪方便者，得勝進禪故。決定者，於他決定故。智慧觀者，自智慧觀故。是名起上上證畢竟盡。彼如是智慧觀依聞慧方便得，此是彼起依止行，聞慧方便是起所依，是故修行是名彼起依止行。是中行者，日夜求法聞法，如是次第依教依義。

經曰：菩薩如是正觀知已，轉復勤修求正法行。日夜常求，聞法、憙法、樂法、依法、隨法、益法、思法、究竟法、歸依法、隨順行法。

論曰：是中求正法行者，依經教依義如前說。日夜常求聞法憙法樂法者，無慢心、無妬心、無折伏他心問義故。依法者，依大乘教法，自見正取不忘失故。隨法益法思法者，依讀誦、依為他說、依靜處思義故。究竟法者，依定修行故。歸依法者，依出世間智故。隨順行法者，依解脫，於諸佛解脫法隨順彼行故。是中求正法行，常求聞法，此初二句，示現常勤行故；喜法等九句，示現正修行故。彼常勤行以何為因？示現恭敬重法畢竟盡故。彼菩薩以財為首，於財寶中及王位處、或生天處，世間淨中及以己身，以法為重。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: The bodhisattva, having thus well grown world-weary of every conditioned activity, deeply calls to mind the entire realm of sentient beings, advancing toward the boundless benefit of the wisdom of omniscience. At that very time, relying on the tathāgata's wisdom to rescue and liberate sentient beings, he gives rise to this reflection: these sentient beings have fallen into great suffering, affliction, and karma — by what skillful means shall I pull them out and rescue them, causing them to abide in the ultimate bliss of nirvāṇa?

Commentary: Here, world-weariness, deep mindfulness, and benefit make manifest three kinds of cause: first, the cause of departing from false thought, since he well grows world-weary of every conditioned activity; second, the cause of not abandoning the world, since he deeply calls to mind every sentient being; and third, the cause of arousing diligence, since he advances toward the boundless benefit of the wisdom of omniscience. Herein, "advancing toward the place of benefit" means that he is able to cultivate and practice that very path. "Deep mindfulness" means that he is able to well transform sentient beings. "Relying on the tathāgata's wisdom to rescue and liberate sentient beings" — this phrase makes manifest the arousing of the practice of skillful means that gathers all in — as the sūtra says, "he gives rise to this reflection: these sentient beings have fallen into great suffering, affliction, and karma; by what skillful means shall I pull them out and rescue them, causing them to abide in the ultimate bliss of nirvāṇa." "Fallen into great suffering, affliction, and karma": "suffering" means the false thought of birth; "affliction" means the false thought of affliction; "karma" means the false thought of karma; and "the ultimate bliss of nirvāṇa" means unsurpassed nirvāṇa. Herein, the practice of skillful means that gathers all in is of three kinds: first, the ultimate realization of exhaustion; second, the rising higher and higher toward the ultimate realization of exhaustion; and third, the practice that supports this arising.

Sūtra: This bodhisattva gives rise to this thought: I shall never depart from the abode of the unobstructed, liberating wisdom. That abode of the unobstructed, liberating wisdom never departs from the true, suchness-realization of every dharma. That true, suchness-realization of every dharma never departs from the wisdom of activity without activity, arising without arising. Such a light of wisdom never departs from the skillful means of dhyāna and the resolute contemplation of wisdom. That skillful means of dhyāna and resolute contemplation of wisdom never departs from the skillful means of the wisdom born of hearing.

Commentary: "The ultimate realization of exhaustion" means abiding within the unobstructed, liberating wisdom — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva gives rise to this thought: I shall never depart from the abode of the unobstructed, liberating wisdom." That exhaustion arises through according with the true, suchness-realization of every dharma as taught by the tathāgata, and it is by means of this true, suchness-realization that the unobstructed, liberating wisdom arises — as the sūtra says, "that abode of the unobstructed, liberating wisdom never departs from the true, suchness-realization of every dharma." This is the wisdom of activity that discriminates neither the character proper to a dharma nor the character it shares with others — as the sūtra says, "that true, suchness-realization of every dharma never departs from the wisdom of activity without activity, arising without arising." As for "such a light of wisdom never departs from the skillful means of dhyāna and the resolute contemplation of wisdom" — this very wisdom is here named "light," and it is in dependence on this light that the ground is named Prabhākarī, the Luminous Ground. This bodhisattva, within meditative concentration, resolutely contemplates with wisdom by skillful means — as the sūtra says, "that skillful means of dhyāna and resolute contemplation of wisdom never departs from the skillful means of the wisdom born of hearing." "The skillful means of dhyāna" means attaining a dhyāna that surpasses and advances; "resolute" means being resolute with respect to others; and "the contemplation of wisdom" means contemplating with his own wisdom. This is called "the rising higher and higher toward the ultimate realization of exhaustion." This very contemplation of wisdom is attained in dependence on the skillful means of the wisdom born of hearing, and this is "the practice that supports this arising" — the skillful means of the wisdom born of hearing is what this arising relies upon, and so this cultivation and practice is called "the practice that supports this arising." Herein, "practice" means seeking and hearing the Dharma day and night, thus in due order relying on its teaching and relying on its meaning.

Sūtra: The bodhisattva, having thus correctly contemplated and come to know this, turns and further diligently cultivates the practice of seeking the true Dharma. Day and night he constantly seeks it: hearing the Dharma, delighting in the Dharma, taking joy in the Dharma, relying on the Dharma, according with the Dharma, benefiting by the Dharma, reflecting on the Dharma, bringing the Dharma to its consummation, taking refuge in the Dharma, and practicing in accordance with the Dharma.

Commentary: Herein, "the practice of seeking the true Dharma" means relying on its teaching and relying on its meaning, as explained before. "Day and night he constantly seeks it: hearing the Dharma, delighting in the Dharma, taking joy in the Dharma" — this is because he has no mind of pride, no mind of envy, and no mind bent on subduing others when he inquires into its meaning. "Relying on the Dharma" means relying on the teaching of the Great Vehicle, seeing and grasping it correctly for himself, never letting it slip from memory. "According with the Dharma, benefiting by the Dharma, and reflecting on the Dharma" mean, respectively, relying on reading and reciting it, relying on expounding it for others, and relying on reflecting on its meaning in a quiet place. "Bringing the Dharma to its consummation" means relying on cultivating and practicing meditative concentration. "Taking refuge in the Dharma" means relying on transmundane wisdom. "Practicing in accordance with the Dharma" means relying on liberation, according with and practicing the buddhas' own dharmas of liberation. Herein, "seeking the practice of the true Dharma" and "constantly seeking to hear the Dharma" — these first two clauses make manifest his constant, diligent practice; the nine clauses beginning with "delighting in the Dharma" make manifest his correct cultivation and practice. By what cause does he constantly, diligently practice? This makes manifest that his reverence for the Dharma, held in high esteem, is brought to its utmost exhaustion. This bodhisattva takes wealth as the first of his renunciations: amid treasures and riches, and the station of kingship, or birth among the heavens, in the purity of the world, and even his own person, he holds the Dharma alone to be of weight.

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Huiyuan reads the sūtra's five-stage chain — unobstructed wisdom depending on suchness-realization, depending on the wisdom of activity without activity, depending on the light of dhyāna-contemplation, depending on the wisdom born of hearing — as the treatise's own compression of five stages into three functional roles: 初無礙智說為證盡，末後聞慧說為第三彼起依止…起中漸增，故云上上。終窮佛慧，名為證盡 — "the first, unobstructed wisdom, is called 'the ultimate realization of exhaustion'; the last, the wisdom born of hearing, is called the third, 'the practice that supports this arising' … within this arising there is a gradual increase, and this is why it is called 'higher and higher'; the final exhaustion of the buddhas' own wisdom is named 'the ultimate realization of exhaustion.'" The middle three terms are not separate stations so much as one continuous climb, hearing feeding contemplation, contemplation feeding realization, realization feeding the unobstructed wisdom itself — with hearing named, deliberately, as what the entire climb depends on from the start.

On why the treatise calls this same wisdom "light" at exactly this point in the sequence, Huiyuan draws the ground's own name directly out of the text rather than treating it as a separate etymological aside: 彼慧此中名光明，依是光明故名明地 — "this very wisdom is here named 'light,' and it is in dependence on this light that the ground is named the Luminous Ground." The wisdom in question is the same wisdom of activity without activity discussed one clause earlier; what changes is only that here, within the Third Ground, it first becomes luminous — which is, on Huiyuan's reading, the entire reason this ground bears the name Prabhākarī in the first place, rather than a coincidence of imagery.

Renouncing All Wealth for the Dharma's Sake

Chinese Source

經曰：菩薩如是方便求佛法故，無有諸財錢穀倉庫寶藏等事而不能捨。於此物中不生難想，但於說法者生難遭想。是菩薩為求佛法故，無有所用外財而不能捨，無有內財而不能捨，無有所作供給尊事而不能行，無有憍慢我慢大慢等而不能捨，質直柔軟故，無有身苦而不能受。是菩薩得成就勝財心，若聞一句未曾聞法，勝得滿三千大千世界珍寶。是菩薩得聞一偈正法，生上財想，勝得轉輪聖王位。復得勝財心，若得未曾聞法能淨菩薩行，勝得釋提桓因、梵天王處無量劫住。是菩薩若有人來作如是言：我與汝佛所說法一句能淨菩薩行，汝今若能入大熾然火坑受大苦者當以相與。是菩薩作如是念：我受一句佛所說法能淨菩薩行故，尚於三千大千世界滿中大火從梵天投下，何況入小火坑。我等法應盡受一切諸地獄苦猶應求法，何況人中諸小苦惱。菩薩如是發精進行修諸正法，隨所聞法，於寂靜處悉能正觀。

論曰：彼菩薩為重法故能捨一切財物，如經「菩薩如是方便求佛法，乃至但於說法者生難遭想」故。彼財所得處田宅等外財，彼亦能捨，如經「是菩薩為求佛法故，無有所用外財而不能捨」故。及彼財所為內法此亦能捨，如經「無有內財而不能捨」故。及彼所為是亦能捨，身行恭敬奉給等，如經「無有所作供給尊事而不能行」故。誰於此物能得能捨？彼高大意此亦能捨，如經「無有憍慢我慢大慢等而不能捨，質直柔軟」故。所應護者彼亦能捨，種種身苦而無不受，如經「無有身苦而不能受」故。於得眾多勝妙財寶然不愛樂，重法心成故，如經「是菩薩得成就勝財心，乃至勝得滿三千大千世界珍寶」故。是名於財中勝。云何王位等中勝？是菩薩得法轉生喜心成就，勝得轉輪王位、釋梵天王等，如經「是菩薩得聞一偈正法，生上財想，乃至勝得釋提桓因、梵天王處無量劫住」故。為求法故，投身滿三千大千世界熾然火中，及地獄中久受苦惱，如經「是菩薩若有人來作如是言：我與汝佛所說法一句，乃至何況人中諸小苦惱」故。是中聞一句法者，謂聞字句法。得聞一偈法者，謂聞偈法故。能淨菩薩行者，謂聞義故。此正修行，以何為因？示現依寂靜處思惟正觀故，如經「菩薩如是發精進行，乃至悉能正觀」故。云何厭分？是菩薩聞諸法已，知如說修行乃得佛法，入禪無色無量神通彼非樂處，於中不染，必定應作故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, seeking the buddha-dharma by such skillful means, holds back nothing whatsoever among wealth, coin, grain, storehouse, or treasury — there is nothing among these that he is unable to relinquish. Toward these things he gives rise to no thought that they are hard to part with; it is only toward one who expounds the Dharma that he gives rise to the thought that such a person is hard to encounter. This bodhisattva, for the sake of seeking the buddha-dharma, holds back no outer wealth that he is unable to relinquish; he holds back no inner wealth that he is unable to relinquish; there is nothing he is unable to do in rendering service and homage; there is no pride, no pride of self, no towering pride of any kind that he is unable to relinquish — being upright and gentle in nature — and there is no bodily suffering that he is unable to bear. This bodhisattva attains the accomplishment of the mind that holds the supreme wealth: were he to hear a single phrase of the Dharma never heard before, this would surpass obtaining treasures filling the great trichiliocosm. This bodhisattva, upon hearing a single verse of the true Dharma, gives rise to the thought of it as the highest wealth, surpassing the attainment of the station of a Wheel-Turning King. He further attains the mind that holds the supreme wealth: were he to obtain a Dharma never heard before, able to purify the bodhisattva's practice, this would surpass the attainment of the station of Śakra or the Brahmā king, abiding there for boundless eons. Should someone come to this bodhisattva and speak thus: "I shall give you a single phrase of the Dharma spoken by the Buddha, able to purify the bodhisattva's practice, if you are now able to enter a great blazing pit of fire and undergo great suffering" — this bodhisattva gives rise to this thought: for the sake of receiving a single phrase of the Dharma spoken by the Buddha, able to purify the bodhisattva's practice, I would even fling myself down from the Brahmā heaven into a great fire filling the great trichiliocosm — how much more so into a small pit of fire. According to my own Dharma, I should undergo every suffering of every hell and still seek the Dharma — how much more so the small sufferings among human beings. The bodhisattva, thus arousing diligent practice, cultivates every true dharma, and, according with whatever Dharma he has heard, is everywhere able to correctly contemplate it in a quiet, still place.

Commentary: This bodhisattva, holding the Dharma in high esteem, is able to relinquish every material possession — as the sūtra says, "the bodhisattva, seeking the buddha-dharma by such skillful means," all the way to "it is only toward one who expounds the Dharma that he gives rise to the thought that such a person is hard to encounter." The outer wealth obtained through such wealth — fields, dwellings, and the like — he is also able to relinquish, as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, for the sake of seeking the buddha-dharma, holds back no outer wealth that he is unable to relinquish." And the inner matters undertaken for the sake of that wealth, this too he is able to relinquish, as the sūtra says, "he holds back no inner wealth that he is unable to relinquish." And what is done for the sake of these, this too he is able to relinquish — bodily conduct in reverent service and homage, and the rest — as the sūtra says, "there is nothing he is unable to do in rendering service and homage." Who is it that is able to obtain and yet able to relinquish these things? His own lofty, self-important intent, this too he is able to relinquish, as the sūtra says, "there is no pride, no pride of self, no towering pride of any kind that he is unable to relinquish, being upright and gentle in nature." What ought to be protected, this too he is able to relinquish, undergoing every manner of bodily suffering without exception, as the sūtra says, "there is no bodily suffering that he is unable to bear." Toward obtaining a great abundance of supreme, sublime treasures, he yet takes no delight in them, since his mind that holds the Dharma in high esteem is already accomplished — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva attains the accomplishment of the mind that holds the supreme wealth," all the way to "this would surpass obtaining treasures filling the great trichiliocosm." This is called excelling with respect to wealth. What is meant by excelling with respect to the station of kingship and the rest? This bodhisattva, attaining the Dharma, gives rise to a mind of joy thereby accomplished, surpassing the attainment of the station of a Wheel-Turning King, or of Śakra or the Brahmā king, and the rest — as the sūtra says, "this bodhisattva, upon hearing a single verse of the true Dharma, gives rise to the thought of it as the highest wealth," all the way to "this would surpass the attainment of the station of Śakra or the Brahmā king, abiding there for boundless eons." For the sake of seeking the Dharma, he flings his own body into a blazing fire filling the great trichiliocosm, and undergoes affliction within the hells for a long time — as the sūtra says, "should someone come to this bodhisattva and speak thus: 'I shall give you a single phrase of the Dharma spoken by the Buddha,'" all the way to "how much more so the small sufferings among human beings." Herein, "hearing a single phrase of the Dharma" means hearing the Dharma in a single word or phrase; "obtaining and hearing a single verse of the Dharma" means hearing the Dharma in a verse; and "able to purify the bodhisattva's practice" means hearing its meaning. As for this correct cultivation and practice, by what cause does it come about? This makes manifest that, relying on a quiet, still place, he reflects and correctly contemplates — as the sūtra says, "the bodhisattva, thus arousing diligent practice," all the way to "is everywhere able to correctly contemplate." What is meant by the division of world-weariness? This bodhisattva, having heard every dharma, knows that only by practicing as it is taught does he attain the buddha-dharma; and so he enters dhyāna, the formless absorptions, and boundless spiritual powers — these are not, for him, a place of delight, and he remains untainted within them, since this is what must necessarily be done.

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Huiyuan reads the sūtra's run of renunciations as a deliberately graded sequence of seven, not a single undifferentiated act of generosity, and names what is given up at each step in turn: 於財寶中，是初校量；及王位處，第二校量…及與己身，第五校量 — "amid treasures, this is the first comparison; the station of kingship, the second comparison … and even one's own person, the fifth comparison" — running from outer wealth, through land and dwelling, through wife and children, through bodily service, through pride itself, to bodily pain, and finally to treasure beyond counting, each relinquishment harder to make than the one before it: 遊財易捨、田宅難離…外財易捨、妻子難離…身敬易為、心慢難遣…情慢易息、身苦難忍 — "wandering wealth is easy to relinquish, but land and dwelling are hard to leave behind … outer wealth is easy to relinquish, but wife and children are hard to leave behind … bodily reverence is easy to perform, but a proud mind is hard to dispel … pride of feeling is easy to quiet, but bodily suffering is hard to endure."

On the odd phrase "the mind that holds the supreme wealth," Huiyuan is careful to block a literalist misreading: 知法勝資名勝財心，非過寶物名勝財也 — "it is knowing that the Dharma surpasses every resource that is called 'the mind that holds the supreme wealth' — it is not that the Dharma is called 'supreme wealth' by literally exceeding treasure in quantity." The bodhisattva does not weigh a verse of Dharma against a trichiliocosm of jewels on the same scale and find the verse heavier; he has already ceased to use that scale at all, which is exactly why the fire-pit episode that follows reads, on this account, not as a stated calculation but as the plain report of a mind for which the comparison no longer applies.

Withdrawing Into Seclusion — The Four Dhyānas and Four Formless Absorptions

Chinese Source

經曰：是菩薩聞諸法已，降伏其心，於空閑處心作是念：如說行者乃得佛法，不可但以口之所言而得清淨。是菩薩住此明地因如說行故，即離諸欲惡不善法，有覺有觀離生喜樂，入初禪行。是菩薩滅覺觀，內清淨心一處無覺無觀定生喜樂，入二禪行。是菩薩離喜，行捨憶念安慧，身受樂，諸賢聖能說能捨念受樂，入三禪行。是菩薩斷苦斷樂先滅憂喜，不苦不樂，捨念清淨，入四禪行。是菩薩過一切色想，滅一切有對想，不念一切別異想，知無邊虛空，即入無邊虛空處行。是菩薩過一切無邊虛空想，知無邊識，即入無邊識處行。是菩薩過一切無邊識想，知無所有，即入無所有處行。是菩薩過一切無所有處，知非有想非無想安隱，即入非有想非無想處行。但隨順法行故而不樂著。

論曰：以何義故，入禪無色無量神通？為五種眾生故。一、為禪樂憍慢眾生故，入諸禪。二、為無色解脫憍慢眾生故，入無色定。三、為苦惱眾生令安善處永與樂故，應解彼苦令不受故，入慈悲無量。四、為得解脫眾生故，入喜捨無量。五為邪歸依眾生故，入勝神通力，令正信義故。此地得不退禪故，名為三昧地。前地非無三昧，此地勝故。是中禪無色差別有四種：一、離障；二、修行對治；三、修行利益；四、彼二依止三昧。是初禪中離諸欲惡不善法是名離障，如經「即離諸欲惡不善法」故。有覺有觀是名修行對治，如經「有覺有觀」故。喜樂是名修行利益，如經「離生喜樂」故。入初禪行是名彼二依止三昧，如經「入初禪行」故。第二禪中滅覺觀是名離障，如經「滅覺觀」故，內淨是名修行對治滅覺觀障，如經「內清淨心一處無覺無觀」故。心一處者，修無漏不斷三昧，行一境故。定生喜樂是名修行利益，如經「定生喜樂」故。入二禪行是名彼二依止三昧，如經「入二禪行」故。第三禪中離喜是名離障，如經「離喜」故。行捨憶念安慧是名修行對治，如經「行捨憶念安慧」故。身受樂是名修行利益，如經「身受樂」故。入三禪行是名彼二依止三昧，如經「入三禪行」故。第四禪中斷苦斷樂先滅憂喜是名離障，如經「斷苦斷樂先滅憂喜」故。捨念清淨是名修行對治，如經「捨念清淨」故。不苦不樂是名修行利益，如經「不苦不樂」故。入四禪行是名彼二依止三昧，如經「入四禪行」故。無色三摩跋提亦有四種：一、離障；二、修行對治；三、修行利益；四、彼二依止三昧。過一切色想者，過眼識想故。滅一切有對想者，耳鼻舌身識和合想滅故。不念一切別異想者，不念意識和合想故，意識分別一切法故，說別異想是名離障。如是對治過色等境界想，不分別色等境界見無我故，是名修行對治。知無邊虛空者，是名修行利益。即入無邊虛空行者，是名彼二依止三昧。過一切無邊虛空想者，是名離障。如是對治，過彼無邊虛空見外念麁分別過患，是名修行對治。知無邊識者，是名修行利益。即入無邊識行者，是名彼二依止三昧。過一切無邊識想者，是名離障。如是對治，過彼無邊識見麁事念分別過患，是名修行對治。知無所有者，是名修行利益。即入無所有行者，是名彼二依止三昧。過一切無所有想者，是名離障。如是對治，過彼無所有見麁念分別過患，是名修行對治。知非有想非無想安隱者，是名修行利益。即入非有想非無想行者，是名彼二依止三昧。已說厭分。云何厭果？謂四無量、五神通等。云何四無量？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sūtra: This bodhisattva, having heard every dharma, subdues his own mind, and, in a quiet and secluded place, gives rise to this thought: only one who practices as it is taught attains the buddha-dharma; one cannot attain purity merely through what is spoken by the mouth. This bodhisattva, dwelling in this Luminous Ground, because he practices as it is taught, thereupon becomes free of desire and every unwholesome, evil dharma; with thought and examination, born of seclusion, filled with joy and bliss, he enters the practice of the first dhyāna. This bodhisattva extinguishes thought and examination; his mind, inwardly purified and unified, without thought and without examination, born of concentration, filled with joy and bliss, he enters the practice of the second dhyāna. This bodhisattva, departing from joy, abiding in equanimity, mindfulness, and clear awareness, experiences bliss in the body — that bliss which the noble ones are able to describe, and, abiding in equanimity and mindfulness, are able to relinquish — and he enters the practice of the third dhyāna. This bodhisattva cuts off suffering and cuts off bliss, having first extinguished dejection and elation; neither suffering nor blissful, with equanimity and mindfulness purified, he enters the practice of the fourth dhyāna. This bodhisattva, passing entirely beyond every perception of form, extinguishing every perception of resistance, giving no thought to any perception of manifold difference, knows the infinity of space, and thereupon enters the practice of the sphere of infinite space. This bodhisattva, passing entirely beyond every perception of infinite space, knows infinite consciousness, and thereupon enters the practice of the sphere of infinite consciousness. This bodhisattva, passing entirely beyond every perception of infinite consciousness, knows there to be nothing whatsoever, and thereupon enters the practice of the sphere of nothingness. This bodhisattva, passing entirely beyond the sphere of nothingness, knows the peace of neither perception nor non-perception, and thereupon enters the practice of the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception. Yet it is only in accordance with the practice of the Dharma that he does so, and he takes no delight in or attachment to it.

Commentary: For what meaning does he enter dhyāna, the formless absorptions, and boundless spiritual powers? It is for the sake of five kinds of sentient being. First, for the sake of sentient beings proud with the pride of the bliss of dhyāna, he enters the various dhyānas. Second, for the sake of sentient beings proud with the pride of formless liberation, he enters the formless absorptions. Third, for the sake of sentient beings afflicted with suffering, wishing to settle them in a good and wholesome place and forever bestow bliss upon them, and, being obliged to release them from that suffering so that they no longer undergo it, he enters the immeasurable states of loving-kindness and compassion. Fourth, for the sake of sentient beings who have attained liberation, he enters the immeasurable states of sympathetic joy and equanimity. Fifth, for the sake of sentient beings of wrongful refuge, he enters the supreme power of spiritual penetration, causing them to attain correct faith in its meaning. Since this ground attains a dhyāna from which there is no falling back, it is named the ground of samādhi; it is not that the preceding grounds are without samādhi, but that this ground's samādhi excels.

Herein, the distinction among dhyāna and the formless states is of four kinds: first, departing from hindrance; second, the antidote cultivated and practiced; third, the benefit cultivated and practiced; and fourth, the samādhi on which these two rely. Within the first dhyāna, becoming free of desire and every unwholesome, evil dharma is called departing from hindrance — as the sūtra says, "becomes free of desire and every unwholesome, evil dharma." Having thought and examination is called the antidote cultivated and practiced — as the sūtra says, "with thought and examination." Joy and bliss are called the benefit cultivated and practiced — as the sūtra says, "born of seclusion, filled with joy and bliss." Entering the practice of the first dhyāna is called the samādhi on which these two rely — as the sūtra says, "enters the practice of the first dhyāna." Within the second dhyāna, extinguishing thought and examination is called departing from hindrance — as the sūtra says, "extinguishes thought and examination." Inward purity is called the antidote cultivated and practiced that extinguishes the hindrance of thought and examination — as the sūtra says, "his mind, inwardly purified and unified, without thought and without examination." "The mind unified" means cultivating the unbroken samādhi free of outflow, practicing with a single object. Joy and bliss born of concentration are called the benefit cultivated and practiced — as the sūtra says, "born of concentration, filled with joy and bliss." Entering the practice of the second dhyāna is called the samādhi on which these two rely — as the sūtra says, "enters the practice of the second dhyāna." Within the third dhyāna, departing from joy is called departing from hindrance — as the sūtra says, "departing from joy." Abiding in equanimity, mindfulness, and clear awareness is called the antidote cultivated and practiced — as the sūtra says, "abiding in equanimity, mindfulness, and clear awareness." Bliss experienced in the body is called the benefit cultivated and practiced — as the sūtra says, "experiences bliss in the body." Entering the practice of the third dhyāna is called the samādhi on which these two rely — as the sūtra says, "enters the practice of the third dhyāna." Within the fourth dhyāna, cutting off suffering and cutting off bliss, having first extinguished dejection and elation, is called departing from hindrance — as the sūtra says, "cuts off suffering and cuts off bliss, having first extinguished dejection and elation." Equanimity and mindfulness purified are called the antidote cultivated and practiced — as the sūtra says, "with equanimity and mindfulness purified." Being neither suffering nor blissful is called the benefit cultivated and practiced — as the sūtra says, "neither suffering nor blissful." Entering the practice of the fourth dhyāna is called the samādhi on which these two rely — as the sūtra says, "enters the practice of the fourth dhyāna."

The formless attainments likewise are of four kinds: first, departing from hindrance; second, the antidote cultivated and practiced; third, the benefit cultivated and practiced; and fourth, the samādhi on which these two rely. "Passing entirely beyond every perception of form" means passing beyond the perception belonging to the eye-consciousness. "Extinguishing every perception of resistance" means extinguishing the perception bound up with the ear-, nose-, tongue-, and body-consciousnesses. "Giving no thought to any perception of manifold difference" means giving no thought to the perception bound up with mind-consciousness — since mind-consciousness discriminates every dharma, this perception of manifold difference is called departing from hindrance. Such an antidote, passing beyond the perception of the object-realm of form and the rest, no longer discriminating the object-realm of form and the rest, seeing there to be no self — this is called the antidote cultivated and practiced. Knowing the infinity of space is called the benefit cultivated and practiced. Thereupon entering the practice of the sphere of infinite space is called the samādhi on which these two rely. Passing entirely beyond every perception of infinite space is called departing from hindrance. Such an antidote, passing beyond that infinite space, seeing the fault of the coarse discrimination of external thought, is called the antidote cultivated and practiced. Knowing infinite consciousness is called the benefit cultivated and practiced. Thereupon entering the practice of the sphere of infinite consciousness is called the samādhi on which these two rely. Passing entirely beyond every perception of infinite consciousness is called departing from hindrance. Such an antidote, passing beyond that infinite consciousness, seeing the fault of the coarse discrimination of that very matter, is called the antidote cultivated and practiced. Knowing there to be nothing whatsoever is called the benefit cultivated and practiced. Thereupon entering the practice of the sphere of nothingness is called the samādhi on which these two rely. Passing entirely beyond every perception of nothingness is called departing from hindrance. Such an antidote, passing beyond that nothingness, seeing the fault of coarse discrimination, is called the antidote cultivated and practiced. Knowing the peace of neither perception nor non-perception is called the benefit cultivated and practiced. Thereupon entering the practice of the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception is called the samādhi on which these two rely. The division of world-weariness has thus been explained. What is meant by the fruit of world-weariness? It means the four immeasurables, the five spiritual powers, and the rest. What is meant by the four immeasurables?

Reading note — after Jingying Huiyuan

Before working through the eightfold sequence itself, Huiyuan pauses on why a bodhisattva who should, by rights, already be advancing along the path beyond the world would trouble to enter states as ordinary as the four dhyānas at all: 禪等是其凡夫之法 — "dhyāna and the rest are dharmas belonging to the ordinary, unawakened person." His answer names five kinds of sentient being the bodhisattva has in mind in entering them, of which only the first two concern the dhyānas and formless states themselves: 為禪慢入諸禪者，有人修得世俗四禪，或著禪樂、或起慢高，難化以道，是故現入。過彼所得轉其所著，息彼慢意化以道也 — "as for entering the various dhyānas for the sake of beings proud in dhyāna: there are those who cultivate and attain the four worldly dhyānas, and who either cling to the bliss of dhyāna or give rise to towering pride from it, hard to convert to the path — and so the bodhisattva manifests entry into these very states, surpassing what they have attained, in order to turn them from their attachment, quiet their pride, and convert them to the path."

He gives the parallel reasoning for the formless states in the same breath: 為無色慢入無色者，有人修得四無色定謂是涅槃，恃己所得難以道攝，故須現入息彼慢意 — "as for entering the formless absorptions for the sake of beings proud in formlessness: there are those who cultivate and attain the four formless absorptions and take them to be nirvāṇa itself, relying on what they have attained and hard to gather in by the path — and so it is necessary to manifest entry into these states in order to quiet their pride." In both cases the bodhisattva's own entry is not practice for its own sake but a kind of matched surpassing: meeting a practitioner exactly where his pride has settled, and going further, precisely so that the ground beneath that pride gives way. Huiyuan notes, too, that this is why the ground is named 三昧地, "the ground of samādhi" — not because the preceding grounds lack samādhi altogether, 前地非無三昧，此地勝故, but because the samādhi attained here is, for the first time, one from which there is no falling back.

Part 21 Complete

This closes Part 21. The bodhisattva's world-weariness has now carried him from deep mindfulness of the entire realm of sentient beings, aimed toward the wisdom of omniscience, through a five-stage chain of dependence resolving into the unobstructed, liberating wisdom for which this ground is named Prabhākarī, the Luminous Ground; through his day-and-night seeking of the true Dharma; through his willingness to renounce all wealth whatsoever, down to his own body, for the Dharma's sake; and, at last, into a quiet and secluded place, where he enters, in unbroken sequence, the four dhyānas and the four formless absorptions, together with the treatise's own account of why he does so — for the sake of five kinds of sentient being, of whom those proud in the bliss of dhyāna and those proud in formless liberation belong here. The division of world-weariness (厭行分) is now complete. Part 22 turns to its fruit (厭果): the four immeasurable states of loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity, and the five spiritual powers, still within the Third Ground.
