A Thousand Hands and Eyes for a Suffering World

Avalokiteśvara asks the Buddha's permission to speak a dhāraṇī, then tells the story of how he came to have a thousand hands and a thousand eyes in the first place — a vow made on hearing this same mantra once, ages before, that leapt him past seven entire stages of the bodhisattva path in a single instant. What follows is the source text of the Great Compassion Mantra (大悲咒) recited daily in Chinese Buddhist monasteries to this day, together with the vows, the promised protections, the symbolism of the bodhisattva's many hands, and an extensive set of ritual and medical instructions for using it — the whole of which a Song-dynasty Tiantai patriarch later organized into the Great Compassion Repentance (大悲懺), still performed in Chinese temples now.

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Bhagavaddharma's translation (伽梵達磨, "one who has reached the Dharma"), an Indian monk active at the Tang court around 650 CE about whom little else is recorded. This is the earliest and most widely used of some half-dozen Chinese translations of the same dhāraṇī that survive in the canon (T1056–T1064), and it remains the version chanted in Chinese Buddhist practice today. This page follows this site's standing convention for dhāraṇī: the mantra itself is left in its classical Chinese phonetic transliteration rather than translated for meaning, exactly as this site's Śūraṅgama Sūtra pages do with the Śūraṅgama Mantra. A mantra's phonetic shape, not a paraphrased meaning, is what practice actually transmits.

Reading notes throughout draw on Siming Zhili (四明知禮, 960–1028), the Song-dynasty monk who held himself the seventeenth-generation heir of the Tiantai lineage and one of the two great figures of its "Home Mountain" (山家) faction. His Qianshouyan Dabei Xin Zhou Xingfa (千手眼大悲心呪行法, "Ritual Method for the Thousand-Hand-Eye Great Compassion Mantra," T1950) is not a line-by-line doctrinal commentary in the manner of this site's other reading-note sources, but something more consequential: a working ritual manual that organizes this sūtra's own content into ten structured stages of practice — and it is this manual, more or less unchanged, that underlies the Great Compassion Repentance (大悲懺) still performed in Chinese monasteries. Zhili states in his own opening lines that he had recited this dhāraṇī from early childhood without ever having been taught how to practice it properly, and that studying Tiantai's own teaching-and-contemplation system later showed him how. Where his manual maps this sūtra's content onto Tiantai's technical apparatus — the four noble truths, the ten modes of contemplation — this page's reading notes reproduce that mapping, since it is exactly this kind of scaffolding a reader coming to a ritual, dhāraṇī-centered text for the first time is otherwise given no way to see. See also this site's general note on translation method.

The Assembly at Potalaka, and a Question About the Light

Chinese Source

如是我聞：一時，釋迦牟尼佛在補陀落迦山，觀世音宮殿，寶莊嚴道場中，坐寶師子座。其座純以無量雜摩尼寶，而用莊嚴，百寶幢旛，周匝懸列。爾時，如來於彼座上，將欲演說總持陀羅尼故。與無央數菩薩摩訶薩俱，其名曰：總持王菩薩、寶王菩薩、藥王菩薩、藥上菩薩、觀世音菩薩、大勢至菩薩、華嚴菩薩、大莊嚴菩薩、寶藏菩薩、德藏菩薩、金剛藏菩薩、虛空藏菩薩、彌勒菩薩、普賢菩薩、文殊師利菩薩，如是等菩薩摩訶薩，皆是灌頂大法王子。

又與無量無數大聲聞僧，皆行阿羅漢十地，摩訶迦葉而為上首。又與無量梵摩羅天，善吒梵摩而為上首。又與無量欲界諸天子俱，瞿婆伽天子而為上首。又與無量護世四王俱，提頭賴吒而為上首。又與無量天、龍、夜叉、乾闥婆、阿修羅、迦樓羅、緊那羅、摩睺羅伽、人、非人等俱，天德大龍王而為上首。又與無量欲界諸天女俱，童目天女而為上首。又與無量虛空神、江海神、泉源神、河沼神、藥草神、樹林神、舍宅神、水神、火神、地神、風神、土神、山神、石神、宮殿等神，皆來集會。

時觀世音菩薩，於大會中密放神通，光明照曜，十方剎土及此三千大千世界，皆作金色。天宮、龍宮、諸尊神宮，皆悉震動；江河、大海、鐵圍山、須彌山、土山、黑山，亦皆大動。日、月、珠、火、星宿之光，皆悉不現。

於是總持王菩薩，見此希有之相，怪未曾有。即從座起，叉手合掌，以偈問佛，如此神通之相，是誰所放？以偈問曰：「誰於今日成正覺？普放如是大光明，十方剎土皆金色，三千世界亦復然。誰於今日得自在？演放希有大神力，無邊佛國皆震動，龍神宮殿悉不安。今此大眾咸有疑，不測因緣是誰力？為佛菩薩大聲聞？為梵魔天諸釋等？唯願世尊大慈悲，說此神通所由以！」

佛告總持王菩薩言：「善男子！汝等當知，今此會中，有一菩薩摩訶薩，名曰『觀世音自在』。從無量劫來，成就大慈大悲，善能修習無量陀羅尼門，為欲安樂諸眾生故，密放如是大神通力。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus have I heard. At one time Śākyamuni Buddha was on Mount Potalaka, in Avalokiteśvara's palace, seated on a lion throne within a jeweled and adorned bodhimaṇḍa. That throne was adorned entirely with measureless, many-colored maṇi jewels, and hundred-jeweled banners and pennants hung arrayed all around it. At that time the Tathāgata, seated there, was about to expound the dhāraṇī of total retention. He was accompanied by countless bodhisattva-mahāsattvas, named as follows: Dhāraṇī-King, Jeweled King, Medicine King, Medicine Superior, Avalokiteśvara, Mahāsthāmaprāpta, Flower Adornment, Great Adornment, Jewel Treasury, Virtue Treasury, Vajra Treasury, Ākāśagarbha, Maitreya, Samantabhadra, and Mañjuśrī — bodhisattva-mahāsattvas such as these, every one a Dharma Prince who has received the consecration.

He was also accompanied by measureless, countless great śrāvaka monks, every one having attained the ten grounds of arhatship, with Mahākāśyapa foremost among them; by measureless Brahmā-heaven gods, with the Brahmā king Suddhāvāsa foremost; by measureless sons of the gods of the desire realm, with the god-son Gopika foremost; by measureless world-protecting Four Heavenly Kings, with Dhṛtarāṣṭra foremost; by measureless gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, and non-humans, with the great nāga-king Devaguṇa foremost; and by measureless goddesses of the desire realm, with the goddess Bālanetra foremost. Measureless spirits of empty space, of rivers and seas, of springs, of streams and marshes, of medicinal herbs, of forests, of dwellings, of water, of fire, of earth, of wind, of soil, of mountains, of stone, and of palaces, all came together and gathered.

At that time Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, in the midst of this great assembly, secretly emitted a spiritual radiance, its light shining brilliantly, so that the lands of the ten directions and this great trichiliocosm all turned the color of gold. The palaces of the gods, the nāgas, and every venerable spirit all shook and trembled; rivers, great seas, the Iron Ring Mountains, Mount Sumeru, earthen mountains, and dark mountains, all likewise shook greatly. The light of the sun, the moon, pearls, fire, and the stars, all alike ceased to appear.

Then the bodhisattva Dhāraṇī-King, seeing this rare and marvelous sign, marveled at what he had never seen before. He at once rose from his seat, joined his palms together, and questioned the Buddha in verse as to who had emitted this sign of spiritual power: "Who has, on this very day, accomplished true awakening, that such great light is broadly emitted everywhere? The lands of the ten directions have all turned to gold, and the trichiliocosm itself has done the same. Who has, on this very day, attained mastery, that such rare and great spiritual power is displayed? Measureless buddha-lands have all been shaken, and the palaces of nāgas and spirits are all disturbed. This whole great assembly now shares one doubt: whose power is this, and by what cause does it come? Is it a buddha's, a bodhisattva's, a great śrāvaka's, or Brahmā's, Māra's, the heavens', or Śakra's own? I beg only that the World-Honored One, in great compassion, explain to us the very source of this spiritual sign."

The Buddha said to the bodhisattva Dhāraṇī-King: "Good son, you and this assembly should know: within this gathering there is a bodhisattva-mahāsattva named Avalokiteśvara. Since measureless kalpas past, he has accomplished great kindness and great compassion, and is well able to cultivate measureless gateways of dhāraṇī; wishing to bring peace and happiness to all beings, he has secretly emitted this display of great spiritual power."

Avalokiteśvara's Vow of a Thousand Hands and Eyes

Chinese Source

佛說是語已。爾時，觀世音菩薩，從座而起，整理衣服，向佛合掌，白佛言：「世尊！我有大悲心陀羅尼呪，今當欲說。為諸眾生得安樂故，除一切病故，得壽命故，得富饒故，滅除一切惡業、重罪故，離障難故，增長一切白法諸功德故，成就一切諸善根故，遠離一切諸怖畏故，速能滿足一切諸希求故。惟願世尊，慈哀聽許！」

佛言：「善男子！汝大慈悲，安樂眾生。欲說神呪，今正是時，宜應速說。如來隨喜，諸佛亦然。」

觀世音菩薩重白佛言：「世尊！我念過去無量億劫，有佛出世，名曰『千光王靜住如來』。彼佛世尊憐念我故，及為一切諸眾生故，說此『廣大圓滿無礙大悲心陀羅尼』。以金色手摩我頂上作如是言：『善男子！汝當持此心呪，普為未來惡世一切眾生，作大利樂。』

「我於是時，始住初地，一聞此呪故，超第八地。我時心歡喜故，即發誓言：『若我當來，堪能利益、安樂一切眾生者，令我即時，身生千手、千眼具足。』發是願已，應時身上，千手千眼，悉皆具足；十方大地，六種震動；十方千佛，悉放光明，照觸我身，及照十方，無邊世界。

「從是已後，復於無量佛所、無量會中，重更得聞，親承受持，是陀羅尼。復生歡喜，踊躍無量，便得超越無數億劫微細生死。從是已來，常所誦持，未曾廢忘，由持此呪故，所生之處，恒在佛前，蓮華化生，不受胎藏之身。若有比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷、童男、童女，欲誦持者，於諸眾生，起慈悲心，先當從我，發如是願：」

Ru-Yi Translation

Once the Buddha had spoken these words, Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva rose from his seat, arranged his robes, joined his palms toward the Buddha, and said to him: "World-Honored One, I possess a Great Compassion Heart Dhāraṇī, and I now wish to speak it — so that beings may attain peace and happiness; so that every illness may be removed; so that long life may be attained; so that abundance and wealth may be attained; so that every evil karma and grave offense may be destroyed; so that every obstruction and hardship may be left behind; so that every wholesome quality may be increased; so that every wholesome root may be accomplished; so that every fear may be left far behind; and so that every wish may swiftly be fulfilled. I beg only that the World-Honored One, in compassion, grant permission and listen."

The Buddha said: "Good son, your great compassion brings peace and happiness to beings. Now is exactly the time to speak this spiritual mantra — you should speak it at once. The Tathāgata rejoices in it, and so do all the buddhas."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva said again to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, I recall that in ages past, measureless hundred millions of kalpas ago, there was a buddha who came into the world named the Tathāgata King of a Thousand Lights, Dwelling in Stillness. That buddha, out of pity for me, and for the sake of all beings, spoke this Vast, Perfect, Unimpeded Great Compassion Heart Dhāraṇī. He placed his golden-hued hand upon my crown and said: 'Good son, you should hold this heart-mantra, and for the sake of all beings of the evil age to come, bring about great benefit and happiness.'

"At that time I had only just attained the first ground; yet on hearing this mantra once, I leapt at once past it to the eighth ground. My heart, filled with joy, at once made this vow: 'If, in the future, I am able to bring benefit and happiness to every being, then let a thousand hands and a thousand eyes appear on my body, all at once, complete.' Having made this vow, in that very instant, a thousand hands and a thousand eyes appeared complete upon my body; the great earth of the ten directions shook in six ways; and a thousand buddhas of the ten directions all emitted light, which touched and illumined my body, and illumined the measureless worlds of the ten directions as well.

"From that time onward, in the presence of measureless buddhas, and within measureless assemblies, I came again and again to hear this dhāraṇī, and received and upheld it in person. Filled again with joy, leaping beyond all measure, I thereby transcended innumerable hundred millions of kalpas of the subtlest cycle of birth and death. From that time to this, I have constantly recited and upheld it, never once forgetting it; and because I have held this mantra, wherever I have been born, I have always been born in the presence of a buddha, transformed into being from within a lotus flower, never again taking on a body conceived in a womb. If there are monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen, boys, or girls who wish to recite and hold this mantra, they should first, giving rise to a mind of compassion toward all beings, make this vow after me:"

Reading note — after Zhili

Zhili reads the leap from the first ground to the eighth as the sūtra's own proof of what this dhāraṇī is capable of before a single clause of it has even been spoken — the origin story is placed first, ahead of the mantra itself, precisely so that a practitioner approaches the words already knowing what they cost the one who first heard them nothing at all. He is also explicit that the vow for a thousand hands and eyes was not a wish for supernatural display: a hand is what reaches out and lifts something, an eye is what sees where the reaching is needed, and a bodhisattva who has vowed to help "all beings" without exception requires, on the same logic, a correspondingly unlimited number of both. The number is the vow's own scope made visible.

Ten Vows, and Six Vows Before the Fiercest Realms

Ru-Yi Translation

Homage to the Greatly Compassionate Avalokiteśvara! May I swiftly know every dharma.
Homage to the Greatly Compassionate Avalokiteśvara! May I soon gain the eye of wisdom.
Homage to the Greatly Compassionate Avalokiteśvara! May I swiftly liberate every being.
Homage to the Greatly Compassionate Avalokiteśvara! May I soon gain skillful means.
Homage to the Greatly Compassionate Avalokiteśvara! May I swiftly board the boat of prajñā.
Homage to the Greatly Compassionate Avalokiteśvara! May I soon cross the sea of suffering.
Homage to the Greatly Compassionate Avalokiteśvara! May I swiftly attain the path of precepts and samādhi.
Homage to the Greatly Compassionate Avalokiteśvara! May I soon ascend the mountain of nirvāṇa.
Homage to the Greatly Compassionate Avalokiteśvara! May I swiftly meet the unconditioned abode.
Homage to the Greatly Compassionate Avalokiteśvara! May I soon be one with the body of dharma-nature.

If I face a mountain of knives, that mountain of knives shall of itself break apart. If I face a cauldron of fire, that cauldron of fire shall of itself be extinguished. If I face the hells, those hells shall of themselves dry up and vanish. If I face the hungry ghosts, they shall of themselves be filled and satisfied. If I face the asuras, their malicious minds shall of themselves be tamed. If I face the animals, they shall of themselves attain great wisdom.

發是願已，至心稱念，我之名字，亦應專念，我本師阿彌陀如來，然後即當誦此陀羅尼神呪。一宿誦滿五遍，除滅身中，百千萬億劫生死重罪。

Having made this vow, hold my name in mind with a sincere heart, and hold in mind, too, my own teacher, the Tathāgata Amitābha — and only then recite this dhāraṇī mantra. Recite it five times in a single night, and every grave sin of birth and death, accumulated over hundreds of thousands of myriads of kalpas, will be destroyed.

Reading note — after Zhili

Zhili maps these ten vows directly onto the four noble truths, and behind them, onto Tiantai's own ten modes of contemplation — the same interpretive move this site's Kṣitigarbha reading notes trace in an entirely different Tiantai commentator's hands, applied here to a dhāraṇī sūtra instead of a doctrinal one. The first two vows (knowing all dharmas, gaining the eye of wisdom) rest on the truth of accumulation, since without a specific wisdom nothing else can be known; the next two (liberating all beings, gaining skillful means) rest on the truth of suffering, since a bodhisattva who has not yet found the means cannot free anyone completely; the middle four (the boat of prajñā, crossing the sea of suffering, precepts and samādhi, the mountain of nirvāṇa) rest on the truth of the path, itself nothing but the three trainings; and the final two (the unconditioned abode, oneness with dharma-nature) rest on the truth of cessation. "Even a thousand further vows," Zhili writes, "could not go beyond these four truths — how much less these ten?" He treats the six shorter vows that follow the same way: three answer the truth of suffering directly (the mountain of knives and the cauldron of fire named separately, then every hell generally), and three answer what remains of the three lower destinies (hungry ghosts, asuras, animals) — each hell named for the specific fault it corrects, hunger met with fullness, rage met with taming, and animal ignorance met with the wisdom it most conspicuously lacks.

He is equally precise about what these vows are not: reciting them, he insists, cannot be allowed to become a private wish for some worldly outcome — "the mind put into motion here must benefit every being; only a settled, reverent heart brings the response, so guard carefully against treating this lightly." A vow spoken by rote, aimed only at the reciter's own comfort, is on his account not yet the vow this sūtra actually asks for.

Vows Made on the Dhāraṇī's Own Behalf

Chinese Source

觀世音菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！若諸人天，誦持大悲章句者，臨命終時，十方諸佛，皆來授手。欲生何等佛土，隨願皆得往生。」

復白佛言：「世尊！若諸眾生，誦持大悲神呪，墮三惡道者，我誓不成正覺。誦持大悲神呪者，若不生諸佛國者，我誓不成正覺。誦持大悲神呪者，若不得無量三昧辯才者，我誓不成正覺。誦持大悲神呪者，於現在生中一切所求，若不果遂者，不得為大悲心陀羅尼也。唯除不善，除不至誠。

「若諸女人，厭賤女身，欲成男子身，誦持大悲陀羅尼章句。若不轉女身成男子身者，我誓不成正覺。生少疑心者，必不果遂也。

「若諸眾生，侵損常住飲食、財物，千佛出世，不通懺悔，縱懺亦不除滅。今誦大悲神呪，即得除滅。若侵損食用，常住飲食、財物，要對十方師懺謝，然始除滅。今誦大悲陀羅尼時，十方師即來，為作證明，一切罪障，悉皆消滅。一切十惡、五逆、謗人、謗法、破齋、破戒、破塔、壞寺、偷僧祇物、污淨梵行，如是等一切惡業、重罪，悉皆滅盡。唯除一事，於呪生疑者，乃至小罪輕業，亦不得滅，何況重罪？雖不即滅重罪，猶能遠作菩提之因。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva said again to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, if there are gods or humans who recite and hold the verses of the Great Compassion Dhāraṇī, then at the moment of their death, buddhas of the ten directions will all come to give them their hand; whatever buddha-land they wish to be born in, they will, according to their wish, attain rebirth there."

He said again to the Buddha: "World-Honored One — if there are beings who recite and hold the Great Compassion Mantra and yet fall into one of the three evil destinies, then I vow I shall not attain true awakening. If there are those who recite and hold it and yet are not born in a buddha-land, then I vow I shall not attain true awakening. If there are those who recite and hold it and yet do not attain measureless samādhi and eloquence, then I vow I shall not attain true awakening. For those who recite and hold it: whatever they seek in this present life, if it is not fulfilled, then it cannot rightly be called the Great Compassion Heart Dhāraṇī — except should the wish itself be unwholesome, or sought without a sincere heart.

"If there are women who are weary of and disdain a woman's body, and wish to become a man's body, and recite and hold the verses of the Great Compassion Dhāraṇī, then, if their woman's body is not transformed into a man's body, I vow I shall not attain true awakening. But one who gives rise even to the smallest doubt will certainly not have this wish fulfilled.

"If there are beings who have encroached upon or damaged the food or wealth of the permanent assembly — a sin that a thousand buddhas coming into the world could not absolve through repentance, and which even repentance itself cannot dissolve — reciting the Great Compassion Mantra will now cause it to be dissolved. If they have encroached upon or made use of the permanent assembly's food or wealth, they must confess and repent before teachers of the ten directions before it can begin to be dissolved; but now, at the moment they recite the Great Compassion Dhāraṇī, teachers of the ten directions will come at once and bear witness on their behalf, and every karmic obstruction will be entirely dissolved. Every one of the ten evils, the five heinous crimes, slandering others, slandering the Dharma, breaking the fast, breaking the precepts, destroying stūpas, ruining monasteries, stealing property of the monastic assembly, and defiling pure conduct — every evil karma and grave offense such as these will be entirely destroyed. Reading note — after Zhili

This site does not pass over what the sūtra itself states plainly: a woman's own wish to be free of a woman's body is treated here as a legitimate request the dhāraṇī promises to fulfill, on the same footing as freedom from an evil destiny or rebirth in a buddha-land — an assumption that treats female embodiment itself as a hardship comparable to hell, rather than a neutral fact of rebirth. It is the same period assumption this site's Kṣitigarbha reading notes flag at its own two occurrences of the same promise; this sūtra's version simply states it as one clause among many rather than as the culmination of a mother's rescue.

Zhili reads the structure of this whole block of vows as answering two questions in turn: the first three vows concern success in the positive sense — good rebirth, meeting a buddha, gaining eloquence — while the fourth turns to removing what obstructs it, offenses so grave that "a thousand buddhas coming into the world could not absolve them through repentance." His gloss on why the dhāraṇī can dissolve what ordinary repentance cannot turns on a distinction between two kinds of practice: repentance carried out only as an outward act (事懺) works by degrees and can fail against the heaviest offenses, while repentance grounded in contemplating the offense's own empty nature (理懺) — which he reads this mantra as invoking directly, since "teachers of the ten directions come at once to bear witness" the instant it is recited — reaches every offense at its root simultaneously, rather than working through them one at a time.

Reading note — after Zhili

This is the passage Zhili treats as the whole sūtra's hinge, and he reads Brahmā's question as itself a skillful device: the sūtra has already shown, one paragraph earlier, that a sharp-faculty listener needs nothing more than the bare sound of the mantra to leap straight to realization — but everyone else in the assembly needs a way in, and Brahmā's question exists to supply it. His count is exact: nine states of mind are named, and he maps all nine onto the last nine of Tiantai's ten modes of contemplation, the first mode (contemplating the inconceivable object) being the mantra's own sound, already given. Equanimity answers the arousing of true bodhicitta; non-action and freedom from defilement together answer skillfully settling calming-and-insight; contemplating emptiness answers thoroughly breaking through delusion; reverence answers discerning what is blocked and open; humility answers the harmonious tuning of the factors of enlightenment; freedom from confusion answers the auxiliary practices that assist an obstructed path; the mind of unsurpassed bodhi, last of the nine, answers both knowing one's own stage and the patient endurance that carries a practitioner past it, since — on Zhili's reading — a mind genuinely oriented toward unsurpassed awakening has already relinquished the subtler attachment to its own progress that the tenth mode exists to correct.

He draws one more consequence from this passage that shapes how the rest of the sūtra should be read: since the dhāraṇī's true "form" turns out to be nine states of mind rather than eighty-two syllables, the elaborate ritual instructions, the forty-some hands, and the long list of remedies that follow are not a different, lesser layer of the teaching bolted onto a purer doctrinal core — they are, on his account, the same nine minds "made visible in the shape of an event" (託事), the second of the two complementary ways he says this text can be practiced, direct contemplation of the one thought being the first. A thousand hands are one thought's compassion given a body; a single ritual cure for a single ailment is that same compassion meeting one specific form of suffering. Neither, in his reading, is the sūtra's decoration — both are the nine minds practiced from a different angle.

Zhili organizes his own repentance ritual around exactly the movement this section makes: from purification (the boundary-setting rites, the bathing, the five-colored thread) to confession, and only then to the sustained recitation meant to reach the "four fruits" in this very life. He treats the closing lines — commanding a ghost with a washed skull, commanding the Four Heavenly Kings by burning sandalwood — as belonging to the sūtra's broadest promise, stated a few lines earlier, that "whatever they seek and wish for, none of it will fail to be fulfilled": a compassionate vow made for every being does not, on his reading, exclude the practical, thaumaturgic uses Tang-dynasty practitioners actually brought to a dhāraṇī of this kind. He is careful to place this material after, not before, the confession and purification stages in his own ten-stage manual — need answered only once the practitioner's own conduct has already been set right, not as a shortcut around it.

Reading note — after Zhili

The reveal that Avalokiteśvara is, in truth, already a buddha — the Tathāgata of the Bright, True Dharma, only appearing as a bodhisattva out of his own compassionate vow — is the same structural move this site's Kṣitigarbha reading notes trace in the figure of the ghost-king Life-Governor, revealed near the end of that sūtra to be a bodhisattva a hundred and seventy kalpas from buddhahood wearing a ghost's form purely by vow. Zhili's own gloss stresses what the placement of the reveal accomplishes: it comes only after every promise, every ritual instruction, and every remedy has already been given and can be judged on its own terms, not before — the reader has already been asked to trust what this figure offers before learning how far outranking the figure making the offer actually is.

On where this sūtra belongs within the wider canon, Zhili places it, per Tiantai's five-period classification, within the Vaipulya (方等) period — the broad middle stretch of the Buddha's teaching career in which sūtras addressed to all four levels of capacity circulate side by side, some still couched in the language of the lesser teachings, others already speaking the perfect teaching outright. He is explicit that this sūtra's own "vast, perfect, unimpeded" epithets belong to none of the first three; only in the perfect teaching, he argues, do "vast" and "unimpeded" mean what they say without qualification. This is also, on his account, the reason a single mantra can be recited with benefit by a sharp-faculty practitioner capable of direct insight and by an ordinary devotee reciting purely on faith at the same time — the text itself was built to be entered from every level at once.

What follows is a long, itemized set of ritual and folk-medical instructions — the sūtra's own concrete answer to the question of what this dhāraṇī is actually for in daily life. Several remedies call for substances identified in the source text itself by a parenthetical gloss (for instance, "拙具羅香，安息香也" — "guggulu incense, that is, benzoin resin"); those glosses are original to the Chinese text and are preserved below. Sun Light Bodhisattva, for the sake of those who recite and hold the Great Compassion Heart Dhāraṇī, spoke a great mantra to protect them (retained below in transliteration, as with the mantras above): "Namo Buddhagunami, namo Dharmamahati, namo Saṃghatayani, tili bhūbi satu-ḍanoma."

"Reciting this mantra dissolves every sin, and can also ward off demons and calamity from the heavens. If one recites it once, then bows once to the Buddha, and does so three times each day, then, in ages to come, the body one receives will be well-formed and pleasing in every feature, a joyful result."

Moon Light Bodhisattva likewise spoke, for the sake of practitioners, a dhāraṇī mantra to protect them: "Śintide-tusuṭa, ajñamite-uduṭa, śinkiṭa, balaite, yamiñaṭe-uduṭa, kulate-kimaṭa, svāhā."

"Recite this mantra five times, take a five-colored thread, and make a mantra-cord of it, and bind it wherever there is pain. This mantra was spoken by forty hundred million buddhas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, in ages past, and I now speak it as well — for the sake of protecting practitioners, removing every obstruction and hardship, removing every terrible illness and pain, accomplishing every wholesome dharma, and leaving every fear far behind."

The Buddha's Final Exhortation, and a Warning Against Slander

Chinese Source

佛告阿難：「汝當深心清淨，受持此陀羅尼，廣宣流布於閻浮提，莫令斷絕。此陀羅尼，能大利益三界眾生，一切患苦縈身者，以此陀羅尼治之，無有不差者。此大神呪，呪乾枯樹，尚得生枝、柯、華、果，何況有情、有識眾生？身有病患，治之不差者，必無是處。

「善男子！此陀羅尼，威神之力，不可思議！不可思議！歎莫能盡。若不過去久遠已來廣種善根，乃至名字不可得聞，何況得見？汝等大眾，天、人、龍、神，聞我讚歎，皆應隨喜。若有謗此呪者，即為謗彼九十九億恒河沙諸佛。若於此陀羅尼，生疑不信者，當知其人，永失大利，百千萬劫，常淪惡趣，無有出期，常不見佛、不聞法、不覩僧。」

一切眾會菩薩摩訶薩、金剛密跡、梵釋四天、龍、鬼、神，聞佛如來讚歎此陀羅尼，皆悉歡喜，奉教修行。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha said to Ānanda: "You should, with a deeply purified heart, hold this dhāraṇī, and spread it far and wide throughout Jambudvīpa, never letting it lapse. This dhāraṇī can bring great benefit to beings of the three realms; anyone bound up in suffering and affliction who is treated with this dhāraṇī will be cured, without exception. This great spiritual mantra, recited over a withered, dead tree, can still make it put forth branches, boughs, flowers, and fruit — how much more so, then, a being with feeling and consciousness? That a bodily illness treated with it should fail to be cured is simply not possible.

"Good son, this dhāraṇī's majestic spiritual power is inconceivable! Inconceivable! No praise could ever exhaust it. Had one not, in ages long past, widely planted wholesome roots, one could not so much as hear its name — how much less see it? You, this whole assembly, gods, humans, nāgas, and spirits, hearing my praise of it, should all rejoice together. Whoever slanders this mantra thereby slanders those ninety-nine hundred million buddhas as numerous as the Ganges' sands. Whoever gives rise to doubt and disbelief toward this dhāraṇī should know that they will forever lose this great benefit, and for a hundred thousand myriad kalpas will constantly sink in evil destinies, with no time of release, forever failing to see a buddha, hear the Dharma, or behold the monastic assembly."

The whole assembly — bodhisattva-mahāsattvas, the Vajra-Guhyapāda spirits, Brahmā, Śakra, the Four Heavenly Kings, nāgas, ghosts, and spirits — hearing the Tathāgata praise this dhāraṇī, all rejoiced, and undertook to practice as they had been taught.
