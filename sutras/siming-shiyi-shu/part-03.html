Meanings Seven to Ten and Conclusion: Stages, Intent, Interpreting the Text, and Investigating Principle

This part completes the Letter of Ten Meanings with Meanings Seven to Ten and Zhili's closing appeal. The Seventh Meaning concerns stages: inner and outer contemplation begin at identity in name, and only when contemplation is accomplished does one enter the five grades; Zhili accuses Qingzhao of quietly adopting this correction without admitting his earlier claim. The Eighth Meaning explains why the school contemplates the mind: the Buddha-dharma is too high and beings too many, so practice relies on the near object of the aggregates; Zhili compares the ten sets of three dharmas with the Commentary on the Vimalakīrti and the Commentary on the Avalokiteśvara Invocation Sūtra to show that they are neither contemplation attached to dharmas nor contemplation relying on phenomena. The Ninth Meaning examines Qingzhao's reading of the Annotations phrase "one need not wait for the object of contemplation," and uses it to argue that the contemplations of phenomena and dharmas can be cultivated at once under a good teacher. The Tenth Meaning returns to the wondrous of the three tracks in the Profound Meaning of the Lotus and to Qingzhao's changing accounts of whether those ten dharmas are fused by the dharma-nature. The letter closes with an appeal to repent and reply, and Zhili's signature.

第七不知觀心之位

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh: Not Knowing the Stages of Contemplating the Mind

Both Contemplations Begin at Identity in Name

Chinese Source

眾生若於善知識所及諸教法中，聞心具諸法與諸生佛無有差別，能知心及諸法當體即理、互具互攝者，正是名字即也。約理雖即，約事天殊，故求妙門破惑顯理，乃於名字之位，以妙解攬於萬法唯內識心，專於內心用於妙觀觀一切法，或於外境修觀亦然。觀道若開若伏若斷，或入觀行、或似或真。此義顯在《止觀》及以諸文。

Ru-Yi Translation

If sentient beings, in the presence of a good friend or in the teachings, hear that the mind contains all dharmas and is without any difference from all beings and buddhas, and are able to know that the mind and all dharmas are in their very substance identical to principle, mutually containing and mutually gathering in, this is precisely identity in name. Although identical in terms of principle, in terms of phenomena they are as far apart as heaven [and earth]; therefore one seeks a wondrous gate to break through delusion and reveal principle. So at the stage of [identity in] name, one uses wondrous understanding to grasp the myriad dharmas as only the inner consciousness-mind, and solely in the inner mind uses the wondrous contemplation to contemplate all dharmas; or cultivating contemplation on outer objects is likewise. As the path of contemplation opens, subdues, or severs, one enters contemplative practice, or resemblance, or the true. This meaning is evident in Calming and Contemplation and the various texts.

Chinese Source

上人素來全迷此義，故乃於答疑書中數云：觀行五品位中，方修內外二觀，觀成入於相似之位。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Venerable has from the beginning been wholly deluded about this meaning, and so in the Letter Answering Doubts said several times: "Only in the stage of the five grades of contemplative practice does one cultivate the two contemplations, inner and outer; when contemplation is accomplished one enters the stage of resemblance."

Chinese Source

故詰難書曰：若五品中方於二境修觀者，只如五品因何得入？又若待至五品方修觀者，則名字之人全無入品之路也。仍為上人開示令知蹊徑，乃說內外二觀俱在名字位中，造修觀成方入觀行、相似、分真，具引《止觀》之文明示觀成方入隨喜等位。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore the Letter Pressing Difficulties said: "If one cultivates contemplation on the two objects only within the five grades, how then does one enter the five grades? Further, if one must wait until the five grades to cultivate contemplation, then a person at [identity in] name has no road at all for entering the grades." And for the Venerable's sake I opened and showed it so that you would know the path: I explained that the two contemplations, inner and outer, both lie within the stage of [identity in] name, and that only when they are taken up, cultivated, and accomplished does one enter contemplative practice, resemblance, and a portion of the truth; and I fully cited the text of Calming and Contemplation clearly showing that only when contemplation is accomplished does one enter the stages such as rejoicing.

Chinese Source

既將明文顯示，上人因茲方悟，自惜親近邪師暗於位次故，妄指五品方修二觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the plain text was shown clearly, the Venerable through this only now awakened, and himself regretted that, having been close to a deviant teacher and in the dark about the order of stages, he had falsely pointed to the five grades as where the two contemplations are first cultivated.

Chinese Source

則答疑書中此過無計曲救，遂於今來義狀全不敢答酬，而返偷詰難書中所示正義將為己解，乃數數顯書五品是觀行成位。以此驗之，知上人覆己之短、掩他之長。

Ru-Yi Translation

This fault in the Letter Answering Doubts you had no way to twist and rescue, and so in the statement of meaning now sent you did not dare answer it at all; instead you stole the correct meaning shown in the Letter Pressing Difficulties and made it your own understanding, writing again and again plainly that the five grades are the stage where contemplative practice is accomplished. Verifying it by this, we know that the Venerable covers his own shortcomings and hides the strengths of others.

Chinese Source

只如五品是觀行成位，因誰得解？何不首伏昔迷，改邪向正，何得偷竊他義誑惑後生？若據上人如此用心，實非傳教之士。尚望人道之心千里不及，何能論於佛法耶？是知徒說令末代之機修於止觀，既自立至五品位方觀二境，則一切眾生無修觀之分也。輒將此之見識，與人論教觀廢立之意，還得也無？

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the five grades being the stage where contemplative practice is accomplished — through whom did you come to understand it? Why not submit regarding your former delusion, change the deviant and turn to the correct? How can you steal another's meaning to deceive and confuse later generations? If one goes by the Venerable's use of mind in this way, you are truly not a transmitter of the teaching. Even measured against the mind of the human path you fall a thousand miles short; how could you discuss the Buddha-dharma? Thus we know that you talk in vain of having the capacities of the final age cultivate calming-and-contemplation; since you yourself set it up that only on reaching the stage of the five grades does one contemplate the two objects, then all sentient beings have no share in cultivating contemplation. With such insight to discuss with others the intent of establishing and abolishing in teaching and contemplation — is that acceptable or not?

Chinese Source

何故不甘杜絕眾生入理之門、趣果之路耶？驗此一失，則七十紙之明文都為虛構，前之義狀亦是誑言，所立諸義何須更破，自然瓦解。答疑書明文見在，還可更生抵踏否？

Ru-Yi Translation

Why do you not willingly accept that you are blocking off the gate by which sentient beings enter principle and the road by which they tend toward the fruit? Verified by this one error, the plain text of seventy sheets is all fabrication, the earlier statements of meaning are also deceptive words, and the various meanings you set up need not be refuted further — they collapse of themselves. The plain text of the Letter Answering Doubts is still extant; can you still produce any resistance?

Stealing the Meaning of Cultivation Two, Nature One

Chinese Source

況上人竊他正義因為己解，前後不少。只如予將修二性一銷於知心無心之文，答疑書難云：若正釋十法中，以金為性、光明為修，則容分對。且玄義譬喻附文當體，何嘗論修二性一等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Moreover, the Venerable has stolen another's correct meaning and made it his own understanding no few times, earlier and later. For example, I interpreted the text on "knowing the mind is without mind" by "cultivation two, nature one," and the Letter Answering Doubts objected: "If in the proper explanation of the ten dharmas gold were taken as the nature and light and brightness as cultivation, then one could allow such a division and matching. But the simile, attaching to the text, and the thing itself in the Profound Meaning — when did they ever discuss 'cultivation two, nature one' and so on?"

Chinese Source

既被詰難書舉正釋中修二性一之文，顯證文理朗然。上人若是傳教之心，必須循理悔責毀教之愆，然後共揚正觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the Letter Pressing Difficulties raised the texts in the proper explanation on "cultivation two, nature one," clearly proving that text and principle are bright and clear, if the Venerable had the mind of a transmitter of the teaching, you would have had to follow principle, repent and blame yourself for the transgression of defaming the teaching, and then together [with me] spread the proper contemplation.

Chinese Source

何者？上人本據正釋無修二性一之義，驗觀心文中修二性一之義為非。今既顯見正釋多是修二性一，則知觀心文初問答稱於正釋，安可更加毀破耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Why? The Venerable originally, on the basis that the proper explanation has no meaning of "cultivation two, nature one," verified the meaning of "cultivation two, nature one" in the text on contemplating the mind to be wrong. Now that it is clearly seen that the proper explanation is in many places "cultivation two, nature one," we know that the opening question and answer of the text on contemplating the mind match the proper explanation; how could you further defame and refute it?

Chinese Source

況上人刀刀明說正釋十法全無修二性一之義，又云不二門只約緣了正三因對論離合，今來何得却據三德三涅槃是修二性一云具觀心義耶？豈非因予前後詰難，得知正釋有於離合之義，便竊此義將為己解，而返將此義立於十種三法為理觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Moreover, the Venerable repeatedly said plainly that the ten dharmas of the proper explanation have no meaning of "cultivation two, nature one" at all, and further said that the Gates of Nonduality discusses separation and combination only in terms of the three causes, conditioning, revealing, and correct. How can you now on the contrary rely on the three virtues and three nirvāṇas being "cultivation two, nature one" and say they contain the meaning of contemplating the mind? Is it not that, because of my difficulties earlier and later, you came to know that the proper explanation has the meaning of separation and combination, and then stole this meaning to make it your own understanding, and on the contrary used this meaning to establish the ten sets of three dharmas as the contemplation of principle?

Chinese Source

又如觀妄心成真心，皆是因予詰難方知，却返用為難，枉予不許唯觀妄心。此過亦前文已說，更不重敘也。只據上人此之誑妄之心，不合更論法義。況不知觀心之位，餘何所言耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, as with contemplating the deluded mind to accomplish the true mind: you came to know all of it only because of my difficulties, yet on the contrary used it as a difficulty, falsely [claiming] that I do not allow contemplating only the deluded mind. This fault has also been discussed in the earlier text and will not be narrated again. Going only by the Venerable's deceptive and false mind here, it is not fitting to discuss the meaning of the Dharma further. Moreover, not knowing the stages of contemplating the mind, what else is there to say?

第八不會觀心之意

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth: Not Understanding the Intent of Contemplating the Mind

Why Our School Contemplates the Mind

Chinese Source

《妙玄》并《釋籤》判云：佛法太高、眾生法太廣，初心為難。然心佛及眾生，是三無差別，觀心則易。又云：佛法定在果，眾生法一往通因果、二往局在因，心法定在因。又云：若以佛法觀之，似如不逮；若以心性觀之，似如可見。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Wondrous Profound [Meaning] and the Annotations judge: "The Buddha-dharma is too lofty and the dharma of beings too vast; for the initial mind they are difficult. Yet mind, buddhas, and sentient beings — these three are without any difference; contemplating the mind is easy." They further say: "The Buddha-dharma is definitely at the fruit; the dharma of beings, in the first sense, extends to both cause and fruit, and in the second sense is restricted to the cause; the mind-dharma is definitely at the cause." They further say: "If one contemplates it by the Buddha-dharma, it seems out of reach; if one contemplates it by the nature of mind, it seems as if it can be seen."

Chinese Source

故一家之教，依此意故，乃立陰心為所觀境。所以《止觀》及以諸文皆令觀心，以取近要之心為觀所託。若無所託陰界入境，觀依何修？理依何顯？故離三障四魔則無所觀境界也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore the teaching of our school, relying on this intent, establishes the mind of the aggregates as the object contemplated. This is why Calming and Contemplation and the various texts all have one contemplate the mind, taking the near and essential mind as what contemplation relies on. If there were no object of aggregates, realms, and entrances to rely on, on what would contemplation be cultivated? On what would principle be revealed? Therefore apart from the three obstructions and four māras there is no realm of objects to contemplate.

Chinese Source

縱修外觀，託諸佛眾生及以依報為境，亦是外陰入法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Even when cultivating outer contemplation, relying on the buddhas, sentient beings, and the dependent reward as the object, these too are outer dharmas of the aggregates and entrances.

Chinese Source

何者？佛雖離陰，為眾生故示應色心，故等覺已還見有他佛。既是六根所對之境，非界入攝耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Why? Although the Buddha is apart from the aggregates, for the sake of sentient beings he shows form and mind in response; therefore those at equal awakening and below see other buddhas. Since they are objects faced by the six faculties, are they not included in the realms and entrances?

Chinese Source

故《輔行》云：聖人變化所造，亦令眾生變心所見。故修觀者，即於色心之境而觀法界。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore the Fuxing says: "What is created by the transformations of sages also makes sentient beings see with transformed minds. Therefore one who cultivates contemplation contemplates the dharma-realm right in the object of form and mind."

Chinese Source

故《觀音疏》以眾生佛為他境，荊溪直以依正色心為外境。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore the Commentary on the Avalokiteśvara takes sentient beings and buddhas as the other's object, and Jingxi directly takes the dependent and principal, form and mind, as outer objects.

Chinese Source

是知不取佛所得圓常自在之法，但用眾生所對色心為所觀之境，故觀所託之境不出三科也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus we know that one does not take the perfect, eternal, and sovereign dharmas attained by the Buddha, but only uses the form and mind that sentient beings face as the object contemplated; therefore the objects that contemplation relies on do not go beyond the three categories.

Chinese Source

又《般舟三昧》以應身三十二相為境，依之而修三觀。是知三觀所顯即是圓融三法也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the Pratyutpanna Samādhi takes the thirty-two marks of the response-body as the object, relying on which one cultivates the three contemplations. Thus we know that what the three contemplations reveal is the perfectly fused three dharmas.

Chinese Source

今家觀法，何處令直緣真理而修？何文令緣佛所證圓融三法而修觀耶？以上人自昔全不知依境修觀之意，但以己解約酌而言。及遭詰難，則望難未到處臨時轉立。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the method of contemplation of our present school, where does it have one directly take true principle as object and cultivate? Which text has one take the perfectly fused three dharmas realized by the Buddha as object and cultivate contemplation? Because the Venerable from the past has not known at all the intent of cultivating contemplation in reliance on an object, you speak only by weighing it with your own understanding. And when met with difficulties, you look to where the difficulty has not yet reached and shift your position on the spot.

Shifting from Pure Contemplation of Principle

Chinese Source

如將此玄十種三法謂純談理觀，豈非全不知理觀是常坐等三種三昧？只見《發揮》云純談真性，便約此立為純談理觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

For example, in calling the ten sets of three dharmas of this Profound [Meaning] "purely discussing the contemplation of principle," is it not that you did not know at all that the contemplation of principle is the three kinds of samādhi such as constant sitting? You only saw that Bringing Out [the Meaning] says "purely discusses the true nature," and on that basis set it up as "purely discusses the contemplation of principle."

Chinese Source

及被問疑書引《輔行》難之，知非理觀，乃轉計云：此玄文直顯心性，義同理觀。而不知錯下直顯心性之語專是約行。端坐十乘及並我一念橫竪照之，正是攝諸三法入一念心成乎圓觀，乃是附法觀心。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the Letter Questioning Doubts cited the Fuxing to challenge you, you knew it was not the contemplation of principle, and so shifted your reckoning, saying: "This Profound text directly reveals the nature of mind, and its meaning is the same as the contemplation of principle." But you did not know that the words "directly reveals the nature of mind," mistakenly used, are solely in terms of practice; and that [the phrases] "sitting upright, the ten modes," and "together with my single thought, illuminate it horizontally and vertically" are precisely gathering all the three dharmas into a single thought of the mind to form the perfect contemplation, which is the contemplation of the mind attached to dharmas.

Chinese Source

故《妙玄》四諦，因緣之後攝諸法相入一念心用觀照之。並是彼文為成妙行，特立觀心一科之意。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore in the four truths and the [twelve] links of the Wondrous Profound [Meaning], afterward the dharma-marks are gathered into a single thought of the mind and contemplated and illuminated. This is all the intent of that text, in order to accomplish the wondrous practice, specially setting up the one section on contemplating the mind.

Chinese Source

既被詰難書難云：此玄既全無一念及觀照之語。乃是為成己義，任情曲撰。上人既撿正釋及料揀全無此義，計窮理極，遂推與行人自能攝入一念橫竪照之。

Ru-Yi Translation

Then the Letter Pressing Difficulties challenged you: "This Profound [Meaning] has no words at all about 'a single thought' or 'contemplating and illuminating.' To accomplish your own meaning you compose them crookedly at will." The Venerable, having examined the proper explanation and the sorting and sifting and found no such meaning at all, your reckoning exhausted and your principle at its end, then pushed it off onto the practitioner, [saying that] he can himself gather [them] into a single thought and illuminate them horizontally and vertically.

Chinese Source

若爾者，何獨此玄行人自攝自照，妙經等諸玄疏豈彼行人不能自攝自照，何故法相之後皆立觀心一科，攝法入心方名觀行？

Ru-Yi Translation

If so, why is it only for this Profound [Meaning] that the practitioner gathers by himself and illuminates by himself? In the profound [meanings] and commentaries on the Wondrous Sūtra and the rest, can the practitioners not gather and illuminate by themselves? Why then, after the dharma-marks, do they all set up the one section on contemplating the mind, so that only gathering dharmas into the mind is called contemplative practice?

Chinese Source

若謂諸文或有闕略者，亦須立乎體式之後，方可準例略之。如十二因緣曾立觀法，四諦之後但云觀心可解。既此兩境合有觀心，故於餘境避繁省略。則稟教之者既見諦緣之後有於觀心，則知諸境合皆攝歸己心以觀照之。

Ru-Yi Translation

If you say that some of the various texts lack or abbreviate it, still a pattern must first be set up, and only then can it be abbreviated by analogy. For example, for the twelve links of dependent arising a method of contemplation was once set up, and after the four truths it only says, "the contemplation of the mind can be understood." Since these two objects should have contemplation of the mind, for the other objects it is abbreviated to avoid complexity. Then those who receive the teaching, having seen that there is contemplation of the mind after the truths and the links, know that all the objects should be gathered back into one's own mind and contemplated and illuminated.

Chinese Source

今此玄文單談十種果證法相，略未示於觀心體式，何得行人自攝自照？此乃上人錯下一念及觀照之語。自見無文、自知無義，只得推與行人。若直顯心性之語者，奈自立云此玄文直顯心性，且推與行人不得，遂全不敢答酬此問，仍潛改直顯心性而作直顯法性也。上人若稍轉改有路，終不偷換文字，蓋是路極遂至於此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now this Profound text discusses only the dharma-marks of the ten sets realized at the fruit, and does not show the pattern of contemplating the mind in the least; how could the practitioner gather by himself and illuminate by himself? This is the Venerable having mistakenly used the words "a single thought" and "contemplating and illuminating"; seeing for yourself that there is no text and knowing for yourself that there is no meaning, you could only push it off onto the practitioner. As for the words "directly reveals the nature of mind": what can you do, since you yourself set up "this Profound text directly reveals the nature of mind," and could not push it off onto the practitioner? So you did not dare answer this question at all, and still secretly changed "directly reveals the nature of mind" into "directly reveals the dharma-nature." If the Venerable had even a slight road for turning and changing, you would never have stealthily swapped the words; it is because the road was at its end that it came to this.

Chinese Source

故知暗心推劃，如盲者自行，動皆罣礙。皆由不知觀心之意故至此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore we know that groping in the dark is like a blind man walking on his own: every move meets obstruction. All this comes from not knowing the intent of contemplating the mind.

The Vimalakīrti Commentary Examines the Mind

Chinese Source

又答疑書及今來義狀，堅執《淨名疏》釋法無眾生，結為三種解脫無非觀心，不須更作觀心釋之。欲將此十種三法亦具觀心義，不須更立觀心一科。此更不知彼此文意也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the Letter Answering Doubts and the statement of meaning now sent firmly insist that the Commentary on the Vimalakīrti, explaining "the Dharma has no living beings," concludes that the three kinds of liberation are none other than contemplating the mind, and does not need a further explanation by contemplating the mind. You wish [to say that] these ten sets of three dharmas likewise contain the meaning of contemplating the mind and do not need a further section on contemplating the mind. This is again not knowing the intent of either text.

Chinese Source

且《淨名疏》與此玄文雖同明三法，須知立義永殊。彼約研心作觀，觀成稱理，依體起用。而談，故具觀心義。此約佛果己證之理而說，安可得同？

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, although the Commentary on the Vimalakīrti and this Profound text both clarify the three dharmas, you must know that the meanings they set up are entirely different. That one, in terms of examining the mind and making contemplation, contemplation accomplished and according with principle, giving rise to function on the basis of the essence, discusses [the three dharmas], and therefore contains the meaning of contemplating the mind. This one speaks in terms of the principle the Buddha has realized at the fruit; how can they be the same?

Chinese Source

既被詰難書用心佛高下難之，朗然已墮，不肯首伏，遂強據三無差義救之。意云：心佛既其無差，佛法便是心法。作此救義，又彰上人不識三法無差所以也。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the Letter Pressing Difficulties used the high and low of mind and buddha to challenge you, you had plainly already fallen; unwilling to submit, you then forcibly relied on the meaning of the three being without difference to rescue it. The idea is: since mind and buddha are without difference, the Buddha-dharma is the mind-dharma. In making this rescue, the Venerable further shows that he does not recognize why the three dharmas are without difference.

Chinese Source

The Venerable originally set up the ten sets of three dharmas of this Profound [Meaning] as purely discussing the contemplation of principle and directly revealing the nature of mind, surpassing the ten dharmas of the Wondrous Profound [Meaning]; now they are on the contrary wholly the same as the dharma-marks of ordinary people and the two vehicles in the Sequential Gates of the Dharma-Realm. Although that text has dharma-marks of the Great Vehicle, do those who study the three contemplations not also pass through the dharma-marks of the ordinary and the small with the mind and cultivate? Yet your further pushing it off onto the practitioner is clearly the Venerable's false speech. Why? The Letter Answering Doubts plainly said that because the Profound text directly reveals the nature of mind, all the dharma-marks are fused in a single thought of the mind and illuminated horizontally and vertically. And you yourself said: "You should know that what this Profound [Meaning] discusses is not only the perfect fusion of dharma-marks, but also the clarity of the contemplation of principle." Since you said "the Profound text directly reveals" and further said "what this Profound [Meaning] discusses," how can you now stubbornly turn it into the practitioner on his own gathering the dharma-marks back into the mind and on his own illuminating them horizontally and vertically in calming-and-contemplation? Treating the supreme teaching lightly and playing with it like this, speaking false and embellished words before all the sages — is there no future retribution? Is there no present retribution? If one looks at this one passage of deviant account in the Letter Answering Doubts, I fear that even if the Great Master set it out with his four kinds of unobstructed eloquence for the length of an eon, the faults of slandering the Dharma and deceiving your own mind could not be exhausted.

I earnestly hope that the Venerable will turn his mind toward principle, cultivate merit to make up for his faults, support the correct teaching together [with me], and benefit the multitude of beings far and wide. Whether you agree or not, I hope for a quick reply. Your answer concerning "king of sūtras" is enough to show a deceiving mind; your reply on "the thing itself" further shows shifted reckoning. Or perhaps the deviant thesis was shown first so that the correct account would be made manifest afterward. The Venerable now meets the correct path; you must change the deluded thesis. If you go against your own mind, you will surely incur evil retribution. I earnestly offer this encouragement and speak up again and again, only wishing to make our own school shine. Reading Notes for Part 3

Translator's notes

Stages (units 1–7): The "five grades" (五品) are the five grades of disciple at identity in contemplative practice; "rejoicing" (隨喜) is the first of them. The Two Hundred Questions (questions 159–160) presses the same point. "Seventy sheets" (unit 7) is the length of the Letter Answering Doubts.

"Cultivation two, nature one" (units 8–11): 修二性一 and "separation and combination" (離合) follow the round-two Two Hundred Questions (questions 165–166). "Knowing the mind is without mind" (知心無心) is a phrase of the Golden Light Profound Meaning section on contemplating the mind. In unit 11, 刀刀 ("repeatedly") is probably a copying error for 叨叨.

The Commentary on the Vimalakīrti (units 31–44): Zhiyi's commentary explains "the Dharma has no living beings" by the contemplation of the emptiness of beings passing through the aggregates, which Zhili counts as contemplation attached to dharmas. The two passages in units 39–40 ("Later translators rendered [the name] as 'Pure and Stainless Fame'…" and "clarifying the three bodies by contemplating the mind…") are from the Profound Meaning of the Vimalakīrti (T1777), which is not among the vault's Taishō files, so the quotation was not checked. The parenthesis in unit 39 is Zhili's own note on why the teaching "belongs to phenomena." "The three kinds of buddha-body are born from the Vaipulya" follows the round-two Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra; "mercury mixed with real gold" is from the Great Treatise as cited in the Textual Commentary and Mohe Zhiguan.

The Great Forest Monastery (unit 46): The Commentary on the Avalokiteśvara Invocation Sūtra contemplates the setting of that sūtra, the Great Forest Monastery at Vaiśālī, as an example of contemplation relying on phenomena. The name follows the round-two draft of the sūtra.

The Annotations phrase (units 61–69, 86–94): 不待觀境 is rendered "one need not wait for the object of contemplation" to keep open the readings the two sides dispute: Qingzhao takes it as "need not wait for the object of the aggregates" (later, "for sifting the consciousness-mind"); Zhili as "need not wait for Calming and Contemplation to show the object specifically." The round-two Annotations draft has "one need not wait to contemplate an object".

"The contemplations of the five li" (unit 63, 五里之觀): The sentence refers to the section on contemplating the mind within the seven divisions of common explanation of the Profound Meaning of the Lotus, which contemplates the five layers (name, essence, purport, function, teaching). 五里 is probably a copying error for 五重 "five layers"; it is kept literally in the translation. "When contemplation is accomplished in a single mind, it is passed on to teach the other minds" follows Part 1; "the mind is like an illusion, like a flame" and "one aggregate belongs to form…" were not found word for word in T1716.

Jingxi on teachers (unit 80): "If one is near a teacher, one should in principle consult him on doubts," "complete in practice and understanding, the virtue lies with him," and "how would one be fit merely to be a Vaipulya teacher for the world?" are in the Fuxing (T1912). "If one suddenly has no teacher, one should use this text and the Gates of Dhyāna to test the marks of good and evil" was not found word for word. "The five conditions" (unit 75) are the five preparatory conditions of Calming and Contemplation, the last of which is drawing near a good friend.

Proverbs (units 84, 95): "The traveler first laughs, and afterward must wail" is from the Book of Changes, hexagram 56 (Lü, "The Traveler"). "Biting one's navel" means regret that comes too late. "The suffering of the plowed tongue" is the hell punishment for slander.

The missing character (unit 85): The CBETA text has a lacuna (□) before 單將三因消之; the translation marks it as "[one character missing]."

The Lotus and the three tracks (units 100–102): "Combined, exclusive, contrasted, and accompanied" (兼但對帶) follows the glossary. The quotation in unit 102 follows the site's Fahua Xuanyi ("clarifying the three tracks in general"), adjusted only so that the three tracks are the subject. "To make the teachings of the Buddha's entire lifetime merge and pass into the wondrous" (unit 104) follows the vault draft of the Ten Gates of Nonduality (also in the Annotations, T1717).

The Sequential Gates (unit 117): The Sequential Gates of the Dharma-Realm (T1925) opens by saying it is written so that students can accomplish the three contemplations; the phrase 為成三觀 was not found word for word.

Closing (unit 119): "King of sūtras" (經王), "the thing itself" (當體), the "essence of contemplation in the two sūtras" (兩經觀體), and "the two truths" are further points from Qingzhao's letter that Zhili lists without answering; the Two Hundred Questions takes them up. "The late teacher" is Wu'en. "Joins his palms and bows" renders 和南 (vandana).
