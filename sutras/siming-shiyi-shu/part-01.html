Prefaces and Meanings One to Three: The Method, the Mind, and the Two Objects

The Letter of Ten Meanings is the letter Zhili sent in the twelfth month of Jingde 3 (early 1007) to Qingzhao of Fantian Monastery, the leading "off-mountain" critic in the dispute over Zhiyi's Golden Light Profound Meaning. Wu'en's Bringing Out [the Meaning] had declared the expanded version's section on contemplating the mind a later addition; Zhili's Supporting the School defended it; five letters followed on each side. This part opens with two prefaces, one by the Japanese editor Gizui (1736) and one by Zhili's Dharma-grandson Jizhong (1076). Zhili's letter then lists ten failings and takes up the first three. First, the method that contemplates: if the ten sets of three dharmas were the contemplation of principle, they would sift the consciousness-mind and set out the ten modes; they do not, so they are teaching and must be followed by a contemplation of the mind. Second, the mind contemplated: the "nature of mind" belongs to the cause, and the object is the deluded single thought of the aggregates, not a true mind neither defiled nor pure. Third, inner and outer objects: form as well as mind contains the three thousand, inner contemplation stays on the inner mind, and Indra's net illustrates four ways of contemplating inner and outer. Quoted speakers are marked throughout.

Ru-Yi Translation

The path of contemplation of the perfect school is vast and far-reaching, deep and absolute. One must receive the opening and resolving of a master of the school, and further sift it carefully oneself; only then can one penetrate it well. Should it be otherwise, even renowned monks and old masters lose the correct road — how much more will beginners and later students rarely fail to enter a deviant path! Formerly the late teacher [Wu']en of Ciguang also lectured on the Avataṃsaka. Not going deeply into his own teaching and wrongly using another school, he rashly fixed a single thought as the true, and from this the object and contemplation of our present school were greatly disordered. Thus he caused the perfect discussion of the dharma-nature to be mistaken for the direct revelation of the nature of mind, and abolished the expanded version of the Golden Light Profound [Meaning].

Ru-Yi Translation

Thereupon the Venerable of Siming, regretting [the decline of] the correct teaching and pitying the ignorant who would come after, exerted himself to question and answer back and forth with Master Zhao of Fantian, five rounds on each side. The present Letter of Ten Meanings gathers the texts of those five rounds and brings them to completion. Once one opens this letter, not only can the whole course of the exchange be clearly seen, but also settling the object and cultivating contemplation, relying on phenomena and attaching to dharmas, wondrous understanding and wondrous practice, form-only and mind-only — whatever in the path of contemplation was obscured in that time — become brilliantly clear. Ah! Because of the deviant teachings of the off-mountain [masters], the correct meaning of contemplating the mind was, on the contrary, revealed in that illustrious age. It is like a boar rubbing against a golden mountain, or the wind enlarging the qiuluo [insect]. Fortunately this letter once came down to this country, yet it is still not widely spread in the world, and no one knows it to be a supreme treasure.

Ru-Yi Translation

When it came to our Preceptor Li, he lectured on it and praised it again and again, and students of the four quarters all recognized it as the shining banner of the path of contemplation. In the present time, if one wishes to understand the doctrine of Tiantai and Jingxi, one must rely on Siming as one's compass; if one wishes to understand Siming's texts, one should first thoroughly master the Ten Meanings and Pointing to the Essentials. If these two instructions are not mastered, one will not be clear on the path of contemplation of our present school. However, the currently circulating [copies of the] Letter of Meanings have no few copying errors, and the characters of the interlinear notes are crowded and hard to see, which readers find a burden. Now I have corrected it and put it again on pear and jujube [blocks], further setting Master [Ji]zhong's outline above the text, to spare students the labor of copying it in. If later students read this letter carefully and diligently, earnestly and devotedly, they will be able to explain the doctrine and penetrate the path of contemplation freely and without obstruction. It is like splitting bamboo: once the first joint is split, the remaining joints all give way without difficulty. Students must not neglect this.

Preface to the Letter of Ten Meanings

Chinese Source

十義書之所由作者，有宋景德之前，《光明玄》廣略二本並行於世。錢唐慈光恩師製記曰《發揮》，專解略本，謂廣本有十法觀心，乃後人擅添爾。天台重解帝王之文輒評，謂有四失：一曰理乖、二曰義疎、三曰詞鄙、四曰事誤，廣破如《續遺記》也。有二弟子即錢唐奉先清師、嘉禾靈光敏師，共構難詞造二十條，輔成師義共廢廣本。

Ru-Yi Translation

The origin of the composition of the Letter of Ten Meanings is this. Before the Jingde [era] of the Song, the expanded and abbreviated versions of the Golden Light Profound [Meaning] circulated together in the world. The late teacher [Wu']en of Ciguang in Qiantang composed a record called Bringing Out [the Meaning], solely explaining the abbreviated version, and said that the ten kinds of contemplating the mind in the expanded version had been added presumptuously by later people. Tiantai's restated explanation of the passage on "emperor-king" was rashly criticized and said to have four faults: first, the principle is contrary; second, the meaning is loose; third, the words are vulgar; fourth, the facts are mistaken — refuted at length as in the Record Continuing the Gleanings. [Wu'en] had two disciples, Master Qing of Fengxian in Qiantang and Master Min of Lingguang in Jiahe, who together composed words of objection in twenty items, supporting their teacher's meaning and together abolishing the expanded version.

Chinese Source

錢唐寶山善信法師，奉書敦請法智評之。法智遜云：「夫評是議非則近於諍競，非我志也。矧以二師學解有聞，盡吾宗之先達，奚可率爾而拒之哉。」信法師重請曰：「法鼓競鳴，何先何後。夫當仁不讓於師，況餘人乎。」堅讓不免，故有《扶宗》釋難之作，專救廣本十種觀心，兼斥不解發軫揀境之非、觀成歷法之失。

Ru-Yi Translation

Dharma Master Shanxin of Baoshan in Qiantang sent a letter earnestly requesting Fazhi to assess it. Fazhi modestly declined, saying: "To assess what is right and discuss what is wrong comes close to contention; that is not my wish. Moreover, these two masters are known for their learning and understanding, and are both seniors of our school; how could I rashly oppose them?" Dharma Master [Shan]xin requested again, saying: "The Dharma drums are beating in competition; what is first and what is last? 'In matters of humaneness, one does not yield even to one's teacher' — how much less to others!" Firmly declining yet unable to escape, he therefore composed Supporting the School, resolving the difficulties, solely to rescue the ten kinds of contemplating the mind in the expanded version, and also rejecting the error of not understanding the sifting of the object at the setting out, and the fault [concerning] passing through dharmas when contemplation is accomplished.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Master Zhao of Fantian in Qiantang and Master Yuan of Manao [Cloister] on Gushan were both students of Fengxian's school. They composed Discerning Errors to test the wrongs of [Supporting the School's] resolving of difficulties and to rescue the merits of Bringing Out [the Meaning]. Fazhi, keeping the courtesy of modest humility, composed the Letter Questioning Doubts to press them; Master Zhao, without deference, replied with the Letter Answering Doubts. Fazhi again issued the challenge of the Letter Pressing Difficulties; Master Zhao composed the answer of the Five Meanings. Fazhi again made the reproof of the Letter Questioning Doubts. Master Zhao delayed for more than a year; Fazhi again issued the Letter of Repeated Questions to urge an answer; Master Zhao then had the present Explaining Difficulties, which turned out to be an unworthy text.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Back and forth, five on each side, extending over seven years: gathering the texts of the ten rounds, before and after, together formed the present work, the Letter of Ten Meanings. There are further the two hundred repeated questions, which do not go beyond the five falls into defeat and four shifts of reckoning, early and late. First, [Zhao] pointed to the proper explanation in terms of the teaching and made it belong to contemplating the mind as the contemplation of principle. Fazhi rejected it, saying: "Substituting teaching for contemplation, unexpectedly it turned into having contemplation without teaching." The second shift of reckoning said: "One must know that the contemplation of principle directly contemplates the true mind," and the very substance of the Golden Light was on the contrary called a deluded dharma. Fazhi rejected it, saying: "Contemplating the mind lies in delusion, yet you say it belongs to the true; the very substance, a dharma of the fruit, you say belongs to the deluded." The third shift of reckoning, to rescue the single fault of the true mind, changed the name "true mind" to "dharma-nature," meaning that the dharma-nature extends to both the true and the deluded; [he] blended them by following conditions, supporting this with the two creations, in phenomena and in principle. Mind belongs to neither the true nor the deluded; beings and buddhas are the true and are the deluded. In rescuing the earlier true mind, [it is] also not solely true or solely deluded. Because it is not solely deluded, he rejected Fazhi's opposition. The fourth shift of reckoning said: "The wondrous principle of the ten modes is the object contemplated." Fazhi refuted it, saying: "Should the three obstructions and four māras then be the contemplation that contemplates?" The shifting of reckoning thus fell into defeat. Together with the initial root [position], these make five rounds.

Further, Fazhi cited "clarifying the object of the aggregates again" to challenge him, and Master Zhao submitted, saying: "Concerning the contemplation of the aggregates in Calming and Contemplation, I failed to examine [it]. Zhongni said: 'Can one fail to comply with words of correct admonition? Reforming is what is valuable; now I reform it.'" Fazhi went on to encourage him, saying: "The Venerable has some native intelligence and can divide the sections and divisions; why not go all the way? Discarding the short and following the long, seeing the skillful and knowing the crude — now that you accept contemplating the aggregates, through whom did you come to know it?"

Ru-Yi Translation

In the fourth year of Jingde, Master Yuan of Gushan was the head seat under Master Zhao's wheel. Fazhi sent Great Master Shenzhao, Benru, of Mount Dongye, who was under his own wheel, to hurry with the Letter of Ten Meanings together with the Two Hundred Questions to Qiantang and press them. Master Shi of Kuaiji hoped to assist and support him in debating together. Gushan, seeing that the debate of the two masters was like facing an enemy face to face, and that [the other side] would surely double its mat, considered himself a dragon of meaning — how would he be willing to submit to a deer? He hurriedly reported to the prefect of Qiantang, who answered with an official document, and [the letters] were not forwarded. Although this only stopped the doubling of the debating mat, [as for] the separately circulated Profound [Meaning]: even if Māra burns the Buddha's sūtras, he still cannot burn the good of inherent virtue; so how could the scrolls of the eternal teaching be wiped out?

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, in the Xining [era] of the Song, some eighty years apart, this text has risen again and circulates widely in the world. Many students and teachers west of the Zhe lecture on and study this text, so it has been possible to gather craftsmen and carve blocks, to spare students the labor of copying. I, Jizhong, first-generation [Dharma-]grandson, abiding at Faming Cloister in Yongjia, instructing my disciples, have compared and assessed the two copies, the manuscript and the printed. Within its meaning, there are phrases lacking or superfluous and characters in error. Formerly, when I hastened to the court of Guangzhi, I was each time instructed earnestly, as if he were pulling my ear and pointing to my palm. Now, dividing the text according to the meaning, I have presumptuously assessed and settled it, perhaps with [some] right and [some] wrong. I hope that those of the same study and the same view will further serve as a guide for students. Preface written on the sixteenth day of the mid-winter month in the ninth year of Xining.

Ru-Yi Translation

On the sixteenth day of the twelfth month of the third year of Jingde, Zhili, a śramaṇa and bhikṣu of Siming, respectfully, with a mind devoted to the Dharma, puts questions on the meaning to the lecture master of Zheyang, the Venerable Zhao (before his seat). On the twenty-third day of the tenth month, the two, Lai and Wen, entered my room and delivered one scroll, the Letter Explaining Questions, which at length constructs coarse words, wishing to block the objections that come. Having set up a thesis and fallen of itself, it merely drags in the teachings — to what end? It is all talk without meaning, entirely words that deceive others. If I were to press [you] following the text [point by point], I fear the major points would be hard to make clear; therefore, on the single section of contemplating the mind, I set up objections in ten sections. Moreover, the Venerable has always displayed a lack of deference, priding himself on Discerning Errors and Answering Doubts. This humble monk has long harbored much modesty, taking requests for benefit and consultation as courtesy; therefore, whether the questions be many or few, the answers must be thorough.

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, I was recently challenged with the Five Meanings; since I at once took up its drift, I now set the objections in ten gates. Do not make excuses to let the words lie; cease laboring to search texts in many collections and spending more than a year making plans. Please straightforwardly and sincerely set out the meaning, and reply quickly according to your understanding. I would be most fortunate, most fortunate. (The Venerable's statements of meaning, before and after, have each taken two years. If the meaning had long been clear, you would never have delayed to this point. It is because you search everywhere through the teaching collections and only then construct views and knowledge, often running contrary to your own school and wholly injuring the wondrous path. I wish you would think of the reply to come, and not give rein to a flattering mind.)

Ru-Yi Translation

Discerning Errors originally held that the ten sets of three dharmas of this Profound [Meaning] purely clarify the contemplation of principle and need not contemplate by attaching to phenomena (and so on).

Chinese Source

Jingxi says: "Such as constant sitting and so on: sometimes they contemplate principle only. Following one's own inclination: from the branch, from phenomena." Since you say it purely clarifies the contemplation of principle, then it is the three kinds of samādhi, which have one cultivate the ten modes specifically on the aggregate of consciousness.

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the Letter Answering Doubts says: "This text of the Profound [Meaning] directly reveals the nature of mind."

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, the three kinds of contemplative method all reveal the nature of mind. But since the two contemplations of phenomena and dharmas contemplate the mind by relying on the meaning of phenomena, or contemplate the mind by attaching to the marks of dharmas, they are not direct revelation. Only the contemplation in terms of practice is direct: on the mind of the aggregates it reveals the nature of the three thousand; only this is called directly revealing the nature of mind. According to these two letters, [you] definitely judge the ten sets of three dharmas of this Profound [Meaning] to be already the contemplation in terms of practice, and so [hold that] the present contemplation of the mind attached to dharmas can be abolished.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Since it is the contemplation of principle in terms of practice, directly revealing the nature of mind, why does the text of the ten sets of three dharmas not sift and show the consciousness-mind as the object? Why does it lack the ten dharmas forming the vehicle? Since this meaning is entirely absent, it is definitely not the contemplation of principle.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the text itself solely discusses the dharma-marks of the buddha at the fruit; it is definitely not directly revealing the nature of mind. The Venerable originally said that because it is already the contemplation in terms of practice, the one section on contemplating the mind can be abolished. Since it is not the contemplation in terms of practice, how can the later text on contemplating the mind be rashly abolished? (Even if you wished to cling to the contemplation of the mind in the various texts, it would only form the meaning of the contemplations of phenomena and dharmas, never the contemplation of principle — let alone that you cannot even cling to them.)

Chinese Source

此則觀心一科已不可廢也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus the one section on contemplating the mind already cannot be abolished.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

又上人數斥《扶宗》唯立識心為境，以三觀觀之，使性德開發之義，謂不知心具三千。亦由上人素不諳境觀藥病，致見頻有妄破也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the Venerable repeatedly rejects Supporting the School's meaning — setting up only the consciousness-mind as the object and contemplating it with the three contemplations, so that inherent virtue is opened and developed — saying that [I] do not know that mind contains the three thousand. This too is because the Venerable has never been familiar with medicine and illness in object and contemplation, so that false refutations frequently appear.

Chinese Source

具如《止觀》去於丈尺唯取於寸，乃是於事造中去其所造，取能造以為所觀之境，故云伐樹得根、灸病得穴。乃是去其千枝百脈，唯取一根一穴立所觀境，故云先重明境。故《扶宗》云：以一念識心為境也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In full: Calming and Contemplation's setting aside the ten feet and the foot and taking only the inch is, within creation in phenomena, setting aside what is created and taking what creates as the object contemplated; therefore it says "felling a tree by getting at its root, treating an illness with moxa by finding the point." That is setting aside its thousand branches and hundred vessels and taking only the one root and the one point to set up as the object contemplated; therefore it says "first clarifying the object again." Therefore Supporting the School says: "taking a single thought of the consciousness-mind as the object."

Chinese Source

至明修觀，乃於能造陰識明具三千，三千是假。《大意》云：此能造心具足諸法。故《輔行》云：心具足假。此之三千，非法性無明自他共離而造，故約此空假遮照不偏名中道。豈非不思議境義含三觀。發心中既全依此理，豈不具三義耶？安心中遂以三止三觀總別安之，此去三觀之義節節轉明也。故知十乘無不以空假中而為大體也。故云以一念識心為境，以三觀觀之。應知廣則十乘、略則三觀，故云不思議境。望後九觀，名所觀境；望前陰識，則妙境并下九乘同是能觀之三觀也，即《輔行》次明修觀文也。

Ru-Yi Translation

When it comes to clarifying cultivating contemplation, it clarifies that the consciousness of the aggregates that creates contains the three thousand, and the three thousand are the provisional. The Gist says: "This mind that creates is complete with all dharmas." Therefore the Fuxing says: "Mind being complete is the provisional." These three thousand are not created by the dharma-nature or by ignorance, by self, other, both, or apart [from both]; therefore, in terms of this, the empty and the provisional, negating and illuminating without partiality, are named the middle way. Does not the meaning of the inconceivable object include the three contemplations? Since arousing the mind relies wholly on this principle, how can it not contain the three meanings? In settling the mind, it then settles it with the three calmings and three contemplations, in general and in particular; from here on the meaning of the three contemplations becomes clearer section by section. Therefore we know that none of the ten modes does not take the empty, the provisional, and the middle as its great essence. Therefore [Supporting the School] says: "taking a single thought of the consciousness-mind as the object, and contemplating it with the three contemplations." You should know that extensively it is the ten modes, briefly the three contemplations; therefore it says "the inconceivable object." Viewed from the following nine contemplations, it is named the object contemplated; viewed from the preceding consciousness of the aggregates, the wondrous object together with the nine modes below are all the three contemplations that contemplate. This is the Fuxing's text "next clarifying cultivating contemplation."

Chinese Source

良由上人殊不知陰入境與不思議境分於能觀所觀，用藥治病之義故也。

Ru-Yi Translation

This is really because the Venerable does not at all know that the object of the aggregates and entrances and the inconceivable object are divided as what is contemplated and what contemplates — the meaning of using medicine to treat illness.

Chinese Source

應知於能造識心觀具三千，此之三千是灸病之火、是伐樹之斧、是捨重擔之法、是破三賊之將、是觀識心之三觀，故《輔行》云：今文妙觀觀之，令成妙境，境方稱理。又云：若以正觀安之，世諦方成不思議也。故未觀未安，全是迷中陰入，何得未論觀法便自說具三千？若也定境便說三千，至修觀法更何所論？

Ru-Yi Translation

You should know that, on the consciousness-mind that creates, one contemplates that it contains the three thousand. These three thousand are the fire of the moxa that treats the illness, the axe that fells the tree, the method of setting down the heavy burden, the general who destroys the three thieves, the three contemplations that contemplate the consciousness-mind. Therefore the Fuxing says: "The present text contemplates it with the wondrous contemplation, making it become the wondrous object; only then does the object accord with principle." And further it says: "If one settles it with the proper contemplation, the conventional truth only then becomes inconceivable." Therefore, before contemplating and before settling, it is entirely the aggregates and entrances within delusion; how can one, before discussing the contemplative method, speak of containing the three thousand? If one speaks of the three thousand when settling the object, what more would there be to discuss when it comes to cultivating the contemplative method?

Chinese Source

又如《輔行》消丈尺寸喻，先將三科中唯取識陰對之，後復云若探取不思議境合此喻者，則以一念十界三科為丈等釋之。既云探取不思議意對喻，故知不思議境是次科之意也。定境之時未合正論三千妙法也。若不爾者，何故名探取不思議耶？又十境不出三障四魔，今之定境報障陰魔，因何未論破障降魔之法，便自說三千耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, as the Fuxing interprets the simile of ten feet, foot, and inch: first it matches it with taking only the aggregate of consciousness among the three categories; afterward it further says: "If one draws out the inconceivable object to fit this simile, then the three categories of the ten realms in a single thought are explained as the ten feet and so on." Since it says one draws out the inconceivable [object] to match the simile, we know that the inconceivable object is the intent of the next section. At the time of settling the object, one ought not yet properly discuss the wondrous dharma of the three thousand. If not so, why is it called drawing out the inconceivable? Further, the ten objects do not go beyond the three obstructions and four māras; the present settling of the object is the obstruction of retribution and the māra of the aggregates. Why, before discussing the methods of breaking obstructions and subduing māras, do you yourself speak of the three thousand?

Chinese Source

祇如於識陰修圓三觀者，約何義說假觀耶？豈說緣生假耶？豈說建立之假耶？既修此等之假，仍須即陰說具三千方為妙假。故荊溪云：具即是假。又《妙經疏》以十二入為境，各具千如為觀。若非此假，則空中亦淺，全非圓觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, for one who cultivates the perfect three contemplations on the aggregate of consciousness, in what meaning is the provisional contemplation spoken of? Would one speak of the provisional of arising from conditions? Would one speak of the provisional of establishing? Even if one cultivates these kinds of provisional, one must still speak of containing the three thousand right in the aggregates for it to be the wondrous provisional. Therefore Jingxi says: "Containing is the provisional." Further, the Commentary on the Wondrous Sūtra takes the twelve entrances as the object and each containing the thousand suchnesses as the contemplation. If it were not this provisional, the empty and the middle would also be shallow, entirely not the perfect contemplation.

Chinese Source

故《扶宗》云：以一念識心為境，用三觀觀之，使性德開發。既是圓教三觀，自合約於三千論其空中也。復明開發性德金光明，豈不含三千妙理耶？何得以未詳之文破已解之語。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore Supporting the School says: "taking a single thought of the consciousness-mind as the object, contemplating it with the three contemplations, so that inherent virtue is opened and developed." Since these are the three contemplations of the perfect teaching, of course they discuss the empty and the middle in terms of the three thousand. Further, it clarifies opening and developing the Golden Light of inherent virtue; how does that not include the wondrous principle of the three thousand? How can you refute words that are already explained with a passage not yet detailed?

Chinese Source

上人今立非染非淨之真心為能造，染淨諸法為所造，意以能造所造俱為所觀之境便謂深得止觀之意。乃錯認之甚也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Venerable now sets up the true mind, neither defiled nor pure, as what creates, and defiled and pure dharmas as what is created, intending to take both what creates and what is created as the object contemplated, and so thinks he has deeply grasped the intent of calming-and-contemplation. This is a great mistake.

Chinese Source

以彼簡却所造，唯取能造為境，乃是去其千枝百脈，唯取一根一穴以為所觀。若俱取者，大乖揀境之意也。

Ru-Yi Translation

For that [text] sifts out what is created and takes only what creates as the object, which is setting aside the thousand branches and hundred vessels and taking only the one root and the one point as what is contemplated. If one takes both, it greatly departs from the intent of sifting the object.

Chinese Source

又彼特立總無明心一念陰識為境。上人固違彼說，自將非染非淨真心為境，還順宗師之教否？又彼立染緣熏起九界心為境，上人乖宗，自立淨緣所成佛界心為境，還順宗師之教否？天台荊溪負上人何辜，而苦見違返毀滅耶？以此驗知，實不識所觀之心也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, that [text] specially sets up the mind of ignorance in general, the single thought of aggregate-consciousness, as the object. The Venerable stubbornly goes against what it says and himself takes the true mind, neither defiled nor pure, as the object — does that still follow the teaching of the masters of our school? Further, it sets up the mind of the nine realms, stirred up by defiled conditions, as the object; the Venerable departs from the school and himself sets up the mind of the buddha-realm, formed by pure conditions, as the object — does that still follow the teaching of the masters of our school? What wrong have Tiantai and Jingxi done the Venerable, that you so bitterly oppose and destroy them? From this we verify that you truly do not recognize the mind that is contemplated.

Chinese Source

今就上人邪說，用正義示之，幸請尋省。非染非淨真心者，不思議境也。能成淨緣，十乘妙觀也。佛界心者，妙觀之果，常住陰也。能熏染緣，無始無明也。九界心者，無明之果也，生死陰也。生佛雖各由緣，而染緣所成本有。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, taking up the Venerable's deviant teaching, I will show it with the correct meaning; please examine it. The true mind, neither defiled nor pure, is the inconceivable object. What can form pure conditions is the wondrous contemplation of the ten modes. The mind of the buddha-realm is the fruit of the wondrous contemplation, the eternal aggregates. What can stir up defiled conditions is beginningless ignorance. The mind of the nine realms is the fruit of ignorance, the aggregates of birth-and-death. Although beings and buddhas each come about through conditions, what is formed by defiled conditions is originally present.

Chinese Source

故荊溪云：清濁雖即由緣，而濁成本有也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore Jingxi says: "Although clear and muddy depend on conditions, the muddy, as it forms, is originally present."

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, wishing to reveal the wondrous principle, one must break cause and fruit within the defiled; therefore the mind of ignorance in general, the single thought of aggregate-consciousness, is the object, and the contemplation of the ten modes breaks it, so that the wondrous principle within the defiled appears and becomes the eternal aggregates of the buddha-realm.

Ru-Yi Translation

How can the Venerable take the mind of the buddha-realm formed by pure conditions, and the wondrous principle revealed, as the mind and object contemplated? If you take this dharma as what is contemplated, what dharma will you take as what contemplates?

Chinese Source

上人本立十種三法，已純談理觀故，廢於附法觀心。十法之中既不簡示陰識為所觀境，又不明於十乘，何名純談理觀？既非理觀，後文觀心安可輒廢耶？能破之宗既壞，觀心之義何傷。若欲改轉，速請相聞。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Venerable originally set up the ten sets of three dharmas as already purely discussing the contemplation of principle, and therefore abolished the contemplation of the mind attached to dharmas. Since it is not the contemplation of principle, how can the later text on contemplating the mind be rashly abolished? Since the thesis that refutes is destroyed, what harm comes to the meaning of contemplating the mind? If you wish to change and shift, please let me hear quickly.

第三不分內外二境

Ru-Yi Translation

Third: Not Distinguishing the Two Objects, Inner and Outer

Form Also Contains the Three Thousand

Chinese Source

夫性具三千，雖有依正色心已他，而皆融泯，舉一全收。無始迷故，全理成事，定分內外，彼此角立也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, in the three thousand inherently included, although there are dependent and principal, form and mind, self and other, all are fused and dissolved; raising one, all are wholly gathered in. Because of beginningless delusion, the whole of principle becomes phenomena, definitely divided into inner and outer, this and that standing opposed like horns.

Chinese Source

若依實教修觀行者，必須於事解理、以理攝事，故了萬法唯心，亦了萬法唯色、萬法唯聲、唯香、唯味、唯觸等。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one cultivates contemplative practice according to the real teaching, one must understand principle in phenomena and gather phenomena by principle; therefore one understands that the myriad dharmas are mind-only, and also understands that the myriad dharmas are form-only, the myriad dharmas sound-only, scent-only, taste-only, touch-only, and so on.

Chinese Source

故修內觀時，先用圓解攬於萬法唯我內心，然後專於內心而觀諸法。若宜修外觀者，亦須先攬萬法唯一色等，然後專於一境而觀諸法。故觀內心則一切法趣心，若觀外色則一切法趣色，是則只一非內非外之三千，隨乎觀慧趣內趣外不同。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore, when cultivating inner contemplation, one first uses perfect understanding to grasp that the myriad dharmas are only my inner mind, and afterward contemplates all dharmas solely in the inner mind. If it is fitting to cultivate outer contemplation, one must also first grasp that the myriad dharmas are only one form and so on, and afterward contemplate all dharmas solely in one object. Therefore, if one contemplates the inner mind, all dharmas tend toward mind; if one contemplates outer form, all dharmas tend toward form. Thus there is only the one three thousand that is neither inner nor outer, which, according to the contemplating wisdom, differs in tending inward or tending outward.

Chinese Source

若不爾者，趣色趣聲之教如何可解？唯色唯心之觀云何修耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

If not so, how could the teaching of tending toward form and tending toward sound be understood? How could the contemplation of form-only and mind-only be cultivated?

Chinese Source

上人雖云唯色，色即是心。堅執觀於外色，亦只趣於內心。又定云心具三千，色不具三千。

Ru-Yi Translation

Although the Venerable says, "Form-only: form is mind," he firmly holds that contemplating outer form also only tends toward the inner mind. And further he definitely says that mind contains the three thousand, and form does not contain the three thousand.

Chinese Source

若爾，應是外色非性本具，舉色不全收諸法耶？若不全收，何名色為法界耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

If so, then outer form would not be originally contained by the nature, and raising form would not wholly gather in all dharmas? If it does not wholly gather them in, how is form called the dharma-realm?

Chinese Source

若色不具三千者，《妙經文句》何故十二入各具千如為萬二千法門耶？且十二入中，唯有一分半屬心，十分半屬色。若如上人所說，只合有一千五百法門，則大師剩說一萬五百也。又《金錍》云：生佛依正，一念具足，一塵不虧。

Ru-Yi Translation

If form does not contain the three thousand, why does the Textual Commentary on the Wondrous Sūtra [say] that the twelve entrances each contain the thousand suchnesses, making twelve thousand Dharma-gates? Moreover, among the twelve entrances, only one and a half belong to mind, and ten and a half belong to form. If it were as the Venerable says, there should only be one thousand five hundred Dharma-gates, and the Great Master would have spoken ten thousand five hundred too many. Further, the Vajra Scalpel says: "Beings and buddhas, dependent and principal, are complete in a single thought, nothing lacking in a single mote of dust."

Chinese Source

又《輔行》云：若色心相對則有色有心，論其體性則離色無心、離心無色。若色心相即，二則俱二、一則俱一。故圓說者亦應得云唯色唯聲唯香唯味唯觸等，何但獨得云唯識耶？若合論者，無不皆悉具足法界。復次若從末說，一切眾生二種不同，上界多著識、下界多著色。若約識，唯識攬外向內，令觀內識皆是一識。識既空已，十界皆空；識若假者，十界皆假；識若中者，十界皆中。專於內心觀一切法，觀外十界即見內心，是故當知若色若識皆是唯色，若識若色皆是唯識。雖說色心，但有二名；論其法體，只是法性（文見《輔行》）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the Fuxing says: "If form and mind are set against each other, then there is form and there is mind; speaking of their essential nature, apart from form there is no mind, apart from mind there is no form. If form and mind are identical, when two, both are two; when one, both are one. Therefore one who speaks perfectly should also be able to say form-only, sound-only, scent-only, taste-only, touch-only, and so on; why may one say only consciousness-only? If spoken of together, none is not entirely complete with the dharma-realm. Further, if spoken of from the branch, all sentient beings are of two different kinds: those of the upper realms are mostly attached to consciousness, those of the lower realms mostly attached to form. In terms of consciousness, consciousness-only grasps the outer and turns it inward, making one contemplate the inner consciousness as all one consciousness. Once consciousness is empty, the ten realms are all empty; if consciousness is provisional, the ten realms are all provisional; if consciousness is the middle, the ten realms are all the middle. Solely in the inner mind one contemplates all dharmas; contemplating the outer ten realms, one sees the inner mind. Therefore one should know: whether form or consciousness, all is form-only; whether consciousness or form, all is consciousness-only. Although form and mind are spoken of, there are only two names; speaking of their dharma-essence, it is only the dharma-nature." (The text is found in the Fuxing.)

Chinese Source

今據此教文。若內若外、若心若色，趣觀一境皆具三千，以互具互收故也。令著外色者專觀內心，外既歸內，外著則亡。著內心者令專觀外色，內既趣外，內著方祛。外觀本治內著，若還攝外歸內，則彌增內著，重添他病。良可痛哉。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, according to this teaching text: whether inner or outer, whether mind or form, tending toward and contemplating one object, each contains the three thousand, because they mutually contain and mutually gather in. Those attached to outer form are made to contemplate solely the inner mind; once the outer returns to the inner, attachment to the outer disappears. Those attached to the inner mind are made to contemplate solely outer form; once the inner tends toward the outer, attachment to the inner is dispelled. Outer contemplation originally cures attachment to the inner; if one still gathers the outer and returns it to the inner, it only increases attachment to the inner and adds a further illness. How truly lamentable!

Contemplating One Pearl of Indra's Net

Chinese Source

又上人堅破，若修內觀恐心外向，所修外觀恐心內向。以為內外相隔非是圓融，則有彼彼草木各一佛性之過也。意謂修內觀時既具三千，三千便是外境，則外諸事境一時遍觀方為圓觀也。皆由上人不諳內外二境不分而分，故妄有破斥也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the Venerable firmly refutes [me], [saying that if] in cultivating inner contemplation I fear the mind turning outward, and in cultivating outer contemplation fear the mind turning inward, then inner and outer are separated from each other and not perfectly fused, and there would be the fault of each and every plant and tree having its own buddha-nature. The idea is that when cultivating inner contemplation, since [mind] contains the three thousand, and the three thousand are outer objects, one must contemplate all outer phenomenal objects pervasively at once for it to be perfect contemplation. All this is because the Venerable is not familiar with how the two objects, inner and outer, are divided without being divided; hence he falsely refutes.

Chinese Source

須知以性融攝故，則內境外境有相趣之義。約事分內外故，則觀內不可放心緣外，觀外不可放心緣內。

Ru-Yi Translation

You must know that because the nature fuses and gathers in, inner and outer objects have the meaning of tending toward each other; because in terms of phenomena inner and outer are divided, in contemplating the inner one may not let the mind take the outer as its object, and in contemplating the outer one may not let the mind take the inner as its object.

Chinese Source

如修內觀，先用妙解了知外法同趣內心，即但於內心觀三千性德。

Ru-Yi Translation

For example, in cultivating inner contemplation, one first uses wondrous understanding to understand that outer dharmas likewise tend toward the inner mind, and then simply contemplates the inherent virtue of the three thousand in the inner mind.

Chinese Source

故《四念處》云：專於內心觀一切法。若放心緣外，則不名專於內心修乎三觀也。又《大意》云：此能造心，具足諸法。實不遍緣於所造外境修觀。內觀既爾，外觀豈不然乎。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore the Four Foundations of Mindfulness says: "Solely in the inner mind one contemplates all dharmas." If one lets the mind take the outer as its object, it is not called cultivating the three contemplations solely in the inner mind. Further, the Gist says: "This mind that creates is complete with all dharmas." It really does not take the created outer objects pervasively as objects in cultivating contemplation. Since inner contemplation is like this, how is outer contemplation not so?

Chinese Source

故修外觀，如觀一塵，亦須先用妙解，了知內心及一切法同趣一塵，但於一塵觀一切法。

Ru-Yi Translation

故《起信論》云：諸佛法身更無彼此色相迭相見故。既無他佛，即無他生。正報既泯，依報豈存。雖三千宛然，絕內外相也。若餘一品無明，則須微有自他之相。故《起信論》云：由轉識故見有他佛。既見他佛，豈無他生？正報既存，依報寧泯？故將已成之內觀，歷外事境任運泯合也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore the Awakening of Faith says: "Because the Dharma-bodies of all buddhas have no form-marks of this and that by which they see each other." Since there are no other buddhas, there are no other beings. Since the principal [reward] has dissolved, how could the dependent [reward] remain? Although the three thousand are fully present, the marks of inner and outer are cut off. If one grade of ignorance remains, there must be slight marks of self and other. Therefore the Awakening of Faith says: "Because of the evolving consciousness, one sees other buddhas." Since one sees other buddhas, how could there be no other beings? Since the principal [reward] remains, how would the dependent dissolve? Therefore one passes through outer phenomenal objects with the already accomplished inner contemplation, and they spontaneously dissolve and merge.

Chinese Source

故《淨名》云：觀身實相，觀佛亦然。豈非以內例外耶？分真尚爾，相似、觀行可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore the Vimalakīrti says: "[As I] contemplate the true reality of [my own] body, so too [I] contemplate the Buddha." Is this not taking the inner as an example for the outer? [Identity in] a portion of the truth is still so; resemblance and contemplative practice can be known.

Chinese Source

上人堅執云：唯觀識性具三千法。則三千之外更無一法，豈得別存一事境在心外，而待識陰理顯方遍融耶？須知此說全不得初心修觀之意也。何者？既不許觀外色等法，恐心外向，唯觀內心理具之義，則須若內若外一時遍觀，方名觀於性具三千。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Venerable firmly holds, saying: "Only contemplate that the nature of consciousness contains the three thousand dharmas; then outside the three thousand there is no other dharma. How could a phenomenal object be kept separately outside the mind, waiting for principle to be revealed in the aggregate of consciousness before it is pervasively fused?" You must know that this teaching does not at all grasp the intent of cultivating contemplation at the initial mind. Why? Since contemplating outer form and other dharmas is not allowed, for fear that the mind turns outward, and only the meaning of the inner mind's inclusion in principle is contemplated, [you hold that] one must contemplate inner and outer pervasively at once for it to be called contemplating the three thousand of inherent inclusion.

Chinese Source

若爾，豈唯觀成無外法可歷。理未顯時，若修外觀，亦無一法可為所觀，外色等法已為內觀遍觀畢故。此則內外兩觀皆不成也。不專內故，內觀不成；無外事境故，外觀不成。

Ru-Yi Translation

If so, not only would there be no outer dharma to pass through after contemplation is accomplished; even when principle is not yet revealed, if one cultivates outer contemplation, there would also be no dharma to be the object contemplated, because outer form and other dharmas have already been pervasively contemplated by inner contemplation. Thus neither inner nor outer contemplation would be accomplished: because it is not solely inner, inner contemplation is not accomplished; because there are no outer phenomenal objects, outer contemplation is not accomplished.

Chinese Source

若如一家教文所談修觀，成與未成皆須遍歷諸法也。若修內觀，先用妙解攝外法入心，但觀內心具諸法性。攝法之義既成，專內之義又成。若修外觀，亦先用妙解攝諸法及內心入外色等一法，修唯色等觀。攝法之義既成，唯專一境之義又成。豈非二觀俱有所以也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for cultivating contemplation as discussed in the teaching texts of our school, whether accomplished or not, all must pervasively pass through all dharmas. If one cultivates inner contemplation, one first uses wondrous understanding to gather outer dharmas into the mind, and only contemplates the inner mind as containing the nature of all dharmas. The meaning of gathering in dharmas is then established, and the meaning of being solely inner is also established. If one cultivates outer contemplation, one also first uses wondrous understanding to gather all dharmas and the inner mind into one dharma such as outer form, and cultivates the contemplation of form-only and so on. The meaning of gathering in dharmas is then established, and the meaning of being solely on one object is also established. Is it not that both contemplations have their reason?

Chinese Source

若修唯識，觀成理顯。既見內心攝諸法已，則將己證之心歷外諸法，自然見於諸法皆趣外色、皆趣外心。故《義例》云：先了萬法唯心，方可觀心能了諸法（此於內心能了諸法，則外色等趣心名不二，名為內心淨也），則見諸法唯色唯心（一切法趣外色名唯色，一切法趣他心名唯心。淨心歷外自然見泯合相也）。又如內外不二門結成不二云：是則外法全為心性，心性無外攝無不周（此是觀心顯性也。與內心諸法不二）。十方諸佛法界有情，性體無殊一切咸遍（此是佛法遍攝及生法遍攝，各各遍攝，故云一切咸遍。正報既爾，依報遍攝亦然。既云咸遍，豈不與內心泯合耶）。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one cultivates consciousness-only, contemplation is accomplished and principle revealed. Having seen that the inner mind gathers in all dharmas, one then passes through outer dharmas with the mind one has realized, and naturally sees that all dharmas tend toward outer form and all tend toward outer mind. Therefore Guiding Principles of Calming and Contemplation says: "first understands that the myriad dharmas are mind-only, and only then can one contemplate the mind; able to understand the dharmas (this is the inner mind being able to understand the dharmas: outer form and so on tending toward mind is called nonduality, and is called the inner mind being pure), one sees that the dharmas are mind-only and form-only" (all dharmas tending toward outer form is called form-only; all dharmas tending toward the minds of others is called mind-only; the pure mind passing through the outer naturally sees the mark of dissolving and merging). Further, as the gate of the nonduality of inner and outer concludes nonduality, saying: "Thus outer dharmas are wholly the nature of mind. The nature of mind has no outside; it gathers in everything, leaving nothing out" (this is contemplating the mind and revealing the nature; it is nondual with the dharmas of the inner mind). "The buddhas of the ten directions and the sentient beings of the dharma-realm do not differ in the essence of their nature, which pervades all" (this is the Buddha-dharma pervasively gathering in and the dharma of beings pervasively gathering in, each pervasively gathering in; therefore it says "pervades all." Since the principal [reward] is so, the dependent [reward] pervasively gathering in is likewise. Since it says "pervades all," how does it not dissolve and merge with the inner mind?).

Raising One Pearl Gathers In All

Chinese Source

故帝網一喻，可喻內外四種之義。何也？以修內外觀時各須隨舉一珠遍收眾珠。觀成遍歷，豈不隨舉一珠收眾珠耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore the single simile of Indra's net can illustrate the four meanings of inner and outer. Why? Because when cultivating inner and outer contemplation, each must, raising whichever one pearl, pervasively gather in all the pearls. In pervasively passing through after contemplation is accomplished, how is it not raising whichever one pearl that gathers in all the pearls?

Chinese Source

《示珠指》解心性無外攝無不周句，舉此喻云：如舉一珠眾珠收盡。至解十方諸佛等句，只云：生佛一如空無內外也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Pointing to the Pearl, explaining the phrase "the nature of mind has no outside; it gathers in everything, leaving nothing out," raises this simile, saying: "As when one pearl is raised, all the pearls are gathered in entirely." But when it comes to explaining the phrase "the buddhas of the ten directions" and so on, it only says: "Beings and buddhas are one suchness, empty, without inner and outer."

Chinese Source

且三法各妙，必無優劣。心法既得為一珠遍收眾珠，生佛何不各喻一珠收眾珠耶？況帝網之喻本顯依正色心內外己他舉一全收之義，以曲會己見，遂令圓喻偏歸一邊也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, the three dharmas are each wondrous and certainly without superior and inferior. Since the mind-dharma can be one pearl pervasively gathering in all the pearls, why can beings and buddhas not each be likened to one pearl gathering in all the pearls? Moreover, the simile of Indra's net originally reveals the meaning that dependent and principal, form and mind, inner and outer, self and other — raising one, all are wholly gathered in; [but] bending it to fit your own view, you make the perfect simile return one-sidedly to one side.

Chinese Source

應知內觀成歷外法者，則任運舉一全收也。若以正觀心歷事作觀者，亦復隨觀一法全收諸法也。若修內觀不入轉修外觀者，亦須舉一全收也。

Ru-Yi Translation

You should know that for one who, with inner contemplation accomplished, passes through outer dharmas, raising one spontaneously gathers in all wholly. For one who passes through phenomena and makes contemplation with the mind of proper contemplation, also, according to whichever one dharma is contemplated, all dharmas are wholly gathered in. For one who cultivates inner contemplation, does not enter, and turns to cultivate outer contemplation, also raising one must gather in all wholly.

Chinese Source

若本著內心發軫便修外觀者，亦須舉一全收也。

Ru-Yi Translation

For one originally attached to the inner mind who cultivates outer contemplation at the setting out, also raising one must gather in all wholly.

Chinese Source

如此方稱帝網之喻，方得名為一色一香無非中道（中以不偏為義。若香等當體，不具三千、不收諸法、不為諸法所趣，則是偏義，不成中義也）。若只內心具於三千能攝諸法，外色不具三千不收諸法，那成佛之時正中現依、依中現正？若法性不爾，修時不然，至果方爾者，則全成有作，非稱性也。又若初心修觀，不專內心便和外色觀者，是則內觀已觀外色，何須更歷外法而觀耶？

Only thus does it fit the simile of Indra's net, and only thus can it be called "a single form, a single scent — none of it is anything other than the middle way" (the middle means not being partial. If scent and so on, in their very substance, did not contain the three thousand, did not gather in all dharmas, and were not what all dharmas tend toward, that would be the meaning of partiality and would not form the meaning of the middle). If only the inner mind contains the three thousand and can gather in all dharmas, while outer form does not contain the three thousand and does not gather in all dharmas, how at the time of attaining buddhahood would the dependent appear within the principal and the principal appear within the dependent? If the dharma-nature were not so and the time of cultivation not so, but it became so only at the fruit, it would be entirely fabricated, not according with the nature. Further, if the initial mind, cultivating contemplation, did not solely [contemplate] the inner mind but contemplated it mixed with outer form, then inner contemplation would already have contemplated outer form; why would one need further to pass through outer dharmas and contemplate them?

Chinese Source

須知但觀內心即空假中已，自然見外一一色心皆空假中也。故《輔行》云：攬外向，內令觀內識皆是一識。識既空已，十界皆空。假中亦然。既觀識空已方云十界皆空等也，則知修內觀時不放心觀外，乃是外法自然趣內也。淨心歷外、正觀觀外、內法趣外，豈不然耶？

You must know that simply contemplating the inner mind as identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle, one naturally sees each and every outer form and mind as empty, provisional, and middle. Therefore the Fuxing says: "Grasping the outer and turning it inward, making one contemplate the inner consciousness as all one consciousness. Once consciousness is empty, the ten realms are all empty." The provisional and the middle are likewise. Since it says "once consciousness is empty" and only then "the ten realms are all empty" and so on, we know that when cultivating inner contemplation one does not let the mind go to contemplate the outer; it is that outer dharmas naturally tend toward the inner. The pure mind passing through the outer, the proper contemplation contemplating the outer, the inner dharma tending toward the outer — how are they not so?

Chinese Source

又上人今既堅將內觀為理、外觀為事。十法文中既不簡示陰入內心為境，又無十法成乘，何名純談理觀？若非理觀，安可廢附法觀耶？論宗既破，更欲何言。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the Venerable now firmly takes inner contemplation as principle and outer contemplation as phenomena. Since the text on the ten dharmas does not sift and show the inner mind of the aggregates and entrances as the object, and further has no ten dharmas forming the vehicle, how can it be called purely discussing the contemplation of principle? If it is not the contemplation of principle, how can the contemplation attached to dharmas be abolished? Since the thesis of your discussion is refuted, what more do you wish to say?

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

The two prefaces: Unit 1 is by the Japanese Tendai editor Gizui (義瑞), dated Genbun bingchen (1736); "this country" is Japan. "Our Preceptor Li" (吾立和尚) is not identified; read in Japanese the name would be Ritsu. "Tangshan" (唐山) and the bracketed "(Kei)" (（慶）) in his signature are rendered as written. Unit 5 is by Jizhong (繼忠), Zhili's Dharma-grandson and compiler of the Two Hundred Questions, dated Xining 9 (1076); "Master Zhong's outline" (忠師科) in unit 1 is his. "Guangzhi" (廣智) is Zhili's disciple Shangxian (尚賢), under whom Jizhong studied.

The parties: "The Venerable" (上人) is Qingzhao (慶昭) of Fantian Monastery, Qiantang, as in the round-two Two Hundred Questions; "Master Yuan of Gushan" is Zhiyuan (智圓); "[Wu']en of Ciguang" is Wu'en (晤恩); "Master Qing of Fengxian" and "Master Min of Lingguang" are Yuanqing (源清, author of Pointing to the Pearl) and Hongmin (洪敏). Fazhi (法智) is Zhili's title. Benru (本如) of Dongye was Zhili's disciple.

The letters: English titles follow the round-two Two Hundred Questions. Zhili's are the Letter Questioning Doubts (問疑書), Letter Pressing Difficulties (詰難書), Letter of Repeated Questions (覆問書), and the present letter; Qingzhao's are Discerning Errors (辨訛), the Letter Answering Doubts (答疑書), the Letter of Five Meanings (五義書), and the Letter Explaining Difficulties (釋難書). Unit 8 calls Qingzhao's latest letter 釋問書, rendered Letter Explaining Questions; it is probably the same as the Explaining Difficulties of the preface. In the preface 問疑書 appears twice, the second time perhaps for another title. "Statement of meaning" (義狀) is Zhili's general word for a letter setting out a position.

Quotations reused from earlier drafts: Guiding Principles of Calming and Contemplation (T1913, "first [take] the inner mind… the path of contemplation gradually opens," unit 150) follows the vault draft of the Zhiguan yili, except that 遍歷 and 任運泯合 follow the round-two Two Hundred Questions ("pervasively passes through," "spontaneously dissolve and merge"), since Zhili's argument turns on these words. Passages from the Ten Gates of Nonduality (units 150, 156, 162–163, 177, 179) follow the vault draft; "the three realms have no separate dharma of their own — they are made by the one mind alone," "mind, buddhas, and sentient beings — these three are without any difference," and "those of superior faculties, with one contemplation…" follow the vault draft of the Gist (T1914); "beings and buddhas, dependent and principal… nothing lacking in a single mote of dust" and "pointing one-sidedly to a pure suchness" follow the vault draft of the Vajra Scalpel, with 依正 rendered "dependent and principal" per the glossary. "Sit upright and be mindful of the true mark…" follows the round-two Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra; the Golden Light Profound Meaning passages (units 47–48, 55) follow the round-two draft of that text.

Quotations reused from the site: "[As I] contemplate the true reality of [my own] body, so too [I] contemplate the Buddha" (unit 173) follows the site's Vimalakīrti Sūtra, "Seeing Akṣobhya Buddha"; the site has "true reality" for 實相 here. "A single form, a single scent — none of it is anything other than the middle way" (unit 183) follows the site's Mohe Zhiguan, which has "color" for 色; "form" is kept here because the whole argument is about form and mind. "The seed of the Tathāgata" (unit 79) follows the site's Vimalakīrti.

Sources located: Jingxi's "such as constant sitting and so on…" (units 11, 28), the long form-and-mind passage (unit 106, also largely in the Four Foundations of Mindfulness, T1918), "deluded, one speaks of inner and outer" (unit 126), "the present text contemplates it with the wondrous contemplation…" (unit 85), and "only because of inclusion in principle is there phenomenal function" (unit 79) are in the Fuxing (T1912). Miaole's "properly one should contemplate the aggregates…" and "the objects of all contemplations do not go beyond the five aggregates" are in the Notes on the Textual Commentary (T1719). "Principle is originally without difference; difference is in terms of phenomenal function" (unit 68) is in the Annotations (T1717, which reads 理體無差). "The Buddha-dharma is too lofty…" and "when contemplation is accomplished in a single mind…" are in the Profound Meaning of the Lotus (T1716). "Without applying effort, spontaneously clear and distinct" is Mohe Zhiguan on the first grade (T1911). "The mind creates the tathāgatas" is the Avataṃsaka (T278).

Sources not located word for word: the Fuxing sentences "mind being complete is the provisional," "containing is the provisional," "if one settles it with the proper contemplation…," and "if one draws out the inconceivable object…" (units 83–87) were not found verbatim in T1912; the Gist sentence "this mind that creates is complete with all dharmas" paraphrases T1914 ("this that creates is complete with all dharmas"). The second Awakening of Faith quotation, "because of the evolving consciousness one sees other buddhas" (unit 172), was not found in T1666 and seems to be Zhili's paraphrase; the first is in T1666. Pointing to the Pearl (units 165, 179) is not in the Taishō.

Similes in the Japanese preface: "A boar rubbing against a golden mountain" makes the gold shine brighter, and the qiuluo (迦羅求羅) is an insect that grows larger in the wind; both are in the Great Treatise (T1509). The point is that opposition made the correct teaching clearer.

Who speaks in unit 150: Zhili copies out a passage of Guiding Principles of Calming and Contemplation together with Qingzhao's interlinear notes on it (unit 149, "I write out in full the text of your statement of meaning"; unit 151, "all this is the Venerable annotating…"). The parenthetical notes in unit 150 are therefore Qingzhao's, not Zhili's; they claim that passing through dharmas is itself cultivating contemplation, the view Zhili refutes in units 151–177. A bracketed line has been added to say so.

Unit 151, punctuation: The CBETA punctuation attaches 內心若淨 to the following phrase; the translation reads it as the end of the Guiding Principles of Calming and Contemplation phrase being matched ("one must first [take] the inner mind; if the inner mind is pure"), so that 以彼歷眾事，一一緣中皆表勝法 is the Vaipulya phrase. The words are unchanged.

Unit 71, 何曾將佛界心不為境: Read as a rhetorical question ("when did they ever take the mind of the buddha-realm as an object?"), which fits the sarcasm that follows. Read strictly, the 不 would give the opposite sense; it may be a copying error.

Unit 174, the subject: Zhili describes his own position (not contemplating outer form during inner contemplation, for fear the mind turns outward) and then Qingzhao's conclusion from it. The translation leaves the first half impersonal and adds "[you hold that]" before the second. Alternatively, the whole sentence may describe Qingzhao's view.

Unit 5, the end of the debate: "Double its mat" (重席) alludes to the Han scholar Dai Ping, who took the mats of debaters he defeated. "[The letters] were not forwarded" (不為遣也) is one reading; the clause could also mean that the prefect did not send the messengers away. "The separately circulated Profound [Meaning]" (別行玄) is taken as the subject of the following sentence.

Unit 5, the Record Continuing the Gleanings: 續遺記 is not otherwise known; it may refer to Zhili's Gleanings on the Profound Meaning of the Golden Light Sūtra, which answers Gushan's four points ("the principle is contrary," "the meaning is loose," "the words are vulgar," "the facts are mistaken," wording as in the round-two Gleanings).

Terms: 約行／附法／託事 "in terms of practice / attached to dharmas / relying on phenomena," 理觀 "the contemplation of principle," 直顯心性 "directly reveals the nature of mind," 陰境 "the object of the aggregates," 揀境 "sifting the object," 轉計 "shift [one's] reckoning," and 泯合 "dissolving and merging" follow the Gleanings and the Two Hundred Questions. 理具 / 事造 / 性具 are "inclusion in principle / creation in phenomena / inherent inclusion"; the verb 具 is "contain."
