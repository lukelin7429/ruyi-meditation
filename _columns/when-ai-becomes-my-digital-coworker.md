# When AI Becomes My Digital Coworker

When we hear the word "employee," most of us naturally think of a person. We imagine someone sitting in an office, receiving instructions, handling documents, organizing information, editing videos, updating websites, and then bringing the work back for review. But over the past six months, my understanding of that word has changed dramatically. AI agents are no longer just tools for conversation. They are beginning to function like digital employees, able to take control of a computer, follow instructions, and complete many tasks from beginning to end.

For me, this has been a major shift. In the past, many things required me to sit in front of the computer and do them step by step: preparing documents, editing videos, organizing files, updating websites, proofreading content. None of these tasks were impossible, but they consumed a great deal of time and mental energy. Now, with AI agents, I can explain the goal clearly, let the agent produce a first draft or an initial version of the work, and then review, correct, and refine it. Many tasks no longer have to begin from zero, and I no longer have to handle every small step myself.

In other words, my role is gradually changing from the person who does every task by hand to the person who supervises, judges, and guides the work. This does not mean human beings no longer need to work. It means the nature of work is changing. Before, what mattered most was how quickly I could operate the computer and how long I could keep my eyes fixed on the screen. Now, what matters more is whether I know clearly what I want, whether I can give good instructions, whether I can judge the quality of the result, and whether I can point out what needs to be corrected. AI can do many things, but the final direction and responsibility still belong to the human being.

In fact, calling an AI agent a "digital employee" only describes its most basic function. At times, it can become something much more valuable: a sounding board, a strategic advisor, even a high-level consultant. When an idea is still unclear in my mind, I can ask it to help me organize the logic. When I am preparing to write an article, I can ask it to lay out short-term, mid-term, and long-term plans for comparison. When I am uncertain about a decision, I can ask it to offer different perspectives. It is not always completely right, but it can very quickly provide a knowledgeable, experienced, and directionally useful response. Used properly, it helps us avoid unnecessary detours and improves the quality of the final result.

This also has important implications for education. In the future, AI agents will not only serve as digital employees in the office. They may also become personal tutors for individual learners. Whether the subject is mathematics, English, physics, computer science, or even Buddhist scriptures, as long as a student is willing to learn, AI can design lessons according to that student's level, explain ideas step by step, and provide patient guidance one question at a time. Good teaching is not simply giving students the answer. It is understanding where they are stuck and guiding them in a way that fits their needs. In the past, this kind of one-on-one, personalized learning was available only to a small number of people. AI may make it possible for far more children to receive that kind of support.

For me personally, the greatest change brought by these digital coworkers is not simply greater efficiency. It is the release of time. I now have more time to read Buddhist scriptures, write, exercise, and think about what I truly want to accomplish next. In the past, computer work swallowed a large part of each day. If AI can take over many of those repetitive tasks, then my energy can return to more important things.

Still, AI is a double-edged sword. Used well, it can increase productivity, raise quality, and support education and public service. Used poorly, it can become a tool for fraud, misinformation, laziness, or even harm. If a person lacks judgment, compassion, and a clear moral direction, then the more powerful the tool becomes, the more dangerous it may be.

That is why the real question in the age of AI is not simply whether machines can help us work. The deeper question is what we will do with the time we save. A digital employee can help us complete many tasks, but it cannot decide the direction of our lives for us. It can serve as an assistant, an advisor, or a consultant, but it cannot become the master of our life.

The more advanced technology becomes, the more human beings need to return to the heart. As more external tasks can be assisted by AI, we should ask ourselves: Do I now have more time to cultivate my body and mind? Can I do more things that benefit others? Can I use my life for what is truly worthwhile? If the answer is yes, then AI is not merely a tool for efficiency. It may become a helpful condition for human beings to cultivate greater wisdom and do more good in the world.
