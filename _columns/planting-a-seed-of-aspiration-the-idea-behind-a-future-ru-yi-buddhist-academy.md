# Planting a Seed of Aspiration: The Idea Behind a Future Ru-Yi Buddhist Academy

Lately, one idea has been returning to my mind again and again: if the right causes and conditions come together, perhaps in two years Ru-Yi Meditation Center can begin preparing a Buddhist academy.

This is not a formal announcement. It is not a promise that everything is already decided. It is not an attempt to add another program, another title, or another signboard to the center.

It feels more like a direction that has been growing naturally out of what Ru-Yi has already been doing.

Since 2017, the venerables at Ru-Yi have been leading study groups and helping people learn the Dharma step by step. Since 2018, Ru-Yi has also offered its summer camp for children. Over the years, we have watched children grow from campers into counselors. We have also watched members of the study group gradually become able to lead discussions, share what they have learned, and take on greater responsibility.

These changes have moved me deeply. They show that Buddhist learning is not only about sitting in a room and listening to teachings. If learning is alive, it changes a person. It helps a person become more willing to serve, more willing to take responsibility, and more willing to share what they have received with others.

## If the Internet Is Full of Dharma, Why Build an Academy?

Some people may naturally wonder: why would Ru-Yi need a Buddhist academy at all?

Today, anyone can find Dharma talks online. Many teachers are giving lectures. Many Buddhist centers and monasteries in Taiwan already have well-developed programs, trained instructors, and much larger resources. If so much is already available, why should a small mountain center like Ru-Yi even consider this?

My answer is simple: causes and conditions are personal.

The fact that teachings are available does not mean that people will actually study them. The fact that other institutions have excellent programs does not mean that the people connected to Ru-Yi will naturally walk through those doors.

Each person encounters the Dharma through a different set of conditions. Some people are connected to a teacher. Some are connected to a place. Some are connected to a community. Some people begin to learn only because someone nearby gently encourages them and walks with them for a while.

The Dharma is precious, but studying the Dharma is not always easy. Many people are not unwilling to learn. They simply do not have a steady structure, a group of companions, or a living environment that keeps inviting them back to the path.

That is why the idea of a Ru-Yi Buddhist Academy matters. It is not meant to compete with other Buddhist institutions. It is not meant to replace any existing form of Buddhist education. It would simply provide, within Ru-Yi's own field of conditions, a more steady and gradual path of learning for those who already feel connected to this place.

## Learning Needs Rhythm

In modern life, inspiration often comes and goes quickly.

We hear a Dharma talk and feel moved. We read a passage and feel encouraged. We attend a retreat and feel renewed. But then ordinary life returns. Work, family, responsibilities, and old habits pull us back into familiar patterns.

This is why learning needs rhythm.

If Buddhist study remains occasional and scattered, it may bring comfort, but it may not deeply reshape a life. A person needs regular classes, a clear sequence of study, reading assignments, opportunities for reflection, and companions who are walking in the same direction.

If a future Ru-Yi Buddhist Academy can take shape, one of its most important contributions would be structure. Regular classes, thoughtful curriculum design, assessments, and certificates are not valuable because of formality itself. They are valuable because they help people place Dharma learning into the rhythm of their lives.

Human beings need reminders. We also need encouragement. When there is a schedule, a class, a teacher, a community, and a goal, many people discover that their desire to learn was not absent. It was simply waiting for the right conditions to awaken.

This may be especially meaningful for retirees, or for people whose lives have become more settled and who are asking how to use the second half of life well. Life is not only work, family duties, and daily routines. To use one's time to deepen wisdom, understand the Dharma, and benefit others is a meaningful way to live.

## Teaching Also Trains the Teacher

A Buddhist academy would not only benefit those who attend classes. It would also help Ru-Yi Meditation Center grow.

In recent years, some members of the study group have begun to practice leading discussions and sharing what they have learned. If a more formal academy becomes possible, these people would have a larger field of responsibility. They would need to prepare, organize their understanding, learn how to speak clearly, and gradually receive guidance from the venerables and other teachers.

This is one of the best forms of training.

When we study only for ourselves, we may feel that we understand something. But when we must explain it to others, our understanding is tested. We have to think more carefully. We have to be more honest about what we know and what we do not know. We have to examine whether our confidence in the Dharma is clear or merely emotional.

In this way, teaching and learning support each other.

If more people at Ru-Yi can study, explain, guide, and serve, then the transmission of the Dharma will not depend only on a few individuals. It can gradually become a shared responsibility carried by a community.

## From Camp to Study Group to Academy

Over the past several years, Ru-Yi has already shown a visible pattern of growth.

Children first come to the summer camp as participants. They are cared for by teachers, counselors, and volunteers. A few years later, some of them return as counselors and begin caring for younger children. Some counselors gradually become able to lead activities and teach classes.

The study group has followed a similar pattern. People who once only listened have slowly begun to host sessions, share reflections, and guide others.

This is what healthy transmission looks like.

Transmission is not created by slogans. It needs a place. It needs tasks. It needs trust. It needs patient training. When a person moves from being cared for to caring for others, and from listening to the Dharma to sharing the Dharma, something important has changed in that person's life.

If the summer camp plants seeds of goodness in children, and the study group helps adults grow roots in the Dharma, then a future Buddhist academy may become a place where those seeds and roots are given room to mature.

## Why Speak About It Two Years Early?

We are speaking about this idea now precisely because it is not yet complete.

The conditions are not all in place. The curriculum has not been finalized. The teachers, volunteers, schedule, administrative details, and long-term support all still need to be considered.

That is why a two-year preparation period is important.

Two years gives us time to ask the right questions. What kind of courses would be most helpful? What level of study should we begin with? Who can teach? Who needs to be trained? Who is willing to study more deeply? Who can help with administration, welcoming newcomers, communication, and service?

A Buddhist academy cannot be built by one person's enthusiasm. It needs the vows of the venerables, the care of teachers, the diligence of students, and the support of lay practitioners. More than anything, it needs a community that believes systematic Dharma learning still matters in this age.

Two years may sound like a long time, but it is not. If we begin now by forming aspiration, preparing carefully, and training people patiently, then perhaps the conditions will gradually come together.

## Let This Begin as a Seed

My hope for a future Ru-Yi Buddhist Academy is not that it becomes impressive on the outside.

I do not hope for a program that looks grand but remains shallow. I do not hope for another name that people can admire from a distance.

What I hope is much simpler: that more people will be able to study the Dharma steadily; that more people will form right understanding; that more people will discover, through learning and service, a deeper and more stable way to live.

In Buddhism, we often speak of causes and conditions. A good undertaking does not come into being through wishful thinking alone. It needs aspiration, but it also needs preparation. It needs patience, but it also needs action.

For now, we are simply planting a seed.

If, over the next two years, this seed receives enough sunlight, water, soil, and care, then perhaps when the conditions mature, a Ru-Yi Buddhist Academy can naturally come into being.

May this not be merely another plan.

May it become the next natural flowering of the learning, service, and transmission that have already been quietly taking root at Ru-Yi Meditation Center.
